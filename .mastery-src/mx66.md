## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to the Derivative's Definition

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to the derivative's definition: the difference quotient, first principles, and differentiability's relation to continuity in one page. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the theorems, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Definition:** f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [f(x + h) − f(x)]/h — the limit of chord slopes; equivalently lim_{x→a} [f(x) − f(a)]/(x − a).
- **One-sided derivatives:** f′₊(a), f′₋(a) from h → 0⁺, 0⁻; **f′(a) exists ⟺ both exist and agree.**
- **Differentiability ⟹ continuity** (proof: f(x) − f(a) = quotient × (x − a) → f′(a)·0 = 0); **the converse fails** (|x|).
- **First-principles toolkit:** create the h-factor (identities, conjugates, angle addition), cancel, take the limit with the stars.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — The Derivative as Local Linearisation

- f′(a) = L means **f(x) ≈ f(a) + L(x − a)** near a with error o(x − a): the tangent line is the best linear approximation, and this is the definition restated. Every standard limit star is a tangent statement — sin h ≈ h says (sin)′(0) = 1.
- **The failure gallery:** corners (|x|: one-sided slopes disagree), cusps (x^{2/3}: vertical tangent), oscillation (slopes swing without settling). Each species is continuous yet non-differentiable — the hierarchy continuous ⊃ differentiable ⊃ C¹ is strict.
- **Rates vs values:** continuity demands values converge; differentiability demands *rates* converge — a stricter contract comparing f at pairs of nearby points.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — The Denominator's Denominator

- Every first-principles proof is the same three moves: write the quotient, **create the factor h** in the numerator, cancel, finish with a star. The identity bank (difference of powers, conjugates, angle addition) is the list of h-making machines.
- **After cancellation, only stars remain:** first-principles proofs never invent limits — they reduce to sin h/h, (eʸ−1)/h, or plain substitution. The limits unit is the derivative unit's engine room.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — first principles, power:** difference of powers, cancel, star naⁿ⁻¹.
- **Type B — first principles, trig:** angle addition, split, two stars.
- **Type C — one-sided disagreement:** compute both, compare, verdict (corner).
- **Type D — diff ⟹ cont proof:** factor the difference, product of limits.

## 6. Common Confusions

**"Continuous ⟹ differentiable":** |x| forever. **"f′(a) is the slope at a":** yes — but it is *defined* by nearby pairs; the tangent is the limit of secants, not a given.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (6m): Prove from first principles that d/dx (x³) = 3x².** — **Quotient:** [(x+h)³ − x³]/h. **Create the h-factor (difference of cubes):** (x+h)³ − x³ = h[(x+h)² + (x+h)x + x²] ⟹ quotient = (x+h)² + (x+h)x + x² for h ≠ 0. **Limit (each term continuous in h):** x² + x² + x² = **3x²** ∎. **Cross-check:** the star naⁿ⁻¹ at n = 3 gives 3x² ✓. **Deep point:** the difference-of-cubes identity exposed the h; after cancellation the limit was plain substitution — first principles never invent limits, they reduce to them.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Compute the one-sided derivatives of f(x) = |x − 1| at x = 1 and classify the point.** — **RHD:** for h > 0, [|1+h| − 1]/h = h/h = **+1**. **LHD:** for h < 0, [|1+h| − 1]/h = (−h)/h = **−1**. **Verdict:** one-sided derivatives exist but disagree ⟹ **f′(1) does not exist** — a **corner**: two competing tangent lines (slope ±1), no single tangent. **Continuity note:** |x − 1| is continuous at 1 (values converge) — the corner separates the two concepts exactly. **Deep point:** the one-sided test is the existence theorem applied to difference quotients; |x − a| is its canonical witness for every a.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Prove that differentiability implies continuity, and show the converse fails with a specific function.** — **Proof:** write f(x) − f(a) = [(f(x) − f(a))/(x − a)]·(x − a). As x → a: the bracket → f′(a) (exists by hypothesis) and (x − a) → 0 ⟹ by the product law, lim [f(x) − f(a)] = f′(a)·0 = **0**, i.e. lim f(x) = f(a) ∎. **Converse fails:** f(x) = |x| is continuous at 0 but f′(0) does not exist (one-sided slopes −1, +1). **Deeper:** x·sin(1/x) (f(0) = 0) is even differentiable at 0 with f′(0) = 0, yet its derivative is discontinuous — differentiability does not buy continuous differentiability. **Deep point:** three strictly nested classes — continuous, differentiable, C¹ — each separated by a named counterexample; the hierarchy is the map of the whole smoothness story.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Using first principles, prove that d/dx (1/x) = −1/x² for x ≠ 0.** — **Quotient:** [1/(x+h) − 1/x]/h = [x − (x + h)]/[h·x(x + h)] = **−h/[h·x(x+h)]** = −1/[x(x + h)] for h ≠ 0. **Limit:** as h → 0, x + h → x (continuity) ⟹ denominator → x·x ⟹ **f′(x) = −1/x²** ∎. **Cross-check:** the power rule at n = −1 gives −1·x⁻² ✓. **Deep point:** the h-factor emerged from combining the fractions — algebra (common denominators) is the first-principles toolkit's quietest tool; and the answer's shape (−1/x²) shows the derivative inherits the hyperbola's symmetry: odd function, even derivative.
@@summary@@
The derivative is defined as the limit of chord slopes — a rate contract stricter than continuity's value contract, with the tangent as the best local linear approximation. First-principles proofs are one pattern: create the h-factor, cancel, finish with a star. One-sided derivatives must exist and agree; differentiability implies continuity by a two-line factoring argument, while |x| and its cousins keep the converse false. The hierarchy continuous ⊃ differentiable ⊃ C¹ is the smoothness map every later theorem navigates by.
