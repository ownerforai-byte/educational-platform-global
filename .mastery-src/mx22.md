## 1. Topic Introduction — √(−1) Becomes Legitimate

The **imaginary unit i** with i² = −1 extends ℝ to ℂ, making every quadratic solvable. The theorems: algebra of complex numbers, **conjugate properties**, **modulus** |z| = √(x² + y²), and the geometric (Argand) representation where multiplication becomes rotation-and-scaling.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 The imaginary unit and standard form

- **i² = −1**; every complex number is **z = x + iy** (x = real part Re z, y = imaginary part Im z).
- Equality: x + iy = u + vi ⟺ x = u and y = v — real and imaginary parts match separately.
- **Theorem (quadratic solvability):** ax² + bx + c = 0 with negative discriminant has roots (−b ± i√|D|)/2a — ℂ is algebraically closed for quadratics.

### 2.2 Algebra of complex numbers

- Addition/subtraction componentwise; **multiplication:** (x + iy)(u + iv) = (xu − yv) + i(xv + yu) — expand and replace i² by −1.
- **Division:** multiply by the conjugate of the denominator: z₁/z₂ = z₁z̄₂/|z₂|².
- Powers of i cycle with period 4: i, −1, −i, 1 — iⁿ = i^(n mod 4).

### 2.3 Conjugate theorems

- **z̄ = x − iy**; z + z̄ = 2x (real), z − z̄ = 2iy (imaginary), **z·z̄ = |z|² = x² + y²**.
- **Theorems:** conjugation distributes: (z̄₁+z̄₂) = z̄₁ + z̄₂, (z₁z₂)‾ = z̄₁z̄₂, (z₁/z₂)‾ = z̄₁/z̄₂; z real ⟺ z = z̄.

### 2.4 Modulus and the Argand plane

- **|z| = √(x² + y²)** — distance from the origin; satisfies the **triangle inequality |z₁ + z₂| ≤ |z₁| + |z₂|** and |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂|.
- Argand diagram: z = x + iy is the point (x, y); |z − z₀| = r is a **circle** of radius r about z₀; conjugation reflects in the real axis.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why ℂ is closed

- ℝ failed √(−1); ℂ fixes it, and the price is small: order is lost (no "z₁ < z₂" for complex numbers). Gain: every quadratic equation now has two roots.

### 4.2 Conjugates as the division tool

- Dividing by z multiplies by z̄/|z|²: the conjugate realises the denominator, and the modulus² normalises it. Conjugation is the workhorse of complex arithmetic.

### 4.3 Multiplication is rotation

- In polar form z = r(cos θ + i sin θ), multiplying multiplies moduli and **adds angles** — multiplication by i is a 90° rotation. The Argand plane turns algebra into geometry.

## 6. Common Confusions

**√(−a) = i√a, not ±:** the principal root carries the i; the ± appears when solving equations, not when evaluating the radical.
%%%
**|z| is not z's "absolute value" algebraically:** it is √(z·z̄) — the distance — and obeys |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂| but not |z₁ + z₂| = |z₁| + |z₂|.
%%%
**iⁿ cycles mod 4:** i⁴⁰⁰ = 1, i⁴⁰¹ = i — reduce the exponent modulo 4 before anything else.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB-style (3m): Express (2 + 3i)(1 − 2i) + (3 − i)/(1 + i) in the form x + iy.** — **Product:** (2 + 3i)(1 − 2i) = 2 − 4i + 3i − 6i² = 2 − i + 6 = **8 − i**. **Quotient:** multiply by the conjugate: (3 − i)(1 − i)/(1 + i)(1 − i) = (3 − 3i − i + i²)/2 = (2 − 4i)/2 = **1 − 2i**. **Sum:** (8 − i) + (1 − 2i) = **9 − 3i**.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Find the modulus and conjugate of z = (1 + 2i)/(3 − 4i).** — **Rationalise:** z = (1 + 2i)(3 + 4i)/(9 + 16) = (3 + 4i + 6i + 8i²)/25 = (3 − 8 + 10i)/25 = (−5 + 10i)/25 = **(−1 + 2i)/5**. **Modulus:** |z| = √(1 + 4)/5 = √5/5 = **1/√5** (equivalently |1+2i|/|3−4i| = √5/5 ✓ — moduli divide). **Conjugate: z̄ = (−1 − 2i)/5.**
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): If z = 2 + i, express z² + z̄² in the form x + iy and deduce that it is real.** — z² = (2 + i)² = 4 + 4i + i² = **3 + 4i**; z̄² = (2 − i)² = 3 − 4i. Sum: **6 + 0i — purely real**. Deduction: z² + z̄² = 2Re(z²) = 2(3) = 6 — the general theorem z² + z̄² = 2Re(z²) ∈ ℝ at work.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Solve x² + x + 1 = 0 over ℂ.** — D = 1 − 4 = −3 < 0 ⟹ complex roots: x = (−1 ± i√3)/2 — the **cube roots of unity** (excluding 1). The quadratic formula extends verbatim to negative discriminants once i is admitted.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (3m): Express (2 + 3i)(1 − 2i) + (3 − i)/(1 + i) in the form x + iy.** — **Product:** (2 + 3i)(1 − 2i) = 2·1 + 2·(−2i) + 3i·1 + 3i·(−2i) = 2 − 4i + 3i − 6i² = 2 − i + 6 = **8 − i** (i² = −1 flips the sign of the last term). **Quotient:** multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate (1 − i): (3 − i)(1 − i)/[(1 + i)(1 − i)] = (3 − 3i − i + i²)/(1 − i²) = (3 − 4i − 1)/2 = (2 − 4i)/2 = **1 − 2i**. **Sum:** (8 − i) + (1 − 2i) = **9 − 3i** — real part 9, imaginary part −3. Every complex computation reduces to: expand with i² = −1, and divide via the conjugate.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Find the modulus and the conjugate of z = (1 + 2i)/(3 − 4i).** — **Rationalise:** z = (1 + 2i)(3 + 4i)/[(3 − 4i)(3 + 4i)] = (3 + 4i + 6i + 8i²)/(9 + 16) = (3 − 8 + 10i)/25 = (−5 + 10i)/25 = **(−1 + 2i)/5**. **Modulus:** |z| = √((−1/5)² + (2/5)²) = √5/5 = **1/√5**. **Cross-check by the modulus theorem:** |z| = |1 + 2i|/|3 − 4i| = √5/√25 = √5/5 ✓ — moduli of quotients divide, exactly as |z₁/z₂| = |z₁|/|z₂| demands. **Conjugate:** conjugating termwise, **z̄ = (−1 − 2i)/5** — equivalently conjugate before rationalising; both routes agree since conjugation respects division.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Solve the equation x² + x + 1 = 0 over the complex numbers.** — **Discriminant:** D = b² − 4ac = 1 − 4 = **−3 < 0** — no real roots, but ℂ supplies them. **Quadratic formula with i:** x = (−1 ± √(−3))/2 = (−1 ± i√3)/2. **Roots: x = (−1 + i√3)/2 and x = (−1 − i√3)/2** — a conjugate pair (real quadratics always have conjugate-pair roots). **Identification:** these are the primitive **cube roots of unity** ω and ω², satisfying 1 + ω + ω² = 0 and ω³ = 1 — the same pair that powers the factorisation x³ − 1 = (x − 1)(x² + x + 1).
@@summary@@
The **imaginary unit** i² = −1 extends ℝ to ℂ = {x + iy}, making every quadratic solvable (D < 0 ⟹ roots (−b ± i√|D|)/2a — x² + x + 1 = 0 yields the cube roots of unity). **Algebra:** multiplication expands with i² → −1, division multiplies by the conjugate over |z₂|², and powers of i cycle mod 4. The **conjugate theorems** do the heavy lifting: z·z̄ = |z|², conjugation distributes over +, ×, ÷, z real ⟺ z = z̄, and z² + z̄² = 2Re(z²) is always real. The **modulus** |z| = √(x² + y²) = √(z·z̄) is distance from the origin, obeying |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂| and the triangle inequality — for (1+2i)/(3−4i) = (−1+2i)/5, |z| = 1/√5 by either route. In the **Argand plane**, z is a point, |z − z₀| = r a circle, conjugation a reflection, and multiplication a rotation-plus-scaling — complex numbers are geometry wearing algebra.
