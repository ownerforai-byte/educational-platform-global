## 1. Topic Introduction — Drill Gallery 15.2

Exercise 15.2 is the **standard-limits drill**: every exercise reduces to sin u/u, (1 − cos u)/u², (eᵘ − 1)/u, ln(1+u)/u, or the e-powers. This canonical packages the reduction patterns with worked exemplars.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 The reduction patterns

- **Pattern 1 — coefficient extraction:** sin(ax)/x, tan(ax)/x, sin(ax)/sin(bx) ⟹ insert ax/ax, bx/bx.
- **Pattern 2 — half-angle:** (1 − cos ax)/x² ⟹ 2sin²(ax/2) route ⟹ a²/2.
- **Pattern 3 — exponential/log:** (e^{ax} − 1)/x → a; ln(1 + ax)/x → a.
- **Pattern 4 — 1^∞ powers:** (1 + u)^v ⟹ e^{lim uv}.

### 2.2 Mixed-form handling

- Products/quotients of several 0/0 pieces: split the fraction into standard factors, evaluate each, multiply — limits respect products of existing limits.

### 2.3 Substitution steering

- Composite arguments (sin of something → 0): set u = the inner expression; as x → a, u → 0 and the standard limit applies in u. The chain rule's limit shadow.

### 2.4 Sanity anchors

- sin(ax)/x → a; (1 − cos ax)/x² → a²/2; (e^{ax} − 1)/x → a; ln(1+ax)/x → a — one coefficient a, memorised as "the a survives, the argument dies".

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 The "a survives" mantra

- In every standard limit with argument ax, the answer carries the coefficient a once (or a² for the cosine-square family): sin 3x/x → 3, (e^{5x} − 1)/x → 5, ln(1+2x)/x → 2, (1 − cos 3x)/x² → 9/2.

### 4.2 Splitting discipline

- Only split a limit into a product when **each factor's limit exists finitely** (the limit product theorem). If a factor's limit fails, the split is illegal — regroup first.

### 4.3 Why substitution is legitimate

- If u = g(x) → 0 as x → a (and u ≠ 0 near a), then lim_{u→0} F(u) = lim_{x→a} F(g(x)) — the composite limit theorem; steering through u is not a trick but a theorem.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Forgetting the a:** sin 3x/x is 3, not 1 — the insertion is the whole point.
%%%
**Illegal splits:** splitting when one factor's limit does not exist — check existence before multiplying.
%%%
**Degree slips in the cosine family:** (1 − cos ax)/x² carries a², not a — squaring doubles the exponent.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (tan 3x − sin 3x)/x³.** — Factor: tan 3x − sin 3x = sin 3x(1/cos 3x − 1) = sin 3x(1 − cos 3x)/cos 3x. **Split (all factors have limits):** [sin 3x/x]·[(1 − cos 3x)/x²]·[1/cos 3x] → 3 · (9/2) · 1 = **27/2**. Three stars in one expression — the signature 15.2 compound.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{2x} − 1)/ln(1 + 4x).** — **Split via 1:** multiply and divide by x: [(e^{2x} − 1)/x] / [ln(1+4x)/x] → 2/4 = **1/2**. The x-bridge converts both numerator and denominator into standard stars.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} sin(x²)/x·... lim_{x→0} sin(x²)/x.** — **Substitution:** u = x² → 0; sin(x²)/x = [sin(x²)/x²]·x → 1·0 = **0**. The star applies to u = x², and the leftover x → 0 kills the product — a star times zero is zero.

## 8. Deep Conceptual — Split Legality: The Fine Print of the Product Theorem

- The limit product theorem needs **both factors' limits to exist**. Splitting sin 3x·(1/x)-type products illegally — separating a factor whose limit does not exist — manufactures false answers. The check is one line: name each factor's limit before multiplying.
- **Legal splits create constants:** after steering, each factor's limit is either a constant or a star-value (1, 1/2, e...). If any factor's limit is indeterminate or nonexistent, regroup the expression until every piece settles — regrouping is algebra, splitting is a theorem.
- **Quotient and sum versions carry the same licence:** the quotient theorem needs the denominator's limit ≠ 0; the sum theorem needs both to exist (not ∞ − ∞). Every split in a drill solution should be silently auditable — the best solutions make it explicit.
%%%
## 9. Deep Conceptual — The Mantra's Deep Meaning: Coefficient Conservation

- "The coefficient survives, the argument dies" works because near 0, sin(ax) ≈ ax — the function **is** its argument's line to first order. The coefficient a is the slope of that line; the argument's details (frequency, nesting) vanish at first order.
- **Coefficient bookkeeping across compositions:** sin(tan 2x) ~ tan 2x ~ 2x — each layer contributes its coefficient multiplicatively (here 1 × 2). Nested stars multiply their steering constants; the final answer is the product of the chain's coefficients.
- **When coefficients compete:** in quotients of same-order stars the surviving ratio is the quotient of coefficients (sin 3x/sin 5x → 3/5); in products, their product (sin 2x·tan 3x/x² → 6). Every drill answer is coefficient arithmetic once the orders match.
%%%
## 10. Deep Conceptual — The 1^∞ Bridge as the Gallery's Crown Pattern

- Any (1 + small)^{large} form surrenders to the identity A^B = e^{B ln A} plus the log star ln(1+u) ~ u: write A = 1 + u with u → 0, then B·ln(1+u) ~ B·u, and the limit is e^{lim B·u}. The whole technology is two stars and one exponent law.
- **Reading the answer's shape:** lim (1 + k/x)^{x} = eᵏ — the exponent's coefficient against the base's displacement. General form: if B·u → c, the limit is e^c. The bridge converts a power race into a single constant c.
- **Why the bridge is trustworthy:** exp and ln are continuous inverses, so taking ln commutes with the limit — the limit of the power equals the exponential of the limit of the log. This commutation is the deep licence; the algebra is just its bookkeeping.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (tan 3x − sin 3x)/x³.** — **Factor the difference:** tan 3x − sin 3x = sin 3x·(1/cos 3x − 1) = **sin 3x·(1 − cos 3x)/cos 3x**. **Rewrite the quotient:** (tan 3x − sin 3x)/x³ = [sin 3x/x] · [(1 − cos 3x)/x²] · [1/cos 3x] — three factors. **Legality check:** each factor's limit exists (stars and a continuous reciprocal) — the split is licensed. **Evaluate:** sin 3x/x → **3**; (1 − cos 3x)/x² → 3²/2 = **9/2** (half-angle route); 1/cos 3x → **1**. **Product:** 3 · (9/2) · 1 = **27/2**. **General form:** (tan ax − sin ax)/x³ → a³/2 — three standard stars chained in one exercise, the signature 15.2 compound.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{2x} − 1)/ln(1 + 4x).** — **Form:** 0/0 with mismatched stars — exponential over logarithm. **The x-bridge:** multiply and divide by x: = [(e^{2x} − 1)/x] / [ln(1 + 4x)/x]. **Each star:** (e^{2x} − 1)/x → **2** (the exponential star with a = 2); ln(1 + 4x)/x → **4** (the logarithm star with a = 4). **Quotient:** 2/4 = **1/2**. **Mantra confirmed:** the coefficient survives (2 and 4), the arguments (2x, 4x) die into the stars — one bridge, two stars, one clean answer.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} sin(x²)/x.** — **Substitution steering:** set u = x²; as x → 0, u → 0 (composite limit theorem licenses the star in u). **Rewrite:** sin(x²)/x = [sin(x²)/(x²)] · x — inserting the x²/x² factor exposes the star. **Evaluate:** sin(x²)/(x²) → **1** (the star at u = x²); the leftover factor x → **0**. **Product:** 1 · 0 = **0**. **Moral:** a standard star times an explicit zero is zero — the star normalises the singular part, and whatever simple factor remains just gets substituted. Not every 0/0 is "interesting": this one is a star wearing a hat.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 4x · tan 2x)/(1 − cos 6x), annotating every split and star.** — **Order diagnosis:** sin 4x ~ 4x (order 1); tan 2x ~ 2x (order 1); 1 − cos 6x ~ (6x)²/2 = 18x² (order 2) ⟹ numerator ~ 8x² vs denominator ~ 18x² — same order ⟹ finite nonzero, ratio of coefficients 8/18 = **4/9**. **Rigorous version:** split into factors with existing limits: [(sin 4x)/(4x)]·[(tan 2x)/(2x)]·[(6x)²/(1 − cos 6x)]·[(4x·2x)/(6x)²]. **Star values:** (sin 4x)/(4x) → 1; (tan 2x)/(2x) → 1; (6x)²/(1−cos 6x) → 2 (star (1−cos u)/u² reciprocal, u = 6x); (8x²)/(36x²) = 2/9. **Product: 1·1·2·(2/9) = 4/9** ✓ — matching the order reading. **Deep point:** the order comparison delivered the answer before any algebra; the star decomposition is the *certificate* that the prediction was legitimate — in drills, predict first, then prove.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{2x} − ln(1 + 3x) − 1)/x.** — **Split the numerator legally:** (e^{2x} − 1)/x − ln(1 + 3x)/x — both pieces' limits exist (each is a star over x). **First piece (star (eᵘ−1)/u → 1, u = 2x):** (e^{2x} − 1)/x = 2·(e^{2x} − 1)/(2x) → **2**. **Second piece (star ln(1+u)/u → 1, u = 3x):** ln(1+3x)/x = 3·ln(1+3x)/(3x) → **3**. **Combine: 2 − 3 = −1.** **Order check:** e^{2x} − 1 ~ 2x and ln(1+3x) ~ 3x ⟹ numerator ~ 2x − 3x = −x, over x ⟹ −1 ✓. **Deep point:** linear approximations turn *differences* of stars into arithmetic of coefficients — the whole expression is (2x) − (3x) = −x at first order, and the limit reads off the surviving coefficient; this is the drill's deepest pattern: first-order Taylor arithmetic in disguise.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (1 + 5/x)^{2x} using the exponential bridge, and generalise to lim_{x→∞} (1 + k/x)^{mx}.** — **Recognise the form:** base → 1, exponent → ∞ ⟹ **1^∞**. **Bridge:** (1 + 5/x)^{2x} = e^{2x·ln(1 + 5/x)}. **Steer the log star:** ln(1 + 5/x) ~ 5/x (u = 5/x → 0) ⟹ 2x·ln(1+5/x) ~ 2x·(5/x) = **10**. **Limit: e¹⁰.** **Generalisation:** (1 + k/x)^{mx} = e^{mx·ln(1+k/x)} ~ e^{mx·(k/x)} = **e^{mk}** — the exponent's coefficient m times the base's displacement k. **Deep point:** the bridge reduces every 1^∞ form to one constant c = lim(exponent × displacement), and the answer is always e^c — here the coefficient bookkeeping (2 × 5 = 10) *is* the entire computation once the stars are trusted.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (tan 3x − sin 3x)/x³ and verify with the identity tan x − sin x = sin x(1 − cos x)/cos x.** — **Identity reshape:** (tan 3x − sin 3x)/x³ = [sin 3x(1 − cos 3x)]/[x³ cos 3x] = [(sin 3x)/(3x)]·[(1 − cos 3x)/(3x)²]·[(3x)³/(x³)]·[1/cos 3x]. **Star values:** (sin 3x)/(3x) → **1**; (1 − cos 3x)/(3x)² → **1/2**; (3x)³/x³ = **27**; 1/cos 3x → **1** (continuity of cos at 0). **Product: 1 · 1/2 · 27 · 1 = **27/2**. **Order check:** tan 3x − sin 3x ~ 3x·(9x²/2) = 27x³/2 ⟹ over x³ ⟹ 27/2 ✓. **Deep point:** the difference of two order-1 functions (tan, sin) is an order-3 quantity — the first orders *cancel*, which is why the answer lives at x³ and why direct star-matching fails without the identity that factors out the common sin 3x; cancellation of leading orders is the general mechanism behind every higher-order drill.
