## 1. Topic Introduction — The Derivative Is Born

**The derivative of a function** via first principles, and the **derivatives of algebraic and trigonometric functions** — the moment calculus becomes computation.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 First principles (the definition)

- **f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [f(x + h) − f(x)]/h** — the limit of difference quotients; equivalently lim_{x→a} [f(x) − f(a)]/(x − a).
- Geometric meaning: the **slope of the tangent** — the secant through (x, f(x)) and (x+h, f(x+h)) pivots into the tangent as h → 0. Kinematic meaning: **instantaneous rate** (velocity is d(s)/dt).

### 2.2 Differentiability implies continuity

- **Theorem:** if f′(a) exists, f is continuous at a. Proof: f(x) − f(a) = [(f(x)−f(a))/(x−a)]·(x−a) → f′(a)·0 = 0. **Converse false:** |x| is continuous at 0 but not differentiable there (corner: left slope −1, right +1).

### 2.3 The algebraic derivative table (from first principles)

- **d/dx (c) = 0; d/dx xⁿ = n·xⁿ⁻¹** (proved via the binomial expansion or the (xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a) star). **d/dx √x = 1/(2√x)** (conjugate route). **d/dx (1/x) = −1/x².**
- **Linearity:** (af + bg)′ = af′ + bg′ — the sum/difference and constant-multiple rules, proved straight from the definition.

### 2.4 The trigonometric derivatives

- **d/dx sin x = cos x; d/dx cos x = −sin x** — from first principles using sin(x+h) = sin x cos h + cos x sin h and the stars sin h/h → 1, (cos h − 1)/h → 0.
- **d/dx tan x = sec²x** (quotient rule on sin/cos).

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why the definition needs a limit

- At a single h the difference quotient is a secant slope — an average. Only the limit kills the averaging and isolates the instantaneous rate: the derivative is the *destination* of the secants, not any secant.

### 4.2 The xⁿ proof as a star reprise

- [f(x) − f(a)]/(x − a) = (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹: the derivative of xⁿ at a *is* the algebraic star — the limits unit was secretly building the derivative.

### 4.3 The sin derivative's engine

- sin(x+h) − sin x = sin x(cos h − 1) + cos x·sin h; dividing by h and using (cos h − 1)/h → 0, sin h/h → 1 gives cos x. The standard limits of the previous unit are the proof's moving parts.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Continuous ≠ differentiable:** |x| bends without breaking — continuity is necessary, never sufficient.
%%%
**The power rule is not "bring down and subtract" by magic:** it is the star limit — quote the route in first-principles questions.
%%%
**cos x loses, sin gains:** d/dx cos x = **−sin x** — the sign slip is the classic error.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Find dy/dx if y = x³ + 2x² − 5x + 3. (2m)** — **Linearity + power rule term by term:** d/dx x³ = 3x²; d/dx 2x² = 4x; d/dx (−5x) = −5; d/dx 3 = 0. **dy/dx = 3x² + 4x − 5.**
%%%
**NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Using first principles, show that d/dx (x^{1/3}) = 1/(3x^{2/3}). (4m)** — **Definition:** f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [(x+h)^{1/3} − x^{1/3}]/h. **Rationalise the cube roots:** set A = (x+h)^{1/3}, B = x^{1/3}; use A − B = (A³ − B³)/(A² + AB + B²) = h/(A² + AB + B²). So the quotient = 1/(A² + AB + B²). **Limit:** as h → 0, A → B, so the denominator → B² + B² + B² = 3B² = 3x^{2/3} ⟹ **f′(x) = 1/(3x^{2/3})** ∎. (The difference-of-cubes identity is the conjugate trick's cubic cousin.)
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Derive d/dx sin x = cos x from first principles.** — Quotient: [sin(x+h) − sin x]/h = [sin x(cos h − 1) + cos x·sin h]/h = sin x·[(cos h − 1)/h] + cos x·[sin h/h]. **Stars:** (cos h − 1)/h → 0 and sin h/h → 1 (h → 0, radians) ⟹ f′(x) = sin x·0 + cos x·1 = **cos x** ∎ — the two standard limits are the entire engine.

## 8. Deep Conceptual — Why Differentiability Is Stronger Than Continuity

- **Continuity** demands the *values* f(x) → f(a); **differentiability** demands the *rates* [f(x) − f(a)]/(x − a) → f′(a). Rates are a stricter contract: they compare f at *pairs* of nearby points, not just against the anchor.
- **The failure gallery:** |x| (corner — one-sided slopes disagree); x^{2/3} (cusp — vertical tangent, difference quotients → ∞); x sin(1/x)-type oscillation (slopes swing without settling). Each species is continuous at the bad point yet non-differentiable there.
- **One-sided derivatives:** f′₋(a) = lim_{h→0⁻} [f(a+h) − f(a)]/h and f′₊(a) similarly. **Theorem:** f′(a) exists ⟺ both one-sided derivatives exist and are equal — the limit-existence theorem transplanted to difference quotients.
%%%
## 9. Deep Conceptual — The Derivative as a Local Linear Approximation

- f′(a) = L means **f(x) ≈ f(a) + L(x − a)** near a with error o(x − a) — the tangent line is the best linear approximation. This is the *definition* of the derivative restated: f(a + h) = f(a) + f′(a)h + h·ε(h) with ε(h) → 0.
- **Why this reading matters:** every standard limit from the limits unit is a tangent statement — sin h ≈ h says (sin)′(0) = 1; (eʰ − 1)/h → 1 says (eˣ)′(0) = 1; ln(1+h)/h → 1 says (ln)′(1) = 1. The stars were derivative facts all along.
- **Consequence:** differentiability at a is exactly the existence of a good linear approximation — functions with corners have two competing lines, cusps have none finite, oscillators have none at all.
%%%
## 10. Deep Conceptual — Reading the Difference Quotient's Denominator

- Every first-principles proof is the same three moves: **(1)** write the difference quotient; **(2)** create the factor h in the numerator (by identity, conjugate, or series); **(3)** cancel and take the limit with the standard stars.
- **The identity bank:** difference of powers Aⁿ − Bⁿ (n = 2, 3, ½ via conjugate, 1/3 via cubes), angle-addition for trig, ln(1+h) factoring for logs. Each identity's job is identical: expose the h that cancels the denominator's h.
- **After cancellation the limit is always a star:** sin h/h → 1, (eʰ−1)/h → 1, (cos h − 1)/h → 0, or plain substitution. First-principles proofs never invent new limits — they *reduce* to the ones already owned.

**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Find dy/dx if y = x³ + 2x² − 5x + 3. (2m)** — **Differentiate term by term** (the linearity theorem licenses this): d/dx x³ = **3x²** (power rule); d/dx 2x² = **4x** (constant multiple + power rule); d/dx (−5x) = **−5**; d/dx 3 = **0** (constant rule). **Sum:** **dy/dx = 3x² + 4x − 5**. Each term's rule is a first-principles theorem in disguise — the polynomial case is where the machinery becomes one-line computation.
%%%
**NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Using the first principles of differentiation, show that d/dx (x^{1/3}) = 1/(3x^{2/3}). (4m)** — **Definition:** f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [(x+h)^{1/3} − x^{1/3}]/h. **Create the h-factor:** let A = (x+h)^{1/3} and B = x^{1/3}; then A³ − B³ = (x + h) − x = h, and the **difference-of-cubes factorisation** A³ − B³ = (A − B)(A² + AB + B²) gives A − B = h/(A² + AB + B²). **Substitute:** the difference quotient = [h/(A² + AB + B²)]/h = **1/(A² + AB + B²)**. **Take the limit:** as h → 0, A → B (continuity of the cube root), so the denominator → B² + B² + B² = 3B² = **3x^{2/3}**. **Conclusion:** f′(x) = 1/(3x^{2/3}) ∎ — the conjugate-rationalisation strategy, upgraded to cube roots, exactly mirroring the limits unit's (√(7+x)−3)/(x−2) technique.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Derive the derivative of sin x from first principles.** — **Definition:** f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [sin(x+h) − sin x]/h. **Angle-addition expansion:** sin(x + h) = sin x cos h + cos x sin h, so the numerator = sin x(cos h − 1) + cos x·sin h. **Split the quotient** (both pieces' limits exist — the split is licensed): = sin x·[(cos h − 1)/h] + cos x·[sin h/h]. **Apply the stars** (h → 0, radians): (cos h − 1)/h → **0** and sin h/h → **1**. **Result:** f′(x) = sin x·0 + cos x·1 = **cos x** ∎. The entire proof rests on the two standard limits from the limits unit — first-principles trigonometric differentiation is applied limit theory.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Using first principles, prove that d/dx (cos x) = −sin x.** — **Definition:** f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [cos(x+h) − cos x]/h. **Angle addition:** cos(x + h) = cos x cos h − sin x sin h, so the numerator = cos x(cos h − 1) − sin x·sin h. **Split the quotient** (both pieces' limits exist — the split is licensed): = cos x·[(cos h − 1)/h] − sin x·[sin h/h]. **Apply the stars** (h → 0, radians): (cos h − 1)/h → **0** and sin h/h → **1**. **Result:** f′(x) = cos x·0 − sin x·1 = **−sin x** ∎. **Deep points:** (1) the minus sign is not an accident — cos starts *decreasing* at x = 0 (cos h < 1 for small h ≠ 0), so its rate there is negative; the first-principles computation and the graph's behaviour tell one story; (2) the proof consumes exactly two standard limits — the trig stars are the entire engine, and every first-principles trig derivative is this proof with a different angle-addition identity.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Using first principles, prove that d/dx (√x) = 1/(2√x) for x > 0.** — **Definition:** f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [√(x+h) − √x]/h. **Create the h-factor (conjugate):** multiply numerator and denominator by √(x+h) + √x: numerator = (x + h) − x = **h**, so the quotient = h/[h(√(x+h) + √x)] = **1/(√(x+h) + √x)** for h ≠ 0. **Take the limit:** as h → 0, √(x+h) → √x (continuity of the square root) ⟹ the denominator → 2√x ⟹ **f′(x) = 1/(2√x)** ∎. **Deep points:** (1) the conjugate is the difference-of-squares identity in working clothes — A − B = (A² − B²)/(A + B) with A = √(x+h), B = √x; (2) the result confirms the star (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ at n = ½: (1/2)x^{−1/2} = 1/(2√x) ✓; (3) the derivative *blows up* as x → 0⁺ — the square-root curve has a vertical tangent at the origin, which is why x^{1/2} is the boundary between differentiable (n > 1) and non-differentiable (n < 1) powers at 0.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Show that f(x) = |x| is continuous everywhere but differentiable nowhere at 0, computing both one-sided derivatives.** — **Continuity:** |x| = √(x²) is a composition of continuous functions ⟹ continuous on ℝ; directly: lim_{x→0} |x| = 0 = |0| ✓. **Right-hand derivative:** for h > 0, [|h| − 0]/h = h/h = 1 ⟹ f′₊(0) = **+1**. **Left-hand derivative:** for h < 0, [|h| − 0]/h = (−h)/h = −1 ⟹ f′₋(0) = **−1**. **Verdict:** the one-sided derivatives exist but **disagree** (+1 ≠ −1) ⟹ f′(0) does **not exist** — a **corner**: the graph has two competing tangent lines (slope +1 from the right, −1 from the left) and no single tangent. **Deep conceptual point:** continuity asks the *values* to converge (they do — both sides approach 0); differentiability asks the *rates* to converge (they do not — the V-shape bends abruptly). The one-sided-derivative test is the limit-existence theorem applied to difference quotients, and |x| is its canonical counterexample separating the two concepts.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Prove that differentiability implies continuity: if f′(a) exists, then f is continuous at a. Is the converse true? Justify.** — **Proof:** write the change in f using the difference quotient: f(x) − f(a) = [(f(x) − f(a))/(x − a)]·(x − a). As x → a, the bracketed factor → f′(a) (which exists) and (x − a) → 0, so by the **product rule for limits**: lim_{x→a} [f(x) − f(a)] = f′(a)·0 = **0**, i.e. lim_{x→a} f(x) = f(a) — f is continuous at a ∎. **The converse fails:** f(x) = |x| is continuous at 0 (lim |x| = 0 = f(0)) but not differentiable there (one-sided slopes −1 and +1 disagree). **Deeper counterexample:** f(x) = x·sin(1/x) with f(0) = 0 is continuous at 0 (squeeze) and *also* differentiable at 0 (f′(0) = 0 by squeeze), yet its derivative is discontinuous — even differentiability does not buy continuous differentiability. **Hierarchy:** continuous ⊃ differentiable ⊃ continuously differentiable — three strictly nested classes, each counterexample more refined than the last.
@@summary@@
The **derivative** is born: f′(x) = lim_{h→0} [f(x+h) − f(x)]/h — the secant's limit, the tangent's slope, the instantaneous rate. **Differentiability implies continuity** (factor the difference; the derivative times the vanishing h), but never conversely — |x|'s corner has slopes −1 and +1. The **algebraic table** comes from first principles: (xⁿ)′ = nxⁿ⁻¹ *is* the (xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a) star; (√x)′ = 1/(2√x) is the conjugate; linearity makes polynomials one-line work (NEB 2024: 3x² + 4x − 5). **Fractional powers** yield to the difference-of-cubes identity: d/dx x^{1/3} = 1/(3x^{2/3}) (NEB 2021's four-marker). The **trigonometric derivatives** run on the standard limits: sin(x+h) − sin x splits via angle addition, and sin h/h → 1, (cos h−1)/h → 0 deliver (sin x)′ = cos x, (cos x)′ = −sin x, (tan x)′ = sec²x. The limits unit was the prologue; this is the play.
