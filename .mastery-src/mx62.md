## 1. Topic Introduction — Drill Gallery: 0/0 Transformation Speed

This canonical is the **second limits drill gallery**: 0/0 forms resolved at speed by factoring, conjugates, and star-steering. It mirrors the transformation machinery of the parent canonicals; the drill's discipline is choosing the fastest legal cure.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror — The 0/0 Toolkit

- **Factoring:** 0/0 at a ⟹ (x − a) divides the numerator (factor theorem); double factoring when both die; higher powers re-diagnose after each cancellation.
- **Conjugates:** √g(x) − √g(a) = [g(x) − g(a)]/[√g(x) + √g(a)] — radicals become polynomials.
- **Stars:** matched first-order contestants ⟹ coefficient ratio; second-order needs (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2.
- **Re-diagnosis rule:** after each cancellation, substitute again — 0/0 means factor again, c/0 means wall.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Speed Comes From Recognition, Not Algebra

- Every 0/0 drill is three moves — diagnose, transform, substitute — and the *variation* is only in the transform. Speed comes from pattern recognition: difference of powers ⟹ factor; radical difference ⟹ conjugate; trig ratio ⟹ steer; exp/log ⟹ coefficient ratio.
- **The re-diagnosis habit separates levels:** beginners cancel once and substitute blindly; strong students substitute after *every* cancellation because the form may have changed species (0/0 → 0/0 → c/0 wall). The form is a property of the current expression.
- **Cross-checks are one line:** the star (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ verifies every factoring answer; the conjugate identity verifies every radical answer. Drills build the verification reflex.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Why 0/0 Always Yields

- A 0/0 form at a means **both** functions vanish at a ⟹ both carry (x − a) as a factor ⟹ cancellation is always available in principle. The form is a *promise* of a shared root, which is why 0/0 never dead-ends: the only question is which identity exposes the factor fastest.
- **When the shared root is hidden:** radicals hide it behind roots (conjugate needed), trig functions behind identities (sin x − tan x needs factoring out sin x), exponentials behind the star (eˣ − 1 needs the standard limit). The toolkit is the list of hiding places.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — polynomial over polynomial:** double factor, cancel once, substitute.
- **Type B — radical difference:** conjugate, cancel, substitute; verify by the radical star.
- **Type C — trig ratios:** steer to matched stars; coefficient ratio.
- **Type D — mixed families:** each family contributes its slope; ratio of slopes.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"Cancel then forget the licence":** write x ≠ a — the cancelled form agrees with the original only off the excluded point. **"0/0 after cancellation means failure":** it means factor again — higher-multiplicity roots.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→3} (x² − 8x + 15)/(x² − 9).** — **Form:** (9 − 24 + 15)/(9 − 9) = **0/0** ✓. **Double factor:** numerator (x − 3)(x − 5); denominator (x − 3)(x + 3) ⟹ for x ≠ 3: (x − 5)/(x + 3). **Substitute:** (−2)/(6) = **−1/3**. **Cross-check:** both stars give numerator 2·3 = 6, denominator 2·3 = 6 ⟹ 6/6? — no: the stars give the *derivatives'* ratio only for the shared-factor part; the correct check is that after cancelling (x−3), the remaining factors at 3 are (3−5) = −2 and (3+3) = 6 ✓. **Deep point:** the star cross-check applies to each factor's vanishing part — the surviving factors are evaluated by substitution; knowing which part the star prices avoids its misuse.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (√(4 + x) − 2)/x.** — **Form:** (2 − 2)/0 = **0/0** ✓. **Conjugate:** multiply by (√(4+x) + 2)/(same): numerator = (4 + x) − 4 = x ⟹ expression = 1/(√(4+x) + 2) for x ≠ 0. **Substitute:** 1/(2 + 2) = **1/4**. **Cross-check:** the radical star √(a+u) − √a ~ u/(2√a) at a = 4 gives 1/(2·2) = 1/4 ✓. **Deep point:** the answer 1/4 is the square root's slope at 4 — d/dx √x = 1/(2√x); every conjugate drill is a derivative in embryo, which is why the star cross-check works.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 5x − tan 5x)/x³.** — **Identity reshape:** sin 5x − tan 5x = sin 5x(1 − cos 5x)/cos 5x. **Assemble:** = [(sin 5x)/(5x)]·[(1 − cos 5x)/(5x)²]·[(5x)³/x³]·[1/cos 5x]. **Stars:** 1 · 1/2 · 125 · 1 = **125/2**. **Order check:** sin 5x − tan 5x ~ 5x·(25x²/2) = 125x³/2 ⟹ over x³ ⟹ 125/2 ✓. **Deep point:** the difference of two order-1 functions is order 3 — the leading orders cancel, which is why the answer lives at x³ and why factoring out the common sin 5x was the enabling move; leading-order cancellation is the mechanism behind every higher-order drill.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (√(x + 2) − 2)/(x² − 4).** — **Form:** (2 − 2)/0 = **0/0** ✓. **Both functions vanish at 2:** the numerator hides its root behind the radical; the denominator factors (x−2)(x+2). **Conjugate the numerator:** √(x+2) − 2 = (x − 2)/(√(x+2) + 2) ⟹ the expression = (x − 2)/[(√(x+2) + 2)(x − 2)(x + 2)] = 1/[(√(x+2) + 2)(x + 2)] for x ≠ 2. **Substitute:** 1/[(2 + 2)(4)] = **1/16**. **Deep point:** two cures in one problem — the conjugate exposed the numerator's shared root, which then cancelled against the denominator's explicit factor; composite 0/0 forms are solved by treating each hiding place in turn until one clean quotient remains.
@@summary@@
The 0/0 drill's speed comes from recognition: polynomials hide their shared root in factors (conjugate-free), radicals behind roots (conjugate), trig functions behind identities (factor out the common term), exponentials and logs behind their stars (slope ratios). The form is a promise — both functions vanish, so cancellation always exists — and re-diagnosis after every cancellation keeps the species honest. Substitute, cross-check against the matching star, and the fastest legal cure is always the right answer.
