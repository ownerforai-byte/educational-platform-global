## 1. Topic Introduction — Making the Invisible Detectable

Carbon and hydrogen reveal themselves by simple combustion tests, but **nitrogen, sulphur and halogens** hide inside covalent molecules where ionic reagents cannot touch them. **Lassaigne's test** (sodium fusion) solves this by converting every covalent N, S and X into **ions** (CN⁻, S²⁻, X⁻) in one operation — then classic inorganic tests finish the job.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Why sodium fusion is needed

- In an organic molecule, N, S and halogens are held in **covalent bonds**; the usual ionic precipitation tests fail.
- Fusing the compound with **molten sodium** converts them to **ionic sodium salts**: **NaCN** (from N), **Na₂S** (from S), **NaX** (from halogen).
- The fused mass is dropped into water, boiled and filtered — the filtrate is **Lassaigne's (sodium fusion) extract**.

### 2.2 Detection of nitrogen

- Extract + **FeSO₄** + **NaOH**, boil; acidify with dilute **H₂SO₄** and add a little Fe³⁺ → **Prussian blue** precipitate.
- Chemistry: Fe²⁺ + CN⁻ → sodium ferrocyanide Na₄[Fe(CN)₆]; then Fe³⁺ gives **Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃** (Prussian blue).

### 2.3 Detection of sulphur

- **Test 1:** extract + **sodium nitroprusside** → **violet/purple** colour (Na₄[Fe(CN)₅NOS]).
- **Test 2:** extract + **acetic acid** + **lead acetate** → **black precipitate of PbS**.

### 2.4 Detection of halogens

- Acidify extract with dilute **HNO₃** and **boil** (expels HCN/H₂S if N or S present — they would interfere); add **AgNO₃**:
  - **White** ppt, soluble in NH₄OH → **Cl** (AgCl)
  - **Pale yellow** ppt, partly soluble in concentrated NH₄OH → **Br** (AgBr)
  - **Yellow** ppt, insoluble in NH₄OH → **I** (AgI)

### 2.5 The nitrogen + sulphur complication

- If **both N and S** are present, fusion gives **NaSCN** (sodium thiocyanate) instead of NaCN + Na₂S; with Fe³⁺ this gives **blood-red Fe(SCN)₃** — no Prussian blue. The red colour is itself the proof that both elements are present.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 The logic chain of the test

- Covalent → ionic (fusion) → ionic test (precipitation/colour). Every observation downstream is a standard inorganic test; Lassaigne's contribution is the conversion step. Quote this logic — it is what the question "why is sodium fusion necessary?" rewards.

### 4.2 Order of operations matters

- For halogen detection, the extract must first be **boiled with dilute HNO₃** to expel HCN and H₂S — otherwise CN⁻/S²⁻ would also precipitate silver salts and confuse the result.

### 4.3 What the test cannot do

- Some compounds (e.g. certain diazonium salts, or N lost as N₂ during heating) give **false negatives**; the test is sensitive but not universal. Volatile halides may escape during fusion.

## 6. Common Confusions

**The blood-red colour is not a failure:** when N and S are both present, Prussian blue cannot form; the red Fe(SCN)₃ is the *correct* positive result for both elements.
%%%
**HNO₃ boiling is not optional:** skipping it leaves CN⁻/S²⁻ to form AgCN/Ag₂S, muddying the halogen colours.
%%%
**Flame/combustion tests detect C and H, not N/S/X:** C and H need no fusion (CO₂ turns lime water milky; H₂O condenses on a cold surface) — only N, S, X need the extract.

%%%
## 7. Exam Application

**NEB-style (3m): Describe Lassaigne's test for the detection of nitrogen in an organic compound.** — A small piece of **sodium metal** is heated with the organic compound in a fusion tube until **red hot**; the hot mass is plunged into distilled water, boiled and filtered — the filtrate is **Lassaigne's extract**, containing **NaCN** if nitrogen is present. To a portion, **FeSO₄ solution and NaOH** are added and the mixture **boiled** (forming sodium ferrocyanide, Na₄[Fe(CN)₆]); it is then acidified with **dilute H₂SO₄** in the presence of Fe³⁺. A **Prussian blue precipitate, Fe₄[Fe(CN)₆]₃**, confirms **nitrogen**. If the compound also contains sulphur, fusion forms **NaSCN**, which gives a **blood-red colour** with Fe³⁺ instead of the blue.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): How are sulphur and halogens detected in an organic compound?** — **Sulphur:** (i) a portion of Lassaigne's extract + **sodium nitroprusside** → **violet colour** confirms S; (ii) another portion + **acetic acid + lead acetate** → **black precipitate of PbS** confirms S. **Halogens:** the extract is acidified with **dilute HNO₃ and boiled** (to expel HCN/H₂S), then **AgNO₃** is added: a **white precipitate soluble in NH₄OH** indicates **chlorine** (AgCl); a **pale-yellow precipitate partly soluble in concentrated NH₄OH** indicates **bromine** (AgBr); a **yellow precipitate insoluble in NH₄OH** indicates **iodine** (AgI).
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Why is sodium fusion necessary before testing for N, S and halogens?** — In organic compounds these elements are locked in **covalent bonds**, so the standard **ionic precipitation tests** (ferrocyanide formation, PbS, AgX) cannot act on them. Fusion with **molten sodium** converts them into **ionic salts — NaCN, Na₂S and NaX** — in Lassaigne's extract; the ordinary inorganic tests then work directly on these ions.

NH₄OH) or **yellow AgI** (insoluble). Carbon and hydrogen need no fusion — CO₂ turns lime water milky and H₂O condenses on cold glass.
