## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to Algebraic and Trig Derivatives

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to derivatives of algebraic and trigonometric functions: the power rule, the trig table, and the first-principles engines behind them. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the table, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Power rule:** (xⁿ)′ = n·xⁿ⁻¹ for all real n (via the star naⁿ⁻¹); linearity: (af + bg)′ = af′ + bg′.
- **Trig table (radians):** (sin x)′ = cos x; (cos x)′ = −sin x; (tan x)′ = sec²x; (cot x)′ = −cosec²x; (sec x)′ = sec x tan x; (cosec x)′ = −cosec x cot x.
- **Engines:** sin and cos from angle addition plus the two stars; tan from the quotient rule; co-functions carry minus signs (co-derivative pattern).

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — The Table Is Three Facts and Closure

- The entire trig table follows from **two first-principles computations** ((sin)′ = cos, (cos)′ = −sin) plus the quotient rule: tan = sin/cos, cot = cos/sin, sec = 1/cos, cosec = 1/sin. Memorise two proofs, derive six entries.
- **The co-derivative pattern:** differentiating a co-function (cos, cot, cosec) flips the sign — because co-functions are complements, cos x = sin(π/2 − x), and the chain rule's inner derivative is −1. The pattern is the chain rule wearing a mnemonic.
- **The power rule's generality:** n real (not just integer) because the star (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ holds for all real n — √x and 1/x are the same theorem as x⁵.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Radians Are the Derivative's Native Unit

- (sin x)′(0) = 1 **only in radians**: the star sin h/h → 1 measures the sine's slope at 0 as 1 when angle equals arc. In degrees every trig derivative carries a π/180 factor — the table as memorised is a radian table.
- **Consequence chain:** the differential equations y′ = cos x, y′′ = −y (the sinusoid's eigen-equation) hold only in radians; physics written in degrees would carry conversion factors through every formula. The radian is the unit where calculus is clean.

## 6. Common Confusions

**"(sin x)′ = cos x in degrees":** no — radians only. **"−sin x's minus is arbitrary":** it encodes cosine decreasing at 0; the first-principles proof and the graph agree.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Differentiate y = 3x⁴ − 2x² + 7 and evaluate y′ at x = 1.** — **Linearity + power rule:** y′ = 3·4x³ − 2·2x + 0 = **12x³ − 4x**. **At x = 1:** 12 − 4 = **8**. **Deep point:** the constant's derivative is 0 because constants do not change — the power rule at n = 0; linearity lets each term ride its own coefficient, which is why polynomials differentiate term by term without ceremony.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Differentiate y = √x + 1/x² and state where each term's rule applies.** — **Rewrite in powers:** y = x^{1/2} + x^{−2}. **Power rule (real exponents):** y′ = (1/2)x^{−1/2} + (−2)x^{−3} = **1/(2√x) − 2/x³**. **Domains:** the first term needs x > 0 (the root's domain), the second x ≠ 0 ⟹ y′ valid for x > 0. **Deep point:** negative and fractional exponents are the power rule's full generality — rewriting roots and reciprocals as powers makes one rule serve the whole algebraic family; the domain statement travels with the answer.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Derive (cot x)′ = −cosec²x using the quotient rule, and explain the minus sign.** — **Quotient rule:** cot x = cos x/sin x ⟹ (cot)′ = [(cos)′·sin − cos·(sin)′]/sin²x = [(−sin x)·sin x − cos x·cos x]/sin²x = [−sin²x − cos²x]/sin²x = **−(sin²x + cos²x)/sin²x = −1/sin²x = −cosec²x** ∎. **The minus sign:** cot x is *decreasing* wherever it is defined (its graph falls through every quadrant) — the derivative's sign reports the function's direction; algebraically the minus came from (cos)′ = −sin, the co-derivative pattern. **Deep point:** one quotient-rule computation plus the Pythagorean identity produced the entry — the table's entries are derived facts, each reducible to the two first-principles proofs and one rule.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Using first principles, prove that d/dx (sin x) = cos x, and state where each standard limit is used.** — **Quotient:** [sin(x+h) − sin x]/h. **Angle addition:** sin(x+h) = sin x cos h + cos x sin h ⟹ numerator = sin x(cos h − 1) + cos x·sin h ⟹ quotient = sin x·[(cos h − 1)/h] + cos x·[sin h/h]. **The two stars (h → 0, radians):** (cos h − 1)/h → **0**; sin h/h → **1**. **Result:** sin x·0 + cos x·1 = **cos x** ∎. **Star roles:** the first star kills the sin x term; the second delivers the cos x term — the proof is exactly two standard limits and one identity. **Deep point:** the split into two pieces was licensed by the sum law (both pieces' limits exist) — even the derivative's founding proof audits its limit laws.
@@summary@@
The algebraic and trigonometric derivative table is three facts and closure: the power rule for all real exponents, two first-principles trig proofs, and the quotient rule generating the remaining four entries — with the co-derivative pattern explaining every minus sign. Radians are the native unit, the domain statement travels with the answer, and linearity makes polynomials term-by-term. Know the two proofs and the rules, and the table is derivable rather than memorised.
