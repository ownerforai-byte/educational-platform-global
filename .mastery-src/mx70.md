## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to Parametric and Implicit Derivatives

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to parametric and implicit differentiation: dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) and the y′-carrying chain rule in one page. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the theorems, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Parametric:** for x = f(t), y = g(t): **dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt)**, provided dx/dt ≠ 0 — the chain rule with dt cancelled.
- **Parametric second derivative:** d²y/dx² = [d(dy/dx)/dt]/(dx/dt) — differentiate the *first* derivative with respect to t, then divide again.
- **Implicit:** differentiate both sides of F(x, y) = 0, treating y as y(x): every y-term yields (chain) × dy/dx; solve the linear equation for y′.
- **Tangents/normals:** slope m = dy/dx at the point; normal slope = −1/m (m ≠ 0).

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — The Parametric Trap

- **d²y/dx² ≠ (d²y/dt²)/(d²x/dt²):** the second derivative is a *rate of change of the rate* — it needs d(dy/dx)/dt divided by dx/dt. The trap is treating it as a fraction of fractions; the cure is remembering that dy/dx is a function of t first.
- **Cycloid's lesson:** for x = a(t − sin t), y = a(1 − cos t), the second derivative at t = 2π is 1/(4a) > 0 — the cusp's neighbourhood is concave up even though the cusp itself has a vertical tangent. The parametric second derivative sees what naive fraction-arithmetic cannot.
- **The chain-rule cancellation:** dy/dx = (dy/dt)·(dt/dx) — the middle variable's rate is the gear that connects the outer rates; "cancelling" dt is the mnemonic, the chain rule is the proof.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Implicit Differentiation Is the Chain Rule Wearing y(x)

- Every y in an implicit equation is a composite: d/dx(y²) = 2y·y′, d/dx(sin y) = cos y·y′. The chain rule's inner derivative is y′ itself — implicit differentiation is nothing but the chain rule applied consistently plus one linear solve.
- **The slope field:** solving for y′ gives y′ as a function of *position* (x, y), not just x — the implicit derivative is a slope field, and each point of the curve carries its own slope even where y is multi-valued.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — parametric first derivative:** divide the rates; simplify with identities.
- **Type B — parametric second derivative:** differentiate dy/dx in t, divide by dx/dt.
- **Type C — implicit first derivative:** chain rule on y-terms, linear solve for y′.
- **Type D — tangent/normal lines:** slope at a point, point-slope equation, normal via −1/m.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"Second derivative = quotient of second derivatives":** the classic parametric error. **"Implicit means solve for y first":** usually impossible — differentiate directly.
@@keyPoints@@

Implicit: chain rule on every y-term (d/dx y^2 = 2y y'), then solve linearly for y'. The implicit answer is a slope field - a function of position (x, y).

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): For x = t², y = 2t, find dy/dx and describe the curve.** — **Rates:** dy/dt = 2; dx/dt = 2t ⟹ dy/dx = 2/(2t) = **1/t**. **Eliminate t:** t = y/2 ⟹ x = y²/4 ⟹ y² = 4x — a right-opening parabola. **Slope behaviour:** at t = 1 (point (1, 2)) the slope is 1; as t → 0⁺ the slope → ∞ (vertical tangent at the vertex). **Deep point:** the parametric form hides the curve's identity until elimination — but the slope formula works even where elimination is impossible, which is the method's real power.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): For the cycloid x = a(t − sin t), y = a(1 − cos t), find d²y/dx² and evaluate it at t = 2π.** — **First derivative:** dy/dt = a sin t; dx/dt = a(1 − cos t) ⟹ dy/dx = sin t/(1 − cos t). **Simplify:** multiply by (1 + cos t)/(same): numerator sin t(1 + cos t) = sin t + sin t cos t; denominator 1 − cos²t = sin²t ⟹ dy/dx = (1 + cos t)/sin t = cot(t/2). **Second derivative:** d/dt[cot(t/2)] = −cosec²(t/2)·(1/2) ⟹ d²y/dx² = [−½cosec²(t/2)]/[a(1 − cos t)] = [−½cosec²(t/2)]/[2a sin²(t/2)] = **−1/(4a sin⁴(t/2))**. **Behaviour:** negative for all 0 < t < 2π ⟹ the arch is concave down throughout; as t → 2π, sin⁴(t/2) → 0⁺ ⟹ d²y/dx² → **−∞** — the curvature's magnitude blows up at the cusp, where the tangent turns vertical. **Trap exhibited:** the naive quotient (d²y/dt²)/(d²x/dt²) = (−a cos t)/(a sin t) = −cot t gives a completely different (and wrong) curve — the two-step recipe is not optional. **Deep point:** the second derivative is a rate of change of a rate, so it needs its own chain-rule pass — differentiate dy/dx in t, then divide by dx/dt; the cycloid's concave-down arch (the brachistochrone's descent always flattening) is the standard witness.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find dy/dx for x² + y² = 25 at (3, 4) and write the tangent and normal lines.** — **Implicit differentiation:** 2x + 2y·y′ = 0 ⟹ y′ = **−x/y** — the slope field of every circle centred at the origin. **At (3, 4):** y′ = −3/4. **Tangent:** y − 4 = (−3/4)(x − 3) ⟹ 3x + 4y = 25. **Normal (slope = −1/m = 4/3):** y − 4 = (4/3)(x − 3) ⟹ 4x − 3y = 0 — the normal passes through the origin, as it must for a circle (the radius is the normal). **Deep point:** the slope field −x/y encodes the whole circle's geometry: radius direction (x, y) and tangent slope (−y, x) are perpendicular dot products — implicit differentiation rediscovered the circle's classical tangent theorem.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find dy/dx for the curve x³ + y³ = 3xy (the folium) at the point where x = y (first quadrant).** — **Find the point:** x = y ⟹ 2x³ = 3x² ⟹ x = 3/2 ⟹ point (3/2, 3/2). **Implicit differentiation:** 3x² + 3y²·y′ = 3y + 3x·y′ ⟹ divide 3: x² + y²y′ = y + xy′ ⟹ y′(y² − x) = y − x² ⟹ y′ = (y − x²)/(y² − x). **At (3/2, 3/2):** numerator = 3/2 − 9/4 = −3/4; denominator = 9/4 − 3/2 = 3/4 ⟹ y′ = **−1**. **Geometry check:** by symmetry about y = x, the curve crosses its symmetry line perpendicularly ⟹ slope −1 ✓. **Deep point:** the implicit answer (y − x²)/(y² − x) is a slope field — it works at every non-singular point of the folium, including the loop's far side where y is a *different* function of x; that multi-valued power is exactly what solving for y first could never deliver.
@@summary@@
Parametric differentiation divides the rates — dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) — and its second derivative is a two-step recipe: differentiate dy/dx in t, then divide by dx/dt, never a quotient of second derivatives. Implicit differentiation is the chain rule wearing y(x): differentiate every y-term with the inner derivative y′, then solve one linear equation, obtaining a slope field that serves every branch at once. The cycloid's cusp and the circle's radius-normal are the pattern's two permanent witnesses.
