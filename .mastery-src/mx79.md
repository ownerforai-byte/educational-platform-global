## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to Area Between Two Curves

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to area between two curves: the top-minus-bottom integral, intersection finding, and the split protocol in one page. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the theorems, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Basic formula:** area = ∫ₐᵇ[top − bottom]dx where top is the larger y on each strip — always nonnegative.
- **Intersections:** solve f(x) = g(x) to find a and b (and any interior crossings that force splits).
- **Split protocol:** if the curves cross inside [a, b], the top/bottom roles swap — partition at each crossing and sum the pieces.
- **With respect to y:** for horizontally simple regions, area = ∫[right − left]dy — choose the axis that minimises splits.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Top Minus Bottom Is |Difference| Made Structured

- The area between curves is ∫|f − g|dx in principle; the **top-minus-bottom form is that absolute value resolved by knowledge of which curve is on top** — the intersection analysis converts |·| into an explicit order. Finding intersections is not bookkeeping; it is the absolute value's disassembly.
- **Interior crossings swap roles:** between two crossings the sandwich may flip — the strip picture (vertical slices, who is on top?) is the reliable test on each subinterval, and one test point per strip settles it.
- **Symmetric setups:** equal curves mirrored about an axis (y² = x vs x = 4-style pairs) often yield double-the-half regions — compute one symmetric half and double, provided the symmetry is verified, not assumed.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Choosing the Slicing Axis

- **Vertical slices (dx)** suit curves given as y = f(x); **horizontal slices (dy)** suit curves given as x = g(y) or regions bounded left-right by functions of y. The choice that avoids splitting is the cheaper computation.
- **Parabola-and-line economy:** for y² = 4x and x = 1-style regions, integrating in dy uses one integral; in dx it needs two (the region's left boundary changes formula at the vertex). The slicing axis is a computational decision, made by counting the required splits.
- **Sanity checks:** the answer must be positive; a rough sketch bounds it (compare with an enclosing rectangle); a known special case (line under line = triangle) validates the setup.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — no interior crossing:** one integral, top minus bottom.
- **Type B — interior crossing:** split at the crossing, two integrals.
- **Type C — dy slicing:** horizontal strips for x = g(y) boundaries.
- **Type D — symmetric doubling:** verify symmetry, compute half, double.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"Top minus bottom needs absolute values":** once the order is known per strip, plain subtraction is correct — the |·| was resolved by the analysis. **"Integrate in dx always":** count the splits; dy may halve the work.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (6m): Find the area of the region bounded by y = x² and y = x.** — **Intersections:** x² = x ⟹ x = 0, 1 ⟹ limits [0, 1]. **Order test:** at x = 1/2: line 1/2 > parabola 1/4 ⟹ **line on top**. **Evaluate:** ∫₀¹(x − x²)dx = [x²/2 − x³/3]₀¹ = 1/2 − 1/3 = **1/6**. **Sanity:** the region sits inside the unit square (area 1) and is a slender sliver — 1/6 is plausible. **Deep point:** the order test at one interior point settles the whole strip (the order cannot change without a crossing, and there is none inside [0, 1]) — one sample point is a proof, not a guess.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find the area between y = 4 − x² and y = x + 2.** — **Intersections:** 4 − x² = x + 2 ⟹ x² + x − 2 = 0 ⟹ (x + 2)(x − 1) = 0 ⟹ x = −2, 1. **Order test:** at x = 0: parabola 4 > line 2 ⟹ parabola on top throughout (no interior crossing). **Evaluate:** ∫₋₂¹[(4 − x²) − (x + 2)]dx = ∫₋₂¹(2 − x − x²)dx = [2x − x²/2 − x³/3]₋₂¹ = (2 − 1/2 − 1/3) − (−4 − 2 + 8/3) = (7/6) − (−10/3) = 7/6 + 20/6 = **27/6 = 9/2**. **Deep point:** the intersection solutions came from a quadratic whose factorisation was checked by expansion — the limits are the roots, and the order test confirmed a single-strip region; two integrals would have been wrong here, one was right.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find the area of the region bounded by x = y² and x = 4.** — **Set-up choice:** the boundaries are x = y² (left) and x = 4 (right) — functions of y ⟹ **horizontal slices (dy)**. **Intersections:** y² = 4 ⟹ y = ±2 ⟹ limits y ∈ [−2, 2]. **Order:** at y = 1: right boundary 4 > left boundary y² = 1 ✓. **Evaluate:** ∫₋₂²(4 − y²)dy = 2∫₀²(4 − y²)dy (even) = 2[4y − y³/3]₀² = 2(8 − 8/3) = 2·(16/3) = **32/3**. **Cost comparison:** in dx this region needs two integrals (left boundary is √x for y > 0 and −√x for y < 0); in dy, one — the axis choice halved the work. **Deep point:** the slicing axis is decided by the boundary functions' form, and the symmetry doubling (even integrand in y) stacked a second saving; two cheap decisions replaced two hard integrals.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find the area enclosed between y² = 2x and y = x − 4 — carefully identifying all boundary pieces.** — **Intersections:** substitute x = y + 4 into y² = 2x: y² = 2y + 8 ⟹ y² − 2y − 8 = 0 ⟹ (y − 4)(y + 2) = 0 ⟹ y = 4, −2 ⟹ points (8, 4) and (2, −2). **Slicing choice:** the parabola is naturally x = y²/2 (left boundary), the line x = y + 4 (right boundary) ⟹ **dy slices**, one integral. **Order test:** at y = 0: line x = 4 > parabola x = 0 ✓. **Evaluate:** ∫₋₂⁴[(y + 4) − y²/2]dy = [y²/2 + 4y − y³/6]₋₂⁴ = (8 + 16 − 64/6) − (2 − 8 + 8/6) = (24 − 32/3) − (−6 + 4/3) = 40/3 + 14/3 = **54/3 = 18**. **Deep point:** the classic trap is integrating in dx and missing that the region's left boundary changes at the vertex — the dy choice kept one formula per boundary; when curves are given as x = g(y), the horizontal slice is the honest native habitat.
@@summary@@

Interior crossings swap the roles and demand partitioning; the slicing axis is chosen by counting splits; and verified symmetry doubles a half.
