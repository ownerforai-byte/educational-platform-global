## 1. Topic Introduction — When One Structure Is Not Enough

Some molecules refuse to be drawn once. **Benzene** has no ordinary alternating bonds; **ozone** has two identical bonds that single-bond/double-bond pictures cannot both describe. **Resonance** solves this: the real molecule is a **hybrid** of several valid structures (**canonical forms**), differing only in electron placement. The **resonance (mesomeric, R) effect** extends this to substituents that donate (+R) or withdraw (−R) electrons through π systems.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Resonance — the concept

- When a single Lewis structure cannot represent a molecule, the molecule is written as a **hybrid of two or more canonical structures** connected by double-headed arrows (↔).
- **Rules:** only **electrons (π or lone pairs) move** — **atoms never move**; all canonical forms must have the same atom positions; the real structure is the **weighted hybrid**, more stable than any single form.
- **Resonance energy:** the extra stabilisation of the hybrid over the best single structure — for benzene ≈ **150 kJ/mol**.

### 2.2 NEB 2023's two classics

- **Ozone:** ⁻O–O⁺=O ↔ O=O⁺–O⁻ — the two O–O bonds are **identical in reality** (intermediate between single and double).
- **Benzene:** the two **Kekulé structures** (alternating double bonds swapped) ↔; all six C–C bonds are equivalent, each about 1.39 Å — between single (1.54 Å) and double (1.34 Å).

### 2.3 The resonance (mesomeric) effect

- Permanent electron displacement **through a π system** by substituents:
  - **+R (electron-donating by resonance):** groups with **lone pairs** conjugated to the ring — **−OH, −OR, −NH₂, −NR₂, −Cl, −Br** (halogens donate by resonance though they withdraw inductively). They **increase** electron density at **ortho/para** positions.
  - **−R (electron-withdrawing by resonance):** groups that **pull π-electrons** — **−NO₂, −CN, −CHO, −COOH, C=O**. They **decrease** electron density at ortho/para and leave the meta position relatively richer.

### 2.4 Consequences

- +R groups **activate** aromatic rings toward electrophilic substitution and direct **ortho/para**; −R groups **deactivate** and direct **meta**.
- Resonance explains benzene's substitution-over-addition behaviour, carboxylate stability, and the colour of dyes (extended conjugation).

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Drawing canonical structures correctly

- Move **electrons only**: a π pair or a lone pair shifts; a σ bond may become a π bond or vice versa; **charges appear where the electrons land**. Atoms, σ skeletons and total charge stay fixed. Arrows are **↔** (resonance), never ⇌ (equilibrium).

### 4.2 Why the hybrid beats every contributor

- Delocalising electrons over more atoms **lowers energy** — like spreading a load over more pillars. Benzene's hybrid gains ≈150 kJ/mol over cyclohexatriene, which is why it **substitutes rather than adds**: addition would destroy the delocalisation that pays the stabilisation.

### 4.3 +R and −R at work on the ring

- **Phenol:** oxygen's lone pair donates into the ring (+R) — the ortho/para carbons become electron-rich, so bromination happens instantly and selectively at those positions.
- **Nitrobenzene:** −NO₂ pulls π-electrons (−R) — the ring is depleted at ortho/para, so substitution is slow and lands **meta**.

## 5. Comparison Tables

**Resonance vs inductive effect**

| Basis | Resonance (R) | Inductive (I) |
|---|---|---|
| electrons moved | π electrons / lone pairs | σ electrons |
| pathway | conjugated π system | σ-bond chain |
| distance behaviour | does not simply fade | fades in 3–4 bonds |
| requirement | conjugation | any polar bond |
| example group | −OH (+R), −NO₂ (−R) | −OH (−I), alkyl (+I) |

**+R vs −R groups**

| Basis | +R | −R |
|---|---|---|
| electron flow | into the π system | out of the π system |
| source | lone pairs on the group | π acceptors (multiple bonds) |
| examples | −OH, −OR, −NH₂, −NR₂, halogens | −NO₂, −CN, −CHO, −COOH, C=O |
| ring effect | activates o/p positions | deactivates, directs meta |

## 6. Common Confusions

**Resonance is not equilibrium:** the molecule does not flip between structures; it is **one hybrid** all the time. ⇌ is wrong; ↔ is right.
%%%
**Atoms never move in resonance:** only electrons shift — if you find yourself relocating an H or a group, you are drawing a different compound (a tautomer), not a canonical form.
%%%
**Halogens are two-faced:** −Cl withdraws by induction (−I) but donates by resonance (+R) — the combination makes it deactivating yet ortho/para-directing.

@@summary@@
**Resonance** exists where one Lewis structure is not enough: the molecule is a **hybrid of canonical forms** that differ only in electron placement — **electrons move, atoms never do** — and the hybrid is stabilised by the **resonance energy** (benzene ≈150 kJ/mol). The NEB 2023 classics: **ozone**, ⁻O–O⁺=O ↔ O=O⁺–O⁻, whose two bonds are really identical; and **benzene**, whose two Kekulé structures average to six equal 1.39 Å C–C bonds with delocalised π electrons — hence substitution over addition. The **resonance (mesomeric) effect** extends the idea to substituents: **+R groups** (−OH, −OR, −NH₂, halogens) donate lone-pair electrons into the ring, enriching and activating the **ortho/para** positions; **−R groups** (−NO₂, −CN, −CHO, −COOH) withdraw π-electrons, deactivating the ring and directing **meta**. Resonance travels through π systems; the inductive effect through σ bonds — together they map the electron geography of every aromatic and conjugated molecule.
