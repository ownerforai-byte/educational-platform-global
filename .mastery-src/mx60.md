## 1. Topic Introduction — The Indeterminate-Forms Drill Gallery

This canonical is the **drill gallery for the indeterminate forms**: a focused problem set where every item is a form diagnosis followed by its cure. The parent canonical teaches the forms; this companion runs them at exam speed with four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror — The Diagnosis Table

| Form | Cure | Signature |
|---|---|---|
| 0/0 | factor, conjugate, steer, L'Hôpital | shared root or matched stars |
| ∞/∞ | degree comparison, dominant division | rational or mixed-family race |
| 0·∞ | flip one factor | x ln x patterns |
| ∞ − ∞ | merge fractions, conjugate | sec − tan, √(x²+x) − x |
| 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ | log bridge f^g = e^{g ln f} | compound interest, xˣ |

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Drilling the Diagnosis, Not the Algebra

- The algebra of each cure is short; the **skill is the diagnosis** — seeing that (e^{5x} − 1)/(ln(1+2x)) is a coefficient-ratio 0/0 before touching it. Drills train the eye: form first, cure second, arithmetic last.
- **Cross-form recognition:** one expression can be read two ways — x·e^{−x} is 0·∞ but becomes ∞/∞ after the flip; the drill's lesson is that forms are *properties of the current expression*, changed by legal rewriting, not labels glued to the problem.
- **Termination awareness:** each cure should visibly reduce the form's severity (0/0 → substitution; ∞/∞ → ladder). If a pass leaves the same form, the cure was misapplied — stop and re-diagnose.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — The Exam Economy of Forms

- **Marks live in the form line:** writing "0/0 (indeterminate)" earns the recognition mark even if algebra slips later; skipping it makes a correct answer look lucky.
- **The cheapest cure wins:** a coefficient ratio (two stars) beats three L'Hôpital passes; a degree comparison beats a dominant division. Drill trains cost ranking — pick the two-line cure over the five-line one when both work.
- **Verification is one line:** differentiate back, or re-derive by a second cure. Every drill answer here carries a cross-check because the habit is the point.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — matched stars:** same-order 0/0 ⟹ coefficient ratio.
- **Type B — flip races:** 0·∞ ⟹ quotient ⟹ ladder or L'Hôpital.
- **Type C — merge and resolve:** ∞ − ∞ ⟹ conjugate ⟹ hidden constant.
- **Type D — power forms:** log bridge ⟹ single constant ⟹ e^c.

## 6. Common Confusions

**"Every 0/0 needs L'Hôpital":** the stars are usually cheaper. **"∞ − ∞ = 0":** the form hides anything — merge first.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (6m): Diagnose the form and evaluate lim_{x→0} (ln(1 + 4x))/(e^{2x} − 1).** — **Form:** (ln 1)/(1 − 1) = **0/0** ✓. **Cure — coefficient ratio:** ln(1+4x) ~ 4x (coefficient 4); e^{2x} − 1 ~ 2x (coefficient 2) ⟹ same order ⟹ **4/2 = 2**. **Rigorous:** [ln(1+4x)/(4x)]·[(2x)/(e^{2x}−1)]·2 → 1·1·2 = 2 ✓. **Deep point:** both stars are first-order with the anchor at 0 — the diagnosis "matched first-order stars" selected the two-line cure over L'Hôpital; cost ranking is part of the method.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Diagnose the form and evaluate lim_{x→∞} x²·e^{−3x}.** — **Form:** ∞ · 0 ⟹ **0·∞ species**. **Flip:** x²/e^{3x} ⟹ **∞/∞** ✓. **Ladder:** exponential dominates any power ⟹ **0**. **L'Hôpital double-check:** x²/e^{3x} → 2x/(3e^{3x}) → 2/(9e^{3x}) → 0 ✓. **Deep point:** the flip turned the product into the ladder's home turf; two L'Hôpital passes confirm — the drill's lesson is that the ladder verdict was available instantly, and the passes are the verification, not the method.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Diagnose the form and evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} x^{1/x}.** — **Form:** base → 0⁺, exponent 1/x → +∞ ⟹ **0^∞** — actually *determinate*: a small positive base raised to a huge power collapses to 0. **Log bridge confirms:** x^{1/x} = e^{(ln x)/x}; as x → 0⁺, ln x → −∞ and 1/x → +∞ ⟹ (ln x)/x → **−∞** (negative divided by small positive) ⟹ e^{−∞} = **0**. **Contrast:** xˣ at 0⁺ gave 1 (0⁰, indeterminate) — the exponent's direction flips the verdict. **Deep point:** 0^∞ is *not* on the indeterminate list — the drill's discipline is checking the list before working: determinate forms answer outright, and here the answer is 0 with the log bridge as the formal certificate.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Diagnose the form and evaluate lim_{x→1} (1/(x − 1) − 1/ln x).** — **Form:** both terms → ∞ (1/(x−1) blows up; 1/ln x → ∞ since ln x → 0) ⟹ **∞ − ∞**. **Merge:** = [ln x − (x − 1)]/[(x − 1)ln x]. **Form re-check:** numerator → 0 − 0 = 0; denominator → 0·0 = 0 ⟹ **0/0** ✓. **L'Hôpital:** numerator′ = 1/x − 1; denominator′ = ln x + (x−1)/x. At x = 1: (1 − 1)/(0 + 1) = **0** — still 0/0? Numerator′(1) = 0, denominator′(1) = 1 ⟹ the ratio → 0/1 = **0**. **Second pass confirms:** numerator′′ = −1/x², denominator′′ = 1/x + 1/x² ⟹ at 1: −1/2 ⟹... the first pass already resolved (0/1). **Answer: 0.** **Deep point:** the merge converted ∞ − ∞ into a working quotient, and the re-check after merging (0/0) licensed L'Hôpital — forms are properties of the *current* expression; the drill's rule is to re-diagnose after every rewrite.
@@summary@@
The indeterminate-forms drill trains the subject's real skill: diagnosis. Each of the seven shapes routes to a named cure — factor or steer for 0/0, degrees for ∞/∞, a flip for 0·∞, a merge for ∞ − ∞, the log bridge for the power trio — and the cheapest cure that works is the right one. Forms are properties of the current expression, re-checked after every rewrite; determinate neighbours (like 0^∞) answer outright. Write the form, apply the cure, cross-check the verdict.
