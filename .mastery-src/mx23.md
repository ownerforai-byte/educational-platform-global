## 1. Topic Introduction — Quadratics Fully Decoded

The discriminant b² − 4ac governs everything: **nature of roots**, and the **Viète relations** sum = −b/a, product = c/a — the pair that converts root information into coefficient equations and back.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 The quadratic formula and discriminant

- **x = (−b ± √D)/2a**, D = b² − 4ac.
- **Nature theorem:** D > 0 ⟹ two distinct real roots; **D = 0** ⟹ one repeated real root (−b/2a); **D < 0** ⟹ two conjugate complex roots (−b ± i√|D|)/2a.
- **Perfect-square sub-case:** D > 0 and a perfect square ⟹ rational roots (factorable over ℚ).

### 2.2 Viète's relations (root–coefficient theorems)

- For roots α, β of ax² + bx + c = 0: **α + β = −b/a** and **αβ = c/a**.
- Proof: ax² + bx + c = a(x − α)(x − β) = a[x² − (α+β)x + αβ]; comparing coefficients gives both relations.
- Consequences: α² + β² = (α+β)² − 2αβ = b²/a² − 2c/a; 1/α + 1/β = (α+β)/αβ = −b/c; |α − β| = √D/|a|.

### 2.3 Building equations from roots

- **Theorem:** the monic quadratic with roots α, β is **x² − (α+β)x + αβ = 0** — sum with a minus, product as is.
- Symmetric transformations: roots kα, kβ or α+k, β+k or 1/α, 1/β — recompute the new sum and product, then rebuild.

### 2.4 Graph connections

- Real roots ⟺ the parabola cuts the x-axis; repeated root ⟺ tangency (vertex on the axis); complex roots ⟺ no intersection. Sign of a and position of c refine the picture.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why the discriminant decides

- √D must be real (D ≥ 0) or imaginary (D < 0); the formula's structure makes D the complete classifier — one number, three regimes.

### 4.2 Viète as a translation device

- Many questions never ask for the roots — only for symmetric combinations (sums of squares, reciprocals). Viète converts such expressions into coefficients without solving anything.

### 4.3 The reconstruction template

- Any "new roots are f(α), f(β)" question: compute the new sum S′ and product P′ via Viète, then write x² − S′x + P′ = 0. Four lines, every time.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Sum carries the minus:** α + β = −b/a — dropping the sign is the most common Viète error.
%%%
**Complex roots come in conjugate pairs** only when coefficients are real — state the hypothesis.
%%%
**|α − β| = √D/|a|:** the |a| in the denominator is forgotten when a is negative.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0. (2m)** — Factor: (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 ⟹ **x = 2, 3**. Viète check: sum 5 = −(−5)/1 ✓, product 6 = 6/1 ✓ — the root–coefficient relations confirm the factorisation.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): If α and β are the roots of 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0, find α² + β² and 1/α + 1/β.** — Viète: α+β = 5/2, αβ = 3/2. **α² + β²** = (α+β)² − 2αβ = 25/4 − 3 = **13/4**. **1/α + 1/β** = (α+β)/αβ = (5/2)/(3/2) = **5/3**. No root was ever computed — Viète did all the work.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): For what value of k does x² + kx + 9 = 0 have equal roots?** — Equal roots ⟺ **D = 0**: k² − 36 = 0 ⟹ **k = ±6**. For k = 6 the double root is −3; for k = −6 it is +3 — the sign of k only moves the root's position, not the tangency.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Form the quadratic equation whose roots are 2 + √3 and 2 − √3.** — Sum = 4; product = 4 − 3 = 1. **Equation: x² − 4x + 1 = 0.** (The roots are conjugate surds, so the coefficients are rational — as guaranteed for quadratics with rational coefficients.)

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Solve x² − 5x + 6 = 0. (2m)** — **Factoring:** two numbers with product 6 and sum −5 are −2 and −3: x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0 ⟹ **x = 2 or x = 3**. **Viète verification:** the roots' sum is 2 + 3 = 5, matching −b/a = −(−5)/1 = 5 ✓; their product is 6, matching c/a = 6/1 ✓ — the root–coefficient relations independently confirm the factorisation. **Discriminant reading:** D = 25 − 24 = 1 > 0 (a perfect square ⟹ rational roots, exactly what factoring found).
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): If α and β are the roots of 2x² − 5x + 3 = 0, find α² + β² and 1/α + 1/β without solving the equation.** — **Viète relations:** α + β = −b/a = 5/2 and αβ = c/a = 3/2. **Sum of squares:** α² + β² = (α + β)² − 2αβ = (5/2)² − 2(3/2) = 25/4 − 3 = **13/4**. **Sum of reciprocals:** 1/α + 1/β = (α + β)/(αβ) = (5/2)/(3/2) = **5/3**. **Method note:** both target expressions are **symmetric** in α, β — expressible through the sum and product alone — so the roots themselves never need to be found; Viète's relations are exactly the translation dictionary from root-symmetric language to coefficient language.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): For what values of k does the equation x² + kx + 9 = 0 have (i) equal roots, (ii) real roots?** — **(i) Equal roots ⟺ D = 0:** D = k² − 4(1)(9) = k² − 36 = 0 ⟹ **k = ±6**. For k = 6 the repeated root is −b/2a = −3; for k = −6 it is +3 — the parabola is tangent to the x-axis in either case. **(ii) Real roots ⟺ D ≥ 0:** k² − 36 ≥ 0 ⟹ **k ≤ −6 or k ≥ 6**, i.e. |k| ≥ 6 — outside the open interval (−6, 6) the parabola meets the axis (twice for |k| > 6, once at |k| = 6), and inside it the roots retreat into a conjugate complex pair.
@@summary@@
The **discriminant** D = b² − 4ac is the complete root classifier: **D > 0** two distinct real roots (rational when D is a perfect square — NEB 2024's x² − 5x + 6 = 0 has D = 1, roots 2 and 3), **D = 0** a repeated root at −b/2a (tangency — k² − 36 = 0 gives k = ±6), **D < 0** conjugate complex roots. **Viète's relations** α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a — proved by matching a(x−α)(x−β) with the original — translate any **symmetric** root expression into coefficients without solving: α² + β² = (α+β)² − 2αβ = 13/4 and 1/α + 1/β = (α+β)/αβ = 5/3 for 2x² − 5x + 3. Reconstruction runs the other way: **x² − (α+β)x + αβ = 0** builds the equation from its roots (2 ± √3 ⟹ x² − 4x + 1). Sum minus, product as-is, D first: the topic's three standing orders.
