## 1. Topic Introduction — From Rock to Metal

This topic is the engine room of the unit: how a concentrated ore becomes a metal. Four verbs carry it — **calcination** (heat the carbonate), **roasting** (heat the sulphide in air), **smelting with carbon reduction** (coke does the chemistry), and **electrochemical reduction** (electrons do what coke cannot). NEB 2024 asked three of these directly; the thermite reaction and Hall–Héroult complete the picture.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Concentration (dressing) of the ore

- **Gravity/washing** — lighter gangue washes away (oxide ores, gold).
- **Magnetic separation** — magnetite (Fe₃O₄) pulled from silica by a magnet belt.
- **Froth flotation** — for sulphides: powdered ore + water + pine oil + air; sulphide particles are wetted by oil and ride the froth, silicate gangue is wetted by water and sinks. (Verified PYQ NEB 2024.)

### 2.2 Calcination vs roasting

| Basis | Calcination | Roasting |
|---|---|---|
| Ore type | carbonates, hydrated oxides | sulphides |
| Air | absent/limited | in excess air |
| Chemistry | thermal decomposition | oxidation to oxide + SO₂ |
| Example | ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂; Al₂O₃·2H₂O → Al₂O₃ | 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂ |
| Purpose | porous oxide, drive off H₂O/CO₂ | oxide form + volatile As/Sb removal |

### 2.3 Reduction of the oxide

- **Carbon reduction (smelting):** coke at 1000–1500 °C — ZnO + C → Zn + CO; Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ (CO made in situ: C + O₂ → CO₂, CO₂ + C → 2CO).
- **Thermite:** Al reduces less active oxides, 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe (ΔH ≈ −850 kJ) — molten iron for track welding; also the proof that Al is a stronger oxygen-lover than Fe.
- **Electrochemical reduction:** for Na, K, Mg, Ca, Al — molten salt electrolysis (Downs cell, Hall–Héroult). Aluminium cannot be won by carbon reduction (NEB 2022): needs ~2000 °C, forms Al₄C₃ — so Al₂O₃ is dissolved in molten cryolite Na₃AlF₆ (~950 °C) and electrolysed: cathode 2Al³⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Al, anode 3O²⁻ → 1½O₂ + 6e⁻ (carbon anodes burn away).

## 3. Scientific Definitions

| Term | Scientific meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Calcination** | Heating the ore without/with little air to decompose carbonates | CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ |
| **Roasting** | Heating the sulphide ore in excess air below the melting point | 2PbS + 3O₂ → 2PbO + 2SO₂ |
| **Smelting** | Melting the roasted ore with flux and reductant to yield metal + slag | blast furnace |
| **Thermite process** | Reduction of metal oxides by aluminium powder | Fe₂O₃ + 2Al |
| **Froth flotation** | Concentration of sulphide ores by oil-wetted air bubbles | ZnS, PbS, CuFeS₂ |

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Froth flotation — surface chemistry doing the sorting

- Sulphide surfaces are hydrophobic once pine oil adsorbs; blown air attaches bubbles to the sulphide grains and floats them as a mineralised froth, while water-wetted silica sinks. Collectors (xanthates) and depressants fine-tune which sulphide floats — one plant can separate ZnS from PbS from FeS₂.

### 4.2 Why roast below the melting point

- Roasting must leave the charge porous and solid so the furnace gas (O₂) reaches every grain; melting would glaze the charge and trap unroasted cores. "Roasting in excess air below the melting point" is the definitional phrase NEB wants.

### 4.3 The thermite temperature

- The reaction Al + Fe₂O₃ releases ~850 kJ per mole of Fe₂O₃ with no external heat once started — the iron produced is white-hot molten (mp 1538 °C exceeded), which is why field railway welders pour it straight into the joint mould.

### 4.4 Hall–Héroult in numbers

- Electrolyte: Al₂O₃ ~2–5 % in molten cryolite + CaF₂, ~950–980 °C; steel cell with carbon lining = cathode; consumable carbon anodes; cell voltage ~4–5 V, current ~100–400 kA. Oxygen formed oxidises the anode (CO₂), so anodes are replaced continuously.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Calcination is not "heating in air":** the defining difference from roasting is the **absence of excess air** — calcination decomposes, roasting oxidises.
%%%
**Thermite does not extract aluminium:** it *consumes* aluminium to make iron; aluminium itself must be electrolysed first. The exam question "why is Al not extracted by carbon reduction" is answered with temperature + carbide, not with thermite.
%%%
**Smelting vs roasting:** roasting is a solid-gas oxidation below melting point; smelting melts the charge and separates metal from slag.

## 7. Exam Application

**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (2m): Distinguish between calcination and roasting with one example of each.** — **Calcination** is heating the ore in the absence or limited supply of air below its melting point, used for carbonate and hydrated ores: zinc carbonate decomposes to the oxide, ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂↑, and hydrated alumina loses water. **Roasting** is heating the sulphide ore in **excess air** below its melting point, oxidising the sulphide to the oxide and driving off sulphur dioxide: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂↑. Both leave the oxide needed for reduction, but calcination is decomposition without air and roasting is oxidation with air.
%%%
**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (2m): What is froth flotation? For which ores is it used?** — **Froth flotation** is a concentration process for finely powdered **sulphide** ores: the ore is agitated with water, pine oil and a collector; air is blown through, and sulphide particles, preferentially wetted by the oil, attach to the air bubbles and rise as a mineralised froth that is skimmed off, while the siliceous gangue, wetted by water, sinks to the bottom. It is used for zinc blende (ZnS), galena (PbS) and copper pyrites (CuFeS₂).
%%%
**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (3m): Describe the Hall-Heroult process for the extraction of aluminium.** — Purified bauxite (Al₂O₃) is dissolved at ~2–5 % in **molten cryolite** (Na₃AlF₆) with some CaF₂, which lowers the working temperature to about 950–980 °C (pure Al₂O₃ would melt at 2050 °C). The electrolytic cell is a steel tank lined with carbon that serves as the **cathode**; thick carbon blocks dipped into the melt are the **anodes**. On passing a large direct current, aluminium is deposited at the cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al (tapped from the bottom), and at the anode oxygen is liberated which burns the carbon away: 2O²⁻ − 4e⁻ → O₂, C + O₂ → CO₂ — hence the anodes are continuously replaced. Overall: 2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂.
%%%
**Verified PYQ NEB 2022 (2m): Explain why aluminium cannot be extracted by carbon reduction of its oxide but is obtained by the electrolysis of a solution of alumina in molten cryolite.** — Aluminium has a very large affinity for oxygen: Al₂O₃ has an extremely negative free energy of formation, so carbon reduction would require a temperature of about 2000 °C, and at such temperatures the aluminium produced reacts with the coke forming aluminium carbide (Al₄C₃) — the yield is poor and the process impractical. Instead, Al₂O₃ is dissolved in molten cryolite (Na₃AlF₆), which lowers the melting point to ~950 °C and increases conductivity, and the melt is electrolysed: Al³⁺ is discharged at the carbon-lined cathode as molten metal, so the electrical work does what carbon cannot.

@@keyPoints@@
Concentration: gravity washing, magnetic separation (Fe3O4), froth flotation (sulphides; pine oil + air, sulphide floats, gangue sinks). Calcination: no/limited air, carbonates/hydrates -> oxide (ZnCO3 -> ZnO + CO2; Al2O3.2H2O -> Al2O3). Roasting: excess air, sulphides -> oxide + SO2 (2ZnS + 3O2 -> 2ZnO + 2SO2), below melting point. Smelting/carbon reduction: ZnO + C -> Zn + CO; Fe2O3 + 3CO -> 2Fe + 3CO2 (CO in situ: C + O2 -> CO2, CO2 + C -> 2CO). Thermite: 2Al + Fe2O3 -> Al2O3 + 2Fe, ~850 kJ, rail welding. Electrochemical reduction: Na/K/Mg/Ca/Al; Hall-Heroult: Al2O3 2-5% in molten cryolite Na3AlF6 + CaF2 at ~950 C, carbon lining cathode, consumable carbon anodes burn to CO2; 2Al2O3 -> 4Al + 3O2. Al not by carbon: 2000 C + Al4C3 (NEB 2022).
@@importantConcepts@@
Every step of the chain — float, roast, reduce — exists to deliver one thing to the furnace: a porous oxide. Sulphides and carbonates are merely the oxide's temporary disguises.

**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (2m): Distinguish between calcination and roasting with one example of each.** — **Calcination** is heating the concentrated ore in the absence or limited supply of air below its melting point; it is applied to carbonate and hydrated ores and simply decomposes them: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂ (zinc carbonate → zinc oxide), or Al₂O₃·2H₂O → Al₂O₃ + 2H₂O. **Roasting** is heating the concentrated sulphide ore in **excess air** below its melting point; it oxidises the sulphide to the oxide and removes sulphur as sulphur dioxide: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂. Both give the oxide needed for the next reduction step, but calcination needs no air while roasting is an oxidation by air.
%%%
**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (2m): What is froth flotation? For which ores is it used?** — **Froth flotation** is the standard concentration method for finely powdered **sulphide** ores. The powdered ore is suspended in water with pine oil (and a collector such as a xanthate); when air is blown through the suspension, the sulphide particles — preferentially wetted by oil rather than water — attach themselves to the air bubbles and are carried up as a persistent mineralised froth which is skimmed off, while the heavier siliceous gangue, wetted by water, sinks and is discarded. It is used for zinc blende (ZnS), galena (PbS) and copper pyrites (CuFeS₂); oxide ores are not treated this way.
%%%
**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (3m): Describe the Hall-Heroult process for the extraction of aluminium.** — Purified alumina (Al₂O₃) is mixed into **molten cryolite** (Na₃AlF₆) with a little calcium fluoride: the cryolite dissolves the alumina and lowers the working temperature from 2050 °C to about 950–980 °C, besides making the melt a better conductor. The cell is a steel tank with a **carbon lining that acts as the cathode**, and thick carbon blocks suspended in the melt act as **anodes**. On passing a heavy direct current: cathode — Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al, molten aluminium collects at the bottom and is siphoned off; anode — oxygen is discharged, 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻, and it immediately burns the carbon anode away (C + O₂ → CO₂), so anode blocks are lowered continuously to replace what is consumed. Overall: 2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂.
@@summary@@
The extraction chain runs: **concentrate** (gravity, magnets, or froth flotation for sulphides — oil-wetted grains ride air bubbles while wetted gangue sinks), then **calcination** (carbonates → oxide, no air: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂) or **roasting** (sulphides → oxide, excess air: 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂), then **reduction**: carbon reduction in the smelter where CO made in situ (CO₂ + C → 2CO) strips the oxygen (Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂); thermite (2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe) for Cr/Mn and rail welding; and **electrolysis of molten salts** where carbon fails — Hall–Héroult dissolves Al₂O₃ in molten cryolite at ~950 °C, deposits aluminium at the carbon-lined cathode and burns carbon anodes at the anode surface, because direct carbon reduction of Al₂O₃ would need 2000 °C and yield carbide.
