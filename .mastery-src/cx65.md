## 1. Topic Introduction — Nitrogen Wears More Hats Than Any Element

Nitrogen's oxidation states run +1 to +5, and each has its oxyacid: HNO₂ (nitrous), HNO₃ (nitric), plus hyponitrous H₂N₂O₂, nitroxylic H₂N₂O₄ and pernitric HNO₄. This topic names and formulas them, then zooms into nitric acid — the Ostwald process (verified NEB 2021) and its oxidising chemistry.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 The oxyacids of nitrogen — name and formula table

| Oxidation state | Formula | Name | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| +1 | H₂N₂O₂ | Hyponitrous acid | Weak, unstable; salts = hyponitrites |
| +2 | HNO₂ | Nitrous acid | Known only in solution; salts = nitrites (NaNO₂, KNO₂) |
| +3 | HNO₂ | (same as +2: N is +3 in HNO₂) — see below | — |
| +3 | HNO₃? No — HNO₃ is +5 | — | — |
| +4 | H₂N₂O₄ | Nitroxylic acid (hyponitric) | Unstable |
| +5 | HNO₃ | Nitric acid | The strong acid, oxidant |
| +5 (peroxo) | HNO₄ | Pernitric acid | Unstable peroxo acid |

- Careful with oxidation states: in **HNO₂**, H(+1) + O(−2×2) = −4 → N = **+3**. In **HNO₃**, N = **+5**. In **H₂N₂O₂**, each N = **+1**. In **H₂N₂O₄**, each N = **+3**. In **HNO₄**, N = **+5** (with a peroxo O at −1).
- The NEB-standard list: **H₂N₂O₂ (hyponitrous), HNO₂ (nitrous), HNO₃ (nitric), HNO₄ (pernitric)** — with nitrous and nitric the two that matter chemically.

### 2.2 Nitrous acid HNO₂

- Prepared in situ: NaNO₂ + dil. H₂SO₄ (cold) → NaHSO₄ + HNO₂; unstable — decomposes to HNO₃ + NO + H₂O on standing.
- **Dual redox:** oxidant (Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺, I⁻ → I₂) and reductant (MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺, itself → NO₃⁻).
- Nitrites: NaNO₂ used in curing meats (colour + botulism control), diazotisation in dyes.

### 2.3 Nitric acid HNO₃ — the flagship

- **Structure:** planar NO₃⁻ with resonance (three equivalent N–O bonds, bond order 1⅓); HNO₃ strong acid (fully ionised in water).
- **Ostwald process (verified NEB 2021):**
  1. 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O (Pt–Rh gauze, 800–900 °C)
  2. 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ (cooling, oxidation)
  3. 3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO (absorption in water; NO recycled)
- **Laboratory:** KNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ → KHSO₄ + HNO₃ (distil ≤ 200 °C; glass retort).
- **Oxidising behaviour:** dilute → NO; concentrated → NO₂; very dilute + active metals (Mg, Mn, Zn) → N₂O/NH₄NO₃; noble metals dissolve in **aqua regia** (3HCl : 1HNO₃): Au + HNO₃ + 4HCl → HAuCl₄ + NO + 2H₂O.
- Passivity: Fe, Al, Cr become passive in conc. HNO₃ (oxide film) — hence acid shipped in aluminium tanks.

### 2.4 Uses

- Fertilisers (ammonium nitrate, urea via NH₃), explosives (TNT, nitroglycerine, ammonium nitrate), nylon precursors, pickling metals, laboratory reagent.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why NO₂ appears with concentrated acid

- Concentrated HNO₃'s reduction product is NO₂ (brown); dilute acid's is NO. The acid's own concentration decides its reduction depth — a favourite viva question: Cu + 4HNO₃(conc) → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O vs 3Cu + 8HNO₃(dil) → 3Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO + 4H₂O.

### 4.2 The Ostwald conditions

- The NH₃–air mixture (~1:10 by volume, ammonia ~10 %) must stay **below the explosive limit**; the Pt–Rh gauze at 800–900 °C gives >95 % conversion to NO; subsequent cooling oxidises NO to NO₂, and counter-current water absorption converts NO₂ to acid while recycling NO.

### 4.3 Acid strength trend (the verified NEB 2024 chlorine question's logic)

- Among oxoacids H–O–X, more oxygens withdraw electron density, stabilising the conjugate base — acid strength rises with oxidation state: HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄. The same logic ranks H₂N₂O₂ < HNO₂ < HNO₃.

## 5. Comparison Tables

**HNO₂ vs HNO₃**

| Basis | HNO₂ (nitrous) | HNO₃ (nitric) |
|---|---|---|
| Stability | Unstable, solution only | Stable, fuming liquid |
| Acid strength | Weak | Strong (fully ionised) |
| Oxidising power | Mild, dual behaviour | Strong oxidant |
| Reduction product | NO (as reductant: →NO₃⁻) | NO₂ (conc), NO (dil) |
| Salts | Nitrites (NaNO₂) | Nitrates (KNO₃) |

| Condition | Product with metals | Example |
|---|---|---|

## 6. Common Confusions

**Oxidation state bookkeeping:** students mislabel HNO₂ as N(+2); arithmetic gives +3 (H +1, O −2). Write the sum before labelling.
%%%
**HNO₂ is not a bottle acid:** it exists only in solution and decomposes on standing — "prepare in situ" is the correct phrasing.
%%%
**Passive metals still dissolve eventually:** passivity is a surface film, not immunity — heating or dilution breaks it; Fe dissolves in dilute HNO₃ readily.

## 7. Exam Application

**Verified NEB 2021 (3m): Describe the industrial preparation of nitric acid by the Ostwald process. Give balanced equations, the catalyst and the conditions.** — **Step 1 — catalytic oxidation of ammonia:** a 1:10 ammonia–air mixture is passed over a red-hot **platinum–rhodium gauze** at about **800–900 °C**: 4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O (conversion > 95 %). **Step 2 — oxidation of NO:** the gas is cooled and mixed with more oxygen: 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ (brown gas). **Step 3 — absorption:** NO₂ is absorbed in water in counter-current absorption towers: 3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO; the NO formed is re-oxidised and recycled. The product is ~60 % acid, concentrated further by distillation with conc. H₂SO₄ (dehydrating agent) to give fuming nitric acid. The ammonia fraction is kept low (~10 %) to stay outside the explosive NH₃–air range.

**Verified NEB 2021 (3m): Describe the industrial preparation of nitric acid by the Ostwald process. Give the balanced equations, the catalyst and the conditions.** — **Step 1 — catalytic oxidation of ammonia:** a mixture of ammonia and clean air (ammonia kept at about **10 %**, safely below the explosive limit of NH₃–air mixtures) is passed over a red-hot **platinum–rhodium gauze** catalyst at **800–900 °C**: **4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O**. The conversion of ammonia to nitric oxide exceeds 95 %. **Step 2 — oxidation of nitric oxide:** the hot gas is cooled (to about 50 °C) and treated with more air, converting NO to brown nitrogen dioxide: **2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂**. **Step 3 — absorption:** the NO₂ is absorbed in water flowing counter-current in absorption towers: **3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO**. The nitric oxide released is re-oxidised and recycled to step 2, so ultimately all NO₂ ends as acid. The acid obtained is about 60 %; it is concentrated to fuming nitric acid by distillation with concentrated sulphuric acid, which holds back the water.

**NEB-style (2m): Name and give the formulas of the oxyacids of nitrogen, showing the oxidation state of nitrogen in each.** — **Hyponitrous acid — H₂N₂O₂:** each nitrogen is in the **+1** state (+1×2 + (−2×2) = −2 balancing the two H⁺... formally: 2(+1) + 2x + 2(−2) = 0 → x = +1). It is weak and unstable; its salts are hyponitrites. **Nitrous acid — HNO₂:** nitrogen is in the **+3** state (+1 + x + 2(−2) = 0 → x = +3); it exists only in aqueous solution and its salts are the nitrites (NaNO₂, KNO₂). **Nitric acid — HNO₃:** nitrogen is in the **+5** state; the strong, stable acid whose salts are nitrates. **Pernitric acid — HNO₄:** nitrogen is also **+5** but one oxygen is a peroxo oxygen (−1); a very unstable peroxo acid.

**NEB-style (2m): Write balanced equations for the action of concentrated and dilute nitric acid on copper, naming the reduction product in each case.** — **Concentrated nitric acid:** **Cu + 4HNO₃(conc) → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O** — the nitrate ion is reduced only to **NO₂**, seen as dense brown fumes; the copper dissolves to blue Cu(NO₃)₂ solution. **Dilute nitric acid:** **3Cu + 8HNO₃(dil) → 3Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO + 4H₂O** — the reduction goes deeper to colourless **NO**, which turns brown at the mouth of the tube as it meets air (2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂). The difference illustrates that the acid's concentration controls how far its nitrate is reduced.
@@summary@@
Nitrogen's oxyacids are a staircase of oxidation states: **H₂N₂O₂** (hyponitrous, N +1), **HNO₂** (nitrous, N +3 — a solution-only, unstable acid with dual redox behaviour, whose nitrite salts cure meats and diazotise dyes), **HNO₃** (nitric, N +5 — the strong, fully-ionised, resonance-stabilised flagship), and the unstable peroxo **HNO₄** (pernitric). Nitric acid's industrial route is the **Ostwald process** (NEB 2021): a ~10 % ammonia–air mixture (below the explosive limit) over a **Pt–Rh gauze at 800–900 °C** gives NO in >95 % yield (4NH₃ + 5O₂ → 4NO + 6H₂O); cooling plus air converts NO to NO₂ (2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂); water absorption turns NO₂ to acid with NO recycled (3NO₂ + H₂O → 2HNO₃ + NO), and H₂SO₄ dehydration finishes the fuming product. Its oxidising personality is concentration-coded: **concentrated acid hands metals brown NO₂** (Cu + 4HNO₃), **dilute acid colourless NO** (3Cu + 8HNO₃), **very dilute acid with Zn/Mg/Mn reaches N₂O or NH₄NO₃**, and **aqua regia (3HCl:1HNO₃)** dissolves even gold — while Fe, Al and Cr turn **passive** behind oxide films, letting aluminium tankers ship the acid. Uses: fertilisers, explosives, nylon, pickling — the +5 state at work everywhere.
