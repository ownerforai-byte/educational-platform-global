## 1. Topic Introduction — One Ion, One Job

This topic walks the roster: **Na, K, Mg, Ca, Fe, Cu, Zn, Ni, Co, Cr** — each metal ion with its own address in the body. The exam's centre of gravity is the two flagship complexes (Fe²⁺ in haemoglobin, Mg²⁺ in chlorophyll) plus the electrolyte pair (Na⁺/K⁺) and the structural ion (Ca²⁺). Learn each ion as a one-line job description and the whole topic collapses into a table.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 The electrolyte pair: Na⁺ and K⁺

- **Na⁺** — the principal **extracellular** cation: controls fluid balance and osmotic pressure of blood, and carries nerve impulses (rising phase of the action potential).
- **K⁺** — the principal **intracellular** cation: sets the resting membrane potential, activates several enzymes.
- Their gradients across the nerve membrane are maintained by the **Na⁺/K⁺ pump** (3 Na⁺ out : 2 K⁺ in per ATP — the next topic).

### 2.2 The structural and signalling ions: Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺

- **Ca²⁺** — bones and teeth as **hydroxyapatite, Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂** (~99 % of body Ca); triggers **muscle contraction**; essential for **blood clotting**; intracellular messenger.
- **Mg²⁺** — the central ion of **chlorophyll** (plants); in animals an **enzyme activator** (phosphatases, ATP-using enzymes) — Mg²⁺ complexes ATP.

### 2.3 The transport and catalytic metals: Fe, Cu, Zn, Ni, Co, Cr

- **Fe²⁺** — haemoglobin (each of 4 haem groups binds one O₂) and myoglobin; **Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺** cycling in cytochromes for respiration. Deficiency → anaemia.
- **Cu** — **haemocyanin**, the blue oxygen carrier of molluscs and crustaceans; cytochrome oxidase cofactor.
- **Zn²⁺** — **carbonic anhydrase** (CO₂ hydration) and carboxypeptidase; insulin storage; wound healing; Zn²⁺ (d¹⁰) works by Lewis acidity, not redox.
- **Ni** — **urease** (the enzyme that hydrolyses urea) is a nickel metalloenzyme.
- **Co** — **vitamin B₁₂ (cyanocobalamin)**; red blood cell formation; deficiency → pernicious anaemia.
- **Cr** — part of **glucose tolerance factor**, which assists insulin in glucose uptake.

### 2.4 Carbon monoxide — the dark side of Fe²⁺

- CO binds Fe²⁺ of haemoglobin ~**200–250 times more strongly** than O₂, forming **carboxyhaemoglobin**. Even 0.1 % CO in air converts much of the Hb, starving tissues of oxygen — CO poisoning is fatal because the blocked sites cannot release or take up O₂.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why iron and magnesium sit at the centres

- Both haem and chlorophyll are **chelate rings** (porphyrin-type) that grip a divalent metal. Fe²⁺ gives haemoglobin its reversible O₂ binding; Mg²⁺ gives chlorophyll its light-absorbing electronics. Swap the metals and neither molecule works — the biology is the coordination chemistry.

### 4.2 Deficiency and excess in one view

- **Fe deficiency** → anaemia; **Mg deficiency in soil** → chlorosis (yellow leaves, failing photosynthesis); **Ca deficiency** → weak bones, poor clotting; **Co deficiency** → pernicious anaemia (via B₁₂); **Cr deficiency** → impaired glucose tolerance.
- **Excess is also disease:** Cu overload → Wilson's disease; Fe overload → haemochromatosis (last topic).

### 4.3 d-block versus s-block roles

- The s-block ions (Na⁺, K⁺, Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺) work by **charge and gradients** — no redox chemistry, just electricity and structure. The d-block ions (Fe, Cu, Zn, Ni, Co, Cr) work by **coordination chemistry** — binding O₂, substrates and ligands at specific sites.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Fe²⁺, not Fe³⁺, carries oxygen:** only the +2 state binds O₂ reversibly; oxidation to Fe³⁺ (methaemoglobin) destroys the carrying ability until reduced back.
%%%
**Ca²⁺ is needed for clotting — removing it prevents clotting:** citrate/oxalate anticoagulants work by tying up Ca²⁺; blood in a citrated tube does not clot.
%%%
**Cobalt is not in haemoglobin:** Co works through vitamin B₁₂ (red-cell formation); the haem metal is iron.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Discuss the biological roles of sodium, potassium, calcium, and magnesium in the human body.** — **Sodium (Na⁺):** the principal extracellular cation; it regulates fluid volume and osmotic pressure of blood and tissue fluid and carries the inflow current of nerve impulses. **Potassium (K⁺):** the principal intracellular cation; it sets the resting membrane potential of nerves and muscles and activates several enzymes. The Na⁺/K⁺ gradients are maintained by the Na⁺/K⁺ pump (3 Na⁺ out : 2 K⁺ in per ATP). **Calcium (Ca²⁺):** ~99 % of it is deposited as hydroxyapatite, Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂, in bones and teeth; the remaining 1 % triggers muscle contraction, is essential for blood clotting, and acts as an intracellular messenger. **Magnesium (Mg²⁺):** central ion of chlorophyll in plants; in the body it activates many enzymes (phosphatases, ATP-requiring reactions) by complexing ATP.
%%%
**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Why is iron present in haemoglobin and magnesium in chlorophyll? What happens on deficiency?** — Both molecules are **porphyrin-type chelate rings**, and the ring's electronics select the metal: Fe²⁺ has the right d-orbitals to bind O₂ **reversibly** — ideal for transport — while Mg²⁺ gives the conjugated ring the electron arrangement that **absorbs visible light** — ideal for photosynthesis. The metal is chosen by function. **On deficiency:** iron deficiency reduces haemoglobin synthesis, causing **anaemia** (pallor, fatigue, breathlessness); magnesium deficiency in soil prevents chlorophyll formation, causing **chlorosis** — yellowing leaves and failing photosynthesis.
%%%
**NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Explain the biological role of haemoglobin in blood. What is carboxyhaemoglobin, and why is carbon monoxide poisoning fatal?** — **Haemoglobin** is the Fe²⁺-containing protein of red blood cells; each molecule carries **four haem groups**, and each Fe²⁺ binds one O₂ **reversibly** — loading oxygen in the lungs (pO₂ high) and releasing it in the tissues (pO₂ low). This is the body's oxygen-transport system. **Carboxyhaemoglobin** is haemoglobin in which **carbon monoxide has bound to the Fe²⁺ in place of oxygen**. CO binds Fe²⁺ roughly **200–250 times more strongly** than O₂, so even small CO concentrations convert a large fraction of haemoglobin into a form that **cannot bind or release O₂**. Tissues are starved of oxygen (chemical asphyxiation) while the blood may still look well-oxygenated — hence headaches, unconsciousness and death in CO poisoning; treatment is fresh air/oxygen, which slowly displaces CO.
%%%
**NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): Give the formula of chlorophyll and explain the role of magnesium in plants. What happens if there is a deficiency of magnesium in the soil?** — **Chlorophyll a: C₅₅H₇₂O₅N₄Mg** (chlorophyll b: C₅₅H₇₀O₆N₄Mg). The **Mg²⁺ ion sits at the centre of the porphyrin-type ring**; it is the heart of the light-harvesting system — the conjugated ring with its central Mg²⁺ absorbs red and blue light and drives the primary reactions of photosynthesis. **Magnesium deficiency in soil:** the plant cannot synthesise chlorophyll, so leaves lose their green colour and turn yellow (**chlorosis**); photosynthesis falls, growth is stunted and yields drop. Farmers correct it with magnesium salts (e.g. MgSO₄ — Epsom salt).
%%%
**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): What is the role of cobalt in the human body? Name its vitamin.** — Cobalt is the central metal of **vitamin B₁₂ (cyanocobalamin)**, held in a corrin ring. B₁₂ (as its coenzyme forms) is required for **red blood cell formation** and for certain reactions of amino-acid and fat metabolism. Its deficiency causes **pernicious anaemia** — immature, oversized, poorly functioning red cells. The vitamin must come from the diet (animal foods; the body cannot build the cobalt complex).

@@summary@@
Ten ions, ten jobs. **Na⁺** runs the extracellular fluid and the nerve impulse; **K⁺** runs the intracellular side and the resting potential (the Na⁺/K⁺ pump holds 3-out/2-in per ATP). **Ca²⁺** is the structural and signalling ion — hydroxyapatite Ca₁₀(PO₄)₆(OH)₂ in bone, trigger of muscle contraction and blood clotting. **Mg²⁺** is chlorophyll's centre (chlorophyll a, C₅₅H₇₂O₅N₄Mg) and ATP's enzyme activator. **Fe²⁺** carries oxygen — four haem groups, four O₂, reversible binding — and its hijacking by CO (carboxyhaemoglobin, ~200–250× tighter binding) is why CO poisoning kills. **Cu** powers haemocyanin and cytochrome oxidase; **Zn²⁺** catalyses in carbonic anhydrase and stores insulin; **Ni** runs urease; **Co** rides in vitamin B₁₂ (pernicious anaemia when missing); **Cr** assists insulin in glucose tolerance factor. Deficiency names to quote: anaemia (Fe), chlorosis (Mg), pernicious anaemia (Co/B₁₂).
