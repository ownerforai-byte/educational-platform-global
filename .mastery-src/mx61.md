## 1. Topic Introduction — Drill Gallery: Assembling Limits by the Laws

This canonical is the **first limits drill gallery**: problems whose limits assemble directly from the limit laws — sums, products, quotients, compositions — with the hypotheses named. It mirrors the algebraic-properties parent; the drill's discipline is legal assembly.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror — The Assembly Kit

- **Sum, product, constant multiple:** combine freely once every piece's limit exists.
- **Quotient:** denominator's limit must be nonzero — check before dividing.
- **Power/root:** apply to a converged base; even roots need a nonnegative limit.
- **Composition:** outer function continuous at the inner limit ⟹ push the limit through.
- **Polynomials:** continuous everywhere ⟹ substitution is all the laws at once.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Naming Hypotheses Is the Drill

- Each assembly step consumes a hypothesis: "both limits exist," "denominator ≠ 0," "outer continuous at 9." The drill's discipline is naming each one — the laws are implications, and the working is the proof that the implications apply.
- **When assembly stalls, re-group:** a decomposition with a nonexistent piece (sin(1/x) inside a product) is not a dead end but a *wrong decomposition* — regroup so every piece converges, or switch to the squeeze.
- **Composition chains build depth:** lim f(g(h(x))) pushes through two licences — outer continuous at g's limit, g continuous at h's limit. Long chains are just repeated licence checks.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Substitution as the Assembly Shortcut

- For continuous integrands, the whole assembly collapses to one substitution — because continuity means every law applies at once. Knowing *when* the shortcut is licensed (the composition theorem, closure of continuity) is what separates substitution-as-habit from substitution-as-theorem.
- **The boundary of the shortcut:** denominator zeros, domain edges, piecewise joints — exactly the suspect list. The drill's items deliberately park near these boundaries so the licence check has teeth.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — quotient with the check:** evaluate numerator and denominator, verify ≠ 0, divide.
- **Type B — composition push:** inner limit, outer continuity, apply.
- **Type C — regroup the illegal split:** identify the nonexistent piece, squeeze or damp it.
- **Type D — root over quotient:** even root needs the inner limit ≥ 0 — check the domain edge.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"Substitute always":** only where continuity is certified — the suspects list is exactly where it is not. **"The laws are obvious":** each is a theorem with a hypothesis; the drill grades the hypotheses.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (x³ − 1)/(x² + 2), naming each law.** — **Numerator (power + difference laws):** 8 − 1 = **7**. **Denominator (sum of power and constant):** 4 + 2 = **6 ≠ 0** — quotient hypothesis ✓. **Quotient law: 7/6.** **Deep point:** the entire computation is three named laws; the check "6 ≠ 0" is the one that could have failed — at any root of x² + 2 (none real) the problem would have changed species, which is why the check is written even when it passes trivially.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→4} √x·(x² − 3), citing each licence.** — **First factor:** lim √x = **2** — root law with inner limit 4 ≥ 0 ✓ (continuity of √t at 4). **Second factor (power + difference):** 16 − 3 = **13**. **Product law:** 2 × 13 = **26**. **Deep point:** the root's licence (inner limit 4, inside the domain's interior) is the nontrivial hypothesis — at x → 0 the same product would need one-sided care because √x's domain edge truncates the two-sided theorem.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (x² + 1)·cos(1/x), and justify the verdict rigorously.** — **The obstruction:** lim_{x→0} cos(1/x) does not exist, so the product law cannot assemble from the obvious decomposition. **Amplitude analysis:** the factor (x² + 1) → **1 ≠ 0** — the oscillation's amplitude does **not** vanish, so no damping occurs and the squeeze theorem cannot rescue a limit (its rails −(x²+1) and (x²+1) converge to *different* values −1 and 1, pinning nothing). **Rigorous non-existence certificate:** along xₙ = 1/(2nπ), cos(1/xₙ) = cos(2nπ) = 1 ⟹ f(xₙ) = xₙ² + 1 → **1**; along yₙ = 1/((2n+1)π), cos(1/yₙ) = cos((2n+1)π) = −1 ⟹ f(yₙ) = −(yₙ² + 1) → **−1**. Two approaches-to-zero sequences with different limits ⟹ **the limit does not exist**. **Deep point:** damping requires the amplitude to *vanish* — x²·cos(1/x) → 0 because x² → 0, but (x²+1)·cos(1/x) keeps swinging between ±(x²+1) → ±1; the subsequence argument is the standard certificate when the squeeze rails fail to meet.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→3} (2x + 1)² + √(x + 6), assembling by the laws with licences named.** — **Inner piece (sum + constant multiple):** lim (2x + 1) = 7. **Power law:** 7² = **49** — the square is continuous everywhere, licence trivial. **Root piece:** lim (x + 6) = 9 ≥ 0 ✓; √t continuous at 9 ⟹ **3**. **Sum law: 49 + 3 = 52.** **One-line shortcut check:** both pieces are continuous at 3 ⟹ substitution gives (7)² + 3 = 52 ✓. **Deep point:** the long route names the licences; the shortcut is the closure theorem compressing them — both routes are the same mathematics, and the drill's value is seeing the compression as earned, not automatic.
@@summary@@
Assembly drills train the limit laws' hypotheses: existing pieces for sums and products, a nonzero denominator for quotients, a converged base for powers and roots, outer continuity for compositions. When a decomposition stalls, regroup or squeeze — and when the amplitude of an oscillation does not vanish, the honest verdict is non-existence, certified by subsequences. Substitution is the closure theorem's compression of all the licences at once; the drill's discipline is knowing when that compression is earned.
