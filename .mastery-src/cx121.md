## 1. Topic Introduction — What "Aromatic" Means Now

The word began as *fragrant* — benzaldehyde, wintergreen oil, benzene itself. Modern chemistry redefined it: **aromatic compounds are benzene-ring systems whose cyclic, planar, fully conjugated π systems obey Hückel's 4n + 2 rule**, and whose chemistry is dominated by *substitution that preserves the ring* rather than addition that destroys it. Fragrance is history; the π-electron bookkeeping is the definition.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror — The Five Characteristics

- **Cyclic and planar:** the π system must close on itself; every ring atom contributes a p orbital held coplanar.
- **Complete conjugation:** every ring atom has a p electron (or a vacant/occupied p orbital) — no sp³ interruptions.
- **Hückel count:** the delocalised π electrons total **4n + 2** (2, 6, 10, 14, ...).
- **Substitution over addition:** electrophilic substitution preserves the aromatic π system; addition would destroy it and is thermodynamically disfavoured.
- **High carbon fraction:** benzene is 92.3% carbon by mass ⟹ luminous, sooty flame; characteristic odour; immiscibility with water.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Why Substitution Beats Addition

- Addition to one double bond of benzene would leave a *cyclohexadiene with two isolated double bonds* — the delocalisation energy (≈ 150 kJ/mol, the resonance energy) is forfeited in one step. Substitution keeps the aromatic sextet intact: the ring absorbs the electrophile and then ejects a proton, restoring the full π cloud.
- **The comparison that proves it:** cyclohexene adds H₂ releasing 120 kJ/mol; cyclohexa-1,3-diene releases 232 (two bonds, some conjugation); *cyclohexatriene-by-extrapolation* would release ~360 — but benzene releases only 208. The missing 152 kJ/mol is the price of breaking aromaticity, and it is charged on *every* addition pathway. Substitution routes never pay it.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Fragrance Is Neither Necessary Nor Sufficient

- Many aromatic compounds smell of nothing (aspirin, naphthalene's mothball note aside, TNT); many fragrant molecules (esters, terpenes) are not aromatic. The historical name survives as a label for the *electronic* property.
- **The modern test is countable:** count planar p electrons; check 4n + 2. Benzene: six sp² carbons, six π electrons, 4(1) + 2 ✓. Cyclooctatetraene: eight π electrons — 4n, not 4n + 2 — and it *escapes* by puckering into a non-planar tub, forfeiting conjugation rather than paying antiaromaticity. The definition's teeth are counterexamples, not examples.

## 6. Common Confusions

**"Aromatic means smelly":** the modern meaning is electronic — Hückel's rule, not the nose. **"All cyclic conjugated molecules are aromatic":** cyclobutadiene (4 π) and cyclooctatetraene (8 π) are cyclic and conjugated but *not* aromatic — the count decides.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): List the characteristics of aromatic compounds and use them to show that benzene is aromatic.** — **Checklist:** (1) cyclic — six carbons in a ring; (2) planar — all carbons sp², the ring is flat; (3) fully conjugated — every carbon contributes a p orbital with no sp³ interruption; (4) π-electron count 4n + 2 — six π electrons = 4(1) + 2 ✓; (5) substitution preferred over addition — nitration, sulphonation, halogenation all proceed with the ring intact. **Verdict:** benzene satisfies every criterion ⟹ aromatic. **Deep point:** the checklist is ordered — planarity can *fail* a molecule that counts correctly (cyclooctatetraene), so count and geometry must both be checked, never one alone.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): The heats of hydrogenation of cyclohexene, cyclohexa-1,3-diene and benzene are 120, 232 and 208 kJ/mol respectively. Show how these numbers measure aromatic stabilisation.** — **Per-bond baseline:** cyclohexene releases 120 kJ/mol for one double bond. **Extrapolated cyclohexatriene:** three isolated bonds ⟹ 3 × 120 = 360 kJ/mol expected. **Actual benzene:** 208 kJ/mol. **Missing energy:** 360 − 208 = **152 kJ/mol** — the resonance (aromatic stabilisation) energy: the energy benzene does *not* release because its π electrons are already delocalised and lower in energy than three localised bonds. **Consequence:** any reaction that destroys the aromatic π system (addition) must repay this energy and is disfavoured; substitution preserves it. **Deep point:** the ladder is the cleanest *experimental* demonstration in the unit — the stabilisation is not a drawing convention but a measured deficit in a calorimeter.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Explain why benzene burns with a smoky flame while cyclohexane burns with a cleaner flame.** — **Carbon fraction:** benzene C₆H₆ is 92.3% carbon (72/78); cyclohexane C₆H₁₂ is 85.7% (72/84). **Mechanism:** the higher the C : H ratio, the more incomplete the combustion in the flame's interior — unburnt carbon particles glow luminous yellow and escape as soot. **Reaction:** 2C₆H₆ + 15O₂ → 12CO₂ + 6H₂O. **Deep point:** the same argument ranks the whole family — benzene smokier than cyclohexene, smokier than cyclohexane; the flame is a rough C% meter, and examiners award the reasoning chain (composition ⟹ incomplete combustion ⟹ soot), not the observation alone.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Classify cyclobutadiene, benzene and cyclooctatetraene as aromatic, antiaromatic or non-aromatic, with reasons.** — **Cyclobutadiene (C₄H₄):** cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, 4 π electrons = 4n (n = 1) ⟹ fails 4n + 2 ⟹ **antiaromatic** — it cannot escape planarity (four-membered ring is rigidly flat) and pays the antiaromatic destabilisation; it exists only transiently at very low temperature. **Benzene:** 6 π = 4(1) + 2, planar, conjugated ⟹ **aromatic**. **Cyclooctatetraene (C₈H₈):** 8 π = 4n — the count fails, *but* the eight-membered ring is flexible: it puckers into a tub shape, breaking p-orbital overlap, becoming a normal conjugated-free polyene ⟹ **non-aromatic** (not antiaromatic — it escaped). **Deep point:** the classification has three outcomes, not two — "antiaromatic" is reserved for molecules forced to stay planar with a 4n count; a 4n molecule that puckers is simply non-aromatic, and the geometry check is what separates the two verdicts.
@@summary@@
Aromatic is an electronic verdict, not a smell: cyclic, planar, fully conjugated, 4n + 2 π electrons, and chemistry that substitutes rather than adds. The hydrogenation ladder (120, 232, 208 against 360 expected) measures the 152 kJ/mol of stabilisation that addition must repay and substitution never does; the counterexamples — antiaromatic cyclobutadiene and puckered, non-aromatic cyclooctatetraene — give the definition its teeth. Count the electrons, check the geometry, and the classification follows.
