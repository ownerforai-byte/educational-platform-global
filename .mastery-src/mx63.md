## 1. Topic Introduction — Drill Gallery: Limits at Infinity

This canonical is the **third limits drill gallery**: behaviour at infinity — degree comparison, dominant-term division, the growth ladder, and horizontal asymptotes. It mirrors the infinity machinery of the parent canonicals; the drill's discipline is rung recognition.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror — The Infinity Toolkit

- **Degree comparison (rational):** top > bottom ⟹ ±∞; tie ⟹ leading-coefficient ratio; top < bottom ⟹ 0.
- **Dominant division:** divide numerator and denominator by the fastest-growing term; everything slower dies like 1/xᵏ.
- **Growth ladder:** ln x ≪ xᵖ ≪ aˣ ≪ xˣ — each rung dominates all below, coefficients irrelevant across rungs.
- **Radicals at ±∞:** √(x² + k) ~ |x| — the absolute value splits the two directions; x → −∞ needs the sign flip.
- **Horizontal asymptotes:** lim_{x→±∞} f = L ⟹ y = L is a horizontal asymptote (a destination, not a barrier).

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Rungs, Not Coefficients

- At infinity, **family beats arithmetic**: an exponential with a tiny base beats a polynomial with a huge degree; a log with a huge argument loses to a humble x¹⁰⁰. Coefficients compete only *within* a rung (degree comparison, leading ratios); across rungs the verdict is fixed.
- **Why the ladder is rigid:** exponentials compound multiplicatively, powers additively, logs by diminishing increments — the growth mechanisms are ordered, and no finite coefficients can reorder them. This is why eˣ/x¹⁰⁰ → ∞ *eventually*, even though x¹⁰⁰ leads for a long while: limits measure the destination, not the journey.
- **The radical's absolute value is the trap:** √(x²) = |x|, so √(x² + 1) − x → 0 as x → +∞ but → 2 as x → −∞. Two-sided infinity questions always split by direction.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Asymptotes as Readable Destinations

- **Horizontal:** lim at ±∞ = L ⟹ the curve flattens toward y = L — the degree tie's leading ratio made visible.
- **Vertical:** denominator zeros with nonzero numerator ⟹ walls — the finite analogue of the ladder's verdicts.
- **Oblique (preview):** when top degree = bottom degree + 1, long division exposes a slanted destination y = mx + c — the degree rule's next case, met properly in later units.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — rational ties:** divide by the top power; leading ratio.
- **Type B — mixed families:** identify the rungs; the higher rung's coefficient survives.
- **Type C — radical two-sided:** |x| split; different answers at ±∞.
- **Type D — ladder extremes:** exponential over polynomial, log over power — instant verdicts with a one-line certificate.

## 6. Common Confusions

**"Biggest coefficient wins":** only within a rung; across rungs the family decides. **"√(x² + 1) = x + …":** it equals |x|√(1 + 1/x²) — the sign matters at −∞.
@@keyPoints@@
Degree rules: top>bottom -> +-inf; tie -> leading ratio; top<bottom -> 0. Dominant division kills slower terms. Ladder: ln x << x^p << a^x << x^x; rungs beat coefficients. Radicals: sqrt(x^2+k) = |x|sqrt(1+k/x^2) - split at -inf. Horizontal asymptote y = L iff lim at infinity = L.
@@importantConcepts@@
At infinity, family beats arithmetic: rungs decide across families, coefficients only within one.
@@examples@@
***The two-sided radical*** - sqrt(x^2+1) - x -> 0 at +inf but -> 2 at -inf: the |x| inside the root flips the race's sign.
@@universalFacts@@
Limits at infinity measure destinations, not journeys - e^x trails x^100 for a long while and still wins.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (3x³ − x)/(5x³ + 2x²) and state the horizontal asymptote.** — **Degree tie (3 = 3):** divide by x³: (3 − 1/x²)/(5 + 2/x) → (3 − 0)/(5 + 0) = **3/5**. **Asymptote:** y = 3/5 — the curve flattens toward this line as x → ∞ (and identically at −∞). **Deep point:** the tie case's answer is the leading-coefficient ratio because every lower term dies like 1/xᵏ — the division by the top power is the mechanical realisation of "coefficients compete only within a rung."
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (ln x)/(√x) with a one-line certificate.** — **Rungs:** log (bottom) vs power x^{1/2} — the ladder says the power dominates ⟹ **0**. **Certificate:** the standard star lim_{x→∞} ln x/xᵖ = 0 for every p > 0 (L'Hôpital once: (1/x)/(p x^{p−1}) = 1/(p xᵖ) → 0) — here p = 1/2 ⟹ 0. **Deep point:** the ladder verdict needed no arithmetic; the one-line L'Hôpital certificate is what converts the rung intuition into an exam-grade proof — know both the verdict and its licence.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→−∞} (√(4x² + 1) + 2x).** — **Extract the |x|:** √(4x² + 1) = |2x|·√(1 + 1/(4x²)). **At −∞:** |2x| = −2x ⟹ the expression = −2x·√(1 + 1/(4x²)) + 2x = 2x[1 − √(1 + 1/(4x²))]. **Expand the root:** √(1 + u) ≈ 1 + u/2 with u = 1/(4x²) → 0 ⟹ 1 − √(1+u) ≈ −u/2 ⟹ the expression ≈ 2x·(−1/(8x²)) = −1/(4x) → **0**. **Contrast at +∞:** |2x| = 2x ⟹ expression = 2x[√(1+1/(4x²)) − 1] ≈ 2x·(1/(8x²)) = 1/(4x) → 0 as well — both directions give 0, but by opposite sign routes. **Deep point:** the |x| step is where two-sided radical questions are won or lost — writing √(4x²+1) = 2x (valid only for x > 0) is the classic error; the root always returns the *absolute* value, and the sign flip at −∞ is the whole computation.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (2·3ˣ + x¹⁰⁰)/(5·3ˣ − x³) and justify the rung reasoning.** — **Identify the rungs:** 3ˣ (exponential, top rung) vs x¹⁰⁰ and x³ (powers) — the exponential dominates both, despite x¹⁰⁰'s enormous degree. **Divide by 3ˣ:** (2 + x¹⁰⁰/3ˣ)/(5 − x³/3ˣ). **Ladder:** x¹⁰⁰/3ˣ → **0** and x³/3ˣ → **0** (exponential beats any fixed power). **Limit: 2/5.** **Journey note:** x¹⁰⁰ leads 3ˣ until x ≈ 210 — the limit reads the destination, not the journey. **Deep point:** the division by the *dominant* term (3ˣ, not x¹⁰⁰) is the drill's key move — dividing by the wrong leader would leave the ladder's verdict invisible; dominance, not degree, picks the divisor.
@@summary@@
Infinity drills are rung-reading exercises: within a family, degrees and leading coefficients compete; across families, the ladder (log ≪ power ≪ exponential ≪ super-exponential) decides with coefficients irrelevant. Divide by the dominant term, split radical limits by |x| at −∞, and state horizontal asymptotes as the tie case's visible destination. The ladder's verdicts are instant; their one-line certificates (a single L'Hôpital or star) are what make them exam-grade.
