## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to Substitution and By Parts

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to the two workhorse integration methods: substitution (the chain rule reversed) and integration by parts (the product rule reversed). The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the methods, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Substitution:** ∫f(g(x))g′(x)dx = ∫f(u)du with u = g(x) — the chain rule read backwards; du = g′(x)dx.
- **By parts:** ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du — the product rule integrated; choose u by the **LIATE** priority (Log, Inverse trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential).
- **Method triggers:** a composite with its inner derivative visible ⟹ substitute; a product of unlike types with no inner derivative ⟹ parts.
- **Definite versions:** substitution changes the limits; parts inherits the boundary term uv|ₐᵇ.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Substitution Is a Change of Variables, Not a Trick

- Substitution re-describes the same integral in variables where the anti-derivative is visible: the composite f(g(x)) becomes the plain f(u), and the Jacobian factor g′(x)dx becomes du. The chain rule's structure (outer′ × inner′) is exactly what makes the rewrite legal.
- **Spotting u:** the inner function of the composite — and its derivative must appear (up to constant) in the integrand. When it does not, algebra first (complete the square, split the fraction) manufactures the match.
- **Definite discipline:** change the limits with the substitution — never substitute back; the transformed integral in u with u-limits is complete and shorter.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — By Parts Trades One Integral for Another

- ∫u dv = uv − ∫v du is a **bargain**: surrender the original integral, accept uv plus a new one. The choice of u makes the bargain good — LIATE picks the factor that *simplifies* when differentiated (logs and inverse trig vanish into algebra), leaving a new integral easier than the old.
- **Cyclic parts:** ∫eˣsin x dx returns to itself after two applications — the equation is then solved algebraically (I = expression, solve for I). The method's self-reference is a feature: some integrals close only through their own reflection.
- **Parts proves reduction formulas:** ∫xⁿeˣdx = xⁿeˣ − n∫xⁿ⁻¹eˣdx — each application peels one power, the same ladder logic as reduction in trigonometry.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — linear substitution:** ∫(ax + b)ⁿ — u = ax + b, the Jacobian 1/a.
- **Type B — inner derivative visible:** ∫2x·e^{x²}dx — u = x², du = 2x dx.
- **Type C — LIATE parts:** ∫x·eˣdx, ∫ln x dx, ∫x ln x dx.
- **Type D — cyclic parts:** ∫eˣsin x dx solved as an equation.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"Substitute anything":** u must be the composite's inner function with its derivative present. **"Parts is for all products":** only when substitution fails — ∫2x·e^{x²}dx is a substitution problem wearing a product's clothes.
@@keyPoints@@
Substitution = chain rule reversed: u = inner function, du = g'dx, the Jacobian is the price. Spot u as the composite's inner with its derivative visible (up to constant). Definite version: change the limits, never substitute back. By parts = product rule reversed: integral u dv = uv - integral v du; LIATE picks u (Log, Inverse trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential). Cyclic parts solve as equations. Parts trades one integral for an easier one.
@@importantConcepts@@
The two methods are the chain rule and the product rule read backwards - nothing new, only reversed.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate ∫(3x + 2)⁴dx.** — **Substitution:** u = 3x + 2 ⟹ du = 3dx ⟹ dx = du/3. **Transform:** ∫u⁴·(du/3) = (1/3)·u⁵/5 = u⁵/15. **Back-substitute:** **(3x + 2)⁵/15 + C**. **Verification:** d/dx[(3x+2)⁵/15] = 5(3x+2)⁴·3/15 = (3x+2)⁴ ✓ — the chain rule's 3 cancelled the Jacobian's 3, as it must. **Deep point:** the linear substitution is the pattern's simplest face — the inner derivative (3) appears as the Jacobian and is repaid exactly by the chain rule on verification; every substitution is this transaction.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate ∫x·e^{3x}dx.** — **Method triage:** the product x·e^{3x} has no visible inner derivative of a composite (e^{3x}'s inner 3 is constant) ⟹ **parts**. **LIATE:** u = x (algebraic), dv = e^{3x}dx ⟹ v = e^{3x}/3, du = dx. **Parts:** ∫x e^{3x}dx = x·e^{3x}/3 − ∫(e^{3x}/3)dx = **x·e^{3x}/3 − e^{3x}/9 + C** = e^{3x}(3x − 1)/9 + C. **Verification:** d/dx[e^{3x}(3x−1)/9] = e^{3x}·3(3x−1)/9 + e^{3x}·3/9 = e^{3x}[(3x−1) + 1]/3 = x e^{3x} ✓. **Deep point:** LIATE's choice made the new integral (∫e^{3x}dx/3) trivially easy — the bargain logic: differentiate the factor that simplifies, integrate the one that does not.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate ∫ln x dx for x > 0.** — **The surprise:** ln x has no standard entry — but parts with **u = ln x** (LIATE: log first), dv = dx ⟹ v = x, du = dx/x. **Parts:** ∫ln x dx = x·ln x − ∫x·(dx/x) = x ln x − ∫dx = **x ln x − x + C**. **Verification:** d/dx[x ln x − x] = ln x + 1 − 1 = ln x ✓. **Deep point:** the trick is seeing 1·dx as dv — every "no table entry" integral of a single factor is a parts problem with dv = dx; the log's simplification under differentiation (to 1/x) is what makes the bargain profitable.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate ∫eˣ·sin x dx using cyclic parts, and verify.** — **Parts pass 1:** u = sin x, dv = eˣdx ⟹ I = eˣ sin x − ∫eˣ cos x dx. **Parts pass 2 (on the new integral):** u = cos x, dv = eˣdx ⟹ ∫eˣcos x dx = eˣcos x + ∫eˣ sin x dx = eˣcos x + I. **Substitute back:** I = eˣsin x − eˣcos x − I ⟹ **2I = eˣ(sin x − cos x)** ⟹ **I = eˣ(sin x − cos x)/2 + C**. **Verification:** d/dx[eˣ(sin−cos)/2] = eˣ(sin−cos)/2 + eˣ(cos+sin)/2 = eˣ sin x ✓. **Deep point:** the second pass returned the original integral with a sign — the self-reference became a linear equation solved for I; the ±1/2 factor that emerges is the signature of every cyclic pair (eˣ with sin or cos), and forgetting it is the classic incomplete answer.
@@summary@@
The two workhorse methods are the chain rule and the product rule read backwards: substitution re-describes a composite in variables where the anti-derivative shows, with the Jacobian as the price; by parts trades an integral for uv plus an easier one, with LIATE choosing the factor that simplifies under differentiation. Cyclic pairs close through their own reflection and are solved as equations. Triage first — substitution when the inner derivative is visible, parts when it is not — and verify every answer by differentiating it back.
