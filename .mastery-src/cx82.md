## 1. Topic Introduction — The Last 0.1 %

Extraction gets the metal; **refining** makes it usable — copper must reach 99.99 % for wiring, otherwise its resistance and machinability collapse. This topic covers the purification toolkit: liquation, distillation, oxidation (poling), and the workhorse — **electrolytic refinement** (verified PYQ NEB 2024 on copper).

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Why crude metal is impure

- Blast-furnace/reduction metal carries dissolved C, S, P, Si (from coke and flux), other reduced metals (Pb in Ag, Fe in Cu), and mechanically trapped slag. Each impurity dictates its own removal method.

### 2.2 Refining methods

- **Distillation** — for volatile metals: Zn (bp 907 °C), Hg, Cd are distilled off from less volatile impurities.
- **Liquation** — for low-melting metals: impure Sn or Pb is run down an inclined hearth at a temperature just above the metal's melting point; the pure metal flows out, high-melting impurities (Fe, Cu, C) stay on the hearth.
- **Poling** — for oxidised molten metal (especially crude copper with Cu₂O): green wood poles are stirred into the melt; the wood releases hydrocarbons and steam, the hydrocarbons reduce Cu₂O back to Cu, and the stirring collects dross. ("Poling" — from the pole.)
- **Electrolytic refinement** — impure metal = anode, thin pure metal = cathode, salt solution = electrolyte; the metal dissolves from the anode and deposits pure on the cathode, while nobler impurities fall as **anode mud** and baser ones stay in solution.

### 2.3 Electrorefining of copper (the exam classic)

- Anode: impure Cu (99 %, with Ag, Au, Pt, Ni, Fe, Zn). Cathode: thin pure Cu sheet. Electrolyte: acidified CuSO₄.
- Anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ (also Zn, Fe, Ni dissolve as ions).
- Cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu (only copper deposits; Zn²⁺/Fe²⁺/Ni²⁺ stay in solution because they are harder to reduce).
- **Anode mud:** Ag, Au, Pt are nobler — they do not ionise and fall to the bottom; the mud pays for the refining (gold and silver recovery).

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 The selectivity logic of electrorefining

- Electrolysis separates by electrode potential: baser metals (Zn, Fe) ionise at the anode but their ions stay dissolved (their reduction potentials are below Cu²⁺/Cu); copper shuttles anode→cathode; nobler metals (Ag, Au, Pt) refuse to ionise and drop as mud. One tank, three-way separation.

### 4.2 Why poling uses *green* wood

- Dry wood would only stir. Green wood releases water vapour (stirs, prevents air oxidation) and hydrocarbon gases (CH₄, etc.) that chemically reduce Cu₂O: 2Cu₂O + C(H₄) → 4Cu + CO₂ + 2H₂O-ish reduction chemistry. The pole chars at the surface — the process is visible and self-limiting.

### 4.3 Choosing the method

- Volatile metal → distillation (Zn, Hg). Low melting → liquation (Sn, Pb). Oxide contamination → poling (Cu, Sn). Everything else, and any metal needed at high purity → electrorefining (Cu, Ag, Au, Al three-layer, Ni).

## 6. Common Confusions

**Poling refines, it does not extract:** poling happens on molten metal already won; it removes dissolved oxide (Cu₂O), not ore.
%%%
**Anode mud is not slag:** it is the *noblest* impurities — they are valuable (Ag, Au, Pt), not waste; their sale often pays the refining bill.
%%%
**Baser impurities do not deposit:** Zn²⁺/Fe²⁺/Ni²⁺ from the anode remain in the electrolyte because Cu²⁺ reduces at a higher potential; the electrolyte is periodically bled and refreshed.

## 7. Exam Application

**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (3m): Describe the electrolytic refining of copper.** — The electrolytic tank contains **acidified copper sulphate solution** as electrolyte. The **anode is a thick block of impure copper** (about 99 %, containing Ag, Au, Pt, Ni, Fe, Zn) and the **cathode is a thin sheet of pure copper**. On passing direct current, copper (and the baser metals) dissolve at the anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻; at the cathode only copper is deposited: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, because the baser ions (Zn²⁺, Fe²⁺, Ni²⁺) need a lower reduction potential and remain in solution. The **noble impurities — silver, gold and platinum — do not ionise** and settle below the anode as valuable **anode mud**. The cathode copper reaches ~99.99 % purity, and the gold and silver recovered from the mud repay the cost of refining.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): What is poling? Why is green wood used?** — **Poling** is the refining of molten crude metal (chiefly copper containing cuprous oxide) by stirring it with poles of **green wood**. The hydrocarbon gases released by the charring wood reduce the dissolved oxide back to metal (Cu₂O → Cu), while the steam released stirs the bath and shields it from air oxidation; the stirring also collects the dross on the pole. Green (not dry) wood is used because dry wood would give neither the reducing gases nor the steam.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Name the refining method suited to (i) zinc, (ii) tin, (iii) crude copper with Cu₂O, with one reason each.** — **(i) Zinc — distillation:** zinc boils at 907 °C, far below its main impurities, so it distils over and condenses pure. **(ii) Tin — liquation:** tin melts at 232 °C; on an inclined hearth just above this temperature the tin flows away from the high-melting impurities (Fe, C) which remain. **(iii) Crude copper — poling:** the green-wood gases reduce the dissolved Cu₂O back to copper.

Selectivity = electrode potentials.
@@importantConcepts@@
Electrorefining is electrode-potential sorting: copper is the only metal that both leaves the anode and accepts electrons at the cathode; everyone baser stays dissolved, everyone nobler stays solid — one tank, three-way separation.
@@examples@@
***The mud that pays*** — a copper refinery processing 200,000 t of anodes a year recovers tonnes of silver and hundreds of kilograms of gold from anode mud; the "impurity" funds the purification.
@@universalFacts@@
Copper for electrical use must be 99.99 % pure: 0.05 % of arsenic raises its resistance enough to waste megawatts on a national grid — hence electrolytic copper, "Cu-ETP".

**Verified PYQ NEB 2024 (3m): Describe the electrolytic refining of copper.** — The refining tank is filled with **acidified copper sulphate solution**. A thick block of **impure copper serves as the anode** and a thin sheet of **pure copper as the cathode**. When direct current flows, copper dissolves from the anode: Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻, together with the baser impurities (Zn, Fe, Ni) which also pass into solution as ions. At the cathode **only copper is deposited**, Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu, because the baser metal ions require a lower (more negative) reduction potential than Cu²⁺ and therefore stay dissolved in the electrolyte. The **noble impurities — silver, gold and platinum — do not dissolve at all**; they fall to the bottom beneath the anode as **anode mud**, from which they are later recovered (and they pay for the refining). The cathode copper is 99.99 % pure — the "electrolytic copper" used for electrical wiring.

**NEB-style (2m): What is poling? Why must the poles be of green wood?** — **Poling** is a refining step for molten crude metal — chiefly copper — in which the melt is stirred with poles of green wood while it is covered with an inert material. The purpose is to remove dissolved cuprous oxide: the hydrocarbon gases given off by the charring wood **reduce Cu₂O back to metallic copper**, and the water vapour released stirs the bath and blankets its surface against further oxidation, while the stirring collects dross. **Green wood** is essential because dry wood supplies neither the reducing hydrocarbons nor the steam — it would only stir.

**NEB-style (2m): By which method are (i) zinc, (ii) tin and (iii) crude copper containing Cu₂O refined, and why?** — **(i) Zinc — distillation:** zinc is volatile (bp 907 °C), far below the boiling points of its impurities (iron, lead), so retort distillation leaves them behind and the vapour condenses as pure zinc. **(ii) Tin — liquation:** tin melts at only 232 °C; the impure block is heated on the sloping hearth of a liquation furnace just above this temperature, the pure tin flows down and away, and the high-melting impurities (iron, carbon) remain on the hearth. **(iii) Crude copper — poling:** the molten metal is stirred with green wood poles whose hydrocarbon gases reduce the dissolved Cu₂O to copper while the steam stirs and shields the melt.
@@summary@@
Refining turns extracted metal into usable metal. **Distillation** exploits volatility (zinc bp 907 °C, mercury); **liquation** exploits low melting point (tin flows off an inclined hearth at 232 °C leaving Fe and C behind); **poling** stirs molten crude copper with green wood whose hydrocarbons reduce dissolved Cu₂O and whose steam stirs and shields; and **electrolytic refinement** — the precision method — dissolves an impure anode (Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻) in acidified CuSO₄ and plates 99.99 % copper on a pure cathode (Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu), while baser ions (Zn²⁺, Fe²⁺, Ni²⁺) linger in solution and the **noble impurities Ag, Au, Pt never ionise**, falling as anode mud — the by-product that pays for the whole plant.
