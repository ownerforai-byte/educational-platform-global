## 1. Topic Introduction — Life at Its Simplest and Most Successful

Monera is the kingdom of **prokaryotes** — organisms whose genetic material is not enclosed in a nucleus. Bacteria are the most successful life forms on Earth: they existed 3.5 billion years ago, live from deep-sea vents to your intestine, and run the planet's nutrient cycles. The syllabus covers bacterial structure, nutrition, growth, and the cyanobacteria.

## 2. General Characteristics of Monera and Bacteria

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cell type | **Prokaryotic** — no nuclear envelope, no membrane-bound organelles (no mitochondria, ER, Golgi) |
| Size | 0.5–5 µm; smallest living cells |
| Genetic material | Single circular **nucleoid DNA** (no histones) + small **plasmids** (extra genes, e.g. resistance) |
| Ribosomes | **70S** (eukaryotes have 80S) — the target of many antibiotics |
| Cell wall | **Peptidoglycan** (murein) — unique to bacteria; lysozyme and penicillin attack it |
| Nutrition | Autotrophic (photo-/chemo-) or heterotrophic (saprophytic, parasitic, symbiotic) |
| Reproduction | Asexual binary fission (~20 min); no true sexual reproduction, but genetic recombination occurs |
| Flagella | Simple rotary flagellum (no 9+2 microtubule arrangement) |
| Habitat | Ubiquitous — soil, water, air, hot springs, ice, bodies of plants and animals |

### 2.1 Structure of the bacterial cell (parts in order)

1. **Capsule/slime layer** (some): polysaccharide coat — protection against phagocytosis and drying (virulence factor).
2. **Cell wall:** rigid peptidoglycan; **Gram-positive** = thick peptidoglycan, stains violet, teichoic acids; **Gram-negative** = thin peptidoglycan + outer lipopolysaccharide (LPS) membrane, stains pink, more drug-resistant.
3. **Plasma membrane:** selectively permeable; carries respiratory enzymes (no mitochondria — **mesosomes** are infoldings associated with respiration and cell division).
4. **Cytoplasm:** no organelles; contains **70S ribosomes**, storage granules (volutin, glycogen), and the nucleoid.
5. **Nucleoid:** circular DNA, not enclosed.
6. **Plasmids:** small accessory circular DNAs — carry antibiotic resistance, fertility (F) factors.
7. **Flagella:** rotary hooks for motility; **pili/fimbriae:** protein hairs for attachment and conjugation (sex pilus).
8. **Endospore (some, e.g. *Bacillus*, *Clostridium*):** highly resistant resting spore surviving boiling and decades of dormancy.

### 2.2 Shapes and arrangements

| Shape | Name | Arrangement examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sphere | Coccus | Diplococcus (pairs), Streptococcus (chains), Staphylococcus (clusters), Sarcina (packets) |
| Rod | Bacillus | Single or chained (*E. coli*, *Lactobacillus*) |
| Spiral | Spirillum / Spirochete | *Spirillum*, *Treponema* |
| Comma | Vibrio | *Vibrio cholerae* |

## 3. Mode of Nutrition in Bacteria

### 3.1 Autotrophic

| Type | Energy source | Example | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Photoautotrophic** | Light | Cyanobacteria (*Nostoc*, *Anabaena*) — oxygenic; purple/green bacteria — anoxygenic | Chlorophyll/ bacteriochlorophyll |
| **Chemoautotrophic** | Oxidation of inorganic chemicals | *Nitrosomonas* (NH₃→NO₂⁻), *Nitrobacter* (NO₂⁻→NO₃⁻), *Thiobacillus* (sulfur), *Ferrobacillus* (iron) | Run the nitrogen and sulfur cycles |

### 3.2 Heterotrophic

| Type | Mode | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Saprophytic** | Decompose dead organic matter (extracellular digestion) | *Bacillus subtilis*, decay bacteria |
| **Parasitic** | Live in/on a host causing disease | *Mycobacterium tuberculosis*, *Vibrio cholerae*, *Salmonella typhi* |
| **Symbiotic/mutualistic** | Mutual benefit | *Rhizobium* in legume nodules; *E. coli* in human gut (vitamin K, B12); cellulose-digesting bacteria in ruminants |

## 4. Bacterial Growth

**Binary fission:** the chromosome replicates, the cell elongates, a septum divides it — two identical daughter cells in **20–30 minutes** under ideal conditions.

**Growth curve (batch culture, four phases):**

| Phase | Event | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Lag** | Adaptation; enzyme and RNA synthesis; no division | Cells prepare |
| **Log (exponential)** | Maximum, constant doubling rate | Most sensitive to antibiotics; industrial harvest stage |
| **Stationary** | Nutrients deplete, wastes accumulate; deaths balance divisions | Secondary metabolites (antibiotics) produced |
| **Decline (death)** | Deaths exceed divisions | Population crashes |

Factors affecting growth: temperature (psychrophiles, mesophiles ~37 °C, thermophiles), pH, oxygen (aerobes, anaerobes, facultative), moisture, nutrients.

## 5. Cyanobacteria (Blue-Green Algae)

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Nature | Gram-negative photosynthetic prokaryotes with **chlorophyll a** and **phycocyanin** (blue) + phycoerythrin |
| Habitat | Fresh water, moist soil, hot springs; blooms on eutrophic water |
| Structure | Unicellular, colonial (mucilaginous colonies) or filamentous (**trichomes** with **heterocysts**) |
| **Heterocyst** | Thick-walled specialised cell — the site of **nitrogen fixation** (nitrogenase is oxygen-sensitive; heterocyst lacks oxygenic photosystem II) |
| Examples | *Nostoc*, *Anabaena* (symbiont in *Azolla* and *Cycas* roots), *Oscillatoria*, *Spirulina* (single-cell protein) |
| Importance | Nitrogen fixation in rice paddies; primary producers; *Spirulina* health food; some blooms toxic |

**Nitrogen fixation (NEB 2024):** *Anabaena*/*Nostoc* fix atmospheric N₂ → NH₃ using the enzyme **nitrogenase** inside heterocysts; *Anabaena azollae* lives in the water fern *Azolla* and fertilises rice paddies naturally — a living biofertiliser.

## 6. Economic Importance of Bacteria (beneficial vs harmful — NEB 2023 & 2024)

| Beneficial | Harmful |
|---|---|
| **Dairy:** curd/yoghurt (*Lactobacillus*), cheese | **Diseases:** cholera (*Vibrio*), typhoid (*Salmonella*), TB (*Mycobacterium*), tetanus (*Clostridium*) |
| **Industrial:** vinegar (acetic acid bacteria), lactic acid, antibiotics (*Streptomyces* — streptomycin) | Food spoilage (putrefaction) |
| **Agriculture:** *Rhizobium* N-fixation, composting, biofertilisers (*Azotobacter*) | Plant diseases: citrus canker, crown gall, blight of rice |
| **Sewage treatment:** aerobic/anaerobic decomposition, biogas (methanogens) | Tooth decay (*Streptococcus mutans*) |
| **Gut flora:** *E. coli* synthesises vitamin K and B12 | |
| **Genetic engineering:** plasmid vectors, *E. coli* insulin/hormone production | |

### 8.2 Model answers

**Q1 (3m) — Characteristic features of bacteria + one beneficial and one harmful example.**
Answer: Bacteria are **unicellular prokaryotes**: (1) no nuclear membrane — naked circular DNA (nucleoid) plus plasmids; (2) cell wall of **peptidoglycan**; (3) **70S ribosomes**, no membrane-bound organelles; (4) nutrition autotrophic or heterotrophic; (5) reproduce by **binary fission** (~20 min). **Beneficial:** *Lactobacillus* converts milk to curd (or *Rhizobium* fixes nitrogen in legumes). **Harmful:** *Vibrio cholerae* causes cholera (or *Mycobacterium tuberculosis* — TB).

**Q2 (3m) — Gram staining; Gram-positive vs Gram-negative.**
Answer: **Gram staining** is a differential staining technique (crystal violet → iodine → alcohol decolourisation → safranin) that divides bacteria by wall structure. **Gram-positive:** thick peptidoglycan retains the crystal-violet–iodine complex, stays **violet**; teichoic acids present; sensitive to penicillin (e.g. *Bacillus*, *Staphylococcus*). **Gram-negative:** thin peptidoglycan plus an outer LPS membrane loses the dye on decolourisation and takes **safranin pink**; more resistant to antibiotics (e.g. *E. coli*, *Salmonella*).

**Q3 (3m) — Cyanobacteria and nitrogen fixation.**
Answer: Cyanobacteria are oxygenic photosynthetic prokaryotes with chlorophyll a and phycocyanin ("blue-green algae"), occurring as unicells, colonies or filaments with **heterocysts**. They fix atmospheric nitrogen with **nitrogenase** inside the thick-walled, low-oxygen heterocysts, converting N₂ → NH₃ usable by plants. **Example:** *Anabaena azollae* lives symbiotically in the water fern *Azolla* and enriches rice paddies with nitrogen (also *Nostoc* in soil crusts).

**Q4 (2m) — Four economic importances of microbes.**
Answer: (1) Dairy — *Lactobacillus* in curd and cheese; (2) Medicine — antibiotics from *Streptomyces*; (3) Agriculture — *Rhizobium* nitrogen fixation and composting; (4) Environment — sewage treatment and biogas production (methanogens).

### 8.3 Common examination mistakes

1. Calling bacteria eukaryotes or giving them mitochondria — prokaryotes have **no membrane-bound organelles**.
2. Reversing Gram colours — Gram-positive = **violet**, Gram-negative = **pink**.
3. Saying heterocysts photosynthesise — they **fix nitrogen** (they lack the oxygen-evolving photosystem).
4. Writing binary fission as "sexual reproduction" — it is asexual; recombination (conjugation) is separate.
5. Giving vague "useful/harmful" answers without **named examples** — examples carry the marks.
@@confusion@@
**Nucleoid vs nucleus:** the nucleoid is an unenclosed DNA region; a nucleus has a double membrane — bacteria have the former only.
%%%
**Heterocyst vs vegetative cell:** heterocyst = thick-walled nitrogen-fixing cell without oxygenic photosynthesis; vegetative cells photosynthesise.
%%%
**Pili vs flagella:** pili are attachment/conjugation hairs; flagella are rotary motility organelles.
%%%
**Mesosome vs mitochondrion:** mesosome = plasma-membrane infolding in bacteria; mitochondrion = eukaryotic organelle absent in prokaryotes.

@@summary@@
Monera holds the prokaryotes: bacteria are unicellular cells of 0.5–5 µm with a peptidoglycan wall, 70S ribosomes, a naked circular nucleoid plus plasmids, and no membrane-bound organelles; they take coccus, bacillus, spirillum and vibrio shapes, divide by binary fission in about twenty minutes, and grow through lag, log, stationary and decline phases. They feed as photoautotrophs (cyanobacteria), chemoautotrophs (Nitrosomonas), saprophytes, parasites or symbionts (Rhizobium, E. coli). Cyanobacteria add chlorophyll a, phycocyanin and heterocysts — nitrogenase chambers that fix N2 for rice paddies via Anabaena in Azolla. Economically bacteria give curd, antibiotics, compost, sewage treatment and insulin, but also cholera, typhoid, TB and food spoilage — the twin faces of the most successful cells on Earth.
