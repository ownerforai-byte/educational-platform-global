%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Definition:** f″(x) = d/dx[f′(x)] — the derivative of the derivative; f⁽ⁿ⁾ by iteration.
- **Notations:** d²y/dx², y″, f″(x), D²y — all the same object.
- **Parametric second derivative:** d²y/dx² = [d(dy/dx)/dt]/(dx/dt) — the two-step recipe.
- **Leibniz:** (fg)⁽ⁿ⁾ = Σ C(n,k) f⁽ⁿ⁻ᵏ⁾g⁽ᵏ⁾ — the product rule's binomial generalisation.
- **Key patterns:** (sin mx)⁽ⁿ⁾ = mⁿ sin(mx + nπ/2); (e^{mx})⁽ⁿ⁾ = mⁿe^{mx}; (1/(x−a))⁽ⁿ⁾ = (−1)ⁿn!/(x−a)ⁿ⁺¹.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — The Second Derivative's Personalities

- f″ measures **how the slope changes** — concavity (f″ > 0: cup up), acceleration (velocity's rate), and curvature's sign. The second derivative is the first place the derivative's own behaviour becomes an object of study.
- **The sinusoid's eigen-equation:** y = A sin(mx + φ) satisfies **y″ = −m²y** — differentiating twice returns the function times −m². This is the defining property of sinusoids and the seed of every oscillation equation in physics; the operator D² acting on sin is multiplication by −m².
- **The exponential's eigen-simplicity:** (e^{mx})⁽ⁿ⁾ = mⁿe^{mx} for every n — the exponential is the unique function (up to constant) that is its own derivative family. Polynomials die under repeated differentiation (degree n → 0 after n+1 passes); exponentials and sinusoids cycle or scale forever.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Repeated Differentiation as an Operator Game

- D (the differentiation operator) can be treated as an algebraic object: D²y = D(Dy), and Leibniz's formula is literally the binomial theorem (D + A)ⁿ applied to fg with A = "multiply by g". The binomial coefficients C(n,k) appear because each pass either differentiates f or leaves it — counting the choices.
- **Pattern tables beat repeated work:** the n-th derivatives of sin, cos, e^{mx}, 1/(x−a), ln(1+x) follow closed patterns; drilling the first four derivatives of each reveals the cycle, and the pattern generalises. The exam skill is seeing the cycle by the third pass.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — repeated power differentiation:** y = x⁴ → 24, the factorial pattern.
- **Type B — sinusoid eigen-check:** verify y″ = −m²y for a given sinusoid.
- **Type C — parametric second derivative:** the two-step recipe applied.
- **Type D — Leibniz:** (x²eˣ)⁽ⁿ⁾ or (x² sin x)″ by the binomial formula.

## 6. Common Confusions

**"d²y/dx² means square the first derivative":** it means differentiate twice — the notation is ordinal, not arithmetic. **"Higher derivatives always simplify":** they usually complicate — patterns, not simplification, are the goal.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Find y″ for y = x⁴ − 3x² + 2x and state the factorial pattern for (xⁿ)⁽ⁿ⁾.** — **First pass:** y′ = 4x³ − 6x + 2. **Second pass:** y″ = 12x² − 6. **Pattern:** (xⁿ)′ = n xⁿ⁻¹, (xⁿ)″ = n(n−1)xⁿ⁻², …, (xⁿ)⁽ⁿ⁾ = **n!** — the falling factorial product exhausts the power exactly at pass n, then further passes give 0. **Check:** (x⁴)⁽⁴⁾ = 4·3·2·1 = 24 ✓. **Deep point:** repeated differentiation of a polynomial is a countdown — each pass multiplies by the current exponent and decrements it; the factorial is the countdown's completed product, which is why n! appears throughout combinatorics via this same differentiation game (the exponential generating function).
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Verify that y = 4 sin(2x) satisfies y″ = −4y, and generalise to y = A sin(mx + φ).** — **First pass:** y′ = 8 cos 2x. **Second pass:** y″ = −16 sin 2x. **Compare:** −4y = −16 sin 2x ✓ — the eigen-equation holds with m² = 4. **General case:** y′ = Am cos(mx + φ); y″ = −Am² cos(mx + φ)·... carefully: d/dx[Am cos(mx+φ)] = Am·(−m sin(mx+φ)) = −Am² sin(mx+φ) = **−m²y** ∎. **Interpretation:** each sinusoid is an eigenfunction of D² with eigenvalue −m²; m is read off the eigenvalue — frequency is measurable from the second derivative alone. **Deep point:** y″ = −m²y is the differential equation of oscillation — every spring, pendulum (small-angle) and AC circuit satisfies it, and the sinusoid is its complete solution; the calculus fact and the physics fact are the same statement.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find the n-th derivative of f(x) = 1/(x + 1) and evaluate f⁽ⁿ⁾(0).** — **Pattern hunt:** f′ = −(x+1)⁻²; f″ = 2(x+1)⁻³; f‴ = −6(x+1)⁻⁴; f⁽⁴⁾ = 24(x+1)⁻⁵. **Pattern:** f⁽ⁿ⁾ = **(−1)ⁿ·n!/(x + 1)ⁿ⁺¹** — sign alternates, factorial grows, power increments by one each pass. **At 0:** f⁽ⁿ⁾(0) = (−1)ⁿ·n! — the Taylor coefficients of 1/(1+x) up to sign, i.e. the geometric series 1 − x + x² − ⋯ read backwards. **Deep point:** the pattern's three ingredients (alternating sign, factorial, shifting power) are visible by the third pass — drill the pattern, then state it with n as a parameter; the connection to the geometric series shows differentiation and series expansion are two views of one object.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Use Leibniz's theorem to find the second derivative of y = x²·eˣ, and state the general n-th derivative.** — **Leibniz (n = 2):** (fg)″ = f″g + 2f′g′ + fg″ with f = x², g = eˣ: f′ = 2x, f″ = 2; g′ = g″ = eˣ ⟹ y″ = 2eˣ + 4x·eˣ + x²·eˣ = **eˣ(x² + 4x + 2)**. **General n:** all derivatives of eˣ are eˣ, and f⁽ᵏ⁾ of x² vanishes for k ≥ 3 ⟹ (x²eˣ)⁽ⁿ⁾ = eˣ[C(n,0)x² + C(n,1)·2x + C(n,2)·2] = **eˣ[x² + 2nx + n(n−1)]**. **Check n = 2:** eˣ[x² + 4x + 2] ✓. **Deep point:** Leibniz's binomial structure shines when one factor's derivatives die (polynomial) — the sum truncates, and the answer is a closed polynomial times the surviving factor; this is the standard engine for n-th derivatives of products.
@@summary@@
Higher-order derivatives are repeated passes of the same operator: the second derivative reads concavity and acceleration, the notation is ordinal, and the parametric second derivative keeps its two-step recipe. Patterns rule the n-th derivative game — falling factorials for powers, phase-shifted sinusoids, the exponential's eternal self, alternating factorials over shifted powers — and Leibniz's binomial formula is the product rule grown up. The sinusoid's eigen-equation y″ = −m²y is the bridge where this calculus fact becomes every oscillation in physics.
