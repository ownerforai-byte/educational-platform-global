## 1. Topic Introduction — Three Ways to Write One Molecule

A molecular formula says what is in a molecule but not how it is joined. Chemistry needs three sharper notations: the **structural formula** (every bond drawn), the **contracted (condensed) formula** (groups written together), and the **bond-line (skeletal) formula** (lines only, carbons implicit). Speed in exams — and in reading real papers — comes from fluent conversion between the three.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Molecular formula — the starting point

- Shows the **actual number of each atom**: C₂H₆O. It cannot distinguish ethanol from dimethyl ether — both are C₂H₆O.

### 2.2 Structural formula

- **Every bond is shown explicitly**, usually as dashes: H–C–C–O–H with all H's placed. Fully displays connectivity — which atom is joined to which.

### 2.3 Contracted (condensed) formula

- Bonds within groups are omitted; groups are written together: **CH₃CH₂OH** (ethanol), **CH₃OCH₃** (dimethyl ether), **CH₃COOH** (acetic acid).
- The standard working notation of organic chemistry — compact but unambiguous about connectivity.

### 2.4 Bond-line (skeletal) formula

- Carbon skeleton drawn as **lines/zig-zags only**: every line end or vertex is a carbon; hydrogens on carbon are **implied** (not drawn); hetero atoms (O, N) and their H's **are shown**.
- Fastest notation for large molecules — the default in journals and in this app's figures.
- Conventions: each line end/vertex = C with as many H as valency 4 demands; multiple bonds drawn as double/triple lines.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 The three notations for one compound

- **Ethanol:** molecular C₂H₆O → structural (all 8 bonds drawn) → condensed CH₃CH₂OH (or C₂H₅OH) → bond-line (two-carbon zig-zag ending in O–H).
- **Butane:** C₄H₁₀ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ → four-point zig-zag.
- **Acetic acid:** C₂H₄O₂ → CH₃COOH → zig-zag with –COOH shown.

### 4.2 Why condensed formulas must keep connectivity clear

- CH₃CH₂OH and CH₃OCH₃ share the molecular formula C₂H₆O but the condensed formulas expose the different connectivity (C–C–O–H vs C–O–C) — this is exactly where the molecular formula fails and the condensed formula succeeds.

### 4.3 Reading bond-line structures correctly

- Count carbons at every end and vertex; add implied hydrogens to satisfy valency 4; hetero atoms are labelled. A six-point zig-zag is hexane (C₆H₁₄); a six-membered hexagon with alternating double bonds is benzene.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Molecular formula is not unique:** C₂H₆O fits two different compounds; only structural/condensed/bond-line formulas identify the compound.
%%%
**In bond-line formulas, hydrogens on carbon are implied but hetero-atom H's are drawn:** the O of an alcohol shows its H; the carbons do not.
%%%
**Condensed ≠ molecular:** CH₃CH₂OH is condensed (connectivity preserved); C₂H₆O is molecular (connectivity lost).

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB-style (3m): What is a condensed (contracted) formula? Write the condensed formulas of ethane, ethanol and acetic acid, and explain how the condensed formula is superior to the molecular formula.** — A **condensed formula** writes an organic molecule as a sequence of groups, omitting the bonds *within* each group but keeping the connectivity between groups clear. **Ethane CH₃–CH₃**, **ethanol CH₃CH₂OH**, **acetic acid CH₃COOH**. **Superiority:** the molecular formula only counts atoms — for C₂H₆O it cannot say whether the compound is ethanol (CH₃CH₂OH) or dimethyl ether (CH₃OCH₃) — while the condensed formula preserves the **order of attachment**, so it identifies the compound uniquely while remaining far quicker to write than the fully drawn structural formula.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): State the conventions of bond-line (skeletal) formulas.** — (i) Every **line end and vertex represents a carbon atom**; (ii) hydrogens attached to carbon are **not drawn** — they are supplied to complete carbon's valency of 4; (iii) **hetero atoms (O, N, S, halogens) are shown explicitly**, including their hydrogens (O–H, N–H); (iv) double and triple bonds are drawn as two and three parallel lines. Example: a five-point zig-zag is pentane; the same skeleton ending in –OH is pentan-1-ol.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Write the molecular, condensed and bond-line representations of butane.** — **Molecular:** C₄H₁₀. **Condensed:** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃. **Bond-line:** a four-carbon zig-zag (three line segments, no atoms written — each end/vertex is CH₃ or CH₂ with hydrogens implied by valency).

@@keyPoints@@
Molecular formula: atom counts only (C2H6O) — cannot distinguish isomers. Structural formula: every bond drawn explicitly (H-C-C-O-H). Condensed/contracted formula: groups written together, connectivity preserved — ethane CH3CH3, ethanol CH3CH2OH (C2H5OH), acetic acid CH3COOH, dimethyl ether CH3OCH3. Bond-line (skeletal): lines/zig-zags only; every end/vertex = C; H on C implied (valency 4); hetero atoms + their H shown; double/triple bonds as 2/3 lines. Five-point zig-zag = pentane.
@@importantConcepts@@
The molecular formula is the only notation that loses information: C2H6O hides two different compounds, and only the condensed formula CH3CH2OH vs CH3OCH3 restores the identity.
@@examples@@
***One formula, two compounds*** — C2H6O is ethanol and dimethyl ether alike; write CH3CH2OH or CH3OCH3 and the ambiguity disappears — the condensed formula is the cheapest cure for isomer blindness.
@@universalFacts@@
Bond-line notation is the default of research literature because a 30-carbon natural product needs one zig-zag instead of a page of dashes.

@@summary@@
One molecule, three serviceable notations above the molecular formula. The **structural formula** draws every bond — complete but slow. The **condensed (contracted) formula** writes groups in sequence — CH₃CH₂OH, CH₃COOH — preserving connectivity while staying fast, and unlike the molecular formula it distinguishes isomers (ethanol CH₃CH₂OH vs dimethyl ether CH₃OCH₃, both C₂H₆O). The **bond-line (skeletal) formula** reduces the molecule to zig-zag lines: every end and vertex is a carbon, hydrogens on carbon are implied by valency 4, hetero atoms and their hydrogens are shown, and multiple bonds appear as parallel lines. Fluency in converting between the three is the daily literacy of organic chemistry.
