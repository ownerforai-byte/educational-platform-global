## 1. Topic Introduction — Area Becomes a Limit

**The definite integral as area under a curve**: Riemann sums, the limit definition, and the Fundamental Theorem that makes evaluation algebraic.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 The area problem

- Area under y = f(x) ≥ 0 from a to b: partition [a, b] into n subintervals, erect rectangles of height f(xᵢ*), sum the areas — the **Riemann sum** Σf(xᵢ*)Δxᵢ. Refine: **∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx = lim_{n→∞} Σ f(xᵢ*)Δx** — the definite integral.
- **Notation:** ∫ (elongated S for sum), a and b the limits, dx the width differential.

### 2.2 Signed area

- The integral counts area **above the axis positively, below negatively**: ∫ can be zero (cancelling lobes) or negative — "area under the curve" means *signed* area; true geometric area needs |f|.

### 2.3 Properties of the definite integral

- **Linearity:** ∫(af + bg) = a∫f + b∫g. **Additivity:** ∫ₐᵇ + ∫ᵦᶜ = ∫ₐᶜ. **Reversal:** ∫ᵇₐ = −∫ₐᵇ. **Same limits:** 0. **Domination:** f ≤ g ⟹ ∫f ≤ ∫g. **Estimate:** m(b−a) ≤ ∫ ≤ M(b−a).

### 2.4 The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus

- **Part 1:** if f is continuous, F(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t)dt satisfies **F′(x) = f(x)** — differentiation undoes integration.
- **Part 2 (evaluation theorem):** ∫ₐᵇ f(x)dx = **F(b) − F(a)** for any anti-derivative F — the bridge from limit to algebra. Constants cancel: any anti-derivative works.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why the limit definition matters

- The Riemann limit is the *definition* — it makes "area under a curve" precise for curved boundaries where geometry fails, and it is the license for every property (linearity, additivity) proved from sums.

### 4.2 FTC as the dictionary

- Part 1: the accumulation function ∫ₐˣ f(t)dt is an anti-derivative of f — integration builds derivatives. Part 2: evaluation needs only one anti-derivative — the constant C cancels, which is why definite integrals need no +C.

### 4.3 The definite-integral workflow

- Find any anti-derivative F (indefinite-integral skills), compute F(b) − F(a), done. The upper-minus-lower order matters: reversing limits flips the sign.

## 6. Common Confusions

**No +C in definite integrals:** the evaluation theorem subtracts — constants cancel by construction.
**Negative integrals are fine:** f < 0 gives ∫ < 0 — signed area; geometric area needs ∫|f|.
**Limits order:** ∫ᵇₐ = −∫ₐᵇ — flipping the limits flips the sign.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): Evaluate ∫₀¹ x² dx. (2m)** — **Anti-derivative:** F(x) = x³/3. **FTC Part 2:** ∫₀¹ x²dx = F(1) − F(0) = 1/3 − 0 = **1/3**. (Geometric check: the area under the parabola from 0 to 1 is a third of the unit square — Archimedes knew this by exhaustion.)
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Compute ∫₀^π sin x dx and interpret.** — **Anti-derivative:** −cos x. **Evaluate:** [−cos x]₀^π = (−cos π) − (−cos 0) = 1 + 1 = **2**. **Interpretation:** the sine hump above the axis from 0 to π has signed area 2 — the full period ∫₀^{2π} = 0 because the two humps cancel (signed area).
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Show from the definition that ∫ₐᵇ c dx = c(b − a).** — Partition [a, b]: every rectangle has height c and width Δxᵢ; the Riemann sum = Σc·Δxᵢ = c·ΣΔxᵢ = c(b − a) exactly — for **every** n, so the limit is c(b − a) ∎. Constant functions are the case where the sum never changes: no refinement needed.

## 8. Deep Conceptual — Symmetry Halves the Work

- **Odd functions:** ∫₋ₐᵃ f dx = 0 when f is odd (f(−x) = −f(x)) — the two halves cancel exactly (sin x over [−π/2, π/2] gives 0). **Even functions:** ∫₋ₐᵃ f dx = 2∫₀ᵃ f dx — compute one half, double it (x² over [−1, 1] gives 2/3).
- **Why this is deep:** symmetry is *structural* information that survives before any anti-derivative is found — an odd integrand's definite integral is zero even when its anti-derivative is uncomputable. The definite integral reads the function's shape, not just its formula.
- **Shifted symmetry:** ∫₀^π sin x dx = 2 uses symmetry about x = π/2 (the hump's two halves are mirror images) — recognising the axis of symmetry is the skill, and it applies to any integrand symmetric about its interval's midpoint.
%%%
## 9. Deep Conceptual — The Accumulation Function

- **F(x) = ∫ₐˣ f(t)dt** is a *function* — the running total of area from the anchor a. FTC Part 1 says F′ = f: **the rate of accumulation is the density**. Velocity integrates to position; marginal cost integrates to total cost; the density of rain integrates to depth.
- **The lower limit is a choice of zero:** changing a shifts F by a constant — anti-derivatives differ by constants, and the anchor is where the constant is spent. Initial conditions (starting position, starting balance) choose the anchor.
- **Part 1 as existence theorem:** every continuous function *has* an anti-derivative — namely its own accumulation function. This is non-constructive in the same spirit as the IVT: continuity guarantees the object exists even when no formula closes it.

## 10. Deep Conceptual — Riemann Sums Before and After the FTC

- **Before FTC (definition):** ∫₀³ x²dx via right-endpoint sums: Δx = 3/n, xᵢ = 3i/n, sum = Σ(9i²/n²)(3/n) = 27/n³·Σi² = 27·n(n+1)(2n+1)/(6n³) → 27·2/6 = **9** — the limit machinery in full, with Σi² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 doing the heavy lifting.
- **After FTC (evaluation):** the same integral is [x³/3]₀³ = 9 — one line. The FTC *compressed* the entire Riemann-limit computation into an anti-derivative lookup; that compression is the Fundamental Theorem's meaning.
- **When to use which:** Riemann sums compute integrals with no known anti-derivative (x², sin x/x numerically) and *prove* the FTC itself; the FTC evaluates everything with a table entry. Knowing both routes is knowing why the shortcut is true.

**NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): Evaluate ∫₀¹ x² dx. (2m)** — **Anti-derivative:** F(x) = x³/3 (power rule reversed). **FTC Part 2 (evaluation theorem):** ∫₀¹ x²dx = [x³/3]₀¹ = F(1) − F(0) = **1/3 − 0 = 1/3**. **No +C:** any constant in F cancels in the subtraction — definite integrals are numbers, not families. **Geometric reading:** the region under y = x² from 0 to 1 fills exactly one-third of the unit square — the fact Archimedes established by exhaustion two millennia before limits had a name.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Compute ∫₀^π sin x dx and interpret the result as signed area.** — **Anti-derivative:** ∫sin x dx = −cos x. **Evaluate:** ∫₀^π sin x dx = [−cos x]₀^π = (−cos π) − (−cos 0) = (−(−1)) − (−1) = 1 + 1 = **2**. **Interpretation:** the single sine hump between 0 and π lies entirely **above** the axis, so its signed area is positive — exactly 2 square units. **Contrast:** over a full period, ∫₀^{2π} sin x dx = [−cos x]₀^{2π} = (−1) − (−1) = **0** — the upper hump (+2) and the lower trough (−2) cancel: the definite integral measures **signed** area, and symmetric oscillation integrates to nothing.

**NEB-style (3m): Using the definition of the definite integral as a limit of Riemann sums, show that ∫ₐᵇ c dx = c(b − a) for any constant c.** — **Setup:** partition [a, b] into n subintervals with widths Δxᵢ and any sample points xᵢ*. **Riemann sum:** since f(x) = c for every x, every rectangle has height c: Σ f(xᵢ*)Δxᵢ = Σ c·Δxᵢ = **c·ΣΔxᵢ = c(b − a)** — the widths telescope to the full interval length. **Limit:** the sum equals c(b − a) for *every* partition and every n — the sequence is constant, so its limit is c(b − a) ∎. **Reading:** constant functions are the degenerate case where refinement changes nothing; the formula ∫ₐᵇ c dx = c(b − a) is also the m(b − a) ≤ ∫ ≤ M(b − a) estimate with m = M = c, binding tight.

**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate ∫₀² |x − 1| dx and explain why the absolute value demands a split.** — **The obstacle:** |x − 1| is not a single formula — it equals 1 − x for x < 1 and x − 1 for x ≥ 1; its anti-derivative changes formula at the zero crossing. **Split at the crossing:** ∫₀² |x−1|dx = ∫₀¹ (1 − x)dx + ∫₁² (x − 1)dx. **First piece:** [x − x²/2]₀¹ = (1 − 1/2) − 0 = **1/2**. **Second piece:** [x²/2 − x]₁² = (2 − 2) − (1/2 − 1) = **1/2**. **Total: 1.** **Geometric check:** the two triangles (base 1, height 1 each) have total area 2·(1/2) = 1 ✓. **Deep points:** (1) the definite integral of |f| is the **true geometric area** — without the absolute value, ∫₀² (x−1)dx = [x²/2 − x]₀² = 0, the signed cancellation of two equal lobes; the absolute value forbids the cancellation by flipping the lower lobe positive; (2) **split at every zero crossing** of the inside function — the split points are where the integrand's formula changes, exactly as piecewise continuity seams demanded in the limits unit.

**NEB-style (6m): Compute ∫₀³ x² dx (i) via a Riemann sum limit and (ii) via the FTC, and reconcile the two.** — **(i) Riemann sum (right endpoints):** partition [0, 3] into n equal parts: Δx = 3/n, xᵢ = 3i/n. Sum = Σᵢ f(xᵢ)Δx = Σ (3i/n)²·(3/n) = (27/n³)Σi². **The sum formula:** Σi² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6, so the sum = 27·n(n+1)(2n+1)/(6n³) = (27/6)(1 + 1/n)(2 + 1/n) → (9/2)(1)(2) = **9** as n → ∞. **(ii) FTC:** anti-derivative x³/3 ⟹ ∫₀³ x²dx = [x³/3]₀³ = 27/3 − 0 = **9** ✓. **Reconciliation:** the two routes agree — and that agreement *is* the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus: the limit of sums (Part 2's left side) equals the anti-derivative difference (right side). The Riemann route needed Σi², a genuine number-theoretic formula; the FTC route needed only the power rule reversed. **Deep point:** the FTC is the theorem that makes this reconciliation universal — every integral with a table entry inherits the compression that x² just demonstrated.

**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate ∫₋₁¹ x³ dx and ∫₋₁¹ x² dx, stating the symmetry property each uses.** — **First integral:** f(x) = x³ is **odd** (f(−x) = −x³ = −f(x)). **Property:** for odd f, ∫₋ₐᵃ f dx = 0 — the sub-integral over [−a, 0] is the exact negative of the one over [0, a] (substitute x = −t: ∫₋ₐ⁰ f = ∫ₐ⁰ f(−t)(−dt) = −∫₀ᵃ f). **Result: ∫₋₁¹ x³dx = 0** — the cubic's negative lobe cancels its positive lobe exactly. **Second integral:** g(x) = x² is **even** (g(−x) = g(x)). **Property:** for even g, ∫₋ₐᵃ g dx = 2∫₀ᵃ g dx. **Result:** 2∫₀¹ x²dx = 2·(1/3) = **2/3**. **Deep point:** symmetry converts evaluation into recognition — no anti-derivative was needed for the odd case, and the even case needed only half the interval. These are the first entries of a general principle: *structure in the integrand is as valuable as computation*, and checking parity before integrating is free marks.

**NEB-style (4m): Using FTC Part 1, find d/dx of F(x) = ∫₁ˣ (t + sin t) dt and G(x) = ∫₀^{x²} e^{−t²} dt.** — **First case (direct Part 1):** the upper limit is x itself ⟹ **F′(x) = x + sin x** — the integrand evaluated at the upper limit. **Second case (Part 1 + chain rule):** the upper limit is x², an inner function — write G(x) = H(u) with H(u) = ∫₀ᵘ e^{−t²}dt and u = x². Then G′ = H′(u)·u′ = e^{−u²}·2x = **2x·e^{−x⁴}**. **Deep points:** (1) FTC Part 1 says the accumulation function's derivative *is* the integrand — differentiation and definite integration are inverse operations at the function level; (2) the composite upper limit engages the chain rule exactly as any composite differentiation would — the accumulation function is a function like any other, and the table + chain rule apply to it unchanged.
@@summary@@
The **definite integral** converts area into analysis: partition, erect rectangles, sum — and the **Riemann sum** Σf(xᵢ*)Δxᵢ, refined without end, *defines* ∫ₐᵇ f dx. The integral measures **signed** area (above +, below −): ∫₀^π sin x = 2 (the hump), while the full period gives 0 (cancelling lobes). Its **properties** — linearity, additivity, reversal (∫ᵇₐ = −∫ₐᵇ), domination, the m(b−a)–M(b−a) bounds — all flow from sum arithmetic. The **Fundamental Theorem of Calculus** is the bridge: Part 1 makes the accumulation function ∫ₐˣ f(t)dt an anti-derivative (F′ = f); **Part 2** evaluates — ∫ₐᵇ f dx = F(b) − F(a) for any anti-derivative, the constant cancelling by subtraction. Workflow: anti-derivative, bracket, upper minus lower (NEB 2022: ∫₀¹x²dx = 1/3 — Archimedes' one-third of the square). No +C, mind the limit order, read the sign: area made algebraic.
