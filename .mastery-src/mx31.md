## 1. Topic Introduction — The Limit Hall of Fame

**Standard limits** are the pre-computed results every other limit reduces to: lim sin x/x = 1, lim (1+x)^{1/x} = e, lim (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = naⁿ⁻¹, and their kin. Memorise the statements; understand the reductions.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 The trigonometric star

- **lim_{x→0} sin x/x = 1** (x in radians — the radian is what makes it true).
- **Companion:** lim_{x→0} tan x/x = 1; lim_{x→0} (1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2.

### 2.2 The exponential star

- **lim_{x→0} (1 + x)^{1/x} = e** and the twin **lim_{x→∞} (1 + 1/x)^x = e**.
- **lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x = 1** — the exponential's rate at 0, the seed of its derivative.
- **lim_{x→0} (aˣ − 1)/x = ln a** (general base).
- **lim_{x→0} ln(1 + x)/x = 1.**

### 2.3 The algebraic star

- **lim_{x→a} (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹** — valid for all rational n; the factorisation theorem wearing a limit.
- **lim_{x→0} (1 + x)ᵏ... ((1+x)ⁿ − 1)/x = n** — the binomial baby case.

### 2.4 Reduction discipline

- Any 0/0 candidate: try to rewrite into a standard form via substitution (u = g(x) → 0), algebraic identity, or multiplication by 1. The standard limits are the *targets* of every transformation.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why radians

- sin x/x → 1 is a *geometry* statement: arc length ≈ chord length for tiny angles — true only when x measures arc in radius units. Degrees smuggle in a factor π/180.

### 4.2 The (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) theorem as a bridge

- For integer n it is factoring; for general rational n it is the derivative of xⁿ at a in disguise — the standard limit that calculus will generalise.

### 4.3 e's double definition

- (1 + x)^{1/x} at 0 and (1 + 1/x)^x at ∞ are the same limit seen from the two ends of u = 1/x — compound interest's continuous limit.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Degrees break sin x/x:** always check the angle unit; convert degrees to radians first.
%%%
**(1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2, not 1:** the half-angle identity sin²(x/2) = (1 − cos x)/2 supplies the factor.
%%%
**(aˣ − 1)/x → ln a:** specialising a = e gives 1; the general base keeps the ln a factor.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x equals: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) e (d) ∞ (1m)** — This is the **standard limit**: lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x = **1 → option (b)**. It is the definition of eˣ's instantaneous rate at 0 — the fact that makes (eˣ)′ = eˣ true.
%%%
**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate (i) lim_{x→0} (sin x − x cos x)/x³, (ii) lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. (4m)** — **(i)** Write (sin x − x cos x)/x³ = [sin x/x − cos x]/x². Now sin x/x → 1 and cos x → 1, giving 0/0 again — refine: sin x − x cos x = x[sin x/x − cos x]; using the standard-limit toolkit (sin x = x − x³/6 + ⋯, cos x = 1 − x²/2 + ⋯): sin x − x cos x = (x − x³/6) − x(1 − x²/2) = x − x³/6 − x + x³/2 = **x³/3**. **(i) = 1/3.** **(ii)** x − sin x = x − (x − x³/6) = **x³/6** ⟹ **(ii) = 1/6**. (Series route is the clean one; equivalently apply the standard limits twice with the compound-angle identity sin x − x cos x = x²·(sin x/x)′-type reductions.)
%%%
**NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (√(7+x) − 3)/(x − 2). (3m)** — 0/0 ⟹ **rationalise:** multiply by (√(7+x) + 3)/(√(7+x) + 3): numerator = (7 + x) − 9 = x − 2, cancelling the denominator (x ≠ 2): 1/(√(7+x) + 3). Limit: 1/(√9 + 3) = **1/6**. (Standard-limit thinking: the conjugate reduction is the root version of the (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) theorem with n = 1/2, a = 2: (1/2)·2^{−1/2} = 1/(2√2)... here the shifted form gives 1/6 — the rationalisation is the honest working.)

**NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x equals: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) e (d) ∞ (1m)** — **Recognition:** this is the exponential standard limit: **lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x = 1 — option (b)**. **Why it is true:** eˣ = 1 + x + x²/2 + ⋯, so eˣ − 1 = x + x²/2 + ⋯ = x(1 + x/2 + ⋯), and dividing by x leaves 1 + x/2 + ⋯ → 1. **Significance:** this limit *is* the statement that eˣ has instantaneous rate 1 at x = 0 — the seed of (eˣ)′ = eˣ, the defining perk of the natural base. (For general base a, (aˣ − 1)/x → ln a; a = e gives ln e = 1.)
%%%
**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate (i) lim_{x→0} (sin x − x cos x)/x³ and (ii) lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. (4m)** — **Series route (small-x expansions, radians):** sin x = x − x³/6 + O(x⁵) and cos x = 1 − x²/2 + O(x⁴). **(i):** sin x − x cos x = (x − x³/6) − x(1 − x²/2) = x − x³/6 − x + x³/2 = x³(1/2 − 1/6) = **x³/3** ⟹ the limit is **1/3**. **(ii):** x − sin x = x − (x − x³/6) = **x³/6** ⟹ the limit is **1/6**. **Standard-limit cross-check for (ii):** x − sin x = x(1 − sin x/x); with sin x/x = 1 − x²/6 + ⋯ (from the half-angle identity 1 − sin x/x... equivalently the cubic term), 1 − sin x/x ~ x²/6, so x(1 − sin x/x)/x³ → 1/6 ✓. Both are higher-order refinements of sin x/x → 1: the leading x-terms cancel, exposing the cubic layer.
%%%
**NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (√(7+x) − 3)/(x − 2). (3m)** — **Substitution:** (√9 − 3)/0 = **0/0** ⟹ transform. **Rationalise:** multiply by the conjugate (√(7+x) + 3)/(√(7+x) + 3): numerator = (7 + x) − 9 = **x − 2**, which cancels the denominator (licensed for x ≠ 2): the expression becomes **1/(√(7+x) + 3)**. **Substitute:** 1/(√9 + 3) = **1/6**. **Connection:** this is the (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) standard limit with a fractional power — equivalently the derivative of √(7+x) at x = 2, which the chain rule will later confirm as 1/(2√9) = 1/6 ✓. The conjugate is the honest algebraic route before calculus tools are available.
@@summary@@
**Standard limits** are the pre-proved stars every 0/0 reduces to. The **trigonometric shelf** (radians mandatory): sin x/x → 1, tan x/x → 1, (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2. The **exponential shelf**: (eˣ − 1)/x → 1 (NEB 2022's MCQ — the seed of (eˣ)′ = eˣ), (aˣ − 1)/x → ln a, ln(1+x)/x → 1, and the double definition of **e**: (1+x)^{1/x} → 0⁺... → e as x → 0 and (1 + 1/x)^x → e as x → ∞. The **algebraic star**: (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ — factoring for integers, a derivative in disguise for fractional powers (√(7+x) − 3 over x − 2 rationalises to 1/6, NEB 2021). Reduction discipline: substitute, factor, multiply by 1 — steer the unknown into a star. When cancellations run deep, series finish the job: x − sin x ~ x³/6 and sin x − x cos x ~ x³/3 (NEB 2024's paired 1/6 and 1/3). Memorise the shelf; master the steering.
