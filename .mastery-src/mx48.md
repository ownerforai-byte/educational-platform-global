## 1. Topic Introduction — The Differentiation Toolkit

**Rules of differentiation** — product, quotient, chain — plus **parametric, implicit and higher-order derivatives**: the machinery that makes every function differentiable in practice.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Product and quotient rules

- **Product:** (fg)′ = f′g + fg′ (proved by adding and subtracting f(x+h)g(x)). **Quotient:** (f/g)′ = (f′g − fg′)/g² (g ≠ 0).
- **Memory:** product keeps order symmetry; quotient is "low d-high minus high d-low over low squared".

### 2.2 The chain rule

- **Theorem:** if g is differentiable at x and f differentiable at g(x), then (f∘g)′(x) = f′(g(x))·g′(x) — dy/dx = dy/du · du/dx.
- The engine of composite differentiation: outer table, inner derivative, multiply.

### 2.3 Parametric differentiation

- **x = x(t), y = y(t):** dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) (dx/dt ≠ 0); **second derivative:** d²y/dx² = d/dx(dy/dx) = [d/dt(dy/dx)]/(dx/dt) — divide by dx/dt again, not by itself.

### 2.4 Implicit differentiation

- For F(x, y) = 0 defining y(x): differentiate both sides in x, treating y as y(x) (chain rule inserts dy/dx), then solve for dy/dx.
- **Logarithmic differentiation** (the power-tool): y = u^v ⟹ ln y = v ln u ⟹ y′/y = v′ ln u + v·u′/u ⟹ y′ = y(v′ ln u + vu′/u) — handles x^x and (sin x)^{cos x}.

### 2.5 Higher-order derivatives

- **f′′, f′′′, …, f⁽ⁿ⁾:** differentiate repeatedly. **Notation:** d²y/dx², Dⁿy. **Leibniz (statement):** (fg)⁽ⁿ⁾ = Σ C(n,k) f⁽ⁿ⁻ᵏ⁾g⁽ᵏ⁾ — the product rule's binomial generalisation.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why the chain rule is the master rule

- Product and quotient rules are special cases of treating expressions as composites; every "hard" derivative is a chain rule in disguise. Master dy/du · du/dx and the table becomes universal.

### 4.2 Logarithmic differentiation's domain

- Use when y = u(x)^{v(x)} with **both base and exponent variable** (xˣ, (sin x)^{cos x}, x^{1/x}) — neither the power rule (variable base) nor the exponential rule (variable exponent) applies alone.

### 4.3 The parametric second-derivative trap

- d²y/dx² ≠ (d²y/dt²)/(d²x/dt²). The correct route: differentiate dy/dx *with respect to t*, then divide by dx/dt — the operator d/dx = (1/(dx/dt))·d/dt applied twice.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Chain rule amnesia:** ln(sin x) is cot x only because the inner derivative cos x survives — dropping u′ is the classic error.
%%%
**Parametric second derivative:** divide by dx/dt again after differentiating dy/dx in t — not the ratio of second t-derivatives.
%%%
**x^x is not x·x^{x−1}:** the power rule needs constant exponent; xˣ demands logarithmic differentiation.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): Find the derivative of y = sin x · cos x using the product rule. (3m)** — **Product rule:** y′ = (sin x)′·cos x + sin x·(cos x)′ = cos x·cos x + sin x·(−sin x) = cos²x − sin²x = **cos 2x**. (Cross-check: y = ½ sin 2x ⟹ y′ = ½·2cos 2x = cos 2x ✓.)
%%%
**NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): Differentiate (i) y = xˣ, (ii) y = sin²x·eˣ, (iii) y = (1+x)^{1/x}. (3m)** — **(i) Logarithmic:** ln y = x ln x ⟹ y′/y = ln x + 1 ⟹ **y′ = xˣ(ln x + 1)**. **(ii) Product + chain:** y′ = (2 sin x cos x)·eˣ + sin²x·eˣ = **eˣ(sin 2x + sin²x)**. **(iii) Logarithmic:** ln y = (1/x)ln(1+x) ⟹ y′/y = [x/(1+x) − ln(1+x)]/x² ⟹ **y′ = (1+x)^{1/x}·[x/(1+x) − ln(1+x)]/x²**.
%%%
**NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): If y = (sin x)^{cos x}, show that dy/dx = y(cos x·cot x − sin x·log_e(sin x)). (3m)** — **Logarithmic differentiation:** ln y = cos x·ln(sin x). **Differentiate:** y′/y = (−sin x)·ln(sin x) + cos x·(cos x/sin x) = −sin x·ln(sin x) + cos x·cot x. **Multiply by y:** dy/dx = **y(cos x·cot x − sin x·log_e(sin x))** ∎ — exactly the claimed form.

**NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): Find the derivative of y = sin x · cos x using the product rule. (3m)** — **Product rule:** y′ = (sin x)′·cos x + sin x·(cos x)′ = **cos x·cos x + sin x·(−sin x)** = cos²x − sin²x. **Simplify:** by the double-angle identity, cos²x − sin²x = **cos 2x**. **Cross-check:** sin x·cos x = ½·sin 2x, so differentiating directly gives ½·2cos 2x = cos 2x ✓ — the two routes agree, and the product rule has delivered a double angle.
%%%
**NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): Differentiate with respect to x: (i) y = xˣ, (ii) y = sin²x · eˣ, (iii) y = (1+x)^{1/x}. (3m)** — **(i) Logarithmic differentiation** (both base and exponent variable — neither single rule applies): ln y = x ln x ⟹ y′/y = 1·ln x + x·(1/x) = ln x + 1 ⟹ **y′ = xˣ(ln x + 1)**. **(ii) Product + chain:** y′ = [d/dx(sin²x)]·eˣ + sin²x·(eˣ)′ = (2 sin x cos x)·eˣ + sin²x·eˣ = **eˣ(sin 2x + sin²x)** (using 2 sin x cos x = sin 2x). **(iii) Logarithmic:** ln y = (1/x)·ln(1+x); differentiate the product: y′/y = (−1/x²)·ln(1+x) + (1/x)·(1/(1+x)) ⟹ **y′ = (1+x)^{1/x}·[x/(1+x) − ln(1+x)]/x²**. Three different rule-combinations, one page — the toolkit triage in a single question.
%%%
**NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): If y = (sin x)^{cos x}, show that dy/dx = y(cos x · cot x − sin x · log_e(sin x)). (3m)** — **Step 1 — take logs** (u^v with both parts variable): ln y = cos x · ln(sin x). **Step 2 — differentiate both sides in x** (product rule on the right; chain rule inserts y′/y on the left): y′/y = (−sin x)·ln(sin x) + cos x·[cos x/sin x] = **−sin x·ln(sin x) + cos x·cot x**. **Step 3 — solve for y′:** multiply by y: dy/dx = **y(cos x·cot x − sin x·log_e(sin x))** ∎ — matching the required form exactly, with y retained (the target keeps y unexpanded; substituting (sin x)^{cos x} back is optional but the shown form is the mark).
@@summary@@
The **differentiation toolkit** turns the table into universal machinery. **Product rule** (fg)′ = f′g + fg′ (sin x·cos x → cos²x − sin²x = cos 2x, NEB 2023) and **quotient rule** (f′g − fg′)/g² are the workhorses; the **chain rule** dy/dx = dy/du·du/dx is the master — outer table, inner derivative, multiply. **Parametric curves** differentiate as (dy/dt)/(dx/dt), with the second derivative requiring a *second* division by dx/dt (the classic trap). **Implicit equations** differentiate through with y = y(x), then solve linearly for y′. And **logarithmic differentiation** is the power-tool for u(x)^{v(x)} — ln y = v ln u, then y′ = y(v′ ln u + vu′/u): xˣ → xˣ(ln x + 1), (1+x)^{1/x}, and NEB 2023's show-that (sin x)^{cos x} → y(cos x·cot x − sin x·ln(sin x)). **Higher orders** repeat the operation, with **Leibniz** generalising the product rule binomially. Triage the situation, pick the rule, execute.
