## 1. Topic Introduction — The Sharp-Smelling Shield

Ozone (O₃) is oxygen's aggressive allotrope: made by lightning and ozonisers, decomposing back to O₂ with nascent oxygen in hand. This topic covers its occurrence, preparation, bent resonance structure, the starch-iodide test, and its uses — from sterilising water to the stratospheric shield whose depletion is topic 62.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Occurrence

- **Stratosphere:** the ozone layer (~15–35 km altitude) absorbs most solar UV-B/UV-C — life's sunscreen.
- **Trace ground-level:** after thunderstorms (lightning discharge), around electric motors and copiers, in smog (a pollutant there).

### 2.2 Preparation

- **Silent electric discharge ozoniser:** 3O₂ ⇌ 2O₃, ΔH = +142 kJ. Dry, pure O₂ streams through an annular gap between electrodes cooled in dilute H₂SO₄; ~10 % ozone at steady state (higher conversion blocked by O₃'s own decomposition under the discharge).
- Conditions matter: **silent** discharge (no sparking — sparks heat the gas and destroy ozone); dry oxygen (moisture aids decomposition); low temperature favours yield (exothermic product side is disfavoured... actually the forward reaction is endothermic, but O₃ decomposes faster when hot — cooling protects the product).

### 2.3 Structure

- **Bent molecule**, bond angle ≈ 117°, O–O bond length 128 pm (between single 148 and double 121 pm).
- **Resonance:** two equivalent structures with one single and one double bond; the real molecule has both bonds equivalent, bond order 1.5; central O carries +, terminal O's carry − formally.
- **Diamagnetic** — all electrons paired (contrast O₂'s paramagnetism).

### 2.4 Test for ozone

- **Starch–iodide paper:** O₃ oxidises iodide to iodine, which turns starch blue-black: 2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂. (H₂O₂ does the same, so moist litmus/peroxide interference is checked by the turpentine test: ozone decolourises turpentine's violet colour.)
- Ozone also turns **turpentine blue** and liberates iodine from iodides — the classic pair of confirmatory tests.

### 2.5 Uses

- **Water sterilisation** (no chlorinated taste or residue); **bleaching** delicate fabrics, oils, waxes, ivory, flour; **air purification** in closed spaces; **oxidant** in organic synthesis (ozonolysis locating C=C positions); **medical** disinfection of operating rooms; stratospheric UV shield.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why the ozoniser yields only ~10 % O₃

- The discharge that builds O₃ also decomposes it; a steady state (~10 % by volume) is reached where formation rate equals decomposition rate. Cooling the electrodes and using dry O₂ pushes the balance toward more O₃.

### 4.2 The bond-angle story

- Central oxygen is sp²-hybridised: two sigma bonds plus one lone pair → bent, ~117° (slightly compressed from 120° by the lone pair). The delocalised π system across three atoms gives the 1.5 bond order and the 128 pm compromise bond length.

### 4.3 Ozone as an oxidant — equations bank

- 2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂ (test)
- PbS + 4O₃ → PbSO₄ + 4O₂ (oxidises sulphide to sulphate)
- 2Hg + O₃ → Hg₂O + O₂ (tailings mirror loses lustre — old test)
- K₂MnO₄ + O₃ + H₂O → 2KMnO₄ + 2KOH + O₃? (careful — manganate to permanganate: 2K₂MnO₄ + H₂O + O₃ → 2KMnO₄ + 2KOH + O₂)

## 6. Common Confusions

**Ozone's bonds are equal:** drawing one single and one double O–O bond as literal is wrong — resonance makes both bonds identical at 128 pm, order 1.5.
%%%
**The starch-iodide test is not ozone-exclusive:** H₂O₂ also liberates iodine; the **turpentine decolourisation** is the discriminating test.
%%%
**Ozone is good up high, bad down low:** stratospheric O₃ protects; ground-level O₃ is a respiratory irritant and smog component — same molecule, opposite reputations.

## 7. Exam Application

**Verified NEB 2022 (2m): Write the structure and properties of ozone (O₃).** — **Structure:** ozone is a bent (angular) molecule with the central oxygen sp²-hybridised and one lone pair, giving an O–O–O bond angle of about 117°. Its bonding is best described by **resonance** between two equivalent structures, so the two O–O bonds are identical, with length 128 pm (intermediate between a single bond, 148 pm, and a double bond, 121 pm) and bond order 1.5; the molecule is diamagnetic. **Properties:** ozone is a pale blue gas with a characteristic pungent smell, thermally unstable and decomposing to oxygen (2O₃ → 3O₂), a very powerful oxidising agent — it liberates nascent oxygen (O₃ → O₂ + [O]) and oxidises iodide to iodine (2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂, the starch-iodide test), bleaches dyes, sterilises water, and absorbs harmful UV radiation in the stratosphere.

**NEB-style (2m): How is ozone prepared in the laboratory? Why is only about 10 % ozone obtained?** — Ozone is prepared by passing dry, pure oxygen through a **Siemen's ozoniser** — silent (corona) electric discharge between two glass electrodes cooled in dilute sulphuric acid: 3O₂ ⇌ 2O₃ (ΔH = +142 kJ). Only about **10 %** ozone is obtained because the same electric discharge that forms ozone also decomposes it back to oxygen; a steady state is reached where the rates of formation and decomposition are equal. The yield is improved by keeping the electrodes cool and the oxygen dry, but sparking (which heats the gas) must be avoided.
%%%
**NEB-style (2m): Describe the starch-iodide test for ozone with equation.** — Bring starch–iodide paper (potassium iodide + starch) into contact with the suspected ozone. Ozone oxidises iodide ions to iodine, which is immediately trapped by starch as the blue-black complex: **2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂**. A blue-black colouration confirms an oxidant; to confirm it is specifically ozone (since H₂O₂ gives the same test), shake the gas with turpentine — ozone decolourises the violet turpentine solution while H₂O₂ does not.

**Verified NEB 2022 (2m): Write the structure and properties of ozone (O₃).** — **Structure:** the ozone molecule is **bent (angular)** — the central oxygen atom is sp²-hybridised and carries one lone pair, compressing the O–O–O angle to about **117°**. Its π bonding is delocalised: the real molecule is a **resonance hybrid** of two equivalent structures (one O–O single and one O–O double bond interchanged), so the two bonds are experimentally identical, **128 pm** long — between a normal single bond (148 pm) and double bond (121 pm) — with bond order 1.5. All electrons are paired: ozone is **diamagnetic**. **Properties:** (1) pale blue gas with a characteristic pungent, fishy smell; (2) thermally unstable — decomposes to oxygen, 2O₃ → 3O₂, especially on heating or with catalysts; (3) a very powerful oxidising agent, readily releasing nascent oxygen (O₃ → O₂ + [O]): it oxidises iodide to iodine (2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂ — the starch-iodide test turns blue-black), converts PbS to PbSO₄, bleaches vegetable dyes and sterilises drinking water without leaving a taste; (4) in the stratosphere it absorbs harmful ultraviolet radiation, forming the ozone shield.

**NEB-style (2m): How is ozone prepared in the laboratory? Why is only about 10 % ozone obtained?** — Ozone is prepared by passing **dry, pure oxygen** through a **Siemen's ozoniser**, which consists of two coaxial glass tubes with tin-foil electrodes; a **silent (corona) electric discharge** is applied while the apparatus is cooled in dilute sulphuric acid. The discharge converts oxygen to ozone: **3O₂ ⇌ 2O₃, ΔH = +142 kJ**. Only about **10 %** ozone is obtained because the same discharge that creates ozone also decomposes it back to oxygen — a dynamic steady state is reached in which the rate of formation equals the rate of decomposition. The yield improves with cooling and dry oxygen; **sparking must be avoided** because the local heating destroys ozone faster than it forms.

**NEB-style (2m): Describe the starch-iodide test for ozone, giving the equation. How would you distinguish ozone from hydrogen peroxide, which gives the same test?** — Take a strip of **starch–iodide paper** (potassium iodide soaked in starch solution) and expose it to the gas. Ozone oxidises the iodide ions to iodine, and the liberated iodine is immediately held by the starch as its characteristic **blue-black** adsorption complex: **2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂**. A positive test shows an oxidising gas is present. Since hydrogen peroxide vapour also liberates iodine and passes this test, the **distinguishing test** is to shake the gas with **turpentine**: ozone decolourises the violet-coloured turpentine solution (oxidising it), whereas hydrogen peroxide has no effect on it.
@@summary@@
Ozone is oxygen with an edge. It occurs as the **stratospheric shield** (15–35 km up, swallowing UV-B/UV-C) and as lightning's after-scent at ground level; the laboratory makes it the same way the sky does — **silent electric discharge** through dry O₂ in a cooled Siemen's ozoniser, 3O₂ ⇌ 2O₃ (+142 kJ), where formation and decomposition fight to a ~10 % steady state. The molecule is **bent at 117°** (sp² central oxygen, one lone pair), a **resonance hybrid** whose two bonds are identical at **128 pm**, bond order 1.5, and **diamagnetic** — all contrasts with its linear, paramagnetic, double-bonded sibling. Its power is one weak bond: O₃ → O₂ + [O], and the nascent oxygen does everything — blue-blackening starch–iodide paper (2KI + H₂O + O₃ → 2KOH + I₂ + O₂), oxidising PbS to PbSO₄, bleaching dyes, sterilising water without chlorine taste — with **turpentine decolourisation** separating ozone from H₂O₂, which shares the iodide test. Uses span water treatment, delicate bleaching, air purification, and ozonolysis that locates double bonds in organic synthesis; overhead, the same molecule remains the only thing standing between sunlight's UV and our skin.
