## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to the Rules of Differentiation

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to the rules of differentiation: product, quotient, chain, and the log-differentiation power tool in one page. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the rules, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Product rule:** (fg)′ = f′g + fg′ — each factor differentiated while the other stands.
- **Quotient rule:** (f/g)′ = (f′g − fg′)/g² — the product rule with a minus and a square.
- **Chain rule:** (f∘g)′ = f′(g(x))·g′(x), i.e. dy/dx = dy/du·du/dx — outer table evaluated at u, times the inner derivative.
- **Logarithmic differentiation:** for y = u(x)^{v(x)}: ln y = v ln u ⟹ y′ = y(v′ ln u + v·u′/u).
- **Higher orders:** repeat the operation; Leibniz: (fg)⁽ⁿ⁾ = Σ C(n,k) f⁽ⁿ⁻ᵏ⁾g⁽ᵏ⁾.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — The Rules as Rate Algebra

- The product rule is **rate composition for products**: a small change in f contributes f′·g, a small change in g contributes f·g′, and the cross-term dies in the limit. Leibniz's binomial coefficients are the same algebra iterated — the operator D behaves like a number in (D + A)ⁿ = Σ C(n,k) Dⁿ⁻ᵏAᵏ.
- **The chain rule is the master:** every composite answer has the shape (outer table entry at u) × u′. Writing both factors explicitly is the discipline that makes dropped-inner-derivative errors impossible.
- **Log-differentiation linearises:** ln converts powers to products and products to sums — the hardest differentiation problems become product-rule problems after one logarithm, with the universal shape y′ = y·(d/dx ln y).

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Choosing the Rule Is the Decision Procedure

- **Triage:** a sum ⟹ term by term; a product of unlike types ⟹ product rule; a quotient ⟹ quotient rule; a composite ⟹ chain rule; a variable base **and** exponent ⟹ logs first.
- **The power-tool's trigger:** u(x)^{v(x)} — both base and exponent varying — defeats the power rule (needs constant exponent) and the exponential rule (needs constant base) individually; ln y = v ln u is the only bridge.
- **Rule stacking:** complicated functions are nestings of the four structures; differentiate outside-in, one rule per layer, and label each layer's rule in the working.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — product rule with a trig cross-term:** sin x·cos x → cos 2x patterns.
- **Type B — quotient rule:** (u/v) with the minus sign audited.
- **Type C — chain stacks:** √(1 + sin 2x)-type nestings.
- **Type D — log differentiation:** xˣ, (sin x)^{cos x}, (1+x)^{1/x}.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"The derivative of a product is the product of derivatives":** the classic error — the product rule has two terms. **"Dropping the inner derivative":** the chain rule's second factor is not optional.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Differentiate y = x²·sin x and simplify the answer.** — **Product rule:** y′ = (x²)′·sin x + x²·(sin x)′ = 2x·sin x + x²·cos x = **x(2 sin x + x cos x)**. **Structure check:** the answer is a sum of two products — each term differentiated one factor while the other stood, exactly the rule's anatomy. **Deep point:** the factored form is the exam-friendly presentation; the rule's two-term shape is the content, and factoring common pieces (x) is presentation, not new mathematics.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Differentiate y = (x² + 1)/(x − 1) and find the stationary points.** — **Quotient rule:** y′ = [(2x)(x − 1) − (x² + 1)(1)]/(x − 1)² = [2x² − 2x − x² − 1]/(x − 1)² = **(x² − 2x − 1)/(x − 1)²**. **Stationary points:** y′ = 0 ⟹ x² − 2x − 1 = 0 ⟹ x = 1 ± √2 (both ≠ 1, so both legal). **Audit:** the minus sign placed the −2x correctly — the classic quotient-rule error is flipping it; the cross-check is that y′(0) = −1/1 = −1, and indeed y is decreasing at 0 (from y(−1) = −1 to y(0.5) = −5). **Deep point:** the stationary-point hunt is the quotient rule's natural sequel — differentiate, set to zero, solve; the rule's minus sign is where every error in the chain lives.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Differentiate y = √(1 + sin 2x) and show the answer simplifies elegantly.** — **Outer (chain):** (1 + sin 2x)^{1/2} ⟹ (1/2)(1 + sin 2x)^{−1/2}·(inner). **Inner (chain again):** (sin 2x)′ = cos 2x·2 = 2cos 2x. **Assemble:** y′ = cos 2x/√(1 + sin 2x). **Simplify:** cos 2x = cos²x − sin²x; 1 + sin 2x = (sin x + cos x)² ⟹ y′ = (cos x − sin x)(cos x + sin x)/|sin x + cos x| — on intervals where sin x + cos x > 0: y′ = **cos x − sin x**. **Deep point:** two chain rules stacked, then an algebraic miracle — recognising 1 + sin 2x as a perfect square collapsed the radical; differentiation finds the rate, algebra makes it readable.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Differentiate y = (sin x)^{cos x} for 0 < x < π/2 using logarithmic differentiation.** — **Log bridge:** ln y = cos x·ln(sin x). **Differentiate both sides:** y′/y = (−sin x)·ln(sin x) + cos x·(cos x/sin x). **Solve:** y′ = y[sin x·(cot²x − ln(sin x))] = **(sin x)^{cos x}·[cos²x/sin x − sin x·ln(sin x)]**. **Verification at x = π/4:** sin = cos = 1/√2 ⟹ bracket = (1/2)/(1/√2) − (1/√2)·ln(1/√2) = √2/2 + (ln 2)/(2√2) > 0 ⟹ y increasing at π/4 — plausible since the base is near 1 and shrinking the exponent toward 0 raises the value. **Deep point:** the log bridge converted a variable-power into a product rule problem; the answer's shape y·(stuff) is universal for u^v — the function itself times the log-derivative, exactly as xˣ yields xˣ(1 + ln x).
@@summary@@
The differentiation rules are rate algebra: products add two contributions, quotients subtract and divide, chains multiply outer-at-u by inner, and logarithms linearise the variable-power forms that defeat every other rule. Triage names the outermost structure, one rule applies per layer, and labels in the working make the algebra auditable. The chain rule is the master — product, quotient and inverse rules all follow from it — and log differentiation is its bridge to u^v.
