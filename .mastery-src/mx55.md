## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to the Discontinuity Taxonomy

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to the types of discontinuity: the decision tree that sorts every break into removable, jump, infinite, or oscillatory in one page. The parent canonical carries the full taxonomy with graphs; this companion mirrors the classification, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **The two questions:** (1) do the one-sided limits exist and agree? (2) does f(a) match the merged limit?
- **Removable:** sides agree (limit L exists) but f(a) is missing or ≠ L — hole or detached dot; **repairable** by f(a) = L.
- **Jump:** sides exist, finite, **unequal** — vertical gap of size |RHL − LHL|; non-removable.
- **Infinite:** at least one side blows up — vertical wall; odd-power zeros split the sides, even-power zeros unify them; non-removable.
- **Oscillatory:** a side has no limit (sin(1/x)-type folding); non-removable.
- **Rational triage:** after factoring, cancelled roots are holes, surviving denominator roots are walls.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — The Taxonomy Is a Decision Tree, Not a List

- Two binary questions — sides agree? value matches? — generate all four types. Computing in the right order (sides first, value second) makes classification mechanical: no agreement ⟹ jump/infinite/oscillatory by the sides' behaviour; agreement ⟹ removable or continuous by the value.
- **The repair test is the deepest cut:** "can redefining finitely many point-values make f continuous?" Removable: yes (the sickness is at the point). Jump/infinite/oscillatory: no (the sickness is in the neighbourhood). One question, clean boundary.
- **Jump size is data:** |RHL − LHL| quantifies the break — in piecewise models it is the fee, threshold, or phase change, often the quantity the question actually wants.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Where Each Type Lives in Mathematics

- **Removable breaks are cancellation shadows:** 0/0 forms are shared roots wearing disguises; the limit sees through them, continuity does not.
- **Jumps defeat every transformation tool by design:** the sides are not trying to agree, so factoring, stars, and L'Hôpital are all inapplicable — the verdict, not a computation, is the answer.
- **Walls encode unboundedness:** c/0 forms are loud failures with side-by-side sign readings; they also expire the definite integral's default licence (improper integrals take over).

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — rational audit:** factor fully; cancelled roots ⟹ holes with limits; surviving roots ⟹ walls with side signs.
- **Type B — piecewise seam:** LHL from the left branch, RHL from the right, f(a) from the owning branch; unanimous agreement ⟹ continuous.
- **Type C — assigned values:** f(a) given at a formula's excluded point ⟹ compare with the limit; mismatch ⟹ removable.
- **Type D — jump measurement:** compute both sides, report the gap, state one-sided continuity of the dot.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"Any break is a jump":** jumps need finite *unequal* sides — compute before naming. **"f(a) defined ⟹ no discontinuity":** a wrong value is a removable break, not immunity.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (6m): Classify the discontinuity of f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x − 1) for x ≠ 1 with f(1) = 3, and state the repair.** — **Sides:** (x²−1)/(x−1) = x + 1 for x ≠ 1 ⟹ LHL = **2**, RHL = **2** — sides exist and agree ⟹ limit L = 2. **Value:** f(1) = 3 ≠ 2. **Tree verdict:** agreement yes, match no ⟹ **removable discontinuity** — a detached dot at (1, 3) over the hole at (1, 2). **Repair:** f(1) = 2 ⟹ continuous. **Deep point:** the sides agreed, which immediately ruled out jump, infinite, and oscillatory — one computation pruned three of four branches; that is the decision tree's economy.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find and classify all discontinuities of f(x) = (x + 1)/(x² − 5x + 6).** — **Factor:** (x − 2)(x − 3) ⟹ suspects {2, 3}; numerator at 2 is 3 ≠ 0, at 3 is 4 ≠ 0 ⟹ no cancellation, both are walls. **At x = 2:** denominator ≈ (x−2)(−1); x → 2⁺ ⟹ small negative ⟹ → **−∞**; x → 2⁻ ⟹ → **+∞** (splitting). **At x = 3:** denominator ≈ (x−3)(1); x → 3⁺ ⟹ → **+∞**; x → 3⁻ ⟹ → **−∞** (splitting). **Verdict:** two infinite discontinuities, both non-removable, continuous elsewhere (rational off its zeros). **Deep point:** the numerator's nonzero values at both roots guaranteed walls before any sign work — the parity of each root's power (both odd) predicted splitting walls.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): For f(x) = x for x < 2, f(x) = 7 − 2x for x ≥ 2: classify the behaviour at x = 2 fully.** — **LHL:** lim_{x→2⁻} x = **2**. **RHL:** lim_{x→2⁺} (7 − 2x) = 7 − 4 = **3**. **Value:** f(2) = 3 (≥ owns the point). **Tree:** sides exist, finite, **unequal** ⟹ **jump discontinuity**, size |3 − 2| = 1. **One-sided continuity:** f(2) = 3 = RHL ⟹ right-continuous (filled dot on the right branch's end). **Deep point:** the jump size 1 is the measurable content of the break — and no redefinition of f(2) can merge sides that disagree; the break is structural.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): For f(x) = (x³ − 1)/(x² − 1) for x ≠ 1 with f(1) = k: find k for continuity and classify what happens at x = −1.** — **Factor:** x³ − 1 = (x−1)(x² + x + 1); x² − 1 = (x−1)(x+1) ⟹ f(x) = (x² + x + 1)/(x + 1) for x ≠ 1. **At x = 1:** cancelled root ⟹ limit = 3/2 ⟹ **k = 3/2** for continuity (removable otherwise). **At x = −1:** surviving denominator root; numerator at −1: 1 − 1 + 1 = 1 ≠ 0 ⟹ wall: x → −1⁺ ⟹ denominator small positive ⟹ → **+∞**; x → −1⁻ ⟹ → **−∞** ⟹ **infinite, non-removable**. **Deep point:** one factorisation served two classifications — the cancelled root asked for a value (k = 3/2), the surviving root asked for signs; the audit is always: cancel, then read what remains.
@@summary@@
Every discontinuity answers two questions: do the sides exist and agree, and does the value match? Agreement plus mismatch is removable (repairable by one value); finite disagreement is a jump (size = |RHL − LHL|); blow-up is a wall (odd splits, even unifies); no side limit is oscillatory. Rational functions triage by factorisation — cancelled roots are holes, surviving roots are walls. The repair test is the deep boundary: point-sickness yields to edits, neighbourhood-sickness does not.
