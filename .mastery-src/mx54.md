## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to the Four Families

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to limits of algebraic, trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions: the stars of each family and the steering craft in one page. The parent canonicals carry the full treatment; this companion mirrors the stars, adds the family-by-family deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Algebraic stars:** (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹; (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2; degree rules at ∞.
- **Trigonometric stars (radians only):** sin u/u → 1; tan u/u → 1; (1 − cos u)/u → 0.
- **Exponential/log stars:** (eᵘ − 1)/u → 1; ln(1 + u)/u → 1; aᵘ − 1 ~ u·ln a; (1 + u)^{1/u} → e.
- **Steering:** rewrite A/B as (A·k)/(B·k) to manufacture the star's exact denominator, carrying the constant outside.
- **All stars compose:** with u = g(x) → 0, sin(3x) ~ 3x, ln(1 + 5x) ~ 5x, e^{−2x} − 1 ~ −2x.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Stars Are First-Order Taylor Approximations

- Each star says a complicated function **agrees with a line (or a parabola) near its anchor**: sin u ~ u, eᵘ − 1 ~ u, ln(1+u) ~ u are all "slope = 1 at the anchor" statements; 1 − cos u ~ u²/2 is the parabolic correction. The star table is a table of local linearisations.
- **Same-order contestants leave coefficient ratios:** sin 3x/tan 5x → 3/5 because both are order 1 with coefficients 3 and 5. Higher-order refinements (u − sin u ~ u³/6) decide the races that first order ties.
- **The radian requirement is structural:** sin u ~ u holds when u measures arc length; in degrees the slope is π/180 and every star silently carries that factor. Radians are the unit where geometry and limits coincide.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — The Growth Ladder Across Families

- At infinity the families race in a fixed order: **ln x < xᵖ < aˣ < xˣ**. Any higher rung dominates: x¹⁰⁰/eˣ → 0, ln(xⁿ)/x → 0, eˣ/xᵏ → ∞ — regardless of coefficients.
- **Mechanism:** powers grow additively, exponentials multiplicatively, logs by diminishing increments. The ladder decides mixed-family limits by rung alone; coefficients only matter within a rung (degree comparison for polynomials, leading ratios for same-type exponentials).

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — steer to sin u/u:** (sin 5x)/x = 5·(sin 5x)/(5x) → 5.
- **Type B — second-order matching:** (1 − cos 4x)/x² = 16·(1 − cos 4x)/(4x)² → 16·(1/2) = 8.
- **Type C — mixed exp/log:** (e^{3x} − 1)/ln(1 + 2x) → 3/2 by coefficient ratio.
- **Type D — 1^∞ bridge:** (1 + k/x)^{mx} → e^{mk} via the log star.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Degrees in the sine star:** sin u/u → 1 fails in degrees — convert first. **"∞ − ∞ = 0":** the form is indeterminate; merge fractions or rationalise before any verdict.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (tan 4x − sin 2x)/(3x).** — **Split legally** (both pieces' limits exist): = lim (tan 4x)/(3x) − lim (sin 2x)/(3x). **First piece (star tan u/u, u = 4x):** (4/3)·(tan 4x)/(4x) → 4/3. **Second piece (star sin u/u, u = 2x):** (2/3)·(sin 2x)/(2x) → 2/3. **Combine: 4/3 − 2/3 = 2/3.** **Order check:** tan 4x ~ 4x, sin 2x ~ 2x ⟹ numerator ~ 2x over 3x ⟹ 2/3 ✓. **Deep point:** differences of same-order stars reduce to coefficient arithmetic — the first-order Taylor view turns the whole expression into (4x − 2x)/3x before any steering.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 − cos 2x)/(x·tan 3x).** — **Order diagnosis:** 1 − cos 2x ~ (2x)²/2 = 2x² (order 2); x·tan 3x ~ 3x² (order 2) ⟹ same order ⟹ ratio of coefficients 2/3. **Rigorous:** [(1 − cos 2x)/(2x)²]·[(2x)²/(x·tan 3x)] = [1/2]·[4x²/(3x²)]·[(tan 3x)/(3x)]^{-1} → (1/2)(4/3)(1) = **2/3** ✓. **Deep point:** matching orders of vanishing means matching powers of the steering factors — the star decomposition certifies the coefficient ratio the order reading predicted.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (ln(2x + 1))/(e^{3x} − 1)... interpreted as lim_{x→∞} ln(2x+1)/(e^{3x} − 1).** — **Identify the race:** log (bottom rung) vs exponential (top rung) — the ladder says the exponential dominates overwhelmingly. **Formalise:** both → ∞ ⟹ ∞/∞; the ladder predicts **0**. **Verification by rates:** ln(2x+1) ~ ln x grows by diminishing increments; e^{3x} − 1 multiplies by e³ per unit step — the ratio of a logarithmic creep to an exponential explosion → **0**. **Deep point:** mixed-family limits at infinity are decided by rung, not by coefficients — no amount of polynomial detail in the log's argument rescues it against an exponential; this is the ladder's starkest verdict.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (2^x − 1)/(√(1 + x) − 1).** — **Form:** (1 − 1)/(1 − 1) = **0/0** ✓. **Numerator (general-base star):** 2ˣ − 1 ~ x·ln 2 ⟹ (2ˣ − 1)/x → **ln 2**. **Denominator (radical star):** √(1+x) − 1 ~ x/2 ⟹ (√(1+x) − 1)/x → **1/2** (conjugate: (√(1+x) − 1)(√(1+x) + 1) = x). **Ratio:** [x ln 2]/[x/2] = **2 ln 2 = ln 4**. **Deep point:** each family contributes its own linearisation — 2ˣ has slope ln 2 at 0, √(1+x) has slope 1/2 at 0 — and the limit is the ratio of slopes; the general-base tax ln a is the exponential family's signature.
@@summary@@
Four families, one architecture: algebraic stars from difference-of-powers identities, trigonometric stars from radian geometry, exponential and logarithmic stars from e's self-replication, all composed through u → 0 and steered by manufactured denominators. Same-order contestants leave coefficient ratios; the growth ladder (ln < power < exponential < super-exponential) decides mixed-family races at infinity.
