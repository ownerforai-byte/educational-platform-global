%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Concavity:** f″ > 0 on an interval ⟹ concave up (cup holds water; tangents below the curve); f″ < 0 ⟹ concave down (tangents above).
- **Point of inflection:** a point where **concavity changes** — f″ changes sign. If f″ is continuous and f″(c) = 0, that is necessary but not sufficient; the sign change is the definition.
- **Second-derivative test connection:** f″(c) > 0 at a stationary point ⟹ local min (the cup's bottom); f″(c) < 0 ⟹ local max.
- **Inflection candidates:** where f″ = 0 **or f″ fails** — corners in the slope, vertical tangents, and zeros alike.

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — Concavity Is the Slope's Story

- f″ measures **how f′ changes**: concave up means slopes are increasing (the curve steepens or flattens upward); concave down means slopes are falling. The second derivative is the first derivative's derivative — concavity is the monotonicity of the slope function.
- **Inflection = slope's extremum:** at an inflection point, f′ itself has a local max or min — the curve is steepest (or flattest) exactly where it switches its bend. This is why inflection points matter in data: they mark where growth *rate* peaks, even while growth continues.
- **The zero-without-change trap:** f″(c) = 0 with no sign change (x⁴ at 0: f″ = 12x² ≥ 0) is not an inflection — the cup merely flattens. The definition is the sign change; the zero is only the candidate.

## 4. Deep Conceptual — The Full Sketching Protocol

- **Five-layer sketch:** domain ⟹ symmetry/intercepts ⟹ f′ table (monotonicity, critical points) ⟹ f″ table (concavity, inflections) ⟹ asymptotes/end behaviour ⟹ assemble. Each layer uses one derivative; the picture is the derivative data made visible.
- **Tangent-at-inflection:** at an inflection point the tangent crosses the curve — the unique line that is locally above on one side and below on the other. This geometric signature distinguishes inflections from mere stationary points.
- **f″ = 0 ⟹ candidate, sign change ⟹ verdict:** the same candidate/verdict split as f′ = 0 for extrema; the parallel structure means one habit serves both tables.

## 6. Common Confusions

**"f″ = 0 ⟹ inflection":** x⁴ at 0 forever — the sign change is the definition. **"Concave up means increasing":** concavity is about the slope's trend, not the function's direction; a decreasing function can be concave up.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (6m): Find the intervals of concavity and the inflection points of y = x³ − 6x² + 9x.** — **Second derivative:** y″ = 6x − 12 = 6(x − 2); zero at x = 2. **Sign table:** y″ < 0 on (−∞, 2) (test 0: −12) ⟹ concave down; y″ > 0 on (2, ∞) (test 3: 6) ⟹ concave up. **Inflection:** the sign changes at x = 2 ⟹ point (2, f(2)) = (2, 8 − 24 + 18) = **(2, 2)**. **Tangent there:** y′ = 3x² − 12x + 9 ⟹ y′(2) = −3 ⟹ tangent y − 2 = −3(x − 2), which crosses the curve at the inflection. **Deep point:** the candidate (y″ = 0) became a verdict only through the sign change — and the crossing tangent is the geometric certificate that the bend truly switched.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Show that y = x⁴ has no inflection point even though y″(0) = 0.** — **Second derivative:** y″ = 12x²; zero at x = 0. **Sign study:** 12x² ≥ 0 for **all** x — the second derivative never changes sign ⟹ concave up on (−∞, 0) and (0, ∞) alike ⟹ **no inflection at 0**. **What happens at 0:** the cup merely flattens (y′ = 4x³ has a horizontal tangent there and y′′′(0) = 0 too) — a stationary point and absolute minimum, but the bend never switches. **Deep point:** the definition of inflection is the *sign change*, and x⁴ is its permanent counterexample to the "y″ = 0 ⟹ inflection" reflex; candidate and verdict are different ranks.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Find all inflection points of y = x⁴ − 6x² and describe the curve's bend story.** — **Second derivative:** y″ = 12x² − 12 = 12(x − 1)(x + 1); candidates x = ±1. **Sign table:** y″ > 0 on (−∞, −1) (test −2: 36) ⟹ concave up; y″ < 0 on (−1, 1) (test 0: −12) ⟹ concave down; y″ > 0 on (1, ∞) ⟹ concave up. **Inflections:** sign changes at both ±1 ⟹ points (1, 1 − 6) = **(1, −5)** and (−1, −5). **Bend story:** the W-shaped quartic is cup-up outside ±1 (the two valleys' arms) and dome-down between them (the central hump at x = 0, y = 0). **Deep point:** two inflections frame the central dome — the quartic's extra pair of roots in y″ (compared to the cubic's one) is exactly the extra bend the W-shape demands; the concavity table is the curve's skeleton.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): For y = x³ − 3x, find the local extrema and the inflection point, then assemble the sketch protocol's summary.** — **f′ = 3x² − 3 = 3(x−1)(x+1):** local max at (−1, 2), local min at (1, −2) (sign changes + → − and − → +). **f″ = 6x:** zero at 0, sign changes − to + ⟹ inflection at (0, 0). **Concavity:** down on (−∞, 0), up on (0, ∞). **Assembly:** the curve rises to the summit (−1, 2), descends through the inflection (0, 0) — where the descent is steepest (slope −3) — and bottoms at (1, −2) before rising again; the inflection sits exactly midway between the extremum pair, as symmetry demands for an odd cubic. **Deep point:** the inflection's slope (−3) is the steepest point of the descent — the slope's own minimum — confirming that inflection points are extrema of f′; the sketch protocol's layers interlock because each derivative narrates the next.
@@summary@@
Concavity is the slope's own story: f″ > 0 cups upward with increasing slopes, f″ < 0 domes downward, and inflection points are where the bend switches — the slope's own extrema, marked by crossing tangents. The candidate/verdict split rules both tables: f′ = 0 and f″ = 0 are candidate lists, sign changes are verdicts, and x⁴'s flattening cup is the permanent warning. The five-layer sketch protocol turns the two derivative tables into a picture whose every feature is a cited theorem.
