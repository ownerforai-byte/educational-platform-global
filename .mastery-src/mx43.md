## 1. Topic Introduction — The Limit Menagerie by Family

Evaluating limits of **algebraic, trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic** functions: each family has its reduction grammar — factoring for polynomials, standard stars for trig, e-limits for exp/log.

%%%
## 2. Fundamental Concepts

### 2.1 Algebraic limits

- **Polynomials:** direct substitution (continuity). **Rationals at a:** substitute; 0/0 ⟹ factor-and-cancel or conjugate; c/0 ⟹ ±∞ by side-sign. **Rationals at ∞:** degree rule.
- **Surds:** conjugate multiplication; (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ for fractional n too.

### 2.2 Trigonometric limits

- Everything reduces to the stars: sin u/u → 1, tan u/u → 1, (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2, all in **radians**. Rescaling sin(kx)/x → k; compound-angle identities as reducers; squeeze as safety net.

### 2.3 Exponential and logarithmic limits

- (eᵘ − 1)/u → 1; (aᵘ − 1)/u → ln a; ln(1+u)/u → 1; (1+u)^{1/u} → e; 1^∞ via the bridge e^{lim uv}. Growth ladder for contests at ∞: ln x ≪ xᵃ ≪ aˣ.

### 2.4 The universal recipe

- Substitute → classify the form → pick the family's grammar → transform to a standard form → answer. The families differ only in their standard forms; the recipe is one.

## 4. Detailed Explanation

### 4.1 Why families need different grammars

- Each family's singularities have different structure: polynomial zeros factor; trig singularities oscillate; exp/log singularities curve. The standard limits encode each structure once, so every member reduces to its family's stars.

### 4.2 Mixed-family expressions

- Split into factors (when limits exist) and evaluate each by its own grammar: (e^{2x} − 1)/ln(1+4x) is an exp star over a log star via the x-bridge → 2/4 = 1/2. Mixed forms are the exam's favourite compound.

### 4.3 The radian and base disciplines

- Two silent assumptions break trig and log limits: degrees (sin x/x fails) and non-natural logs (ln(1+u)/u → 1 only for natural log). State them; convert when needed.

## 6. Common Confusions

**Mixing grammars:** a trig expression needs trig stars, not factoring — (1 − cos x)/x² is ½ by half-angle, not by "cancelling cos".
%%%
**Forgetting the coefficient:** sin 3x/x → 3 (the a survives); (e^{2x}−1)/x → 2.
%%%
**Log base slips:** ln(1+x)/x → 1 is natural-log only; log₁₀(1+x)/x → 1/ln 10.

## 7. Exam Application

**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 3x)/x. (2m)** — **Rescaling:** sin 3x/x = 3·[sin 3x/(3x)]; as x → 0, 3x → 0 and the star gives sin 3x/(3x) → 1 ⟹ limit = **3**. (General: sin(kx)/x → k — the coefficient survives, the argument dies.)
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{5x} − 1)/(sin 2x).** — **Split via stars:** = [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)]·(5x)/[(sin 2x/(2x))·(2x)] — insert the argument factors: = (5/2)·[e^{5x}−1)/(5x)]/[sin 2x/2x] → (5/2)·(1/1) = **5/2**. Exponential star over trig star, coefficient ratio deciding.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (x − 1)/ln x.** — **Substitute:** 0/0. **Steer:** set u = x − 1 → 0; ln x = ln(1 + u) ⟹ expression = u/ln(1+u) = 1/[ln(1+u)/u] → 1/1 = **1**. The log star resolves the classic log-versus-linear race at 1.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 3x)/x. (2m)** — **Rescaling insertion:** multiply and divide by 3: sin 3x/x = **3·[sin 3x/(3x)]**. **Star:** as x → 0, the argument 3x → 0, so by the fundamental star (radians) sin 3x/(3x) → **1**. **Result:** 3·1 = **3**. **General theorem harvested:** lim_{x→0} sin(kx)/x = k — the coefficient survives, the argument dies into the star. This is the 15.2/45 reduction grammar in its purest one-line form.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{5x} − 1)/(sin 2x).** — **Form:** 0/0 mixing two families — exponential over trigonometric. **Double insertion:** (e^{5x} − 1)/(sin 2x) = [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)] · (5x) / { [sin 2x/(2x)] · (2x) } = **(5/2) · [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)] / [sin 2x/(2x)]**. **Stars:** e^{5x} − 1 over 5x → **1** (exponential star, u = 5x → 0); sin 2x/(2x) → **1** (trigonometric star, radians). **Result:** (5/2)·(1/1) = **5/2**. The x-bridge converts a mixed-family 0/0 into a ratio of coefficients — the standard move whenever exp meets trig in one fraction.
%%%
**NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (x − 1)/ln x.** — **Substitute:** (1 − 1)/ln 1 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. **Steer into the log star:** set u = x − 1, so u → 0 as x → 1 and ln x = ln(1 + u) (composition theorem licenses the rewrite). The expression becomes **u/ln(1+u) = 1/[ln(1+u)/u]**. **Star:** ln(1+u)/u → **1** as u → 0 (natural log mandatory). **Result:** 1/1 = **1**. **Reading:** near x = 1 the logarithm grows exactly as fast as the linear term — ln x ≈ x − 1 is the first-order tangent approximation, and this limit is its certificate.
@@summary@@
Limit evaluation is **one recipe with four grammars**. **Algebraic:** substitute; 0/0 factors or rationalises ((xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a) → naⁿ⁻¹ even for fractional n); c/0 is determinately infinite by side-sign; ∞/∞ compares degrees. **Trigonometric** (radians only): everything steers into sin u/u → 1, tan u/u → 1, (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2, with rescaling sin(kx)/x → k. **Exponential:** (eᵘ−1)/u → 1, (aᵘ−1)/u → ln a, the e-powers via (1+u)^{1/u} → e. **Logarithmic:** ln(1+u)/u → 1 (natural base mandatory). **Mixed-family forms** surrender to the x-bridge and legal splitting: (e^{5x}−1)/sin 2x → 5/2 (NEB-style), (x−1)/ln x → 1 (the tangent approximation of ln at 1). NEB 2024's sin 3x/x → 3 is the mantra made flesh: *the coefficient survives, the argument dies*.
