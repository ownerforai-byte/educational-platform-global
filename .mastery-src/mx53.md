## 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to the Limit Laws

This canonical is the **short-form doorway** to the algebraic properties of limits: the sum, product, quotient, power, root, squeeze, and composition theorems in one page. The parent canonical carries the full theorem-grade treatment; this companion mirrors the laws, explains why their hypotheses matter, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

%%%
## 2. Theorem Mirror

- **Sum/difference:** lim (f ± g) = lim f ± lim g — *provided both limits exist* (finite; ∞ − ∞ is banned).
- **Product:** lim (f·g) = (lim f)(lim g) — both must exist; constants pull out: lim c·f = c·lim f.
- **Quotient:** lim f/g = (lim f)/(lim g) — both exist **and lim g ≠ 0**.
- **Power/root:** lim fⁿ = (lim f)ⁿ; lim ⁿ√f = ⁿ√(lim f) (even roots need lim f ≥ 0).
- **Squeeze:** g ≤ f ≤ h with lim g = lim h = L ⟹ lim f = L.
- **Composition:** g continuous at a, f continuous at g(a) ⟹ lim f(g(x)) = f(lim g(x)).

%%%
## 3. Deep Conceptual — The Laws Do Not Reverse

- The laws say: *if* the pieces' limits exist (and the quotient's denominator is nonzero), *then* the combined limit is the combination. They do **not** say a combined limit's existence forces the pieces' limits to exist — lim x·sin(1/x) = 0 exists while lim sin(1/x) does not.
- **Why the hypothesis discipline matters:** splitting a limit into pieces whose limits do not exist is the most common illegal move in limit work. The laws license *assembly* from known parts, never *disassembly* into unknown ones.
- **The exception that proves the rule:** the squeeze theorem exists precisely for pieces without limits — it is the sanctioned way to handle a factor that oscillates, by caging it between converging rails instead of splitting it.

%%%
## 4. Deep Conceptual — Composition as the Substitution Licence

- The composition theorem is the *proof* behind "just substitute": pushing a limit through f is legal exactly when f is continuous at the inner limit. Every substitution step in every solution is an appeal to this theorem.
- **Where the licence expires:** if f is discontinuous at the inner limit, lim f(g(x)) and f(lim g(x)) can differ — the one-sided machinery takes over. Locating a function's discontinuities is locating where substitution reasoning must stop.

%%%
## 5. Worked Example Types

- **Type A — legal assembly:** each piece's limit exists ⟹ combine by the laws (polynomials, continuous pieces).
- **Type B — illegal split avoided:** a factor oscillates ⟹ do not split; squeeze or damp it.
- **Type C — quotient with dying denominator:** check lim g ≠ 0 before dividing; if it is 0, re-diagnose (0/0 or wall).
- **Type D — composition chain:** evaluate the inner limit, then apply the outer function *only if* it is continuous there.

%%%
## 6. Common Confusions

**"lim(A + B) always = lim A + lim B":** only when both exist — ∞ − ∞ and missing sides break it. **"c/0 is ∞/∞":** no — one contestant is standing still; L'Hôpital does not apply.
@@keyPoints@@
Limit laws: sum, difference, product, quotient (denominator nonzero), constant multiple, power, root - each needs the pieces' limits to exist. Squeeze: converging rails cage the middle. Composition: continuous outer function commutes with the inner limit. The laws assemble limits from known pieces; they never license splitting into nonexistent pieces.
@@importantConcepts@@
Assembly is licensed, disassembly is not: the laws build limits from existing pieces and never reverse.

@@practiceQuestions@@
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→3} (x² + 2x)/(x + 1), citing each law used.** — **Numerator (sum + power laws):** lim (x² + 2x) = (lim x²) + (lim 2x) = 9 + 6 = **15** — polynomials are continuous, so substitution is the laws in one stroke. **Denominator:** lim (x + 1) = **4 ≠ 0** — the quotient theorem's hypothesis holds. **Quotient: 15/4.** **Deep point:** the check "lim g ≠ 0" is not decoration — at x = −1 the same expression would need re-diagnosis (0/0 or wall), and naming the check is what distinguishes a lawful computation from a habitual one.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): Show that lim_{x→0} x·cos(1/x) = 0, and explain why the product law cannot be used directly.** — **The product law's failure:** lim_{x→0} cos(1/x) does **not** exist (oscillation), so lim (x)·lim cos(1/x) is not a legal assembly — one piece is missing. **The squeeze route:** −1 ≤ cos(1/x) ≤ 1 ⟹ −|x| ≤ x·cos(1/x) ≤ |x| (multiplying by |x| ≥ 0). **Rails:** both ±|x| → 0 ⟹ **lim = 0** by the squeeze theorem. **Deep point:** the squeeze theorem is the sanctioned replacement for an illegal split — it handles the oscillating factor by caging it, converting "the piece has no limit" into "the piece is bounded," which is all the product needs.
%%%
**NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} √(x² + 8), citing the composition theorem explicitly.** — **Inner limit:** lim_{x→1} (x² + 8) = 9 (polynomial ⟹ substitution). **Outer continuity:** √t is continuous at t = 9 (9 > 0, inside the root's domain interior). **Composition theorem:** lim √(x² + 8) = √(lim (x² + 8)) = **√9 = 3**. **Contrast:** at x = −3 the same composition works (inner limit 17); but lim_{x→0⁺} √x·ln-style compositions at domain edges need one-sided care — the theorem's hypotheses travel with the point, not the formula. **Deep point:** the composition theorem is the general licence of which "substitute into the outer function" is the everyday instance; continuity of the outer function at the inner limit is the exact condition, no stronger and no weaker.
%%%
**NEB-style (6m): A student writes lim_{x→0} (sin x + x·sin(1/x))/x = lim sin x/x + lim sin(1/x) = 1 + (does not exist) and concludes the limit does not exist. Assess and correct.** — **The error:** the sum law requires **both** pieces' limits to exist; the second piece sin(1/x) has no limit, so the split is illegal and the "does not exist" conclusion does not follow — the failure of a *method* is not the failure of the limit. **Correct route:** lim sin x/x = 1 (star) and lim x·sin(1/x) = 0 (squeeze, as shown in the standard example) ⟹ the sum law **is** applicable to these two *converging* pieces: lim = 1 + 0 = **1**. **Deep point:** regroup before you split — the art of using the limit laws is choosing a decomposition in which every piece converges; the laws never fail, but careless decompositions do.
@@summary@@
The algebraic limit laws — sum, product, quotient, power, root, squeeze, composition — assemble limits from pieces whose limits already exist, with the quotient demanding a nonzero denominator and the composition demanding outer continuity. Their hypotheses are the mathematics: assembly is licensed, disassembly into nonexistent pieces is not, and the squeeze theorem is the sanctioned cure for oscillating factors. Every substitution step in limit work is the composition theorem in action; every split should name its pieces' limits first. Master the hypotheses and the laws become a complete algebra of approach.
