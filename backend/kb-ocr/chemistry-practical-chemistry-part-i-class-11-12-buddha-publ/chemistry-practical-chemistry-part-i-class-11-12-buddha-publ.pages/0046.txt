THEORY
If both the components of the mixture are found to be water-insoluble,
then the mixture is separated by dissolving the mixture in a suitable
solvent followed by filtration and precipitation. Here, the mixture of sand
and -calcium carbonate is treated with dilute hydrochloric acid in which
CaC03 gets dissolved but not the sand.
CaC03 (s) + 2HC1 (aq) -----...--.-------.-> CaC12 (aq) + 1-120 (1) + C02 (g)
Calcium carbonate is recovered back by treating the filtrate with
sodium carbonate solution or ammonium carbonate solution.
CaC12 (aq) + Na2C03 (aq) ---—--—CaC03 (s) + 2 NaCl (aq)

Sand and CaC03 are made pure after several wash. Their purity can
be checked by freating their wash with few drops of AgN03 and BaC12
solutions. Presence of chloride ion in sand and CaC03 is indicated by
formation of a white precipitate. Similarly, presence of soluble carbonate
in CaC03 is indicated by formation of a white precipitate.
