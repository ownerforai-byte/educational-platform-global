PROCESS
Place the given piece of metal in a beaker. Cover it with a short stem
funnel. Add water so that the stem of the funnel completely dips under
water. Now fill the eudiometer tube half with bench sulphuric acid and
half with water. Close its mouth with thumb (there should not be any air
bubbles inside the tube). Invert the eudiometer tube over the stem of the
funnel. Clamp the tube with stand.
Reaction between the metal and the acid takes place and hydrogen
gas starts to evolve. The bubbles are seen in the tube. The gas collects in
the tube by displacing water downward. (At the end of the reaction, the
small piece of metal may come up in the tube. If it happens, give a slight
knock near the metal and make it combined with acid). When no more
bubbles are seen, remove the eudiometer tube closing its mouth with
thumb. Then invert the tube immediately into a tall jar containing water.
Wait for few minutes and make the level of water inside and outside the
tube equal. At this stage, pressure exerted by the given volume of the
moist gas inside the tube equals to the atmospheric pressure.
Note the volume of hydrogen gas inside the eudiometer tube. Note
the atmospheric pressure. Note the room temperature and aqueous
tension at this temperature. Precede the calculations as below.
