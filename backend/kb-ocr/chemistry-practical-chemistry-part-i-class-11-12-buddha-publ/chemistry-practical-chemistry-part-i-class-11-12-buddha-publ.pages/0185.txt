THEORY
Hydrolysis of a salt is defined as the combination of ions of the salt with
hydrogen and hydroxyl ions of water to give acidic or basic solution.
Salts of strong acid and weak base (e.g. NH4Cl) are hydrolysed to give
weakly acidic solution whereas salts of weak acid and strong base (e.g.
Na2C03) are hydrolysed to give weakly alkaline solution. Salts of weak
acid and weak base (e.g. CH3COONH4) are hydrolysed to give almost
neutral solutions. Salts of strong acid and strong base (e.g. NaCl) are not
hydrolysed and therefore, their solutions are neutral. The acidic, alkaline
or neutral nature can be identified with the help of the indicators like
litmus papers, phenolphthalein solution, methyl orange solution etc.
Methyl orange solution produces pink colour with an acid whereas it
produces light yellow colour with a base. (Phenolphthalein gives a pink
colour with alkali and it has no colour with acid. Acid turns blue litmus
to red and base turns red litmus to blue).
