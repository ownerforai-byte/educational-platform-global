10. If a ppt is formed in any of the test tubes, wash the residue again and

II. If no ppt is seen in both the test tubes, then make a hole in the filter
paper and vyash down the residue in a porcelain basin with a
minimum quantity of water with the help of a wash bottle.
12. Decant the black oxide of copper and drain out water as maximum
as possible (there should not be any loss of copper oxide).
13. Dissolve the cupric oxide in minimum quantity of dilute sulphuric
acid stirring with the help of a glass rod. Blue copper sulphate
solution is formed.
14. The solution is filtered. The filtrate taken in a basin is heated upto the
crystallization point by placing it over the sand bath. Stir the
evaporated solution to avoid the crust formation.
The crystallization point is ascertained by introducing few drops of
15.
the evaporated solution with the help of a glass rod on the outer wall
of the test tube which is filled with cold water,
Blue crystals are seen on the wall of the test tube when the solution
16.
attains the crystallization point. As soon as the solution attains the
crystallization point, avoid heating.
Float the porcelain basin on water taken in a beaker. The blue
crystals of copper sulphate separate out slowly on cooling.
Drain off the mother liquor after complete separation of the blue
crystals.
Collect all the crystals on a filter paper without disturbing the shape
of the crystals.
Finally, dry the crystals of blue vitriol in the folds of the filter paper
or in air.
