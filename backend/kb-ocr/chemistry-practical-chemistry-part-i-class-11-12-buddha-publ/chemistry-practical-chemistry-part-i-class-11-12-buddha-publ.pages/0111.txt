Selection of an Indicator
For the selection of an indicator, required for a particular titration, a
curve is drawn by taking the volume of alkali along X-axis and pH value
along Y-axis. This curve is called as titration curve. As the alkali is added
to the acid taken in a conical flask, the pH of the solution increases and
different types of curves will be obtained in different acid-base titrations.
The steep rise in pH in the curve corresponds to the end point of titration.
The pH range at the end point is compared with the pH range of each
indicator and the selection of a suitable indicator is carried out.
1. Titration of a strong acid and a strong base (HCl Vs. NaOH)
On drawing the titration curve the steep rise in pH is found to be in
between 3-11 i.e., the pH range at the end point is 3-11. Therefore,
either of the indicators (methyl orange or phenolphthalein) can be
used in the titration.
