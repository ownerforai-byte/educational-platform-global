End point of titration is the point of contpletion of reaction in titration
which is shown by the colour change of third chemical substance i.e. indicator: In
case of alkalimetery and acidimetry, it is also called as neutralization
point.
Indicator is the third chemical substance used in titration which helps to
predict the end point of titration by its colour change. In some cases, titrant
(the solution in the burette) or titrand (the solution in the conical flask)
ibelf acts as an indicator and is called self-indicator or auto-indicator.
Examples of indicator: Methyl orange, phenolphthalein

It is also called law of equivalents.
Equivalence point of reaction is a theoretical end point of the reaction.
At this point, n gram equivalent of a substance (say acid) reacb with
n gram equivalent of another substance (say base). The difference
between observed and theoretical end points is called titration error.
This error can be minimized by selecting a suitable indicator as far as
possible.
At the equivalence (end) point, the following equation holds true:
VISI = V2S2
Where,
VI = Volume of acid
Sl = Normality of acid
V 2 = Volume of base
Sz = Normality of base
This is called normality equation and is used to find the unknown
strength.
