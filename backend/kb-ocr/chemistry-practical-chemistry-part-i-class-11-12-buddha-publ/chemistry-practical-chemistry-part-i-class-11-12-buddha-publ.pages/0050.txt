THEORY
The solid or liquid substances which convert easily into vapour form by
the little supply of heat or on exposure to atmosphere are called volatile
substances. For example: camphor, ammonium chloride, iodine, petrol
etc.
The mixture of sand, camphor and common salt is separated by
sublimation process followed by filtration. The volatile component of the
mixture (here, camphor) is separated from the non-volatile one by
sublimation processe Sublimation can be defined as the process Of
converting the solid directly into the vapour and cooling back the vapour
directly into the solid state. Thus obtained solid is called sublimate.
Carnphor is obtained as a sublimate from the given mixture.
The rest of the components of the mixture (i.e. sand and common
salt) are separated by dissolution of the mixture in water followed by
filtration. Sand is obtained as residue whereas solution of common salt is
obtained in solid form by evaporating its solutione Filtration is defined as
the method of separating the insoluble component and solution of the
soluble component of the mixture by means of a porous medium like
filter paper, glass wool etc.
