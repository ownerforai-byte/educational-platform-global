THEORY
Chromatography is found to be a simple technique to separate various
components in a mixture. Chromatography is categorized as paper
chromatography, gas chromatography, thin layer chromatography and
column chromatography.
The separation of a mixture by paper chromatography is based on
the fact that different substances are adsorbed by the cellulose fibres Of
the paper to different extents. The components which are more firmly
adsorbed on the paper are carried away less nearer across the paper by a
moving solvent. On the other hand, the components which are less firmly
adsorbed on the paper are carried away more farther across the paper by
a moving solvent. In this way, different components in the mixture (here
dyes in the ink) become separated in different coloured bands.
The Rf value of a component gives the idea on how firmly it gets
adsorbed on the paper. Hence, different substances have different Rf
values. The Rf value of a component is given by
Distance travelled by the component from the original line
Rf = Distance travelled by the solvent from the original line
