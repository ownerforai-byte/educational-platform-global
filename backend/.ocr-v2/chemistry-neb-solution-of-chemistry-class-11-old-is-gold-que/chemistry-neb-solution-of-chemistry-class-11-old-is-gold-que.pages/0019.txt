Le-Chatelier•s principle
ii.
In 1884, a French chemist Le-Chatelier studied the effect of concentration, pressure and
our
temperature on the state of equilibrium. It states that "If a system in equilibrium is subjected to
a change in concentration, pressure or temperature then the equilibrium shifts in such a way so
as to nullify the effect of the change." That is in an equilibrium reaction:
On increasing the concentration, the equilibrium shift to the direction where the increased
concentration is consumed. On doing so, the effect of increased concentration will be
minimized.
On increasing the pressure, the equilibrium shifts to the direction where volume is decreased.
As we know that when the pressure is increased the volume will decreased. If the equilibrium
shifts to the side where volume is increased then the effect of increased pressure will be
minimized.
On increasing the temperature, the equilibrium shifts to the direction where heat is absorbed.
As we know that the increased temperature raises the heat. If the equilibrium shifts to the side
where heat rs absorbed then the effect of increased temperature will be minimized.
