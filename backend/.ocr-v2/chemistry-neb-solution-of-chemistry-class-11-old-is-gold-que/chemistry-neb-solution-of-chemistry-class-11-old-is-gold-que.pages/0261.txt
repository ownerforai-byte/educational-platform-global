Allotropes of Carbon: The phenomenon of the existence of an elements in different forms
which have different physical properties but similar chemical properties is known as allotropy.
Following are the allotropes of carbon:
Crystalline forms: Diamond and Graphite
Diamond: It is purest, crystalline and hardest form of carbon. It occurs in the form of
transparent octahedral crystals which usually have curved surface and do not shine much in
their natural forms. They are cut at proper angle so as to give rise to large total internal
reflection which lends them the usual shine. The carbon atoms are arranged tetrahedrally in
diamond by forming sp3-hybridization. Each sp3 hybrid orbital carbon atom is linked to its
neighbours by four single covalent bonds. This leads to three dimensional network of covalent
bonds which makes it very hard and has high melting point and boiling point.
Fig: Diamond
Graphite: The carbon atoms are arranged rn regular hexagonal in flat parallel layers resulting
the formation of the large plates. Each carbon atoms in these layers is bonded to three others by
sp2hybridization. Each layer or large plate is bonded to adjacent layers by weak Vander Waal
force. Due to this each layer can slide each other easily. So, one electron is left non-bonded in
each carbon atom. Due to the presence of mobile electrons, graphite is good conductor of heat
and electricity.
