Electronegativity (E.N.)
The electronegativity of an element is defined as the relative tendency of an atom in a molecule
to attract a shared pair of electron towards itself i.e. ability of an atom to attract the shared pair
of the electron. It is expressed in number of scales like Pauling's scale, Mulliken's scale but most
commonly used scale is Pauling's scale.
Factor Affecting Electronegativity
Following are factors:
1. Atomic size: Smaller the size of an atom higher the value of E.N.
2 Number of inner shells: Atom having larger number of inner shells has the lower value of E.N.
3. Number and nature of atoms bonded to it: E.N. of an element depends upon the number and
nature of the atom to which it is bonded. Example- E-N- of P-atom in PCIs is higher than that in
PF5 because F is more E.N. than Cl.
