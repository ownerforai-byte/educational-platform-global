Electron Affinity
The amount of energy which is released when an electron is added to an isolated gaseous atom
in its ground state in order to change it into the negatively charged ion is called electron
affinity. It is denoted by E.A. and expressed in terms of eV.
Factors Affecting E.A:
1. Nuclear Charge: The value of. E.A. increase with increase in nuclear charge.
2. Size of atom: Larger the size of atom, lesser the value of E.A.
3. E.C.: Stable E.C such half-filled and complete-filled orbital have low value of E.A.
Variation of E.A. across the period:
When we move from left to right across the period, the size of an atom decreases and nuclear
charge increases. Due to both factor the value of E.A. is increased.
Variation of E.A. along group
When we move from top to bottom along group of periodic table, the value of E.A. is decreased
with increase in size of an atom.
Important Trends in E.A.
I. Halogens have the highest E.A.: It requires one electron to attain stable E.C.
2 Noble gases have zero values of E.A: They have stable E.C. i.e.ns2np6
3. The E.A. of Cl is greater than that of F: Due to the small stze of F-atom. If the size is small, the
inter-electronic repulsion will be strong. Therefore, the incoming electron does feel much
attraction towards the nucleus. Hence, Cl has greater E.A. than F.
