# Atomic Structure

The search for what lies inside an atom produced one of science's great stories — from Thomson's plum-pudding model through Rutherford's nuclear atom to Bohr's quantised orbits and finally the quantum-mechanical picture of orbitals. This NEB Class 11 chemistry unit develops each model, explains the hydrogen spectrum, and introduces quantum numbers plus the rules that generate electronic configurations up to atomic number 30.

## From Rutherford to Bohr

Rutherford's α-scattering experiment showed that nearly all mass and positive charge sit in a tiny nucleus with electrons revolving around it. Classical physics predicts such orbiting electrons should radiate energy and spiral inward — atoms would collapse. Niels Bohr rescued the model with quantum postulates: electrons occupy only fixed circular orbits of definite energy; they radiate or absorb energy only when jumping between orbits, the photon energy being ΔE = hν. For hydrogen-like species,

- radius: rₙ ∝ n²/Z,
- energy: Eₙ = −13.6 Z²/n² eV.

Bohr's model brilliantly explained the hydrogen line spectrum (Lyman, Balmer, Paschen series) but failed for multi-electron atoms and could not explain fine structure. Louis de Broglie then proposed that electrons behave as waves, λ = h/mv, and Heisenberg's uncertainty principle (Δx·Δp ≥ h/4π) showed the very idea of a precise trajectory is meaningless — leading to probability-based orbitals.

## Quantum Numbers and Rules

| Quantum number | Symbol | Allowed values | Information given |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Principal | n | 1, 2, 3, … | Size and main energy of shell |
| Azimuthal (angular momentum) | l | 0 to n−1 | Subshell shape: s, p, d, f |
| Magnetic | m_l | −l … 0 … +l | Orientation of orbital |
| Spin | m_s | +½ or −½ | Electron spin direction |

Three filling rules follow: the **Aufbau principle** (fill lowest-energy subshells first, following n + l order), **Pauli's exclusion principle** (no two electrons in an atom share all four quantum numbers), and **Hund's rule** (degenerate orbitals fill singly with parallel spins before pairing).

## Worked Calculation

```text
Question: Energy needed to ionise a hydrogen atom from the n = 2 level.

Step 1: E_n = -13.6 eV / n^2
Step 2: E_2 = -13.6 / 4 = -3.4 eV
Step 3: Ionisation removes the electron to E = 0:
        ΔE = 0 - (-3.4 eV) = +3.4 eV

Answer: 3.4 eV per atom.
```

Chromium ([Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹) and copper ([Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹) violate naive Aufbau filling because exactly half-filled and fully-filled d subshells gain extra exchange-energy stability.

## Key points

- Bohr orbits are 2-D circular paths; quantum orbitals are 3-D probability clouds.
- s orbitals are spherical; p orbitals are dumbbell-shaped along x, y, z axes.
- A shell holds at most 2n² electrons (K = 2, L = 8, M = 18).
- The Lyman series lies in the ultraviolet; Balmer lines lie in the visible region.
- Heisenberg's principle destroys the concept of fixed electron paths, motivating Schrödinger's wave model.

## Common mistakes

- Writing l values as 1 to n instead of 0 to n−1.
- Confusing an orbit (Bohr path) with an orbital (probability region).
- Forgetting the exceptional configurations of Cr and Cu when writing configs up to Z = 30.
- Treating Eₙ as positive — bound-state energies are negative relative to a free electron.
- Applying ΔE = hν without converting eV to joules (1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J) when required.
