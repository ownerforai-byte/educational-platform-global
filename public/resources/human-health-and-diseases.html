Pathogens and diseases

#Concept
1### 1. Pathogens Overview
2Pathogens are disease-causing microorganisms: bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa, helminths.
3### 2. Bacterial Diseases
4- **Tuberculosis**: Mycobacterium tuberculosis, airborne, affects lungs
5- **Cholera**: Vibrio cholerae, waterborne, causes severe diarrhea
6- **Typhoid**: Salmonella typhi, fecal-oral, fever, abdominal pain
7- **Diphtheria**: Corynebacterium diphtheriae, respiratory, pseudomembrane
8- **Pertussis (Whooping Cough)**: Bordetella pertussis, highly contagious
9### 3. Viral Diseases
10- **Influenza**: Influenza virus, airborne, respiratory
11- **Common Cold**: Rhinovirus, nasal congestion
12- **Dengue**: Dengue virus, mosquito-borne (Aedes aegypti)
13- **Chikungunya**: Chikungunya virus, mosquito-borne, joint pain
14- **Hepatitis B**: Hepatitis B virus, blood/body fluids, liver
15- **HIV/AIDS**: Human Immunodeficiency Virus, destroys CD4+ T cells
16- **Rabies**: Rabies virus, animal bite, affects nervous system
17### 4. Protozoan Diseases
18- **Malaria**: Plasmodium (P. falciparum, P. vivax), Anopheles mosquito
19- **Amoebiasis**: Entamoeba histolytica, contaminated food/water
20- **Sleeping Sickness**: Trypanosoma, tsetse fly
21- **Kala-azar**: Leishmania donovani, sandfly
22### 5. Worm Diseases (Helminths)
23- **Ascariasis**: Ascaris lumbricoides, roundworm
24- **Filariasis**: Wuchereria bancrofti, elephantiasis
25- **Taeniasis**: Taenia solium, tapeworm (pork)
26- **Hookworm**: Ancylostoma duodenale, soil-transmitted
27### 6. Fungal Diseases
28- **Ringworm**: Trichophyton, Microsporum, skin infections
29- **Athlete's foot**: Dermatophytes, foot fungus
30### 7. Entrance Exam Focus
31Match pathogen to disease; Identify vector; Distinguish bacterial vs viral treatments.

Key Points

Point
Bacterial causes: Mycobacterium tuberculosis (TB), Vibrio cholerae (cholera), Salmonella typhi (typhoid), Corynebacterium diphtheriae (diphtheria), Bordetella pertussis (whooping cough).
Viral causes: influenza virus, rhinovirus (common cold), dengue virus, hepatitis B virus, HIV and rabies virus.
Protozoan causes: Plasmodium (malaria), Entamoeba histolytica (amoebiasis), Trypanosoma (sleeping sickness), Leishmania donovani (kala-azar).
Helminthic causes: Ascaris lumbricoides (roundworm), Wuchereria bancrofti (filariasis or elephantiasis), Taenia solium (tapeworm from undercooked pork), Ancylostoma duodenale (hookworm).
Fungal causes: Trichophyton and Microsporum cause ringworm, and dermatophytes cause athlete's foot.
Fungal infections need antifungals rather than antibiotics, and viral diseases are managed by prevention, supportive care and, where available, antivirals.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
x Antibiotics treat viral infections. x Antibiotics only work on bacteria.
x Malaria is caused by a worm. x Malaria is caused by Plasmodium (protozoan).
x All mosquitoes carry disease. x Only specific species of specific genera.

Summary: Pathogens are disease-causing organisms — bacteria, viruses, fungi, protozoa and helminths — and each group produces a characteristic pattern of illness. Bacterial diseases include tuberculosis, cholera, typhoid, diphtheria and pertussis; viral diseases include influenza, dengue, hepatitis B, HIV/AIDS and rabies; protozoan diseases such as malaria and amoebiasis are usually food-borne or vector-borne, and helminthic diseases such as ascariasis and filariasis involve parasitic worms. Antibiotics act only on bacteria because viruses have none of the bacterial structures that these drugs target. Prevention relies on sanitation, safe drinking water, vector control, personal hygiene and vaccination.

Immune system and immunity

#Concept
1### 1. Immune System Overview
2The immune system protects the body against pathogens and foreign substances.
3### 2. Innate Immunity (Non-specific)
4- Present from birth
5- First line of defense
6- **Physical barriers**: Skin, mucous membranes
7- **Chemical barriers**: Stomach acid, lysozyme in tears
8- **Phagocytes**: Neutrophils, macrophages engulf pathogens
9- **Inflammation**: Redness, swelling, heat, pain
10- **Fever**: Elevated temperature inhibits pathogen growth
11- **Natural Killer cells**: Destroy infected host cells
12### 3. Adaptive Immunity (Specific)
13- Develops over lifetime
14- Specific to particular pathogens
15- Has memory (immunological memory)
16- **Humoral immunity**: B cells produce antibodies
17- **Cell-mediated immunity**: T cells destroy infected cells
18### 4. Antibodies (Immunoglobulins)
19- **IgG**: Most abundant, crosses placenta, long-term immunity
20- **IgM**: First responder, large pentamer
21- **IgA**: Mucosal immunity (tears, saliva, breast milk)
22- **IgE**: Allergic reactions, parasitic infections
23- **IgD**: B cell receptor
24### 5. Active vs Passive Immunity
25- **Active**: Body produces its own antibodies (infection or vaccination)
26- **Passive**: Receives pre-made antibodies (mother to baby, antivenom)
27### 6. Vaccination
28- Introduces weakened/killed antigen to stimulate active immunity
29- Creates memory cells for faster response on re-exposure
30- Herd immunity: When enough people are immune, disease spread is limited
31### 7. Autoimmune Diseases
32- Immune system attacks body's own cells
33- Examples: Rheumatoid arthritis, Type 1 diabetes, Lupus, Multiple sclerosis
34### 8. Allergies
35- Hypersensitive response to harmless antigens (allergens)
36- Mediated by IgE, mast cells release histamine
37- Examples: Hay fever, asthma, food allergies, anaphylaxis
38### 9. Entrance Exam Focus
39Distinguish innate vs adaptive; Identify antibody types; Explain vaccination principle.

Key Points

Point
Innate immunity: present at birth, non-specific, no memory. Adaptive immunity: acquired, antigen-specific, has memory.
The four classic signs of inflammation are redness, swelling, heat and pain, usually with some loss of function at the site.
Fever raises body temperature and slows the growth and reproduction of many pathogens.
IgG is the most abundant antibody in plasma, crosses the placenta and gives long-term immunity; IgA is found in tears, saliva, mucus and breast milk.
Natural passive immunity reaches the baby as maternal IgG through the placenta and as IgA in colostrum and breast milk.
Herd immunity arises when a large enough fraction of the population is immune, breaking the chain of transmission of infection.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
x Innate immunity has memory. x Only adaptive immunity has immunological memory.
x Antibodies kill pathogens directly. x Antibodies mark pathogens for destruction.
x Vaccines cause the disease. x Vaccines use weakened/killed pathogens to build immunity.

Summary: The immune system protects the body in two ways: innate (non-specific) immunity, which is present from birth and acts immediately, and adaptive (specific) immunity, which is acquired during life and carries immunological memory. Innate defences include physical and chemical barriers, phagocytes, inflammation, fever and natural killer cells, while adaptive immunity has a humoral branch in which B cells produce antibodies and a cell-mediated branch in which T cells destroy infected cells. Active immunity is the body's own antibody response to infection or vaccination, while passive immunity is the receipt of ready-made antibodies from a mother or from an antivenom. Failure of self-tolerance produces autoimmune disease and hypersensitivity to harmless antigens produces allergy.
