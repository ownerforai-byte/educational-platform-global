Unit 19: Electric Charges

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Electric & Magnetism

Electric charge is the first conserved quantity a student meets that is exactly conserved in every known interaction. The unit also contains one of the cleanest pieces of evidence in all of physics: charge is quantised, and the quantum is exact.

What the model asserts

  1. Charge is quantised: the elementary charge e = 1.602176634e-19 C exactly, and every observed charge in ordinary matter is an integer multiple of it.
  2. Coulomb's law F = k q1 q2 / r^2 holds with k = 8.9875517923e9 N.m^2/C^2, and like charges repel while unlike charges attract.
  3. Charge is conserved in every known interaction, and electric charge is an exact conserved quantum number in the Standard Model.
  4. Charging by friction transfers electrons between surfaces; charging by induction moves electrons without any contact at all.
  5. Conductors allow charge to move freely and insulators do not; in electrostatic equilibrium the field inside a conductor is zero and excess charge sits on the surface, concentrating at sharp points.
  6. Earthing works because the Earth is an enormous conductor held at nearly constant potential.
  7. Charge is quantised in units of e; the fractional charges seen in the fractional quantum Hall effect are a many-body property of a correlated electron liquid, not evidence that the electron is divisible.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • Coulomb's law assumes static point charges in a uniform medium; extended bodies require integration and conductors require redistribution.
  • Charging by friction depends on the material pair, surface condition and humidity - the triboelectric order is a tendency, not a law.
  • The field inside a conductor is zero only in electrostatic equilibrium; a time-varying field drives currents inside it.
  • In semiconductors both electrons and holes carry current, so 'the charge carrier' model of a conductor has to be replaced by a two-carrier picture.
  • Coulomb's law is the static, vacuum result; for moving charges the magnetic field joins in, and an accelerating charge radiates, so the static formula alone no longer describes the system.

Open questions and mysteries

Why is electric charge quantised, and can fractional charge ever be observed?

Open question

The Standard Model contains charge quantisation but does not derive it; quasiparticles with fractional charge do exist in certain condensed-matter states, which is a different phenomenon.

Is electric charge exactly conserved?

Under active scrutiny

Experiments constrain any violation to an extraordinarily small level; neutron-decay searches continue to sharpen that bound.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Explain why a charged rod attracts a neutral paper strip, and draw the induced polarisation on the strip.
  2. Predict the charge distribution on a hollow conducting sphere and on a sphere containing an off-centre cavity.
  3. Compute how many electrons correspond to a charge of 1 nC and comment on what that says about the quantisation of charge.
