Unit 10: Thermal Expansion

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Heat & Thermodynamics

Most solids expand when heated and water does something stranger. The coefficient of expansion is a property with a pedigree - it tracks bond strength - and it is the hidden variable in precision engineering, from railway gaps to cryogenic mirrors.

What the model asserts

  1. For modest temperature changes dL = alpha L dT; alpha is the linear coefficient of expansion and depends on material, temperature and direction.
  2. Expansion coefficients are broadly inversely related to bond strength, so stiff, high-melting-point materials such as fused silica expand far less than rubber or lead.
  3. Water is anomalous: it contracts on cooling from 100 degrees C to 4 degrees C and expands on freezing, which is why lakes freeze from the top down and pipes burst in winter.
  4. A bimetallic strip converts a temperature change into mechanical motion - the working principle of every household thermostat.
  5. The Gruneisen parameter links expansion to compressibility and heat capacity through alpha V K_s = gamma C_V.
  6. Gaps in rails, joints in bridges and expansion joints in concrete are all the same idea: the material will change size, so the structure must allow it.
  7. Thermal expansion comes from anharmonicity in the interatomic potential: the average separation between neighbours grows with temperature, and the expansion coefficient is that average increase per kelvin.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The linear law is a first-order approximation; it fails for large temperature changes, near phase transitions and for anisotropic materials such as graphite or wood.
  • For an isotropic solid the volume coefficient is approximately three times the linear coefficient, and the equality holds only in the small-expansion limit.
  • Apparent expansion of a liquid must subtract the container's own expansion, which for mercury in glass can be about 2 % of the reading.
  • As temperature approaches absolute zero the expansion coefficient vanishes, which is why cryogenic instruments use alloys such as Invar and Kovar whose expansion is nearly zero.
  • The linear expansion law is a first-order approximation for small temperature changes; a body cooling towards absolute zero needs the integrated form, because the coefficient itself varies with temperature.

Open questions and mysteries

Can thermal expansion be harnessed directly to produce useful electrical power?

Frontier

Thermal diodes, phase-change engines and flexoelectric effects are active research areas, but the efficiencies remain far below those of conventional heat engines.

Why does water expand when it freezes, when almost every other substance contracts?

Open question

Hydrogen bonds give ice an open lattice; the same anomaly is what lets a lake freeze from the top down.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Design a bimetallic strip switch and calculate the tip displacement for a given temperature change.
  2. Explain why a mercury thermometer left in a freezer shows a rise in reading rather than a fall.
  3. Compare the thermal design of the JWST's cryogenic mirror segments with a classroom thermometer and state the common principle.
