Unit 17: Lenses

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Waves & Optics

Lenses bend light to make images, and the thin-lens equation is the workhorse of the entire unit. What separates school physics from optical engineering is the list of assumptions that equation hides: thinness, paraxial rays, and a single refractive index.

What the model asserts

  1. The thin-lens equation is 1/f = 1/v - 1/u under the usual sign convention, and the lensmaker's equation gives 1/f = (n - 1)(1/R1 - 1/R2) for a lens in air.
  2. The power of a lens is P = 1/f in dioptres; for a lens immersed in a medium of index n_m it becomes (n_l/n_m - 1)(1/R1 - 1/R2).
  3. A converging lens is thicker at the centre and a diverging lens is thinner at the centre; for a real object a diverging lens always gives a virtual, upright, diminished image.
  4. In contact, P = P1 + P2; separated by a distance d, the equivalent power is 1/F = 1/f1 + 1/f2 - d/(f1 f2).
  5. The human eye is a roughly 17 mm lens with a focal length of about 2.3 cm; accommodation works by changing the lens curvature so the image always lands on the retina.
  6. Magnification m = v/u, and a real image is inverted and can be projected on a screen.
  7. A lens forms a real or virtual image depending on where the object sits relative to the focal length, and the thin-lens treatment hides the thick-lens corrections and aberrations that real objectives need.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The thin-lens equation requires a lens thin compared with its focal length and paraxial rays; real camera and telescope lenses are thick assemblies whose elements cancel aberrations.
  • Chromatic aberration means f depends on wavelength, so a single glass lens focuses blue and red at different distances; an achromatic doublet combines glasses of different dispersion to cancel it.
  • Spherical aberration grows with aperture, so stopping down a camera trades brightness for sharpness - until diffraction takes over.
  • Depth of field depends on aperture and focal length as well as on the sensor, so it is a property of the optical system, not of the camera body.
  • The lensmaker's equation assumes a thin lens in air; a thick or multi-element system is described by its principal planes and effective focal length instead.

Open questions and mysteries

How do observatories image exoplanets that are thousands of light-years away and a few pixels across?

Engineering frontier

Coronagraphs, adaptive optics and interferometry are combined to suppress starlight by many orders of magnitude, and each step adds its own noise.

Is there a fundamental limit to how small an optical element can be before diffraction dominates?

Open question

Abbe's limit sets the classical bound and super-resolution can cross it only in restricted ways; how far this can be pushed for imaging remains an active question.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Two thin lenses of focal length 10 cm and 15 cm are placed 5 cm apart: find the equivalent focal length and locate the image of an object 30 cm from the first lens.
  2. Design an achromatic doublet from two glass types and explain the trade-off between focal length, spherical aberration and cost.
  3. Explain why closing the aperture improves depth of field but introduces diffraction blur, and find the aperture that balances the two.
