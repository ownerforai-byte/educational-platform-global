Unit 6: Circular Motion

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Mechanics

Circular motion is where the vector treatment of force has to be taken seriously: the speed can be constant while the force is anything but, and the word 'centripetal' names a direction rather than a new kind of force.

What the model asserts

  1. Uniform circular motion has constant speed but constantly changing velocity, so the acceleration is a = v^2/r directed toward the centre.
  2. 'Centripetal force' is simply the name for the net inward force; it is supplied by tension, friction, gravity, magnetism or a normal reaction, not by an extra interaction.
  3. In an inertial frame there is no outward force; the 'centrifugal force' of a rotating frame is a bookkeeping pseudo-force.
  4. Banking a road removes the need for friction: the horizontal component of the normal reaction supplies the centripetal force.
  5. For a conical pendulum omega^2 = g/r; for a circular orbit v^2 = GM/r.
  6. The ground itself accelerates: Earth's rotation contributes about 0.034 m/s^2 at the equator, roughly 0.35 % of g, which is why effective g falls with latitude.
  7. In uniform circular motion the acceleration has constant magnitude but a continuously rotating direction, so the velocity vector changes even though its size does not.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The centripetal analysis assumes a circular path at constant speed; if the speed also changes, a tangential acceleration must be added to the radial one.
  • The maximum safe speed of a vehicle on a curve is set by the friction limit mu, not by the acceleration itself, and the banking angle shifts that limit.
  • Working in Earth's rotating frame requires fictitious forces; ignoring them underestimates the effective weight by about 0.35 % at the equator.
  • Orbital motion neglects drag, tidal perturbation and orbital precession, all of which matter for long-lived satellites.
  • The centrifugal force is real inside a rotating frame and fictitious in an inertial one; describing a rotating system only works if you say which frame you are using.

Common misconceptions — 3 to unlearn

“A body moving in a circle at constant speed has no acceleration because its speed is constant.”

Why it fails: Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, and the velocity is a vector: its direction changes continuously even when its magnitude does not. The acceleration is therefore centripetal and its magnitude is v^2/r.

Correct: In uniform circular motion the speed is constant but the velocity is not, so a = v^2 / r directed towards the centre. Acceleration is the rate of change of the whole velocity vector, not of its magnitude alone.

“A passenger in a turning car feels pushed to the outside by a centrifugal force.”

Why it fails: In the inertial frame of the road there is no outward force on the passenger. What the passenger feels is the reaction to the inward (centripetal) force that the car door or seat exerts on them - a real inward force, felt as an apparent outward push.

Correct: In an inertial frame the only horizontal force on the passenger is the inward contact force. The 'centrifugal force' is a fictitious force that appears only if you work in the rotating frame of the car, where it balances the centripetal force.

“In a banked curve the banking replaces gravity so that the car can turn without friction.”

Why it fails: Banking does not replace gravity. The normal reaction is tilted, and its horizontal component supplies the centripetal force while its vertical component balances the weight.

Correct: For a frictionless banked curve of angle theta, N sin theta = m v^2 / r and N cos theta = m g, so tan theta = v^2 / (r g). Friction is only needed when the speed differs from the design speed of the banking.

Workbook — 3 questions with answers

numerical

A stone is whirled in a horizontal circle of radius 0.50 m at 2.0 revolutions per second. Find its speed, its centripetal acceleration and the tension in the string (mass of stone 0.10 kg, string horizontal).

Answer: Angular speed = 2 x 2 pi = 12.6 rad/s. Speed v = omega r = 12.6 x 0.50 = 6.28 m/s. Centripetal acceleration a = v^2 / r = (6.28^2)/0.50 = 78.9 m/s^2. The only horizontal force is the tension, so T = m a = 0.10 x 78.9 = 7.89 N (about 7.9 N).

derivation

Derive the condition for a vehicle to travel round a banked circular road of radius r at speed v without any friction, where the road is banked at angle theta to the horizontal.

Answer: Let N be the normal reaction. In the vertical direction there is no acceleration, so N cos theta = m g. Horizontally the component N sin theta must provide the centripetal force, so N sin theta = m v^2 / r. Dividing the second equation by the first gives tan theta = v^2 / (r g). Note that the banking angle is fixed by the design speed: a vehicle slower or faster than v must use friction to follow the curve.

reasoning

A conical pendulum consists of a bob on a string making a constant angle with the vertical while it swings in a horizontal circle. Show that the string must make the same angle for every angular speed only if the radius is fixed, and derive the relation between the angle, the radius and g.

Answer: With the bob in circular motion of radius r, the tension T has a vertical component T cos theta balancing the weight and a horizontal component T sin theta supplying the centripetal force: T cos theta = m g and T sin theta = m omega^2 r. Dividing gives tan theta = omega^2 r / g, or omega^2 = g tan theta / r. For a fixed string length L, r = L sin theta, so omega^2 = g / (L cos theta): the angle changes with the angular speed, and the bob rises as the swing is made faster. A given angle corresponds to one r and one omega together.

Open questions and mysteries

Why do the outer parts of spiral galaxies rotate faster than their visible matter allows?

Open question

The standard explanation is an extended dark-matter halo, but the nature of that halo is unknown and no candidate particle has been confirmed (NASA).

Why do large, stable polygonal vortices form in rotating fluids, such as the hexagon on Saturn?

Open question

Laboratory experiments reproduce polygonal vortices but do not yet explain the range of sizes and their stability in full.
