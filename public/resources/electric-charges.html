Electric Charges and Charging by Induction

#Concept
1**Electric Charge & Elementary Charge:** Electric charge is an intrinsic physical property of elementary matter that governs all electromagnetic interactions. Charges exist in two polarities designated by Benjamin Franklin as positive (carried by protons, $+e$) and negative (carried by electrons, $-e$). The elementary charge quantum is $e \approx 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$. Charge is a scalar quantity with SI unit Coulomb ($\text{C} = \text{A}\cdot\text{s}$) and dimensions $[I\,T]$.
2**Quantization of Charge:** Electric charge is not continuous; it exists only in discrete integral multiples of the elementary electronic charge: $q = \pm n e$, where $n \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, \dots\}$. Quantization was first demonstrated macroscopically by Robert Millikan's oil-drop experiment (1909). At macroscopic scales involving microcoulombs, the graininess is obscured ($1\,\mu\text{C} \approx 6.24 \times 10^{12}$ electrons), allowing charge to be treated as continuous.
3**Conservation & Additivity of Charge:** In any isolated thermodynamic system, the algebraic sum of all electric charges remains strictly constant regardless of physical or chemical transformations: $\sum q_{initial} = \sum q_{final}$. In addition, charge is additive (total charge of an extended body is the scalar algebraic sum $\sum q_i$) and relativistically invariant (the magnitude of charge is independent of the reference frame speed, unlike mass).
4**Methods of Charging — Friction & Conduction:** Neutral matter is charged by electron transfer: (1) **Triboelectric effect (Friction):** When two differing materials (e.g., glass and silk, or amber and wool) are rubbed, work function differences drive electron transfer from the lower to higher work function material; (2) **Conduction (Contact):** Direct contact between a charged conductor and a neutral conductor allows electron flow until both bodies achieve identical electrostatic potentials.
5**Charging by Electrostatic Induction:** Charging by induction produces a net static charge on a conductor without physical contact with the charging source. Bringing a positively charged rod near an isolated neutral conductor repels positive ions conceptually while attracting mobile free conduction electrons toward the near face (polarization). Grounding the far face discharges positive potential by drawing electrons from Earth. Severing the ground connection before withdrawing the rod permanently traps an opposite negative charge on the conductor.
6**Gold-Leaf Electroscope & Charge Detection:** The gold-leaf electroscope is a classical electrostatic apparatus comprising a brass rod, brass disc, and two ultra-thin gold leaves housed in an evacuated glass enclosure. Divergence of the leaves signals the presence of charge via mutual electrostatic repulsion. The electroscope can identify charge sign by observing leaf divergence when an unknown charge is brought near a pre-charged electroscope.

Key Points

Point
Electric charge is quantized in integral multiples of $e = 1.602 \times 10^{-19}\,\text{C}$.
Charge is an isolated invariant scalar conserved across all nuclear and electromagnetic interactions.
Induction charges a conductor with the sign opposite to that of the inducing charged body without contact loss.
Grounding neutralizes electrostatic potential by allowing electron exchange with Earth's infinite charge reservoir.
Electrostatic attraction occurs between unlike charges and between any charged body and a neutral conductor via polarization.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Thinking induction creates new charge: Induction does not generate charge from nothing; it redistributes existing mobile valence electrons within the conductor while preserving net charge conservation of the combined system.
Believing grounding always pulls electrons down to Earth: If a positively charged rod is inducing negative charge near it, grounding pulls electrons UP from the Earth into the conductor. If a negative rod is near, electrons flow DOWN into the Earth.
Assuming insulators can be charged permanently by induction: Only materials with delocalized mobile charges (conductors) can be charged by induction and grounding. Insulators experience only molecular dielectric polarization.

Summary: Electric charge is a fundamental, quantized ($q = ne$), and conserved property of matter. Objects can be charged by triboelectric friction, conduction, or electrostatic induction. Charging by induction polarizes mobile electrons in an insulated conductor; momentary grounding allows electron exchange with Earth, permanently trapping an opposite net charge on the conductor without losing inducing charge.

Electric charges

#Concept
1**Nature of charge.** Charge is the property that produces electric force; like charges repel and unlike attract, and the SI unit is the coulomb (C).
2**Quantisation and conservation.** Charge is quantised in units of e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (q = ne) and is conserved — the net charge of an isolated system never changes.
3**Conductors and insulators.** Conductors (metals) allow charge to move freely, while insulators hold charge in place; excess charge resides on the outer surface of a conductor.
4**Charging methods.** Bodies are charged by friction (transfer of electrons), conduction (contact) and induction (redistribution without contact, followed by earthing).
5**Coulomb's law.** Two point charges q₁ and q₂ separated by r exert force F = (1/4πε₀)q₁q₂/r²; the constant 1/4πε₀ = 9 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻².

Coulomb's Law

#Concept
1**Coulomb's Law of Electrostatics:** Formulated by Charles-Augustin de Coulomb in 1785, the electrostatic force between two stationary point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them: $F = k \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2}$. The electrostatic force acts along the line connecting the centers of the two charges, being repulsive for like charges ($q_1 q_2 > 0$) and attractive for unlike charges ($q_1 q_2 < 0$).
2**Electrostatic Constant & Permittivity of Free Space:** The proportionality constant in vacuum/air is $k = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \approx 8.98755 \times 10^9\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2 \approx 9 \times 10^9\,\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{C}^2$. Here $\varepsilon_0$ denotes the electric permittivity of free space, with value $\varepsilon_0 = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{C}^2/(\text{N}\cdot\text{m}^2) = 8.854 \times 10^{-12}\,\text{F/m}$ and dimensions $[M^{-1} L^{-3} T^4 I^2]$.
3**Vector Formulation & Newton's Third Law:** In vector notation, the force $\vec{F}_{12}$ exerted on charge $q_1$ by charge $q_2$ located at position vector $\vec{r}_2$ is: $\vec{F}_{12} = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{|\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2|^3} (\vec{r}_1 - \vec{r}_2) = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \frac{q_1 q_2}{r^2} \hat{r}_{21}$. Because $\hat{r}_{12} = -\hat{r}_{21}$, it follows rigorously that $\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}$. Thus, electrostatic forces between two charges constitute an action-reaction pair satisfying Newton's Third Law in its strong central-force form.
4**Dielectric Medium & Relative Permittivity:** When point charges are embedded within an insulating medium of permittivity $\varepsilon$, dielectric polarization shields the charges, reducing the net force to $F_m = \frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon} \frac{|q_1 q_2|}{r^2} = \frac{F_0}{\varepsilon_r} = \frac{F_0}{\kappa}$. The dimensionless quantity $\kappa = \varepsilon_r = \frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_0} \ge 1$ is the dielectric constant (relative permittivity) of the medium (for vacuum $\kappa = 1$, dry air $\approx 1.0006$, water $\approx 80$, ideal conductor $\kappa \to \infty$).
5**Principle of Linear Superposition:** Electrostatic interaction between any pair of charges is completely unaffected by the presence of other surrounding charges. Consequently, the net electrostatic force on a test charge $q_0$ due to a discrete distribution of $N$ point charges $q_1, q_2, \dots, q_N$ is the vector sum: $\vec{F}_{net} = \sum_{i=1}^N \vec{F}_{0i} = \frac{q_0}{4\pi\varepsilon_0} \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{q_i}{|\vec{r}_0 - \vec{r}_i|^3} (\vec{r}_0 - \vec{r}_i)$.
6**Electrostatic Equilibrium & Earnshaw's Theorem:** A system of charges is in equilibrium if the net electrostatic force on each constituent charge vanishes ($\sum \vec{F} = 0$). By Earnshaw's theorem (derived from Gauss's law $\vec{\nabla} \cdot \vec{E} = 0$ in charge-free regions), a collection of stationary point charges cannot be maintained in a state of stable equilibrium solely under the influence of inverse-square electrostatic interactions.

Key Points

Point
Coulomb's law is an inverse-square central force acting along the line connecting two stationary point charges.
Vector formulation demonstrates mutual symmetry: $\vec{F}_{12} = -\vec{F}_{21}$, fulfilling Newton's third law.
Presence of a dielectric medium with relative permittivity $\kappa > 1$ reduces electrostatic force by a factor of $\kappa$.
The principle of superposition allows independent pairwise summation of forces in multi-charge configurations.
Earnshaw's theorem establishes that stable electrostatic equilibrium of stationary point charges is impossible without non-electrostatic constraints.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Adding electrostatic forces as scalar algebraic quantities: Electric forces are vectors! When calculating the net force exerted by multiple charges, one must resolve each pairwise force into Cartesian orthogonal components ($F_x, F_y$) before summing: $F_{net} = \sqrt{(\sum F_x)^2 + (\sum F_y)^2}$.
Ignoring the medium's dielectric screening: In water ($\kappa \approx 80$), the electrostatic force between ions drops to $\frac{1}{80}$ of its vacuum value, explaining why table salt ($NaCl$) spontaneously dissociates into hydrated $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$ ions in water.
Applying Coulomb's law to extended charge distributions at close range: Coulomb's inverse-square formula applies strictly to point charges or non-overlapping spherical charge distributions (via Shell Theorem). At close range, induction distorts charge distribution on real conductors.

Summary: Coulomb's Law quantifies the electrostatic force between two stationary point charges as directly proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation: F = k|q₁q₂|/r². In vector form, forces obey Newton's third law (F₁₂ = -F₂₁). When immersed in a dielectric medium of relative permittivity κ, the force reduces to F₀/κ. For multiple charges, the net force is determined via the principle of linear superposition by vector addition of all individual pairwise interactions.

Coulomb's law: force between two point charges

#Concept
1**Coulomb's law.** The force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance: F = (1/4πε₀)q₁q₂/r².
2**Direction.** The force acts along the line joining the charges — repulsive for like charges, attractive for unlike charges — and obeys Newton's third law.
3**Permittivity.** In a medium of dielectric constant K the force falls to F = q₁q₂/4πε₀Kr²; for vacuum K = 1 and ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² C² N⁻¹ m⁻².
4**Vector form.** F₁₂ = (q₁q₂/4πε₀) × (r₂ − r₁)/|r₂ − r₁|³ gives both magnitude and direction automatically, with the sign of the charges deciding attraction or repulsion.
5**Limitations.** The law holds for point charges at rest and for spherically symmetric charge distributions; it is an inverse-square law like gravitation but far stronger.

Force between multiple electric charges

#Concept
1**Superposition principle.** The force on any one charge due to several others equals the vector sum of the individual pairwise forces, each calculated as if the rest were absent.
2**Method.** Draw each Coulomb force on the chosen charge, resolve into components along convenient axes, sum the components, then combine for the net force.
3**Collinear charges.** For three charges on a line the middle charge feels opposing forces; the net force points toward the weaker side and vanishes only in a carefully balanced position.
4**Link to electric field.** The net force can be written F = qE, where E is the total field produced at the charge's position by all the other charges.
5**System energy.** The potential energy of a charge system is the sum of the pairwise terms kqᵢqⱼ/rᵢⱼ — counted once per pair, never twice.

Coulomb’s law models the force between point charges; superposition lets the vector contributions from several charges be added. In circuit problems, charge and energy conservation support Kirchhoff’s rules.

Conditions, assumptions & limits

Ohm’s law V = IR is a useful relation for ohmic materials over conditions where resistance is approximately constant. It is not universal: temperature changes and components such as diodes can produce non-ohmic behaviour.

Open research question — What is dark matter made of?

Astronomical evidence suggests that most matter does not interact strongly with light, yet its gravitational effects are measurable. Whether dark matter consists of new particles—and how those particles interact with ordinary matter—remains open; the point-charge electrostatic model remains reliable in its own domain.
