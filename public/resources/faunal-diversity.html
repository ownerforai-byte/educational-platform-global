Faunal Diversity — Animal Kingdom Classification

#Concept
1**Protista:** unicellular eukaryotes. Includes protozoa (animal-like), algae (plant-like) and slime moulds (fungus-like).
2**Protozoa characteristics:** unicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic, locomotion by cilia, flagella or pseudopodia, reproduce by binary fission or multiple fission.
3**Classification:** (1) Mastigophora (flagella: Euglena, Trypanosoma), (2) Sarcodina (pseudopodia: Amoeba, Entamoeba), (3) Ciliophora (cilia: Paramecium, Vorticella), (4) Sporozoa (spores, parasitic: Plasmodium, Toxoplasma).
4**Paramecium caudatum:** slipper-shaped, ciliated, two contractile vacuoles for osmoregulation, trichocysts for defence, binary fission and conjugation reproduction.
5**Plasmodium vivax:** malarial parasite. Complex life cycle: mosquito injects sporozoites → liver stage (exo-erythrocytic) → blood stage (erythrocytic schizogony) → gametocytes in blood → ingested by mosquito → sexual stage in gut → sporozoites migrate to salivary glands.
6**Euglena gracilis:** mixotrophic protist with a prominent stigma (eyespot) for light detection, pellicle for shape flexibility, single contractile vacuole, and both asexual (longitudinal binary fission) and sexual reproduction under adverse conditions.
7**Trypanosoma brucei:** causative agent of African sleeping sickness (kala-azar in India by T. lewisi variant). Transmitted by tsetse fly (Glossina). Has kinetoplast DNA, undulating membrane for motility, and antigenic variation (VSG switching) to evade host immune response.
8**Amoeba proteus:** shapeless due to pseudopodia used for locomotion and phagocytosis. Food vacuoles form around ingested bacteria; contractile vacuole expels excess water. Reproduces by binary fission under favourable conditions, and by encystment (cyst formation) under hostile environments.
9**Entamoeba histolytica:** causative agent of amoebic dysentery. Lives in human large intestine. Trophozoite stage is invasive (invades intestinal mucosa), cyst stage is infective. Passes out in faeces; infection occurs via contaminated water and food.
10**Vorticella:** bell-shaped ciliate on a contractile stalk. Colonial or solitary in freshwater. Uses oral cilia (adoral zone of membranelles) to create water currents for feeding. Contractile stalk coil contracts rapidly for escape response.
11**Sporozoa (Apicomplexa):** entirely parasitic, no locomotory organelles in adult stage. All possess an apical complex used for penetrating host cells. Plasmodium (malaria), Toxoplasma gondii (toxoplasmosis), and Eimeria (coccidiosis) are important genera.
12**Protista cell structure:** all protists have a eukaryotic cell with membrane-bound nucleus, mitochondria, endoplasmic reticulum, and Golgi apparatus. Some (Euglena) have chloroplasts for photosynthesis. Cell covering varies: pellicle (Euglena), no fixed covering (Amoeba), or cilia-covered cell membrane (Paramecium).
13**Plasmodium life cycle — sexual vs asexual:** Asexual reproduction (schizogony) occurs in the human host (liver and RBCs). Sexual reproduction (sporogony) occurs in the female Anopheles mosquito gut after gametes fuse to form an ookinete, which develops into an oocyst releasing sporozoites.
14**Protozoan reproduction strategies:** binary fission (Amoeba, Paramecium), multiple fission/schizogony (Plasmodium in liver), budding (Vorticella), and conjugation (Paramecium — temporary union for genetic exchange, not reproduction per se).
15**Ecological role of Protista:** Euglena and dinoflagellates are primary producers in aquatic ecosystems. Foraminiferans and radiolarians contribute to marine sediments (chalk/limestone formation). Amoebas are important bacterial regulators in soil and aquatic microbiomes.
16**Dinoflagellates (Class Dinoflagellata):** mostly marine plankton. Two flagella (one transverse, one longitudinal) in grooves. Some have cellulose plates (theca). Responsible for red tides (bioluminescence). Examples: Gonyaulax (toxic), Ceratium, Peridinium.
17**Diatoms (Class Bacillariophyceae):** unicellular algae with silica cell walls (frustule = two halves). Major primary producers in oceans. Fossil diatoms form diatomaceous earth. Centric (radial symmetry) and pennate (bilateral) forms.
18**Slime moulds:** fungus-like protists. Cellular slime moulds (Dictyostelium) exist as individual amoeboid cells that aggregate into a pseudoplasmodium (slug) during food scarcity, then form a fruiting body. Acellular slime moulds (Physarum) form a multinucleate plasmodium.
19**Chrysophytes and Euglenoids:** Chrysophytes include golden algae and diatoms, stored food as oils and chrysolaminarin. Euglenoids have proteinaceous pellicle, can be photosynthetic or heterotrophic, and store paramylon (a carbohydrate).
20**Parasitic protozoa in India:** Plasmodium vivax, P. falciparum, and P. malariae cause malaria. Leishmania donovani causes kala-azar. Entamoeba histolytica causes amoebiasis. Trypanosoma causes sleeping sickness (rare in India but important globally).
21**Paramecium nuclear dimorphism:** Paramecium has two types of nuclei — the macronucleus (controls everyday metabolic functions and is polyploid) and the micronucleus (controls reproduction and is diploid). During conjugation, the micronucleus undergoes meiosis to form gametic nuclei; during binary fission, the macronucleus divides amitotically while the micronucleus divides mitotically.
22**Contractile vacuole osmoregulation:** freshwater protozoa like Amoeba and Paramecium constantly take in water by osmosis because their cytoplasm is hypertonic to the surrounding medium. The contractile vacuole collects excess water from the cytoplasm via radial canals and periodically expels it to the exterior, preventing cell lysis. In marine forms, contractile vacuoles are usually absent.
23**Phagotrophy and food digestion in protozoa:** Amoeba feeds by phagocytosis — pseudopodia surround a food particle to form a food vacuole. Lysosomes fuse with the food vacuole releasing hydrolytic enzymes (proteases, lipases, amylases) for intracellular digestion. Digestive products diffuse into the cytoplasm; indigestible material is egested through the cell membrane (cytoproct).
24**Locomotion mechanisms in protozoa:** (1) Pseudopodia — temporary cytoplasmic projections in Sarcodina; flow of cytoplasm into the projection creates movement (Amoeba). (2) Cilia — hair-like organelles covering the body; coordinated beating produces locomotion and feeding currents (Paramecium). (3) Flagella — long whip-like structures; undulating waves propel the cell (Euglena, Trypanosoma). (4) Gliding — some protozoa move by secreting mucus trails.
25**Plasmodium — clinical stages of malaria:** (1) Incubation period (8-30 days depending on species) — sporozoites develop in liver. (2) Clinical stage — merozoites burst from RBCs, causing cyclic fevers (every 48h in P. vivax, 72h in P. malariae, irregular in P. falciparum). (3) Complications — P. falciparum causes cerebral malaria, blackwater fever, and severe anaemia. (4) Radical cure — only P. vivax and P. ovale have hypnozoites (dormant liver stages) requiring primaquine for complete cure.
26**Protozoan cyst formation — adaptive significance:** When environmental conditions become adverse (desiccation, temperature extremes, food scarcity), many protozoa encyst. The trophozoite retracts extensions, secretes a thick protective wall, and becomes metabolically inactive. Cysts are resistant to desiccation, chemicals, and stomach acid. Excystation occurs when conditions improve, producing active trophozoites. This is crucial for transmission of intestinal parasites like Entamoeba.
27**Kinetoplastida (Order Kinetoplastida):** A group of flagellated protozoa characterised by a single large mitochondrion containing a mass of DNA called the kinetoplast. Includes Trypanosoma (causes sleeping sickness and Chagas disease) and Leishmania (causes kala-azar and cutaneous leishmaniasis). They have a single flagellum attached by an undulating membrane. Vector-borne: tsetse fly for Trypanosoma, sandfly for Leishmania.
28**Ciliophora — structural complexity of Paramecium:** Beyond the nucleus and contractile vacuole, Paramecium has: (1) Cytostome (cell mouth) — a permanent oral groove leading to a cytopharynx; (2) Cytoproct (cell anus) — site of egestion; (3) Trichocysts — elongated organelles just beneath the pellicle that can be discharged as defensive or offensive threads; (4) Oral membranelles — thickened cilia that sweep food into the cytostome; (5) Anal pore — opens near the oral groove for waste expulsion.
29**Protistan nutritional diversity:** Protista exhibit diverse nutritional modes — photoautotrophic (diatoms, dinoflagellates, euglenoids), heterotrophic (protozoa), mixotrophic (Euglena combines photosynthesis and phagocytosis), and parasitic (Plasmodium, Trypanosoma, Entamoeba). Some dinoflagellates are bioluminescent and can form red tides when nutrient-rich conditions trigger blooms.
30**Diatom frustule and diatomaceous earth:** Diatoms have a unique silica cell wall (frustule) consisting of two overlapping halves (epitheca and hypotheca). Centric diatoms (e.g., Coscinodiscus) show radial symmetry; pennate diatoms (e.g., Navicula) show bilateral symmetry and some possess a raphe for gliding. Upon death, their silica shells accumulate as diatomaceous earth — a highly porous sediment used for filtration, abrasives, and insulating materials.
31**Slime moulds — cellular vs acellular:** Cellular slime moulds (Dictyostelium) exist as individual amoeboid cells that aggregate upon starvation into a multicellular slug (pseudoplasmodium), then form a fruiting body with spores. Acellular slime moulds (Physarum) form a giant multinucleate plasmodium that crawls over decaying matter, engulfing bacteria and organic particles. Both demonstrate differentiation and communication without nervous systems.
32**Dinoflagellate features and red tides:** Dinoflagellates (Class Dinoflagellata) are mostly marine, biflagellate protists with one transverse and one longitudinal flagellum. Many have cellulose plates (theca) and are bioluminescent. Some species (Gonyaulax, Alexandrium) produce toxins (saxitoxin, brevetoxin) that accumulate in shellfish, causing paralytic shellfish poisoning in humans. Mass blooms cause 'red tides' — harmful algal blooms that deplete oxygen and kill fish.
33**Protista vs bacteria in phylogeny:** Protists are eukaryotes with membrane-bound organelles, linear chromosomes with histones, and 80S ribosomes — fundamentally different from bacteria (prokaryotes, 70S ribosomes, circular DNA). However, many protists have acquired bacterial genes through endosymbiosis or horizontal gene transfer. The endosymbiotic theory posits that mitochondria and chloroplasts originated from free-living bacteria engulfed by ancestral eukaryotes.
34**Symbiotic protists:** Many protists form mutualistic symbioses — zooxanthellae (Symbiodinium, dinoflagellates) live in coral tissues and provide photosynthates in exchange for shelter and nutrients; trichonymphids (flagellates) in termite guts digest cellulose; ciliates in ruminant stomachs help break down plant material. These symbioses are ecologically critical for reef ecosystems and nutrient cycling.
35**Endosymbiotic theory and protist evolution:** The origin of eukaryotes involved primary endosymbiosis (cyanobacterium → chloroplast) in the ancestor of Archaeplastida (red algae, green algae, plants). Secondary endosymbiosis (engulfment of a red or green alga by a non-photosynthetic eukaryote) gave rise to dinoflagellates, apicomplexans, diatoms, and euglenoids with complex plastids surrounded by 3-4 membranes.
36**Foraminiferans and radiolarians in sediment formation:** Foraminiferans (order Foraminifera) secrete calcareous (CaCO₃) chambers that form the basis of thick marine limestone deposits (e.g., the White Cliffs of Dover). Radiolarians (subclass Radiolaria) have intricate siliceous (SiO₂) skeletons that form radiolarian ooze on the deep ocean floor. Both are important index fossils for biostratigraphy.
37**Vascular plant endosymbiont — mycorrhizae analogy:** While not protists, some slime moulds and chytrid fungi (formerly grouped with protists) are now understood to be closely related to nucleariids. The former kingdom Protista is paraphyletic; modern classification (SAR supergroup: Stramenopiles, Alveolates, Rhizarians) reflects true evolutionary relationships uncovered by molecular phylogenetics.
38**Kinetoplastida — Trypanosoma and Leishmania pathogenicity:** The Order Kinetoplastida (subgroup of Euglenozoa) includes Trypanosoma and Leishmania, both characterised by a single large mitochondrion with a condensed mass of kinetoplast DNA (kDNA). Trypanosoma brucei causes African sleeping sickness (tsetse fly vector); T. cruzi causes Chagas disease (triatomine bug vector). Leishmania donovani causes visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar); L. major causes cutaneous leishmaniasis. Both exhibit extensive RNA editing of kDNA transcripts.
39**Paramecium — conjugation as genetic exchange:** In Paramecium, conjugation involves two individuals temporarily joined at their oral regions. The macronucleus degenerates; the micronucleus undergoes meiosis to form 4 haploid micronuclei; 3 degenerate, and 1 divides twice to produce 3 more (total 4). One migratory and one stationary gametic nucleus are formed in each; they exchange migratory nuclei. After fusion (syngamy), each exconjugant undergoes fission to restore macronuclear function. This sexual process increases genetic diversity without increasing population size.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
**Protista is a single group of organisms.** — Protista is a polyphyletic kingdom-level group; it contains very diverse organisms (algae, protozoa, slime moulds) that are grouped together mainly because they are unicellular eukaryotes, not because they share close evolutionary relationships.
**Binary fission and conjugation in Paramecium are both reproduction.** — Binary fission is asexual reproduction producing new individuals. Conjugation is a sexual process for genetic exchange between two individuals; it does not increase the number of organisms and is therefore NOT reproduction.
**Plasmodium reproduces only in mosquitoes.** — Plasmodium undergoes asexual reproduction (schizogony) extensively in the human host (liver and blood cells). Sexual reproduction (sporogony) occurs only in the mosquito vector.
**All protozoa are harmful parasites.** — Most protozoa are free-living and ecologically beneficial (e.g., Amoeba regulates bacterial populations, Euglena contributes to photosynthesis). Only a few species are parasitic (Plasmodium, Entamoeba, Trypanosoma).
**Euglena is a plant or an animal.** — Euglena is neither a plant nor an animal; it is a protist with mixotrophic nutrition. It has chloroplasts for photosynthesis (plant-like) but lacks a cell wall and can also feed heterotrophically (animal-like).
**Contractile vacuoles are found in all protozoa.** — Contractile vacuoles are present only in freshwater protozoa that need osmoregulation. Marine protozoa generally lack contractile vacuoles because the external environment is isotonic or hypertonic to their cytoplasm.
**The cyst stage is always the active feeding stage of a protozoan.** — The cyst stage is a dormant, non-feeding, protective stage. The trophozoite is the active, feeding, and reproducing stage. Cysts are important for survival and transmission but do not feed or grow.
**All algae are protists.** — Not all algae are protists. Green algae (Chlorophyta) and red algae (Rhodophyta) are now classified in the kingdom Plantae; blue-green algae (cyanobacteria) are bacteria. Only brown algae (Phaeophyceae), diatoms (Bacillariophyceae), and dinoflagellates remain in Protista — highlighting that 'algae' is a polyphyletic term.
**Plasmodium has flagella in its life cycle.** — Plasmodium (Sporozoa) is largely non-motile in all stages except for the microgametes (male gametes) in the mosquito gut, which are briefly flagellated. In the human host, all stages (sporozoites, merozoites, trophozoites, gametocytes) are non-flagellated and move by gliding or intracellular growth.
**Protozoa are single-celled so they cannot have complex behaviour.** — Single-celled protozoa exhibit complex behaviours: Paramecium can avoid obstacles (axonal reflex), learn to associate stimuli (habituation), and communicate during conjugation. Amoeba can solve mazes and store information. These behaviours are mediated by ion channels and cytoskeletal dynamics, not neurons.
**Viruses are classified as protists.** — Viruses are not protists (or even living organisms). They lack cellular structure, cannot carry out metabolism independently, and require a host cell to replicate. They are not included in the Five-Kingdom classification system at all, standing outside the domains of life.

Summary: Protozoa are animal-like, mostly unicellular eukaryotes classified by their locomotion into four classes: Sarcodina (pseudopodia, e.g. Amoeba), Mastigophora (flagella, e.g. Trypanosoma), Ciliophora (cilia, e.g. Paramecium), and Sporozoa (non-motile spores, e.g. Plasmodium). Paramecium and Amoeba are model organisms. Plasmodium (malaria) with its Anopheles vector is a key NEB examination topic. Protozoa are the most diverse single-celled eukaryotes in aquatic environments.

Protista and Protozoa

#Concept
1Protista: eukaryotic, mostly unicellular (some colonial); the "kingdom of convenience" for single-celled eukaryotes.
2Protozoa (animal-like protists): heterotrophic, motile; grouped by locomotion — Sarcodina (Amoeba, pseudopodia), Mastigophora (Trypanosoma, flagella), Ciliophora (Paramecium, cilia), Sporozoa (Plasmodium, non-motile spores).
3Amoeba proteus: pseudopodia for movement + food; contractile vacuole; no definite shape.
4Paramecium caudatum: ciliated, oval, oral groove → food vacuole; two nuclei (macro + micronucleus); contractile vacuoles for osmoregulation.
5Plasmodium (malaria): sporozoan; life cycle involves Anopheles mosquito (vector) and human; causes malaria.
6Algae and fungi-like protists also belong to Protista in the five-kingdom system.
7NEB focus: Protozoa outline, classification up to class, diagnostic features, and examples.

Key Points

Point
Protista: eukaryotic, mostly unicellular; animal/plant/fungus-like.
Protozoa = animal-like; locomotion defines the 4 classes.
Sarcodina (Amoeba) — pseudopodia; Ciliophora (Paramecium) — cilia.
Mastigophora (Trypanosoma) — flagella; Sporozoa (Plasmodium) — non-motile spores.
Paramecium has two nuclei (macro + micro) and contractile vacuoles.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Protists are eukaryotes (have a nucleus); they are NOT bacteria (prokaryotes).
Protozoa are animal-like; algae are plant-like; slime moulds are fungus-like — all are in Protista.
Amoeba moves by pseudopodia (no flagella/cilia); Paramecium by cilia; Trypanosoma by flagella.
Sporozoa (Plasmodium) are non-motile in their adult/infective (spore) stage; they do not have flagella/cilia/pseudopodia.
Paramecium has TWO nuclei: a large macro-nucleus (metabolic) and a small micro-nucleus (reproductive).
Contractile vacuole in freshwater protozoa expels excess water (osmoregulation); it is not a "digestive" vacuole.

Summary: Protozoa are animal-like, mostly unicellular eukaryotes classified by their locomotion into four classes: Sarcodina (pseudopodia, e.g. Amoeba), Mastigophora (flagella, e.g. Trypanosoma), Ciliophora (cilia, e.g. Paramecium), and Sporozoa (non-motile spores, e.g. Plasmodium). Paramecium and Amoeba are model organisms. Plasmodium (malaria) with its Anopheles vector is a key NEB examination topic. Protozoa are the most diverse single-celled eukaryotes in aquatic environments.

Animalia: Phyla Classification

#Concept
1Levels of organisation: cellular (sponges) → tissue → organ system (most animals).
2Body symmetry: asymmetry (sponges), radial (cnidarians, echinoderms), bilateral (most animals).
3Body cavity: acoelom (no cavity, flatworms), pseudocoelom (partly lined, nematodes), coelom (fully lined, most higher animals).
4Segmentation: annelids, arthropods, chordates (metamerism).
5Phylum classification (up to class): Porifera (sponges), Cnidaria (coelenterates — jellyfish, Hydra), Platyhelminthes (flatworms), Nematoda (roundworms), Annelida (earthworms, leeches), Arthropoda (insects, crustaceans, arachnids), Mollusca (snails, octopus, clams), Echinodermata (starfish), Chordata (vertebrates + tunicates).
6Chordata diagnostic features: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, post-anal tail (present at some stage).
7Vertebrate classes: Pisces (fishes), Amphibia (frogs), Reptilia (reptiles), Aves (birds), Mammalia (mammals).
8NEB focus: level of organisation, symmetry, body cavity, segmentation, and phylum classification up to class.

Key Points

Point
Levels: cellular (Porifera) → tissue (Cnidaria) → organ system (higher).
Body cavity: acoelom, pseudocoelom, coelom.
Symmetry: asymmetry, radial, bilateral.
Segmentation in Annelida, Arthropoda, Chordata.
9 phyla: Porifera, Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes, Nematoda, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata.
Chordate: notochord, dorsal nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, post-anal tail.
Vertebrate classes: Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Acoelomates have NO body cavity; pseudocoelomates have a cavity not fully lined by mesoderm; coelomates have a fully mesoderm-lined cavity.
Cnidaria are radial (adult) but their larvae may be bilateral; echinoderms are radial in adults but bilateral in larvae (secondary radial symmetry).
Segmentation (metamerism) is seen in annelids, arthropods, and chordates — NOT in sponges or cnidarians.
Porifera (sponges) have a cellular level of organisation (no true tissues); Cnidaria have tissue-level organisation.
Nematodes (roundworms) are pseudocoelomates; Annelida are coelomates.
All chordates have a notochord at some stage; in vertebrates it is replaced by the vertebral column.

Summary: Animals are classified by level of organisation (cellular to organ system), body symmetry (asymmetry, radial, bilateral), body cavity (acoelom, pseudocoelom, coelom), and segmentation. The animal kingdom spans 9 major phyla from Porifera (cellular level) to Chordata (organ system level). Chordates are defined by a notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, and a post-anal tail; they include the 5 vertebrate classes. NEB examinations focus on phylum classification up to class and the chordate diagnostic features.

Earthworm

#Concept
1Pheretima posthuma (earthworm): annelid (segmented worm); ~10–15 cm long; ~100–150 segments.
2External features: clitellum (segments 15–31 in Pheretima) for reproduction; setae (bristles) on each segment for anchoring; chaetae; prostomium (first segment, sensory).
3Body wall: cuticle + circular and longitudinal muscles; coelom (true body cavity) with coelomic fluid; septa between segments.
4Habitat: moist soil; night-active; breathes through the skin (cutaneous respiration).
5Significance: aerates soil, adds humus, improves soil structure and fertility; a key invertebrate studied in NEB.
6Taxonomy: Animalia > Annelida > Clitellata > Haplotaxida (Lumbricidae) > Pheretima.
7NEB focus: external anatomy, general body plan, and habitat of Pheretima.

Key Points

Point
Pheretima: annelid, ~10–15 cm, ~100–150 segments.
Clitellum (segments 15–31): saddle-like; forms the cocoon.
Setae on each segment: bristles for anchoring in peristaltic movement.
Body plan: coelom (true body cavity), circular + longitudinal muscles, septa.
Cutaneous respiration through moist skin; night-active; lives in moist soil.
Hermaphrodite (both sexes); cross-fertilisation.
Soil benefit: aeration, humus, structure, fertility.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Earthworms are Annelids, NOT arthropods; they have a soft body with setae, not a hard exoskeleton.
The clitellum is a thickened, saddle-like region used to form the cocoon — it is NOT a reproductive organ itself.
Earthworms have a closed circulatory system (blood vessels + heart-like vessels), not an open one.
Respiration is through the moist skin (cutaneous); there are no gills or lungs.
Earthworms are hermaphrodites (both male and female organs) but exchange sperm with another worm (cross-fertilisation).
Segments are called "metameres"; each has a set of setae for anchoring during locomotion.

Summary: Pheretima (earthworm) is a model annelid: a segmented worm with ~100–150 metameres, a clitellum (segments 15–31) for cocoon formation, and setae on each segment for peristaltic locomotion. It has a true coelom with septa, circular and longitudinal muscles, and breathes through its moist skin. Earthworms are hermaphrodites that cross-fertilise. They are ecologically vital: they aerate soil, build humus, and improve soil structure and fertility. NEB focuses on external anatomy, body plan, and habitat.

Economic importance of P. falciparum

#Concept
1[Remember] Plasmodium falciparum is the protozoan causing malignant (cerebral) malaria — the deadliest human malaria, transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes; Nepal's Terai is the endemic zone.
2[Remember] Life cycle in two hosts: asexual schizogony in humans (liver then red blood cells) and sexual sporogony in mosquitoes; P. falciparum shows accole/several ring forms and crescent (banana-shaped) gametocytes.
3[Understand] Harmful importance: high fever with chills, haemolysis and anaemia, cerebral malaria, blackwater fever; resistant strains and drug resistance make it medically the most significant species.
4[Understand] Diagnosis and control value: blood-smear study of P. falciparum teaches parasite structure; control — DDT/oil larvicides, bed nets, drainage, chloroquine/artemisinin therapy and surveillance in Terai districts.

#Concept

Frog — Digestive, Circulatory and Respiratory Systems

#Concept
1Digestive system: mouth (tongue) → buccal cavity → oesophagus → stomach → intestine (small + large) → rectum → cloaca; liver and gall bladder; pancreas; bile for fat digestion.
2Circulatory system: 3-chambered heart (2 atria + 1 ventricle) → double circulation; oxygenated + deoxygenated blood partially mixed in the ventricle; systemic + pulmonary circuits.
3Heart structure: right atrium (deoxygenated from body), left atrium (oxygenated from lungs/skin), single ventricle (partially mixed blood); ventricle has a spongy myocardium (crista ventriculi) that reduces mixing.
4Respiratory system: lungs (pulmonary), moist skin (cutaneous), buccal cavity (buccal); tadpoles use gills.
5Physiology of respiration: inhalation (buccal floor lowers → air enters through nostrils), exhalation (buccal floor rises → air pushed into lungs); skin respiration is significant in water and at night.
6Working mechanism of the heart: atria contract first (fill ventricle), then ventricle contracts (pump blood to lungs + body); valves (atrioventricular, conus valves) prevent backflow.
7NEB focus: digestive system, blood vascular system (heart structure + working), respiratory system (organs + physiology).

Key Points

Point

Respiratory: lungs + skin (cutaneous) + buccal cavity; tadpole gills.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
The frog heart is 3-chambered (2 atria + 1 ventricle), NOT 4-chambered like mammals.

The cloaca is the common exit for digestive, excretory, and reproductive systems.

4: Animalia: Phyla ClassificationAnimals are classified by level of organisation (cellular to organ system), body symmetry (asymmetry, radial, bilateral), body cavity (acoelom, pseudocoelom, coelom), and segmentation. Chordates are defined by a notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, and a post-anal tail; they include the 5 vertebrate classes. 6: EarthwormPheretima (earthworm) is a model annelid: a segmented worm with ~100–150 metameres, a clitellum (segments 15–31) for cocoon formation, and setae on each segment for peristaltic locomotion. It has a true coelom with septa, circular and longitudinal muscles, and breathes through its moist skin. Earthworms are hermaphrodites that cross-fertilise. 8: Earthworm — Excretory, Nervous and Reproductive SystemsThe earthworm's internal systems are well adapted to its soil-dwelling life. The digestive system (buccal cavity → crop → gizzard → intestine with typhlosole) processes decaying matter. The closed circulatory system has 5–6 heart-like vessels. The excretory system uses metanephridia (intersegmentary, septal, genital). The nervous system (cerebral ganglion + ventral nerve cord) coordinates peristaltic movement. Reproduction is hermaphroditic with cross-fertilisation and cocoon formation by the clitellum. Earthworms are ecologically and agriculturally important.
9: FrogRana tigrina is the model amphibian for NEB. It is semi-aquatic, living near water but spending most of the time on land. Its external features — moist, glandular, scaleless skin; a tympanum; a nictitating membrane; 4-digit forelimbs and 5-digit webbed hindlimbs — reflect its amphibious life. The tadpole has a tail and gills; the adult loses the tail and gains lungs. These adaptations make frogs both efficient land-and-water animals and sensitive bio-indicators of environmental change.

13: Frog — Digestive, Circulatory and Respiratory SystemsThe frog's digestive system runs from the mouth (with a sticky tongue) to the cloaca, with the liver, gall bladder, and pancreas aiding digestion. 15: Frog — Reproductive SystemThe frog's reproductive system is adapted for external fertilisation in water. Frogs are ecologically important as pest controllers and water-quality bio-indicators. Academic Extension: Evidence, Conditions & Open Questions

Established academic fact

Ecosystems contain interacting organisms and non-living conditions. Energy flows through food webs and is dissipated as heat, while elements such as carbon and nitrogen cycle; biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.

Conditions, assumptions & limits

Population or ecosystem trends depend on place, season, sampling method and time scale. Distinguish a measured observation from a projection, and report uncertainty rather than treating one survey as a complete census.

Open research question — How Himalayan ecosystems respond to rapid change

Changes in snow and ice across the Hindu Kush Himalaya are documented, but timing and local consequences for water, habitats and particular species remain uncertain. Scenario-based projections are not guaranteed outcomes; monitoring and stating the scenario are essential.
