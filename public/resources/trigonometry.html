Trigonometric ratios and identities

#Concept
1### 1. Basic Trigonometric Ratios
2In a right triangle with angle theta:
3- sin theta = opposite/hypotenuse
4- cos theta = adjacent/hypotenuse
5- tan theta = opposite/adjacent = sin theta/cos theta
6- cosec theta = 1/sin theta, sec theta = 1/cos theta, cot theta = 1/tan theta
7### 2. Key Values
8| theta | 0 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 90 |
9| sin theta | 0 | 1/2 | 1/sqrt(2) | sqrt(3)/2 | 1 |
10| cos theta | 1 | sqrt(3)/2 | 1/sqrt(2) | 1/2 | 0 |
11| tan theta | 0 | 1/sqrt(3) | 1 | sqrt(3) | undefined |
12### 3. Fundamental Identities
13- sin^2 theta + cos^2 theta = 1
14- 1 + tan^2 theta = sec^2 theta
15- 1 + cot^2 theta = cosec^2 theta
16### 4. Compound Angle Formulas
17- sin(A +/- B) = sin A cos B +/- cos A sin B
18- cos(A +/- B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
19- tan(A +/- B) = (tan A +/- tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B)
20### 5. Double Angle Formulas
21- sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
22- cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A = 2cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2sin^2 A
23- tan 2A = 2tan A/(1 - tan^2 A)
24### 6. Entrance Exam Focus
25Prove identities; Solve trig equations; Use compound/double angle formulas.

Key Points

Point
sin(A + B) is not sin A + sin B; the compound angle formula must be used instead.
In the cosine formula the signs are opposite, so cos(A + B) = cos A cos B - sin A sin B while cos(A - B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B.
cos 2A has three forms, and the useful one is the one matching what you are given: 2cos^2 A - 1 if you know cos A, or 1 - 2sin^2 A if you know sin A.
tan 2A = 2 tan A/(1 - tan^2 A), not 2 tan A, and it is undefined when tan A = ±1.
tan θ = sin θ/cos θ requires cos θ != 0, which is why tan 90° is undefined while sin 90° = 1 and cos 90° = 0.

Summary: The six trigonometric ratios come from a right triangle: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and tan θ = sin θ/cos θ, with cosec, sec and cot their reciprocals, and their special values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90° are tabulated. Three fundamental identities tie the ratios together: sin^2 θ + cos^2 θ = 1, 1 + tan^2 θ = sec^2 θ and 1 + cot^2 θ = cosec^2 θ. From these come the compound angle formulas sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B, cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B and tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B), and the double angle formulas sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A and cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A.

Established academic fact

Sine and cosine encode the coordinates of a point moving around the unit circle, which explains their periodicity and the identity sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. In right-triangle problems the same ratios describe side lengths for acute angles.

Conditions, assumptions & limits

Check whether angles are measured in degrees or radians. Calculus identities and many formulas assume radians; trigonometric arguments must be dimensionless, and inverse-trigonometric functions return principal values unless a broader solution set is requested.

Open research question — The Riemann Hypothesis

This unsolved conjecture concerns the non-trivial zeros of the zeta function and has deep connections to the distribution of prime numbers. Checking many cases computationally cannot replace a proof for all cases.
