Work Done by Constant and Variable Force

#Concept
1**Work done by a constant force:** W = F·d·cos θ = Fd cos θ, where F is the magnitude of the force, d is the displacement, and θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors. Work is a scalar quantity (SI unit: joule, J = N·m). Work is positive when the force has a component in the direction of displacement (θ < 90°), negative when opposite (θ > 90°), and zero when perpendicular (θ = 90°).
2**Work done by a variable force:** when force varies with position, work is the area under the F-x graph, or mathematically, W = ∫ F(x) dx from x₁ to x₂. For a spring force F = −kx, the work done by the spring when stretched from x₁ to x₂ is W = ½k(x₁² − x₂²).
3**Work-energy theorem:** the net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy: W_net = ΔK = ½mv² − ½mu². This is one of the most powerful results in mechanics — it connects forces (through work) to motion (through kinetic energy).
4**Work done by gravity:** W_g = mgΔh = mg(h_i − h_f), where Δh is the change in height. Gravity does positive work when the body falls (h decreases) and negative work when it rises (h increases). The work done by gravity is independent of the path — only the vertical displacement matters.
5**Work done by a spring:** W_s = ½kx² (work done ON the spring to stretch/compress it by x). The spring does work W = ½k(x_i² − x_f²) when it returns from x_i to x_f. At the natural length (x = 0), the spring does maximum work.
6**Work done by friction:** W_f = −f_k·d = −μ_k·N·d. Friction always does negative work (it opposes motion), converting mechanical energy into heat. The work depends on the path length d, not just the displacement — friction is non-conservative.
7**Zero work situations:** (1) Force perpendicular to displacement (e.g., normal force on a horizontal surface, centripetal force in circular motion). (2) No displacement (pushing a wall). (3) Force applied to a body that doesn't move.
8**NEB exam focus:** work done by constant force (W = Fd cos θ), work done by a spring (W = ½kx²), work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK), area under F-x graph for variable forces, and identifying zero-work situations.

Key Points

Point
Work is a scalar — the dot product of force and displacement (W = Fd cos θ).
Work is positive when the force helps motion, negative when it opposes motion, zero when perpendicular.
The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) connects forces to motion — one of the most powerful results in mechanics.
Gravity is conservative — work depends only on vertical displacement, not on the path.
Friction is non-conservative — work depends on the path length and is always negative (for kinetic friction).

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
❌ 'Work and energy are different concepts.' ✅ Work is a TRANSFER of energy. When you do work on a body, you transfer energy to it. The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) makes this precise.
❌ 'Work is a vector because force and displacement are vectors.' ✅ Work is a SCALAR — it is the dot product of force and displacement (W = F·d·cos θ). It has magnitude but no direction.
❌ 'If a body moves, work must be done.' ✅ Work is done only if the force has a component along the displacement. Centripetal force does zero work in circular motion (always perpendicular to velocity).
❌ 'Work done by gravity depends on the path.' ✅ Gravity is a conservative force — work depends only on the vertical displacement (mgΔh), not on the path taken.
❌ 'A spring does work W = ½kx² when stretched by x.' ✅ The work done ON the spring to stretch it is ½kx². The work done BY the spring when it returns is also ½kx² (energy is recovered).
❌ 'Friction always does negative work.' ✅ Static friction can do positive work — e.g., the static friction on your feet pushes you forward when walking. Kinetic friction always does negative work.

Summary: Work (W = Fd cos θ for constant force, W = ∫F dx for variable force) is the transfer of energy by a force. The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) connects forces to motion. Gravity (conservative) and friction (non-conservative) are the two most important forces in work problems. Spring work (W = ½kx²) is a standard NEB topic.

Power

#Concept
1Power is the rate of energy transfer: average P=ΔW/Δt and instantaneous P=dW/dt. Its SI unit is watt, 1 W=1 J s⁻¹.
2For translation, dW=F⃗·dr⃗ gives P=F⃗·v⃗=Fv cosθ. Only the force component parallel to velocity transfers energy; a perpendicular centripetal force does no work in uniform circular motion.
3Worked example: a 200 kg load rises steadily at 0.50 m s⁻¹. The lifting force is mg=1960 N for g=9.8 m s⁻², so useful power is Fv=980 W. In 10 s the load rises 5 m and gains 9800 J, confirming P=W/t=980 W.
4At constant speed, net power is zero because engine power balances resistive forces. For rotation, P=τ⃗·ω⃗; efficiency η=Pout/Pin distinguishes useful output from input.
5A 50 N force at 60° to a crate moving at 3.0 m s⁻¹ transfers energy at P=50×3×cos60°=75 W, not 150 W.

Key Points

Point
Use the force component parallel to velocity.
At constant speed net power is zero though a motor may deliver positive power.
State whether input or output power is requested.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Do not use P=Fv unless force and velocity are parallel or include cosθ.
Motor output is not the net power on a constant-speed vehicle.

Summary: Power measures energy-transfer rate. For translation it is the force–velocity dot product; practical machines distinguish useful output from losses.

Work-Energy Theorem

#Concept
1**Work done by a constant force:** $W = Fd \cos \theta$, where $F$ is the magnitude of the force, $d$ is the displacement, and $\theta$ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors. Work is a scalar quantity (SI unit: joule, $J = N\cdot m$). Work is positive when the force has a component in the direction of displacement ($\theta < 90^\circ$), negative when opposite ($\theta > 90^\circ$), and zero when perpendicular ($\theta = 90^\circ$).
2**Work done by a variable force:** when force varies with position, work is the area under the $F-x$ graph, or mathematically, $W = \int_{x_1}^{x_2} F(x) \, dx$. For a spring force $F = -kx$, the work done by the spring when stretched from $x_1$ to $x_2$ is $W = \frac{1}{2}k(x_1^2 - x_2^2)$.
3**Work-energy theorem:** the net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy: $W_{net} = \Delta K = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 - \frac{1}{2}mu^2$. This is one of the most powerful results in mechanics — it connects forces (through work) to motion (through kinetic energy).
4**Work done by gravity:** $W_g = mg\Delta h = mg(h_i - h_f)$, where $\Delta h$ is the change in height. Gravity does positive work when the body falls ($h$ decreases) and negative work when it rises ($h$ increases). The work done by gravity is independent of the path — only the vertical displacement matters.
5**Work done by a spring:** $W_s = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$ (work done ON the spring to stretch/compress it by $x$). The spring does work $W = \frac{1}{2}k(x_i^2 - x_f^2)$ when it returns from $x_i$ to $x_f$. At the natural length ($x = 0$), the spring does maximum work.
6**Work done by friction:** $W_f = -f_k\cdot d = -\mu_k\cdot N\cdot d$. Friction always does negative work (it opposes motion), converting mechanical energy into heat. The work depends on the path length $d$, not just the displacement — friction is non-conservative.
7**Zero work situations:** (1) Force perpendicular to displacement (e.g., normal force on a horizontal surface, centripetal force in circular motion). (2) No displacement (pushing a wall). (3) Force applied to a body that doesn't move.
8**NEB exam focus:** work done by constant force ($W = Fd \cos \theta$), work done by a spring ($W = \frac{1}{2}kx^2$), work-energy theorem ($W_{net} = \Delta K$), area under $F-x$ graph for variable forces, and identifying zero-work situations.

10Understanding work-energy theorem is essential for solving problems and building higher-level mathematical skills.
11Key definitions and theorems related to work-energy theorem should be memorized and practiced regularly.
