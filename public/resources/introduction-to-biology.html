Introduction to Biology — Scope and Fields

#Concept
1Biology: the study of living organisms (Greek: bios = life, logos = study).
2Scope: from molecules (DNA, proteins) to cells, tissues, organs, organisms, populations, communities, ecosystems, and the biosphere.
3Branches: Botany (plants), Zoology (animals), Microbiology (microorganisms), Genetics (heredity), Ecology (organism-environment), Physiology (function), Anatomy (structure), Taxonomy (classification), Biotechnology (application).
4Characteristics of life: cellular organisation, metabolism, homeostasis, growth, reproduction, response to stimuli, evolution, and adaptation.
5Scientific method: observation → question → hypothesis → experiment → data → conclusion → (publish/peer review).
6Biology is an experimental science; it uses controlled experiments, measurement, and statistical analysis.
7NEB focus: definition, scope, fields (branches), and characteristics of life.

Relation of Biology with Other Sciences

#Concept
1Biology is interconnected with other sciences: it relies on and contributes to Chemistry (biochemistry), Physics (biophysics), Mathematics (biostatistics), and Earth Science (geobiology).
2Biochemistry: the chemistry of life — metabolism, enzymes, DNA/RNA/protein structures.
3Biophysics: physics applied to biology — biophysics of the cell, nerve conduction, muscle mechanics, imaging (MRI, X-ray).
4Biostatistics: mathematics used in biology — genetics (Punnett), population models, experimental design.
5Geobiology: interaction of life with Earth — evolution (fossils, climate), ecology (biogeochemical cycles), palaeontology.
6Interdisciplinary fields: medicine (biology + chemistry + physics), agriculture (biology + ecology), conservation biology (biology + policy), biotechnology (biology + engineering).
7NEB focus: how biology connects with chemistry, physics, mathematics, and earth science.
8Biology uses scientific tools from other disciplines: microscopes (physics), spectrometers (chemistry), statistical software (mathematics).
9The exploration of life science involves knowledge of other basic sciences like Physics and Chemistry.
10The combination of these disciplines contributes to a comprehensive perspective.
11Relation with Chemistry The physiological processes of plants and animals are intricately linked with chemical reactions.
12Biochemistry, situated at the intersection of Biology and Chemistry, is the discipline that addresses this intersection.

14Living organisms contain both organic and inorganic compounds, which play a crucial role in shaping various life processes.
15Energy is transferred within the body through organic chemicals like glucose.
16Chemical changes are integral to metabolic pathways, including photosynthesis, respiration, and metabolism.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Biochemistry is NOT the same as organic chemistry; it focuses on the chemistry of living systems (metabolism, enzymes, macromolecules).
Biophysics applies physics to biological problems; it is not "physics of life" in a vague sense.
Biostatistics is a branch of statistics, not a branch of biology; it is used BY biologists.
Geobiology (biology + earth science) is different from ecology (biology + environment).
Interdisciplinary fields combine multiple sciences; they are NOT a new "fourth" science.
Biology uses tools from other sciences (microscopes, spectrometers) but has its own methods (experiments, observations, classifications).
