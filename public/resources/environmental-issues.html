Air, water, and solid waste pollution

#Concept
1### 1. Air Pollution
2- **Primary pollutants**: Directly emitted (SO2, NOx, CO, particulates)
3- **Secondary pollutants**: Formed in atmosphere (ozone, acid rain)
4- **Sources**: Vehicles, industries, burning fossil fuels, volcanic eruptions
5- **Effects**: Respiratory diseases, acid rain, smog, global warming
6- **Control**: Catalytic converters, scrubbers, renewable energy, afforestation
7### 2. Water Pollution
8- **Point source**: Single identifiable source (factory pipe)
9- **Non-point source**: Diffuse runoff (agricultural chemicals)
10- **Organic waste**: Sewage, food processing - increases BOD
11- **Eutrophication**: Excess nutrients -> algal bloom -> oxygen depletion
12- **Heavy metals**: Mercury (Minamata disease), Lead (neurotoxic)
13- **Control**: Wastewater treatment, reduce chemical use, maintain riparian zones
14### 3. Solid Waste Management
15- **Biodegradable**: Food waste, paper, garden waste - compostable
16- **Non-biodegradable**: Plastic, glass, metal - recycling needed
17- **Hazardous**: Medical waste, batteries, electronics - special disposal
18- **Methods**: Landfilling, incineration, composting, recycling, 3Rs (Reduce, Reuse, Recycle)
19- **E-waste**: Rapidly growing problem with toxic components
20### 4. Acid Rain
21- Caused by SO2 and NOx reacting with water vapor
22- Forms sulfuric and nitric acid in rain
23- Damages buildings, aquatic ecosystems, forests, soil
24- pH less than 5.6 is considered acid rain
25### 5. Greenhouse Effect and Global Warming
26- **Greenhouse gases**: CO2, CH4, N2O, CFCs, water vapor
27- **Enhanced greenhouse effect**: Human activities increase GHG concentrations
28- **Effects**: Rising temperatures, sea level rise, extreme weather, melting glaciers
29- **Kyoto Protocol (1997)**: International agreement to reduce GHG emissions
30- **Paris Agreement (2015)**: Limit warming to well below 2°C above pre-industrial
31### 6. Ozone Layer Depletion
32- **Ozone (O3)**: Protects Earth from harmful UV radiation
33- **CFCs (Chlorofluorocarbons)**: Release chlorine atoms that destroy ozone
34- **Ozone hole**: Over Antarctica, discovered in 1985
35- **Montreal Protocol (1987)**: Phased out CFCs successfully
36- **Effects of depletion**: Skin cancer, cataracts, immune suppression, crop damage
37### 7. Entrance Exam Focus
38Causes and effects of pollution types; Greenhouse gases; Ozone depletion mechanism.

Key Points

Point
Sources of air pollution are vehicles, industries, the burning of fossil fuels, brick kilns and volcanic eruptions.
Control of air pollution uses catalytic converters (CO to CO2 and NOx to N2), scrubbers that remove SO2, low-sulphur fuels, afforestation and renewable energy.
Eutrophication sequence: nutrient enrichment leads to an algal bloom, which blocks light, and the dead algae are decomposed by bacteria, raising BOD and lowering dissolved oxygen until fish die.
Heavy-metal pollutants are mercury, which caused Minamata disease, lead, which is neurotoxic, and cadmium, which damages the kidney; all are bioaccumulated along the food chain.
The 3Rs, Reduce, Reuse and Recycle, are preferred to landfilling, and composting of biodegradable waste returns nutrients to the soil.
E-waste contains lead, mercury and cadmium, so it must be collected and treated separately from ordinary municipal solid waste.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
x Ozone is always bad. x Stratospheric ozone protects us; ground-level ozone is a pollutant.
x Global warming = ozone depletion. x Different problems with different causes.
x CO2 is the only greenhouse gas. x CH4, N2O, CFCs are also significant.

Summary: Pollution of air, water and land has distinct sources, effects and control measures. Air pollutants are classified as primary, such as SO2, NOx, CO and particulates emitted directly, and secondary, such as ground-level ozone and acid rain, which form in the atmosphere. Water pollution from point and non-point sources causes organic pollution with a raised BOD, eutrophication and heavy-metal toxicity, while solid waste is managed by the 3Rs, landfilling, incineration and composting. At global scale, greenhouse gases drive the enhanced greenhouse effect, and ozone-depleting CFCs cause ozone-layer depletion, addressed by the Kyoto Protocol, the Paris Agreement and the Montreal Protocol respectively.

Established academic fact

Ecosystems contain interacting organisms and non-living conditions. Energy flows through food webs and is dissipated as heat, while elements such as carbon and nitrogen cycle; biodiversity includes genetic, species and ecosystem diversity.

Conditions, assumptions & limits

Population or ecosystem trends depend on place, season, sampling method and time scale. Distinguish a measured observation from a projection, and report uncertainty rather than treating one survey as a complete census.

Open research question — How Himalayan ecosystems respond to rapid change

Changes in snow and ice across the Hindu Kush Himalaya are documented, but timing and local consequences for water, habitats and particular species remain uncertain. Scenario-based projections are not guaranteed outcomes; monitoring and stating the scenario are essential.
