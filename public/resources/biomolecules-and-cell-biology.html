Biomolecules: Classification, Structure & Physiological Functions

#Concept
1Biomolecules are organic molecules essential to life: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, plus minerals, enzymes and water.
2Carbohydrates (CH2O)n: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, ribose), disaccharides (sucrose=glucose+fructose, lactose, maltose), polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose). Provide energy (4 kcal/g) and structural support.
3Proteins: polymers of 20 L-amino acids joined by peptide (-CO-NH-) bonds. Four structural levels (primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary). Functions: enzymes, transport (haemoglobin), contraction, defence, structural.
4Lipids: hydrophobic; triglycerides (fat storage, 9 kcal/g), phospholipids (membrane bilayer), steroids (cholesterol, hormones). Not true polymers.
5Nucleic acids: DNA (double helix, deoxyribose, A=T 2 H-bonds, G=C 3 H-bonds) stores heredity; RNA (single strand, ribose, A=U) in protein synthesis (mRNA, tRNA, rRNA).
6Enzymes: biological catalysts (mostly proteins); work by the lock-and-key / induced-fit model; denatured by high temperature or extreme pH; follow -S- (substrate) specificity.
7Water (major solvent, ~70% of cell) and minerals (Ca, P, K, Na, Fe, I) act as structural, catalytic and osmotic regulators; deficiency causes deficiency diseases.

Key Points

Point
Functions: C=energy+structure, P=enzymes/transport/structure, L=storage+membrane, N=heredity+protein synthesis.
A hydrolysis breaks a bond + water; a condensation joins molecules - water.
Enzymes are specific, reversible regulators, not consumed in the reaction.
DNA is the gene; RNA is the messenger/scaffold in protein synthesis.
Essential amino acids (8 in adults, 9 in children) must come from food.
Deficiency of minerals leads to named diseases (Fe→anaemia, I→goitre, Ca→rickets).

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
Cellulose is a structural polymer in plant cell walls, not an energy reserve; glycogen (animal) and starch (plant) are the storage forms.
Starch is unbranched amylose + branched amylopectin; both yield maltose on hydrolysis.
Ribose and deoxyribose differ only by one oxygen atom on C-2; hence RNA vs DNA sugars.
Denaturation unfolds protein (loss of secondary/tertiary structure); it is often reversible at low temperature.
Lipids are not polymers; they do not have repeating monomers like the other three macromolecule classes.
A peptide bond forms between the -COOH of one amino acid and the -NH2 of the next, releasing one water (condensation).

Summary: Biomolecules are the chemical basis of life. Carbohydrates supply and store energy and form structural fibres; proteins (from 20 amino acids) act as enzymes, transporters, contractile and defence molecules in four structural levels; lipids store energy and build membranes; nucleic acids (DNA/RNA) store and express genetic information; enzymes, water and minerals complete the set. Mastery of structure-function links and enzyme kinetics is a frequent NEB examination focus.

11### 1. Biomolecules Overview
12Biomolecules are organic compounds produced by living organisms. They are classified into four major groups: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and nucleic acids. Minerals and water are inorganic biomolecules essential for life.
13### 2. Carbohydrates
14- **Monosaccharides**: Simple sugars - glucose (C6H12O6), fructose, galactose
15- **Disaccharides**: Two monosaccharides - sucrose (glucose+fructose), lactose (glucose+galactose), maltose (glucose+glucose)
16- **Polysaccharides**: Many monosaccharides - starch (energy storage), glycogen (animal starch), cellulose (structural)
17- Functions: Energy source, structural support, cell recognition
18### 3. Proteins
19- Built from amino acids (20 types) linked by peptide bonds
20- **Primary structure**: Sequence of amino acids
21- **Secondary structure**: Alpha helix, beta sheet (hydrogen bonding)
22- **Tertiary structure**: 3D folding (disulfide bridges, hydrophobic interactions)
23- **Quaternary structure**: Multiple polypeptide chains (hemoglobin)
24- Functions: Enzymes, structural support, transport, immunity, signaling
25### 4. Lipids
26- **Fats and oils**: Triglycerides (glycerol + 3 fatty acids)
27- **Phospholipids**: Cell membrane component (hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tail)
28- **Steroids**: Cholesterol, hormones (testosterone, estrogen)
29- **Waxes**: Water-proofing
30- Functions: Energy storage (9 kcal/g), membrane structure, insulation, hormones
31### 5. Nucleic Acids
32- **DNA**: Deoxyribonucleic acid - double helix, stores genetic information
33- **RNA**: Ribonucleic acid - single strand, protein synthesis
34- Monomers: Nucleotides (sugar + phosphate + nitrogenous base)
35- DNA bases: Adenine, Guanine, Cytosine, Thymine
36- RNA bases: Adenine, Guanine, Cytosine, Uracil
37### 6. Enzymes
38- Biological catalysts (mostly proteins, some RNA = ribozymes)
39- Lower activation energy, not consumed in reaction
40- **Lock and key model**: Active site fits specific substrate
41- **Induced fit model**: Active site adjusts to substrate
42- Factors affecting enzyme activity: temperature, pH, substrate concentration, inhibitors
43### 7. Water
44- Universal solvent, high specific heat capacity, cohesion and adhesion
45- Essential for all metabolic reactions, transport, temperature regulation
46### 8. Minerals
47- **Macrominerals**: Ca, P, K, Na, Cl, Mg, S (needed in large amounts)
48- **Microminerals**: Fe, I, Zn, Cu, Mn, Se, F (trace amounts)
49- Functions: Bone formation, oxygen transport, nerve transmission, enzyme cofactors

51MCQs: Identify biomolecule types; Enzyme properties; Carbohydrate classification; Protein structure levels.

Cell — Prokaryotic and Eukaryotic

#Concept
1Cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow): all organisms are made of one or more cells; the cell is the basic structural and functional unit; every cell arises from a pre-existing cell.
2Cell = the smallest unit of life; bounded by the plasma membrane; size typically 1-100 µm.
3Prokaryotic cell: no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles; 70S ribosomes; e.g. bacteria, cyanobacteria; smaller (1-10 µm).
4Eukaryotic cell: true nucleus with a nuclear envelope, membrane-bound organelles, 80S ribosomes; e.g. plant and animal cells.
5Key differences: nucleus, organelles, chromatin vs naked DNA, cell size, ribosome size, cytoskeleton.
6Basic cell components: plasma membrane, cytoplasm (cytosol + organelles), genetic material, and (in prokaryotes) cell wall, capsule, pili, flagella.

#Concept
1DETAILED ORGANELLE ANALYSIS (plant + animal cell).
2Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer (heads out, tails in) + integral proteins (channels, pumps, receptors) + peripheral proteins + cholesterol + glycocalyx (cell recognition). Fluid-mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972).
3Mitochondrion: double membrane; outer smooth, inner folded into cristae; matrix with Krebs enzymes; own DNA + 70S ribosomes (endosymbiosis); aerobic respiration: glycolysis (cytoplasm) → Krebs (matrix) → ETS (cristae) → ~34 ATP.
4Chloroplast: double membrane; thylakoids stacked into grana; stroma with Calvin-cycle enzymes; chlorophyll a + b; own DNA + 70S ribosomes; light reactions (grana → ATP, NADPH, O2) + Calvin cycle (stroma → glucose).
5Nucleus: nuclear envelope (4 nm, two membranes, pores); nucleoplasm; chromatin (DNA + histone proteins); nucleolus (rRNA + ribosome assembly).
6Rough ER (RER): ribosomes on outer surface → protein synthesis (secretory + membrane proteins); continuous with nuclear envelope.
7Smooth ER (SER): lipid synthesis, carbohydrate metabolism, detoxification (liver), Ca2+ storage (muscle — sarcoplasmic reticulum).
8Golgi apparatus: cisternae + vesicles; modifies (glycosylation), packages, sorts, and secretes proteins; forms lysosomes + cell plate (plants).
9Lysosomes: 8–12 nm vesicle, hydrolytic enzymes (acidic pH); digest food particles, worn organelles (autophagy); "suicide bags" when damaged.
10Vacuole: plant — large central (turgor, storage); animal — small (excretion, osmoregulation, contractile in protozoa).
11Cytoskeleton: microfilaments (actin), intermediate filaments, microtubules (tubulin); shape, movement, intracellular transport, division (spindle).
12Centrioles (animal): microtubule rings; form the spindle during mitosis; absent in most plants.
13Cell inclusions: storage granules (starch, fat, glycogen), pigments (leuco-, chro-, heterochromoplasts), crystalloids.
14Cell wall (plant): cellulose microfibrils (primary wall) + pectin; secondary wall with lignin; fully permeable; provides rigidity + protection.

Common Confusions

❌ / ✅
RER makes PROTEIN; SER makes LIPID. A cell secreting lots of protein has abundant RER + Golgi (e.g., pancreatic acinar cell).
The Golgi does NOT synthesise proteins; it MODIFIES, SORTS, and PACKAGES what the RER made.
Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their OWN DNA + 70S ribosomes — the key endosymbiosis evidence.
Lysosomes are animal-cell organelles; plants use the large vacuole for hydrolytic digestion.
Cilia and flagella share the 9+9 axonemal microtubule structure; centrioles (9+0) are related but lack the central pair.
The nucleus is NOT the only place with DNA: mitochondria + chloroplasts carry their own genomes.

Summary: A detailed examination of the eukaryotic cell shows how each membrane-bound organelle specialises a metabolic function. The fluid-mosaic plasma membrane controls transport; mitochondria and chloroplasts convert energy (and carry their own DNA, evidence of endosymbiosis); RER makes protein while SER makes lipid; the Golgi modifies and packages; lysosomes digest; the nucleus and nucleolus control heredity; and the cytoskeleton (microfilaments, microtubules) maintains shape and drives division. The protein-secretion pathway (ribosome → RER → Golgi → vesicle → membrane) and the plant/animal cell contrast are core NEB written-answer topics.
