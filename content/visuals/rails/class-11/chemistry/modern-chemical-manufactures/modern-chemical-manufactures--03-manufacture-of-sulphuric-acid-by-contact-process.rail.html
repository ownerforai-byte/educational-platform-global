The Contact process makes the king of acids in four stages: burn sulphur to SO₂, purify the gas exhaustively for the V₂O₅ catalyst's sake, oxidise 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ at 450 °C and barely any pressure (the equilibrium is already ~99% converted — the deliberate contrast with Haber's bought pressure), and absorb the SO₃ in 98% acid to oleum because direct water would boil off an uncondensable fog.…

Deep Conceptual — Why Oleum and Not Water: The Fog Problem - Absorbing SO₃ directly into water is thermodynamically fine but engineering-wise a disaster: the reaction is violently exothermic and the product leaves the gas–liquid interface as a mist of tiny H₂SO₄ droplets that is nearly impossible to condense — it escapes with the exit gas as acid fog.… · ## 4. Deep Conceptual — Why the Pressure Is Low: Reading an Equilibrium Economically - 2SO₂ + O₂ → 2SO₃ reduces 3 mol to 2 mol, so pressure does favour the product — yet the plant runs at 1–2 atm. The reason: at 450 °C with excess air the equilibrium conversion is already ~99% without added pressure, and pressure equipment for a marginal 1% is uneconomic.… · ## 5.
