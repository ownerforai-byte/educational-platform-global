Key Points

  • Geometrical isomerism arises from restricted rotation around C=C double bonds or rings.
  • Optical isomerism requires a chiral center with four different substituents.
  • Both are forms of stereoisomerism: same connectivity, different spatial arrangement.
  • Cis/trans notation describes relative positions across a rigid bond.
  • Enantiomers (d/l) rotate plane-polarized light equally but in opposite directions.
  • Meso compounds have stereocenters but are achiral due to internal symmetry.
  • Not every alkene shows geometric isomerism: each C of C=C must bear two different groups.
  • Tautomerism (keto-enol) is dynamic structural isomerism, not stereo.
  • E/Z system assigns priority by atomic number for complex alkenes.
  • These concepts underpin drug design: enantiomers can have opposite biological effects.

Specific Conditions

  • Geometrical isomerism requires restricted rotation (double bond or ring).
  • Each carbon of the C=C must carry two different groups.
  • Optical isomerism requires at least one chiral (asymmetric) carbon.
  • A meso compound has stereocenters but an internal mirror plane.
  • 1-butene cannot show geometric isomerism (two H on terminal C).

Important Statements

  • Same molecular formula + different spatial arrangement = stereoisomers.
  • Restricted rotation is the prerequisite for geometrical isomerism.
  • A chiral center has four different groups bonded to it.
  • Enantiomers are non-superimposable mirror images.
  • Resonance delocalizes electrons and stabilizes molecules like benzene.

Important Notes

  • Do not confuse conformational isomerism (free rotation) with configurational (restricted).
  • Racemic mixtures are optically inactive despite containing chiral molecules.
  • Lassaigne test converts covalent N/S/halogens to ionic forms for detection.
  • Benzene equal C-C bond lengths (1.39 A) prove delocalization.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Count geometric isomers: n different double bonds gives up to 2^n (minus symmetric cases).
  • Chiral center? Check sp3 carbon with 4 different groups instantly.
  • Meso? Look for a line of symmetry cutting through stereocenters.
  • E/Z: higher atomic number = higher priority; same side = Z, opposite = E.

Exam Notes

  • NEB typically asks define + one example (2-3 marks).
  • Comparison tables score well in 5-mark long answers.
  • Always state conditions of applicability alongside definitions.

Examples

  • cis-2-butene vs trans-2-butene: CH3 groups same/opposite side of C=C.
  • D-lactic acid vs L-lactic acid: mirror-image enantiomers.
  • meso-tartaric acid: two stereocenters, internal mirror plane, optically inactive.
  • Ozone resonance: O=O(+)-O(-) <-> O(-)-O(+)=O, bent with equal bonds.

Practice Questions

  • How many geometric isomers does 2-pentene have? Draw them.
  • Why does propene NOT exhibit geometric isomerism?
  • Identify the chiral center in 2-bromobutane.
  • Explain why meso compounds are optically inactive.

Practice

  • Draw cis and trans forms of 2-hexene and label substituents.
  • Given C4H8O structures, classify each as chain/position/functional/geometric isomer.
  • Build flashcards: definition -> condition -> example -> exception.

Universal Facts

  • Isomers share identical molecular formulas.
  • Stereoisomers differ only in 3D arrangement, not connectivity.
  • Enantiomers have identical physical properties except optical rotation direction.
  • Diastereomers (including cis/trans) have different physical properties.
  • This topic is assessed yearly in NEB Class 11 Chemistry boards.

Confusion

  • Confusing geometric (cis/trans) with optical (d/l): different symmetry requirements.
  • Assuming all C=C bonds give geometric isomers: need 2 different groups per C.
  • Mixing constitutional isomers with stereoisomers: connectivity vs space.
  • Thinking any molecule with stereocenters is chiral: meso compounds are exceptions.

Summary

  • Geometrical isomerism (cis/trans) stems from restricted rotation; optical isomerism (d/l) from chiral centers.
  • Both are stereoisomer subtypes critical for understanding reactivity and biological activity.
  • Meso compounds show that stereocenters alone do not guarantee chirality.
  • Mastering these enables prediction of physical properties and pharmaceutical outcomes.
