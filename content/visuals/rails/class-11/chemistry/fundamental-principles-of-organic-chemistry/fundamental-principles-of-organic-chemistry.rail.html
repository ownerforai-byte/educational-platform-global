Title

Resonance and isomerism — same atoms, different molecules

Stat Key

Rows

1.
Label

Concept

Text

Same molecular formula, different arrangement = isomers: structural (chain/position/functional/metamerism/tautomerism) vs stereo (cis–trans across rigid double bonds; d/l optical twins around chiral centres). Resonance delocalises π electrons over contributing structures — the real molecule is their weighted hybrid, stabilised beyond any single drawing. Lassaigne fuses the compound with Na to unmask N/S/halogens as CN⁻/S²⁻/X⁻. Fission splits bonds homolytically (radicals) or heterolytically (electrophile seeks electrons, nucleophile brings them); inductive (±I) and resonance (±R) effects steer them.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

resonance hybrid energy < any contributor · benzene: two Kekulé rings ⇄ one 6π delocalised ring · ozone: O=O⁺–O⁻ ⇄ O⁻–O⁺=O (bent, equal bonds) · Lassaigne: Na + C + N → NaCN (Prussian blue test) · Na + S → Na₂S · IUPAC ≤ C6: CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ = butan-2-ol

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Geometrical isomerism needs restricted rotation AND two different groups per carbon (2-butene qualifies; 1-butene cannot). Optical isomerism needs a chiral centre (four different groups) — meso forms with internal compensation stay optically dead. Resonance contributors must share atom positions (electrons only move); Lassaigne needs complete fusion.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Tautomerism (keto⇌enol) is dynamic isomerism — interconverting in solution. Benzene's equal 1.39 Å bonds prove delocalisation (alternating single/double would differ). Butan-2-ol's CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ numbering gives −OH the lowest locant (NEB 2024).

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Resonance in benzene: draw both Kekulé hexagons with alternating double bonds, then the circle-in-hexagon hybrid — consequences: all C–C bonds equal, extra stability (~150 kJ/mol resonance energy), substitution preferred over addition (NEB 2023, 3-mark pattern; ozone's bent pair drawn identically). IUPAC: longest chain 4 with −OH at C2 → butan-2-ol (NEB 2024, 2 marks).

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Contributing structures are fictions — no single Kekulé form exists. Resonance explains stability, not reaction rates alone (kinetics needs orbitals/transition states). Lassaigne fails for volatile/low-melting samples that escape fusion; N-detection fails if S also present without the FeSO₄/FeCl₃ discrimination steps.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

From overlap: adjacent p-orbitals merge π clouds across conjugated systems; the delocalised wavefunction's energy sits below any localised contributor by the resonance energy (measured via heats of hydrogenation vs predicted). Isomer counting follows from graph enumeration: C₄H₁₀ gives n-butane + 2-methylpropane (chain); C₄H₈ adds position/geometrical branches.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Isomer type? Ask: same connectivity? No → structural (which part moved: skeleton/position/group?). Yes → stereo (double bond? cis-trans; chiral centre? optical). Electrophile = electron-lover (H⁺, Br⁺, carbocation); nucleophile = electron-giver (OH⁻, CN⁻, carbanion).

9.
Label

Board question

Text

NEB 2024 (2): IUPAC — butan-2-ol from CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃. NEB 2023 (3): resonance — structures of ozone and benzene. Lassaigne steps, isomerism types and ±I/±R effects rotate yearly.
