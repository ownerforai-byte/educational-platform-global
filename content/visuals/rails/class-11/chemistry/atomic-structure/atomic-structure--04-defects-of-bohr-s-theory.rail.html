Bohr's model is a classical–quantum hybrid, and its defects are the seams: it works only for one-electron species because it ignores electron–electron repulsion and shielding; it cannot explain fine structure (no l or s), the Zeeman and Stark line splittings (no mₗ), or line intensities; and its exact orbits violate the uncertainty principle, which replaces the electron track with a probability orbital.…

Quick concepts

Text

Each defect points to a missing quantum number: fine structure → l; Zeeman/Stark → mₗ; spin splitting → mₛ; multi-electron failure → shielding. The quantum model adds exactly these.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Defects: fails for multi-electron atoms; no fine structure; no Zeeman (magnetic) or Stark (electric) splitting; orbits violate the uncertainty principle; cannot explain dual nature; no line-intensity rule; circular orbits only. Bohr's surviving truths: quantised levels, hν = ΔE, 13.6 eV ionisation, Rydberg constant.

6. Common Confusions Zeeman vs Stark: Zeeman = magnetic field splitting; Stark = electric field splitting. Both are line splittings, both unexplained by Bohr — but the fields differ. · Fine structure vs Zeeman: fine structure appears without any field (spin/relativistic splitting); Zeeman needs an external magnetic field. · "Bohr failed completely" — no. It succeeded for one-electron species and introduced quantised levels; its defects are specific and each maps to a missing quantum number.
