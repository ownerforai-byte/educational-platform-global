Conditions

Text

❌ The atom is 'totally empty'. ✅ It is mostly empty space, but ≈99.9% of the mass lies in the nucleus. · ❌ α-particles are electrons. ✅ They are helium nuclei (+2e, ≈7300 times heavier than an electron), so electrons cannot deflect them appreciably.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

α-scattering experiment: 1909–1911; Geiger and Marsden counted ≈1 flash in 10⁴ at large angles; gold foil ≈4 × 10⁻⁵ cm thick. · Rutherford received the 1908 Nobel Prize in Chemistry for work on radioactivity.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Problem: Find r₀ for a 5.0 MeV α-particle on gold (Z = 79). Solution: KE = 5.0 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹³ = 8.0 × 10⁻¹³ J; r₀ = (2 × 9×10⁹ × 79 × (1.6×10⁻¹⁹)²)/(8.0 × 10⁻¹³) ≈ 4.55 × 10⁻¹⁴ m. Since r₀ is larger than the nuclear radius, the nucleus acts as a point charge and the α-particle is never captured.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

❌ The model failed because α-particles were absorbed. ✅ Scattering itself was explained well; the model failed on stability and line spectra. · ❌ Atom radius = nuclear radius. ✅ 10⁻¹⁰ m versus 10⁻¹⁵ m.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Thomson's plum-pudding model (1904) treated the atom as diffuse positive charge with electrons embedded in it and could not explain large-angle deflection of fast α-particles (He²⁺, ≈5.5 MeV). Rutherford with Geiger and Marsden (1909–1911) probed atomic structure by α-scattering — the experiment that established the nuclear atom. · ### 2. Set-up

Observations · • ≈99.86% of α-particles passed straight through — the atom is mostly empty space.

14.
Label

More ideas

Text

• Atom is neutral and largely empty; a tiny, heavy, positive nucleus carries almost all the mass. · • Nuclear radius ≈10⁻¹⁵ m against atomic radius ≈10⁻¹⁰ m (1 : 100 000); nuclear volume ≈10⁻¹⁵ of atomic volume, nuclear density ≈10¹⁷ kg m⁻³. · • Electrons revolve in circular orbits, the Coulomb attraction of the nucleus giving the centripetal force.

More ideas

Quantitative results · • Distance of closest approach (KE → electrostatic PE): r₀ = 2Ze²/(4πε₀·KE) ≈ 4.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ m for 5 MeV α on gold — an experimental upper limit on nuclear size.
