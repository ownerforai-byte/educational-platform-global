de Broglie (1924) turned Bohr's postulate into physics: every moving particle has wavelength λ = h/mv, so an orbiting electron is a wave that survives only when a whole number of wavelengths fits its circumference (2πr = nλ) — which rearranges exactly to Bohr's mvr = nh/2π. Board question

Text

NEB-style (2m): State de Broglie's relation and calculate the wavelength of an electron moving at 10⁶ m s⁻¹. — λ = h/mv = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.1 × 10⁻³¹ × 10⁶) = 7.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m ≈ 0.73 nm. The relation asserts that every moving particle has a wave character with λ inversely proportional to its momentum. · NEB-style (3m): Derive Bohr's quantisation condition from de Broglie's hypothesis. — (1) For a stable orbit the electron wave must be a standing wave: 2πr = nλ. (2) Substituting λ = h/mv: 2πr = nh/mv. (3) Rearranging: mvr = nh/2π — Bohr's postulate of quantised angular momentum, now a consequence of wave geometry.

Quick concepts

Text

Quantisation becomes geometry: only orbits fitting a whole number of electron wavelengths are self-reinforcing standing waves — Bohr's rule is a consequence, not a postulate.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

λ = h/mv (de Broglie, 1924); standing-wave condition 2πr = nλ derives Bohr's mvr = nh/2π; Davisson–Germer (1927) proved electron diffraction off nickel; Schrödinger's wave equation yields orbitals = ψ² probability regions; electron λ ~ 10⁻¹⁰ m (observable), macroscopic λ ~ 10⁻³⁴ m (unobservable).

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

Electron microscopes exploit the electron's short de Broglie wavelength (~picometres at high voltage) to image far beyond the diffraction limit of light microscopes.
