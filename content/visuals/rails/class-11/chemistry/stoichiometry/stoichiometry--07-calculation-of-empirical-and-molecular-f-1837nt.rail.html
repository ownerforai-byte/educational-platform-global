Conditions

Text

❌ Empirical and molecular formula are the same for every compound. ✅ They coincide only when n = 1; for glucose the empirical formula is CH₂O but the molecular formula is C₆H₁₂O₆. · ❌ The empirical formula can be found without the molecular mass. ✅ It can — but the molecular formula needs the molecular mass (or vapour density) to give n.

Special cases

Glucose, fructose, formaldehyde, acetic acid and lactic acid all have the empirical formula CH₂O but different molecular formulas. · Molecular mass from vapour density: M = 2 × VD (hydrogen reference).

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Problem: 0.2 g of an organic compound gives 0.44 g CO₂ and 0.18 g H₂O. Find the empirical formula. Solution: mass C = 0.44 × 12/44 = 0.12 g (0.01 mol); mass H = 0.18 × 2/18 = 0.02 g (0.02 mol); oxygen by difference = 0.2 − 0.12 − 0.02 = 0.06 g = 0.00375 mol. Divide by 0.00375 → C : H : O = 2.67 : 5.33 : 1 → ×3 → 8 : 16 : 3, so the empirical formula is C₈H₁₆O₃ — illustrating how careful the ratio step must be. · Problem: An oxide of nitrogen contains 30.4% N and has VD = 46. Solution: N = 30.4/14 = 2.17, O = 69.6/16 = 4.35 → divide by 2.17 → 1 : 2, empirical formula NO₂, formula mass 46; n = 92/46 = 2 → molecular formula N₂O₄.
