Rows

1.
Label

Concept

Text

Non-metal chemistry is the chemistry of electron-hunger: halogens oxidise and bleach, HNO₃/H₂SO₄ concentrate into oxidising/dehydrating agents, and oxoacid strength climbs with the central atom's oxidation state. HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄ (Ka from 3.0×10⁻⁸ upward) because each added oxygen pulls electron density off the O–H bond. Helium replaces nitrogen in diving gas — insoluble, narcotic-free.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

oxoacid strength ∝ oxidation state of central atom: HClO(+1) < HClO₂(+3) < HClO₃(+5) < HClO₄(+7) · Ka(HClO) = 3.0×10⁻⁸ … rising per O · diving: He instead of N₂ (low solubility, no narcosis) · ring test: brown [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺ ring for NO₃⁻

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

The oxidation-state rule compares oxoacids of the SAME central atom; across different atoms, size and electronegativity reorder things. Bleaching by Cl₂/SO₂ needs moisture (nascent oxygen / reduction chemistry). Ring-test nitrate detection needs fresh FeSO₄ + conc. H₂SO₄ layering.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Nitrogen's N≡N triple bond explains its inertness (huge bond energy); 'active nitrogen' is the atomic/plasma form. Ozone's bent structure with delocalised π gives its oxidising punch and UV-shield role; CFCs catalyse its destruction (one Cl kills ~10⁵ O₃). CO is a silent killer (haemoglobin binder) and a metallurgical reductant.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Order HClO, HClO₂, HClO₃, HClO₄ by strength: Cl oxidation states +1, +3, +5, +7 — more terminal oxygens withdraw more electron density, weakening O–H and stabilising the anion → HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄, matching Ka 3.0×10⁻⁸ upward (NEB 2024, 3 marks).

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Oxidation-state rules predict trends, not Ka values. 'Inert' nitrogen reacts at Haber temperatures/pressures and in lightning (NOₓ). Ozone is beneficial up high, toxic down low — location decides hero vs pollutant.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Acid strength = ease of O–H heterolysis + anion stability. Each =O on chlorine delocalises the conjugate base's negative charge over more oxygens (resonance), lowering the anion's energy — so deprotonation equilibrium shifts right per added oxygen. Same logic ranks H₂SO₄ above H₂SO₃ and HNO₃ above HNO₂.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Count oxygens: more O on the same centre = stronger oxoacid. Diving gas: He for N₂ (narcosis/solubility). Ammonia tests: white fumes with conc. HCl, deep-blue with CuSO₄. Nitrate: brown ring. Halogens: starch-iodide and litmus-bleach tests.

9.
Label

Board question

Text

NEB 2024 (3): order HClO→HClO₄ by strength with Ka trend + explanation. NEB 2023 (2): He in diving gear instead of N₂. HNO₃ oxidising action, ammonia reactions, ozone prep/tests rotate yearly.
