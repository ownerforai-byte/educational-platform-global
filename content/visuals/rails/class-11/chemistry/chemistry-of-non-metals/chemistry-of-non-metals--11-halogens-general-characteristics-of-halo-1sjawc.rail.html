Text

The halogens are the salt-formers: diatomic, seven-electron, electronegative to a fault. Down the group the atoms swell, electronegativity and oxidising power sink (F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂), and the elements thicken from pale-yellow gas to violet-black solid. Each halogen displaces the halide of every heavier sibling — Cl₂ water turns KBr orange and KI blue-black.…

Shortcut

Text

F2 > Cl2 > Br2 > I2 oxidising power. Cold dil NaOH -> NaCl + NaOCl; hot conc -> NaCl + NaClO3. Dry Cl2 does NOT bleach. Acid strength: more O, stronger acid (HClO4 > HClO3 > HClO2 > HClO). I2 dissolves in KI -> KI3.

Quick concepts

Text

Oxidising power falls down Group VIIA because the growing atom holds the incoming electron less tightly and stabilises X- less in water — so every lighter halogen displaces every heavier halide, and fluorine sits at the top with an anomalously weak F-F bond making it the strongest oxidiser of all.
