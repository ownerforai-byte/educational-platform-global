1. Topic Introduction — Hückel's Rule: The Count That Decides Hückel's rule is the aromaticity test: a planar, cyclic, fully conjugated system is aromatic if its π-electron count is 4n + 2 (n = 0, 1, 2, ...), and antiaromatic if 4n. Benzene's six π electrons (4·1 + 2) pass; the rule extends the verdict beyond benzene to ions and heterocycles. · ## 2. Theorem Mirror - Statement: planarity + cyclic conjugation + 4n + 2 π electrons ⟹ aromatic stabilisation. - Benzene: six sp² carbons each donate one p electron ⟹ 6 π = 4(1) + 2 ✓. - Extension to ions: cyclopentadienyl anion C₅H₅⁻ — six π electrons (two from the negative carbon's lone pair) ⟹ aromatic; cycloheptatrienyl cation (tropylium) C₇H₇⁺ — six π electrons (one carbon's empty p orbital) ⟹…
