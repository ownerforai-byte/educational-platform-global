Title

Batch vs continuous — how chemical plants really run

Href

/class-11-notes/chemistry

Icon

CalendarCheck

Stat Key

pyq:chemistry

Rows

1.
Label

Concept

Text

Industrial chemistry converts bench reactions into cash flow: research → pilot plant → full production, with economics (raw-material, energy, capital, waste costs) deciding survival. Batch processing runs one charge at a time (flexible, pharma/speciality); continuous processing flows non-stop (Haber ammonia, Contact sulphuric acid — huge volumes, steady state). Every plant balances yield against effluent: environmental impact (emissions, effluent, solid waste) is designed out, not cleaned up.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

plant pipeline: lab → pilot → production (scale-up ×10–1000) · batch: charge → react → discharge → clean (flexible, small volume) · continuous: steady in/out (bulk, Haber/Contact/Solvay) · economics: profit = product value − (materials + energy + capital + waste + compliance)

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Continuous suits massive steady demand with stable chemistry (equilibrium-limited reactions recycle unreacted feed). Batch suits low-volume, high-variety, frequent changeovers. Cash-flow analysis needs full-cycle costing — a cheap reaction with expensive separation loses to a dear reaction with easy workup.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Photography's AgBr chemistry (expose → develop → fix) is batch processing in miniature. Alloys beat pure metals (brass over gold for durability) — materials choice is applied chemistry too. Pharma stays batch (regulation + variety); fertilisers and acids run continuous (volume).

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Choose the process: 10 tonnes/day of one fertiliser for a decade → continuous (steady state, heat integration, low unit cost). 50 kg batches of twenty different dyes → batch (changeover flexibility beats flow efficiency). The decision variable is volume × variety, not chemistry alone.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Continuous plants are inflexible (product switches cost weeks) and catastrophic on failure (runaway propagates). Batch suffers batch-to-batch variation and higher labour/unit cost. 'Green' redesigns (catalysis, atom economy, E-factor) constrain both — profit and planet negotiate every flow sheet.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

From the syllabus stages: a product idea passes technical feasibility (does it work at scale?), economic feasibility (cash flow positive over plant life?), and environmental clearance (EIA, effluent limits) — three gates in series, any one fatal. Plant design then fixes reactor type, separation train and control loops around the chosen batch/continuous mode.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Bulk + steady = continuous; varied + small = batch. Stages chant: research–pilot–production. Economics: count energy and waste beside raw materials. Environment: emissions/effluent/solids — one example each (SO₂ scrubbing, ETP, slag reuse).

9.
Label

Board question

Text

NEB-style: batch vs continuous processing with one industrial example each; stages from lab to plant; cash-flow factors in production economics; one environmental impact of the chemical industry with a control measure.
