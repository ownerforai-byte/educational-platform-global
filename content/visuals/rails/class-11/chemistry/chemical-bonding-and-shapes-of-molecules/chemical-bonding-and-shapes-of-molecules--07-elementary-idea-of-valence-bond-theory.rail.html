Text

Valence bond theory turns the dot picture into physics: a covalent bond is the overlap of half-filled orbitals with opposite-spin pairing, energy minimum at the bond length where nuclear attraction to the shared cloud beats repulsion. Head-on overlap builds the sigma bond — density on the axis, the strongest link, free rotation, able to exist alone (H₂ s–s, HCl s–p, Cl₂ p–p).…

6. Common Confusions A double bond is not two identical bonds: it is one strong σ + one weaker π — breaking a double bond first breaks the π (addition reactions attack the π first). · π bonds cannot exist alone: between two atoms, the first bond is always σ; π is always the second or third. "A molecule with only π bonds" is impossible. · Overlap requires half-filled orbitals: fully filled orbitals repel (no net bond); the spin-pairing condition is part of the definition.
