Electron pairs around a central atom repel one another and spread as far apart as they can, so counting σ bonds plus lone pairs — the steric number — fixes both the shape and the hybridisation.

Caption

Steric number 2, 3 and 4 give linear, trigonal planar and tetrahedral; lone pairs squeeze the angle.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

SN = σ bonds + lone pairs; 2 → sp, 180°; 3 → sp², 120°; 4 → sp³, 109.5°; 5 → sp³d; 6 → sp³d²

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Start from a correct Lewis structure, take the repulsion order lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair, and apply it to the central atom only.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

CH₄ 109.5°, NH₃ 107° with one lone pair, H₂O 104.5° with two; CO₂ linear, BF₃ trigonal planar, SF₆ octahedral, XeF₄ square planar.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

H₂O: 2 σ bonds + 2 lone pairs = steric number 4 → sp³, but the molecular shape is bent and the angle closes to 104.5°.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

It is a repulsion model rather than a calculation: transition-metal complexes, d-orbital participation and strongly electronegative substituents break its predictions.

7.
Label

Derivation

Kind

formula

Text

Count valence electrons → draw the Lewis structure → σ bonds + lone pairs = SN → electron-pair geometry → remove lone pairs for the molecular shape

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Lone pairs decide the angle but are never named in the shape: steric number 4 with two lone pairs still reads bent, not tetrahedral.

9.
Label

Board question

Text

Predict the shape, hybridisation and bond angle of H₂O and NH₃, and explain why they differ from CH₄.
