The octet rule is a stability statement, not a shape theory — it decides how many electrons gather around an atom; VSEPR decides where they point. Electron-deficient species (BF₃) are Lewis acids precisely because their octets are short.

6. Common Confusions Octet ≠ eight bonds: the octet counts electrons around an atom (bonding pairs counted twice, once per atom), not bonds. In CO₂ carbon owns 8 electrons via two double bonds — but forms only two bonds. · Valence electrons vs valency: Cl has 7 valence electrons but valency 1 — valency is 8 − valence electrons (for non-metals), not the count itself. · Expanded octets are third-row-only: C, N, O, F (period 2) have no accessible d orbitals; P, S, Cl and beyond can exceed 8. Never write five bonds on carbon.
