1.
Label

Concept

Text

Electron domains around a central atom repel and settle as far apart as possible, so the steric number (sigma bonds + lone pairs) fixes the electron geometry, and hiding the lone pairs reveals the molecular shape. Count domains, not atoms: a double or triple bond is still one domain.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

SN 2 → linear 180° (sp) · SN 3 → trigonal planar 120° (sp²) · SN 4 → tetrahedral 109.5° (sp³) · SN 5 → trigonal bipyramidal (sp³d) · SN 6 → octahedral 90° (sp³d²) · repulsion: lone–lone > lone–bond > bond–bond

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Applies to covalent main-group molecules with an identifiable central atom: draw the Lewis structure first, then count sigma bonds plus lone pairs on the central atom only. Works for the syllabus set — BeF2, BF3, CH4, CH3Cl, PCl5, SF6, H2O, NH3, CO2, H2S, PH3.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

H2O is bent at 104.5° (two lone pairs squeeze hardest); NH3 is pyramidal at 107° (one lone pair). CH4 stays tetrahedral but CH3Cl distorts it — the electronegative Cl pulls the bonding pair. PCl5 splits bonds into axial and longer/weaker axial vs equatorial positions; SF6 is perfectly octahedral with all 90° angles.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Shape of NH3: N has 5 valence electrons, three N–H bonds use 3, leaving 1 lone pair → steric number 4, tetrahedral electron geometry, pyramidal molecule. The lone pair compresses the angle to ~107°, below the ideal 109.5°.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

VSEPR predicts shape, never bond energy or why bonds form (that needs valence-bond / MO theory). It stumbles on transition-metal complexes where d-orbitals dominate, and hydrides like PH3/H2S deviate from ideal angles because nearly pure p-orbitals bond with little hybridization.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Start from the parent geometry of the steric number, then delete the lone-pair positions: AX4 tetrahedron minus nothing gives CH4; minus one vertex gives pyramidal AX3E (NH3); minus two gives bent AX2E2 (H2O). AX5 gives trigonal-bipyramidal PCl5 and AX6 octahedral SF6 — every syllabus molecule falls out of one rule.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Memorise the ladder Be–B–C–N–O: BeF2 linear, BF3 trigonal, CH4 tetrahedral, NH3 pyramidal, H2O bent. Each added lone pair shaves ~2.5° off 109.5° (109.5 → 107 → 104.5). CO2 is linear like BeF2 — two double bonds still count as two domains.

9.
Label

Board question

Text

NEB-style: predict with reasons the shapes and bond angles of CH4, NH3 and H2O; state the hybridization in each; explain the angle order CH4 (109.5°) > NH3 (107°) > H2O (104.5°) using lone-pair repulsion.
