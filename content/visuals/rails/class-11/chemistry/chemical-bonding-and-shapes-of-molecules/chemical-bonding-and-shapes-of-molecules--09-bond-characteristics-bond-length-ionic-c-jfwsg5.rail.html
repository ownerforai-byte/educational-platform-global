Conditions

Text

Bond energy vs. bond length are inversely related but not perfectly: While generally shorter bonds are stronger, there are exceptions. For example, the F–F bond (159 pm, 159 kJ/mol) is anomalously weak compared to Cl–Cl (199 pm, 243 kJ/mol) due to lone pair–lone pair repulsion between the small fluorine atoms. Never assume bond energy monotonically decreases with bond length. · Ionic character percentage is approximate: Pauling's equation gives an estimate, not an exact value. The actual ionic character depends on the crystal environment, solvation, and other factors. For instance, gaseous NaCl has ~71% ionic character, but in the solid lattice, the ionic contribution is even more pronounced due to the collective electrostatic environment.

Special cases

Text

The N≡N triple bond in molecular nitrogen (945 kJ/mol) is one of the strongest bonds in chemistry. This extraordinary bond strength is why N₂ is so inert at room temperature and why nitrogen fixation (breaking N≡N) requires extreme conditions or biological enzymes (nitrogenase). · The bond length of H₂ (74 pm) is the shortest known covalent bond because hydrogen has the smallest atomic radius. The H–H bond energy (436 kJ/mol) is also remarkably high for such a short bond, reflecting the excellent 1s–1s overlap.

Limitation

Text

Bond order is not always an integer: In resonance structures and molecular orbital theory, bond orders can be fractional (1.5 for benzene C–C, 1.33 for carbonate, 2 for O₂ in MO theory). Fractional bond orders correspond to intermediate bond lengths and energies between the nearest integer values.

Derivation

Text

Bond energy (bond enthalpy): The energy required to break one mole of a particular bond in the gas phase, producing gaseous atoms. Typical values: H–H 436 kJ/mol, C–H 413 kJ/mol, C–C 347 kJ/mol, C=C 614 kJ/mol, C≡C 839 kJ/mol, O=O 498 kJ/mol, N≡N 945 kJ/mol. Bond energy increases with bond order and decreases with increasing atomic size.… · Bond order: The number of chemical bonds between a pair of atoms. In Lewis terms, bond order = (number of bonding electrons − number of antibonding electrons)/2 in MO theory. Higher bond order → shorter bond length and greater bond energy. For resonance structures, bond order is the average (e.g., O₃ has bond order 1.5, benzene C–C has bond order 1.5).

Shortcut

Text

The dipole moment of water (1.85 D) is responsible for its extraordinary solvent properties. No other common liquid has a combination of high dipole moment, small size, and hydrogen bonding capability, making water the 'universal solvent' for biological and chemical systems. · Bond energy values are average values derived from many compounds. The C–H bond energy of 413 kJ/mol is an average across methane, ethane, propane, etc. The actual C–H bond energy in CH₄ (427 kJ/mol for the first bond) differs from the average because each successive bond removal requires different energy.
