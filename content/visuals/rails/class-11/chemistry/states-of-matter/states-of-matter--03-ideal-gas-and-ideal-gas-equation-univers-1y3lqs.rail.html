Text

Three empirical laws fold into one equation. Boyle contributes V ∝ 1/P, Charles contributes V ∝ T, Avogadro contributes V ∝ n; multiplied together they give V ∝ nT/P, and the constant that makes it an equality is R — the universal gas constant, 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K in laboratory units, 8.314 J/mol·K in SI, 1.987 cal/mol·K in thermochemistry, and identical for every gas — the experimental soul of Avogadro's law.…

NEB 2023/2024 (4m): State Boyle's law and Charles's law. Derive the ideal gas equation from them. — Boyle's law: at constant temperature, the volume of a given mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure — V ∝ 1/P, or P₁V₁ = P₂V₂.… · NEB-style (2m): Calculate the pressure exerted by 0.5 mol of an ideal gas occupying a 5 L vessel at 300 K (R = 0.0821 L atm/mol K). — From PV = nRT: P = nRT/V = (0.5 mol × 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K × 300 K)/5 L = 12.315/5 = 2.46 atm. 6. Common Confusions R is not "8.314 always": the value must match the pressure–volume units; 0.0821 with atm–litres, 8.314 with Pa–m³. · Temperature in Kelvin again: PV = nRT inherits Charles's absolute scale; a Celsius slip scales the answer by 273/300 or worse. · Ideal gases do not exist: the equation is a limiting model; real gases approach it at low pressure and high temperature but never coincide.
