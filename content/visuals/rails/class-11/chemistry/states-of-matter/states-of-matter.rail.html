Unit Title

States of Matter

Syllabus Topics

  • Gaseous state: Kinetic theory of gas and its postulates
  • Gas laws: Boyle's law, Charles' law, Avogadro's law, combined gas equation, Dalton's law of partial pressure, Graham's law of diffusion
  • Ideal gas and ideal gas equation; universal gas constant and its significance
  • Deviation of real gas from ideality (solving related numerical problems based on gas laws)
  • Liquid state: physical properties of liquids — evaporation and condensation, vapour pressure and boiling point, surface tension and viscosity (qualitative idea only)
  • Liquid crystals and their applications
  • Solid state: types of solids, amorphous and crystalline solids
  • Efflorescent, deliquescent and hygroscopic solids; crystallization and crystal growth; water of crystallization
  • Introduction to unit crystal lattice and unit cell

Rows

1.
Label

Concept

Text

Boyle (P∝1/V at fixed T), Charles (V∝T at fixed P) and Avogadro (V∝n) each freeze two variables and watch the third — multiply all three proportionalities and the combined law (P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂) appears, with R crowning it as PV = nRT. Dalton splits mixture pressures by mole fraction; Graham sorts diffusion speeds by 1/√M. Liquids add vapour pressure/boiling/surface tension/viscosity; solids add lattices, unit cells and water of crystallization.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

Boyle: P₁V₁ = P₂V₂ · Charles: V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂ (kelvin!) · combined: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ · PV = nRT · Dalton: P_total = ΣPᵢ, Pᵢ = xᵢ·P · Graham: r₁/r₂ = √(M₂/M₁) · 500 cm³ at 27 °C → 127 °C, same P: V = 500×400/300 ≈ 667 cm³

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Charles and the combined law demand KELVIN — Celsius silently breaks them. Gas laws assume ideal behaviour (low P, high T); real gases deviate via molecular volume and attractions (van der Waals). Dalton needs non-reacting components; Graham needs same T and P for both gases.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

500 cm³ at 27 °C (300 K) → 127 °C (400 K), constant P: V₂ = 500 × 400/300 ≈ 667 cm³ (NEB 2021). Efflorescent salts lose water to air, deliquescent dissolve in absorbed water, hygroscopic merely attract it. Liquid crystals flow like liquids but order like solids (LCDs).

5.
Label

Solved

Text

State Boyle (V∝1/P) and Charles (V∝T), then fuse: V ∝ T/P → PV/T = constant = nR for n moles → PV = nRT, with R = 8.314 J/mol·K the bridge between the gas world and energy (NEB 2023, 3 marks). Derivation beats memorisation — three laws, one product.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Ideal equations fail near condensation (high P, low T) — liquefaction is the visible rebellion. Viscosity/surface-tension pictures here are qualitative only (syllabus says so). Unit-cell counting assumes perfect crystals; defects run the real semiconductor world.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Combine: V ∝ 1/P (Boyle) and V ∝ T (Charles) and V ∝ n (Avogadro) → V = R·nT/P. Dalton: each gas exerts its mole-fraction share, Pᵢ = xᵢP, proved by applying PV = nRT per component in the shared V, T. Graham: equal KE (½mv²) at same T → v ∝ 1/√m → diffusion rates follow.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Temperature to kelvin FIRST — every year someone loses marks on 27 °C. Combined-law ladder: cross-multiply P₁V₁T₂ = P₂V₂T₁. Graham: lighter diffuses faster by √mass-ratio. R = 8.314 pairs with joules/m³; R = 0.0821 with L·atm.

9.
Label

Board question

Text

NEB 2023 (3): Boyle + Charles → derive ideal gas equation. NEB 2021 (2): 500 cm³ at 27 °C → volume at 127 °C, constant P. Dalton/Graham and liquid/solid-state definitions rotate yearly.
