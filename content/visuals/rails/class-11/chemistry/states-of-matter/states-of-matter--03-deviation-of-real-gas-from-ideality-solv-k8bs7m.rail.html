Conditions

Text

❌ A real gas always has Z > 1. ✅ Z < 1 at moderate pressure (attraction dominates) and Z > 1 at high pressure (molecular volume dominates); hydrogen is a special case with Z > 1 throughout. · ❌ Deviation from ideality increases as temperature rises. ✅ It decreases with rising temperature and increases at high pressure and low temperature.

Special cases

Zc(observed) for most gases lies between 0.23 and 0.31, below the van der Waals value 0.375. · Hydrogen and helium have the smallest a and b values and stay closest to ideal behaviour; SO₂ and CO₂ deviate most among common gases.

Solved

Text

Problem: Compute Z for 1 mol of gas at 300 K occupying 0.5 L at 50 atm. Solution: Z = PV/RT = (50 × 0.5)/(0.0821 × 300) ≈ 1.015 → slightly greater than 1, so the finite molecular volume effect dominates. · Problem: For a gas with a = 3.6 L² atm mol⁻² and b = 0.043 L mol⁻¹, find Tc and Pc. Solution: Tc = 8a/27Rb = (8 × 3.6)/(27 × 0.0821 × 0.043) ≈ 302 K; Pc = a/27b² = 3.6/(27 × 0.043²) ≈ 72 atm.
