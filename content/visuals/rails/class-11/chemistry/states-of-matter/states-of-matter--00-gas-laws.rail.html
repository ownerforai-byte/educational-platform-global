Conditions

Text

❌ PV = constant for any gas under any conditions. ✅ Only for fixed mass at constant temperature and ideal behaviour. · ❌ Doubling pressure halves the temperature. ✅ Boyle's law holds T constant; the P–T relation is Gay-Lussac's law.

Boyle published the law in 1662 — the first quantitative gas law. · At constant T the product PV equals nRT for every ideal gas; its graph versus P is flat.

Solved

Text

Problem: A diver's air bubble of 5 mL forms at 4 atm (depth ≈30 m). What volume at the surface (1 atm), ignoring temperature change? Solution: V₂ = P₁V₁/P₂ = 4 × 5/1 = 20 mL — a fourfold expansion, the classic Boyle's law safety example.

Study notes

Text

At constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure: V ∝ 1/P, i.e. PV = constant, or P₁V₁ = P₂V₂. Conditions: fixed mass, constant T, ideal behaviour (low P). · ### Graphical forms (all three are examinable) · • P versus V: rectangular hyperbola (each temperature gives one hyperbola — an isotherm; higher T curves lie farther from the origin).

13.
Label

More ideas

Text

Why does a real gas deviate from Boyle's law at high pressure? · • P versus 1/V: straight line through the origin, slope = nRT. · • PV versus P: horizontal straight line for an ideal gas; real gases show a dip (attraction) then a rise (volume effect) — the first visible sign of non-ideality.

14.
Label

More ideas

Text

Molecular explanation · Halving the volume doubles the number density of molecules, so wall collisions per unit area per second double, doubling the pressure — the inverse relation is pure collision statistics at fixed average molecular speed (fixed T). · ### Numericals

15.
Label

More ideas

Text

• V₂ = P₁V₁/P₂: 500 cm³ at 700 mm Hg → at 1400 mm Hg: 250 cm³. · • Density application: since m fixed, d ∝ P at constant T — a compressed gas is proportionally denser. · • Bubble problem: an air bubble rising from depth doubles its volume as pressure halves (plus water-column corrections with 10 m water ≈ 1 atm).

16.
Label

More ideas

Text

Limitations · • Ideal-gas law: fails at high pressure (PV rises above nRT) and near liquefaction; gases with strong attraction (CO₂, SO₂, NH₃) deviate most; H₂ and He deviate least. · • Requires fixed mass and constant temperature — a temperature change during compression invalidates it.
