Sodium-Potassium Pump (Na+/K+-ATPase): An integral membrane protein that actively transports 3 Na+ ions out of the cell and 2 K+ ions into the cell per ATP hydrolyzed. This creates a net negative charge inside the cell (resting membrane potential, approximately −70 mV in neurons).

Limitation

Text

Ca2+ pump (SERCA): Pumps calcium from cytoplasm into the sarcoplasmic reticulum in muscle cells, using one ATP per two Ca2+ ions. Essential for muscle relaxation — after contraction, Ca2+ must be removed from cytoplasm. · H+/K+-ATPase (proton pump): Found in stomach parietal cells. Pumps H+ into stomach lumen in exchange for K+, producing hydrochloric acid (pH ~1). Target of proton pump inhibitors (omeprazole) for treating acid reflux.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Mechanism of Na+/K+ pump: (1) Three intracellular Na+ ions bind. (2) ATP phosphorylates the pump, causing conformational change. (3) Na+ released outside. (4) Two extracellular K+ ions bind. (5) Phosphate group released, pump returns to original shape. (6) K+ released inside. · Sodium-Glucose Cotransporter (SGLT): Found in intestinal epithelium and kidney tubules. Uses the downhill Na+ gradient (created by Na+/K+-ATPase) to drive uphill glucose transport against its concentration gradient. Example: SGLT1 in intestine, SGLT2 in kidney.

The biological half-life of cadmium in the human kidney is 10-30 years, meaning even low-level chronic exposure accumulates to toxic levels over decades. · Chelation therapy using EDTA or desferrioxamine can remove heavy metals from the body by forming water-soluble metal-chelate complexes that are excreted in urine.

Iron toxicity and Fenton reaction: Excess Fe2+ reacts with hydrogen peroxide to produce highly reactive hydroxyl radicals: Fe2++H2​O2​ightarrowFe3++OH−+⋅OH. These radicals damage DNA, proteins, and lipid membranes, leading to cell death. · Arsenic toxicity: Arsenite (As3+) has high affinity for vicinal dithiol groups (-SH HS-) in lipoic acid, which is a cofactor in pyruvate dehydrogenase. Binding blocks the citric acid cycle, shutting down aerobic cellular respiration. · Mercury toxicity: Hg2+ ions bind strongly to sulfhydryl (-SH) groups in enzyme cysteine residues: Enzyme−SH+Hg2+ightarrowEnzyme−S−Hg+. This denatures enzymes and disrupts protein structure. Causes neurological damage (Minamata disease).

12.
Label

More ideas

Text

Lead toxicity: Pb2+ mimics Ca2+ due to similar ionic radius (Pb2+: 120 pm, Ca2+: 100 pm). Substitutes for calcium in bones and teeth. Inhibits δ-aminolevulinic acid dehydratase (ALAD) in haem synthesis, causing anaemia.… · Cadmium toxicity: Cd2+ accumulates in kidneys with a biological half-life of 10-30 years. Displaces Zn2+ in zinc-dependent enzymes, causing loss of enzyme function. Causes renal tubular damage and itai-itai disease (bone softening). Found in cigarette smoke and industrial waste. · Aluminum toxicity: Al3+ accumulates in brain tissue and has been linked to Alzheimer's disease. Interferes with calcium signaling and phosphorus metabolism. Common exposure from aluminium cookware and antacids.

13.
Label

More ideas

Text

Detoxification mechanisms: (1) Metallothioneins — sulfur-rich proteins that bind and sequester heavy metals. (2) Ferritin — stores excess iron safely. (3) Ceruloplasmin — copper transport protein that oxidizes Fe2+ to Fe3+ for safe transport. · Biological half-life matters: Lead persists in bones for 20-30 years; cadmium in kidneys for 10-30 years; mercury in the brain for years. This makes chronic low-dose exposure as dangerous as acute poisoning.
