Label

Conditions

Text

Anions do not always reach the anode's oxidation: SO₄²⁻, NO₃⁻ and F⁻ resist discharge — water's OH⁻ is oxidised instead, so oxygen, not the anion's element, appears. · Concentration bends the rules: in concentrated brine Cl⁻ out-competes OH⁻ at the anode even though OH⁻ is nominally easier to discharge — the textbook order assumes comparable concentrations.

6. Common Confusions Cathode is negative — but only in electrolysis: in a galvanic cell the cathode is positive. The process (reduction at cathode) never changes; the sign does. Always state the cell type. · Na⁺ rarely discharges from water: in aqueous NaCl the hydrogen comes from water/H⁺, not sodium — molten salt is required to win sodium metal. · Anode is where oxidation happens even if the anode itself is the victim: a copper anode oxidises into solution — still oxidation, still the anode.
