6. Common Confusions Oxidation number ≠ ionic charge: Mn(+7) in KMnO₄ is bookkeeping; there is no free Mn⁷⁺ ion. Charges belong to actual ions; numbers belong to atoms inside any species. · The peroxide exception is the favourite exam trap: defaulting O to −2 in H₂O₂ wrecks the sum — check for O–O bonds first. · Fractional answers are correct answers: +8/3 and +2.5 are legitimate averages; do not "fix" them into integers.

7. Exam Application Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): State the rules for assigning oxidation numbers. Find the oxidation number of Mn in KMnO₄ and Cr in K₂Cr₂O₇. — Rules: (1) free elements 0; (2) monatomic ions equal their charges; (3) fluorine −1 in all compounds; (4) oxygen −2 except −1 in peroxides and +2 in OF₂;… · NEB-style (2m): Find the oxidation number of S in Na₂S₄O₆ and Fe in Fe₃O₄. — Tetrathionate: 2(+1) + 4S + 6(−2) = 0 → 4S = +10 → S = +2.5 (an average: two S at 0, two at +5). Magnetite: 3Fe + 4(−2) = 0 → Fe = +8/3 (FeO·Fe₂O₃: one +2, two +3). · NEB-style (2m): Identify the redox pair in 2KI + Cl₂ → 2KCl + I₂. — Iodine: −1 in KI → 0 in I₂ (increase — oxidised; Cl₂ is the oxidising agent). Chlorine: 0 → −1 (decrease — reduced; I⁻ is the reducing agent). K⁺ and the chloride counterions are spectators at +1 throughout.
