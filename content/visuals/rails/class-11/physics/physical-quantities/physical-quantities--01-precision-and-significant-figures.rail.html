Precision and significant figures control how many digits a measurement may honestly claim. Accuracy is closeness to the true value; precision is repeatability. Rules govern counting significant figures, rounding (round-half-to-even), combining quantities (fewest s.f. for ×/÷, fewest d.p. for +/−) and propagating uncertainties (relative errors add; powers multiply them).…

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Intermediate Calculations: Keep at least one extra 'guard' digit during intermediate calculation steps. Only round the final answer to the required number of significant figures to avoid compounding rounding errors. · Constants & Conversion Factors: Treat mathematical constants (e.g., π, √2) and exact conversion factors (e.g., 100 cm = 1 m) as having infinite significant figures. They should never limit the precision of the measured data.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Note 1: Pure numbers (2, π, counted quantities) have unlimited significant figures and never limit the result. · Note 2: In NEB numericals, always carry one extra guard digit through the working and round only the FINAL answer.

Solved

Text

Example 1: A measured current is 0.0450 A — this has 3 s.f.; the trailing zero after 5 is significant because it is after a decimal point, telling us the ammeter resolved to 0.0001 A. · Example 2: Mass of a coin measured 5 times: 5.42 g, 5.40 g, 5.41 g, 5.42 g, 5.40 g — highly precise (spread only 0.02 g); if the true mass is 5.00 g the readings are precise but inaccurate (zero error).

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

❌ '0.00250 has 6 significant figures because it has 6 digits.' ✅ Leading zeros only locate the decimal point — 0.00250 has 3 s.f. (the 2, the 5, and the trailing zero after the decimal point). · ❌ '500 has 3 significant figures.' ✅ Without a decimal point, trailing zeros are ambiguous — 500 could be 1, 2 or 3 s.f. Write 5.00×102 if you mean 3 s.f.

11.
Label

Quick concepts

Text

Concept 1: Accuracy = closeness to true value; precision = closeness of repeated readings to each other. · Concept 2: Least count = smallest interval an instrument can measure; it fixes the last significant figure of any reading. · Concept 3: Absolute error (Δx), relative error (Δx/x) and percentage error (Δx/x × 100%) are three views of the same uncertainty. · Concept 4: Error propagation rules: absolute errors add for sums/differences, relative errors add for products/quotients and powers.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Significant figures express the precision of a MEASUREMENT, not of a pure number — a counted quantity like '12 students' is exact. · Multiplication/division → fewest significant figures; addition/subtraction → fewest decimal places. · Leading zeros never count; captive zeros always count; trailing zeros count only after a decimal point. · Uncertainty of a reading is normally ± half the least count of the instrument.

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

The SI has seven base quantities: length (metre), mass (kilogram), time (second), electric current (ampere), temperature (kelvin), luminous intensity (candela) and amount of substance (mole). Since 2019 every base unit is defined by fixing a fundamental constant. · 'Significant' means reliable/meaningful, not mathematically important — instrument precision, not the calculator display, decides how many digits you may report.

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Accuracy vs Precision: Accuracy is how close a measurement is to the true value; precision is how close repeated measurements are to each other. A watch running 5 minutes fast can still be precise (consistent) but inaccurate. In physics we must report BOTH. · Significant figures are the digits that carry meaning about precision. Rules: (1) All non-zero digits are significant — 24.7 has 3 s.f. (2) Zeros between non-zero digits are significant — 2005 has 4 s.f. (3) Leading zeros are NOT significant — 0.0052 has 2 s.f. (4) Trailing zeros AFTER a decimal point ARE significant — 2.300 has 4 s.f.… · Why significant figures matter: every measurement has an uncertainty limited by the instrument's least count. Reporting T=2.034567 s measured with a stopwatch (least count 0.01 s) falsely claims precision. Round only to figures the instrument can justify.

Text

Parallax Error: For analog measurements (metre rules, thermometers), ensure the line of sight is perpendicular to the scale; this is a necessary physical condition that must be met before determining the significant figure precision of the measurement. · Relative errors add in products and are multiplied by the power for An — highest-power quantities dominate error. · Scientific notation (a×10n, 1≤a<10) removes ambiguity about trailing zeros.

21.
Label

More ideas

Text

❌ Rounding 2.45 to 2.5 'because 5 always rounds up'. ✅ For an exact 5 with nothing after, round to the nearest EVEN digit: 2.45 → 2.4 (but 2.4501 → 2.5). · ❌ 'A precise measurement is always accurate.' ✅ Precision (repeatability) says nothing about correctness — a wrongly calibrated balance gives precise but systematically wrong (inaccurate) masses. · ❌ Keeping decimal places in multiplication: 2.0×3.00=6.00. ✅ In multiplication count significant figures (2 s.f. here) → 6.0; decimal-place counting applies only to addition/subtraction.
