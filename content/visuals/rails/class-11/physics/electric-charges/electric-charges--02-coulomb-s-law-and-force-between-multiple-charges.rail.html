Text

Coulomb's Law quantifies the electrostatic force between two stationary point charges as directly proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation: F = k|q₁q₂|/r². In vector form, forces obey Newton's third law (F₁₂ = -F₂₁). When immersed in a dielectric medium of relative permittivity κ, the force reduces to F₀/κ.…

Conditions

Text

Adding electrostatic forces as scalar algebraic quantities: Electric forces are vectors! When calculating the net force exerted by multiple charges, one must resolve each pairwise force into Cartesian orthogonal components (Fx​,Fy​) before summing: Fnet​=(∑Fx​)2+(∑Fy​)2​. · Ignoring the medium's dielectric screening: In water (κ≈80), the electrostatic force between ions drops to 801​ of its vacuum value, explaining why table salt (NaCl) spontaneously dissociates into hydrated Na+ and Cl− ions in water.

Accelerating charges radiate electromagnetic energy. · Definition of 1 Coulomb: That charge which, when placed in vacuum at a distance of 1 meter from an identical charge, repels it with a force of 9 × 10⁹ N.

Limitation

Text

Applying Coulomb's law to extended charge distributions at close range: Coulomb's inverse-square formula applies strictly to point charges or non-overlapping spherical charge distributions (via Shell Theorem). At close range, induction distorts charge distribution on real conductors.

Key points
