3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Independence of Components: Horizontal motion (constant vx​=ucosheta) and vertical motion (free fall vy​=usinheta−gt) are completely independent. They share only the time variable t. This is the foundational principle — no horizontal force means vx​ is constant. · Trajectory is a Parabola: Eliminating t gives y=xanheta−racgx22u2cos2heta, a downward-opening parabola. This assumes no air resistance, constant g, and flat ground. With drag, the trajectory is not a parabola and range decreases.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Note 1: In horizontal-launch problems, get the time from the VERTICAL fall first, then multiply by vx​. · Note 2: Memorise sin37°=0.6, cos37°=0.8 and sin2θ values for 30°/45°/60° — NEB numericals use them constantly.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Example 1 (horizontal launch): a stone thrown horizontally at 15 m/s from a 20 m tower: t=2×20/10​=2 s; lands 30 m out — vertical fall sets the TIME, horizontal speed sets the DISTANCE. · Example 2 (full trajectory): u=25 m/s at θ=37° (sin37°=0.6, cos37°=0.8): T=3 s, H=11.25 m, R=60 m — the classic 3-4-5 projectile.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

❌ 'A projectile's horizontal velocity decreases during flight.' ✅ vx​=ucosθ is CONSTANT (no horizontal force in the ideal model); only vy​ changes. · ❌ 'At the top of the trajectory the velocity is zero.' ✅ Only vy​=0; the horizontal component ucosθ persists — speed at the top is ucosθ, the MINIMUM of the flight.

11.
Label

Quick concepts

Text

Concept 1: Independence of horizontal (uniform) and vertical (accelerated) motions. · Concept 2: Component kinematics — vx​ constant, vy​ with −gt; time is the shared variable. · Concept 3: Standard results: T=2usinθ/g, H=u2sin2θ/2g, R=u2sin2θ/g. · Concept 4: Symmetry — equal speeds at equal heights; landing mirrors launching.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Horizontal and vertical motions are independent; they share only TIME. · vx​ is constant; vy​ changes by g every second; the path is a parabola. · At the top: vy​=0, speed =ucosθ (minimum), a=g downward. · Landing speed equals launch speed; landing angle equals −launch angle (symmetry).

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

Galileo's Dialogues (1638) first showed projectile motion is a parabola by superposing uniform horizontal motion with vertical fall — the first known composition of motions. · Artillery fire above ~30 km (Paris Gun, WWI) deviates from the parabola: thin air changes drag and g's direction varies — ideal parabolic flight is a near-Earth, no-air model.

Study notes

Text

Type 2 — alternate treatment: this version builds projectile motion from the independence of the two components and derives all standard results step by step — the derivation chain NEB expects. · The governing idea: horizontal and vertical motions are INDEPENDENT. Gravity acts only vertically, so the horizontal velocity stays constant (vx​=ucosθ) while the vertical motion is free fall with initial speed usinθ. The path is the combination — a parabola. · Component equations (up positive, launch at angle θ with speed u): horizontal: x=(ucosθ)t, vx​=ucosθ; vertical: y=(usinθ)t−21​gt2, vy​=usinθ−gt.

Symmetry and Range Maxima: Time of flight T=rac2usinhetag, max height H=racu2sin2heta2g, range R=racu2sin2hetag. Maximum range at heta=45∘. More ideas
