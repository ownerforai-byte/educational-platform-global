Conditions

Text

Limit Definition Universality: Instantaneous velocity v=dx/dt and acceleration a=dv/dt are defined as limits (Δto0). They apply to any motion — uniform or non-uniform, constant or variable acceleration. The SUVAT equations (v=u+at, etc.) are special cases valid only for constant a. · Graph Duality — Slopes vs Areas: On x-t graph: slope of tangent = v. On v-t graph: slope of tangent = a; area under curve = displacement. On a-t graph: area under curve = change in velocity. Mixing up slope/area is the most common graph error.

Limitation

Text

❌ 'Instantaneous velocity equals average velocity.' ✅ Only when motion is uniform. In general vinst​=dx/dt while vavg​=Δx/Δt; they coincide only if the x–t graph is a straight line. · ❌ 'Negative acceleration always means slowing down.' ✅ Slowing means v and a have OPPOSITE signs. A ball thrown upward (up = positive) has v>0 with a=−g — it slows going up and speeds up coming down, with the SAME downward acceleration throughout.

Quick concepts

Text

Concept 1: Limit definition — instantaneous quantities are average quantities over a vanishing interval. · Concept 2: Derivative chain: xd/dt​vd/dt​a; integrals run the reverse way with initial conditions. · Concept 3: Graph duality — slopes give rates (v, a); areas give accumulations (x, v). · Concept 4: Sign logic — direction of motion from sign of v; speeding up/slowing down from relative signs of v and a.

Key points

Text

Instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent of the x–t graph; instantaneous acceleration is the slope of the v–t graph. · Displacement is the area under the v–t graph; change in velocity is the area under the a–t graph. · v=0 does not imply a=0 — turning points have zero velocity but non-zero acceleration. · Speeding up ⟺ v and a share the same sign; slowing down ⟺ opposite signs.
