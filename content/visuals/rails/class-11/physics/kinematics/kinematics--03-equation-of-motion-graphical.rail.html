Text

All three equations of motion follow from the straight-line v–t graph of uniform acceleration: the slope gives v=u+at; the area (rectangle + triangle, or the trapezoid mean-height 2u+v​t) gives s=ut+21​at2; eliminating t yields v2=u2+2as. The a–t graph (area = change in velocity) and the x–t parabola complete the graphical picture.…

3.

Conditions

Text

❌ 'The area under the v–t graph gives acceleration.' ✅ Area gives DISPLACEMENT; the SLOPE gives acceleration. Swap-proof: slope of v–t = a (m/s²), area of v–t = s (m). · ❌ Using s=ut+21​at2 for the distance in the n-th second directly with t=n. ✅ That gives the total distance in n seconds. The n-th SECOND distance is sn​=u+2a​(2n−1), i.e. s(n)−s(n−1).

❌ Applying v2=u2+2as with sign errors during braking. ✅ Take one positive direction and keep it: braking at 20 m/s with a=−5 m/s² gives 0=400+2(−5)s⇒s=40 m — the minus sign is essential. · ❌ 'The equations work if acceleration changes gradually.' ✅ All three equations REQUIRE constant acceleration; if a varies, use graphs/areas or calculus.

Key points

Text

Slope of v–t = acceleration; area under v–t = displacement. · The three equations hold ONLY for constant acceleration. · v2=u2+2as is the equation of choice when time is unknown. · s=(2u+v​)t is the graphical 'average velocity × time' form.

Universal facts

Text

The velocity–time 'trapezoid' derivation appears in Galileo's reasoning (via inclined planes and odd-number distances) two centuries before formal calculus existed. · Air-force and driving-school 'stopping distance' charts are exactly the v2=u2+2as equation with v=0 — doubling speed quadruples braking distance.

Study notes

Text

Type 2 — alternate treatment: this version derives all three equations of motion graphically (from the velocity–time graph) — the derivation style NEB asks for 'using a graphical method'. · Setup: a body starts with initial velocity u and accelerates uniformly at a; after time t its velocity is v. On a v–t graph this is a straight line from (0,u) to (t,v) — the straightness is exactly the meaning of uniform acceleration. · First equation (from the slope): slope of the line = acceleration a=tv−u​. Rearranging: v=u+at — 'final velocity = initial velocity + rate of change of velocity × time'.
