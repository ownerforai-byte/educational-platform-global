5.
Label

Solved

Text

The three equations of motion apply only when the acceleration is constant. They are v = u + at, s = ut + (1/2)at^2, and v^2 = u^2 + 2as. Each symbol must be given a sign before it is used. · On a velocity–time graph, constant acceleration is a straight line. The slope of that line is the acceleration, (v − u)/t, which rearranges to v = u + at.

Study notes

Text

The area under the velocity–time line is the displacement. For a straight line from u to v, the area is a rectangle ut plus a triangle (1/2)t(v − u). Using v − u = at turns that area into s = ut + (1/2)at^2. · Eliminating t between v = u + at and the displacement equation gives v^2 = u^2 + 2as. This form is the one to choose when the time is not given and not asked for. · For a body thrown upward, take upward as positive. Then u is positive, a is −g, and the velocity at the top is zero. The same equations describe free fall with u = 0 and a = +g if downward is positive.
