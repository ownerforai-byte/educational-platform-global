Conditions

Text

Universality of Free Fall: All bodies fall with the same acceleration g≈9.8extm/s2 in vacuum (no air resistance). This is a consequence of the equivalence principle — inertial mass equals gravitational mass. The Apollo 15 hammer-feather drop on the Moon confirmed this. · Sign Convention Discipline: Choose one positive direction (e.g., up = +) and use it consistently. For up-positive: a=−g, v=u−gt, h=ut−rac12gt2. The sign of g goes into the equation ONCE — never mix conventions.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

❌ 'Heavier bodies fall faster.' ✅ In free fall ALL bodies have the same acceleration g; observed differences come from air resistance — demonstrated by the Apollo 15 hammer–feather drop on the Moon. · ❌ 'At the top of the flight, acceleration is zero because the body stops.' ✅ v=0 but a=g downward at ALL points of the flight. If a were zero at the top, the body would stay there!

Quick concepts

Concept 1: Universality of free fall — g is the same for all masses (Galileo). · Concept 2: Sign-convention discipline — one axis, one consistent g sign per problem. · Concept 3: Top-of-flight state — v=0, a=g: zero velocity is not zero acceleration. · Concept 4: Up–down symmetry — equal times and speeds at equal heights.

Key points

Text

Free fall acceleration g is independent of mass, size and material. · At every instant of the flight, a=g downward — including the top (v=0, a=0). · Up–down symmetry: equal speeds at equal heights, equal times up and down. · Use ONE sign convention throughout a problem; put g's sign in consistently.

Up-Down Symmetry (No Air Resistance): Time up = time down = u/g. Velocity at equal heights going up and down are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction. At the top, v=0 but a=g downward — zero velocity does NOT mean zero acceleration. · 'Dropped' = u=0; 'thrown' = u=0 — the equations differ by the ut term. · Air resistance is neglected in free fall; it is what produces terminal velocity in reality.
