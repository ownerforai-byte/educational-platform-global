Text

❌ Snell's law is n1​sinθ1​=n2​sinθ2​ with no mention of which is larger. ✅ Snell's law shows that when n2​>n1​: sinθ2​<sinθ1​, so r<i (ray bends toward normal). When n2​<n1​: r>i (ray bends away from normal). The direction of bending is determined by the relative densities. · ❌ The refractive index n can be less than 1. ✅ For real materials at optical frequencies, n>1 because v<c. In some special cases (metamaterials at microwave frequencies), engineered materials can have n<1, but this doesn't apply to standard optical media.

Study notes

Text

Snell's law (First law of refraction): When light passes obliquely from one medium to another, sinrsini​=constant=n1​n2​​, where i is the angle of incidence, r is the angle of refraction, and n1​, n2​ are the refractive indices of the first and second media respectively. This law is the basis of all refraction phenomena. · Refractive index definition: n=vc​, where c=3.00×108m/s is the speed of light in vacuum and v is its speed in the medium. Since v<c always, n>1 for all real materials. Higher n means light travels slower in that medium. · Relative refractive index: n21​=n1​n2​​=v2​v1​​. This is the refractive index of medium 2 as seen from medium 1. When light enters a denser medium (n2​>n1​), it bends TOWARD the normal. When entering a rarer medium, it bends AWAY from the normal.
