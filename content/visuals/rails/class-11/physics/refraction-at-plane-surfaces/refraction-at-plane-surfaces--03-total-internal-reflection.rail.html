When light in a denser medium meets a rarer boundary, the refracted ray bends away; at the critical angle C it grazes the surface (sinC=1/n), and beyond C the light is totally internally reflected — returned completely into the denser medium. Two conditions: travel denser → rarer and i > C (NEB 2022, 2024, 2m).…

Text

1. Topic Introduction — Light Trapped Inside Past a certain angle, light hitting the inside of a glass–air boundary doesn't escape at all — it reflects perfectly, better than any mirror. Total internal reflection powers optical fibres, the glitter of diamonds and the prisms in binoculars. · ## 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021, 2022, 2024) ### 2.1 From refraction to total reflection As the angle of incidence i (in the denser medium) grows, the angle of refraction r grows faster. At the critical angle C, the refracted ray grazes along the surface (r = 90°). Beyond C there is no refracted ray — the light is completely returned: total internal reflection.… · ## 3. Conceptual Relationships - To Topic 70/71: the critical angle is Snell's law with r = 90°. - To Topic 72: a slab beyond C becomes a mirror — the basis of light guides. - To modern physics: fibre-optic TIR carries the internet across oceans.
