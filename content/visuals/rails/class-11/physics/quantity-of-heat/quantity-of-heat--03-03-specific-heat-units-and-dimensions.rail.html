5.
Label

Solved

Text

Specific heat capacity c is the heat needed to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K. The defining relation is Q = mc Δθ, so c = Q / (m Δθ). · The SI unit is joule per kilogram per kelvin, J kg^-1 K^-1. A degree Celsius interval equals a kelvin interval, so J kg^-1 °C^-1 is the same size of unit.

Study notes

Text

Heat Q has the dimensions of energy, [M L^2 T^-2]. Mass is [M] and a temperature interval brings in [K]. Therefore [c] = [L^2 T^-2 K^-1]. · Water's specific heat is about 4200 J kg^-1 K^-1, which is large. That is why a large mass of water changes temperature slowly for a given heat input. · The heat capacity of a whole body is C = mc, in J/K. Specific heat is a material property. Heat capacity belongs to one particular object.
