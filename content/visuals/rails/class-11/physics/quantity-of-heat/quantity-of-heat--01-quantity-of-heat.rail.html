6.
Label

Limitation

Text

❌ Heat and temperature are the same thing. ✅ Temperature is the average molecular kinetic energy (intensive). Heat is the total energy transferred (extensive). A spark at 2000°C has tiny heat content; a bathtub at 40°C has huge heat content. · ❌ A body "contains" heat. ✅ A body contains internal energy (kinetic + potential of its molecules). Heat is the TRANSFER of energy due to temperature difference. Once energy is in the body, it's no longer "heat." Think of it like "payment" - once received, it's no longer a payment; it's just money in your account.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Heat is energy in transit: Heat (H) is the thermal energy transferred between systems due to a temperature difference. It is NOT a property of a body - you cannot say "a body contains heat." A body contains internal energy; heat is the transfer of that energy. · Mass dependency (H ∝ m): For a given substance and temperature change, the heat required is directly proportional to the mass: H∝m. Doubling the mass doubles the heat needed. This is because more molecules means more total kinetic energy must change.

Universal facts

Text

The calorie was originally defined as the heat needed to raise 1 gram of water by 1°C - specifically from 14.5°C to 15.5°C, since water's specific heat varies slightly with temperature. The modern value is 1 cal=4.184 J (exact). · Water's anomalously high specific heat (4200 J/kg⋅K) is due to hydrogen bonding. Breaking these bonds requires extra energy, so more heat goes into the system without raising temperature. This is why oceans moderate Earth's climate.

14.
Label

Study notes

Text

Temperature dependency (H ∝ Δθ): For a given mass, heat is directly proportional to the temperature change: H∝Δθ, where Δθ=θf​−θi​ (final − initial). Heating water from 20°C to 80°C requires 3× the heat of heating from 20°C to 40°C. · Combined relation: Combining both: H∝m⋅Δθ. Introducing the constant of proportionality - the specific heat capacity (c) - gives the fundamental equation: H=m⋅c⋅Δθ · Sign convention: When heat is absorbed (temperature rises), H>0 and Δθ>0. When heat is released (temperature falls), H<0 and Δθ<0. The sign tells you the direction of energy flow.

More ideas

Text

❌ All materials heat up at the same rate with the same power. ✅ Different materials have different specific heats. 1 kW heats 1 kg of aluminum (c=900) much faster than 1 kg of water (c=4200). The aluminum's temperature rises ~4.7× faster. · ❌ The formula H = mcΔθ works for phase changes too. ✅ This formula ONLY works when temperature changes WITHOUT a phase change. During melting (ice → water at 0°C) or boiling (water → steam at 100°C), temperature stays constant - you need H=mL instead. · ❌ Specific heat capacity depends on the amount of substance. ✅ Specific heat capacity is an INTENSIVE property - it depends only on the MATERIAL, not the mass. 1 kg of copper has c=390 J/kg⋅K, same as 1 g of copper.
