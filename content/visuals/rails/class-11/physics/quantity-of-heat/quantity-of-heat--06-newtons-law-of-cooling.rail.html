❌ The constant k is the same for all bodies. ✅ k depends on the body's surface area, texture, and the surrounding medium. Two identical bodies in different environments have different k values. · ❌ A black body cools slower than a shiny body. ✅ A black/dull body has HIGHER emissivity and cools FASTER. This is why radiators are painted black and survival blankets are shiny (to retain heat).

Derivation

Text

Statement: The rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the difference between the body's temperature (θ) and the surrounding temperature (θ0​), provided the temperature difference is small (typically <30°C). · Mathematical form: dtdH​=−k(θ−θ0​), where k>0 is a constant depending on the body's properties and surface area. The negative sign indicates heat is LOST (temperature decreasing).

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

Newton actually stated his law of cooling in 1701, based on experiments with thermometers cooling in air. It is one of the earliest examples of a differential equation in physics. · The exponential decay form of Newton's law of cooling is mathematically identical to many other natural processes: radioactive decay, RC circuit discharge, population decay, and drug elimination from the bloodstream.

14.
Label

Study notes

Text

In terms of temperature rate: Since dH=mcdθ, we get dtdθ​=−mck​(θ−θ0​)=−K′(θ−θ0​), where K′=mck​ is the cooling constant. · The constant k: k depends on (i) the nature of the body - material, surface texture, emissivity; (ii) the surface area exposed to surroundings; (iii) the nature of the surrounding medium (air, water). A blackened surface cools faster than a polished one. · Applicability condition: Newton's law is an APPROXIMATION valid only when (θ−θ0​) is small. For large temperature differences, Stefan-Boltzmann radiation law (dtdH​∝θ4−θ04​) must be used.

15.
Label

More ideas

Text

❌ Newton's law and Stefan-Boltzmann law give the same result always. ✅ They agree when (θ−θ0​) is small. For large differences, Stefan-Boltzmann gives a much faster cooling rate (proportional to T4). · ❌ The surrounding temperature must be zero for Newton's law. ✅ θ0​ is the temperature of the surroundings (e.g., room temperature ≈ 25°C). The law uses the DIFFERENCE (θ−θ0​), not absolute temperature. · ❌ Newton's law applies to heating as well as cooling. ✅ Yes! If θ<θ0​, the body GAINS heat and the rate is positive: dtdθ​=+K′(θ0​−θ). The same law describes both heating and cooling.

16.
Label

More ideas

Text

❌ The body reaches room temperature in finite time. ✅ The exponential function e−K′t approaches zero asymptotically but never reaches zero. The body gets arbitrarily close to θ0​ but never exactly reaches it. · ❌ The cooling curve is a straight line. ✅ The cooling curve θ(t) is an exponential decay, not a straight line. The slope dθ/dt decreases with time. Only the plot of ln(θ−θ0​) vs. t is a straight line. · ❌ The half-life depends on the initial temperature. ✅ The half-life t1/2​=ln2/K′ depends ONLY on the cooling constant, not on θi​. Each half-life reduces the excess by half, regardless of the starting excess.

17.
Label

More ideas

Text

❌ A larger K′ means slower cooling. ✅ A larger K′ means FASTER cooling. K′=k/(mc) — larger k (better heat transfer) or smaller mc (less thermal inertia) gives faster cooling. · ❌ The exponential solution only works for cooling, not heating. ✅ The same equation θ(t)−θ0​=(θi​−θ0​)e−K′t works for both. For heating, (θi​−θ0​) is negative, so θ(t) increases toward θ0​. · ❌ After one time constant, the body has cooled halfway. ✅ After one time constant τ, the excess is 1/e≈36.8% of its initial value, not 50%. The half-life is t1/2​=τln2≈0.693τ.

Label

More ideas

Text

❌ You can use the approximate method (average rate) for large temperature differences. ✅ The approximate method ΔtΔθ​=K′(θavg​−θ0​) is only accurate for small temperature intervals. For large intervals, use the exact exponential formula. · ❌ Identical cooling conditions mean the bodies cool at the same rate of temperature fall. ✅ Identical conditions mean equal heat-loss constant k. The temperature fall rates can differ if the heat capacities (mc) differ. · ❌ If two bodies cool at the same temperature rate, they must be the same material. ✅ They must have the same heat capacity (mc), but different combinations are possible: a small mass of high-c material can equal a large mass of low-c material.

Label

More ideas

Text

❌ The calorimeter's heat capacity can be ignored in the method of cooling. ✅ The calorimeter absorbs/releases the same heat as the liquid. For accurate results, include W (water equivalent) in the equation. · ❌ Two bodies with the same mass always cool at the same rate under identical conditions. ✅ Same mass is not enough — they must also have the same specific heat (same mc). Different materials with the same mass cool at different rates. · ❌ The method of cooling requires equal masses of the two liquids. ✅ It requires equal volumes in identical calorimeters. The masses should be measured and used in the calculation. Equal volumes don't guarantee equal masses if densities differ.
