Conditions

Text

❌ Newton's law of cooling tells you the exact time of death. ✅ It gives an ESTIMATE. The model ignores many factors (clothing, body size, air movement, humidity) that affect real cooling rates. · ❌ The law works equally well for very large temperature differences (e.g., a red-hot iron in air). ✅ For large ΔT, radiation (∝T4) dominates over convection, and Newton's linear law becomes inaccurate.

Limitation

Text

❌ A larger object always cools faster. ✅ Larger objects have larger heat capacity (mc), which slows cooling. The cooling RATE depends on the ratio k/(mc), not just on size. · ❌ Newton's law of cooling is the same as the Stefan-Boltzmann law. ✅ Newton's law is linear (dθ/dt∝θ−θ0​). Stefan-Boltzmann is quartic (∝T4−T04​). Newton's law is an approximation valid for small ΔT.

Newton's law of cooling is an example of a "linear first-order differential equation" — the same mathematical form appears in RC circuits (dQ/dt=−Q/RC), radioactive decay (dN/dt=−λN), and chemical reaction kinetics. · The time constant τ=mc/k has units of time and appears in countless physical, chemical, and biological processes. Study notes

Text

Purpose of Newton's law of cooling: The law provides a simple mathematical model to predict how the temperature of a body changes with time when it is placed in a surroundings of constant temperature. · Forensic science — estimating time of death: A corpse cools according to Newton's law after death (the "algor mortis" process). If body temperature is measured at time t, and the ambient temperature is known, the law estimates how long ago death occurred: θ(t)=θ0​+(θbody​−θ0​)e−K′t. · Engineering — cooling system design: Engineers use Newton's law to size radiators, heat sinks, and cooling fans. For a component dissipating power P, the steady-state temperature rise is ΔT=P/(kA), where k is the heat transfer coefficient.
