Limitation

Text

❌ The equilibrium temperature is the arithmetic mean of the two initial temperatures. ✅ Only true if m1​c1​=m2​c2​ (equal heat capacities). Generally: θeq​=m1​c1​+m2​c2​m1​c1​θ1​+m2​c2​θ2​​ - a WEIGHTED average. · ❌ Heat lost by hot body equals heat gained by cold body even if heat escapes to surroundings. ✅ This equality holds ONLY for an isolated (adiabatic) system. In practice, some heat is always lost, making the equation an approximation.

Derivation

Text

Statement: When two or more bodies at different temperatures are brought into thermal contact in an isolated system, heat flows from hotter to colder bodies until they reach a common final temperature (thermal equilibrium). · Core principle: In the absence of heat loss to the surroundings: Heat lost by hot bodies = Heat gained by cold bodies.

13.

Universal facts

Text

The calorimeter was invented by Joseph Black in the 1760s. He used an ice calorimeter - measuring heat by the amount of ice melted - which was more accurate than temperature-based methods because ice melts at a fixed temperature. · The principle of calorimetry is a special case of the conservation of energy. It was one of the first applications of energy conservation to thermal phenomena, predating the formal statement of the first law of thermodynamics by decades.
