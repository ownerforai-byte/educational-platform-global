Conditions

Text

❌ Heat and temperature are the same thing. ✅ Temperature is the average molecular kinetic energy (intensive). Heat is the total energy transferred (extensive). A spark at 2000°C has tiny heat content; a bathtub at 40°C has huge heat content. · ❌ A body "contains" heat. ✅ A body contains internal energy (kinetic + potential of its molecules). Heat is the TRANSFER of energy due to temperature difference. Once energy is in the body, it's no longer "heat." Think of it like "payment" - once received, it's no longer a payment; it's just money in your account.

The calorie was originally defined as the heat needed to raise 1 gram of water by 1°C - specifically from 14.5°C to 15.5°C, since water's specific heat varies slightly with temperature. The modern value is 1 cal=4.184 J (exact). · Water's anomalously high specific heat (4200 J/kg⋅K) is due to hydrogen bonding. Study notes

Text

Heat is energy in transit: Heat (H) is the thermal energy transferred between systems due to a temperature difference. It is NOT a property of a body - you cannot say "a body contains heat." A body contains internal energy; heat is the transfer of that energy. · Mass dependency (H ∝ m): For a given substance and temperature change, the heat required is directly proportional to the mass: H∝m. Doubling the mass doubles the heat needed. This is because more molecules means more total kinetic energy must change. · Temperature dependency (H ∝ Δθ): For a given mass, heat is directly proportional to the temperature change: H∝Δθ, where Δθ=θf​−θi​ (final − initial). Heating water from 20°C to 80°C requires 3× the heat of heating from 20°C to 40°C.
