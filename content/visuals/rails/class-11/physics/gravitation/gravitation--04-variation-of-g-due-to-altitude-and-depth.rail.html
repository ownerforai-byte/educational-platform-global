Concept

Text

g varies with altitude (g′=g(R+hR​)2), depth (g′=g(1−Rd​)), and latitude. Above the surface g falls as the inverse square of distance; below the surface the shell theorem makes it fall linearly to zero at the centre. The g–r graph is a straight rise then a 1/r2 fall.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Altitude Formula: Above the surface (h≥0), g′=g(R/(R+h))2 follows the inverse-square law exactly. For h≪R, the binomial approximation gives g′≈g(1−2h/R) — the factor of 2 comes from the square in the denominator. · Depth Formula: Below the surface (d≤R), only the mass interior to radius R−d contributes (Shell Theorem). Since mass ∝(R−d)3 and distance ∝(R−d), g′=g(1−d/R) — a linear decrease to zero at the center.

Label

Special cases

Text

Small-height formula: g′≈g(1−R2h​) — remember the factor 2. · Shell theorem is the tool for points inside Earth — shells outside contribute zero net force.

Study notes

Text

g above the surface (altitude): at height h above Earth's surface, g′=(R+h)2GM​=g(R+hR​)2. As h increases, g decreases. For small h (h≪R), a binomial expansion gives g′≈g(1−R2h​) — a useful approximation. · g below the surface (depth): at depth d, only the mass inside the concentric sphere of radius (R−d) contributes: g′=g(1−Rd​) — gravity DECREASES linearly with depth. At the centre (d=R), g′=0. · Why they differ: above the surface, whole-earth mass acts from a larger distance (1/r2 law); below the surface, the shell above the point exerts zero net force (shell theorem) and only the inner sphere matters, whose mass scales as (R−d)^3 while distance scales as (R−d) — giving linear decrease.

Surface Maximum: g reaches its maximum at Earth's surface. It decreases in both directions: 1/r2 outside, linear inside. The graph of g vs r is triangular-like: linear rise from center to surface, then inverse-square fall. · The g–r graph: linear rise inside, 1/r2 fall outside. · ❌ 'Deep inside Earth, g keeps growing.' ✅ It decreases linearly: g′=g(1−Rd​), zero at the centre. The shell above you pulls equally in all directions (zero net).
