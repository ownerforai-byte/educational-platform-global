Conditions

Text

COM Definition: The centre of mass Rcm​=∑mi​ri​/∑mi​ is the mass-weighted average position. It is the point where the entire mass can be considered concentrated for translational motion analysis. · CG Definition: The centre of gravity is the point where the entire weight acts. In a uniform gravitational field, CG exactly coincides with COM. In a non-uniform field (e.g., very large bodies), they differ because the torque balance for weight involves the varying g field.

Special cases

Text

In a uniform gravitational field the location of CG equals COM — that is almost always true in school problems. · For symmetric uniform bodies, COM = geometric centre (no calculation needed).

Limitation

Text

❌ 'COM and CG are always at the material centre.' ✅ Only for uniform, symmetric bodies. A ring's COM is at its empty centre; an L-shaped body has its COM outside its material. · ❌ 'COM and CG are always the same point.' ✅ They coincide in a UNIFORM gravitational field. For very large objects in a non-uniform field, they differ slightly.

Study notes

Text

Centre of mass (COM): the point where the entire mass of a body (or system) may be considered to be concentrated for translational (linear) motion analysis. Position: Rcm​=Σmi​Σmi​ri​​. For continuous bodies: Rcm​=M∫rdm​. · Centre of gravity (CG): the point where the entire weight of a body may be considered to act. In a uniform gravitational field, the CG coincides exactly with the COM. If the field is non-uniform (very large bodies, varying g), the CG may shift slightly relative to the COM. · Why CG equals COM in uniform g: weight W=mg at each point, and since g is the same everywhere, the torque balance condition that defines the CG reduces to the same weighted average that defines the COM. The two centres coincide.
