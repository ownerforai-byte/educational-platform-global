Concept

Text

The Global Positioning System uses ≥ 4 satellites whose positions and times are known to compute a receiver's (x, y, z, t) by measuring signal travel times (d = c·Δt). The constellation orbits at ≈ 20,200 km with ≈ 12 h periods, and relativistic clock corrections of ~38 µs/day are essential to maintain metre-level accuracy.

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

What GPS is: the Global Positioning System is a satellite-based navigation system that tells a receiver its exact 3D position on Earth. It uses the same gravitational/orbital physics as any satellite, which is why it lives in the gravitation chapter. · Principle of position fixing: each GPS satellite broadcasts a radio signal stamped with its position and precise transmission time. The receiver measures the travel time of signals from at least four satellites and solves for (x, y, z, t) — the fourth equation corrects the receiver clock offset. · How distance is measured: distance = speed of light × time delay. Since the receiver's clock is not atomic-precise, a fourth satellite provides the clock-error term, giving accuracy of a few metres.
