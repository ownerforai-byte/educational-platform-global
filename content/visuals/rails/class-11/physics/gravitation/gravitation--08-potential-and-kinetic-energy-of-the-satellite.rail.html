Concept

Text

Gravitational potential V = −GM/r is the work per unit mass to bring a test mass from infinity; potential energy U = −GMm/r is the negative binding energy of two masses. Near the surface U ≈ mgh. The field relates to the potential by g = −dV/dr, and circular orbits have total energy E = −GMm/2r, confirming their bound nature.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

❌ 'Potential and potential energy are the same.' ✅ Potential V is work per unit mass (J/kg); potential energy U = mV (J). V belongs to a point in the field; U belongs to the mass–field system. · ❌ 'Negative energy is unphysical.' ✅ Negative potential energy simply means the system is BOUND — energy must be added to separate it. It is a matter of the reference at infinity.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Both V and U are defined with reference zero at infinity — that is why they are negative. · Use U = mgh only for small heights (h ≪ R); use −GMm/r for space-scale problems.

Limitation

Text

❌ 'U = mgh and U = −GMm/r disagree.' ✅ They agree for small heights: mgh is the CHANGE in −GMm/r near the surface. Use the right one for the régime. · ❌ 'Potential is maximum at Earth's surface.' ✅ Potential is most negative at the surface (−GM/R) and rises toward zero with altitude — at infinity it reaches the (higher) value 0.

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Gravitational potential V: the work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to a point, without acceleration. For a point mass M: V = −GM/r. At infinity V = 0, and everywhere else V is negative because gravity does positive work as the mass approaches — the field does the work, so potential decreases. · Gravitational potential energy U: the work done against gravity in assembling a system; for two masses separated by r: U = −GMm/r. It is the energy of the whole system, not of one mass alone. U is negative because the system is bound — you must supply GMm/r of energy to separate the masses to infinity. · Why the minus sign matters: potential energy is defined with zero at infinity. Since the force is attractive, energy is released (and PE decreases) as masses come together. A satellite in orbit at radius r has U = −GMm/r — its total mechanical energy is E = K + U = −GMm/2r, which is negative, confirming a bound orbit.

It needs enormous kinetic energy (orbital speed) that comes from the launch energy.
