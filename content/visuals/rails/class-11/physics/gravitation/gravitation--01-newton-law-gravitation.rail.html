Newton's law of gravitation states that every mass attracts every other mass with F=r2Gm1​m2​​. The universal constant G=6.67×10−11 N⋅m2/kg2 couples mass and distance to force. 3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Universal Scope: Newton's law (F=Gm1​m2​/r2) applies to all masses in the universe, at all distances. It is exact for point masses or spherically symmetric bodies (Shell Theorem). For non-spherical objects, integration or numerical methods are required. · Inverse-Square Nature: The 1/r2 dependence means the force has infinite range but drops rapidly. It never reaches zero at any finite distance. This inverse-square law is equivalent to saying gravitational flux through a closed surface is proportional to enclosed mass (Gauss's law for gravity).

Special cases

Text

Use the centre-to-centre distance in r — for spheres this is the distance between centres. · G is the same everywhere; g is NOT — it varies with location and height.

Solved

Label

Limitation

Text

❌ 'There is a force of gravity only near Earth.' ✅ Gravity acts between ANY two masses anywhere in the universe — it never switches off, it only weakens with the square of distance. · ❌ 'G can change with temperature or medium.' ✅ G is a universal constant — independent of temperature, pressure, medium, or the objects involved.

Study notes

Text

Law statement: every particle in the universe attracts every other particle with a force that is proportional to the product of their masses and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between their centres: F=r2Gm1​m2​​. · The universal constant G: G=6.67×10−11 N m2/kg2. It is the same everywhere in the universe (hence 'universal') and was first measured by Cavendish (1798) using a torsion balance. G is very small, which is why the gravitational attraction between everyday objects is imperceptible. · Vector form & direction: the force on mass m₁ due to m₂ is F12​=−Gr2m1​m2​​r^12​, directed along the line joining the centres, always attractive. Newton's third law applies: F12​=−F21​.
