Thin Lens Approximation: The lens maker's formula f1​=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​) assumes a thin lens (thickness ≪ radii of curvature). For thick lenses, the full Gullstrand formula with principal planes must be used. · Sign Convention for Radii: Using the Cartesian sign convention (light travels left to right): R is positive if the center of curvature is to the right of the surface (convex toward incident light), negative if to the left (concave). This is opposite to some older conventions.

❌ The formula 1/f=(n−1)(1/R1​−1/R2​) is the lens formula. ✅ This is the LENS MAKER'S FORMULA — it calculates f from the lens geometry. The signs are opposite, giving a positive 1/f and positive f (converging lens).

The lens maker's formula was first derived geometrically by Descartes in 1637, building on earlier work by Kepler. Newton's formulation introduced the connection to the lens's refractive power and laid the groundwork for modern lens design. · High-index glass (n=1.7 to 1.9) is used in modern eyeglass lenses to reduce thickness, especially for strong prescriptions. A -10 D lens in standard glass (n=1.5) might be 5 mm thick at the edge, but only 2-3 mm thick in high-index glass.

Study notes

Text

Lens maker's formula. For a thin lens of refractive index μ₂ in a medium μ₁, 1/f = (μ₂/μ₁ − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂); in air this becomes 1/f = (μ − 1)(1/R₁ − 1/R₂). · Sign convention. Radii are measured from the optical centre; R is positive when the centre of curvature lies on the far (transmitted) side of the surface. · What decides f. The focal length depends only on the lens material and the two surface radii, not on the object position or the object's distance.
