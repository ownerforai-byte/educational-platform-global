Title

Convex lens ray diagram: locating the real image

Href

Rows

1.
Label

Concept

Text

Two rays fix the image of any object: the ray parallel to the principal axis leaves through the far focus F', and the ray through the optical centre passes straight on. Where they cross is where the image forms.

Caption

Object beyond F: the parallel ray and the central ray cross where the real, inverted image forms.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

1/v − 1/u = 1/f; m = v/u = h'/h; P = 1/f in dioptres

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Thin lens, paraxial rays, and the Cartesian sign convention — distances measured against the incident light are negative.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Object at 2F gives an image at 2F, same size and inverted; an object inside F gives a virtual, upright, magnified image on the same side.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

f = 20 cm, u = −30 cm → 1/v = 1/20 − 1/30 = 1/60 → v = +60 cm and m = v/u = −2, an inverted image twice as large.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

The construction ignores lens thickness and aberrations, so it predicts a sharp image where a real lens gives a blurred one.

7.
Label

Derivation

Kind

formula

Text

Parallel ray refracted through F'; central ray undeviated; similar triangles give h'/h = v/u and the thin-lens relation 1/v − 1/u = 1/f

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Draw the parallel ray first and the central ray second — their crossing IS the image; sketch before you calculate.

9.
Label

Board question

Text

Draw a ray diagram for a convex lens with the object beyond 2F, locate the image and state its nature.
