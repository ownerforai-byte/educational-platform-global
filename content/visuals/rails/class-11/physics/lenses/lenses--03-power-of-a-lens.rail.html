The power of a lens is P=1/f with f in metres, measured in dioptres (D): convex lenses positive, concave negative; a shorter focal length is a stronger lens. Thin lenses in contact simply add powers: P = P₁ + P₂ — NEB 2024's +5 D and −2 D pair gives +3 D, f = 100/3 ≈ 33.3 cm (2m).…

Text

Lens Power Definition: Power P=1/f (in meters) measured in diopters (D). A converging lens has P>0, a diverging lens has P<0. Power is additive for thin lenses in contact: Ptotal​=∑Pi​. · Combination of Thin Lenses: For thin lenses in contact, the total focal length is given by 1/ftotal​=∑1/fi​, or equivalently Ptotal​=∑Pi​. This assumes negligible separation between lenses. For separated lenses, use the equivalent focal length formula including the separation d.

1. Topic Introduction — Measuring a Lens in Dioptres Opticians never quote focal lengths; they quote power — the reciprocal of focal length in dioptres. A +3 D lens bends light three times as strongly as a +1 D lens, and contact prescriptions, camera lenses and spectacles all speak this language. · ## 2. Conceptual Relationships - To Topic 77: P = 1/f converts between the two lens "currencies". - To Topic 78: the lens maker's formula gives f, hence P, at design time. - To combinations: adding powers is the optical analogue of adding conductances in parallel.

More ideas

Text

Power vs Magnification: Lens power P=1/f relates to focal length, not directly to magnification. Magnification m=v/u=f/(f−u) depends on object distance. A high-power lens (short f) gives larger magnification for a given object distance, but m is not simply proportional to P. · Verified PYQs: combination of powers 2024 2m; myopia and hypermetropia 2024 3m. · "The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length expressed in metres."
