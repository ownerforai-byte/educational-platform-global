The triangle, parallelogram and polygon laws are the graphical and analytical foundations of vector addition. All three lead to the master resultant formula R=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ​ with direction α=tan−1(Qsinθ/(P+Qcosθ)). Conditions

Text

❌ 'The triangle law and parallelogram law give different resultants.' ✅ They are mathematically EQUIVALENT — the triangle is half the parallelogram, so the same diagonal/resultant appears in both. · ❌ Measuring the angle between the two sides INSIDE the parallelogram. ✅ θ is the angle between the vectors placed TAIL-TO-TAIL at the common origin — not the parallelogram's obtuse interior angle.

Text

Note 1: Always draw the vectors tail-to-tail when using the parallelogram law — the wrong angle is the #1 exam error. · Note 2: Memorise the three equilibrium fingerprints: closed polygon, three equal forces at 120°, and Lami's theorem.

Solved

Example 1 (Pythagoras case): a boat is rowed at 4 m/s across a river flowing at 3 m/s — resultant speed =42+32​=5 m/s at tan−1(3/4)≈36.9° from the boat's heading. · Example 2 (equal vectors): two ropes each pull with 200 N at 60° apart — resultant =2(200)cos30°=346 N along the bisector; this is why wide angles waste pulling force.

Limitation

Text

❌ 'The resultant of three vectors is always bigger than each.' ✅ Not necessarily — vectors can partially or fully cancel; a closed polygon gives R=0, smaller than every individual magnitude. · ❌ 'The equilibrant is another name for the resultant.' ✅ They are OPPOSITE: equilibrant E=−R. Same magnitude, opposite direction — the force that balances the system.

Key points

Text

Triangle, parallelogram and polygon laws are three views of the same addition rule — use whichever suits the number of vectors. · The cross-term 2PQcosθ is what makes vector addition different from ordinary addition. · A closed polygon of vectors ⇒ zero resultant ⇒ equilibrium. · Lami's theorem works only for three concurrent forces in equilibrium.

The parallelogram of forces was stated by Isaac Newton in the Principia (1687); Simon Stevin had already used the triangle of forces for inclined-plane problems a century earlier. · Surveyors' 'traverse closure' is the polygon law in action: after measuring a circuit of angles and distances, the failure of the final polygon to close (closure error) quantifies the survey's accuracy.

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Scalars and vectors: a scalar has only magnitude (mass, time, speed, energy, temperature); a vector has magnitude AND direction (displacement, velocity, force, momentum, electric field). Vectors are written A or bold A, with magnitude ∣A∣=A. · The three addition laws answer one question — 'what is the single vector that has the same effect as several together?' (1) Triangle law (two vectors): place them head-to-tail; the resultant runs from the free tail to the free head. (2) Parallelogram law (two vectors from one point): draw both from a common origin; the diagonal through that origin is the resultant.… · The master formula (obtained from either the triangle or parallelogram derivation): R=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ​ with direction α=tan−1(P+QcosθQsinθ​) from P. Every special case follows from it.
