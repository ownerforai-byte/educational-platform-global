Study notes

Text

Statement: If two vectors acting at a point are represented in magnitude and direction by the two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from that point, their resultant is represented in magnitude and direction by the diagonal of the parallelogram passing through the same point. · Setup: let P and Q​ be two vectors acting at point A, represented by adjacent sides AB and AD of parallelogram ABCD. The diagonal AC represents the resultant R. The angle between P and Q​ is θ. · Derivation of magnitude: draw CE perpendicular to AB extended. In right triangle BCE: CE=Qsinθ and BE=Qcosθ. Then AE=AB+BE=P+Qcosθ.

The direction formula changes between the two laws — The direction formula α=tan−1(Qsinθ/(P+Qcosθ)) is identical for both the triangle and parallelogram laws, since they are geometrically equivalent. · You need to draw a full parallelogram to use the law — No. You can use just the triangle (half the parallelogram) for calculations. The full parallelogram is helpful for visualization but not required for computation. · The parallelogram law and triangle law are different — They are mathematically identical. The triangle is exactly half the parallelogram. Both give the same R and α.

Text

The parallelogram law fails for three or more vectors — It extends naturally: for three vectors, first find R12​=P+Q​ using the parallelogram, then combine R12​ with R using the law again. Or use the polygon law for 3+ vectors directly. · The angle θ in the formula is the angle inside the triangle — θ is the angle between the two vectors when placed tail-to-tail, NOT the interior angle of the triangle. If the interior angle is ϕ, then θ=180°−ϕ. · The resultant always points between the two vectors — This is only true for 0°<θ<180°. When vectors are opposite (θ=180°), the resultant lies along the larger vector, not between them.

More ideas

Text

Triangle law only works for forces — Triangle law applies to ALL vectors: displacement, velocity, acceleration, electric field, magnetic field, momentum. Any quantity that is a vector obeys this law. · If the triangle doesn't close, the vectors are wrong — An open triangle means the resultant is the closing side. The triangle law says the resultant IS the third side taken in the opposite order — the triangle is open by design. · The direction formula tanα=Qsinθ/(P+Qcosθ) is only for the triangle law — This formula is actually derived from the triangle law but is identical to the one from the parallelogram law. Both methods give the same α.

A zero resultant means the triangle has no area — A zero resultant means the two vectors are equal and opposite (P=Q, θ=180°), forming a degenerate triangle (a line segment), not a triangle with zero area in the usual sense. · You can use the triangle law for more than two vectors directly — The triangle law is specifically for two vectors. For three or more, you apply it successively or use the polygon law. A+B+C requires two applications of the triangle law. · The order of vectors in the triangle matters for the resultant's direction — The triangle law is commutative: P+Q​=Q​+P. Swapping the order just reflects the triangle but gives the same resultant vector.
