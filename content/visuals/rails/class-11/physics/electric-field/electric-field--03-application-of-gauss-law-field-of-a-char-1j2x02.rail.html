Derivation

Text

Gauss's law. The total electric flux through any closed surface equals the enclosed charge divided by ε₀: Φ_E = ∮E·dA = q_in/ε₀; the surface is called a Gaussian surface. · Charged sphere. For a uniformly charged spherical shell, E = kQ/r² outside the shell and E = 0 inside; for a solid insulating sphere the inner field grows linearly with r.

Label

Study notes

Text

Line charge. For an infinite line of charge with linear density λ, a cylindrical Gaussian surface gives E = λ/2πε₀r — the field falls off as 1/r. · Infinite plane sheet. For a uniformly charged infinite sheet with surface density σ, E = σ/2ε₀ on each side — uniform and independent of distance. · Conductor surface. Just outside a charged conductor E = σ/ε₀, and the field inside the conductor's material is zero at electrostatic equilibrium.
