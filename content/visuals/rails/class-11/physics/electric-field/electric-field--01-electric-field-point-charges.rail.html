3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Confusing field line direction with trajectory of a moving charge: An electric field line represents the direction of electrostatic force on a stationary positive charge, NOT the trajectory of a moving charge. A moving charge accelerates along F=qE, so velocity and field vectors generally diverge due to inertia. · Assuming field lines can intersect: If two field lines crossed, a test charge at the intersection would experience two distinct electrostatic forces in different directions simultaneously, which violates the uniqueness theorem of vector fields.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Electrostatic field lines do not form closed loops because electrostatic fields are conservative: the closed loop line integral ∮ E · dr = 0. · A charged particle projected perpendicularly into a uniform electric field follows a parabolic trajectory, analogous to projectile motion in a uniform gravitational field.

Solved

Text

Inkjet printers: Electrically deflected microscopic ink droplets pass between high-voltage deflection plates generating a uniform electric field, steering droplets precisely onto paper to render text and images. · Cathode Ray Oscilloscope (CRO): An electron beam accelerated by an electron gun is deflected horizontally and vertically by orthogonal electric fields between parallel plates, projecting waveforms onto a fluorescent screen.

Label

Limitation

Text

Confusing the radial fall-off of dipoles versus point charges: Point charge field scales as 1/r2, whereas an electric dipole field decays as 1/r3 because the positive and negative charges largely cancel each other out at long distances (r≫a).

Key points

Text

Electric field intensity is force per unit positive test charge (E=F/q0​) in N/C or V/m. · Point charge field obeys inverse square law (E∝1/r2); short dipole field obeys inverse cube law (E∝1/r3). · Field lines point outward from positive charges and inward toward negative charges, never intersecting. · Axial field of a short dipole is exactly twice the magnitude of equatorial field at equal distance (Eaxial​=2Eequatorial​).
