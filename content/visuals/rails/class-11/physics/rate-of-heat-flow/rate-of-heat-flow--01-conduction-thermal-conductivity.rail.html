Concept

Text

Conduction: Heat transfer through a material without any bulk motion of the material itself. Energy is transferred from molecule to molecule via collisions and lattice vibrations (phonons). In metals, free electrons also carry thermal energy, making conduction highly efficient.

Derivation

Text

Fourier's law of heat conduction: The rate of heat flow through a material is given by H=tQ​=kALT1​−T2​​, where k is the thermal conductivity, A is the cross-sectional area, T1​−T2​ is the temperature difference, and L is the length. In differential form: H=−kAdxdT​. The negative sign ensures heat flows from hot to cold. · Thermal conductivity k: A material property that quantifies how well a material conducts heat. Units: W/(m⋅K). Metals have high k (copper: ~400 W/(m·K)), insulators have low k (wood: ~0.1 W/(m·K)), and gases have very low k (air: ~0.025 W/(m·K)).

Study notes

Text

Why metals conduct heat well: In metals, free electrons are mobile and can move rapidly through the lattice, colliding with ions and carrying energy. These free electrons act as efficient energy carriers, giving metals their high thermal conductivity. · Fayer's apparatus: Used to measure thermal conductivity of liquids. A steam jacket surrounds the liquid-filled cylinder. Thermometers at known distances measure the temperature gradient. By measuring the heat flow and temperature difference, k can be calculated. · Temperature dependence of k: For metals, k DECREASES with increasing temperature because lattice vibrations scatter electrons more frequently. For non-metals and gases, k generally INCREASES with temperature as molecular motion increases.

More ideas

Text

❌ The rate of heat flow is the same as the temperature. ✅ Heat flow rate (H, in Watts) and temperature (in Kelvin) are completely different quantities. A large temperature difference across a small resistance gives high heat flow. · ❌ Fourier's law applies only to steady-state conditions. ✅ Fourier's law applies at every instant — both steady state and transient conditions. The differential form H=−kAdxdT​ holds at each point in time. · ❌ Air is a good conductor of heat. ✅ Air is a poor conductor (k≈0.025 W/(m·K)). However, air is an excellent convective medium — convection dominates heat transfer in air, not conduction.

Thermal resistance: Analogous to electrical resistance. Rth​=kAL​. The heat flow equation becomes H=Rth​ΔT​, directly analogous to Ohm's law I=RV​. For composite walls, resistances add: Rtotal​=R1​+R2​+R3​+... · Thermal conductivity vs. temperature gradient: The heat flow depends on the TEMPERATURE GRADIENT (ΔT/L), not just the temperature difference. A large ΔT across a thick slab may produce less heat flow than a small ΔT across a thin slab. · Application — building insulation: Double-glazed windows use an air gap (kair​≈0.025) between glass panes. The air gap provides high thermal resistance. Adding argon gas (even lower k) further reduces heat transfer.
