Limitation

Text

EMF is not a force - it is a voltage (energy per unit charge). The name is historical. EMF is measured in volts, not newtons. · Terminal voltage V=mathcalE−Ir is less than EMF when current flows out of the cell. But when charging the cell, current flows into the positive terminal, so V=mathcalE+Ir - the terminal voltage is greater than EMF.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Internal resistance r: the resistance inside the cell due to electrolyte and electrodes. It causes the terminal voltage to drop when current flows. · Terminal voltage: V=E−Ir. When no current flows (I=0), V=E. Under load, V<E.

10.
Label

Hints

Text

In series, both EMF and internal resistance add. In parallel, the equivalent EMF is the same as one cell, but the equivalent internal resistance is reduced, allowing more current. · Maximum power transfer (R=r) occurs at 50% efficiency. For efficient power delivery, you want Rggr (high efficiency, low power). For maximum power, you sacrifice efficiency.

11.
Label

Universal facts

Text

In a battery pack, series strings are often used to increase voltage, and parallel strings within each series group are used to increase capacity and reduce internal resistance.

Study notes

Text

Cells in series: Etotal​=E1​+E2​+…, rtotal​=r1​+r2​+…. Series adds both EMF and internal resistance. · Cells in parallel: Etotal​=E (if all equal), rtotal​=r/n for n identical cells. Parallel reduces internal resistance, allowing higher current delivery. · Maximum power transfer: the power delivered to an external load R is maximum when R=r. The maximum power is Pmax​=E2/(4r).
