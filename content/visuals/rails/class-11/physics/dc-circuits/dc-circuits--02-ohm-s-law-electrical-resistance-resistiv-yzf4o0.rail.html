Ohm's law: at constant temperature, V ∝ I in a metallic conductor, or V = IR with R the resistance (ohm). Resistance grows with length and shrinks with area: R=ρl/A, where resistivity ρ (Ω·m) is a material property (copper 1.7×10⁻⁸) and conductivity σ = 1/ρ its inverse. Temperature raises metals' resistance and lowers semiconductors'.…

Conditions

Text

Ohm's Law Validity: Ohm's law (V=IR) applies strictly to ohmic conductors at constant temperature. For metals, resistance increases with temperature, so the law holds only if temperature is held constant (isothermal conditions). · Resistivity vs. Resistance: Resistivity (ho) is an intensive material property (independent of geometry), while resistance (R=hoL/A) is an extensive property depending on the conductor's length and cross-section. Temperature changes affect ho, which in turn changes R.
