Text

Quantity of heat is the energy transferred due to temperature difference. Specific heat capacity, heat capacity, latent heat, and calorimetry principle govern heat transfer calculations. Q = mcΔT for temperature change and Q = mL for phase change.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Heat Capacity Definition: Heat capacity C = ΔQ/ΔT is the heat required to raise temperature by 1 K. Specific heat capacity c = C/m is per unit mass (J/kg·K). Molar heat capacity Cₘ = C/n is per mole (J/mol·K). · Phase Change Latent Heat: During phase change (melting, boiling), temperature is constant while heat is absorbed/released as latent heat: Q = mL. Latent heat of fusion L_f (solid↔liquid) and vaporization L_v (liquid↔gas) are material properties.

Solved

Text

Heat needed to raise 2 kg water from 20°C to 80°C: Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4186 × 60 = 502,320 J ≈ 502 kJ. · Heat to melt 0.5 kg ice at 0°C: Q = mL_f = 0.5 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 167,000 J = 167 kJ.

Limitation

Text

Confusing heat capacity (for whole body) with specific heat capacity (per unit mass). · Thinking temperature rises during phase change: Temperature stays constant during melting/boiling.

Derivation

Text

Heat: Energy transferred between bodies due to temperature difference. SI unit: joule (J). Heat flows from higher temperature to lower temperature. · Heat Capacity: C = Q/ΔT is the heat required to raise the temperature of a body by 1 K (or 1°C). Unit: J/K. Depends on mass and material of the body.

Specific Heat Capacity: c = C/m = Q/(mΔT) is the heat required to raise unit mass of a substance by 1 K. Unit: J/(kg·K).
