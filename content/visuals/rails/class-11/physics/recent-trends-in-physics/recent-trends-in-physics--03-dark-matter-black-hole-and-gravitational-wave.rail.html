Dark matter: A form of matter that does not emit, absorb, or reflect electromagnetic radiation — it is completely invisible to all telescopes. Its presence is inferred solely from gravitational effects on visible matter, light, and the large-scale structure of the universe. Dark matter comprises approximately 27% of the universe's total energy density. · Evidence for dark matter — galaxy rotation curves: Stars in spiral galaxies orbit the galactic center. Newtonian mechanics predicts orbital velocity should decrease with distance from the center (v∝1/r​). Instead, rotation curves remain FLAT at large radii — stars maintain the same orbital speed far from the visible disk.… · Evidence — gravitational lensing: Dark matter bends light from background galaxies through gravitational lensing. The pattern and strength of lensing arcs around galaxy clusters reveal the total mass distribution, which is consistently much larger than the visible mass.…
