Study notes

Text

Elementary particles: Particles with no known internal structure or substructure. They are the fundamental constituents of matter and radiation under the Standard Model of particle physics. Elementary particles fall into two classes: fermions (matter) and bosons (force carriers). · Fermions (spin-21​): Obey the Pauli exclusion principle. They include quarks and leptons. Quarks combine to form hadrons (baryons, mesons); leptons do not participate in the strong interaction. There are three generations of each. · Bosons (integer spin): Do NOT obey the exclusion principle; they can occupy the same quantum state (lasers, BEC). Force-carrying bosons: photon (gamma, EM), gluon (g, strong), W^\\pm/Z^0 (weak), and the Higgs boson (H0, scalar, spin-0).

Confusion: Not all particles with the same quantum numbers can annihilate. An electron and proton (both fermions, opposite charge) can form positroniumium but cannot annihilate into pure photons - charge conservation forbids e−+p→gamma+gamma, so they form Hydrogen instead. · Confusion: Top quark (t) and bottom quark (b) are also called truth (t) and beauty (b) in older literature. The IUPAP standard names (top/bottom) are now universally used. Charmonium (ccˉ) and bottomonium (bbˉ) are real quarkonium states. · Confusion: Color charge is an abstract SU(3) gauge charge analogous to electric charge, but with THREE types (+red, +green, +blue) instead of two. It's called "color" because it produces a color-force law analogous to electromagnetism - not because quarks have visible color.
