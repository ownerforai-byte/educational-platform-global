Conditions

Text

Vertical Angle Limit: For the conical pendulum to exist (cosheta≤1), the angular velocity must satisfy ω≥g/L​. Below this speed, the bob cannot maintain a horizontal circular path and will simply hang at the bottom. · String Tension Constraint: The tension in the string is always greater than the weight of the bob (T=mg/cosheta>mg) because it must support the weight and provide the centripetal force.

Limitation

Text

❌ 'The tension in the string equals mg.' ✅ The vertical component of tension equals mg. The total tension is greater: T = mg/cos θ (since cos θ < 1, T > mg). · ❌ 'The bob is in equilibrium.' ✅ The bob is in vertical equilibrium (T cos θ = mg) but NOT horizontal equilibrium — it accelerates toward the centre (centripetal acceleration).

Quick concepts

Text

The period of a conical pendulum (T = 2π√(L cos θ/g)) is independent of the mass of the bob. · The centripetal force is provided by the horizontal component of tension: T sin θ = mω²r. · The vertical component of tension balances the weight: T cos θ = mg. · The faster the rotation, the larger the angle θ — the cone becomes flatter.

Label

Key points

Text

The period of a conical pendulum depends only on L cos θ (the vertical height of the cone), not on mass. · The minimum period (θ → 0) equals the period of a simple pendulum of the same length. · The tension is always greater than mg: T = mg/cos θ. · The centripetal force is the horizontal component of tension — no new force is added.

Label

Study notes

Text

Conical pendulum: a small bob of mass m attached to a string of length L, moving in a horizontal circle at constant speed such that the string traces out a cone. The string makes angle θ with the vertical and the bob moves in a circle of radius r = L sin θ. · Forces and geometry: two forces act on the bob: tension T along the string, and weight mg downward. The tension has vertical component T cos θ (balances weight) and horizontal component T sin θ (provides centripetal force). · Key equations: T cos θ = mg (vertical equilibrium) ... (1); T sin θ = mω²r = mω²L sin θ (centripetal) ... (2). From (2): T = mω²L. Substituting into (1): mω²L cos θ = mg → cos θ = g/(ω²L). Also: ω = √(g/L cos θ) and T_period = 2π√(L cos θ/g).
