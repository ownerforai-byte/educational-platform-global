The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution was first derived by James Clerk Maxwell in 1860 and later generalized by Ludwig Boltzmann. Limitation

Text

The ratio γ=CP​/CV​ is NOT always 5/3 or 7/5. It depends on temperature and molecular structure. At very high temperatures, vibrational modes activate, changing γ. · The formula CV​=23​R applies ONLY to monatomic ideal gases. For diatomic or polyatomic gases, rotational (and sometimes vibrational) modes contribute.

Derivation

Text

RMS speed: vrms​=3RT/M​, where M is molar mass in kg/mol. At STP (273 K), vrms​ for air is about 479 m/s. At 300 K, about 484 m/s. Lighter gases move faster on average. · Most probable speed: vp​=2RT/M​. This is the speed at which the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution peaks. At 300 K for air: vp​≈364 m/s. At 300 K for Hâ‚‚: vp​≈1650 m/s.

Shortcut

The Boltzmann constant kB​ was first introduced by Max Planck in his derivation of the blackbody radiation formula (1900). He used it to connect the energy of oscillators to temperature: E=kB​T. · Dulong and Petit's law (C≈3R for solids) was discovered in 1819.
