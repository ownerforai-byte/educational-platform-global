Concept

Text

The Boltzmann constant k = R/N_A = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K links macroscopic temperature to microscopic molecular energy. RMS speed v_rms = √(3kT/m) = √(3RT/M) characterizes the typical molecular speed in a gas at temperature T. At the same temperature, lighter molecules move faster; v_rms determines diffusion rates, effusion, and kinetic energy.

Conditions

Text

Ideal Gas Assumption: Formulas assume point particles with elastic collisions and no intermolecular forces. Real gases deviate at high P or low T. · Thermal Equilibrium Required: Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution and well-defined v_rms apply only at equilibrium. Non-equilibrium flows have different velocity distributions.

Special cases

Text

Statement 1: Boltzmann constant relates temperature to average molecular kinetic energy via ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT. · Statement 2: RMS speed is the square root of the mean of squared molecular speeds, not the arithmetic mean speed.

Solved

Text

Oxygen at 27°C (300 K): v_rms = √(3×8.314×300/0.032) = √(233,812) ≈ 483 m/s. · Hydrogen at 300 K: v_rms = √(3×8.314×300/0.002) ≈ 1920 m/s. Ratio to O₂: √(32/2) = 4×.

Label

Limitation

Text

Confusing v_rms with average speed: v_rms = √(⟨v²⟩) ≠ ⟨v⟩. They differ by ~8% for Maxwellian gases. · Using M in g/mol instead of kg/mol: Must convert to SI (kg/mol) for v_rms in m/s.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Boltzmann Constant Definition: k = R/N_A where R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) and N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Value: k = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K (exact since 2019 SI redefinition). It converts temperature (kelvin) into energy (joules) per particle. · Physical Significance of k: Average translational KE per molecule = (3/2)kT. At 300 K, ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)(1.38×10⁻²³)(300) ≈ 6.21 × 10⁻²¹ J ≈ 0.039 eV. This is the thermal energy scale governing molecular motion.

Study notes

Text

RMS Speed Formula: v_rms = √(⟨v²⟩) = √(3kT/m) = √(3RT/M), where m = molecular mass, M = molar mass. Derived from equating pressure expression P = (1/3)ρv²_rms with ideal gas law PV = nRT. · Speed Hierarchy: For Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution: v_mp = √(2kT/m) < v_avg = √(8kT/πm) < v_rms = √(3kT/m). Numerical ratio: v_mp : v_avg : v_rms ≈ 1.41 : 1.60 : 1.73 (or 1 : 1.13 : 1.22 normalized to v_mp). · Temperature Dependence: v_rms ∝ √T. Doubling absolute temperature increases v_rms by factor √2 ≈ 1.414. Cooling to 1/4 original T halves the rms speed.

Translational Motion Only: v_rms refers to center-of-mass translational speed. Rotational and vibrational energies contribute to total internal energy but not to v_rms. · Classical Regime: Quantum effects become significant when de Broglie wavelength λ = h/(mv) approaches interparticle spacing.
