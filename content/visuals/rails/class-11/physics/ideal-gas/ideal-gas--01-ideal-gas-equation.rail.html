Three empirical laws anchor gas behaviour: Boyle (PV = const at fixed T), Charles (V/T = const at fixed P) and the pressure law (P/T = const at fixed V). Combined for a fixed amount of gas they give P1​V1​/T1​=P2​V2​/T2​, and with amount included the ideal gas equation PV=nRT (R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹K⁻¹) or, per molecule, PV=NkB​T.…

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Ideal Gas Assumptions: The equation PV=nRT assumes (1) molecules are point particles with zero volume, (2) no intermolecular forces except during elastic collisions, (3) motion is random and obeys Newton's laws. These hold best at high temperature and low pressure; real gases deviate near condensation. · Universal Gas Constant: R=8.314extJ/mol⋅K is the same for all ideal gases. It connects macroscopic (P,V,n,T) to microscopic (kB​,NA​) via R=NA​kB​. The value depends on units: R=0.0821extL⋅atm/mol⋅K in common chemistry units.
