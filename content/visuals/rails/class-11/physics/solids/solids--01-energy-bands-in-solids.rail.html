In a solid the atomic levels spread into bands. A conductor has a partly filled band, or overlapping bands, so electrons can take a little extra energy and move. An insulator has a full valence band and a large gap. 15.

Study notes

Text

When many atoms form a solid, interactions split atomic energy levels into closely spaced allowed bands separated by forbidden energy gaps. Electrons cannot occupy energies in an ideal band gap. · The valence band contains bonding electrons; the conduction band contains mobile carriers. The gap Eg=Ec−Ev and the band filling help explain conductivity. · Metals have partially filled bands or overlapping bands. Insulators have a large gap and very few room-temperature carriers. Semiconductors have a smaller gap, so thermal or optical excitation can promote electrons into the conduction band.

More ideas

Text

In an intrinsic semiconductor, promoting an electron leaves a hole in the valence band. Both the electron and the hole can carry current; heating can therefore increase carrier concentration. · A photon can excite an electron across the gap if hν≥Eg. This underlies photoconductivity and semiconductor photodetectors.
