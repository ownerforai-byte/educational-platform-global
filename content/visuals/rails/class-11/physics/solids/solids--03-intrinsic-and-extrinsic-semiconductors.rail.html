Intrinsic semiconductor: A pure semiconductor crystal with no significant impurity atoms (ppm or less). Examples: pure silicon (99.9999999), pure germanium. At any finite temperature, thermal excitation creates equal numbers of electrons and holes: n=p=ni​.

Fact (Everyday): Your phone's battery management IC uses thermistors (temperature sensors) made of doped semiconductors. As temperature rises, semiconductor resistance drops - protecting the battery from overheating. · Fact (Scale): Doping with just 1 part per billion (ppb) of arsenic in silicon changes the resistivity from 2.3 imes105,Omegacdot extcm (intrinsic) to 0.01,Omegacdot extcm (extrinsic). An incredibly sensitive control.

Study notes

Text

p-type doping: Trivalent acceptor atoms (Group III: B, Al, Ga) replace silicon atoms. Each acceptor has 3 valence electrons, creating a vacant bond (hole) in the lattice. The acceptor ionization energy Ea​approx0.01 ext−−0.05, exteV. Holes are majority carriers; electrons are minority carriers. · Mass-action law: In thermal equilibrium, np=ni2​ holds for any semiconductor (intrinsic or extrinsic). For an n-type sample with donor concentration Nd​, napproxNd​ and papproxni2​/Nd​. · Carrier concentration: At room temperature, all donors in n-type are ionized (freeze-out is negligible). For Nd​=1016,extcm−3, napprox1016,extcm−3, which is 105 times higher than intrinsic Si (ni​approx1.5 imes1010,extcm−3). Conductivity increases by the same factor.
