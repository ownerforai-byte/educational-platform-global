Universal facts

Fact (Historical): Felix Bloch developed band theory in 1928, applying quantum mechanics to electron motion in crystals. Edward Fermi, Julian Schwinger, and others refined it into the nearly-free electron model and tight-binding model that form the foundation of modern solid-state physics. · Fact (Application): Laser diodes (VCSELs, edge emitters) work only in direct-gap semiconductors (GaAs, InP). Silicon is an indirect-gap material — electron-hole recombination in silicon is inefficient for light emission, which is why silicon photonics is challenging.

Study notes

Text

Band formation: In isolated atoms, electrons occupy discrete energy levels. When N atoms aggregate into a solid, each atomic level splits into N closely spaced levels, forming near-continuous energy bands. The broadening width is ∼1–10eV, with individual levels separated by ∼10−22eV. · Valence band: The highest energy band that is occupied (filled) at absolute zero. Formed from the valence-shell atomic orbitals. When completely filled with electrons, it cannot conduct electricity unless electrons are promoted to the conduction band. · Conduction band: The next higher energy band, empty at absolute zero. Electrons here are delocalized across the crystal and are free to move under an applied electric field, contributing to electrical conduction.
