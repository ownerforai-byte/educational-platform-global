Definition of work (constant force): W=Fscos heta, where F is the magnitude of the constant force, s is the displacement, and  heta is the angle between the force vector and displacement vector. Work is a scalar quantity - it has magnitude but no direction.

Derivation

Text

Sign of work: Work is POSITIVE when 0^\\circ \\le \ heta < 90^\\circ (force component along displacement). Work is ZERO when \ heta = 90^\\circ (force perpendicular to displacement, e.g., centripetal force, normal force on horizontal surface). Work is NEGATIVE when 90^\\circ < \ heta \\le 180^\\circ (force opposes displacement, e.g., kinetic friction, gravity when lifting). · Work by multiple forces: Total work is the sum of works by individual forces: W extnet​=sumWi​=sumFi​scos hetai​. This is equivalent to the work done by the net force: W extnet​=F extnet​scos heta extnet​.

Shortcut

Text

The work-energy theorem W extnet​=DeltaK is mathematically equivalent to Newton's second law F=ma. It's not a new law - it's a reformulation that is often easier to use when forces vary or the path is complex. · One joule (1 J) is the work done by a force of 1 newton moving an object 1 meter in the direction of the force. It's a small amount: lifting a 100 g apple by 1 m does about 1 J of work against gravity.

Variable force definition: When force varies with position, work is the integral of force over the displacement: W=intxi​xf​​F(x),dx (for 1D motion). Graphically, this is the area under the F vs x curve between the initial and final positions. · Work by a spring force (Hooke's law): For an ideal spring, F=−kx. Work done BY the spring when moving from xi​ to xf​: W extspring​=intxi​xf​​(−kx)dx= rac12kxi2​− rac12kxf2​. Work done ON the spring (by external agent) is the negative of this. · Work by gravity (near Earth): Gravity is a constant force Fg​=mg downward. If an object moves vertically by Deltay (up positive), Wg​=−mgDeltay. If it moves horizontally, Wg​=0. For a path with vertical drop h, Wg​=+mgh (gravity does positive work).

Path dependence for variable forces: For a variable force, work depends on the path taken. For conservative forces (gravity, spring), work is path-independent and equals −DeltaU. For non-conservative forces (friction), work depends on the actual path length. · Graphical interpretation: The area under the F-x curve (with sign) gives the work. Positive area = positive work; negative area = negative work. For a spring, the F-x graph is a straight line through origin with slope k, and the area is a triangle  rac12kx2. · Work by a varying force in 2D: W=intC​vecFcdotdvecr=intC​(Fx​dx+Fy​dy). This is a line integral. For a force vecF(x,y), the work along path C depends on the specific curve C unless vecF is conservative ( abla imesvecF=0).

Constant force vs variable force summary: Constant force: W=Fscos heta (simple product). Variable force: W=intF(x)dx (area under curve). Both are special cases of the general definition W=intvecFcdotdvecr. · Example - work by gravity on an incline: Block slides down incline of angle  heta, length L. Vertical drop h=Lsin heta. Work by gravity: Wg​=mgh=mgLsin heta.
