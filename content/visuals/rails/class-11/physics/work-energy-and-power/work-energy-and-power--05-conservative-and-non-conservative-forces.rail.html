Concept

Text

Conservative forces (gravity, spring, electrostatic) do path-independent work and allow potential energy definition. Non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance) dissipate energy as heat. The general energy equation W_nc = ΔE_mech handles both types — if W_nc = 0, mechanical energy is conserved.

Conservative force: work depends only on endpoints; ∮F·dr = 0 (closed loop) Non-conservative force: work depends on path; ∮F·dr ≠ 0 Work-energy relation: W_nc = ΔE_mech = (K_f + U_f) − (K_i + U_i)

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Friction does NOT have a potential energy. Many students try to write U_friction = μmgx or something similar. This is WRONG — friction is non-conservative, and the energy it 'removes' from the mechanical system becomes heat, not stored potential energy. · A force being 'conservative' does NOT mean the total energy of the system is conserved. It means mechanical energy can be conserved IF that force is the ONLY force doing work. If you have conservative forces PLUS non-conservative forces, mechanical energy is not conserved even though some forces are conservative.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Friction does NOT have a potential energy — the energy becomes heat, not stored PE. · A conservative force does NOT guarantee energy conservation if non-conservative forces are also present.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Gravity is conservative: Lift a 5 kg mass 3 m up and lower it down. Net work = 0 (closed loop). · Friction is non-conservative: Slide a 2 kg block 5 m on a surface (μ_k = 0.3). Work = −0.3 × 2 × 9.8 × 5 = −29.4 J (depends on path length).

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Normal force and tension are NOT conservative forces — they are constraint forces that do zero work (they are perpendicular to displacement). They don't appear in the energy equation because W = 0, not because they have potential energy. · The term 'non-conservative' does NOT mean 'energy is destroyed.' It means mechanical energy is converted to other forms (heat, sound) that are NOT tracked by potential energy. Total energy is ALWAYS conserved (first law).

Quick concepts

Text

A force is conservative if the work done depends only on initial and final positions, not the path. · The work done by a conservative force around any closed loop is zero: ∮F·dr = 0. · Non-conservative forces dissipate mechanical energy as heat, sound, or deformation. · The general energy equation: W_nc = (K_f + U_f) − (K_i + U_i).

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Conservative forces (gravity, spring, electrostatic) allow potential energy definition. · Non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance) dissipate energy as heat. · The closed-loop test: if net work around a closed path is zero, the force is conservative. · When both types act, use W_nc = ΔE_mech (general energy equation).

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

The fundamental forces of nature (gravity, electromagnetism, strong, weak) are all conservative at the fundamental level. Non-conservative forces like friction are emergent — they arise from many conservative interactions at the microscopic level, with energy going into microscopic degrees of freedom (heat). · Noether's theorem: Conservation of energy is linked to time translation symmetry. Conservative forces are those derived from a potential that is time-independent. Time-dependent forces (like a time-varying field) can be non-conservative even if they are fundamental.

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Conservative force definition: A force is conservative if the work done by it depends ONLY on the initial and final positions, not on the path taken. Equivalently, the work done around any closed loop is zero: ∮F·dr = 0. · Path independence: For a conservative force, W = U_i - U_f = -(U_f - U_i) = -ΔU. The work is the negative of the change in potential energy. This means we can define a potential energy function U(r) such that F = -∇U (force is the negative gradient of potential). · Examples of conservative forces: Gravity (near Earth: F = -mg, U = mgh), spring force (F = -kx, U = ½kx²), electrostatic force (F = kQq/r², U = kQq/r), uniform electric field (F = qE, U = qEy).

More ideas

Text

Conservative forces allow potential energy definition: F = −∇U. · Conservative force and PE: W = −ΔU (work = negative change in potential energy) · Friction work: W_f = −μNd (always negative; energy dissipated as heat)
