Formula

Kind

formula

Text

Elastic collision (1D): v₁ = [(m₁−m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂]/(m₁+m₂); v₂ = [(m₂−m₁)u₂ + 2m₁u₁]/(m₁+m₂) Perfectly inelastic: m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁+m₂)v (common velocity v) Coefficient of restitution: e = (v₂ − v₁)/(u₁ − u₂) = relative speed of separation / relative speed of approach

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

In a perfectly inelastic collision, the objects DO NOT lose ALL their kinetic energy. They lose the MAXIMUM possible KE consistent with momentum conservation. The center-of-mass kinetic energy (½Mv_cm²) MUST remain because momentum is conserved. Only the KE relative to the center of mass can be lost. · The coefficient of restitution e is NOT the ratio of final to initial KE. e = (v₂ - v₁)/(u₁ - u₂) is the ratio of relative speeds. The fraction of KE retained is e² (for equal masses) or a more complex formula for unequal masses. Many students confuse e with KE ratio.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

In a perfectly inelastic collision, the objects do NOT lose ALL their kinetic energy — only the KE relative to the center of mass can be lost. · The coefficient of restitution e depends on the materials, not on the velocities of the colliding bodies.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Equal mass elastic: A 2 kg ball at 6 m/s hits a stationary 2 kg ball. After collision: first ball stops, second ball moves at 6 m/s (velocities exchanged). · Perfectly inelastic: A 3 kg ball at 4 m/s hits a stationary 1 kg ball. They stick together. Common velocity = (3×4 + 0)/(3+1) = 3 m/s. KE lost = ½×3×16 − ½×4×9 = 24 − 18 = 6 J.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Momentum is ALWAYS conserved in an isolated system (no external forces). Kinetic energy is ONLY conserved in perfectly elastic collisions. Students often think KE is always conserved in collisions — it is NOT. Real collisions are usually somewhat inelastic (0 < e < 1). · For a ball bouncing on the floor: The floor/earth is so massive it doesn't move. The ball's speed before and after impact relates by e = v_after/v_before. The ball loses (1-e²) of its KE each bounce. The floor provides the impulse to change momentum; the earth's recoil is negligible.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Elastic collision: both momentum AND kinetic energy are conserved. · Inelastic collision: momentum conserved, kinetic energy NOT conserved (some → heat/sound). · Perfectly inelastic: bodies stick together, KE loss is maximum. · Coefficient of restitution e = 1 (elastic), e = 0 (perfectly inelastic), 0 < e < 1 (inelastic).

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

The coefficient of restitution e was introduced by Newton in the Principia. For elastic collisions, e = 1, and the relative velocity of separation equals the relative velocity of approach. This is a consequence of both momentum and kinetic energy conservation. · In any isolated system, total momentum is ALWAYS conserved (if no external forces). Kinetic energy is NOT always conserved. The only perfectly elastic collisions in nature are between fundamental particles (or at the atomic/molecular level where quantum mechanics makes collisions elastic).

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Elastic collision: A collision in which both total momentum AND total kinetic energy are conserved. The bodies rebound without permanent deformation, no sound/heat is produced. The relative velocity of approach equals the relative velocity of separation: v₂ - v₁ = u₁ - u₂. · Inelastic collision: A collision in which momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is NOT conserved. Some kinetic energy is converted to internal energy (heat, sound, deformation). The coefficient of restitution e < 1. · Perfectly inelastic collision: The extreme case where the colliding bodies stick together after collision and move with a common velocity. Momentum is conserved; KE loss is maximum (but not 100% because momentum must be conserved, so some KE remains as center-of-mass motion).
