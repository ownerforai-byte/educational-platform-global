Elastic potential energy is stored in deformed bodies. For a spring, U=21​kx2. For a material under stress, energy density u=21​σϵ. Hysteresis loops represent energy dissipation, while the area under the stress-strain curve represents toughness.

Energy Density Validity: The formula u=rac12σε=rac12Eε2=racσ22E holds only within the linear elastic region (Hooke's law). Beyond the proportional limit, energy density must be found by integrating the actual stress-strain curve: u=∫σdε. · Spring vs Wire Energy: For a spring, U=rac12kx2 where k=EA/L is the spring constant. For a wire, U=rac12racEALx2=rac12σε(AL)=uimesextvolume. Both formulas are equivalent when geometry is accounted for.

Study notes

Text

Elastic Potential Energy (U): The energy stored in a deformed elastic body (like a spring or stretched wire) due to the work done against internal restoring forces. For a spring, U=21​kx2. · Energy Density (u): The elastic potential energy stored per unit volume. For a wire under tension, u=21​×stress×strain=21​σϵ=21​Eσ2​=21​Eϵ2. · Work Done: The work done to stretch a spring by x is the area under the F−x graph: W=∫0x​Fdx=∫0x​kxdx=21​kx2.

17.
Label

More ideas

Text

Hysteresis = Energy Dissipation: The area of a hysteresis loop represents energy dissipated (lost as heat) per cycle, not energy stored. For conservative elastic materials (ideal springs, metals below yield), loading and unloading paths coincide, and the loop area is zero. · Toughness = area under the stress-strain curve up to fracture. · ❌ 'Hysteresis loop area is energy stored.' ✅ It is energy DISSIPATED (lost as heat) per cycle.
