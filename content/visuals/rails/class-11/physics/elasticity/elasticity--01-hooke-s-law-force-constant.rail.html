Concept

Text

Hooke's law (F = kx) states that deformation is proportional to applied force within the elastic limit. The spring constant k (N/m) measures stiffness. Springs combine in series (reciprocal sum, softer) and parallel (direct sum, stiffer). The stored elastic energy is ½kx², and the law underpins spring balances, suspension systems, and simple harmonic motion.

Hooke's law: F = kx Spring constant: k = F/x (units N/m) Series springs: 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Elastic Limit / Proportional Limit: Hooke's law (F=kx) holds only within the linear (proportional) limit. Beyond this, the force-extension graph curves; at the elastic limit, the material returns to its original shape but the relationship is no longer linear. Past the yield point, permanent (plastic) deformation occurs. · Spring Constant Geometry Dependence: k is NOT purely a material property — it depends on geometry. For a uniform wire: k=EA/L (E = Young's modulus, A = cross-section, L = length). For a helical spring: k=Gd4/(8nD3) (G = shear modulus, d = wire diameter, n = coils, D = coil diameter). Cutting a spring into n equal parts multiplies k by n.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

The restoring force of a spring is −kx: it always opposes the displacement from equilibrium. · A cut spring doubles its k — shorter springs are stiffer for the same material and geometry.

Solved

Text

F = kx: A 25 N force stretches a spring by 5 cm → k = 25/0.05 = 500 N/m. · Mass on spring: A 2 kg mass (weight 19.6 N) on a spring of k = 490 N/m sinks x = 19.6/490 = 0.04 m = 4 cm.

Label

Limitation

Text

❌ 'F = kx means force depends on extension.' ✅ Precisely that — for a spring, doubling the extension doubles the stretching force needed. The spring PULLS back with force kx (restoring force) opposite to the applied load. · ❌ 'k is a property only of the material.' ✅ k depends on the geometry too (length, coil radius, number of coils, wire thickness). It is the material property (Young's modulus) together with geometry that fixes k.

Quick concepts

Text

Hooke's law: F = kx holds up to the proportionality limit, which is within the elastic limit. · The spring constant k is the force per unit extension (N/m), a measure of stiffness. · Series springs soften the combination; parallel springs stiffen it. · The restoring force is −kx, producing SHM with ω = √(k/m).

12.
Label

Key points

Text

F = kx holds only within the elastic limit — marked by the straight-line region of the F–x graph. · k measures stiffness in N/m; a larger k is a stiffer spring. · Series springs combine like 1/k = 1/k₁ + 1/k₂ (softer). · Parallel springs combine like k = k₁ + k₂ (stiffer).

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

Hooke (1635–1703) announced his law in 1676 as the anagram 'ceiiinosssttuv' which decoded to 'ut tensio, sic vis' — 'as the extension, so the force'. · Hooke's law is the basis of every spring, weighing machine, shock absorber, and suspension system. It is the first-order Taylor expansion of any potential energy function U(x) near a stable equilibrium point.

Study notes

Text

Hooke's law statement: For small deformations, the extension (or compression) of an elastic body is directly proportional to the applied force: F = kx, where F is the applied force, x is the deformation, and k is the spring (force) constant. This linear region is governed by the interatomic potential energy well, where atoms act as if connected by springs.… · Force (spring) constant k: k = F/x, measured in N/m. It measures the stiffness of the spring — a stiffer spring has a larger k and needs a bigger force for the same extension. k depends on the material, the wire diameter, the number of coils and the length of the spring. For a wire: k=EA/L, where E is Young's modulus, A is cross-sectional area, and L is length.… · Restoring Force: The spring exerts a restoring force F=−kx that always opposes the displacement. This is the basis of simple harmonic motion when combined with inertia.

More ideas

Text

Restoring Force Direction: The spring exerts a restoring force F=−kx that always opposes the displacement. This negative sign is crucial for deriving simple harmonic motion (mx¨+kx=0). The applied force and restoring force are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction at equilibrium. · Energy stored in a stretched spring is ½kx² — the triangle under the F–x graph. · ❌ 'A stiffer spring stretches more.' ✅ A stiffer spring stretches LESS for the same force — it has a larger k, so x = F/k is smaller.

More ideas

Text

❌ 'Extension is proportional to force at all loads.' ✅ Only within the elastic (proportional) limit. Beyond it the graph bends, and past the yield point the spring deforms permanently. · ❌ 'Series springs are stiffer.' ✅ Series springs are SOFTER (smaller total k). Two identical springs in series have k/2; in parallel they have 2k. · ❌ 'Force constant and friction constant (μ) are the same.' ✅ k (N/m) relates force to displacement of a spring; μ is a dimensionless friction coefficient relating friction to normal reaction.
