Concept

Text

Poisson's ratio σ = −(lateral strain)/(longitudinal strain) is a dimensionless material constant between 0 and 0.5 describing how a body thins when stretched. It links the elastic moduli through E = 2G(1+σ) = 3B(1−2σ). σ = 0.5 corresponds to an incompressible material, while cork (σ ≈ 0) barely changes width.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Definition and Sign Convention: σ=−(extlateralstrain)/(extlongitudinalstrain). The negative sign makes σ positive for ordinary materials (lateral and longitudinal strains have opposite signs). The ratio is dimensionless — dividing one dimensionless strain by another. · Volume Change Constraint: The fractional volume change under uniaxial stress is ΔV/V=ε(1−2σ). For σ=0.5 (incompressible), ΔV/V=0 — volume is conserved. For σ<0.5, volume increases slightly during stretching.

Special cases

Text

Remember the minus sign in σ = −(lateral)/(longitudinal) — it makes typical σ positive. · σ is dimensionless — do not attach units.

Solved

Text

Wire: L = 2 m, d = 1 mm, ΔL = 2 mm, σ = 0.3 → diameter change = σ × ε × d = 0.3×10⁻³×1 = 3×10⁻⁴ mm. · From moduli: E = 2×10¹¹, G = 8×10¹⁰ → σ = E/(2G) − 1 = 1.25 − 1 = 0.25.

Limitation

Text

❌ 'Poisson's ratio has units of strain.' ✅ It is a RATIO of two strains — dimensionless. · ❌ 'Poisson's ratio can be greater than 1.' ✅ No — the thermodynamic limit is 0.5 for isotropic materials (a few exotic anisotropic materials exceed 0.5 but never 1).

Key points

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Poisson's ratio (σ): when a body is stretched (or compressed), it becomes thinner (or thicker) in the perpendicular directions. Poisson's ratio is the ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain: σ = −(lateral strain)/(longitudinal strain) = −(β/α). The minus sign keeps σ positive because lateral strain is opposite in sign to longitudinal strain. · Why it is dimensionless: Poisson's ratio divides one strain by another — strain is dimensionless, so σ has no units and no dimensions. Its value for most materials lies between 0 and 0.5. · Typical values: cork ≈ 0 (no lateral change), concrete ≈ 0.1–0.2, steel ≈ 0.25–0.3, copper ≈ 0.33, aluminium ≈ 0.34, rubber ≈ 0.5 (nearly incompressible). A hypothetical material with σ = 0.5 would conserve volume exactly.

Label

More ideas

Text

Material State Limits: The thermodynamic bounds are −1<σ<0.5 for isotropic materials. Most common materials have 0<σ<0.5. Auxetic materials (negative σ) expand laterally when stretched — rare and engineered. Fluids have no defined Poisson's ratio (no shear rigidity). · ΔV/V = ε(1 − 2σ) — volume change during stretching depends only on σ and strain. · ❌ 'Lateral strain equals longitudinal strain.' ✅ Lateral strain = −σ × longitudinal strain, with σ typically between 0 and 0.5.

More ideas

Text

❌ 'When stretched, volume always increases.' ✅ If σ = 0.5 the volume is constant; for σ < 0.5 the volume increases slightly (ΔV/V = ε(1−2σ)). · ❌ 'Cork and rubber have similar σ.' ✅ Cork ≈ 0, rubber ≈ 0.5 — at opposite ends of the range. · ❌ 'Poisson's ratio is a force.' ✅ It is a pure, dimensionless ratio of deformation (strain) — not a force or modulus.
