Concept

Text

The three elastic moduli — Young's (E), bulk (B), and shear (G) — quantify how materials resist stretching, uniform compression, and shape change respectively. Each is stress/strain with units N/m². They are related by E = 2G(1+σ) = 3B(1−2σ). Solids possess all three; fluids have only bulk modulus.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Material State Determines Available Moduli: Solids have all three moduli (E,B,G). Fluids (liquids/gases) have only bulk modulus B — they cannot sustain shear stress (G=0) and have no defined Young's modulus for stretching. · Isotropic Material Relations: For isotropic solids, the three moduli are linked via Poisson's ratio σ: E=2G(1+σ)=3B(1−2σ). These relations do not hold for anisotropic materials (crystals, composites) where moduli depend on direction.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Choose the modulus by the type of deformation: stretch → E, squeeze → B, twist → G. · The sign in B = −P/(ΔV/V) makes the modulus positive since ΔV is negative under compression.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Young: A 1 mm² wire, 2 m long, carrying 200 N extends 2 mm → E = (200/10⁻⁶)/(2×10⁻³/2) = 2×10¹¹ N/m². · Bulk: 3×10⁶ Pa pressure shrinks 1 m³ of steel by 1.9×10⁻⁵ m³ → B = 1.6×10¹¹ Pa.

11.
Label

Quick concepts

Text

Young's modulus E = Fl/(AΔl) describes stretching/compression stiffness. · Bulk modulus B = −P/(ΔV/V) describes compression under uniform pressure. · Shear modulus G = τ/θ describes change of shape under tangential stress. · The three moduli are interrelated: E = 2G(1+σ) = 3B(1−2σ).

12.
Label

Key points

Text

E, B, G all have units N/m² (Pa) — they differ by the type of deformation they describe. · E (stretching), B (volume), G (shape) measure the corresponding resistance of a material. · The three moduli are linked: E = 2G(1+σ) and E = 3B(1−2σ). · Liquids and gases have G = 0 (they cannot resist shear) — only solids have a shear modulus.

13.
Label

Universal facts

Text

Thomas Young established the concept of the modulus of elasticity in 1807 — his modulus E is one of the most fundamental material constants. · Rigidity (shear) modulus explains why liquids flow: their G = 0 — they cannot resist shear.

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Young's modulus (E): the ratio of longitudinal stress to longitudinal strain: E = (F/A)/(Δl/l) = Fl/(AΔl). It measures resistance to stretching/compression. For steel, E ≈ 2×10¹¹ N/m²; for aluminium ≈ 7×10¹⁰ N/m²; for rubber ≈ 5×10⁶ N/m². · Bulk modulus (B): the ratio of volume stress (pressure) to volume strain: B = −P/(ΔV/V). It measures resistance to uniform compression. The minus sign keeps B positive (ΔV is negative under pressure). For water B ≈ 2.2×10⁹ Pa; steel B ≈ 1.6×10¹¹ Pa. · Shear modulus / modulus of rigidity (G or η): the ratio of shear stress to shear strain: G = τ/θ = (F/A)/θ. It measures resistance to change of shape without change of volume. For steel G ≈ 8×10¹⁰ N/m².

More ideas

More ideas

Text

Geometry Separation: E is an intensive material property (Pa), while spring constant k=EA/L is extensive. Changing a wire's length or cross-section changes k but not E — this distinction is essential for connecting spring problems to material science. · Compressibility is the reciprocal of B. · ❌ 'Shear strain is a length ratio.' ✅ Shear strain is the ANGLE of displacement θ (radians), not a length ratio.
