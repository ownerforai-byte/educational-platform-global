Concept

Text

The three elastic moduli — Young's (E), bulk (B), and shear (G) — quantify how materials resist stretching, uniform compression, and shape change respectively. Each is stress/strain with units N/m². They are related by E = 2G(1+σ) = 3B(1−2σ). Solids possess all three; fluids have only bulk modulus.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Material State Determines Available Moduli: Solids have all three moduli (E,B,G). Fluids (liquids/gases) have only bulk modulus B — they cannot sustain shear stress (G=0) and have no defined Young's modulus for stretching. · Isotropic Material Relations: For isotropic solids, the three moduli are linked via Poisson's ratio σ: E=2G(1+σ)=3B(1−2σ). These relations do not hold for anisotropic materials (crystals, composites) where moduli depend on direction.

4.

Special cases

Text

Choose the modulus by the type of deformation: stretch → E, squeeze → B, twist → G. · The sign in B = −P/(ΔV/V) makes the modulus positive since ΔV is negative under compression.

Label

Quick concepts

Text

Young's modulus E = Fl/(AΔl) describes stretching/compression stiffness. · Bulk modulus B = −P/(ΔV/V) describes compression under uniform pressure. · Shear modulus G = τ/θ describes change of shape under tangential stress. · The three moduli are interrelated: E = 2G(1+σ) = 3B(1−2σ).

Universal facts

Text

Stress = F/A, strain = ΔL/L (dimensionless). Hooke's law (elastic limit): stress ∝ strain. · Elastic moduli: Young's Y = (F/A)/(ΔL/L); Bulk K = −P/(ΔV/V); Shear G = (F/A)/(Δx/L).

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Stress and strain. Stress is the restoring force per unit area (F/A) and strain is the fractional deformation (ΔL/L); within the elastic limit, stress is proportional to strain (Hooke's law). · Young's modulus. Y = longitudinal stress ÷ longitudinal strain = FL/AΔL; it measures a material's resistance to stretching or compression along its length. · Bulk modulus. K = −P ÷ (ΔV/V); the negative sign shows volume decreases under pressure, and its reciprocal is the compressibility of the material.

19.
Label

More ideas

Text

Fluids possess only bulk modulus; compressibility = 1/B. · Young's modulus: E = (F/A)/(Δl/l) = Fl/(AΔl) · Bulk modulus: B = −P/(ΔV/V)

Label

More ideas

Text

Shear modulus: G = (F/A)/θ = τ/θ · Relation: E = 2G(1 + σ) = 3B(1 − 2σ) · Combined: E = 9BG/(3B + G); compressibility k = 1/B

21.
Label

More ideas

Text

Geometry Separation: E is an intensive material property (Pa), while spring constant k=EA/L is extensive. Changing a wire's length or cross-section changes k but not E — this distinction is essential for connecting spring problems to material science. · E, B, G all have units N/m² (Pa) — they differ by the type of deformation they describe. · E (stretching), B (volume), G (shape) measure the corresponding resistance of a material.

22.
Label

More ideas

Text

The three moduli are linked: E = 2G(1+σ) and E = 3B(1−2σ). · Liquids and gases have G = 0 (they cannot resist shear) — only solids have a shear modulus. · Compressibility is the reciprocal of B.
