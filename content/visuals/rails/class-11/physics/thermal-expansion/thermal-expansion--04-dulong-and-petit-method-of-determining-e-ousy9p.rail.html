Linear expansion definition: When a solid is heated, its length increases. The fractional increase in length per unit rise in temperature is called the coefficient of linear expansion, α. · Formula: ΔL=L0​αΔT, where L0​ is the original length, α is the coefficient of linear expansion (unit: K⁻¹ or °C⁻¹), and ΔT is the temperature change.

Study notes

Text

New length: L=L0​(1+αΔT). For small αΔT, this is an excellent approximation. For large temperature ranges, α may vary and must be treated as a function of temperature. · Physical origin: Heating increases the amplitude of atomic vibrations in the lattice. Because the interatomic potential is asymmetric (steeper on the compression side), the average separation increases with temperature, causing expansion. · Coefficient of linear expansion for common materials: Steel: α≈12×10−6 K⁻¹. Aluminum: α≈24×10−6 K⁻¹. Copper: α≈17×10−6 K⁻¹. Invar (Fe-Ni alloy): α≈1.2×10−6 K⁻¹. Quartz: α≈0.5×10−6 K⁻¹.

State the limit / condition where a formula or rule stops being valid before applying it. · Why metals expand more: Metals with weaker interatomic bonds (larger bond length, lower melting point) tend to have larger α. Lead (α≈29×10−6) expands more than steel because its atomic bonds are weaker. · Bimetallic strip: Two metals with different α bonded together bend when heated — the higher-α metal expands more, forcing the strip to curve. Used in thermostats, circuit breakers, and temperature indicators.

Label

More ideas

Text

Railway tracks — expansion gaps: Steel rails expand by ΔL=L0​αΔT. For a 12 m rail and ΔT=40°C: ΔL=12×12×10−6×40=5.76 mm. Gaps are left between rails to accommodate this expansion, otherwise buckling occurs. · Measuring α — Searle's apparatus: A metal rod is heated uniformly. The expansion is measured by a micrometer screw gauge. α=L0​ΔTΔL​. The apparatus minimizes errors from thermal expansion of the frame. · Measuring α — Hoke's apparatus: Uses a glass tube filled with liquid. As the solid rod inside expands, it displaces liquid. The volume of displaced liquid gives ΔL indirectly. This is an elegant optical-mechanical method.
