Concept

Text

Four questions cover dynamics: three laws with examples (2024), limiting-friction numerical (2023: 5 kg, mu 0.4 gives 20 N), incline friction (2021: 10 kg at 30 degrees gives ~17 N), mass-vs-weight distinction (2020). Friction always equals mu times the NORMAL force — on inclines that means mg cos theta, never mg.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

F = ma = dp/dt · J = F dt = dp · limiting f = mu_s N · kinetic f = mu_k N (mu_k < mu_s) · incline: N = mg cos theta, downslope = mg sin theta · mass (kg, invariant) vs weight (mg, newton, varies)

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Newton holds in inertial frames only (why the bank stresses it). Limiting friction is a ceiling: below it, static friction exactly matches the push (meaning 5 N of push meets 5 N of friction, not 20 N). On inclines the normal shrinks by cos theta — the meaning of resolving weight perpendicular to the surface.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Bus-brake lurch (first law), bat-ball acceleration (second), gun recoil (third) — the 2024 example set. Incline rest: friction balances mg sin theta but caps at mu N (check both: required vs available). Mass-vs-weight: 5 kg is 49 N on Earth, ~8 N on the Moon, 5 kg everywhere.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

NEB 2023 (3): N = mg = 50 N; limiting f = 0.4 x 50 = 20 N to start motion. NEB 2021 (3): incline 30 degrees, N = 10 x 9.8 x cos30 = 84.9 N; resting friction = mu N = 0.2 x 84.9 = 17.0 N. NEB 2024 (3): three laws, one everyday example each.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Mark-losers: using mg instead of mg cos theta for N on inclines; quoting limiting friction when the block needs less (static self-adjusts); mixing mass and weight units. Third-law pairs never cancel (different bodies) — a yearly trap.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

No heavy derivation is asked — the 'theory' answers are statements with examples (laws, mass-vs-weight table). Recoil/conservation sketches (gun-bullet momentum split) serve as the mechanism behind the third law. Moment equilibrium (clockwise = anticlockwise) extends force balance to rotation.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Friction numericals: N first (cos theta on slopes), then mu N, then compare with needed force. 'State laws' means statement + example each. Mass-weight: scalar-kg-invariant vs vector-newton-varying table.
