Conditions

Text

Static vs. Kinetic Threshold: The maximum static friction fsmax​=μs​N is the upper bound for static friction. Static friction is a variable force that matches the applied force up to this limit. Kinetic friction fk​=μk​N applies only when sliding occurs, with μk​<μs​. · Normal Force Dependency: Friction is proportional to the normal reaction, not the weight. Any vertical force component (pulling up, pushing down, incline) changes N, which directly changes the friction force. Always compute N first.

Special cases

Text

Static friction is NOT always μ_sN — it is a variable force that matches the applied force up to that maximum. · The normal force is NOT always mg — on a slope it is mg cos θ; with pulling/pushing at an angle it changes.

Slope: A 5 kg block on a 30° slope (μ_s = 0.4). tan 30° = 0.577 > 0.4 → slides. a = g(sin30° − μ_k cos30°) = 9.8(0.5 − 0.4×0.866) = 1.51 m/s². · Horizontal pull: A 10 kg block (μ_k = 0.25) pulled by 40 N at 20° above horizontal. Label

Study notes

Text

What friction is: a contact force that opposes relative motion (or its tendency) between two surfaces in contact. It acts parallel to the contact surface. Static friction (no sliding) and kinetic friction (sliding) are the two main types. · Laws of friction: (1) Friction is independent of the apparent area of contact (to a good approximation). (2) Maximum static friction and kinetic friction are both proportional to the normal reaction: f_s(max) = μ_sN, f_k = μ_kN. (3) μ_k < μ_s — it is harder to start sliding than to keep sliding. · Static friction is a variable force: it matches the applied force up to a maximum: f_s ≤ μ_sN. If you push with 30 N and the object doesn't move, f_s = 30 N (not μ_sN). Only at the point of slipping does f_s = μ_sN.
