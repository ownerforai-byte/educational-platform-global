The law of conservation of linear momentum states that the total momentum of an isolated system remains constant. It is a powerful tool for solving collision, explosion, and recoil problems. While momentum is conserved in all collisions, kinetic energy is only conserved in perfectly elastic ones.…

Conditions

Text

Isolated System Requirement: Momentum conservation (∑p​i​=∑p​f​) holds strictly only when the net external force on the system is zero. External forces like gravity, friction, or applied pushes must be absent or balanced. · Collisions vs. Continuous Interaction: In a collision, the interaction is brief and impulsive; internal forces dominate. In continuous interactions (e.g., rocket propulsion), momentum is conserved at each instant, but mass changes must be tracked using the variable-mass form F=dp/dt.

Study notes

Text

Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum: In an isolated system (no external net force), the total linear momentum remains constant: ∑p​i​=∑p​f​. This is a fundamental law derived from the homogeneity of space (Noether's theorem). It is a direct consequence of the fact that the laws of physics are the same at all points in space. · Isolated System: A system where the net external force is zero. Internal forces (like collision forces) do not change the total momentum because they act in equal and opposite pairs (Newton's 3rd law). The system can be as small as two colliding particles or as large as the entire universe. · Coefficient of Restitution (e): A measure of elasticity in collisions: e=(v2​−v1​)/(u1​−u2​). e=1 (perfectly elastic), 0<e<1 (inelastic), e=0 (perfectly inelastic). This coefficient depends on the materials of the colliding objects and their surface properties.
