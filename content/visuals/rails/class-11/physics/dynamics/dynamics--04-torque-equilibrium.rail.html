Concept

Text

Torque (τ = rF sin θ) is the rotational analogue of force. A rigid body is in equilibrium only when both ΣF = 0 (no linear acceleration) and Στ = 0 (no angular acceleration). The principle of moments, couples, and centre of gravity are the key tools for solving beam, ladder, and structure problems.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Two Independent Equilibrium Conditions: A rigid body is in static equilibrium if and only if BOTH ∑F=0 (translational) AND ∑au=0 (rotational) are satisfied. Zero net force alone is insufficient (e.g., a couple produces rotation with zero net force). · Pivot Independence of Net Torque: While the torque of individual forces depends on the pivot choice, the net torque in equilibrium is zero about ANY point. Choose the pivot at an unknown force to eliminate it from the torque equation.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Always choose the pivot at the point of an unknown force — that force then contributes zero torque and drops out of the equation. · Torque sign convention: anticlockwise = positive, clockwise = negative (or vice versa — just be consistent).

Label

Limitation

Text

❌ 'Torque and moment are different.' ✅ In physics problems, they are the same quantity: τ = rF sin θ. Engineers sometimes distinguish 'moment' (bending) from 'torque' (twisting), but here they are interchangeable. · ❌ 'A body with zero net force is in equilibrium.' ✅ It must ALSO have zero net torque. A couple (equal and opposite forces) produces zero net force but a non-zero torque — the body rotates.

Torque (τ = rF sin θ) is the rotational analogue of force — it causes angular acceleration. · A rigid body is in equilibrium only when both ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0 — both conditions are independent. · The principle of moments: in equilibrium, clockwise torques balance anticlockwise torques about any pivot. · A couple produces a pure torque τ = Fd that is independent of the pivot position.

Key points

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

Torque (moment of a force): τ = rF sin θ, where r is the distance from the pivot to the point of application, F is the force, and θ is the angle between r and F. The SI unit is Nm. Torque is positive for anticlockwise rotation, negative for clockwise. · Equilibrium of a rigid body: two conditions must BOTH hold: (1) translational equilibrium: ΣF = 0 (no net force → no linear acceleration); (2) rotational equilibrium: Στ = 0 (no net torque → no angular acceleration). A body at rest or rotating at constant angular velocity satisfies both. · Couple: two equal and opposite forces separated by a distance d produce a pure torque (independent of pivot): τ = F × d. The net force is zero, so there is no linear acceleration — only rotation. The turning effect is the same about ANY point.

Label

More ideas

Text

The centre of gravity is the single point where the total weight may be considered to act. · Centre of gravity: x_CG = Σ(mᵢxᵢ)/Σmᵢ (weighted average of positions) · Principle of moments: for equilibrium, sum of clockwise torques = sum of anticlockwise torques

More ideas

Text

Couple = Pure Torque: A couple (equal and opposite forces separated by distance d) produces a torque au=Fd that is independent of pivot position. It causes pure rotation without translation and cannot be balanced by a single force. · Choose the pivot at an unknown force's point of application to eliminate that force from the torque equation. · ❌ 'The torque depends on the pivot chosen.' ✅ The torque of a single force depends on the pivot, but the NET torque in equilibrium is zero about ANY pivot. Choose the pivot to eliminate unknowns.

Label

More ideas

Text

❌ 'Heavier bodies always produce more torque.' ✅ Torque depends on BOTH force and distance. A small force far from the pivot can produce more torque than a large force close to it. · ❌ 'The centre of gravity is always inside the body.' ✅ For a ring, the CG is at the centre (in empty space). For an L-shaped body, it lies outside the material. · ❌ 'A ladder leaning against a wall is safe if ΣF = 0.' ✅ You also need Στ = 0 AND friction at the ground to be ≤ μN. The ladder slips when the required friction exceeds μN_ground.

NEB equilibrium problems always require all three equations: ΣF_x = 0, ΣF_y = 0, Στ = 0. · Torque is a vector: τ = r × F. The direction (into or out of the page) is given by the right-hand rule. · The two equilibrium conditions (ΣF = 0, Στ = 0) are independent — both must be checked.
