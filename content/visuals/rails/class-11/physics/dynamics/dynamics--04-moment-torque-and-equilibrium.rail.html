Torque of a force: τ=rFsinθ (positive = anticlockwise, negative = clockwise) Equilibrium conditions: ∑F=0 (translational) AND ∑τ=0 (rotational) Couple torque: τ=F×d (independent of pivot position)

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Two Independent Equilibrium Conditions: A rigid body is in static equilibrium if and only if BOTH ∑F=0 (translational) AND ∑au=0 (rotational) are satisfied. Zero net force alone is insufficient (e.g., a couple produces rotation with zero net force). · Pivot Independence of Net Torque: While the torque of individual forces depends on the pivot choice, the net torque in equilibrium is zero about ANY point. Choose the pivot at an unknown force to eliminate it from the torque equation.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Always choose the pivot at the point of an unknown force — that force then contributes zero torque and drops out of the equation. · Torque sign convention: anticlockwise = positive, clockwise = negative (or vice versa — just be consistent).

Text

❌ 'Torque and moment are different.' ✅ In physics problems, they are the same quantity: τ=rFsinθ. Quick concepts

Text

Torque (τ=rFsinθ) is the rotational analogue of force — it causes angular acceleration. · A rigid body is in equilibrium only when both ∑F=0 and ∑τ=0 — both conditions are independent. · The principle of moments: in equilibrium, clockwise torques balance anticlockwise torques about any pivot. · A couple produces a pure torque τ=Fd that is independent of the pivot position.

Key points

Text

Torque = rFsinθ — it depends on force, distance, AND the angle between them. · Equilibrium requires BOTH ∑F=0 and ∑τ=0 — one without the other is not enough. · A couple produces pure rotation (zero net force, non-zero torque) — the torque is the same about any point. · The centre of gravity simplifies a distributed weight to a single point force.

Label

Study notes

Text

Torque (moment of a force): τ=rFsinθ, where r is the distance from the pivot to the point of application, F is the force, and θ is the angle between r and F. The SI unit is Nm. Torque is positive for anticlockwise rotation, negative for clockwise. · Equilibrium of a rigid body: two conditions must BOTH hold: (1) translational equilibrium: ∑F=0 (no net force → no linear acceleration); (2) rotational equilibrium: ∑τ=0 (no net torque → no angular acceleration). A body at rest or rotating at constant angular velocity satisfies both. · Couple: two equal and opposite forces separated by a distance d produce a pure torque (independent of pivot): τ=F×d. The net force is zero, so there is no linear acceleration — only rotation. The turning effect is the same about ANY point.
