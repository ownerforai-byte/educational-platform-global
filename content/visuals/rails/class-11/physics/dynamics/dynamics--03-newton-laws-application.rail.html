Newton's second law (ΣF = ma) applied through free-body diagrams solves every mechanics problem: elevators (apparent weight), Atwood's machine, connected bodies, inclined planes (smooth and rough), and objects in fluids. The key is correct isolation of the body and accurate force identification.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Inertial Frame Requirement: Newton's laws (F=ma) hold only in inertial reference frames. In accelerating (non-inertial) frames, a pseudo-force −maframe​ must be added to the FBD to make the equations valid. · FBD Completeness: The free-body diagram must include all forces acting ON the chosen body: weight, normal, friction, tension, applied forces. Forces exerted BY the body on other objects do NOT belong in its FBD.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Always draw the FBD BEFORE writing equations — a wrong diagram guarantees a wrong answer. · Choose one axis along the direction of acceleration — this makes ma appear on only one axis.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Elevator: A 70 kg person stands on a scale in an elevator accelerating upward at 2 m/s². Scale reading = m(g + a) = 70(9.8 + 2) = 826 N (apparent mass ≈ 84.3 kg). · Atwood: m₁ = 6 kg, m₂ = 4 kg. a = (6−4)×9.8/(6+4) = 1.96 m/s²; T = 2×6×4×9.8/10 = 47.04 N.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

❌ 'The normal force is always mg.' ✅ N = mg only on a horizontal surface with no vertical acceleration. On a slope N = mg cos θ; in an accelerating elevator N = m(g ± a). · ❌ 'Friction always opposes motion.' ✅ Friction opposes RELATIVE motion (or its tendency). Static friction can act in the direction of motion — e.g., the static friction on your feet pushes you forward when walking.

Shortcut

Text

Elevator shortcut: apparent weight = m(g ± a) — just add or subtract ma from the real weight. · Atwood shortcut: a = (m₁ − m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂) — the net driving force is the weight difference; total inertia is the total mass.

Quick concepts

Text

Newton's second law (ΣF = ma) is the master equation connecting forces to motion in mechanics. · The free-body diagram (FBD) is the essential first step — it identifies all forces acting on a single body. · Apparent weight changes with acceleration: N = m(g ± a) — the basis of weightlessness. · Atwood's machine and connected bodies demonstrate how tension transmits force between bodies.

Key points

Text

The FBD method is universal: isolate → draw forces → choose axes → ΣF = ma → solve. · The normal force adapts to the situation — it is NOT always mg. · Tension in an accelerating string differs from the weight of the attached mass. · Action-reaction pairs act on different bodies — never cancel in a single FBD.

18.
Label

Study notes

Text

The problem-solving framework (the 'FBD method'): (1) isolate the body, (2) draw ALL forces acting ON it (weight, normal, friction, tension, applied), (3) choose axes (one along acceleration), (4) write ΣF = ma for each axis, (5) solve. Every single NEB numerical follows this pattern. · Elevator weight paradox: in an accelerating upward elevator, the normal force N = m(g + a) — you feel heavier. Accelerating downward: N = m(g − a) — you feel lighter. Free fall (a = g): N = 0 (weightlessness). The weight mg never changes; what changes is the apparent weight (the normal force). · Atwood's machine (two masses on a pulley): acceleration a = (m₁ − m₂)g/(m₁ + m₂); tension T = 2m₁m₂g/(m₁ + m₂). The string is massless and inextensible; the pulley is frictionless — if not, extra terms appear.

More ideas

Text

On an inclined plane, weight decomposes into mg sin θ (along slope) and mg cos θ (into slope). · Smooth inclined plane: a = g sin θ down the slope; N = mg cos θ · Rough inclined plane: a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ); slides when tan θ > μ

Label

More ideas

Text

Friction Direction Logic: Friction always opposes relative motion or its tendency. Static friction can act in the direction of motion (e.g., walking forward, car accelerating) — determine direction by asking 'which way would the surface slip without friction?' · On a frictionless incline, all bodies slide at the same rate (independent of mass). · ❌ 'The tension in a string equals the weight of the hanging mass.' ✅ Only if the system is in equilibrium. In an accelerating Atwood's machine, T ≠ mg — use ΣF = ma on each mass to find T.

21.
Label

More ideas

Text

❌ 'Action and reaction forces cancel each other.' ✅ They act on DIFFERENT bodies, so they never cancel in a single FBD. Include only forces acting ON the body you are analyzing. · ❌ 'Heavier bodies slide slower down a slope.' ✅ a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ) has no mass term — all bodies slide at the same rate (if μ is the same), just like free fall. · ❌ 'A body at rest has no forces acting on it.' ✅ Static equilibrium means ΣF = 0, not zero forces — weight and normal (and possibly friction and tension) all act, they just balance.

If yes, ΣF = ma; if no, ΣF = 0 — same law, different cases.
