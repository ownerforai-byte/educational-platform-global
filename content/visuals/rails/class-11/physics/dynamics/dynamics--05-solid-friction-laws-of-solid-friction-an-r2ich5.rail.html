Concept

Text

Solid friction opposes relative motion between surfaces in contact. The laws state that friction is independent of apparent area, proportional to normal reaction, and depends on surface nature. Static friction exceeds kinetic friction. Verified experimentally using inclined plane and horizontal surface methods.

Limiting static friction: fs(max)​=μs​N Kinetic friction: fk​=μk​N Coefficient of friction: μ=f/N

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Normal Reaction Must Be Constant: Laws hold only when comparing surfaces under the same normal load. Changing N changes f proportionally but μ remains constant for given materials. · Dry Surfaces Assumed: Standard laws apply to dry (Coulomb) friction. Wet or lubricated surfaces follow fluid friction rules where viscosity matters.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Statement 1: Limiting friction is directly proportional to the normal reaction between surfaces. · Statement 2: Friction is independent of the apparent area of contact for a given normal load.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Block (5 kg) on horizontal table, μ = 0.4. Limiting friction = 0.4 × 5 × 9.8 = 19.6 N. Applied force of 15 N produces no motion (static friction adjusts to 15 N). Applied force of 25 N causes acceleration: a = (25 − 0.4×5×9.8)/5 = (25−19.6)/5 = 1.08 m/s². · Inclined plane: Block slides at θ = 30°. μ = tan 30° = 0.577. Independent of block mass.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Thinking friction depends on contact area: It does NOT for dry friction — only normal reaction matters. · Confusing μ_s and μ_k: μ_s applies to stationary/impending motion; μ_k applies during sliding. μ_s ≥ μ_k always.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Definition of Solid Friction: Force opposing relative motion (or tendency of motion) between two solid surfaces in contact. Acts tangentially at the interface. Always opposes actual or impending motion. · Types of Solid Friction: (1) Static friction — acts when bodies are at rest relative to each other; self-adjusting up to a maximum value f_s(max) = μ_s N. (2) Kinetic (dynamic) friction — acts during sliding; constant magnitude f_k = μ_k N. (3) Rolling friction — much smaller than sliding friction.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

f ∝ N → plot f vs N → slope = μ. · tan(angle of repose) = μ — no force measurement needed.

Key points

Text

Friction is independent of apparent contact area. · Limiting friction ∝ normal reaction (f_max = μN). · μ depends only on material pair and surface condition. · Static friction > kinetic friction (μ_s > μ_k).

12.
Label

Universal facts

Text

Amontons formulated the first two laws of friction in 1699; Coulomb added the distinction between static and kinetic friction in 1785. · Friction converts mechanical energy into heat — brakes get hot because of this.

More hints

Distinguish clearly between static (self-adjusting ≤ μ_s N) and kinetic (constant = μ_k N) friction. · Always draw free-body diagram showing normal reaction, weight components, applied force, and friction direction.

15.
Label

Extra solved

Text

Car braking: μ = 0.7 on dry road. Maximum deceleration = μg = 0.7 × 9.8 ≈ 6.86 m/s². Stopping distance from 20 m/s: v²/(2μg) = 400/(2×6.86) ≈ 29 m.

17.
Label

Study notes

Text

Law 1 — Independence of Apparent Area: The force of friction is independent of the apparent area of contact, provided the normal reaction remains the same. A brick lying flat or on its edge experiences the same frictional force if weight is unchanged. · Law 2 — Proportionality to Normal Reaction: The limiting frictional force is directly proportional to the normal reaction between the surfaces: f_max = μN, where μ is the coefficient of friction. This defines μ as the ratio f_max/N. · Law 3 — Dependence on Nature of Surfaces: Friction depends on the materials and roughness of the contacting surfaces. Smooth glass-on-glass has low μ; rubber-on-concrete has high μ. Lubrication reduces μ dramatically.

More ideas

Text

Angle of repose: tanθ=μ · Friction on incline (just sliding): mgsinθ=μmgcosθ · Net force with friction: Fnet​=Fapplied​−fk​

19.
Label

More ideas

Text

Low Speeds: Kinetic friction is approximately constant at low speeds. At very high speeds, heating and surface deformation change μ. · Angle of Repose Equals Friction Angle: On an inclined plane, the angle at which sliding begins equals arctan(μ). This provides a direct experimental measure of μ without measuring forces. · Angle of repose θ satisfies tan θ = μ.

20.
Label

More ideas

Text

Friction always opposes relative or impending motion. · Believing friction always opposes motion: Static friction can act in the direction of motion (e.g., car tire pushing forward on road). · Assuming μ > 1 is impossible: μ can exceed 1 for very sticky surfaces (e.g., silicone rubber).

21.
Label

More ideas

Text

Forgetting friction is passive: It only exists in response to an applied force or tendency to move. · μ is dimensionless — it is a ratio of two forces. · Real contact area is microscopic; friction arises from molecular adhesion and interlocking asperities.

Label

More ideas

Text

Rolling friction coefficient is typically 100× smaller than sliding friction. · In NEB exams, verification experiments (horizontal pull and inclined plane) are frequently asked as long-answer questions. · Statement 3: Coefficient of friction depends solely on the nature and roughness of contacting surfaces.
