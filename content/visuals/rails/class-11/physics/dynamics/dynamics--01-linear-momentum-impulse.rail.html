Text

Momentum: p​=mv [kg·m/s] Second law (general): F=dtdp​​; constant mass: F=ma Impulse (constant force): J=FΔt; general: J=∫Fdt (area under F–t)

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Newton's 2nd Law - General Form: F=dp​/dt is the fundamental form of Newton's second law. It remains valid for variable-mass systems (rockets, raindrops) where F=ma fails because mass is not constant. · Impulse-Momentum Theorem: J=Δp​ is derived by integrating F=dp/dt over the collision time. It is valid regardless of whether the force is constant, time-varying, or impulsive — the integral captures the total momentum change.

Label

Special cases

Text

Note 1: In rebound problems the velocity CHANGES SIGN — Δp=m(vf​+vi​), not the difference. The commonest numerical error. · Note 2: Quote impulse in N·s and momentum in kg·m/s — numerically identical, and examiners check units.

Limitation

Text

❌ 'Momentum and kinetic energy are interchangeable measures of motion.' ✅ Momentum mv is a vector conserved in ALL collisions; kinetic energy 21​mv2 is a scalar conserved only in elastic ones. Both grow with speed but differently. · ❌ 'Impulse equals force.' ✅ Impulse = force × time (or the area under F–t). A small force acting long can deliver a huge impulse.

Key points

Text

Momentum is a vector — always assign signs/directions before computing Δp. · Impulse and momentum share units (N·s = kg·m/s); impulse is the area under the force–time graph. · For a FIXED momentum change, force and time trade off: Favg​=Δp/Δt. · F=dp/dt is the general second law — essential for variable-mass (rocket) problems.

Study notes

Text

Type 2 — alternate treatment: this version emphasises the momentum form of Newton's second law and the impulse–momentum theorem with force–time graphs — the framing NEB theory questions use. · Impulsive Force: A force that acts for a very short time but produces a finite change in momentum. Examples: hammer hitting a nail, bat hitting a ball, foot kicking a football. The force is extremely large, often thousands of Newtons, but the duration is milliseconds. The impulse is the area under the force-time curve, J=∫Fdt. · Linear momentum: p​=mv — mass in motion, SI unit kg·m/s. It is a VECTOR along the velocity. A 1 kg ball at 10 m/s and a 10 kg cart at 1 m/s carry equal momentum but very different kinetic energies (21​mv2: 50 J vs 5 J). Momentum is a measure of the 'difficulty' to stop an object. It is the 'inertia of motion'.

More ideas

Text

Conservation Requirement: Linear momentum is conserved only when the net external force on the system is zero. Internal forces always cancel in pairs (Newton's 3rd law) and cannot change the system's total momentum. · Momentum conservation holds in all collisions; kinetic energy only in elastic ones. · Recoil problems are momentum bookkeeping: MV+mv=0 from rest.

Label

More ideas

Text

❌ 'The ball exerts no force on the bat while being hit because the bat is harder.' ✅ Newton's third law: contact forces are equal and opposite during the collision — the ball's velocity change comes from the contact force acting on IT. · ❌ 'A heavy object always has more momentum.' ✅ Momentum = mass × VELOCITY — a light fast bullet can out-momentum a heavy slow trolley; direction matters too. · ❌ Using F=ma for a rocket ejecting fuel. ✅ Mass changes with time, so F=ma fails; the momentum form F=dp/dt handles it and yields the rocket equation.
