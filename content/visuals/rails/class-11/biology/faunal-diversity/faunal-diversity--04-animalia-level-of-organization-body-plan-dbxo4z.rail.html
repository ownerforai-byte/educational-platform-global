17.

Study notes

Text

Animalia is sorted, before any phylum name is used, by a short list of body characters: how the cells are organised, the body plan, the symmetry, the number of germ layers, the body cavity, and whether the body is segmented. Two animals that look nothing alike can still sit near each other if those characters match.… · Level of organisation runs cellular, tissue, organ, then organ system. Porifera stop at the cellular level: the cells divide labour, but they do not form true tissues. Cnidaria reach the tissue level. Platyhelminthes reach organs, such as a pharynx and flame cells, without packing those organs into systems the way a worm with a closed blood system does.… · Symmetry is the way a body can be cut into matching halves. Most sponges are asymmetrical. Cnidaria are radial: several planes through the oral-aboral axis give similar halves, which suits a life of waiting for food to arrive from any side. From flatworms onward the body is bilateral. One plane only divides it into left and right, and the front end gathers sense organs.…

A complete gut plus a body cavity is the organ-system grade. · Radial symmetry in Cnidaria is a primary condition. Radial symmetry in an adult starfish is secondary.

Text

Body plan names the layout of the gut. Sponges are a cell aggregate with canals, not a gut. Cnidaria and flatworms have a blind sac: one opening both takes food in and puts waste out. From roundworms onward the plan is a tube within a tube.… · Germ layers and the coelom decide how much space the organs have. Diploblastic animals, the cnidarians, have ectoderm and endoderm with jelly between them. Triploblastic animals add mesoderm. Flatworms are acoelomate: mesoderm fills the space as a solid parenchyma, and there is no body cavity.… · Segmentation, or metamerism, is the repetition of body units that each carry a share of the same organs. It is the annelid character, and it continues in arthropods and chordates.…
