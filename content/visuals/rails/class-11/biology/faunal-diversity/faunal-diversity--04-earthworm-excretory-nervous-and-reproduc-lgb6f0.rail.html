The earthworm's internal systems are well adapted to its soil-dwelling life. The digestive system (buccal cavity → crop → gizzard → intestine with typhlosole) processes decaying matter. The closed circulatory system has 5–6 heart-like vessels. The excretory system uses metanephridia (intersegmentary, septal, genital). Digestive system: buccal cavity → pharynx → oesophagus → crop (storage) → gizzard (grinding) → intestine (with typhlosole for increased surface) → rectum → anus. · Nervous system: cerebral ganglion (brain) above the oesophagus; ventral nerve cord (double sub-oesophageal ganglion) with segmental ganglia; sensory organs in prostomium. · Excretory system: metanephridia (nephridia) — one pair per segment; types: integmentary (perivisceral), septal, and genital; collect coelomic fluid and excrete ammonia/urea.
