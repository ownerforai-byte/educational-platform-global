Text

Protozoa are animal-like, mostly unicellular eukaryotes classified by their locomotion into four classes: Sarcodina (pseudopodia, e.g. Amoeba), Mastigophora (flagella, e.g. Trypanosoma), Ciliophora (cilia, e.g. Paramecium), and Sporozoa (non-motile spores, e.g. Plasmodium). Paramecium and Amoeba are model organisms. Plasmodium (malaria) with its Anopheles vector is a key NEB examination topic.…

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Protista is a single group of organisms. — Protista is a polyphyletic kingdom-level group; it contains very diverse organisms (algae, protozoa, slime moulds) that are grouped together mainly because they are unicellular eukaryotes, not because they share close evolutionary relationships. · Binary fission and conjugation in Paramecium are both reproduction. — Binary fission is asexual reproduction producing new individuals. Conjugation is a sexual process for genetic exchange between two individuals; it does not increase the number of organisms and is therefore NOT reproduction.

Protista: unicellular eukaryotes. Includes protozoa (animal-like), algae (plant-like) and slime moulds (fungus-like). · Protozoa characteristics: unicellular, eukaryotic, heterotrophic, locomotion by cilia, flagella or pseudopodia, reproduce by binary fission or multiple fission. · Classification: (1) Mastigophora (flagella: Euglena, Trypanosoma), (2) Sarcodina (pseudopodia: Amoeba, Entamoeba), (3) Ciliophora (cilia: Paramecium, Vorticella), (4) Sporozoa (spores, parasitic: Plasmodium, Toxoplasma).

Label

More ideas

Text

All protozoa are harmful parasites. — Most protozoa are free-living and ecologically beneficial (e.g., Amoeba regulates bacterial populations, Euglena contributes to photosynthesis). Only a few species are parasitic (Plasmodium, Entamoeba, Trypanosoma). · Euglena is a plant or an animal. — Euglena is neither a plant nor an animal; it is a protist with mixotrophic nutrition. It has chloroplasts for photosynthesis (plant-like) but lacks a cell wall and can also feed heterotrophically (animal-like). · Contractile vacuoles are found in all protozoa. — Contractile vacuoles are present only in freshwater protozoa that need osmoregulation. Marine protozoa generally lack contractile vacuoles because the external environment is isotonic or hypertonic to their cytoplasm.
