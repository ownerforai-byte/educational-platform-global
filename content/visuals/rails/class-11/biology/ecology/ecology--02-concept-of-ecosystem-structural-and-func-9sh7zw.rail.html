Concept

Text

An ecosystem (Tansley, 1935) is nature's self-sustaining unit: a biotic community running on its abiotic environment through one-way energy flow and cyclic nutrients. Its structure has three layers — abiotic components (inorganic nutrients, organic detritus, climate), biotic components (producers, consumers, decomposers) and functional links (energy flow, nutrient cycling, homeostasis).…

Self-sustaining test: a real ecosystem needs only sunlight — everything else (matter) is internally recycled; artificial ecosystems (aquarium) need constant human input.

12.

Key points

Text

Ecosystem (Tansley, 1935): self-sustaining unit = biotic community + abiotic environment + energy flow + nutrient cycling. · Structure: abiotic (inorganic, organic, climate) + biotic (producers, consumers, decomposers) + functional (energy flow, cycling, homeostasis). · Pond zones: littoral, limnetic, profundal/benthic. Forest layers: canopy, understorey, floor (detritus chain dominates). · 5m PYQ repeated 2023 and 2024 — definition + structure + diagram.
