Text

Energy and matter flow through ecosystems in food chains (linear) and food webs (networks). Trophic levels (producers → primary consumers → secondary → tertiary) structure these. Ecological pyramids (number, biomass, energy) visualise trophic structure; the pyramid of energy is always upright because ~90% of energy is lost at each transfer (10% rule).…

Conditions

Text

A food chain is LINEAR; a food web is a NETWORK of chains (more realistic). · Producers (plants, phytoplankton) are at trophic level 1; herbivores at level 2; carnivores at level 3+.

Grassland food chain: grass → rabbit → fox → eagle (4 trophic levels). · Pond food web: algae → mosquito larva → frog → snake → eagle; also algae → zooplankton → small fish → big fish.

Limitation

Text

The pyramid of ENERGY is always upright; the pyramids of numbers and biomass CAN be inverted. · An inverted pyramid of numbers occurs when one large organism supports many small ones (e.g., a tree with many insects).

Key points

Text

Food chain = linear; food web = network of chains. · Trophic levels: TL1 producers, TL2 herbivores, TL3 carnivores, TL4+ top carnivores. · Three pyramids: number, biomass, energy. · Pyramid of energy is ALWAYS upright; number and biomass can be inverted.

Universal facts

Text

The 10% rule (Lindeman, 1942) explains why food chains are usually 4–5 trophic levels long. · A pyramid of energy is ALWAYS upright because energy is lost at each transfer (heat, respiration).

Study notes

Text

Food chain: a linear sequence of organisms where each is eaten by the next; transfers energy and matter (sun → producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer). · Food web: a network of interconnected food chains; more realistic than a single chain; shows multiple feeding relationships. · Trophic levels: TL1 producers (autotrophs), TL2 primary consumers (herbivores), TL3 secondary consumers (carnivores), TL4+ tertiary consumers.

More ideas

Text

NPP is the energy available to herbivores. · An inverted pyramid of biomass occurs in aquatic ecosystems where phytoplankton (producers) have low standing biomass but high turnover. · The 10% rule: only ~10% of energy transfers from one trophic level to the next; ~90% is lost as heat.
