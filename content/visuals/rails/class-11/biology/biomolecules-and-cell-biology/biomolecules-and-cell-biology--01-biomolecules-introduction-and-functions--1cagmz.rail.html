Biomolecules are the chemical basis of life. Carbohydrates supply and store energy and form structural fibres; proteins (from 20 amino acids) act as enzymes, transporters, contractile and defence molecules in four structural levels; lipids store energy and build membranes; nucleic acids (DNA/RNA) store and express genetic information; enzymes, water and minerals complete the set.…

20.

More ideas

Text

Biomolecules are organic compounds of living organisms — carbohydrates, proteins, lipids and nucleic acids — and the inorganic biomolecules water and essential minerals. · Carbohydrates follow the general formula Cm(H2O)n; monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, galactose) condense into disaccharides (sucrose, lactose, maltose) and polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose). · Proteins are polymers of 20 amino acids joined by peptide bonds (-CO-NH-), organised into primary, secondary, tertiary and quaternary levels of structure.

21.
Label

More ideas

Text

Lipids are not true polymers; triglycerides, phospholipids, steroids and waxes store energy at about 9 kcal/g, build membranes and act as hormones. · Nucleic acids are polymers of nucleotides (pentose sugar + phosphate + nitrogenous base); DNA stores genetic information while RNA directs protein synthesis. · Enzymes are biological catalysts that lower activation energy without being consumed; most are proteins but some catalytic RNA molecules (ribozymes) also act as enzymes.
