Conditions

Text

Cellulose is a structural polymer in plant cell walls, not an energy reserve; glycogen (animal) and starch (plant) are the storage forms. · Starch is unbranched amylose + branched amylopectin; both yield maltose on hydrolysis.

Study notes

Text

Biomolecules are organic molecules essential to life: carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, nucleic acids, plus minerals, enzymes and water. · Carbohydrates (CH2O)n: monosaccharides (glucose, fructose, ribose), disaccharides (sucrose=glucose+fructose, lactose, maltose), polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose). Provide energy (4 kcal/g) and structural support. · Proteins: polymers of 20 L-amino acids joined by peptide (-CO-NH-) bonds. Four structural levels (primary, secondary, tertiary, quaternary). Functions: enzymes, transport (haemoglobin), contraction, defence, structural.

More ideas

Text

Lipids are not polymers; they do not have repeating monomers like the other three macromolecule classes. · A peptide bond forms between the -COOH of one amino acid and the -NH2 of the next, releasing one water (condensation). · Stress that enzymes lower activation energy; they do NOT supply energy.
