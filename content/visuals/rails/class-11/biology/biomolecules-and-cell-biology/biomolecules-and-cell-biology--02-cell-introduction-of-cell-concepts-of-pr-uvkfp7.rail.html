Conditions

Text

"Prokaryotes have no nucleus" vs "no DNA": prokaryotes have DNA — just no nuclear envelope around it. The nucleoid is DNA + cytoplasm, not a membrane compartment. · Viruses are NOT prokaryotes: viruses are acellular (no membrane, no cytoplasm, no ribosomes); prokaryotes are complete living cells. This is also why viruses are exceptions to cell theory.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

70S vs 80S: the S (Svedberg) unit measures sedimentation rate, not size — 70S ribosomes are not "smaller than 80S by 10"; they differ in subunit composition (50S+30S vs 60S+40S). · Cell wall vs cell membrane: every cell has a membrane; only some have a wall. The wall is outside the membrane, fully permeable; the membrane is the selective barrier.

Quick concepts

Text

Compartmentalisation — membranes create internal environments so incompatible reactions coexist; the defining advantage of eukaryotic organisation. · Surface-area-to-volume limit — the physical reason for cell size and multicellularity. · Selective toxicity — 70S vs 80S ribosomes let antibiotics kill bacteria without harming human cells.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Cell = smallest structural + functional unit of life; unicellular organisms do everything with one cell. · Discovery chain: Hooke (1665, dead cork, named "cell") → Leeuwenhoek (1674, living cells) → Brown (1831, nucleus) → Schleiden & Schwann (1838–39, theory) → Virchow (1855, cells from cells). · Cell theory: (1) organisms made of cells, (2) cell = structural + functional unit, (3) cells arise from pre-existing cells. Exceptions: viruses, viroids/prions, coenocytes, mitochondria/chloroplasts. · Cells are small because volume grows faster than surface area — diffusion would fail in a big cell.

More ideas

Text

Every cell has: membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, DNA, same genetic code. · Prokaryote: no nuclear envelope, no membrane-bound organelles, 70S ribosomes, naked circular DNA + plasmids, peptidoglycan wall, binary fission. · Eukaryote: true nucleus + nucleolus, membrane-bound organelles, 80S ribosomes, histone-bound linear chromosomes, mitosis/meiosis.

More ideas

Text
