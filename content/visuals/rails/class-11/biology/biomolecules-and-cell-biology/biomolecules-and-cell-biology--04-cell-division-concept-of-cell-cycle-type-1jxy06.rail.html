Conditions

Text

Chromatid vs chromosome: after S phase, each chromosome consists of two sister chromatids joined at one centromere — it is still ONE chromosome. The count changes only when centromeres split (anaphase). · Mitosis vs meiosis II: both split centromeres and separate sister chromatids, but meiosis II starts from haploid, recombined cells and follows meiosis I without new DNA replication.

Limitation

Text

Crossing over vs independent assortment: crossing over = physical exchange between non-sister chromatids (pachytene); independent assortment = random orientation of bivalents at metaphase I. Both create variation, by different mechanisms. · Interkinesis vs interphase: interkinesis between meiosis I and II has NO DNA replication; interphase of the cell cycle always includes S phase.

Quick concepts

Text

Reduction then equation: meiosis I halves the number (homologs separate); meiosis II halves the chromatid count (centromeres split) — together they convert one 2n cell into four n cells. · Variation machinery: crossing over (pachytene) + independent assortment (metaphase I) are the two engines of genetic diversity. · Cancer = cell-cycle control failure: checkpoints lost → endless mitosis; the clinical shadow of this topic.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Cell cycle = interphase (G₁ growth, S DNA synthesis, G₂ preparation) + M phase (mitosis) + cytokinesis; checkpoints guard the transitions; G₀ = quiescent exit. · Interphase is the most active phase — DNA replication and growth happen there. · Amitosis: direct splitting, no spindle, imprecise (Remak, 1841). · Mitosis (Flemming, 1882): prophase → metaphase → anaphase → telophase; centromeres split in anaphase; 2 identical 2n daughters; growth, repair, asexual reproduction.

Universal facts

Text

Typical human cell cycle ≈ 24 h: G₁ 11 h, S 8 h, G₂ 4 h, M 1 h. · Flemming observed and named mitosis (1882); term "meiosis" — Farmer and Moore (1905).
