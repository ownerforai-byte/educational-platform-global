1.

Concept

Text

Life runs on four carbon macromolecule classes, each with a signature monomer and job: carbohydrates (quick energy + structure), proteins (work: enzymes, transport, contraction, defence), lipids (dense storage + membranes), nucleic acids (heredity + protein synthesis) — completed by enzymes, water (~70% of the cell) and minerals as regulators.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

carbs (CH2O)n, C:H:O = 1:2:1 · peptide unit –C(=O)–NH– · condensation: monomer + monomer → dimer + H2O (reverse = hydrolysis) · DNA: A=T (2 H-bonds), G≡C (3 H-bonds); RNA: A=U, ribose, single strand

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Polymers assemble only by condensation (water released per bond) and break only by hydrolysis. Enzymes act within a narrow temperature–pH window and denature outside it. The 8 adult essential amino acids must arrive from food — the body cannot synthesise them.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Lipids are NOT true polymers — no repeating monomer (the favourite trap; the unit MCQ bank asks exactly this). Cellulose (β-glucose) builds walls while starch/glycogen (α-glucose) store energy. Denaturation unfolds a protein but is often reversible. tRNA folds into cloverleaf despite RNA being single-stranded.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

Join glycine + alanine: the –COOH of glycine faces the –NH2 of alanine, one H2O leaves, and the –CO–NH– link left behind is the peptide bond (circle it). Reverse the arrow — add water across that bond — and you have hydrolysis back to two amino acids.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

'All enzymes are proteins' has ribozyme exceptions (catalytic RNA). Lock-and-key is only the first approximation — induced fit completes it. Energy values (4 kcal/g carbs/protein, 9 kcal/g lipid) are Atwater averages, not exact per molecule.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Start from one amino acid (NH2–CHR–COOH) and condense a chain: each new link ejects H2O, giving primary structure (sequence). Hydrogen bonding coils it (secondary: α-helix/β-sheet), R-group interactions fold it (tertiary), and multi-chain assembly gives quaternary structure — haemoglobin's four chains carrying haem are the textbook example.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

C–P–L–N: Carbs = quick energy, Proteins = do the work, Lipids = store + membranes, Nucleic acids = inform. In NEB answers pair EVERY macromolecule with both its monomer and its function — the unit's special-notes file says that pairing earns full marks. Starch test: iodine → blue-black.
