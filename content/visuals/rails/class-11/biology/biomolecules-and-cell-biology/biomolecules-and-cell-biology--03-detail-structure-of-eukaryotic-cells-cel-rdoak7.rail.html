11.
Label

Quick concepts

Text

Endomembrane system flow: nucleus → RER → Golgi (cis→trans) → vesicles → membrane/lysosome/secretion — the cell's production line. · Compartmentalisation again: lysosome pH ~5 vs cytosol pH ~7.2 is why suicide bags stay harmless until they rupture. · Semi-autonomous organelles: mitochondria and chloroplasts carry circular DNA + 70S ribosomes and divide by fission — the endosymbiotic signature.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Cell wall: cellulose (plants), chitin (fungi), peptidoglycan (bacteria); middle lamella → primary → secondary; fully permeable. · Membrane: fluid-mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson 1972) — phospholipid bilayer + integral/peripheral proteins + cholesterol + glycocalyx; selective permeability, transport, signalling. · Mitochondria: double membrane, cristae with oxysomes, matrix with Krebs enzymes + circular DNA + 70S ribosomes; aerobic respiration → ATP ("powerhouse"). · Plastids: chloroplast (photosynthesis), chromoplast (colour), leucoplast (storage: amylo/elai/aleuroplast); grana = thylakoid stacks in stroma.

Membrane thickness ≈ 7–8 nm; mitochondrion ≈ 0.5–1 µm; ribosome ≈ 15–20 nm. · About 50 acid hydrolases are packed in lysosomes.

More ideas

Text

9+2 axoneme (cilia/flagella); 9+0 centriole · RER: ribosome-studded cisternae — protein synthesis for export; SER: tubules — lipids, steroids, detox, Ca²⁺ (sarcoplasmic reticulum). · Golgi (Golgi 1898): cis face receives from ER, modifies (glycosylation), trans face ships — vesicles, lysosomes, cell plate.

19.
Label

More ideas

Text

Lysosomes (de Duve): acid hydrolases at pH ~5; digestion, autophagy, autolysis — "suicide bags". · Ribosomes (Palade 1953): rRNA + protein, no membrane; 80S = 60S+40S (eukaryote), 70S = 50S+30S (prokaryote/organelles); polysomes; translation. · Nucleus (Brown 1831): double envelope + pores, nucleolus (rRNA factory), chromatin (eu- vs heterochromatin) → chromosomes with centromere; metacentric/submetacentric/acrocentric/telocentric.

20.
Label

More ideas

Text

Cilia/flagella: 9+2 microtubule axoneme from basal bodies; cilia short/numerous/oar-like, flagella long/few/undulating. · Inclusions: starch, glycogen, fat droplets, protein crystals, pigments, crystals — non-living stores; plant central vacuole maintains turgor. · Lysosome vs vacuole: lysosomes are single-membraned enzyme sacs for digestion; the plant central vacuole is mainly storage/turgor, though it also contains hydrolases.

21.
Label

More ideas

Text

RER vs SER: ribosomes on the surface define RER and protein synthesis; smooth tubules without ribosomes make lipids and detoxify. · Cilia vs flagella: same 9+2 axoneme, but cilia are short, numerous and beat like oars; flagella are long, few and undulate. · Nucleolus vs nucleus: the nucleolus is a membraneless sub-region of the nucleus that makes rRNA/ribosomes — not a separate organelle.

22.
Label

More ideas

Text

Inclusions vs organelles: inclusions (starch, fat, crystals) are non-living, non-membrane-bound deposits; organelles are living, organised functional structures. · Tomato chromoplast — carotenoid pigments colour the ripe fruit. · Tracheal ciliated epithelium — cilia sweep mucus; destroyed by smoking.
