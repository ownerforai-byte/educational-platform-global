Label

Conditions

Text

Lytic vs lysogenic: lytic kills the host immediately; lysogenic integrates as prophage and replicates with the host until induced to lyse. · Capsid vs envelope: capsid = protein coat made of capsomeres; envelope = host-derived lipid membrane with spikes (present in influenza, HIV; absent in phage).

Q. What is a prophage? Answer. Phage DNA integrated into the host bacterial chromosome during the lysogenic cycle. It replicates passively with the host's DNA at every cell division and can later be induced (by stress) to excise and enter the lytic cycle.

Quick concepts

Text

Obligate parasitism: a virus is a bundle of heredity without a metabolism — it borrows the host cell's ribosomes, enzymes and energy to copy itself.

12.
Label

Key points

Text

Viruses: 20–300 nm, DNA or RNA (never both), nucleocapsid, no metabolism/ribosomes, replicate only in living host cells, crystallisable — borderline of life. · T2 phage: head (dsDNA) + collar + contractile sheath + base plate + 6 tail fibres; host = E. coli. · Lytic cycle: adsorption → penetration (DNA injection) → biosynthesis → maturation → lysis (lysozyme, 100–200 phages). Lysogenic: prophage integration. · Importance: phage therapy, genetic-engineering vectors, Hershey–Chase DNA proof; but influenza, HIV, polio, TMV, foot-and-mouth.
