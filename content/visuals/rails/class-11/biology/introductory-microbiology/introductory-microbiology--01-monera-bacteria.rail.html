Kingdom Monera comprises prokaryotic organisms: bacteria, cyanobacteria, mycoplasma, and archaebacteria. Bacteria show diverse shapes, nutrition, and reproduction (binary fission + genetic exchange). 18.

Study notes

Text

Kingdom Monera: prokaryotic organisms (no membrane-bound nucleus); includes bacteria, cyanobacteria (blue-green algae), mycoplasma, and archaebacteria. · Bacterial cell: capsule (slime layer) → cell wall (peptidoglycan) → plasma membrane → cytoplasm; 70S ribosomes; naked circular DNA (nucleoid); may have pili, fimbriae, flagella, mesosomes. · Shapes: cocci (spherical), bacilli (rod), spirilla/spirochaetes (spiral). Arrangements: diplococci, streptococci, staphylococci.

More ideas

Text

Gram staining · Gram +ve: thick peptidoglycan → retains crystal violet (purple); Gram -ve: thin + LPS → pink (safranin) · Binary fission = asexual reproduction; conjugation/transformation/transduction = genetic exchange.

Label

More ideas

Text

Cyanobacteria: photosynthetic + nitrogen-fixing (Nostoc, Anabaena). · Gram stain: +ve (purple, thick wall) vs -ve (pink, thin wall + LPS). · Binary fission produces two genetically identical daughter cells; conjugation transfers DNA but does not increase cell number.

More ideas

Text

Gram-positive bacteria have a THICK peptidoglycan layer; Gram-negative have a THIN layer plus an outer LPS membrane (more resistant to many antibiotics). · Cyanobacteria (Nostoc, Anabaena) and their nitrogen-fixing role are frequently asked. · Mention Mycoplasma as the smallest living cell and its lack of a cell wall.
