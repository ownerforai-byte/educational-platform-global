Text

Trick 3: The last age is the harshest: sans teeth, sans eyes, sans taste, sans everything — the repetition 'sans' drives the message of decline; quote it. · ## The themes worth an essay The stages of life — the surface theme, and the exam's favourite: identify the seven ages and their traits. The predictability of human life — Jaques implies every life runs the same route; the comfort and the horror are the same fact. Role-playing and identity — we are our costumes as much as our selves; the lover is only a lover while the part lasts.…

Special cases

Text

The title, defended The speech is known by its first line because the first line is the poem: "All the world's a stage" is not a title hung on an argument but the argument itself, stated as fact. A candidate defending it should say exactly that: the title performs the metaphor's confidence — no "perhaps", no "like" — and the certainty is what makes the seven ages follow so obediently. · ## How NEB asks it "List the seven ages of man with one trait each." (7 marks) — infant (helpless), schoolboy (unwilling), lover (sentimental), soldier (ambitious), justice (pompous), pantaloon (shrinking), second childhood (dependent). Learn them as a walk, not a list.…

Derivation

Text

Reading it closely, age by age The opening metaphor sets the rules: "All the world's a stage, and all the men and women merely players." Merely is the knife in the sentence — not just players, but only players: our roles are assigned, our exits not ours to schedule.… · ## The devices, with evidence - Extended metaphor — the master device: world = stage, life = play, and every detail obedient to it (entrances, exits, parts, acts). - Simile — "creeps like snail", "sighs like furnace", "bearded like the pard": each age given an animal or an object that is its temperament. - Imagery — theatrical and bodily at once: satchels, beards, round bellies, slippers, hose.…

Shortcut

Text

Trick 1: The extended metaphor: life = drama, people = actors, God = playwright — state the controlling metaphor before anything else. · Trick 2: The seven ages in order: infant, schoolboy, lover, soldier, justice, pantaloon (old man), second childhood — memorise the list; sequencing questions are guaranteed.
