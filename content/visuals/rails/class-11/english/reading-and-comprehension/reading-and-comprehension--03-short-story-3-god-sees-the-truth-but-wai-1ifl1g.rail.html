The story at a glance "God Sees the Truth, but Waits" by Leo Tolstoy is a parable of injustice and forgiveness: Ivan Dmitrich Aksionov, a prosperous merchant with a slight weakness for drink, is falsely accused of murdering a fellow merchant, flogged, and sent to Siberia for twenty-six years. In the prison he becomes a holy man — and then the real murderer, Makar Semyonich, is placed in the same cell.…

The plot, retold with an eye on its turns The tragedy is set up in two strokes: Aksionov is young, merry, and fond of drink, and a rival merchant stays the night with him. On the road, a soldier asks whose blood is on his coat — the fellow merchant has been found murdered, and a blood-stained knife is discovered in Aksionov's bag.… · ## The devices, with evidence - Irony of fate — the master device: the innocent man is convicted on evidence the guilty man planted, and lives the sentence; the blood-stained knife is the story's whole injustice in one image. - Dramatic irony — the reader watches the wife doubt him, the courts err, and the years pass, knowing what none of them know.…
