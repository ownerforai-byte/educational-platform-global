Conditions

Text

Copying vs. paraphrasing: Copying = writing the exact words from the passage (0 marks). Paraphrasing = expressing the SAME MEANING in YOUR OWN WORDS (full marks). Example: Passage says 'The children played joyfully in the garden.' Copy: 'The children played joyfully in the garden' (wrong). Paraphrase: 'The kids had fun playing outdoors in the garden' (correct).… · Inference vs. opinion: An inference is a LOGICAL conclusion drawn from evidence in the passage. An opinion is YOUR PERSONAL belief that may or may not be supported by the passage. Inference: 'The character was likely anxious because she kept checking her watch.' Opinion: 'I think the character was anxious because I get nervous too.' Inferences use passage evidence; opinions…

True vs. Not Given: True = the passage CONFIRMS the statement. False = the passage CONTRADICTS the statement. Not Given = the passage NEITHER confirms nor contradicts — the information is simply absent. Example: Passage says 'Rahul went to school.' Statement: 'Rahul walked to school.' This is NOT GIVEN — we know he went, but not HOW.… · Short answer vs. long answer format: A short answer (1 mark) is one complete sentence that directly answers the question. A long answer (2-3 marks) is answer + evidence/reason. Format for long answers: 'The answer is X because Y [with reference to the passage].' For short answers, skip the 'because' — just state the answer directly.…

The most common comprehension error across all levels is copying from the passage. Studies of examiner reports show that 40-60% of students who lose marks in comprehension do so because they copied sentences instead of paraphrasing. Mastering paraphrasing in exam preparation benefits your overall English proficiency.
