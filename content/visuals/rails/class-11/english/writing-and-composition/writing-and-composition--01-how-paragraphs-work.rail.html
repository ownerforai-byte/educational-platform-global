Concept

Text

English paragraphs work by announcing one controlling idea early, supporting it with evidence, ANALYSING the evidence, and linking onward — held together by pronoun/synonym glue and signalled by transitions, arranged in one clear logical order.

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

One paragraph = one controlling idea, announced early. MEAL: Main idea → Evidence → Analysis → Link.

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

❌ A paragraph is 'however many sentences the teacher said'. ✅ A paragraph is one idea developed — sentences follow the idea, not a count. · ❌ Starting a new paragraph whenever you like. ✅ New paragraph = new idea, new time, new place, or new speaker.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

❌ Piling transitions into every sentence ('Moreover... Furthermore... In addition...'). ✅ Use signals only where the logical relationship actually changes. · ❌ Evidence without analysis ('...this shows nothing'). ✅ After every example, one sentence explains HOW it supports the main idea.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

MEAL in action: MAIN — 'Public libraries remain essential in the digital age.' EVIDENCE — 'In 2024, the Kathmandu public library recorded over 200,000 visits.' ANALYSIS — 'This shows that free access to quiet study space, not books alone, is what communities still need.' LINK — 'Libraries, in short, have changed their job, not lost it.' · Cohesion chain: 'Migration has reshaped the valley. THIS FLOW of people has strained housing. SUCH PRESSURE has, in turn, driven up rents.' (pronoun + synonym glue)

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

❌ Topic sentence at the end ('building up to the point'). ✅ In English, state the main idea first — early and explicitly.

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

The big idea: a paragraph is ONE idea, fully developed — not a fixed number of sentences. Every sentence inside exists to serve that single idea. If you start answering a new question, start a new paragraph. · The 4-part anatomy: 1) TOPIC SENTENCE states the main idea (usually first); 2) SUPPORTING SENTENCES give reasons, facts, examples; 3) EXPLANATION/ANALYSIS links evidence back to the idea (this is the sentence weak writers skip); 4) CONCLUDING/LINK sentence rounds off or bridges to the next paragraph.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

English paragraphs lead with the conclusion (writer-responsible style). · Time order in narration: 'First the alarm failed. Then the bus was missed. By the time the exam began, panic had taken over.'

Identify topic sentence, evidence and analysis in a published article paragraph. · Rewrite a jumbled 8-sentence paragraph in MEAL order and justify each move.

Hints

Text

Unity (cut off-topic sentences) + cohesion (pronouns, synonyms, key-noun repetition) + coherence (one logical order). · Transitions are traffic signals, not decoration — sparing use.

11.
Label

More practice

Text

Write a 6-sentence paragraph on 'The value of morning exercise' using exactly two transitions.

12.
Label

Study notes

Text

The MEAL formula for any body paragraph: Main idea → Evidence → Analysis → Link. Say it, prove it, explain why the proof matters, connect onward. Works for essays, reports and answers alike. · UNITY test: cover a paragraph and ask 'what is this about?' If you need two answers, split it. Every sentence that cannot be traced to the topic sentence is cut material. · COHESION — the glue devices: pronoun reference (this policy, such measures), repeated key nouns (deliberate repetition of 'migration' anchors the paragraph), synonym chains (a student → the learner → this young person), and collocations that hold naturally in English.

13.
Label

More ideas

Text

COHERENCE — logical order: time order (first, then, finally) for narration; order of importance (most → least) for arguments; spatial order (near → far) for description; problem → cause → solution for reports. Choose ONE order per paragraph and stay in it. · TRANSITIONS — the traffic signals: adding (moreover, in addition), contrasting (however, on the contrary), cause/effect (therefore, as a result, consequently), exemplifying (for instance, namely), sequencing (firstly, subsequently), concluding (in short, to sum up).… · WHY English paragraphs feel 'backwards' to new writers: English is writer-responsible — the topic sentence must come EARLY and announce the point directly. Many Nepali/Asian academic styles build up to the point at the end. In English, leading with the conclusion is a feature, not rudeness.

14.
Label

More ideas

Text

Paragraph shape: the classic shape is diamond — broad opening statement, narrowing specifics in the middle, slight broadening at the close. Extremely long paragraphs (250+ words) and one-sentence paragraphs both look unbalanced in essays. · Paragraph length rule of thumb: 4-8 sentences for essays; 2-3 for news writing; 1 for notices and warnings. Length follows function. · Application to exam answers: in long-answer questions, a new marker expects a new paragraph per point: introduction paragraph → one paragraph per argument (MEAL) → conclusion. Examiners reward visible structure as much as content.
