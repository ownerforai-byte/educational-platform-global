Concept

Text

English grammar is one sentence-mechanics system with six branches — word classes, the 12-tense grid, agreement, voice, narration, modals/conditionals — plus a micro-zone layer of articles, prepositions, tags and fixed pairs that supplies most exam questions. Learn each branch as a machine, walk the error-scan in fixed order, and the whole syllabus compresses onto one revisable page.

Solved

Text

One sentence, all six branches: 'Yesterday, each of the players, who had trained hard, was given a medal by the captain.' — agreement (each...was), relative pronoun, tense (had trained), voice (was given), narration, modifiers. · The tense grid in one breath: I write / I am writing / I have written / I have been writing — shift all four into past and future for 12.

Derivation

Text

How to use this: grammar is one connected system, not 30 separate chapters. This mindmap is the central trunk (SENTENCE) with six branches — every exam question hangs off one of them. Revise by walking the branches, not by re-reading chapters. · CENTRAL NODE — THE SENTENCE: every English sentence = SUBJECT + VERB + (OBJECT/COMPLEMENT) + (modifiers). Everything below is either how these parts CHANGE FORM (morphology) or how they COMBINE (syntax). If you can locate the subject and verb of any sentence, you can solve any grammar question.

15.

Study notes

Text

BRANCH 1 — PARTS OF SPEECH (the 8 word classes): Noun (common, proper, collective, abstract, material; countable vs uncountable), Pronoun (personal I/we/they, possessive mine/ours, reflexive myself, relative who/which/that, demonstrative this/those, indefinite each/everyone, interrogative who?/what?), Verb (transitive vs intransitive; state vs action; regular/irregular),… · BRANCH 1 sub-node — determiners: articles a/an/the, quantifiers some/any/much/many/few/little, and the demonstrative/possessive words that sit before nouns. The article rules and quantifier choices generate a large share of fill-in-the-blank questions. · BRANCH 2 — TENSES (12-cell grid): 3 times (past/present/future) × 4 aspects (simple/continuous/perfect/perfect continuous). Key cells: Simple Present = habits & universal truths ('The sun rises in the east'); Present Perfect = past action, present relevance ('I have finished'); Present Perfect Continuous = since/for duration ('has been raining since morning'); Past Perfect…

CONDITIONALS: 1) if+present→will; 2) if+past→would; 3) if+had V3→would have V3. · AGREEMENT: verb follows the HEAD noun; each/every = singular; or/nor → nearest subject. · Universal truths don't backshift.
