Grammar · Voice

Title

Active to passive — moving the spotlight to the action

Href

/class-11-notes/english

Icon

Workflow

Stat Key

pyq:english

Rows

1.
Label

Concept

Text

Voice shifts attention: active spotlights the doer ('The teacher gave homework'), passive spotlights the receiver ('Homework was given to the students'). Daily life runs on passive — news ('Three people were injured'), science ('The solution was heated'), notices ('Candidates are requested to…'). The verb always becomes be + past participle; the old subject survives only as an optional by-phrase.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

active S + V + O → passive O + be(TENSED) + V3 (+ by S) · gives→was given · is writing→is being written · has done→has been done · will do→will be done · 'The teacher gave the students homework' → 'The students were given homework' / 'Homework was given to the students'

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

Only transitive verbs passivise — 'She sleeps well' has no object to promote. The be-verb carries the original tense (gave→was/were given, not 'is given'). Double-object verbs offer two passives (promote either object). Intransitives, reflexives and state verbs (have, lack, resemble) refuse the transformation.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

Questions passivise too ('Who broke this?' → 'By whom was this broken?'). Imperatives go through 'let' ('Do it' → 'Let it be done') or modals ('You are requested to…'). Agentless passives dominate real use — the doer is unknown, obvious or deliberately hidden ('Mistakes were made').

5.
Label

Solved

Text

NEB 2024 (2): 'The teacher gave the students homework' → 'The students were given homework by the teacher' (promote the indirect object, gave→were given to match plural 'students'). Alternative full-mark answer: 'Homework was given to the students by the teacher.' Tense frozen, participle correct, agent optional.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

Passivising unaccusatives ('He died' → nothing) or intransitives is ungrammatical, not just awkward. Overuse breeds fog — good style prefers active unless the receiver truly matters. Some passives change emphasis so far they mislead ('The window was broken' hides who did it — sometimes the point, sometimes evasion).

7.
Label

Derivation

Text

Derive any passive mechanically: (1) identify S–V–O and the tense; (2) promote O to subject position; (3) rewrite the verb as be-in-that-tense + V3; (4) demote old S to by-phrase or drop it. Run 'She is writing a letter': O = a letter, tense = present continuous → 'A letter is being written by her' — continuous needs being, perfect needs been.

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Spot the tense first — be-verb must mirror it (was/were for past, is/are for present). V3 is non-negotiable (given, written, done — never gave/wrote). If the sentence has no object, write 'no passive possible' and move on.

9.
Label

Board question

Text

NEB 2024 (2): passive of 'The teacher gave the students homework.' Voice transformation (both directions), tense-matched be-forms, and agentless-vs-agent choice complete the set.
