The essay at a glance "What is Poverty?" by Jo Goodwin Parker is one of the most powerful definitions in modern English — and it is powerful because the definer is inside the thing defined. Parker, writing in the voice of a woman on welfare with three children, answers the title's question not with economics but with the body: poverty is dirt, fatigue, humiliation, and the daily arithmetic of not enough.…

Text

The title, defended "What is Poverty?" asks the essay's question in four words and refuses to answer it the way institutions do. The defence: the title promises a definition, and the essay delivers one — but a definition by testimony, each "Poverty is..." a clause of lived evidence. The title's plainness is strategic: the simplest question, asked by the person with the most authority to answer it. · ## NEB short answers, solved Q. How does Parker define poverty? (2 marks) A. By its lived texture rather than its statistics: poverty is dirt, fatigue, lack of privacy, the humiliation of asking for help, and the daily arithmetic of not enough. It is a condition that corrodes pride and honour like acid and a chisel. Q. What does the essay say about fatigue? (2 marks) A.…
