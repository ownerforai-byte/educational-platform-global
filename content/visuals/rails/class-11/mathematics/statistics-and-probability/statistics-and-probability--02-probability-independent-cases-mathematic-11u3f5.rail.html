6. Common Confusions Mutually exclusive ≠ independent: if A and B are exclusive and P(A), P(B) > 0, then P(A∩B) = 0 ≠ P(A)P(B) — exclusive events are maximally dependent. · Addition law needs the overlap: forgetting −P(A∩B) overcounts; only the exclusive case drops it. · P(A|B) ≠ P(B|A): the probability of disease given a positive test is not the test's accuracy — Bayes exists because these differ.
