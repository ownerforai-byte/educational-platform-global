Title

The unit circle behind every identity

Href

1.
Label

Concept

Text

On a circle of radius 1 the point at angle θ has coordinates (cos θ, sin θ), so the horizontal leg is cos θ and the vertical leg is sin θ — every trigonometric identity is that one right triangle rearranged.

Caption

Unit circle: radius 1, cos θ as the horizontal leg and sin θ as the vertical leg.

2.
Label

Formula

Kind

formula

Text

x = cos θ, y = sin θ; x² + y² = 1; sin²θ + cos²θ = 1; tan θ = sin θ / cos θ

3.
Label

Conditions

Text

θ is measured anticlockwise from the positive x-axis and must be in radians in calculus; wherever cos θ = 0 the tangent is undefined.

4.
Label

Special cases

Text

30°, 45° and 60° give the standard ratios, and the maximum of a sin θ + b cos θ is √(a² + b²) — the amplitude circle's radius.

5.
Label

Solved

Text

θ = 40°: cos 40° ≈ 0.766 and sin 40° ≈ 0.643, and indeed 0.766² + 0.643² = 1.000.

6.
Label

Limitation

Text

The circle fixes the sign by quadrant but says nothing about triangles beyond 180°, which is where the CAST rule and the general solution take over.

7.
Label

Derivation

Kind

formula

Text

Right triangle with hypotenuse 1 ⇒ x = cos θ, y = sin θ; Pythagoras gives x² + y² = 1 ⇒ sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

8.
Label

Shortcut

Text

Learn the circle instead of four quadrant rules: read the sign of cos θ off the x-axis and the sign of sin θ off the y-axis.

9.
Label

Board question

Text

Using the unit circle, prove sin²θ + cos²θ = 1 and find every θ in [0, 2π] for which sin θ = 1/√2.
