Concept

Text

Parametric and implicit differentiation both exist because a curve need not be expressible as y=f(x). In the parametric case the parameter cancels, leaving a ratio of parameter derivatives, and a vanishing dtdx​ marks a vertical tangent. In the implicit case one differentiates an equation solved for neither variable, treating y as a function of x throughout, then isolates dxdy​.

14.
Label

Study notes

Text

Parametric rule: if x=x(t) and y=y(t) then dxdy​=dx/dtdy/dt​, provided dtdx​=0. The parameter cancels, which is what allows a curve with no closed-form y=f(x) to be differentiated at all. · Why dtdx​=0 is special: it means dxdy​ is infinite, i.e. a vertical tangent. A circle x=cost, y=sint gives dxdy​=−sintcost​ and a vertical tangent at t=0, matching the geometry. · Implicit differentiation: differentiate F(x,y)=0 with respect to x, treating y as a function of x throughout, then collect the dxdy​ terms. For x2+y2=25 one gets 2x+2ydxdy​=0, hence dxdy​=−yx​.
