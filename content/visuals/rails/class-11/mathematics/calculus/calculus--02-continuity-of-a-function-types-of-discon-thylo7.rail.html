Study notes

Text

Fact. Continuity: f continuous at c iff lim_{x→c}f(x) = f(c). Types of discontinuity: removable, jump, infinite. · Removable discontinuity (hole): The limit limx→a​f(x) exists, but either f(a) is undefined or f(a)=limx→a​f(x). The graph has a "hole" at x=a. Example: f(x)=x−1x2−1​ at x=1. · Jump discontinuity: The left-hand and right-hand limits both exist but are not equal: limx→a−​f(x)=limx→a+​f(x). The graph "jumps" at x=a. Example: the floor function f(x)=⌊x⌋ at every integer.

More ideas

Text

A vertical asymptote always indicates an infinite discontinuity, but not all discontinuities involve vertical asymptotes. · A function can be continuous at a point but not differentiable there (e.g., f(x)=∣x∣ at x=0). Continuity is a weaker condition than differentiability. · A function can have a limit at a point without being continuous there — the function might be undefined at that point, or its value might differ from the limit.
