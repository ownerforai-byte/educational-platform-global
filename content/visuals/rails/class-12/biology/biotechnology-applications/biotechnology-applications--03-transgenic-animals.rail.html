Transgenic animals let a foreign gene be studied or harvested in a living mammal. The course uses are development, disease models, proteins such as Rosie's human alpha-lactalbumin at about 2.4 g/L of milk, vaccine safety and chemical safety. Welfare and the patenting of animals are part of a complete answer.

17.

Study notes

Text

Transgenic animals are used because a chosen gene can be added and then watched in a living body. The course expects five uses: normal development, models of disease, biological products, vaccine safety, and chemical safety. · To study normal physiology, an extra copy or an altered copy of a gene such as a growth-factor gene is introduced. The change in growth or development shows what that gene was doing. · Disease models carry a gene that contributes to a human illness, for example genes studied in cancer or in Alzheimer's disease. The mouse then shows features of the illness, and treatments can be tried before they are tried in people.

21.
Label

More ideas

Text

Give two ethical objections. · Biological products can be made in the animal's milk. Rosie, the first transgenic cow, reported in 1997, produced milk with human alpha-lactalbumin at about 2.4 grams per litre. The milk was more like human milk in that protein. Other projects put human proteins such as alpha-1-antitrypsin into the milk of livestock. · Vaccine safety can be checked in transgenic animals before a batch is widely used, by asking whether the vaccine causes the harm it is supposed to avoid. Chemical safety testing uses the animals to see how toxic a substance is, more quickly than some older assays.
