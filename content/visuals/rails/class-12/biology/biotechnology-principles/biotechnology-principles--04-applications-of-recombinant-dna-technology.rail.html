Recombinant DNA supplies human proteins such as insulin, vaccine antigens such as hepatitis B surface protein from yeast, and tests that either copy nucleic acid or detect antigen and antibody. Somatic gene therapy, Bt crops and bioremediation are the same idea outside the fermenter. The product still has to be the right molecule, made in a suitable host, and purified.

17.

Study notes

Text

Recombinant DNA is used wherever a cell can be instructed to make a molecule that is hard to collect from its natural source. The applications sit in medicine, farming, industry and the cleanup of pollutants. · In medicine the landmark product is human insulin made in E. coli, approved as Humulin in 1982. The amino-acid sequence is the human one, so the immune reactions that came with some animal insulins became less of a problem. The same logic produces other proteins: human growth hormone, and vaccines such as the hepatitis B surface antigen made in yeast. · Diagnosis uses the same toolkit. PCR amplifies a pathogen's DNA until a test can see it. ELISA detects an antigen or an antibody with an enzyme-linked reaction. One finds nucleic acid. The other finds protein. They answer different clinical questions.

More ideas

Text

Explain why recombinant insulin reduced the problem of animal insulin. · Gene therapy delivers a working copy of a defective gene into a patient's somatic cells. The correction is for that person. It is not passed to children, because the germline is not the target of the permitted treatments. The first approved attempt, in 1990, was for ADA deficiency. · In agriculture the same technology produces Bt crops and other modified plants. In the environment, microbes have been designed or selected to break down oil and other pollutants, which is bioremediation. The microbe eats the waste. It does not make the waste vanish by a physical filter alone.
