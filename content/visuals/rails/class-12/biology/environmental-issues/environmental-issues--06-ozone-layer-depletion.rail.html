Stratospheric ozone, about 15–35 km up and normally about 300 Dobson units, absorbs ultraviolet-B. Study notes

Text

Ozone in the stratosphere, about 15 to 35 km above the surface, absorbs ultraviolet-B that would otherwise reach living tissue. The amount in a vertical column is measured in Dobson units. One DU is a layer 0.01 mm thick at standard temperature and pressure. A normal column is about 300 DU. · Ozone is made and destroyed all the time. Ultraviolet light splits O2 into oxygen atoms, and an atom joining another O2 makes O3. Left alone, formation and breakdown stay in balance. The layer is not a fixed shell of gas that never changes. It is a balance. · CFCs upset that balance. They are stable near the ground and drift upward for years. High in the stratosphere, ultraviolet light breaks a chlorine atom off. Chlorine reacts with ozone to make chlorine monoxide and oxygen, then chlorine monoxide reacts with a free oxygen atom and releases the chlorine again. The chlorine is a catalyst.…
