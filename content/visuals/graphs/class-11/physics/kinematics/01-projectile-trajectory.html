Notes

  • The path of a projectile is a parabola: y=xtanθ−2u2cos2θgx2​. The graph below shows a 45° launch (maximum range): the body rises to the peak H, then falls symmetrically.
  • A projectile is given an initial speed u at an angle θ above the horizontal and then moves under gravity alone. Air resistance is ignored in this treatment, so the horizontal acceleration is zero and the vertical acceleration is −g.
  • The horizontal and vertical motions are solved separately and then recombined. x = (u cosθ) t and y = (u sinθ) t − (1/2) g t^2. Eliminating t produces the trajectory y = x tanθ − g x^2 / (2 u^2 cos^2 θ), which is a parabola.
  • The time to return to the same horizontal level is T = 2 u sinθ / g. The horizontal range on level ground is R = u^2 sin 2θ / g. Because sin 2θ is greatest at 2θ = 90°, the greatest range is at θ = 45°.
  • The greatest height is H = u^2 sin^2 θ / (2g), reached when the vertical velocity has fallen to zero. At that point the horizontal velocity is still u cosθ.
  • The sketch of a 45° launch is symmetric: the body rises to H and comes down on a mirror path. The labelled peak on a teaching graph is a scale drawing of that shape, not a measured height for every choice of u.

Confusion

  • The acceleration is not zero at the top. Only the vertical velocity is zero.
  • Range and height use different expressions. R is not 2H.
  • 45° is the angle for maximum range only on level ground with no air resistance.
  • Horizontal velocity does not decrease in this model.

Key Points

  • The path is a parabola.
  • Horizontal velocity stays constant if air resistance is ignored.
  • Vertical motion has acceleration −g.
  • Range is greatest at 45° on level ground.

Exercises

1.

A body is launched at 20 m/s at 45° above the horizontal. Take g = 10 m/s^2. Find the range and the greatest height.

Steps
  • sin 2θ = sin 90° = 1, and sin 45° = 1/sqrt(2), so sin^2 45° = 1/2.
  • R = u^2 sin 2θ / g = 400 × 1 / 10 = 40 m.
  • H = u^2 sin^2 θ / (2g) = 400 × (1/2) / 20.
  • 400 × 0.5 = 200, and 200 / 20 = 10.
  • Range 40 m, greatest height 10 m. This numerical height is not the scale label on a sketch that uses a different launch speed.
Answer

Range 40 m, greatest height 10 m.

Summary

With no air resistance a projectile follows a parabola because the horizontal velocity is constant and the vertical motion is uniformly accelerated. On level ground the range is u^2 sin 2θ / g, greatest at 45°, and the greatest height is u^2 sin^2 θ / (2g).
