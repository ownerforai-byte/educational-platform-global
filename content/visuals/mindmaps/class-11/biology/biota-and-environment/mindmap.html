Topic

Animal Adaptations — Aquatic, Terrestrial and Volant

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Aquatic: fusiform body, gills (countercurrent), lateral line, webbed feet, fins.
  • Primary aquatic = always aquatic (fish); secondary = evolved from land (whales, seals).
  • Terrestrial: cursorial (run), fossorial (dig), arboreal (tree-dwell); waterproof skin, lungs.
  • Volant: wings, hollow bones, air sacs, strong pectoral muscles, beak.
  • Countercurrent exchange: ~80% O2 uptake in fish gills.

  • Terrestrial: waterproof skin + lungs + internal fertilisation + strong limbs
  • Volant: wings + hollow bones + air sacs + strong pectoral muscles + streamlined body + beak

Topic

Animal Behaviour and Migration

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Reflex action: automatic, involuntary; reflex arc (spinal level, no brain).
  • Taxis: directional movement (phototaxis, chemotaxis, thigmotaxis, geotaxis).
  • Kinesis: non-directional (random) movement; different from taxis.
  • Dominance and leadership: social hierarchies in group-living animals.
  • Fish migration: seasonal movement to feeding/breeding grounds.
2.
Name

Formulas & relations

Points
  • Reflex arc: Receptor → Sensory neuron → CNS (spinal cord) → Motor neuron → Effector
  • Taxis: directional movement (positive = toward, negative = away)
  • Kinesis: non-directional (random) movement in response to a stimulus
  • Migration: seasonal, genetically programmed movement between habitats
3.
Name

Statements to quote

Points
  • A reflex action is an automatic, involuntary response via a reflex arc.
  • Taxis is directional movement; kinesis is non-directional.
  • Social animals form dominance hierarchies.
4.
Name

Exam focus

Points
  • Explain a reflex action with a reflex arc diagram.
  • Differentiate taxis from kinesis.
  • List the 4 types of taxes with examples.
  • Reflex = R-E-F-L-E-X (Receptor, Effector, Fast, Local, eXternal).
  • Taxis = directional (Toward/Away); Kinesis = random (K for "kicking around").
5.
Name

Common misconceptions

Points
  • A reflex arc is SHORT (spinal level); it does NOT involve the brain (faster response).
  • Taxis is DIRECTIONAL (toward/away from a stimulus); kinesis is NON-DIRECTIONAL (random movement).
  • Phototaxis: movement in response to light (positive = toward, negative = away).

Topic

Environmental Pollution and Pesticides

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Air pollution: SO2, NOx, CO, PM2.5, O3, CFCs; sources: industry, vehicles, biomass.
  • Effects: respiratory disease, acid rain, smog, global warming, ozone depletion.
  • Kathmandu Valley: PM2.5 exceeds WHO limits ~10× in winter.
  • Water pollution: domestic, industrial, agricultural; effects: eutrophication, O2 depletion, disease.
  • Soil pollution: heavy metals, pesticides, plastics; effects: reduced fertility, food contamination.
2.
Name

Formulas & relations

Points
  • Air pollutants: SO2, NOx, CO, PM2.5, O3, CFCs
  • Water pollutants: domestic waste, industrial effluent, agricultural runoff, oil
  • Soil pollutants: heavy metals, pesticides, plastics, industrial waste
  • Biomagnification: toxin concentration INCREASES up the food chain
3.
Name

Statements to quote

Points
  • Air pollution causes respiratory disease, acid rain, and global warming.
  • Water pollution leads to eutrophication, O2 depletion, and disease.
  • Pesticides biomagnify up food chains and cause resistance.
4.
Name

Exam focus

Points
  • List the sources and effects of air pollution.
  • Explain water pollution (sources, effects, control).
  • Describe the effects of soil pollution.
  • Air = A-S-N-P (SO2, NOx, CO, PM2.5, O3, CFCs).
  • Water = domestic + industrial + agricultural.
5.
Name

Common misconceptions

Points
  • Acid rain (pH < 5.6) is caused by SO2 and NOx, NOT by CO2 alone.
  • Ozone is beneficial in the STRATOSPHERE (blocks UV) but harmful at ground level (smog, air pollutant).
  • Biomagnification: toxin concentration INCREASES at higher trophic levels (e.g., DDT in fish-eating birds).
