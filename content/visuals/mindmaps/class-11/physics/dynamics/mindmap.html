Application of Newton's Laws

Subtopics

Name

Key points

Points
  • The FBD method is universal: isolate → draw forces → choose axes → ΣF = ma → solve.
  • The normal force adapts to the situation — it is NOT always mg.
  • Tension in an accelerating string differs from the weight of the attached mass.
  • Action-reaction pairs act on different bodies — never cancel in a single FBD.
  • On a frictionless incline, all bodies slide at the same rate (independent of mass).

  • Smooth inclined plane: a = g sin θ down the slope; N = mg cos θ
  • Rough inclined plane: a = g(sin θ − μ cos θ); slides when tan θ > μ

Moment, Torque and Equilibrium

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Torque = rF sin θ — it depends on force, distance, AND the angle between them.
  • Equilibrium requires BOTH ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0 — one without the other is not enough.
  • A couple produces pure rotation (zero net force, non-zero torque) — the torque is the same about any point.
  • The centre of gravity simplifies a distributed weight to a single point force.
  • Choose the pivot at an unknown force's point of application to eliminate that force from the torque equation.
2.
Name

Formulas & relations

Points
  • Torque of a force: τ = rF sin θ (positive = anticlockwise, negative = clockwise)
  • Equilibrium conditions: ΣF = 0 (translational) AND Στ = 0 (rotational)
  • Couple torque: τ = F × d (independent of pivot position)
  • Centre of gravity: x_CG = Σ(mᵢxᵢ)/Σmᵢ (weighted average of positions)
  • Principle of moments: for equilibrium, sum of clockwise torques = sum of anticlockwise torques

Topic

Solid Friction

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Static friction is VARIABLE: it matches the applied force up to μ_sN.
  • Kinetic friction is approximately CONSTANT: f_k = μ_kN during sliding.
  • μ_k < μ_s — harder to start sliding than to keep sliding.
  • Friction is (approximately) independent of the apparent area of contact.
  • Pulling at an angle reduces N and friction; pushing at an angle increases both.
2.
Name

Formulas & relations

Points
  • Maximum static friction: f_s(max) = μ_sN (acts just before slipping)
  • Kinetic friction: f_k = μ_kN (acts during sliding; μ_k < μ_s)
  • Slope sliding condition: body slides when tan θ > μ_s; a = g(sin θ − μ_k cos θ)
  • Angle of friction: tan λ = μ_s (angle between normal and resultant reaction)
  • Angle of repose: tan θ_r = μ_s → θ_r = λ (steepest angle for rest)
3.
Name

Statements to quote

Points
  • Friction opposes relative motion (or its tendency) between two surfaces in contact.
  • Maximum static friction and kinetic friction are both proportional to the normal reaction: f_max = μN.
  • μ_k < μ_s for most surface pairs — it is harder to start sliding than to keep sliding.
