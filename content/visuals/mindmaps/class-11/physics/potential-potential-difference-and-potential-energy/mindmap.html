Key points

Points
  • Potential V is work done per unit positive charge; SI unit is volt (1 V = 1 J/C).
  • Potential is a path-independent scalar; electric field is a vector with direction given by -∇V.
  • Potential due to a point charge falls as 1/r, while its electric field falls as 1/r².
  • Like charges give positive U (repulsive, unbound); unlike charges give negative U (attractive, bound).
  • 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, an energy unit; 1 MeV = 10⁶ eV is standard in nuclear physics.

Potential Gradient

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Potential gradient is the rate of change of potential with distance, measured in V/m (identical to N/C).
  • Electric field equals the negative of the potential gradient: E = -dV/dx.
  • The field always points in the direction of steepest decrease of potential.
  • Differentiating V = kQ/r reproduces the inverse-square law E = kQ/r² exactly.
  • Slope of the potential–distance graph is minus the electric field at that point.
