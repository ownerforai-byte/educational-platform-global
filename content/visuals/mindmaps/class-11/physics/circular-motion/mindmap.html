Topic

Angular Displacement, Velocity and Acceleration

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Angular displacement is measured in radians; 2π rad = 360° = 1 revolution.
  • Angular velocity is a vector along the axis (right-hand rule), not tangential.
  • The kinematic equations for rotation mirror linear motion: x→θ, v→ω, a→α.
  • v = rω links linear and angular speed — the basis of all circular motion problems.
  • Always convert rpm to rad/s before using formulas: multiply by 2π/60.
2.
Name

Formulas & relations

Points
  • Angular velocity: ω = dθ/dt (instantaneous), ω = Δθ/t (average)
  • Angular acceleration: α = dω/dt = d²θ/dt²
  • Kinematic equations (constant α): ω = ω₀ + αt; θ = ω₀t + ½αt²; ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ
  • Linear-angular links: v = rω; a_t = rα; a_c = v²/r = rω²
  • rpm conversion: ω (rad/s) = rpm × 2π/60; revolutions = θ/(2π)

Topic

Centripetal Acceleration and Force

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • Centripetal force is NOT a new force — it is the resultant of real forces (tension, gravity, friction).
  • Centripetal acceleration changes only the direction of velocity, not its magnitude.
  • Centrifugal force is a pseudo-force that appears only in rotating reference frames.
  • If the centripetal force is removed, the body moves tangent to the circle.
  • Centripetal force is proportional to v² — doubling speed quadruples the required force.

  • Maximum speed (flat curve): vmax​=μs​rg​ — when friction reaches its maximum
  • Cyclist lean angle: tanθ=v2/(rg) — the angle needed to balance torque

  • If the centripetal force is removed, the body moves tangent to the circle (Newton's first law).

Conical Pendulum

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • The period of a conical pendulum depends only on L cos θ (the vertical height of the cone), not on mass.
  • The faster the rotation, the larger the angle θ — the cone becomes flatter.
  • The minimum period (θ → 0) equals the period of a simple pendulum of the same length.
  • The tension is always greater than mg: T = mg/cos θ.
  • The centripetal force is the horizontal component of tension — no new force is added.

Topic

Motion in a Vertical Circle

Subtopics
1.
Name

Key points

Points
  • The minimum speed at the top (string) is √(gr), independent of mass.
  • The minimum speed at the bottom (string) is √(5gr) — the most tested result.
  • For a rigid rod, v_bottom(min) = √(2gr) — the rod can push, not just pull.
  • Tension at the bottom is always maximum; at the top it is minimum.
  • At the minimum case (string), T_bottom = 6mg — six times the weight.

Name

Key points

Points
  • The ideal banking angle tan θ = v²/(rg) is independent of vehicle mass.
  • At design speed, no friction is needed — the horizontal component of normal force provides all centripetal…
  • Banking with friction increases the maximum safe speed beyond the design speed.
  • Railway superelevation h = w tan θ applies the same physics to train tracks.
  • Over-banking (θ too large for the speed) causes inward sliding; under-banking causes outward skidding.
2.
Name

Formulas & relations

Points
  • Ideal banking angle (no friction): tan θ = v²/(rg)
  • Maximum speed with friction: v_max = √[rg(tan θ + μ)/(1 − μ tan θ)]
  • Minimum speed with friction: v_min = √[rg(tan θ − μ)/(1 + μ tan θ)]
  • Railway superelevation: h = w tan θ, where w = track gauge
  • Normal force on banked road: N = mg/cos θ
