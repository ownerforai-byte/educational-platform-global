Electronegative Elements

One Line Summary

Electronegative elements that gain or share electrons to form anions or covalent bonds.

Config

ns² np¹⁻⁶ (plus H: 1s¹, He: 1s²)

Characteristics
1.
Title

Oxide Character

Detail

Exclusively acidic (SO₂, CO₂, P₄O₁₀) or neutral (CO, NO, N₂O). Never basic.

2.
Title

Diverse States

Detail

Gases (H₂, N₂, O₂, F₂, Cl₂, noble gases), Liquid (Br₂), Solids (C, P, S, I₂).

3.
Title

Conductivity

Detail

Poor thermal and electrical conductors due to localized valence electron pairs.

Exam Traps
  • Bromine (Br₂) is the only liquid non-metal at standard conditions.
  • Graphite conducts electricity via delocalized pi-electrons between hexagonal carbon sheets.
  • Iodine crystals display a lustrous metallic sheen despite being a halogen.

Name

Metalloids

Badge

Semiconductors

One Line Summary

Staircase borderline elements displaying intermediate metallic and non-metallic behaviors.

Config

ns² np¹⁻⁴

Characteristics
1.
Title

Semiconductivity

Detail

Electrical conductivity increases with temperature (negative temperature coefficient of resistance).

2.
Title

Doping Substrates

Detail

Basis for microelectronics and semiconductors (p-type with B; n-type with P/As).

3.
Title

Chemical Dualism

Detail

Form amphoteric or weakly acidic oxides (e.g. As₂O₃, Sb₂O₃).

Exam Traps
  • Primary set: B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po.
  • Silicon constitutes ~28% of Earth's crust mass, second only to oxygen.
  • Unlike metals whose resistance rises with heat, metalloid conductivity improves with temperature.

Coinage Metals

Badge

Group 11 (IB)

One Line Summary

Group 11 noble transition metals historically used for coinage due to extreme corrosion resistance.

Config

(n-1)d¹⁰ ns¹

Characteristics
1.
Title

Members

Detail

Copper (Cu), Silver (Ag), and Gold (Au).

2.
Title

Conductivity Peak

Detail

Silver has the highest electrical and thermal conductivity of all elements, followed by Copper.

3.
Title

Corrosion Resistance

Detail

Noble metals resistant to air, moisture, and non-oxidizing acids.

Exam Traps
  • Anomalous electron configuration: Cu is 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ (not 3d⁹ 4s²) for d¹⁰ exchange stability.
  • Zn, Cd, Hg are NOT coinage metals.
  • Gold dissolves in Aqua Regia (3:1 HCl:HNO₃) to yield soluble [AuCl₄]⁻.

Volatile Metals

Badge

Group 12 (IIB)

One Line Summary

Group 12 metals with completely filled d¹⁰ s² subshells, weak metallic bonds, and low boiling points.

Config

(n-1)d¹⁰ ns²

Characteristics
1.
Title

Members

Detail

Zinc (Zn), Cadmium (Cd), and Mercury (Hg).

2.
Title

Weak Lattice

Detail

No unpaired d-electrons participate in metallic bonding, resulting in unusually low melting/boiling points.

3.
Title

Pseudo-Transition

Detail

Often called non-typical transition elements because d-subshell remains full (d¹⁰) in both elemental and +2 ionic states.

Exam Traps
  • Mercury (Hg) is liquid at room temperature (MP: -38.8°C).
  • Zn²⁺, Cd²⁺, Hg²⁺ compounds are strictly diamagnetic and colorless (no d-d transitions).
  • Amalgams: Mercury dissolves almost all metals except Iron, Platinum, and Tungsten.
Key Elements
  • Zn
  • Cd
  • Hg

s-Block Elements

Badge

Groups 1 & 2

One Line Summary

Highly reactive, electropositive light metals where differentiating electrons enter the outermost s-orbital.

Config

ns¹ (Alkali) and ns² (Alkaline Earth)

Characteristics
1.
Title

Low Ionization Enthalpy

Detail

Largest atomic radii per period; strong reducing agents.

2.
Title

Flame Test

Detail

Impart vivid colors: Li (Crimson), Na (Yellow), K (Lilac), Ca (Brick Red), Ba (Apple Green).

3.
Title

Oxidation States

Detail

Strictly fixed +1 (Group 1) and +2 (Group 2); never show variable valency.

Exam Traps
  • Be and Mg do NOT give flame colors because their valence electrons are bound too tightly.
  • Diagonal relationships: Li resembles Mg; Be resembles Al in polarizing power.
  • Alkali metals in liquid ammonia produce deep blue, conductive, paramagnetic solutions.

Name

p-Block Elements

Badge

Groups 13 to 18

One Line Summary

The most diverse block containing all non-metals, all metalloids, and post-transition metals.

Config

ns² np¹⁻⁶

Characteristics
1.
Title

Multi-Phase Block

Detail

Houses metals (Al, Sn, Pb), metalloids (Si, Ge, As), and non-metals (C, O, halogens).

2.
Title

Inert Pair Effect

Detail

Reluctance of valence s-electrons to bond in heavier elements (Pb²⁺ > Pb⁴⁺, Tl⁺ > Tl³⁺).

3.
Title

Oxidation Variety

Detail

Oxidation states vary in steps of two units; max state = Group number - 10.

Exam Traps
  • Inert pair effect makes PbCl₂ stable and PbCl₄ an unstable strong oxidizer.
  • Fluorine never exhibits positive oxidation states (highest electronegativity, no d-orbitals in 2nd shell).
  • Carbon has exceptional catenation capability due to strong C-C bond energy (348 kJ/mol).

Name

d-Block Elements

Badge

Transition Metals (Groups 3-12)

One Line Summary

Transition elements with progressive filling of penultimate (n-1)d orbitals between s and p blocks.

Config

(n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻²

Characteristics
1.
Title

Variable Valency

Detail

Both (n-1)d and ns electrons participate in bonding (e.g. Mn shows +2 through +7).

2.
Title

Colored Compounds

Detail

d-d orbital splitting in crystal fields absorbs visible wavelengths (Cu²⁺ blue, Fe³⁺ amber).

3.
Title

Catalytic & Magnetic

Detail

Unpaired d-electrons impart paramagnetism and high catalytic surface reactivity.

Exam Traps
  • Sc³⁺ and Ti⁴⁺ compounds are colorless because their d-subshell is empty (d⁰).
  • Zn²⁺ is colorless and diamagnetic because its d-subshell is completely full (d¹⁰).
  • Osmium and Ruthenium exhibit the highest known oxidation state (+8 in OsO₄ and RuO₄).

Lanthanides & Actinides

One Line Summary

Inner transition series filling anti-penultimate (n-2)f subshells: 4f (Lanthanides) and 5f (Actinides).

Config

(n-2)f¹⁻¹⁴ (n-1)d⁰⁻¹ ns²

Characteristics
1.
Title

Common Valency

Detail

+3 is the universal stable oxidation state for lanthanides.

2.
Title

Lanthanide Contraction

Detail

Poor shielding of 4f electrons causes radii to steadily shrink across the row.

3.
Title

Actinide Radioactivity

Detail

All actinides are radioactive; transuranics (Z > 92) are synthetic.

Exam Traps
  • Due to lanthanide contraction, Zr (4d) and Hf (5d) have identical radii (~160 pm), making separation hard.
  • Ce⁴⁺ is a powerful analytical oxidizer returning to Ce³⁺.
  • Promethium (Pm) is the only synthetic/radioactive lanthanide.
