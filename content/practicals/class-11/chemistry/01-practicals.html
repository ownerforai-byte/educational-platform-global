1.

Salt is NaCl — why do governments force iodide into it? What single thyroid molecule makes this trace additive a public-health triumph?

ID

Type

Marks

Unit SLUG

Concept

Why iodised salt

Solution

Thyroxine (T4), the metabolism-setting hormone, needs FOUR iodine atoms per molecule — no iodine, no thyroxine, causing goitre and cretinism (irreversible mental impairment in children). Humans can't make iodine; mountain soils lack it. Adding micrograms of iodide to universal salt delivers the missing atom to everyone silently. Two-cent chemistry preventing brain damage at population scale: the cheapest molecule in medicine.

2.

Benzene has THREE double bonds yet refuses addition reactions (unlike alkenes) and does substitution instead. What is it 'protecting' — and what energy price would addition charge?

Concept

Benzene's refusal

Solution

It protects AROMATICITY: the 6 π-electrons delocalised in a ring (Hückel 4n+2), worth ~150 kJ/mol of resonance stabilisation. Addition would saturate one double bond and destroy the ring current — paying the full aromaticity price. Substitution (replace H, keep all three double bonds) preserves the sextet. Benzene's chemistry is conservation of delocalisation: every reaction is audited against the ring's special stability.

3.

Opposite charges attract — so why doesn't the electron spiral into the proton in a trillionth of a second? What quantum rule forbids the crash?

Type

Concept

Why electrons don't crash

Solution

The Heisenberg/de Broglie confinement: squeezing an electron near the nucleus spikes its kinetic energy (Δx·Δp ≥ ħ/2) — localisation costs energy that outweighs Coulomb gain below ~the Bohr radius. Plus only standing-wave orbits fit (integer wavelengths around the nucleus); the 'crash trajectory' is not a valid wave. The atom is an equilibrium between attraction pulling in and quantum confinement pushing out. Classical orbits decay; quantum waves persist.

4.

Ethanol (drinking alcohol) and dimethyl ether (toxic gas) share C₂H₆O. How can identical atoms be a beverage and a poison — and what does this prove about formulas?

Concept

Same formula, different person

Solution

Connectivity is identity: ethanol (C–C–O–H) offers an –OH for hydrogen bonding (liquid, drinkable in dilution); ether (C–O–C) hides oxygen centrally (gas, anaesthetic, toxic). Same atoms, different HANDSHAKES — properties emerge from structure, not composition. This is why molecular formulas alone are chemically illiterate: C₂H₆O names two strangers. Structure determines function — the founding lesson of all organic chemistry.

5.

Carbon monoxide kills at tiny concentrations though oxygen is 200,000× more abundant in air. How does CO win a competition it should lose 200,000-to-1?

ID

Type

Concept

Haemoglobin's iron trap

Solution

Affinity, not abundance: haemoglobin binds CO ~250× more strongly than O₂ (better orbital overlap with iron). Each captured CO also locks neighbouring subunits in high-affinity mode (cooperative binding backfires), so a little CO disables whole haemoglobin molecules. At 0.1% CO in air, half your haemoglobin is captured — oxygen present but undeliverable. Poisoning is competitive binding where the trace gas holds the tighter grip.

Concept

Water's bent empire

Solution

One 104.5° angle underwrites oceans, climate stability and winter survival. Molecular geometry scales to planetary destiny.

7.

Concept

Haber pressure gamble

Solution

N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ shrinks 4 gas moles to 2: pressure squeezes equilibrium toward ammonia (fewer moles favoured). At 1 atm conversion is ~10%; at 200 atm it multiplies several-fold — billions in extra fertiliser. But costs scale too: thicker vessels, bigger compressors, safety margins. 200 atm is the economic optimum where marginal ammonia revenue equals marginal compression cost. Le Chatelier points the direction; accounting picks the pressure.

8.

Aluminium is the most abundant metal in Earth's crust, yet it was once costlier than gold (Napoleon's aluminium cutlery). What single chemical property delayed its age by a century?

ID

Concept

Aluminium paradox

Solution

Its extreme reactivity: Al³⁺ clings to oxygen (ΔG of Al₂O₃ hugely negative), so carbon reduction — which freed iron and copper for millennia — cannot touch it. Only ELECTROLYSIS (Hall-Héroult, 1886), ripping oxygen off with electricity instead of carbon, unlocked it. Abundance never mattered; extractability did. The metal's age waited on cheap electricity, turning a precious curiosity into window frames within decades.

Concept

Chlorine cleans and kills

Solution

All three, but FORM dominates: dissolved HOCl/OCl⁻ at ppm levels oxidises microbial enzymes selectively (guardian); inhaled Cl₂ gas floods lungs, forming HCl on wet tissue (weapon). Dose scales the damage; target vulnerability shapes it. Paracelsus' rule rules chemistry: no substance is purely safe or purely poisonous — chlorine's split personality is concentration plus delivery route, not a changed molecule.

Mendeleev's gamble

Solution

Because holes are PREDICTIONS: a gap at mass ~72 with specified density, melting point and oxide formula (eka-silicon → germanium, found matching almost exactly) proves the pattern governs nature, not just catalogues it. 11.

Atomic radius SHRINKS across a period but GROWS down a group — opposite trends from the same table. Explain both with the two competing forces.

ID

Type

Marks

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

classification-of-elements-and-periodic-table

Concept

Atomic size zigzag

Solution

Across a period: protons added to the SAME shell pull harder (rising effective nuclear charge) while shielding barely changes → contraction. Down a group: each row adds a whole NEW shell, and distance beats charge — outer electrons sit farther despite more protons → expansion. One table, two tug-of-wars: across, charge wins; down, distance wins. Every periodic trend is this kind of contest between nuclear pull and shell real estate.

12.

Chemists count atoms in 'moles' (6.022×10²³) instead of billions. A dozen eggs works the same way. What practical weighing problem does the mole solve that direct counting never could?

ID

Concept

Mole as dozen

Solution

Atoms are uncountable individually but weighable collectively: the mole converts particle counts into GRAMS via atomic mass (12 g carbon = 1 mol = 6.022×10²³ atoms). Reactions need atom RATIOS (2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O), and the mole lets you measure those ratios on a balance instead of counting particles for eternity. It is a dozen scaled to the atomic world — the bridge between the invisible equation and the visible weighing scale.

13.

Tertiary carbocations form easily; methyl almost never. The positive charge is identical (+1). What do the neighbouring alkyl groups DO to the empty p-orbital?

ID

Type

Marks

3

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

Concept

Carbocation stability order

Solution

They DONATE electron density through hyperconjugation (adjacent C–H σ bonds overlapping the empty p) and inductive release — spreading the deficiency over more atoms. Tertiary has 9 hyperconjugative C–H bonds feeding the centre; methyl has 3. More neighbours = more sharing = lower energy. Stability order 3° > 2° > 1° > methyl is really a census of helpers: the charge never sits alone if alkyl groups can dilute it.

14.

Natural gas (methane) burns with a clean blue flame; a petrol spill burns orange and sooty. Both are hydrocarbons meeting oxygen. What molecular difference smokes one and not the other?

Concept

Methane vs petrol flames

Solution

The carbon-to-hydrogen RATIO and chain length. Methane (CH₄, C:H = 1:4) mixes with air instantly as a gas and oxidises completely to CO₂ + H₂O. Petrol's long chains (∼C₈, more carbon per molecule) vaporise unevenly; in oxygen-starved zones carbon atoms find each other instead of oxygen — clustering into glowing soot (incandescence = orange). Soot is unburned carbon's confession: too much fuel skeleton, too little oxygen access.

Concept

Why fertilisers rule

Solution

Usable nitrogen: N₂ gas is inert (triple bond ~945 kJ/mol), and only lightning plus nitrogen-fixing microbes trickle it into soil — far below crop demand at scale. Haber-Bosch brute-forces the triple bond with heat, pressure and catalysts, flooding fields with ammonia. The green revolution is applied equilibrium chemistry feeding half of humanity.

16.

Iron kept in dry air doesn't rust; in oxygen-free water it doesn't rust either. Rust demands BOTH. What electrochemical partnership makes each partner useless alone?

ID

prac-che11-redox

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

oxidation-and-reduction

Concept

Rusting needs both

Solution

Rusting is a mini-battery: iron oxidises at anodic spots (Fe → Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻) while OXYGEN + WATER accept those electrons at cathodic spots (O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻). No water = no electrolyte, circuit open. No oxygen = no electron acceptor, reaction stalls. Dry air lacks the ion highway; deaerated water lacks the electron sink. Rust proves corrosion is electrochemistry: two half-reactions holding hands across a water film.

17.

Rice cooks faster in a pressure cooker at the same flame. The heat source is identical — so what does raised pressure change about WATER itself?

ID

Concept

Pressure cookers

Solution

It raises water's BOILING POINT (~121°C at 2 atm): liquid water can get hotter before escaping as steam, and hotter water cooks faster (reaction rates roughly double per 10°C). The flame's power is unchanged; the pressure edits water's phase boundary, letting liquid exist where it normally couldn't. At altitude the reverse bites: low pressure drops boiling below 100°C and rice refuses to cook. Same flame, different ceiling.

You have 10 eggs and 3 cups of flour; one cake needs 2 eggs + 1 cup flour. How many cakes — and why does chemistry's limiting reagent decide everything from factories to airbags?

ID

Concept

Limiting reagent in kitchen

Solution

3 cakes (flour runs out; 4 eggs strand unused) — product is set by the scarcest input RATIO, not the biggest pile. Industry lives by this: excess cheap reactant (air's O₂) drives costly ones to full conversion; airbags meter NaN₃ against fixed gas volume. The limiting reagent is the bottleneck principle in chemical dress: output = min over inputs of (available ÷ required). Find the flour, find the future.
