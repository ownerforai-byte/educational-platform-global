1.

A 104°F fever endangers you, yet the pathogens die too. If heat harms both sides equally, why does raising body temperature ever HELP fight infection?

ID

prac-bio11-biomol

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

biomolecules-and-cell-biology

Concept

Fever denatures, not kills directly

Solution

Asymmetric vulnerability: many pathogens (and their enzymes) are tuned to exactly 37°C with narrow tolerance, while your cells tolerate a few degrees more — fever pushes invaders off their optimum while your immune cells (which work FASTER warm: higher kinetic rates) accelerate. It is thermal warfare exploiting different denaturation curves. But past ~105°F your own proteins start failing — the weapon turns on its wielder, which is why extreme fever is an emergency.

2.

Tube worms thrive at scalding, crushing deep-sea vents with no sunlight. Why can't a mammal simply evolve the same trick and colonise the vents?

ID

Type

Unit SLUG

biota-and-environment

Concept

Why no mammals in deep sea vents

Solution

Energy economics: mammals burn enormous energy staying warm and active (endothermy), demanding rich, constant food — vent oases are tiny and patchy. Tube worms cheat by outsourcing: symbiotic bacteria chemosynthesise vent chemicals, feeding the worm passively at near-zero metabolic cost. A mammal's furnace can't run on a vent's trickle. Body plan sets the energy bill; only low-budget lifestyles fit extreme, food-poor addresses.

3.

Keeping 100 tigers in zoos costs crores yearly; protecting one reserve shelters 100 wild tigers plus thousands of other species. Why is in-situ conservation overwhelmingly cheaper PER SPECIES saved?

ID

Type

Marks

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

conservation-biology

Concept

Zoos vs habitat math

Solution

Shared infrastructure: a reserve's forests, guards and water serve EVERY resident species simultaneously — the cost per species divides by thousands. A zoo replicates habitat per species at full price (custom diets, vets, enclosures) and preserves no ecological interactions (pollinators, prey webs). Ex-situ is an intensive-care unit: vital for the nearly extinct, absurd as a general strategy. Habitat protects relationships; cages protect individuals.

4.

An acre supports millions of grass blades, thousands of rabbits, but at most one fox. Why does body size so strictly cap population — and what breaks if you add ten foxes?

ID

Type

Marks

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

ecology

Concept

Why big animals are rare

Solution

The 10% law compounds: each level keeps ~10% of the level below, so energy reaching foxes is ~0.1% of grass energy — enough for few large bodies. Bigger bodies also burn more per individual. Add ten foxes: rabbits crash, grass explodes then overgrazes, and foxes starve — the classic overshoot. Pyramid shape is thermodynamics, not preference: size concentrates energy demand while supply thins upward. Top predators are rare by arithmetic.

5.

Penicillin worked universally in 1945; resistant strains appeared within years. Human evolution takes millennia. What TWO bacterial traits compress evolution into months?

ID

prac-bio11-evo

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

evolutionary-biology

Concept

Antibiotic resistance speed

Solution

(1) Generation time ~20 minutes: a year holds 26,000 bacterial generations vs less than one human generation — selection iterates furiously. (2) Horizontal gene transfer: bacteria swap resistance plasmids directly, spreading a winning mutation through a population sideways in days, not waiting for inheritance. Antibiotics then provide brutal selection, killing 99.9% and crowning the survivors. Fast generations plus shared genes turn hospitals into evolution accelerators — finish every prescription course.

6.

Insects outnumber all other animal species combined by millions. What THREE design features make the insect body plan evolution's most successful experiment?

ID

Concept

Why insects rule

Solution

Flight (first animals airborne — escape predators, colonise everywhere, find mates/morales efficiently), exoskeleton (armour + waterproofing + muscle-attachment in one cuticle, enabling tiny sizes), and metamorphosis (larva and adult occupy DIFFERENT niches — caterpillar eats leaves, butterfly sips nectar — so young never compete with parents). Small size + flight + life-stage division = multiply niches per species. Diversity follows design that avoids competing with itself.

7.

Nectar costs the plant precious sugar — given away free to insects. Why is this apparent waste actually cheaper than the wind-pollination alternative?

ID

Type

Marks

3

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

floral-diversity

Concept

Why flowers bribe

Solution

Precision beats volume: wind pollination wastes 99.99% of pollen (broadcast billions, hit one stigma by luck — hence hay fever). A nectar bribe hires a guided courier that flies flower-to-flower of the SAME species, delivering most pollen on target. The sugar salary is tiny beside the saved pollen mountain. Flowers outsourced delivery; grasses pay the broadcast tax. Coevolution turned waste into wages.

8.

Viruses reproduce and evolve but don't eat, breathe or maintain themselves. If you must vote ALIVE or NOT — what single criterion decides your vote, and why do textbooks quarrel?

ID

Type

Marks

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

introduction-to-biology

Concept

Is a virus alive

Solution

Vote hinges on METABOLISM vs REPLICATION: the alive-camp demands independent metabolism (viruses: none — inert crystals outside hosts); the other camp accepts replication-with-evolution (viruses: yes). Textbooks quarrel because 'life' is a human category, not a natural joint — viruses sit exactly on the definitional seam. The productive answer: viruses are obligate molecular parasites, evolving replicators without cells. The label matters less than the mechanism.

9.

Soap-and-water beats alcohol sanitiser against stomach bugs (norovirus) though sanitiser kills more germs on contact. What does soap REMOVE that sanitiser leaves behind?

ID

Type

Unit SLUG

Concept

Handwash vs sanitiser

Solution

The MICROBES THEMSELVES: soap molecules pry grease and debris off skin and water RINSES everything down the drain — physical removal, including non-enveloped viruses alcohol can't dissolve (norovirus lacks the lipid coat alcohol attacks). Sanitiser kills in place but leaves corpses, spores and chemicals on your hands — and fails on dirty/greasy hands where germs hide under grime. Removal beats murder when the enemy has armour.

10.

Mountains have a sharp TREELINE: lush forest below, zero trees above — though alpine flowers thrive higher. Same cold, same wind. What resource runs out for trees but not herbs?

ID

prac-bio11-veg

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

vegetation

Concept

Treeline mystery

Solution

A warm-enough GROWING SEASON for wood: trees must build perennial woody tissue and mature new shoots yearly, needing several warm weeks; above the treeline summers are too short/cold to ripen wood, so shoots freeze back annually. Herbs dodge this by living fast (complete life in weeks) or hibernating underground (perennial roots, disposable tops). The treeline is a time limit, not a cold limit — wood is slow capital that short summers can't fund.
