1.

Your phone charger converts AC to DC, wasting energy as heat. Why does the whole national grid still run on AC instead of distributing DC directly?

ID

prac-phy12-ac

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

alternating-current

Concept

Why the grid uses AC

Solution

Because of the TRANSFORMER, which works only on changing current. Power loss in wires is I²R, so grids transmit at hundreds of kilovolts (tiny current) and transformers step voltage up/down cheaply and efficiently (~99%). DC cannot be transformed this way — stepping DC voltage needs complex electronics. The charger's small conversion loss is the price paid once, at the endpoint, to buy massive transmission savings everywhere else.

2.

A 20 Hz–20 kHz audio signal cannot be broadcast directly from an antenna. What are the TWO physical reasons, and how does modulation fix both?

ID

prac-phy12-comm

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

communication-systems

Concept

Modulation necessity

Solution

(1) Antenna size: efficient radiation needs antenna ≈ wavelength/4 — a 1 kHz wave is 300 km long, needing a 75 km antenna; (2) All stations would overlap in the same 20 kHz band — total interference. Modulation shifts each station's audio onto a different high-frequency carrier: the short carrier wavelength needs only a metre-scale antenna, and each carrier frequency is a separate 'lane' your tuner selects. Same voice, new highway.

3.

A bird sits on a live 11 kV power line, unharmed — yet a human touching the same line dies. The voltage is identical. What actually kills, and why is the bird safe?

ID

prac-phy12-cur

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

current-electricity

Concept

Why birds survive wires

Solution

What kills is CURRENT through the body, which needs a voltage DIFFERENCE across it. The bird touches one wire at one point — both feet at ~the same 11 kV, so ΔV ≈ 0 and no current flows. A human on the ground touching the wire completes a path: 11 kV across the body drives lethal current to earth. Touch TWO wires (or wire + pole) and the bird dies too — lineworkers use insulated buckets precisely to avoid becoming the second contact.

4.

Push a magnet into a coil and the induced current repels it — you must push HARDER. Where does your extra muscular work go? What would happen if the current attracted instead?

ID

Type

Marks

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

electromagnetic-induction

Concept

Lenz's law as energy guard

Solution

Your work becomes electrical energy (lighting the bulb in the circuit) plus heat — Lenz's opposition IS energy conservation: mechanical work in, electrical energy out. If the induced current attracted the magnet instead, the magnet would accelerate in on its own, inducing more current, attracting harder — free energy from nothing, a perpetual-motion machine. Lenz's minus sign is nature's bookkeeping rule: you cannot get current without paying work.

5.

A metal rod on a rooftop does not 'attract' lightning from the sky — so how does it protect the building? Explain using field concentration.

ID

prac-phy12-elec

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

electrostatics

Concept

Lightning rods

Solution

The sharp tip concentrates the electric field enormously (charge crowds onto sharp points), ionising nearby air first. This launches an upward streamer that meets the descending lightning leader early — the strike connects to the rod, and the thick cable conducts it harmlessly to earth. Protection = offering lightning the easiest, most conductive path, not summoning it. A building without a rod forces lightning to make its own path — through wiring, pipes, or the roof itself.

6.

Take a compass to Earth's magnetic north pole. What does the needle do, and why does this prove the field is three-dimensional, not a flat arrow on a map?

ID

Type

practical

Marks

Difficulty

Unit SLUG

magnetism-and-magnetic-effect

Concept

Compass at the pole

Solution

The needle tries to point straight DOWN (dip ~90°) and swings uselessly for direction. Earth's field lines emerge nearly vertically at the magnetic poles — a compass only reads the HORIZONTAL component, which vanishes there. This kills the flat-map picture: field lines leave the southern hemisphere, loop through space, and re-enter the north vertically. Navigation compasses are even manufactured with regional balancing because dip varies worldwide.

7.

Shining ultra-bright red light on a metal ejects NOTHING, but faint violet light ejects electrons instantly. Why does intensity fail where frequency succeeds?

ID

prac-phy12-mod

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

hard

Unit SLUG

modern-physics

Concept

Photoelectric threshold

Solution

Because light arrives in photon packets of energy hf. One electron absorbs ONE photon: if hf < work function φ, no single interaction can free it — a trillion red photons are a trillion failed attempts, never pooled (one photon, one electron). Violet photons each exceed φ, so even a faint beam ejects electrons immediately; extra intensity only ejects MORE of them. This all-or-nothing threshold destroyed the wave-only picture: energy comes in lumps, and frequency sets the lump size.

8.

On a hot road you 'see water' that vanishes as you approach. There is no water and no mirror. Trace the actual ray path from sky to your eye.

ID

prac-phy12-ray

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

ray-optics

Concept

Mirage physics

Solution

Hot road heats adjacent air; hot air is rarer, so refractive index DECREASES toward the ground. Rays from the sky entering this gradient bend continuously upward (total internal reflection at grazing incidence) and arrive at your eye from BELOW eye level. Your brain assumes light travels straight, so it projects the ray backward into the ground — interpreting sky light as ground water. A mirage is the sky, refracted. It vanishes on approach because the grazing geometry breaks.

9.

A soap film is colourless liquid, yet it shines in rainbow colours that swirl and change. Where does the colour come from, and why does a black spot appear just before the bubble pops?

ID

prac-phy12-wave

Type

practical

Marks

3

Difficulty

medium

Unit SLUG

wave-optics

Concept

Soap film colours

Solution

Colour comes from INTERFERENCE, not pigment: light reflecting off the film's front surface interferes with light reflecting off its back surface. Film thickness ≈ wavelength scale, so some colours cancel and others reinforce — and thickness varies as liquid drains, so colours swirl. The black spot: when the film thins below ~λ/4 everywhere, the two reflections cancel for ALL visible wavelengths (one reflection picks up a half-wave phase flip) — total destructive interference. Black = the film is one molecule layer from bursting.
