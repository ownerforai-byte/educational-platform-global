Faunal Diversity, Evolution & Environment

Class 11Biology

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Natural selection means the physically strongest survive.' ✅ Natural selection means the 'fittest'—those best adapted to the current environment—survive and reproduce.
  • ❌ 'Energy in a food chain is destroyed at each step.' ✅ Energy is transformed (largely into heat) per the 10% law, not destroyed (conservation of energy applies).
  • ❌ 'Humans evolved directly from monkeys seen today.' ✅ Humans and modern monkeys share a common ancestor; humans did not evolve from the monkeys seen today.
  • Miller and Urey did not create a cell. They made amino acids.
  • Ten percent transfer is a loss of ninety percent, not a gain.
  • A notochord is not a backbone in every chordate. In vertebrates it is replaced.
  • Selection is not a wish by the animal. It is differential survival.

Notes

  • Porifera (sponges): pore-bearing aquatic animals with canal system; skeleton of spicules.
  • Arthropoda: largest phylum — jointed legs, chitinous exoskeleton, open circulatory system (insects, crabs).
  • Chordata characters: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits and post-anal tail at some stage of life.
  • Oparin–Haldane theory: life originated from non-living organic molecules in the primitive reducing atmosphere ('primordial soup'); Miller–Urey experiment (1953) supported it by producing amino acids from CH₄, NH₃, H₂, H₂O with electric discharge.
  • Darwinism: natural selection — survival of the fittest; variations that help survival are inherited.
  • Ecology levels: organism → population → community → ecosystem → biome. Food chain shows energy flow; ~10% energy transfers to each trophic level. Conservation: in-situ (national parks) and ex-situ (zoos, seed banks).

Key Points

  • Porifera are porous and sit at the cellular grade. Arthropods have jointed legs and a chitin exoskeleton.
  • Chordates, at some stage, have a notochord, a dorsal hollow nerve cord, gill slits and a post-anal tail.
  • Oparin and Haldane placed the origin of life in a reducing atmosphere. Miller and Urey made amino acids from simple gases.
  • Darwin's selection keeps hereditary variants that leave more offspring.

Exercises

1.

What did the Miller–Urey experiment actually produce, and what does that support?

Steps
  • The apparatus held simple reducing gases.
  • Sparks stood in for lightning.
  • Amino acids appeared.
  • That supports the idea that organic molecules can form without a cell. It does not show a finished organism.
Answer

Amino acids, supporting a chemical origin of organic molecules, not a finished cell.
