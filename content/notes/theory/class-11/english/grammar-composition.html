English Grammar & Composition Essentials

Class 11English

Notes

  • Tenses: 12 forms built from present/past/future × simple/continuous/perfect/perfect-continuous. Watch time markers: since/for with perfect, yesterday/tomorrow fixing the frame.
  • Articles: 'a/an' before singular countable nouns (an + vowel sound); 'the' for specific or previously mentioned nouns and superlatives.
  • Active & passive voice: object of active becomes subject in passive; verb changes to be + past participle. E.g. 'She writes a letter' → 'A letter is written by her'.
  • Direct & indirect speech: reporting statements shifts tense back one step; questions use if/whether or wh-word order; imperatives use infinitive with told/requested/ordered.
  • Paragraph writing: one controlling idea per paragraph — topic sentence, supporting details (examples, facts), concluding sentence. Essay structure: introduction → body paragraphs → conclusion.
  • Comprehension strategy: skim for gist first, scan for keywords from the question, then read the relevant lines closely; answer in your own words.
  • The 12 Tense System — complete map: (1) Present Simple: habitual actions, general truths — 'She walks to school every day.' (2) Present Continuous: ongoing now, temporary situations — 'She is walking to school.' (3) Present Perfect: past action with present result — 'She has walked to school today.' (4) Present Perfect Continuous: duration up to now — 'She has been walking for an hour.' (5) Past Simple: completed past action — 'She walked to school yesterday.' (6) Past Continuous: ongoing past action — 'She was walking when it rained.' (7) Past Perfect: action before another past action — 'She had walked before the rain started.' (8) Past Perfect Continuous: duration before past point — 'She had been walking for an hour when she fell.' (9) Future Simple: prediction, instant decision — 'She will walk tomorrow.' (10) Future Continuous: ongoing future action — 'She will be walking at 6 AM.' (11) Future Perfect: completed before future point — 'She will have walked 5 km by noon.' (12) Future Perfect Continuous: duration before future point — 'She will have been walking for 2 hours by noon.' Master all 12 forms and their time markers.
  • Time markers — the tense key: Simple present: always, usually, often, never, every day. Present continuous: now, at the moment, currently, today. Present perfect: already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for. Past simple: yesterday, last week, in 2020, ago. Past continuous: while, when, as. Future simple: tomorrow, next week, in 2030, soon. These markers are your biggest help in choosing the correct tense.
  • Reported speech — the backshift rule: When converting direct to indirect speech, shift the tense ONE STEP BACK: present simple → past simple; past simple → past perfect; will → would; can → could. Exceptions: general truths stay in present ('He said the sun rises in the east'), and commands/requests use infinitive ('He told me to go'). Questions: Yes/No questions use 'if/whether'; Wh- questions keep the wh-word with statement word order ('She asked where I was going', NOT 'where was I going').
  • Voice transformation — the 6 rules: (1) Object becomes subject. (2) Main verb becomes past participle. (3) Add correct form of 'be' (is/am/are → was/were; will → would; can → could). (4) 'By' + original subject. (5) Impersonal passive for general opinions: 'It is said that…' / 'People say that…' (6) Not all verbs can be made passive (intransitive verbs like 'happen', 'sleep' cannot). Practice: 'They built the bridge' → 'The bridge was built by them.' 'Someone stole my pen' → 'My pen was stolen.'
  • Conditional sentences — the 4 types: Type 0 (general truth): If + present, present — 'If you heat water, it boils.' Type 1 (real future): If + present, will + base — 'If it rains, I will stay home.' Type 2 (unreal present): If + past, would + base — 'If I were rich, I would travel.' Type 3 (unreal past): If + past perfect, would have + past participle — 'If I had studied, I would have passed.' Inversion without 'if': 'Were I rich…' / 'Had I known…' / 'Should you need help…'
  • Modal verbs — the nuance guide: Ability: can (present), could (past), be able to (all tenses). Permission: may (formal), can (informal), could (polite). Obligation: must (internal), have to (external), should (advice). Prohibition: mustn't (forbidden), don't have to (not necessary). Possibility: may/might/could (varying degrees of certainty). Deduction: must be (near certainty), can't be (impossibility), might be (uncertainty). Master the difference between 'must' (strong obligation) and 'have to' (external obligation), and between 'should' (advice) and 'ought to' (moral duty).
  • Gerund vs. Infinitive — the verb pattern chart: Gerunds (-ing) after: enjoy, avoid, finish, mind, suggest, keep, practice, consider, admit, deny, risk, feel like, can't help. Infinitives (to + base) after: want, hope, decide, plan, agree, refuse, learn, pretend, seem, appear, offer, afford, manage, expect, need, needn't, must. Special cases (change meaning): stop smoking (= quit the habit) vs. stop to smoke (= pause in order to smoke); remember to call (= don't forget) vs. remember calling (= recall the memory). This distinction is heavily tested.
  • Prepositions — the invisible connectors: Time: at (specific time: at 5 PM), on (day/date: on Monday, on May 5th), in (month/year/period: in May, in 2025, in the morning). Place: at (point: at the door), on (surface: on the table), in (enclosure: in the room). Movement: to (direction: go to school), into (entry: walk into the room), onto (surface: jump onto the chair). Other common prepositions: depend ON, interested IN, good AT, afraid OF, proud OF, responsible FOR, different FROM, similar TO. Each verb/adjective pairs with a specific preposition — memorise the pairs.
  • Conjunctions — joining ideas with purpose: Coordinating (FANBOYS): for, and, nor, but, or, yet, so — join equal clauses. Subordinating: because, although, if, when, while, since, unless, until, as, before, after — join dependent to independent. Correlative pairs: both…and, either…or, neither…nor, not only…but also, whether…or. These pairs MUST be used symmetrically: 'Not only did he study, but he also passed.' NOT: 'Not only he studied, but he also passed.'
  • Common grammar errors in exams: (1) Subject-verb agreement: 'The list of items are long' → 'is long' (subject is 'list', not 'items'). (2) Wrong article: 'I saw a elephant' → 'an elephant'. (3) Double negative: 'I don't need nothing' → 'I don't need anything'. (4) Wrong tense sequence: 'If I will go, I will tell you' → 'If I go, I will tell you.' (5) Wrong preposition: 'I am good in English' → 'good at English'. (6) Fragment: 'Because he was late.' → needs a main clause. (7) Comma splice: 'I was tired, I slept early' → fix with conjunction or semicolon.
  • Essay proofreading checklist — the final 3 minutes: After finishing your essay, spend the last 3 minutes checking: (1) Subject-verb agreement in every sentence. (2) Consistent tense throughout (no unexpected shifts). (3) Correct article usage (a/an/the/none). (4) Proper punctuation — especially commas before FANBOYS, after introductory phrases, and around non-restrictive clauses. (5) Spelling of commonly misused words: its/it's, their/there/they're, your/you're, affect/effect, affect/effect, principal/principle. (6) Every sentence has a subject and a verb. (7) No fragments or run-ons.
