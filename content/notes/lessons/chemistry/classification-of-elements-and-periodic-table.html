Classification of Elements & Periodic Table (NEB Class 11 & 12 Chemistry)

Class 11ChemistryLessons

The Periodic Table represents the bedrock of modern inorganic chemistry. In the NEB curriculum, mastery of this unit is essential not only for the direct theoretical, short, and long questions in Grade 11, but also for transition elements, coordination complexes, and heavy metallurgy (Copper, Silver, Gold, Zinc, Mercury, Iron) in Grade 12.

1. Historical Development & Moseley's Modern Periodic Law

Historical Milestones

  • Dobereiner's Triads (1829): Atomic mass of middle element ≈ arithmetic mean of outer two (e.g., Li(7),Na(23),K(39); Ca(40),Sr(88),Ba(137); Cl(35.5),Br(80),I(127)).
  • Newlands' Law of Octaves (1865): Every 8th element repeats properties (valid only up to Calcium, failed after noble gases discovered).
  • Lothar Meyer's Curves (1869): Plotted atomic volume vs atomic mass; alkali metals occupied peaks, halogens ascending slopes, alkaline earth metals descending slopes.
  • Mendeleev's Periodic Law (1869): "Properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic masses."
  • Merits: Systematic study, predicted missing elements (Eka-Boron = Scandium, Eka-Aluminium = Gallium, Eka-Silicon = Germanium).
  • Demerits: Inverted pairs (Ar 39.9 before K 39.1; Co 58.9 before Ni 58.7; Te 127.6 before I 126.9), anomalous position of Hydrogen, isotopes could not be accommodated.

Moseley's Experiment (1913)

Henry Moseley bombarded various elements as anticathodes using cathode rays and analyzed characteristic X-ray emission spectra. ν​=a(Z−b) Where:

  • ν = Frequency of characteristic Kα​ X-ray lines
  • Z = Atomic number (nuclear charge, number of protons)
  • a,b = Constants characteristic of the target/spectral series (b≈1 for K-series)

A plot of ν​ vs Z gives a strictly linear graph, while ν​ vs atomic weight does not.

Modern Periodic Law: "The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers."

2. Division of Elements into s, p, d, and f Blocks

s-Block Elements (Groups 1 & 2)

  • General Configuration: ns1−2 (n=2 to 7).
  • Group 1 (Alkali Metals): Li,Na,K,Rb,Cs,Fr (ns1). Low density, soft, lowest IE in periods.
  • Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals): Be,Mg,Ca,Sr,Ba,Ra (ns2). Harder, +2 fixed oxidation state.
  • Flame Coloration:
  • Li: Crimson Red
  • Na: Golden Yellow
  • K: Lilac / Violet
  • Rb: Red-Violet
  • Cs: Sky Blue
  • Ca: Brick Red
  • Sr: Crimson Red
  • Ba: Apple Green
  • Exception: Be and Mg do NOT show flame coloration because of high effective nuclear charge and high ionization energy holding electrons tightly.
  • Diagonal Relationships: Li resembles Mg, and Be resembles Al due to matching charge-to-radius ratio (polarizing power ϕ).

p-Block Elements (Groups 13 to 18)

  • General Configuration: ns2np1−6 (n=2 to 6).
  • Only block containing Metals, Metalloids, and Non-metals.
  • Inert Pair Effect: As we descend groups 13, 14, and 15, intervening d and f electrons provide poor shielding, holding outer s-electrons more tightly. The lower oxidation state becomes more stable:
  • Group 13: Tl+>Tl3+
  • Group 14: Pb2+>Pb4+ (hence PbO2​ is a strong oxidizing agent)
  • Group 15: Bi3+>Bi5+
  • Anomalous 2nd Period Behavior: B,C,N,O,F lack vacant d-orbitals, restricting maximum covalency to 4. They also form strong pπ−pπ multiple bonds (C=C,C≡C,N≡N,C=O).

d-Block Elements (Transition Metals, Groups 3 to 12)

  • General Configuration: (n−1)d1−10ns1−2 (n=4,5,6).
  • Definition: Elements having incompletely filled d-subshell in atomic ground state or in any common oxidation state.
  • Non-typical / Pseudo-Transition Metals: Zn,Cd,Hg have completely filled d10s2 configurations in ground state and d10 in +2 state. Thus, they lack typical transition properties (variable valency, colored ions, paramagnetism).
  • Key Characteristics:
  • Variable Oxidation States: Minimal energy gap between (n−1)d and ns orbitals. Mn shows +2 to +7; Os and Ru reach +8 (OsO4​).
  • Color of Ions: Caused by d−d transitions when degenerate d-orbitals split in ligand fields. Cu2+ (3d9, blue), Fe3+ (3d5, yellow/brown), Ni2+ (3d8, green). Ions with d0 (Sc3+,Ti4+) or d10 (Zn2+,Cu+,Ag+) are colorless.
  • Magnetic Moment: Paramagnetism due to unpaired electrons. Calculated using the spin-only formula:

μ=n(n+2)​ BM (where n = number of unpaired electrons; BM = Bohr Magnetons).

  • Catalytic Activity: Fe in Haber process, V2​O5​ in Contact process, Ni in Sabatier-Senderens hydrogenation, Pt in Ostwald's ammonia oxidation.

f-Block Elements (Inner Transition Elements)

  • General Configuration: (n−2)f1−14(n−1)d0−1ns2.
  • Lanthanides (4f, 58​Ce to 71​Lu): Common +3 oxidation state.
  • Lanthanide Contraction: Progressive decrease in atomic and ionic radii across the series due to poor shielding by 4f electrons.
  • Consequences:
  1. Nearly identical radii for 4d and 5d transition pairs (Zr≈Hf, Nb≈Ta, Mo≈W).
  2. Decreasing basicity of hydroxides: La(OH)3​ is most basic, Lu(OH)3​ is least basic (covalent character increases via Fajans' rules).
  3. Separation of lanthanides is difficult due to identical chemical behavior.
  • Actinides (5f, 90​Th to 103​Lr): All radioactive, greater range of oxidation states (+3 to +7) because 5f,6d,7s orbitals have comparable energies.

3. Specialized Classes in NEB Syllabus

Coinage Metals (Group 11: Cu, Ag, Au)

  • Outer configuration: (n−1)d10ns1.
  • Transition metals because common ions have incomplete d-subshells (Cu2+=3d9,Au3+=5d8).
  • Highly noble, unreactive with water and non-oxidizing acids (HCl,dil H2​SO4​).
  • Dissolve in oxidizing acids (HNO3​, hot conc. H2​SO4​) and Aqua Regia (3 HCl:1 HNO3​):

Au+HNO3​+4HCl→H[AuCl4​]+NO↑+2H2​O

  • Form analytical complexes: Tollens' reagent [Ag(NH3​)2​]+, Fehling's / Schweizer's reagent [Cu(NH3​)4​]2+.

Volatile Metals (Group 12: Zn, Cd, Hg)

  • Outer configuration: (n−1)d10ns2.
  • Weak metallic bonding because all d and s electrons are paired.
  • Low melting and boiling points; Hg is liquid at room temperature (MP −38.8∘C,BP 356.7∘C).
  • White/colorless diamagnetic salts (ZnSO4​,CdCl2​,HgCl2​).
  • Form amalgams with most metals, except Iron (Fe), Platinum (Pt), and Tungsten (W).

Metalloids (Semimetals: B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po)

  • Intermediate electronegativity (1.8−2.2).
  • Electrical conductivity increases with temperature (semiconductors, negative temperature coefficient of resistance).
  • Doped with Group 13 (p-type) or Group 15 (n-type) to manufacture p-n diodes and microchips.
  • Form amphoteric or weakly acidic oxides (As2​O3​,Sb2​O3​).
