Notes

  • A speech or music signal changes too slowly to be radiated from a practical antenna. Modulation copies that message onto a high-frequency carrier so the antenna can be a reasonable length.
  • In amplitude modulation the carrier frequency stays constant and the amplitude rises and falls with the message. A louder message makes a deeper variation, up to the point where the envelope would cross zero.
  • The modulated wave contains the carrier and two sidebands, one at f_c + f_m and one at f_c − f_m. If the highest message frequency is f_m, the bandwidth occupied is 2 f_m.
  • Frequency modulation keeps the amplitude constant and shifts the carrier frequency with the message. It uses a wider band than AM and is less bothered by amplitude noise.
  • The modulation index in AM is the fractional change in amplitude. An index above 1 means overmodulation: the envelope no longer matches the message and the receiver distorts it.
  • Demodulation at the receiver strips the carrier and returns the message. A diode detector followed by a filter is the simple AM method taught with this page.

Exercises

1.

A 800 kHz carrier is amplitude-modulated by a 5.0 kHz tone. Find the sideband frequencies and the bandwidth.

Steps
  • Upper sideband = 800 + 5.0 = 805 kHz.
  • Lower sideband = 800 − 5.0 = 795 kHz.
  • Bandwidth = 2 × 5.0 kHz = 10 kHz.
  • The carrier remains at 800 kHz.
  • The transmission occupies 795 kHz to 805 kHz.
Answer

795 kHz and 805 kHz, bandwidth 10 kHz.
