Notes

  • Self-induction and mutual induction are the same Faraday effect with different flux. In self-induction the coil links its own changing flux. In mutual induction a second coil links flux from the first.
  • The self emf is −L dI/dt and it opposes the change of that coil's current. The mutual emf is −M dI_1/dt in the second coil.
  • A coil can have both at once. Its total induced emf is −L dI/dt from itself plus −M dI_other/dt from its neighbour.
  • L and M are both in henries. M cannot exceed √(L1 L2), and it reaches that limit only when every flux line of one coil links the other.
  • Opening a switch in a self-inductive circuit produces a large dI/dt and a spark. A mutual pair does the same in the second coil if the first current is interrupted.
  • The transformer, the induction coil and the coupled windings of a radio are mutual induction. The choke that opposes a change in a single circuit is self-induction.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Self-induction only happens in a coil.' ✅ It happens whenever the current within a conductor changes, creating a changing magnetic flux through itself.
  • ❌ 'Mutual induction is the same as self-induction.' ✅ Self-induction is EMF induced by a coil's own current change; mutual induction is EMF induced in a secondary coil due to current changes in a primary coil.
  • ❌ 'Inductance depends on the current flowing.' ✅ Like capacitance, inductance is a geometric property of the coil (turns, core material, shape), not dependent on the magnitude of the current.
  • Do not use M for a single isolated coil.
  • Do not use L for the emf in the neighbouring coil.
  • A steady current gives neither emf.
  • M is not added to L as if they were resistors.

Exercises

1.

A coil with L = 0.40 H has a neighbour with M = 0.10 H. Its own current rises at 2.0 A/s and the neighbour's current is steady. Find the magnitude of the emf in the first coil.

Steps
  • The neighbour's current is steady, so the mutual contribution is zero.
  • The self emf has magnitude L dI/dt.
  • emf = 0.40 × 2.0 = 0.80 V.
  • It opposes the rise of the first coil's current.
  • If the neighbour's current also changed, a second term M dI_2/dt would be added.
Answer

0.80 V, from self-induction only.
