Notes

  • A combination problem begins with a sketch of which resistors share a current and which share a node. Two resistors with no branch between them are in series even if the drawing is bent.
  • Replace each obvious series pair by one resistor, and each obvious parallel pair by one resistor, and redraw. The equivalent of the whole network is the last remaining resistor.
  • The source current is the source voltage divided by that equivalent. Then work backward. A series resistor carries the current of its string. A parallel branch takes V/R of that branch.
  • Power in each resistor is I^2 R using the current that actually flows through it. The powers add to the power leaving the source, V I, if the resistors are the only loads.
  • Three equal resistors can be all in series, all in parallel, or two in parallel with the third in series. Those three arrangements give 3R, R/3 and (3/2)R or a similar result depending on the drawing. The drawing decides.
  • A resistor that is shorted by a wire has zero voltage and carries no current. It is not part of the equivalent. A resistor in an open branch carries no current either.

Summary

Reduce series groups and parallel groups in the order the drawing allows, then find the source current from the final equivalent. Branch currents come from working backward, and the sum of I^2 R should match the source power.

Exercises

1.

A 6.0 Ω resistor is in series with a parallel pair of 6.0 Ω and 3.0 Ω. The source is 12 V. Find the source current.

Steps
  • The parallel pair: 1/R = 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/6 + 2/6 = 1/2, so R_pair = 2.0 Ω.
  • The series total is 6.0 + 2.0 = 8.0 Ω.
  • Source current I = 12 / 8.0 = 1.5 A.
  • That 1.5 A flows through the lone 6.0 Ω resistor.
  • The parallel branches then split this 1.5 A, but the question asks only for the source current.
Answer

1.5 A.
