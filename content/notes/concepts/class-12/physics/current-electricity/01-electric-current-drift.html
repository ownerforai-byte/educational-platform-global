Electric Current and Drift Velocity

Notes

  • Electric current is the rate at which charge passes a point. One ampere is one coulomb per second. In a metal the moving charges are electrons, but the conventional current is drawn in the direction positive charge would move.
  • Free electrons move fast in random directions. An electric field adds a small average velocity along the wire, the drift velocity. The random speed is kilometres per second. The drift speed is often a fraction of a millimetre per second.
  • If n electrons per unit volume, each of charge e, drift at v_d through area A, the current is I = n e A v_d. A larger density, a wider wire or a faster drift means a larger current.
  • The drift speed is e E τ / m, where τ is the average time between collisions. A stronger field or a longer free time means a faster drift. Collisions turn the ordered drift energy into heat.
  • The signal travels along the wire at a speed set by the electromagnetic field, close to the speed of light. That is not the drift speed of the electrons. A lamp comes on at once even though each electron drifts slowly.
  • Current density J is I/A, and J = n e v_d. Resistivity connects J to the field by E = ρ J in an ohmic metal.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Electrons move at the speed of light in a wire.' ✅ Electrons move at a very slow drift velocity (mm/s); the electric field (the signal) travels near the speed of light.
  • ❌ 'Electric current is the flow of electrons only.' ✅ By convention, current is defined as the flow of positive charge. In many cases (like electrolytes), negative charges also flow, but current is still in the opposite direction of electron flow.
  • ❌ 'Drift velocity is the average velocity of all electrons at all times.' ✅ It is the net average velocity of the electrons due to an applied electric field, superimposed on their random thermal motion.
  • ❌ skip the key fact → ✅ ohm's law: v = ir; r = ρl/a (resistivity ρ depends on material)..

Exercises

1.

A wire has n = 1.0 × 10^29 m^−3, area 1.0 × 10^−6 m^2 and current 1.6 A. Take e = 1.6 × 10^−19 C. Find the drift speed.

Steps
  • v_d = I / (n e A).
  • n e A = 1.0 × 10^29 × 1.6 × 10^−19 × 1.0 × 10^−6.
  • n e A = 1.6 × 10^4.
  • v_d = 1.6 / 1.6 × 10^4 = 1.0 × 10^−4 m/s.
  • The electrons drift at 0.10 mm/s, opposite the conventional current.
Answer

1.0 × 10^−4 m/s.
