Electric Field Intensity

Notes

  • The electric field intensity at a point is the force that a small positive test charge would feel there, divided by that test charge: E = F/q. The unit is N/C, or V/m.
  • The test charge must be small enough not to disturb the charges that produce the field. The field is a property of the place, not of the test charge.
  • A point charge Q produces E = k Q / r^2. The direction is outward if Q is positive and inward if Q is negative.
  • Field lines start on positive charge and end on negative charge. They do not cross, because the field at a point has one direction. Closer lines mean a stronger field.
  • The net field of several charges is the vector sum of the individual fields. On the perpendicular bisector of a dipole the fields are not along the same line, so components are required.
  • A uniform field, as between parallel plates, has the same vector everywhere in the central region. The force on a charge there is qE, constant in magnitude and direction.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Electric field intensity is the force on a charge.' ✅ It is the force per unit positive test charge at a point: E = F/q₀.
  • ❌ 'Electric field lines represent the path of a particle.' ✅ They show the direction of the force on a positive charge; they don't necessarily represent the actual trajectory (except for stationary charges).
  • ❌ 'Electric field is zero inside a conductor.' ✅ It is zero inside the material of a static conductor, but not necessarily inside a cavity within it or in non-static conditions.
  • E is not the force. Force is qE.
  • A negative charge feels a force opposite the field.
  • Field lines are not trajectories in every case.
  • You cannot add field magnitudes that point different ways.

Exercises

1.

Find the field 0.20 m from a point charge of 2.0 μC. Use k = 9.0 × 10^9. State the direction if the charge is positive.

Steps
  • Q = 2.0 × 10^−6 C and r^2 = 0.040.
  • E = k Q / r^2 = 9.0 × 10^9 × 2.0 × 10^−6 / 0.040.
  • 9e9 × 2e−6 = 1.8e4.
  • E = 18000 / 0.040 = 4.5 × 10^5 N/C.
  • The direction is away from the positive charge.
Answer

4.5 × 10^5 N/C, outward.
