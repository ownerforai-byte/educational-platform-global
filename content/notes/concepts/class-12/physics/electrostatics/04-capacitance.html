Notes

  • Capacitance is C = Q/V, the charge stored per unit potential difference. The unit is the farad. A 1 F capacitor would store 1 C at 1 V, which is far larger than a classroom capacitor. Microfarads and picofarads are the usual sizes.
  • A parallel-plate capacitor in vacuum has C = ε0 A / d. A bigger plate area stores more charge for the same voltage. A wider gap lowers the capacitance.
  • Filling the gap with a dielectric of constant K multiplies the capacitance by K, if the dielectric fills the space. The molecules of the dielectric polarise and reduce the field for a given free charge.
  • Connecting a capacitor to a battery fixes V. The charge is then Q = C V. Disconnecting the battery fixes Q, and a later change of C changes V.
  • Capacitors in parallel add their capacitances because they share the voltage and the charges add. Capacitors in series add the reciprocals, and the equivalent is smaller than the smallest of them.
  • The energy stored is (1/2) C V^2. A capacitor does not conduct steady current once it is charged. It does pass changing current in an AC circuit.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Capacitors store charge.' ✅ They store energy in an electric field; the net charge is zero (q on one plate, -q on other).
  • ❌ 'Capacitance depends on charge and potential.' ✅ It depends only on physical geometry (area, distance, dielectric) of the capacitor, not on the charge currently stored.
  • ❌ 'Adding a dielectric decreases capacitance.' ✅ A dielectric increases capacitance by reducing the internal electric field (C = εᵣC₀).
  • ❌ skip the key fact → ✅ capacitance c = q/v; parallel-plate c = ε₀a/d (larger with dielectric: c = ε₀εᵣa/d)..

Exercises

1.

A vacuum parallel-plate capacitor has area 0.020 m^2 and separation 1.0 mm. Take ε0 = 8.85 × 10^−12. Find C.

Steps
  • d = 1.0 × 10^−3 m.
  • A/d = 0.020 / 1.0 × 10^−3 = 20.
  • C = ε0 × 20 = 8.85 × 10^−12 × 20.
  • C = 1.77 × 10^−10 F.
  • That is 177 pF. A dielectric would multiply this value by K.
Answer

1.77 × 10^−10 F.
