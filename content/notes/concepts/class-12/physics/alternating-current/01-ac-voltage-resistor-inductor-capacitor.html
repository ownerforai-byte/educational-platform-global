Notes

  • AC through R, L, C: This topic covers the behavior of AC circuits containing Resistors (R), Inductors (L), and Capacitors (C).
  • Understanding the phase relationships between voltage and current in R, L, and C components is essential for analyzing complex AC circuits.
  • Key concepts include impedance, reactance, and the conditions for electrical resonance.
  • An alternating voltage can be written v = V_0 sin ωt. In a pure resistor the current is i = (V_0/R) sin ωt, so voltage and current reach their peaks together and the average power is I_rms^2 R.
  • In a pure inductor the current lags the voltage by 90°. The opposition is the inductive reactance X_L = ωL = 2πfL, and it grows with frequency. A pure inductor stores energy in its magnetic field and returns it, so the average power over a cycle is zero.
  • In a pure capacitor the current leads the voltage by 90°. The capacitive reactance is X_C = 1/(ωC), and it falls as the frequency rises. The average power over a cycle is again zero because energy is stored in the electric field and then returned.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'In an AC circuit, the voltage and current are always in phase' ✅ In a pure resistor, they are in phase. In an inductor, voltage leads current by 90°. In a capacitor, voltage lags current by 90°.
  • ❌ 'Impedance (Z) is the algebraic sum of resistance (R) and reactance (X)' ✅ Impedance is the vector sum: Z = √(R² + (X_L - X_C)²).
  • ❌ 'Resonance occurs when resistance is maximum' ✅ Resonance occurs when inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance (X_L = X_C), which minimizes impedance in a series circuit.
  • Voltage does not lead the current in a resistor.
  • X_L rises with frequency. X_C falls.
  • A pure inductor does not consume average power.
  • Peak and rms differ by √2. Do not mix them in one formula.

Summary

This topic covers the analysis of AC voltage applied to resistors, inductors, and capacitors. It focuses on understanding impedance, phase relationships through phasor diagrams, and the concept of resonance.

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Ensure you understand the difference between pure and practical components.
  • Note 2: Pay attention to the phase difference (φ) and its dependence on frequency.
  • Note 3: Practice numerical problems focusing on Z, φ, and resonance frequency.
  • Note 4: Units for reactance and impedance are Ohms (Ω).
  • Note 5: Resonance frequency problems are common in examinations.

Important Statements

  • An alternating voltage can be written v = V_0 sin ωt. In a pure resistor the current is i = (V_0/R) sin ωt, so voltage and current reach their peaks together and the average power is I_rms^2 R.
  • In a pure inductor the current lags the voltage by 90°. The opposition is the inductive reactance X_L = ωL = 2πfL, and it grows with frequency. A pure inductor stores energy in its magnetic field and returns it, so the average power over a cycle is zero.
  • In a pure capacitor the current leads the voltage by 90°. The capacitive reactance is X_C = 1/(ωC), and it falls as the frequency rises. The average power over a cycle is again zero because energy is stored in the electric field and then returned.

Important Notes

  • Note 1: Always check for series vs parallel combinations.
  • Note 2: Remember that X_L and X_C are 180° out of phase.
  • Note 3: Phasor diagrams are invaluable for solving LCR problems.
  • Note 4: Understand the impact of frequency on the circuit's behavior (inductive vs capacitive).
  • Note 5: Resonance is a frequency-selective property.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Use the mnemonic 'ELI' (Voltage leads Current in Inductor) and 'ICE' (Current leads Voltage in Capacitor).
  • Trick 2: At resonance, X_L cancels X_C; Z = R.
  • Trick 3: If frequency increases, X_L increases and X_C decreases.
  • Trick 4: The power factor is 1 at resonance.
  • Trick 5: Use phasor diagrams to quickly determine the phase of voltage relative to current.

Exam Notes

  • Be precise with units and calculations in reactance formulas.
  • Understand the definition and importance of Q-factor.

Exercises

1.

A 50 Hz supply is connected to a 0.10 H pure inductor. Find X_L. Take π = 3.14.

Steps
  • X_L = 2πfL.
  • 2πf = 2 × 3.14 × 50 = 314.
  • X_L = 314 × 0.10 = 31.4 Ω.
  • The current lags the voltage by 90°.
  • The average power in this pure inductor is zero.
Answer

31.4 Ω, with current lagging the voltage by 90°.
