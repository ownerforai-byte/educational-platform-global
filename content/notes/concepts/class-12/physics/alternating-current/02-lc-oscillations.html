Notes

  • LC Oscillations: This topic covers the oscillatory exchange of energy between a capacitor (storing electric field energy) and an inductor (storing magnetic field energy).
  • In an ideal LC circuit, electrical oscillations occur at a natural frequency determined by the values of L and C.
  • This is an analogy to a mechanical simple harmonic oscillator (mass-spring system).
  • If a charged capacitor is connected to a pure inductor, the capacitor discharges through the coil, the current builds a magnetic field, and that field then pushes charge back onto the capacitor with the opposite sign.
  • The charge on the capacitor is of the form Q = Q_0 cos ωt when it starts from rest at maximum charge. The current is the derivative and is maximum when the charge is zero.
  • The angular frequency of this exchange is ω = 1/√(LC). The frequency in hertz is f = 1/(2π√(LC)). It does not depend on the starting charge.

Confusion

  • ❌ Thinking LC circuits lose energy immediately without resistance ✅ An ideal LC circuit (zero resistance) oscillates indefinitely; only practical circuits lose energy due to resistance (damped oscillations).
  • ❌ Current is maximum when charge on capacitor is maximum ✅ When charge on the capacitor is maximum, the potential energy is maximum and the current is zero. When the current is maximum, the magnetic energy is maximum and charge is zero (energy transfer).
  • ❌ Misinterpreting the oscillation frequency ✅ The angular frequency is ω = 1/√(LC), and the frequency is f = 1 / (2π√(LC)).
  • The frequency does not depend on the initial charge.
  • Maximum current and maximum charge do not happen together.
  • Energy is not lost in the ideal LC circuit.
  • ω here is 1/√(LC), not √(LC).

Summary

LC oscillations involve the continuous exchange of energy between a capacitor's electric field and an inductor's magnetic field. This process is analogous to mechanical harmonic motion and produces oscillations at a natural frequency determined by L and C.

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Always check if the circuit is considered 'ideal' (no resistance).
  • Note 2: Remember the relationship between q, I, and energy at different stages.
  • Note 3: The total energy is constant in the ideal case.
  • Note 4: Frequency (f) is in Hz, angular frequency (ω) is in rad/s.
  • Note 5: Understand why real circuits have damped oscillations.

Exercises

1.

L = 2.0 mH and C = 8.0 μF. Find the oscillation frequency. Take π = 3.14.

Steps
  • Convert: L = 2.0 × 10^−3 H and C = 8.0 × 10^−6 F.
  • LC = 1.6 × 10^−8, so √(LC) = 1.26 × 10^−4.
  • 2π√(LC) = 6.28 × 1.26 × 10^−4 = 7.94 × 10^−4.
  • f = 1 / 7.94 × 10^−4 = 1.26 × 10^3 Hz.
  • The frequency is 1.26 kHz.
Answer

1.26 kHz.
