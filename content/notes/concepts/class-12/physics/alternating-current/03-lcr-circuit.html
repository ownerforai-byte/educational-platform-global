Notes

  • LCR Series Circuit: An AC circuit containing a resistor (R), inductor (L), and capacitor (C) connected in series.
  • The behavior is determined by the combination of resistance and reactance (inductive and capacitive).
  • It exhibits resonance when the net reactance is zero.
  • A series LCR circuit has one current through R, L and C. The resistor voltage is in phase with the current, the inductor voltage leads by 90°, and the capacitor voltage lags by 90°.
  • The two reactance voltages are opposite, so the net reactive voltage has size I|X_L − X_C|. With the resistor voltage IR, Pythagoras gives the supply voltage V = I Z, where Z = √(R^2 + (X_L − X_C)^2).
  • The phase angle satisfies tanφ = (X_L − X_C)/R. If X_L is larger, the current lags the supply voltage. If X_C is larger, the current leads.
  • At resonance X_L = X_C, Z falls to R, and the current is V/R, the largest series current for that supply. The frequency is f_0 = 1/(2π√(LC)), the same expression as the LC oscillation.

Confusion

  • ❌ Believing resonance occurs at all frequencies ✅ Resonance occurs only at a specific frequency (f_r = 1 / (2π√LC)) where inductive reactance equals capacitive reactance (X_L = X_C).
  • ❌ Misinterpreting phase angle as constant ✅ The phase angle φ (where tan φ = (X_L - X_C) / R) depends on the frequency of the AC source; it is zero at resonance.
  • ❌ Assuming voltage across L and C is zero at resonance ✅ While the net voltage across the L-C combination is zero (because they are 180° out of phase), individual voltages across L and C can be non-zero and large.
  • Z is not R + X_L + X_C.
  • Resonance is not maximum resistance.
  • A lagging current means the circuit is inductive, not capacitive.
  • The large voltages on L and C are not added arithmetically to get the supply.

Universal Facts

  • Series LCR circuits are widely used in frequency tuning applications (e.g., radio receivers).
  • At resonance, the impedance is purely resistive (Z = R).
  • The phase relationship between voltage and current changes from capacitive (current leads) to inductive (voltage leads) as frequency passes resonance.

Formulas

  • Impedance Z = √(R² + (X_L - X_C)²)
  • Resonance frequency f_r = 1 / (2π√LC)
  • Phase angle φ = tan⁻¹((X_L - X_C) / R)
  • Current I = V / Z
  • Quality Factor Q = (1/R)√(L/C)

Summary

A series LCR circuit combines resistance, inductance, and capacitance. It demonstrates resonance when inductive and capacitive reactances cancel, leading to minimum impedance and maximum current.

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Resonance occurs when X_L = X_C.
  • Note 2: At resonance, current is in phase with voltage (φ=0).
  • Note 3: Impedance is minimum and equal to R at resonance.
  • Note 4: X_L increases with frequency; X_C decreases with frequency.
  • Note 5: The Q-factor measures the sharpness of the resonance peak.

Important Statements

  • A series LCR circuit has one current through R, L and C. The resistor voltage is in phase with the current, the inductor voltage leads by 90°, and the capacitor voltage lags by 90°.
  • The two reactance voltages are opposite, so the net reactive voltage has size I|X_L − X_C|. With the resistor voltage IR, Pythagoras gives the supply voltage V = I Z, where Z = √(R^2 + (X_L − X_C)^2).
  • The phase angle satisfies tanφ = (X_L − X_C)/R. If X_L is larger, the current lags the supply voltage. If X_C is larger, the current leads.

Important Notes

  • Note 1: Always sketch the phasor diagram.
  • Note 2: Understand the phase relationship at frequencies below and above resonance.
  • Note 3: Resonance is used for selectivity in communication systems.
  • Note 4: Practice calculations for impedance at frequencies other than resonance.
  • Note 5: Pay close attention to the formula for Q-factor.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: f_r = 1 / (2π√LC).
  • Trick 2: At resonance, Z = R.
  • Trick 3: If X_L > X_C, circuit is inductive (voltage leads).
  • Trick 4: If X_C > X_L, circuit is capacitive (current leads).
  • Trick 5: Q = (1/R)√(L/C).

Exercises

1.

R = 3.0 Ω, X_L = 8.0 Ω and X_C = 4.0 Ω in series. Find Z and say whether the current lags or leads.

Steps
  • X_L − X_C = 8.0 − 4.0 = 4.0 Ω.
  • Z = √(3.0^2 + 4.0^2) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5.0 Ω.
  • tanφ = 4/3, so φ is about 53°.
  • X_L is larger than X_C, so the circuit is inductive.
  • The current lags the supply voltage.
Answer

Z = 5.0 Ω, and the current lags the voltage.
