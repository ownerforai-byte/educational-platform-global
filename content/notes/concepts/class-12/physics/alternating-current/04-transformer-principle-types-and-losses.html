Notes

  • A transformer has a primary coil and a secondary coil on an iron core. Alternating current in the primary changes the flux, and that changing flux induces the secondary emf. Direct current does not keep the flux changing, so a transformer does not pass steady DC.
  • The voltage ratio equals the turns ratio when leakage is ignored. More secondary turns raise the voltage and, in the ideal case, lower the current so that input power equals output power.
  • A step-up transformer raises voltage. A step-down transformer lowers it. The names refer to voltage, not to power and not to current.
  • Copper loss is the heating I squared R in both windings. It grows with the load current. Core loss is eddy-current heating plus hysteresis, and it is present whenever the flux is alternating, even if the secondary is open.
  • Laminating the core and using soft iron cut those core losses. The efficiency is useful output power divided by input power. It is less than 1 once either loss is present.
  • A small transformer on no load still draws a little primary current to supply the core loss and the magnetising flux. That no-load current is not the load current.

Key Points

  • Changing flux links the two coils.
  • Step-up and step-down refer to voltage.
  • Copper loss is I squared R in the windings.
  • Core loss is eddy current plus hysteresis.

Summary

A transformer passes energy by changing flux, with the voltage ratio equal to the turns ratio. Copper loss heats the windings in proportion to the square of the current, and core loss heats the iron whenever the flux alternates. Efficiency is output power divided by input power.

Exercises

1.

A transformer takes 200 W from the mains and delivers 180 W to a lamp. Find the efficiency and the wasted power.

Steps
  • Useful output = 180 W.
  • Input = 200 W.
  • Wasted power = 200 − 180 = 20 W.
  • Efficiency = 180/200 = 0.90.
  • As a percentage, the efficiency is 90%.
Answer

90%, with 20 W wasted.
