Notes

  • The Biot–Savart law gives the small field of a current element: it is proportional to I dl sinθ / r^2, and the direction is perpendicular to the element and to the line drawn to the point.
  • Integrating around a long straight wire gives B = μ0 I /(2π r). The field lines are circles around the wire, given by the right-hand grip. The field falls as 1/r.
  • At the centre of a circular loop of one turn, B = μ0 I /(2 R), along the axis. N turns wound together multiply the field by N.
  • A long solenoid is many circular turns in a row. Inside, the field is μ0 n I, nearly uniform, with n turns per metre. Outside an ideal long solenoid the field is negligible.
  • μ0 is 4π × 10^−7. The combination μ0 / 2π, used for the straight wire, is 2 × 10^−7. Using μ0 where the formula already contains 2π, or the other way round, is the usual slip.
  • Ampere's law reaches the wire and the solenoid faster when the symmetry is obvious. Biot–Savart is the law you start from when you have to build the field from elements.

Key Points

  • Each current element contributes.
  • A long wire gives circles of field.
  • A loop's centre field is along the axis.
  • A solenoid is the stack of loops.

Summary

Biot–Savart builds magnetic field from current elements. A long wire gives μ0 I /(2π r) in circles, a coil gives μ0 N I /(2R) at its centre, and a long solenoid gives μ0 n I inside. The right-hand grip fixes the direction.

Exercises

1.

A coil of 50 turns and radius 5.0 cm carries 2.0 A. Find B at the centre. Use μ0 = 4π × 10^−7.

Steps
  • R = 0.050 m, so 2 R = 0.10 m.
  • B = μ0 N I / (2 R).
  • N I = 50 × 2.0 = 100.
  • μ0 × 100 = 1.26 × 10^−4.
  • B = 1.26 × 10^−4 / 0.10 = 1.26 × 10^−3 T, along the axis.
Answer

1.26 × 10^−3 T, along the axis.
