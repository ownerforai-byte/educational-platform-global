Notes

  • The Lorentz force is the total electromagnetic force on a charge, q E plus the magnetic force q v B. The electric part can speed the charge up. The magnetic part only turns it.
  • If E and B are perpendicular to each other and to the velocity, the two forces can point in opposite directions. They cancel when q E = q v B, so v = E/B.
  • A velocity selector is a pair of crossed fields. Charges of the chosen speed go straight through. Faster charges are bent one way and slower charges the other way. The charge and the mass cancel, so the selected speed does not depend on them.
  • After the selector, a magnetic field alone bends the beam into a circle whose radius is m v / (q B). Measuring that radius at the known speed gives the mass-to-charge ratio.
  • A cyclotron uses the magnetic circle and an electric kick twice per revolution to increase the speed. The frequency of the orbit for a non-relativistic particle is q B / (2 π m), independent of the speed.
  • At speeds near the speed of light the mass effectively increases and the cyclotron frequency falls. The simple selector and the simple radius formula are for speeds well below that.

Summary

The Lorentz force is the electric force plus the magnetic force. In crossed fields the forces cancel at v = E/B, which is the speed a velocity selector passes. A later magnetic field bends that beam into a circle whose radius gives m/q.

Steps
  • The forces balance when q E = q v B.
  • v = E/B.
