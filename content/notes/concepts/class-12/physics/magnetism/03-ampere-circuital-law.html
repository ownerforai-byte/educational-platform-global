Notes

  • Ampere's circuital law says that the line integral of B around a closed path equals μ0 times the current enclosed by that path. It is useful when symmetry makes B constant on the path and parallel to it.
  • For a long straight wire, a circular path of radius r gives B times 2π r = μ0 I, so B = μ0 I /(2π r), the same answer as Biot–Savart.
  • For a long solenoid, a rectangular path with one side inside gives B = μ0 n I, where n is the number of turns per unit length. The field outside the ideal solenoid is zero, so that side of the rectangle contributes nothing.
  • A toroid, a solenoid bent into a ring, has B = μ0 N I /(2π r) inside the core and a very small field outside.
  • Currents outside the path are not enclosed, so they do not appear on the right-hand side. They may still shape the field if the symmetry is poor, which is why the law is then hard to use.
  • The law as stated here is for steady currents in vacuum. A displacement current is the later correction for changing electric fields.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Ampere's law works for any current distribution.' ✅ It is most useful for highly symmetric situations (infinite wire, solenoid, toroid); for arbitrary shapes, Biot-Savart is needed.
  • ❌ 'The enclosed current must be at the center of the Amperian loop.' ✅ The current only needs to pass through the loop; its position inside the loop does not matter for the line integral.
  • ❌ 'Magnetic field outside a solenoid is always zero.' ✅ For an ideal infinite solenoid it is zero, but a real finite solenoid has a weak external field.
  • n is turns per metre, not the total turns, in the solenoid formula.
  • Current outside the Amperian loop is not enclosed.
  • The outside of an ideal long solenoid is not a place of strong field.
  • The law does not replace Biot–Savart when there is no symmetry.

Exercises

1.

A long solenoid has 1000 turns per metre and carries 2.0 A. Find B inside. Use μ0 = 4π × 10^−7 and π = 3.14.

Steps
  • B = μ0 n I.
  • μ0 = 1.256 × 10^−6.
  • n I = 1000 × 2.0 = 2000.
  • B = 1.256 × 10^−6 × 2000 = 2.51 × 10^−3 T.
  • The field outside the ideal solenoid is zero.
Answer

2.51 × 10^−3 T.
