Notes

  • Mutual induction is the emf in one circuit caused by a changing current in a nearby circuit. The changing current changes the flux that links the second coil.
  • The mutual inductance M is defined so that the flux linkage in the secondary is M times the primary current. The induced emf is −M dI/dt.
  • M is the same either way: a current changing in coil 2 induces in coil 1 with the same M. That symmetry is M_12 = M_21.
  • M is large when the coils share flux, as on a common iron core, and small when they are far apart or at right angles so that little flux links across.
  • A transformer is mutual induction used continuously with alternating current. The secondary emf exists only while the primary current is changing.
  • The henry is again the unit. M = 1 H if 1 A/s in the primary induces 1 V in the secondary.

Key Points

  • One coil's changing current induces emf in a second coil.
  • M is mutual inductance.
  • It is symmetric.
  • A transformer uses it.

Summary

A changing current in one coil induces an emf −M dI/dt in the other. M is the same in both directions and is large when the coils share flux. A steady primary current induces nothing, which is why a transformer needs alternating current.

Exercises

1.

Two coils have M = 0.20 H. The primary current rises at 10 A/s. Find the magnitude of the secondary emf.

Steps
  • Magnitude = M |dI/dt|.
  • emf = 0.20 × 10 = 2.0 V.
  • The emf lasts only while the primary current is changing.
  • If the rate became zero, the secondary emf would become zero.
  • Reversing which coil is primary would use the same M.
Answer

2.0 V.
