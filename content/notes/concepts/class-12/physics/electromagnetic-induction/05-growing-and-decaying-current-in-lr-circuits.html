Notes

  • When a battery is connected to a resistor and an inductor in series, the current starts at zero and rises toward I_0 = V/R. The self-induced emf opposes the rise, so the rise is gradual.
  • The growth equation is I = I_0 (1 − e^(−Rt/L)). At t = 0, I = 0. After a long time the exponential vanishes and I = I_0.
  • If the battery is then removed and the coil is left connected across the resistor, the current decays as I = I_0 e^(−Rt/L). It starts at I_0 and falls toward zero.
  • The time constant τ = L/R is the time for the growth to reach about 63% of I_0, because 1 − e^(−1) is about 0.63. In decay, one time constant leaves about 37% of the current.
  • A large L or a small R makes the change slow. The inductor behaves as an open circuit at the first instant and as a wire after a long time, for a steady battery.
  • Energy (1/2) L I^2 builds during growth and returns as heat in the resistor during decay. The spark when a large inductor is switched is that energy.

Summary

An LR current grows as I_0 (1 − e^(−Rt/L)) toward V/R and decays as I_0 e^(−Rt/L) toward zero. The time constant is L/R. The inductor stops the current from jumping, and the stored energy returns as heat during decay.

Exercises

1.

V = 10 V, R = 5.0 Ω and L = 1.0 H. Find I_0, the time constant, and the current at t = τ during growth. Use e^(−1) = 0.37.

Steps
  • I_0 = V/R = 10/5.0 = 2.0 A.
  • τ = L/R = 1.0/5.0 = 0.20 s.
  • I(τ) = I_0 (1 − e^(−1)) = 2.0 × (1 − 0.37).
  • I(τ) = 2.0 × 0.63 = 1.26 A.
  • The current is still short of 2.0 A after one time constant.
Answer

I_0 = 2.0 A, τ = 0.20 s, I(τ) = 1.26 A.
