Notes

  • In the photoelectric effect, light falling on a metal can eject electrons. The effect starts at once, and it happens only if the frequency is above a threshold for that metal.
  • Einstein's explanation is that light arrives as photons of energy h f. A photon gives its energy to one electron. Part of that energy, the work function φ, is spent escaping the metal. The rest is kinetic energy.
  • K_max = h f − φ. Below the threshold, h f is less than φ and no electron escapes, however bright the light. Brightness is the number of photons, not the energy of each one.
  • A brighter beam above threshold ejects more electrons, so the current rises. It does not raise the maximum kinetic energy. That energy depends on frequency.
  • A stopping potential V_s is the retarding voltage that just cuts the current to zero. Then e V_s = K_max. Measuring V_s measures the fastest electrons.
  • A useful number is h c = 1240 eV nm, so a photon's energy in electronvolts is 1240 divided by the wavelength in nanometres.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Higher intensity light always causes photoelectric emission' ✅ Emission only occurs if the light frequency is above the threshold frequency, regardless of intensity.
  • ❌ 'Photoelectric current increases with frequency' ✅ Current (number of electrons) increases with intensity (number of photons), not frequency.
  • ❌ 'Stopping potential depends on light intensity' ✅ Stopping potential depends only on the maximum kinetic energy of emitted electrons, which is determined by the light frequency.
  • ❌ skip the key fact → ✅ photoelectric effect: hν = φ + k_max; k_max = ev_s; stopping voltage v_s = (h/e)(ν − ν₀)..

Exercises

1.

A metal has work function 2.0 eV. Light of wavelength 400 nm falls on it. Use E = 1240/λ with λ in nm. Find the maximum kinetic energy.

Steps
  • Photon energy = 1240 / 400 = 3.10 eV.
  • K_max = 3.10 − 2.0 = 1.10 eV.
  • The frequency is above threshold because the photon energy exceeds the work function.
  • A brighter beam of the same wavelength would eject more electrons with the same maximum energy.
  • The stopping potential would be 1.10 V.
Answer

1.10 eV.
