Notes

  • A logic gate takes binary inputs, called 1 and 0, and gives a binary output. In a circuit, 1 is a higher voltage and 0 is a lower voltage. The exact voltages depend on the family of chips.
  • The AND gate outputs 1 only when every input is 1. The OR gate outputs 1 when at least one input is 1. The NOT gate has one input and outputs the opposite.
  • NAND is AND followed by NOT. It outputs 0 only when every input is 1. NOR is OR followed by NOT. It outputs 1 only when every input is 0.
  • NAND alone can build AND, OR and NOT. NOR alone can do the same. They are called universal gates.
  • A truth table lists every input combination. Two inputs give four rows: 00, 01, 10 and 11. Writing the output for each row is the whole definition of the gate.
  • Combinations make decisions: a NAND arrangement can add bits or store a bit in a latch. The exam usually stops at the truth table and a simple combination.

Confusion

  • ❌ Confusing NAND/NOR as universal gates ✅ NAND and NOR are universal gates because any logic gate (AND, OR, NOT) can be constructed solely using them.
  • ❌ Thinking logic levels are continuous ✅ Logic gates operate on discrete digital voltage levels (typically 0 or 1), not continuous analog signals.
  • ❌ Misinterpreting Truth Tables for complex gates ✅ Truth tables must be evaluated step-by-step for complex gate combinations, not by guessing the output based on a single input.
  • OR is not exclusive. It is also 1 when both inputs are 1.
  • NAND is not 'both zero'. It is the opposite of AND.
  • NOT has one input.
  • 1 is a voltage band, not necessarily 1 volt.
