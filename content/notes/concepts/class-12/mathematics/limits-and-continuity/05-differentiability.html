Notes

  • Differentiability at a point: f is differentiable at x=a if f'(a) exists, i.e., lim(h→0) [f(a+h)-f(a)]/h exists.
  • Differentiable implies continuous: If f is differentiable at a, then f is continuous at a.
  • Continuous does not imply differentiable: |x| is continuous at x=0 but not differentiable.
  • Smooth functions: Differentiable everywhere (e.g., polynomials, sin x, cos x, eˣ).
  • Corner/cusp points: Non-differentiable points where left derivative ≠ right derivative.
  • Vertical tangent: Point where derivative approaches infinity (not differentiable).
  • Fact. Indeterminate forms 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞ → L'Hôpital: lim f/g = lim f'/g' (when applicable).
  • Fact. Standard limits: lim_{x→0} sin x / x = 1; lim_{x→0} (1 + x)^{1/x} = e; lim_{x→∞} (1 + a/x)^x = e^a.
  • Fact. Continuity: f continuous at c iff lim_{x→c}f(x) = f(c). Types of discontinuity: removable, jump, infinite.

Confusion

  • ❌ Assuming continuous functions are always differentiable ✅ |x| at x=0 is continuous but has a corner (not differentiable).
  • ❌ Differentiable implies the function is smooth ✅ Differentiable means derivative exists at that point; corners still exist elsewhere.
