  • Air pollution is the addition of harmful substances to the atmosphere — gases (CO, SO2, NOx), particulates (PM2.5, PM10) and secondary products (photochemical smog, acid rain). Sources are combustion, industry and vehicles; effects range from respiratory disease to acid rain and global climate change. Control combines technology (filters, converters), fuel policy and law.

  • Worked example — why lead killed catalytic converters. A catalytic converter uses platinum-group catalysts to finish the combustion of CO and NOx. Lead in old petrol coats the catalyst surface in weeks and destroys it — which is why unleaded petrol was a precondition for converter-based emission norms everywhere. One pollutant (lead) had to be removed to control three others.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Pollutant table: CO (haemoglobin affinity → death), SO2/NOx (acid rain), particulates (respiratory disease), CFCs (ozone), lead (nervous system) — one effect each.
  • Trick 2: Acid rain chemistry: SO2 + OH → H2SO4; NOx → HNO3, pH < 5.6 — the two acid names earn the mark.
  • Trick 3: Control devices: electrostatic precipitator (99% particulates), scrubber (SO2 with lime spray), catalytic converter (CO/NOx/unburnt HC) — three devices, three mechanisms.
  • Trick 4: Delhi-style vehicle control: Bharat/CNG norms, catalytic converters, lead-free petrol — the policy line.
