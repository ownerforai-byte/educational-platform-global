  • The greenhouse effect is natural and life-giving: CO2, CH4, water vapour and other gases let sunlight in but slow the infrared escape, keeping Earth ~33°C warmer than it would be. The problem is the enhanced effect: fossil fuel burning and deforestation have raised CO2 from ~280 to over 420 ppm, driving global warming and with it climate change — shifting rainfall, melting ice, rising seas.

  • Worked example — why methane cuts pay fast. CO2 persists for centuries, so its warming is slow to reverse. Methane's lifetime is only ~12 years. Cutting methane leaks (landfills, gas systems, livestock feed additives) therefore slows near-term warming within a decade — the fastest lever available while the slow CO2 transition proceeds. Two gases, two time-scales, one strategy.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Mechanism: greenhouse gases (CO2, CH4, N2O, CFCs) transmit incoming short-wave sunlight but absorb outgoing long-wave infrared → warming — the one-way valve picture.
  • Trick 2: Gas potency: CH4 ~25× CO2 (20-yr), CFCs thousands× — quote one multiplier; CO2 dominates by volume.
  • Trick 3: Consequences: sea-level rise (thermal expansion + ice melt), extreme weather, coral bleaching, disease spread — four consequences.
  • Trick 4: Controls: Kyoto Protocol/Paris Agreement, afforestation, renewable energy, energy efficiency — the policy plus practice list.
