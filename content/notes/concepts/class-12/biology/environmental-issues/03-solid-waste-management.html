Notes

  • Solid waste is the solid material a household, shop, farm, hospital or factory throws away. Managing it means collecting it, keeping the dangerous fractions apart, and only then burying, burning or recycling what is left. A roadside heap is not management.
  • The useful split is by what the waste is. Wet organic waste, such as food scraps, can rot. Dry recyclable waste includes paper, glass, metal and many plastics. Biomedical waste from clinics carries infection. Electronic waste carries lead, mercury and cadmium. Industrial hazardous waste may be corrosive or toxic. Mixing these in one bag makes every later step worse.
  • An open dump breeds flies and rats, blows plastic into drains, and leaks a dirty liquid called leachate into soil and groundwater. Buried organic waste without air control also makes methane, which is flammable and a strong greenhouse gas. The smell is the least of the damage.
  • A sanitary landfill is a different structure. The base is lined so leachate can be collected rather than soaked away. Waste is compacted and covered. Gas is vented or burned. It still uses land, and it still needs watching, but it is not a hole in the ground.
  • The order that cuts the problem is reduce, then reuse, then recycle, and only then recover energy or dispose. Segregation at the house is what makes composting, recycling and safe medical disposal possible. A mixed truckload cannot be unsorted cheaply at the dump.
  • Hospitals must not send dressings, needles and cultures out with kitchen waste. Those items are incinerated at high temperature or sterilised. Electronic goods should go to a recovery workshop that captures the metals. Burning phones and cables in the open releases the metals and the plastic fumes together.

Confusion

  • A sanitary landfill is not any place people dump waste. It is lined, covered and drained.
  • All plastic is not compost. Compost is for wet organic waste. Plastic is a recycling or disposal problem.
  • Incineration is not a backyard fire. A proper incinerator holds a high temperature and treats the smoke.
  • Methane from a dump is a real product of rotting waste, not only a carbon-dioxide story.

Key Points

  • Segregate at source: wet organic, recyclable, biomedical, electronic, hazardous.
  • Open dumping produces leachate, methane, flies and blocked drains.
  • A sanitary landfill is lined and covered, and its leachate is collected.
  • Order of preference: reduce, reuse, recycle, then recover or dispose.
  • E-waste and hospital waste do not belong in the household pile.

Important Statements

  • Mixed waste defeats every treatment that follows.
  • Leachate is the liquid that carries pollutants out of a dump into water.
  • Methane from anaerobic rotting is both a fire risk and a greenhouse gas.

Examples

  • Food scraps kept separate can be composted. The same scraps in a plastic bag at a dump become leachate and methane.
  • A clinic that bags needles with ordinary rubbish puts waste workers at risk of infection.
  • A lined city landfill with gas collection is a sanitary landfill. A riverbank heap is not.

Universal Facts

  • Organic waste under air-free conditions generates methane.
  • Lead, mercury and cadmium are typical hazardous metals in electronic waste.

Formulas

  • Preference: reduce → reuse → recycle → energy recovery → disposal.
  • Open dump: organic waste + no control → leachate + methane + vectors.

Practice

  • List five categories of solid waste and one safe route for each.
  • Contrast an open dump with a sanitary landfill in three points.
  • Explain why segregation has to happen in the home.

Practice Questions

  • What is leachate, and where does it go from an unlined dump?
  • Why is a phone not suitable for a compost pit?
  • Why does a landfill produce methane?

Exam Notes

  • Define solid waste, then give categories, then harms, then the sanitary landfill and the 3Rs. That order matches the usual long question.
  • Say 'sanitary landfill' only when you have mentioned the liner and the cover.

Exam Short Tricks

  • 3Rs first. Dump last.
  • Hospital waste and e-waste are separate streams.

Special Notes

  • Do not claim every city already runs a perfect landfill. Describe the standard the method requires.
  • Biomedical waste is infectious. Hazardous industrial waste is chemical. They are not the same bin.

Important Notes

  • Covering waste daily is part of what makes a landfill sanitary. It limits flies and wind-blown litter.
  • Recycling works only on waste that was never soaked in food.

Exercises

1.

A neighbourhood burns mixed waste, including wires and food, every evening. What goes wrong, in steps?

Steps
  • Food waste should have been composted, not burned.
  • Wires and circuit boards release heavy metals and plastic fumes in an open fire.
  • The ash and the smoke still leave the site. Burning did not make the hazard disappear.
  • Segregation would have sent food to compost and electronics to a recovery workshop.
Answer

Open burning of mixed waste spreads metals and fumes and wastes the organic fraction that could have been composted.

Summary

Solid waste management starts by keeping organic, recyclable, biomedical and electronic wastes apart. Open dumps leak leachate, make methane and feed disease vectors. A sanitary landfill is lined and covered. The better order is to reduce, reuse and recycle before anything is burned or buried, and hospital waste and e-waste stay on their own routes.
