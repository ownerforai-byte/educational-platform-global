  • Water pollution is any change in water quality that harms life or usefulness. The big concepts: BOD (biochemical oxygen demand — the oxygen microbes need to decompose the waste; high BOD = dirty water), eutrophication (nutrient enrichment → algal bloom → oxygen crash), and biomagnification (toxics like DDT and mercury climbing the food chain). Control runs through sewage treatment and watershed management.

  • Worked example — reading a BOD test. Sample A: BOD 5 mg/L, dissolved oxygen 8 mg/L. Sample B: BOD 300 mg/L, dissolved oxygen 2 mg/L. Sample B carries heavy organic pollution — microbes will strip the remaining oxygen, fish will suffocate, and anaerobic decay will produce foul H2S. Sample A is healthy water. BOD is the single most useful number in water quality: it measures pollution by measuring the microbes' own appetite.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Eutrophication chain: nutrient (N, P) enrichment → algal bloom → O2 depletion → fish death → anaerobic decay (foul smell) — the five-step causal chain is the answer.
  • Trick 2: BOD: oxygen needed by microbes to decompose organic matter — higher BOD = more pollution; clean water BOD < 5 ppm; sewage treatment uses BOD reduction as its goal.
  • Trick 3: Biomagnification: DDT 0.003 ppb water → 25 ppm fish-eating birds (10 million×) — the classic magnification numerical; also mercury (Minamata).
  • Trick 4: Sewage treatment: primary (physical, settling) → secondary (biological, activated sludge) → tertiary (chemical); Flocs = masses of bacteria in aeration tanks.
