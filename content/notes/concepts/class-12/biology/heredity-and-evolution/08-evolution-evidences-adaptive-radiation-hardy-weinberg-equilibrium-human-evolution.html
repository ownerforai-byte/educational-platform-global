  • Darwin's 1859 theory — descent with modification by natural selection — is supported by evidence from every branch of biology. Population genetics later gave it a mathematical spine: the Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium defines the genetic baseline of a population that is NOT evolving, so any deviation names the evolutionary force at work. Adaptive radiation (Darwin's finches) shows one ancestor diversifying into many niches, and the fossil record of human ancestors shows our own radiation.

  • Worked example — Hardy–Weinberg. In a population of 1,000 people, 160 cannot roll the tongue (recessive, tt). q² = 160/1000 = 0.16, so q = 0.4 and p = 0.6. Carriers 2pq = 2 × 0.6 × 0.4 = 0.48 → 480 carriers, TT = p² = 360. Check: 360 + 480 + 160 = 1,000. If next generation shows the same frequencies, the population is in equilibrium; if not, name the force that moved them.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Evidences list: palaeontology (fossils), comparative anatomy (homologous/analogous organs), embryology (Haeckel), molecular (cytochrome c), biogeography — five with one line each.
  • Trick 2: Hardy–Weinberg: p² + 2pq + q² = 1 — p² = AA, 2pq = Aa, q² = aa; disturbed by mutation, migration, drift, selection, non-random mating — the five forces.
  • Trick 3: Adaptive radiation: Darwin's finches (beaks), Australian marsupials — one ancestor → many niches; convergent evolution is the analogous-organ flip side.
  • Trick 4: Human evolution order: Australopithecus → Homo habilis → H. erectus → Neanderthal → H. sapiens — brain size increases, jaw decreases.
