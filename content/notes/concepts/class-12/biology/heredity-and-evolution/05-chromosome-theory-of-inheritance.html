    Fill order per concept: definition → structure → process → significance (§3.2). Step-by-step numbered points, Pioneer style.

  • Mendel's factors were invisible abstractions in 1866. Forty years later, two young researchers — Walter Sutton (USA) and Theodor Boveri (Germany), working independently in 1902–1903 — pointed out that Mendel's factors behave exactly like chromosomes do during meiosis. This is the chromosome theory of inheritance (Sutton–Boveri theory): genes are carried on chromosomes. Thomas Hunt Morgan's white-eyed Drosophila mutant (1910) and Calvin Bridges' nondisjunction proof (1916) turned the theory into fact.

  • Worked example — Bridges' nondisjunction. In wild-type crosses, a rare white-eyed F₁ female appeared among red-eyed sisters. Bridges found her cells had XXY sex chromosomes: the two X's had failed to separate in the mother's meiosis. The exceptional phenotype came packaged with the exceptional chromosome count — genes and chromosomes could not be separated. This is the single most convincing piece of evidence for the chromosome theory.
