Air and Water Pollution

Notes

  • Air pollution is the addition of gases and particles that harm health, crops or buildings. The main primary pollutants in this course are sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, unburnt hydrocarbons and particulate matter. Vehicles, coal, industry and, in many Nepali towns, brick kilns are the sources to name.
  • Carbon monoxide binds to haemoglobin as carboxyhaemoglobin and so blocks oxygen transport. Fine particles, especially those small enough to reach the alveoli, damage lungs. Sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides irritate the airway and, dissolved in cloud water, become acid rain. Nitrogen oxides and hydrocarbons in sunlight also make ground-level ozone, which is a smog pollutant, not the stratospheric shield.
  • Control follows the pollutant. An electrostatic precipitator charges dust and collects it on plates, so it suits particulates from a chimney. A scrubber sprays liquid through the gas and takes out sulphur dioxide. A catalytic converter, using platinum, palladium and rhodium, turns carbon monoxide, nitrogen oxides and unburnt hydrocarbons into carbon dioxide, nitrogen and water. Lead in petrol poisons that catalyst, which is why the fuel has to be unleaded. Compressed natural gas burns with less smoke than petrol or diesel and has been used to clean city bus fleets.
  • Water pollution is the addition of sewage, industrial chemicals, detergents, farm runoff or heat until the water can no longer support its normal life. Organic sewage is measured by biochemical oxygen demand: the oxygen microbes need to oxidise the organic matter in a litre of water, usually over five days at 20 °C. A high BOD means the waste will strip dissolved oxygen from the river.
  • Nitrates and phosphates from fertiliser and detergents drive eutrophication. Algae bloom, die, and bacteria decay them. Dissolved oxygen collapses and fish die. Non-biodegradable poisons take a different path. DDT and mercury build up in tissues and become more concentrated at each higher trophic level, which is biomagnification. The Minamata mercury poisoning is the human example of that concentration.
  • Sewage is treated in stages. Primary treatment settles solids. Secondary treatment uses microbes to lower the BOD. Tertiary treatment, where it exists, strips nutrients that would otherwise feed an algal bloom. None of that works if untreated sewage is still discharged beside the plant. Using less phosphate in detergents and less excess fertiliser cuts the problem before the pipe.

Confusion

  • Ground-level ozone in smog is a pollutant. It is not the ozone layer.
  • A high BOD means the water is more polluted with organic matter, not that the water is richer in oxygen.
  • Biomagnification is not the same as a large spill. It is the rise in concentration along a food chain.
  • A catalytic converter does not remove carbon dioxide as its job. It makes carbon dioxide from carbon monoxide.

Key Points

  • Air: SO2, NOx, CO, hydrocarbons, particulates.
  • CO → carboxyhaemoglobin. Fine particles → alveoli. SO2 and NOx → acid rain.
  • Controls: precipitator, scrubber, catalytic converter, CNG.
  • BOD measures organic pollution. High BOD, low oxygen left for fish.
  • Eutrophication is nutrient bloom then oxygen collapse. Biomagnification is DDT or mercury up the food chain.

Important Statements

  • Lead petrol cannot be used with a catalytic converter.
  • Secondary sewage treatment is the biological step that cuts BOD.
  • Top carnivores carry the highest load of a biomagnified poison.

Examples

  • Brick-kiln smoke and diesel exhaust are particulate sources in a Nepali valley.
  • A phosphate-rich drain grows a green skin of algae, then the water underneath loses its oxygen.
  • Fish-eating birds accumulate more DDT than the algae the food chain started with.

Universal Facts

  • Biochemical oxygen demand is commonly read over five days at 20 °C.
  • Mercury at Minamata reached people through contaminated fish, not through a single gulp of bay water.

Formulas

  • Nutrient excess → algal bloom → decay → oxygen collapse → fish kill.
  • DDT or mercury concentration rises toward the top of the food chain.
  • Catalytic converter: CO, NOx and hydrocarbons → CO2, N2 and water, on unleaded fuel.

Practice

  • Match SO2, CO and particulates with one effect and one control each.
  • Define BOD and say what a high value means for a river.
  • Contrast eutrophication with biomagnification.

Practice Questions

  • Why must petrol for a catalytic converter be unleaded?
  • What happens to dissolved oxygen after an algal bloom dies?
  • Why is mercury in a lake more dangerous to people who eat the fish than the water concentration suggests?

Exam Notes

  • Air answers need pollutant, effect and control device. A list of gases alone is half an answer.
  • For water, define BOD before you discuss treatment stages.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Precipitator = dust. Scrubber = SO2. Converter = CO, NOx, hydrocarbons.
  • High BOD = dirty organic water, not healthy oxygen.

Special Notes

  • Name the three metals of the converter if the question asks how it works: platinum, palladium, rhodium.
  • Primary, secondary and tertiary treatment are easy to swap. Settling, then microbes, then nutrients.

Important Notes

  • Heat dumped from a factory is thermal pollution because warm water holds less dissolved oxygen.
  • Biomagnification requires a pollutant that is stored in tissue and not broken down. Nutrients that cause a bloom are a different mechanism.

Exercises

1.

A river receives untreated sewage and the fish die two days later. Explain the oxygen path in steps.

Steps
  • Sewage adds organic matter, so the BOD of the river water rises.
  • Bacteria multiply and use dissolved oxygen to oxidise that matter.
  • Dissolved oxygen falls below what the fish need.
  • The fish die from lack of oxygen, not because the sewage is solid in their gills.
Answer

High BOD drives bacterial decay that strips dissolved oxygen, and the fish suffocate.

Summary

Air pollutants to learn are SO2, NOx, CO, hydrocarbons and particulates, controlled by scrubbers, precipitators, catalytic converters on unleaded fuel, and cleaner fuels such as CNG. Water pollution by sewage raises BOD and can empty a river of oxygen. Nutrients cause eutrophication. Persistent poisons such as DDT and mercury biomagnify toward the top of the food chain. Sewage treatment settles solids, then uses microbes, then can strip nutrients.
