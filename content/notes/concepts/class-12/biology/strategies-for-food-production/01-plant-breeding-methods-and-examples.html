  • Plant breeding is the purposeful genetic improvement of crop plants: cross parents carrying desirable traits, select the best offspring over generations, and release a stable, superior variety. The classic steps: collection of germplasm → hybridisation → selection → testing → release. Success stories: high-yielding wheat and rice of the Green Revolution, and fortified crops like iron-rich spinach varieties.

  • Concept flow — the five steps of classical plant breeding (study in this order)

    StepStageWhat happens
    1Germplasm collectionGather all available wild relatives, landraces and improved varieties — the raw material
    2HybridisationCross selected parents carrying complementary traits (emasculation + bagging for controlled pollination)
    3SelectionOver generations, keep only the progeny with the best combination of traits
    4Testing and evaluationGrow candidates in multi-location field trials vs the best existing varieties
    5Release and distributionOfficially released variety; seed multiplied and sold to farmers

  • Worked example — the semi-dwarf wheat that fed billions. Borlaug's team crossed a Japanese dwarf wheat (carrying height-reducing genes) with high-yielding American varieties. Selection over generations fixed semi-dwarf stature: short, stiff straw that stands up under heavy grain and fertiliser without lodging (falling over). Released as Sonora 64 and Lerma Rojo, adapted to India and Pakistan, yields doubled — the Green Revolution. One trait (short straw) unlocked the whole yield package.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Six-step classical protocol: collection of variability → evaluation and selection of parents → cross-hybridisation → selection of superior recombinants → testing/release (multi-year field trials) — the ordered list IS the answer.
  • Trick 2: Examples: semi-dwarf wheat (Norman Borlaug, Sonalika/Kalyan Sona), rice (Jaya, IR-8), sugarcane (Saccharum × Saccharum barbari), millets hybrid — quote two.
  • Trick 3: Fortification breeding: Atlas 66 wheat (high protein) and iron-fortified spinach varieties — the nutrition angle.
  • Trick 4: Mutation breeding: gamma rays/chemicals create variation (mohan rice variety); polyploidy via colchicine — the non-conventional methods.
