  • Medical biotech manufactures what the body fails to make, or fixes the gene itself. Recombinant insulin (1982) replaced animal pancreas extract. Gene therapy attempts to deliver a working gene into a patient's cells — the classic case being ADA-SCID ('bubble boy' immune deficiency), treated with the ADA gene delivered in a disabled vector. Therapeutic proteins — growth hormone, clotting factors, interferon — are all made by cloned genes in fermenters.

  • Worked example — why ADA-SCID was the right first case. The disease is caused by ONE known gene; the target cells (bone marrow/lymphocytes) can be removed, treated outside the body and reinfused; and even partial ADA restoration gives clear clinical benefit. Perfect first trial: single gene, accessible target, measurable outcome. Most diseases fail at least one of those three — which is why gene therapy has been slow to generalise despite this landmark success.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Human insulin: two-chain A (21 aa) + B (30 aa) linked by disulphide; proinsulin → C-peptide removed; engineered in E. coli since 1983 — the chain numbers are the marks.
  • Trick 2: Gene therapy: ADA deficiency — lymphocytes from the patient, functional ADA gene inserted by retrovirus, cells returned (ex vivo); marrow transplant as alternative.
  • Trick 3: Other products: hGH, interferon, blood clotting factor VIII, vaccines (Hepatitis B surface antigen in yeast) — the product bank.
  • Trick 4: Molecular diagnosis: ELISA (antigen–antibody), PCR (pathogen/mutation detection) — the two diagnostic tools.
