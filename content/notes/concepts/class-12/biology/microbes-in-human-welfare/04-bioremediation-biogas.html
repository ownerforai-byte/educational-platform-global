  • Microbes can be put to work on our waste. Bioremediation uses microorganisms to break pollutants down into harmless substances — oil-eating bacteria (Pseudomonas) after spills, microbes cleaning sewage in treatment plants. Biogas is the fuel side of the same idea: anaerobic methanogen bacteria digest animal dung and plant waste and release methane-rich gas for cooking and lighting.

  • Worked example — why the digester must be airtight. Methanogens are obligate anaerobes: oxygen poisons them. A cracked dome lets air in, the methane producers stop, acid-formers take over, and gas output collapses while the slurry turns sour. The floating dome is not decoration — it seals the digester while letting gas volume change. This single fact explains the whole engineering of the plant.
