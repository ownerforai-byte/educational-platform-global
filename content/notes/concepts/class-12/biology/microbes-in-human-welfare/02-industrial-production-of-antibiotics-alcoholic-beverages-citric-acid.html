  • Worked example — penicillin's scale-up. Fleming's plate gave a halo of killed bacteria around a Penicillium colony, but the yield was tiny. Industrial production needed: (1) a higher-yielding strain (from P. notatum to P. chrysogenum, then X-ray-mutated strains), (2) a better medium (corn-steep liquor), (3) aerated stirred fermenters, because the mould is aerobic, and (4) downstream extraction. Lesson: a discovery becomes a drug only when the fermenter economics work — strain, medium and engineering all three.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Antibiotic: Penicillium notatum/chrysogenum → penicillin (Fleming 1928, Chain & Florey purified); 'antibiotic' = against life — the discovery story earns the mark.
  • Trick 2: Alcohol: Saccharomyces cerevisiae (brewer's yeast) ferments malted grains/fruit juice; distilled spirits vs beer/wine — the yeast is the same in all.
  • Trick 3: Citric acid: Aspergillus niger; also acetic acid (Acetobacter), lactic acid (Lactobacillus) — the industrial chemicals table.
  • Trick 4: Fermenters provide: sterile medium, aeration, temperature control, pH — the four conditions for industrial scale-up.
