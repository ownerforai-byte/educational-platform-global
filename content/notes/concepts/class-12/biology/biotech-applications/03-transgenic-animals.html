Desc

A transgenic animal carries a foreign gene inside the same double helix as its own DNA.

Notes
  • A transgenic animal carries a gene that came from another organism, and that gene is present in the animal's cells and is expressed. The gene is extra DNA, not a temporary injection of protein.
  • The usual route in mice starts with a fertilised egg. Copies of the foreign DNA are microinjected into one pronucleus. The egg is placed in a surrogate mother. Offspring are then tested to see which ones actually incorporated the gene.
  • Only some injected eggs take up the DNA, and only some of those pass the gene on. Screening, often with a marker or by looking for the DNA itself, is part of the method. An animal that merely received a protein is not transgenic.
  • The inserted gene must be expressed, not just sit in the genome. A promoter that works in the host decides where and when the new protein is made. A gene without a working promoter does not change the animal's phenotype.
  • Mice are the common first host because they breed quickly and their embryos are well studied. The same idea has been used in fish, pigs, sheep and cattle when a product or a disease model is wanted in that species.
  • Making the animal is only the first half of the topic. The reason for doing it — disease models, proteins in milk, and safety tests — belongs with the uses. The method here is the transfer of a chosen gene into the germline so every cell of the offspring can carry it.

Confusion

  • A transgenic animal is not any animal that has been cloned. Cloning copies a genome. Transgenesis adds a foreign gene.
  • Injecting a hormone into an adult does not make that adult transgenic. The gene has to be in the DNA.
  • Not every offspring of an injected egg carries the new gene. The litter has to be screened.
  • The foreign gene is not automatically expressed in every tissue. Expression follows the promoter attached to it.

Key Points

  • Transgenic means a foreign gene is in the genome and is expressed.
  • Microinjection into a fertilised-egg pronucleus is the standard mouse method.
  • The embryo is carried by a surrogate, then the young are screened.
  • A promoter controls whether the new gene is actually used.
  • Cloning and transgenesis are different operations.

Important Statements

  • The defining feature is an inherited, expressed foreign gene, not a one-off treatment.
  • Most injected embryos do not become transgenic founders. Screening is required.
  • The gene is introduced at the start of development so it can be in the germline.

Examples

  • A mouse embryo is injected with a human disease gene so the adult mouse develops a version of that disease for study.
  • A growth-hormone gene placed in a fish embryo can produce a line that grows faster than its unmodified relatives.
  • A gene for a human milk protein, joined to a mammary promoter, is aimed at expression in the milk of a transgenic cow rather than in every organ.

Universal Facts

  • The foreign DNA is inserted into a very early embryo so that, if it integrates, daughter cells inherit it.
  • Expression depends on a promoter the host cell recognises. The coding sequence alone is not enough.

Formulas

  • Path: foreign gene + promoter → microinjection into pronucleus → surrogate → screen offspring → transgenic line.
  • Transgenic means integrated foreign DNA that is expressed. A protein injection does not qualify.

Practice

  • Say how a clone and a transgenic animal differ.

Practice Questions

  • Why are not all pups from an injected egg transgenic?
  • What does the promoter contribute that the coding sequence does not?
  • At what stage of the life cycle is the foreign DNA usually introduced, and why?

Exam Notes

  • Define transgenic before you describe the injection. The definition is the foreign gene plus its expression.
  • Name the pronucleus injection and the need to screen. Both are expected in a method answer.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Clone = copy. Transgenic = added gene.
  • If the question says 'expressed', mention the promoter, not only the injection.

Special Notes

  • Write 'fertilised egg' or 'early embryo', not 'the adult is injected with DNA'.
  • Keep the method in this note and the five uses in the applications note so the two answers do not collapse into one.

Important Notes

  • Integration is not guaranteed. Say that screening follows birth.
  • Germline presence is what lets the gene pass to the next generation.

Exercises

1.

A student says a mouse is transgenic because it was injected with human insulin. Correct that claim in steps.

Steps
  • A protein injection does not change the mouse genome.
  • A transgenic mouse must carry the foreign DNA in its cells.
  • That DNA is introduced into a fertilised egg, not given as a drug to the adult.
  • The gene must also be expressed, which needs a working promoter.
Answer

The mouse is transgenic only if the foreign gene is in its DNA and is expressed. An insulin injection is a treatment, not transgenesis.

Summary

A transgenic animal is produced by putting a foreign gene into an early embryo, usually by microinjection into a fertilised-egg pronucleus, then screening the offspring for integration and expression. A promoter decides where the gene is used. Cloning copies a genome and is a different procedure.
