Desc

Three nested circles: genetic diversity inside species diversity inside ecosystem diversity.

Notes
  • Biodiversity is variety of life at three nested levels. Genetic diversity is the variety of alleles inside a species. Species diversity is the variety of species in a place. Ecosystem diversity is the variety of habitats, communities and ecological processes.
  • Genetic diversity is the raw material of adaptation. A crop variety or a wild population with many alleles is more likely to survive a new disease or a hotter year. A population bred from a few founders is fragile.
  • Species diversity has two parts. Richness is how many species are present. Evenness is whether individuals are spread across those species or piled into one or two. A forest of 20 tree species can still be dominated by a single species.
  • Ecosystem diversity in Nepal runs from Terai sal forest and wetlands, through mid-hill broadleaf forest, to alpine meadows and nival rock. Each habitat holds a different set of species and a different set of services such as water storage and soil holding.
  • The levels are nested, not alternatives. Losing a habitat (ecosystem level) removes the species that lived there, and losing those populations removes the alleles they carried.
  • Conserving only one famous species misses the other two levels. A park that keeps rhinoceros habitat also keeps the grasses, the soil microbes and the genetic variety inside the rhino population.

Confusion

  • Species richness is not the whole of species diversity. Evenness matters. A count of species names ignores whether one species makes up almost every individual.
  • Genetic diversity is not the number of species. It is variation inside one species.
  • A monoculture field can have high yield and very low genetic diversity. Productivity and diversity are not the same measurement.
  • Ecosystem diversity is not a list of animals. It is the variety of whole habitats and the processes that run in them.

Key Points

  • Three levels: genetic, species, ecosystem.
  • Genetic diversity is allele variation within a species.
  • Species diversity combines richness and evenness.
  • Ecosystem diversity is the variety of habitats and processes.
  • The levels are nested: habitats hold species, species hold alleles.

Important Statements

  • Genetic diversity lets populations adapt. Species diversity and ecosystem diversity keep functions such as pollination, decomposition and water regulation.
  • Evenness and richness together describe species diversity.
  • Loss at the ecosystem level pulls down the two levels inside it.

Examples

  • Rice landraces in Nepal carry different alleles for flood tolerance and taste. That is genetic diversity inside one species.
  • A mid-hill forest with many tree species in similar numbers is richer and more even than a plantation of one species.
  • Terai grassland, a phewa-type lake, and an alpine meadow are three ecosystems, not three species.

Universal Facts

  • A species can be widespread and still genetically narrow if it passed through a population bottleneck.
  • Ecosystem services used by people, including fodder, fish and drinking water, depend on diversity at more than one level.

Formulas

  • Species diversity is described by richness (number of species) and evenness (how equal their abundances are).
  • Nested order: alleles ⊂ populations ⊂ species ⊂ ecosystems.

Practice

  • Give one Nepal example of each level.
  • Explain why a single-species plantation is low diversity even if it is tall and green.
  • Say what happens to genetic diversity when a habitat is cleared.

Practice Questions

  • How do richness and evenness differ?
  • Why is genetic diversity a conservation target inside a national park, not only the species list?
  • A lake, the forest around it, and the rice fields below are lost together when a dam floods the valley. Which level is lost first, and what does that remove?

Exam Notes

  • Define all three levels and give one example of each. Definitions without examples lose credit.
  • Mention richness and evenness when the question says species diversity.

Exam Short Tricks

  • G-S-E: genes, species, ecosystems. Inside, inside, landscape.
  • Richness = how many. Evenness = how balanced.

Special Notes

  • Do not use 'biodiversity' as a synonym of 'number of animals'. Plants, fungi and microbes belong in the same three levels.
  • A labelled sketch of three nested circles matches the static figure for this topic.

Important Notes

  • Pair each level with what is actually counted: alleles, species abundances, or habitat types.
  • State the nesting so a loss question can be answered in one direction: ecosystem loss removes species and genes.

Exercises

1.

A forest fragment keeps 15 tree species, but 90 percent of the stems belong to one of them. A second fragment keeps 8 tree species in nearly equal numbers. Which is richer, and which is more even?

Steps
  • Richness is the count of species.
  • The first fragment has 15 species, the second has 8, so the first is richer.
  • Evenness is high when abundances are similar.
  • The second fragment, with nearly equal numbers, is more even.
Answer

The first fragment is richer. The second fragment is more even.

Summary

Biodiversity is measured at three nested levels: genetic diversity inside species, species diversity (richness plus evenness) in a community, and ecosystem diversity across habitats. Losing a habitat removes the species and the alleles inside it. Nepal's range from Terai wetlands to alpine meadows is ecosystem diversity that holds the other two levels.
