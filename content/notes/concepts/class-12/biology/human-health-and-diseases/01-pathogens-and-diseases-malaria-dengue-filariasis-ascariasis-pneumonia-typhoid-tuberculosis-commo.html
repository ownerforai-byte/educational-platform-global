  • Worked example — reading a case. A patient has fever that returns every 48 hours with violent shills, lives near a stagnant pond, and a blood smear shows parasites inside red blood cells. Pathogen: Plasmodium vivax (tertian pattern). Vector: female Anopheles. Control: antimalarial drug + drain the pond + bed nets. Exam trap: only the female mosquito bites (needs blood for eggs); the male feeds on plant sap.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Vector table — malaria: Plasmodium via female Anopheles; dengue: dengue virus via female Aedes; filariasis: Wuchereria via Culex; typhoid: Salmonella typhi (contaminated food/water); ascariasis: Ascaris (fecal-oral); pneumonia: Streptococcus/Diplococcus.
  • Trick 2: Symptoms one-liners: malaria — recurring chill and fever (every 48 h for P. vivax); typhoid — step-ladder fever, Widal test; filariasis — elephantiasis (lymph swelling).
  • Trick 3: Control pairs: mosquito-borne → nets, repellents, larvivorous fish; water-borne → safe drinking water, ORS, sanitation; the control answer mirrors the transmission route.
  • Trick 4: AIDS: HIV via sexual contact, infected needles, transfusion — NOT by touch; the 'not spread by' clause earns the mark.
