    Allergy and autoimmune diseases

    Fill order per concept: definition → structure → process → significance (§3.2). Step-by-step numbered points, Pioneer style.

  • The immune system can fail in two opposite directions. Allergy is an over-reaction to a harmless antigen (pollen, dust, food) — IgE-coated mast cells dump histamine. Autoimmunity is the loss of self-tolerance: the immune system attacks the body's own tissues (rheumatoid arthritis, Type 1 diabetes). One is an attack on something harmless; the other on something that is self.

  • Worked example. A teenager wheezes every spring and a skin-prick test raises a red weal within minutes for grass pollen. Mechanism: first exposures made IgE against pollen; the IgE now sits on mast cells in the airway; each spring the allergen bridges two IgE molecules, the mast cell degranulates, and histamine causes bronchoconstriction. Treatment: antihistamine blocks receptors; inhaled steroids calm the chronic inflammation; adrenaline is reserved for anaphylaxis.

  • Trick 3: Autoimmunity: immune system attacks SELF (memory loss of self-tolerance) — rheumatoid arthritis, type-1 diabetes, lupus; quote two.
  • Trick 4: Hereditary angle: allergies run in families (atopy) — the genetic susceptibility line.
