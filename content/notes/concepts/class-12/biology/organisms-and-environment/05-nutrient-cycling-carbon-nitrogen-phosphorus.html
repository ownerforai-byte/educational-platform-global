  • Nutrient cycling (biogeochemical cycling) returns matter from organisms to the environment and back. Cycles are gaseous (reservoir in the atmosphere — carbon, nitrogen) or sedimentary (reservoir in rocks and soil — phosphorus, sulphur). The nitrogen cycle is the one with named bacterial specialists; the carbon cycle is the one we are now disturbing; the phosphorus cycle has no gas phase at all.

  • Worked example — why legumes enrich soil. A farmer rotates maize with beans. The bean roots host Rhizobium in nodules; the bacteria fix atmospheric N2 into ammonia the plant builds into protein. After harvest the residues decompose (ammonification), nitrifiers convert the nitrogen to nitrate, and the next maize crop takes it up. The farmer bought nitrogen with a seed instead of a fertiliser bag — the cycle did the work.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Carbon: atmosphere ↔ photosynthesis/respiration; CO2 ~0.03–0.04% of air; largest reservoir = ocean; human addition = fossil fuels — the reservoirs in one line each.
  • Trick 2: Nitrogen: N2 (78% of air) unusable directly → fixation (Rhizobium, cyanobacteria, lightning) → nitrification (NH3 → NO2− → NO3−) → assimilation → denitrification (Pseudomonas back to N2) — the five stations.
  • Trick 3: Phosphorus: NO atmospheric phase — sedimentary cycle only; rock → soil → plants → animals → back by decomposers; slowest of the three.
  • Trick 4: Gaseous vs sedimentary: C and N are gaseous cycles (atmosphere reservoir), P is sedimentary (lithosphere) — the classification 2-marker.
