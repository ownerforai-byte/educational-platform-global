Enriched Content

Notes
  • DETAILED MONERA (bacterial + cyanobacterial) STRUCTURE AND PHYSIOLOGY.
  • Bacterial cell (Gram-negative example, E. coli): capsule (slime layer, polysaccharide, virulence) → cell wall (thin peptidoglycan + outer LPS membrane with porins) → plasma membrane (phospholipid + proteins, respiratory + metabolic enzymes) → cytoplasm (70S ribosomes, plasmids, nucleoid).
  • Bacterial cell (Gram-positive example, Staphylococcus): capsule → thick peptidoglycan cell wall (no outer membrane) → plasma membrane → cytoplasm; mesosomes (invaginations, respiration/secretion).
  • Pili (fimbriae): short, many; attachment + conjugation (sex pilus); flagella: 1–many; locomotion (peritrichous, polar, lophotrichous).
  • Nucleoid: naked, circular, double-stranded DNA; no histones; 1–10 plasmids (small circular DNA, carry antibiotic-resistance + virulence genes).
  • Shapes: cocci (diplococci, streptococci, staphylococci, tetrads, sarcinae), bacilli (diplobacilli, streptobacilli), spirilla (spiral, rigid), spirochaetes (spiral, flexible).
  • Nutrition: autotrophs (photoautotrophs — cyanobacteria; chemoautotrophs — nitrifiers) + heterotrophs (saprophytes, parasites).
  • Growth curve: lag (adaptation) → log (exponential, 2ⁿ) → stationary (nutrient depletion + waste) → death (lysis); generation time (E. coli ~20 min).
  • Reproduction: asexual (binary fission, budding in some) + genetic exchange (conjugation [F+ → F-], transformation [DNA uptake], transduction [phage-mediated]).
  • Endospores: dormant, resistant structures (Bacillus, Clostridium); survive heat, radiation, chemicals; germinate when conditions improve.
  • Cyanobacteria: photosynthetic (chlorophyll a + phycobilins); Nostoc (beaded filaments), Anabaena (heterocysts for N2 fixation), Oscillatoria (trichoid); important in rice paddies as biofertilisers.
  • Mycoplasma: no cell wall; smallest living cell; pleomorphic; causes atypical pneumonia.
  • Archaebacteria: extreme environments (halophiles, thermoacidophiles, methanogens); no peptidoglycan; membrane lipids (ether linkages).
  • NEB focus: detailed bacterial cell (Gram + and -), shapes, nutrition, growth curve, reproduction, cyanobacteria, mycoplasma, and archaebacteria.
Confusion
  • Gram-positive = THICK peptidoglycan (retains crystal violet, purple); Gram-negative = THIN peptidoglycan + outer LPS membrane (stains pink with safranin).
  • Mesosomes are invaginations of the plasma membrane; they are NOT true organelles (no membrane-bound structure).
  • Plasmids are small circular DNA; they are NOT the chromosome (nucleoid).
  • Endospores are NOT a mode of reproduction; they are survival structures (one endospore → one bacterium).
  • Cyanobacteria are PROKARYOTES (Bacteria domain), NOT true algae; they have no membrane-bound organelles.
  • Mycoplasma has NO cell wall; it is the smallest known living cell (0.1–0.3 µm).
  • Archaebacteria differ from eubacteria in cell wall (no peptidoglycan) and membrane lipids (ether linkages); they live in extreme environments.
  • Binary fission produces two genetically identical daughter cells; conjugation transfers DNA but does NOT increase cell number.

Universal Facts
  • Bacteria are the oldest and most abundant life form on Earth (~3.5 billion years).
  • A single E. coli cell can divide in ~20 minutes under optimal conditions.
  • More than 99% of the organisms on Earth are prokaryotes.
  • Mycoplasma (0.1–0.3 µm) is the smallest known living cell.
Examples
  • Gram-negative: E. coli (rod, peritrichous flagella, LPS outer membrane).
  • Gram-positive: Staphylococcus aureus (coccus, clusters, thick peptidoglycan, mesosomes).
  • Cyanobacteria: Nostoc (beaded filaments), Anabaena (heterocysts for N2 fixation), Oscillatoria (trichoid).
  • Mycoplasma pneumoniae: no wall, pleomorphic, atypical pneumonia.
  • Archaebacteria: Halobacterium (salt lakes), Thermoplasma (hot springs), Methanobacterium (anaerobic, methane).
  • Endospore formers: Bacillus (soil, aerobic), Clostridium (anaerobic, e.g., C. botulinum, C. tetani).

Key Points
  • Bacterial cell: capsule → wall (peptidoglycan) → membrane → cytoplasm (nucleoid, plasmids, 70S ribosomes).
  • Gram +ve: thick peptidoglycan (purple); Gram -ve: thin + LPS (pink).
  • Shapes: cocci, bacilli, spirilla, spirochaetes; arrangements: diplo-, strepto-, staphylo-.

Summary

A detailed study of Monera reveals the bacterial cell in all its complexity: a capsule, peptidoglycan wall (thick in Gram +ve, thin + LPS in Gram -ve), plasma membrane (with mesosomes in Gram +ve), and cytoplasm containing the naked nucleoid, plasmids, and 70S ribosomes. Bacteria show diverse shapes (cocci, bacilli, spirilla) and arrangements, feed by autotrophy or heterotrophy, and grow through a 4-phase curve (lag, log, stationary, death). They reproduce asexually by binary fission and exchange genes by conjugation, transformation, and transduction. Endospores (Bacillus, Clostridium) are survival structures. Cyanobacteria (Nostoc, Anabaena) photosynthesise and fix nitrogen; Mycoplasma (no wall) is the smallest living cell; archaebacteria thrive in extreme environments. NEB focuses on the detailed bacterial cell, shapes, nutrition, growth, reproduction, cyanobacteria, and special groups.

Summary

A detailed study of Monera reveals the bacterial cell in all its complexity: a capsule, peptidoglycan wall (thick in Gram +ve, thin + LPS in Gram -ve), plasma membrane (with mesosomes in Gram +ve), and cytoplasm containing the naked nucleoid, plasmids, and 70S ribosomes. Bacteria show diverse shapes (cocci, bacilli, spirilla) and arrangements, feed by autotrophy or heterotrophy, and grow through a 4-phase curve (lag, log, stationary, death). They reproduce asexually by binary fission and exchange genes by conjugation, transformation, and transduction. Endospores (Bacillus, Clostridium) are survival structures. Cyanobacteria (Nostoc, Anabaena) photosynthesise and fix nitrogen; Mycoplasma (no wall) is the smallest living cell; archaebacteria thrive in extreme environments.
