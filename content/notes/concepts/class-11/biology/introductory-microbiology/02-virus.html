Enriched Content

Notes
  • Viruses: acellular, obligate intracellular parasites; not classified in any kingdom; consist of a nucleic acid core (DNA or RNA) + protein coat (capsid); some have a lipid envelope.
  • Structure: capsomeres (protein subunits of capsid); bacteriophages have a head (icosahedral) + tail; animal viruses may have an envelope with glycoprotein spikes.
  • Types by nucleic acid: DNA viruses (e.g., T4 phage, smallpox) and RNA viruses (e.g., influenza, HIV, SARS-CoV-2).
  • Bacteriophage (T4): lytic life cycle — adsorption → injection → replication → assembly → release (lysogeny is the lysogenic cycle in lambda phage).
  • Replication: viruses hijack the host cell machinery; they cannot replicate independently.
  • Importance: many human and animal diseases (common cold, flu, HIV/AIDS, hepatitis, rabies, smallpox — eradicated in 1980).
  • Applications: viral vectors in gene therapy, bacteriophages in food safety testing, viruses in molecular biology tools.
  • NEB focus: virus structure, lytic cycle of T4 phage, and importance of viruses.
Confusion
  • Viruses are NOT living organisms: they lack metabolism, cannot reproduce outside a host, and are not made of cells.
  • A virus has EITHER DNA or RNA, never both (with rare exceptions like some retroviruses that convert RNA to DNA).
  • Bacteriophages infect BACTERIA; animal viruses infect animals; plant viruses infect plants.
  • The capsid is protein; the core is nucleic acid; the envelope (if present) is lipid + glycoproteins from the host membrane.
  • Lytic cycle destroys the host cell; lysogenic cycle integrates viral DNA into the host genome (prophage) without immediate lysis.
  • Viruses are NOT visible under a light microscope; they require an electron microscope (~20–300 nm size).
Practice
  • Draw and label a T4 bacteriophage (head, collar, tail, baseplate, tail fibers).
  • Describe the lytic life cycle of a bacteriophage in 5 steps.
  • Differentiate a DNA virus from an RNA virus (3 points).
  • Explain why viruses are not considered living organisms.
  • List three human diseases caused by viruses and their viral agents.
Universal Facts
  • The largest virus (Pithovirus) is ~1.2 µm in diameter — visible only under a light microscope.
  • Smallpox was the first human disease eradicated by vaccination (1980).
  • Retroviruses (e.g., HIV) carry RNA but use reverse transcriptase to make DNA in the host.
  • Viruses are ~1/100th the size of a typical bacterium.
Examples
  • T4 bacteriophage — DNA virus, infects E. coli; lytic cycle model.
  • Influenza virus — RNA virus, causes seasonal flu.
  • HIV (Human Immunodeficiency Virus) — retrovirus, causes AIDS.
  • SARS-CoV-2 — RNA virus, causes COVID-19.
Practice Questions
  • Why are viruses called "acellular"?
  • Describe the structure of a T4 bacteriophage with a labelled diagram.
  • Explain the lytic life cycle of a bacteriophage (5 steps).
  • Differentiate a DNA virus from an RNA virus.
  • What is the significance of the viral envelope?
Formulas
  • Virus = Nucleic acid (DNA or RNA) + Capsid (protein) [+ Envelope (lipid + glycoprotein)]
  • Lytic cycle: Adsorption → Penetration (injection) → Biosynthesis → Assembly → Release
  • Lysogenic cycle: Prophage integration → Replication with host → Induction → Lytic cycle
Key Points
  • Viruses are acellular, obligate intracellular parasites.
  • Structure: nucleic acid + capsid (+ envelope in some).
  • DNA or RNA (not both); capsomeres form the capsid.
  • Bacteriophage: icosahedral head + tail + baseplate + tail fibers.
  • Lytic cycle: 5 steps, destroys host; Lysogenic: integrates, delays lysis.
  • Not living: no metabolism, no independent reproduction.
  • Importance: cause many diseases; used in gene therapy and research.
Summary

Viruses are acellular, obligate intracellular parasites that contain either DNA or RNA enclosed in a protein capsid (and sometimes a lipid envelope). They cannot replicate outside a host cell and hijack host machinery. The T4 bacteriophage lytic cycle (adsorption, injection, replication, assembly, release) is the NEB model. Viruses cause many important diseases but are also valuable tools in gene therapy and molecular biology. NEB examinations focus on virus structure, the lytic cycle, and the distinction between viruses and living organisms.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects a detailed T4 phage diagram and the 5-step lytic cycle.
  • The "are viruses alive?" debate is a favourite exam discussion; present both arguments.
  • DNA vs RNA viruses: know the key difference and one example of each.
  • Mention the significance of the viral envelope (immune evasion, cell entry).
Important Statements
  • Viruses are the smallest infectious agents known.
  • They contain either DNA or RNA, but never both.
  • The lytic cycle destroys the host cell; the lysogenic cycle integrates into the host genome.
  • Viruses are not classified in any biological kingdom.
Important Notes
  • Memorise the T4 phage structure (head, tail, baseplate, fibers).
  • Learn the 5 steps of the lytic cycle.
  • Differentiate DNA and RNA viruses with examples.
  • Explain why viruses are not considered living.
  • List 3 human viral diseases and their agents.
Exam Short Tricks
  • Virus = V-O-I (Very small, Obligate, Infectious).
  • T4 phage = icosahedral head + tail = "phage = eater of bacteria".
  • Lytic = L-Y-TIC (Lysis, Yields nothing, Terminates host).
  • RNA viruses: flu, HIV, SARS; DNA viruses: T4, smallpox, herpes.
Exam Notes

  • Describe the lytic life cycle in 5 steps.
  • Differentiate DNA and RNA viruses.
  • Explain why viruses are not living organisms.
  • List the importance of viruses (diseases, gene therapy, research).
  • Differentiate lytic from lysogenic cycle.
