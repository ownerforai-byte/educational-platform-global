Notes

  • Monera is the prokaryotic kingdom. The cell has no true nucleus and no mitochondria, chloroplasts or other membrane-bound organelles. DNA lies in a nucleoid. Ribosomes are 70S. The usual wall contains peptidoglycan. Binary fission is the ordinary division. Cyanobacteria, still called blue-green algae in older lines, belong here even though they photosynthesise.
  • Shapes used in class are coccus (sphere), bacillus (rod), spirillum (spiral) and vibrio (comma). A capsule outside the wall resists drying and phagocytes. The wall keeps the shape. The membrane is the site of respiration, because there are no mitochondria. A mesosome is an inward fold of that membrane. Flagella move the cell. Pili attach it and, in some bacteria, transfer DNA.
  • Nutrition is either autotrophic or heterotrophic. Photoautotrophs use light. Chemoautotrophs oxidise inorganic chemicals. Heterotrophs are saprophytic, parasitic or symbiotic. Rhizobium in legume nodules is the symbiotic example: it fixes nitrogen and receives shelter and food. Parasitic bacteria cause disease. Saprophytes decay dead matter.
  • Growth in a closed culture has four phases. Lag is preparation, with little division. Log is exponential binary fission. Stationary is a balance of division and death when food or space runs out. Death follows when wastes accumulate. The curve is of a population in a flask, not of one cell.
  • Cyanobacteria have chlorophyll a and phycocyanin, so they look blue-green, but the pigments sit on membranes in the cytoplasm, not in chloroplasts. Nostoc and Anabaena fix nitrogen in heterocysts, thick-walled cells that protect the nitrogenase enzyme from oxygen. A bloom in a warm pond is often this group.
  • Reproduction is mainly binary fission. Some form endospores, resting cells that survive heat and drying. Those are not gametes. Gene exchange, when it happens, is conjugation through a pilus, or transformation, or transduction by a virus. There is no mitosis, because there is no spindle and no nuclear membrane to break.

Important Statements

  • Respiration in a bacterium is on the plasma membrane.
  • Binary fission is the usual reproduction.
  • A heterocyst is the nitrogen-fixing cell of a cyanobacterium.

Formulas

  • Shapes: coccus, bacillus, spirillum, vibrio.
  • Growth: lag, log, stationary, death.

Exam Notes

  • Structure is a labelled cell. Nutrition is four words with an example.
  • Growth phases must be in order: lag, log, stationary, death.

Exam Short Tricks

  • No nucleus, 70S, peptidoglycan.
  • Blue-green, but still Monera.

Special Notes

  • Mycoplasma has no wall. It is the exception, not the diagram.
  • Do not draw mitochondria inside a bacterium.

Important Notes

  • Conjugation is gene transfer, not the reproduction that increases cell number. Fission does that.
  • A bloom is a population explosion, not a flower.

Exercises

1.

A cell has a nucleoid, 70S ribosomes and a peptidoglycan wall, and it photosynthesises. Place it and name the nitrogen-fixing cell if it is Anabaena.

Steps
  • No nucleus and 70S ribosomes make it a prokaryote.
  • The kingdom in this course is Monera.
  • Photosynthesis with no chloroplast fits a cyanobacterium.
  • In Anabaena the heterocyst is the cell that fixes nitrogen.
Answer

Monera, a cyanobacterium. Nitrogen fixation is in the heterocyst.
