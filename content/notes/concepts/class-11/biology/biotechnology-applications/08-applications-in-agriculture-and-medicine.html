Summary

Biotechnology applies the use of living organisms and their products to make or modify useful products, and in modern practice this means recombinant DNA technology. In agriculture, Bt crops carrying Bacillus thuringiensis genes give insect resistance, Golden Rice supplies provitamin A, and herbicide-resistant, delayed-ripening and drought-tolerant varieties raise yields and food quality. In medicine, recombinant DNA gives human insulin, growth hormone and hepatitis B vaccine, while gene therapy, monoclonal antibodies and CRISPR-Cas9 treat and diagnose disease at the genetic level. Transgenic animals serve as disease models and as factories producing therapeutic proteins, and the same tools clean up pollution through bioremediation, although the unauthorised use of indigenous knowledge raises the ethical problem of biopiracy.

Important Concepts

  • A transgenic organism carries an artificially inserted gene from another organism, introduced through a suitable vector; Bt cotton, for example, expresses the cry gene of Bacillus thuringiensis.
  • The Bt toxin is a protein that becomes active only in the gut of susceptible insect larvae, which is why Bt crops are considered safe for humans and most other organisms.
  • Recombinant DNA technology produces human insulin and growth hormone in bacteria or yeast instead of extracting them from slaughtered cattle and pigs, as was done for earlier animal insulin.
  • Transgenic animals are used as disease models (transgenic mice for cancer and Alzheimer's), as producers of useful substances (pharming, with therapeutic proteins secreted in milk), and as aquarium or companion organisms (GloFish).
  • Biopiracy is the unauthorised commercial use of biological resources and traditional knowledge; the neem and basmati rice patent cases were challenged and the patents revoked.
  • Bioremediation uses organisms to remove pollutants — Pseudomonas species degrade oil hydrocarbons, and some microbes accumulate heavy metals such as mercury and cadmium.

Important Statements

  • Bt cotton contains the cry gene from Bacillus thuringiensis, whose toxin protein kills insect larvae that feed on the plant.
  • Golden Rice is engineered to accumulate beta-carotene (provitamin A) in the grain so that it can help combat vitamin A deficiency.
  • Human insulin made by recombinant DNA technology in Escherichia coli has the same biological activity as natural human insulin.
  • Monoclonal antibodies are produced by the fusion of a single antibody-producing B cell with a myeloma cell to form a hybridoma, and are used in cancer therapy and in diagnostic tests.
  • Gene therapy aims to correct a faulty gene by replacing, removing or silencing it using a suitable vector delivered to the affected tissue.
  • Biopiracy is the unauthorised commercial appropriation of biological resources and traditional knowledge without permission or benefit sharing, and it threatens national sovereignty.

Important Tasks

  • Explain, step by step, how human insulin is produced using recombinant DNA technology, naming the restriction enzyme, vector, host organism and the joining of the A and B chains.
  • Describe how Bt cotton resists insect attack and explain why the toxin does not harm humans.
  • Define biopiracy and discuss the neem, basmati rice and wheat patent cases as disputes over the misappropriation of biological resources.
  • List the three main uses of transgenic animals and give one example of each use.
  • Describe how bioremediation can be used to clear an oil spill and how microbes remove heavy metals from contaminated sites.

Key Points

  • Agricultural applications: Bt cotton, Bt corn, Golden Rice, herbicide-resistant crops, delayed-ripening tomato (Flavr Savr) and drought-resistant crops.
  • Medical applications: insulin, growth hormone for dwarfism, hepatitis B vaccine made in a yeast expression system, gene therapy, monoclonal antibodies and CRISPR-Cas9.
  • Flavr Savr, engineered to delay softening of tomatoes, was the first commercial GM food approved for sale, in 1994 in the United States.
  • CRISPR-Cas9 is a gene-editing tool in which a guide RNA directs a nuclease to a chosen DNA sequence, so that sequence can then be changed precisely.
  • Transgenic animals: GloFish (fluorescent zebrafish, a novelty and research organism), transgenic mice as disease models, and pharming animals that secrete therapeutic proteins in milk.
  • Bioremediation uses microbes such as Pseudomonas putida to degrade oil hydrocarbons, while other organisms bioaccumulate or biosorb heavy metals.

Special Notes

  • The accepted terms are 'genetically modified organism' (GMO) and 'transgenic organism'; labels such as 'GWO' are not standard biology terms.
  • Bt toxin is activated only in the alkaline gut of susceptible insect larvae, which is the basis of its safety for humans, mammals and most other organisms.
  • Biopiracy is the unauthorised appropriation of biological resources without permission or benefit sharing; the neem and basmati rice patents were challenged and revoked.
  • Insulin extracted from slaughtered cattle and pigs was the earlier source, and recombinant DNA technology replaced it because the recombinant product is cheaper, purer and free of animal contaminants.
  • Flavr Savr (delayed-ripening tomato) reached the market before Bt crops, and Golden Rice targets a micronutrient deficiency rather than insect resistance.

Exam Short Tricks

  • 'Bt' means Bacillus thuringiensis, so Bt crops give insect resistance rather than disease resistance.
  • Insulin route: isolate the A and B chain genes, cut with a restriction enzyme, insert into plasmids, ligate A and B together, transform into E. coli, obtain proinsulin, then cleave to insulin.
  • Agriculture shortcut: Bt = insect, Herbicide-resistant = weed, Delayed-ripening = Flavr Savr, Golden Rice = vitamin A.
  • Three uses of transgenic animals: Model (disease models), Manufacture (pharming), Companion or aquarium (GloFish).
  • Biopiracy case tag: the neem and basmati rice patents were both revoked, so biopiracy can be challenged in law.

Exam Notes

  • Bt crops, Golden Rice and herbicide-resistant crops are standard 2-5 mark agriculture questions; the cry gene, the target insect and the mode of action are asked repeatedly.
  • Insulin production by recombinant DNA is one of the highest-frequency 5-mark questions and expects a flow diagram from gene isolation to the final hormone.
  • One-mark MCQs ask for the source of a transgene: Bacillus thuringiensis for Bt, the Ti plasmid of Agrobacterium tumefaciens for plant transformation, and Escherichia coli as the host.
  • Biopiracy questions are usually 2-3 marks and want a definition plus a named case such as neem, basmati rice or turmeric.
  • Transgenic animals and bioremediation are usually set as 2-3 mark application questions rather than detailed process questions.

Mcs

1.

The cry gene used in Bt cotton is obtained from:

Options
  • Agrobacterium tumefaciens
  • Bacillus thuringiensis
  • Escherichia coli
  • Pseudomonas putida
Answer

B

Explanation

The cry gene of Bacillus thuringiensis codes for a toxin protein that kills susceptible insect larvae. Agrobacterium supplies the Ti plasmid used as a plant vector, and Pseudomonas putida is used in bioremediation.

2.

Golden Rice has been genetically modified to increase:

Options
  • Resistance to herbicides
  • The content of beta-carotene (provitamin A)
  • Resistance to insect pests
  • The time of ripening of the fruit
Answer

B

Explanation

Golden Rice accumulates beta-carotene, a precursor of vitamin A, in the grain and aims to reduce vitamin A deficiency. Bt crops target insect pests and herbicide-resistant crops target weeds.

3.

The first step in producing recombinant human insulin in bacteria is:

Options
  • Injecting the insulin into the host cell
  • Isolating the human insulin gene and cutting it with a restriction enzyme
  • Screening the cells for antibiotic resistance
  • Fermenting the bacteria at 37 degrees C
Answer

B

Explanation

The human A and B chain genes are isolated, amplified and cut with a restriction enzyme so that they can be inserted into a plasmid vector, which is then introduced into the host bacterium.

4.

Biopiracy refers to:

Options
  • The scientific study of biodiversity
  • The authorised use of plant genes in research
  • The unauthorised commercial use of biological resources and traditional knowledge
  • The legal patenting of human genes
Answer

C

Explanation

Biopiracy is the unauthorised appropriation of genetic resources or indigenous knowledge for commercial gain, without permission or benefit sharing, as challenged in the neem and basmati rice patent cases.

5.

Transgenic animals used to secrete therapeutic human proteins in their milk are an example of:

Options
  • Bioremediation
  • Biopiracy
  • Pharming
  • Bioinformatics
Answer

C

Explanation

Pharming, or biopharming, uses transgenic animals as living factories that secrete human therapeutic proteins in milk; bioremediation instead uses organisms to clean up pollutants.

  • Medicine uses recombinant cells that carry the human gene to make human insulin for people who need it.

Universal Facts

Exercises

1.

Separate what Bt cotton does from what recombinant insulin does.

Steps
  • Bt cotton makes a protein that kills certain caterpillars in the field.
  • Recombinant insulin is a medicine made by cells that carry the human gene.
  • One is a crop protection. The other is a drug.
  • Neither is an antibiotic, and neither is a vaccine.
Answer

Bt protects the crop from caterpillars. Recombinant insulin is a medicine.
