  • 1. Topic Introduction — From Forest Ape to Global Species

    This topic completes the human story: how an anthropoid (man-like) ancestor left the trees, stood up, made tools, tamed fire, spoke and spread across every continent. It builds directly on Topic 35's stage list — here the emphasis is the process: why each step happened.

  • 2. The Anthropoid Ancestor and the Split

    PhaseDetail
    Anthropoid ancestorsMonkey-and-ape line (anthropoids = higher primates) descended from prosimian-like mammals (~50 mya, Eocene)
    Common ape-human ancestorA forest ape (Dryopithecus-like, ~15–20 mya) shared by humans, chimpanzees and gorillas
    The split (~6–7 mya)Forest shrinkage in Africa (Miocene cooling) split the line: one branch stayed forest-adapted (→ chimpanzee/gorilla), the other exploited open woodland and savanna (→ hominins)

    Why bipedalism came first: on open ground, standing and walking upright (1) scans over grass for predators and food, (2) frees the hands for carrying food, tools and infants, (3) reduces sun exposure, (4) is energetically efficient for long-distance travel between food patches.

    StageTimeKey acquisitionDriving pressure

    Ramapithecus14–10 myaJaws/teeth of a ground feederDiet shift to seeds/roots in open country

    Homo neanderthalensis400–40 kyaMousterian tools, burial, care, cold adaptationIce-age Europe; social complexity
    Homo sapiens~300 kya Africa; global by ~15 kyaLanguage, art, agriculture, technologyCultural evolution — cumulative, faster than genes

    Out-of-Africa spread: Homo erectus left Africa ~1.8 mya; modern Homo sapiens left ~60–70 kya, reaching Australia (~50 kya), the Americas (~15 kya) and, in Nepal's case, settling every ecological zone from Terai to high Himalaya.

  • 4. The Four Grand Trends (the exam's conceptual core)

    1. Posture: quadrupedal → habitual bipedal → fully erect striding (S-shaped spine, bowl pelvis, angled femur, arched foot, big toe aligned).
    2. Hands: knuckle support → free precision grip → tool manufacture.
    3. Brain and face: ~450 cc → ~1400 cc; prognathous muzzle → flat face, chin, reduced canines, parabolic dental arch.
    4. Culture: none → simple tools → fire → language → art → agriculture → civilisation — cumulative and now faster than biological evolution.

    Feedback loop: bipedalism freed hands → hands made tools → tools selected for intelligence → intelligence improved tools and language → culture rewarded bigger brains.

  • 6. Exam Orientation

    6.1 Previous year questions

    No direct question exists in the authored bank for this exact topic (the 2024 stages question belongs to Topic 35). Questions below are NEB-style practice, clearly labelled.

    6.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Why did bipedalism evolve before large brains? Give two advantages of standing upright. Answer: Fossil evidence (Australopithecus, ~4 mya) shows upright walking with an ape-sized brain, so bipedalism came first. Advantages: (1) free hands for carrying food, tools and infants; (2) better vision over savanna grass for spotting predators and food; (3) energy-efficient long-distance travel between food patches; (4) reduced heat exposure in open country. Free hands then enabled tools, and tool use selected for larger brains — the sequence, not the reverse.

    Q2 (2m) — What role did fire play in human evolution? Answer: Fire (Homo erectus) cooked food — increasing digestible energy and shrinking guts and teeth — provided night warmth enabling spread into cold regions, hardened wooden spear points, and extended social hours around the hearth, feeding language and culture.

    Q3 (2m) — Why is cultural evolution said to have overtaken biological evolution? Answer: Cultural change (language, technology, medicine) accumulates and transmits in generations of years, not centuries; humans now adapt to environments mainly by culture — clothing, shelter, agriculture — while biological change requires many generations.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Reversing the order — brain before bipedalism is the classic error.
    2. Saying humans descended from chimpanzees — both descend from a common ancestor.
    3. Attributing fire to sapiens — Homo erectus controlled fire.
    4. Omitting the savanna/open-habitat logic that motivates the whole sequence.

Confusion

  • Anthropoid vs hominid: anthropoids = all higher primates (monkeys + apes + humans); hominids = great apes + humans; hominins = the human branch only.
  • Out of Africa (erectus) vs Out of Africa (sapiens): two expansions — Homo erectus ~1.8 mya; modern sapiens ~60–70 kya.

Practice

  • Could human-level intelligence have evolved without bipedalism?

    Answer. Almost certainly not in the form it took. Bipedalism freed the hands for manufacturing and carrying tools; tool use enabled meat eating and sharing; better nutrition fuelled the expensive brain; free hands and cooperative living permitted prolonged infant care. Each step of that feedback loop depends on the first one. Without freed hands there is no tool complex, no hunting cooperation and no selection for the large expensive brain — so human-level intelligence as we know it could not have followed the same path.

Universal Facts

  • Every non-African human population descends from a small group that left Africa roughly 60,000–70,000 years ago.

Examples

  • Savanna hypothesis — open-ground living selected for upright walking, tool use and meat eating.

Practice Questions

  • Q. What is the significance of the Laetoli footprints?

    Answer. Fossil footprints about 3.6 million years old from Australopithecus afarensis preserved in volcanic ash at Laetoli, Tanzania. They show an arched, bipedal gait — direct proof that upright walking evolved long before large brains and stone tools, confirming that bipedalism is the oldest hallmark of the human line.

Key Points

  • Split ~6–7 mya from a common ape-human ancestor as African forests shrank.
  • Sequence with drivers: Dryopithecus → Ramapithecus (ground feeding) → Australopithecus (bipedalism, savanna) → H. habilis (tools) → H. erectus (fire, out of Africa) → Neanderthal (burial, cold) → H. sapiens (language, agriculture, global spread).
  • Four trends: posture, hands, brain/face, culture — with the bipedalism→hands→tools→brain feedback loop.

Summary

Modern humans evolved from an anthropoid ape that left the shrinking Miocene forests for African savannas. Bipedalism came first — Australopithecus walked upright to scan the grass, free its hands and travel far — then hands made stone tools (Homo habilis), tools and hunting rewarded bigger brains, fire (Homo erectus) widened the diet and drove the first migration out of Africa, Neanderthals buried their dead in ice-age Europe, and Homo sapiens added language, art, agriculture and civilisation, spreading to every continent by ~15,000 years ago. Four trends — posture, hands, brain, culture — form a feedback loop in which each step enabled the next, until cultural evolution began to outrun biology itself.

1.

The human and chimpanzee lineages split about:

Options
  • 1 mya
  • 6–7 mya
  • 20 mya
  • 100 kya
Answer

B

Explanation

Genetic and fossil evidence place the ape-human split at ~6–7 million years.

2.

Bipedalism evolved primarily as an adaptation to:

Options
  • Forest canopy life
  • Open savanna ground life
  • Aquatic life
  • Cave life
Answer

B

Explanation

Open country rewarded upright posture — vigilance, carrying and efficient travel.

3.

The first migration of hominins out of Africa was by:

Options
  • Australopithecus
  • Homo erectus
  • Neanderthals
  • Homo habilis
Answer

B

Explanation

Homo erectus spread to Asia and Europe from ~1.8 mya (Java man, Peking man).

4.

Cooking food became possible with:

Options
  • Homo habilis
  • Homo erectus (fire)
  • Australopithecus
  • Ramapithecus
Answer

B

Explanation

Fire control by Homo erectus increased digestible energy and diet breadth.

5.

The correct order of acquisitions is:

Options
  • Brain → tools → bipedalism
  • Bipedalism → tools → large brain
  • Tools → bipedalism → brain
  • Speech → fire → bipedalism
Answer

B

Explanation

Walking upright came first, freeing hands for tools that then selected for larger brains.

6.

Modern Homo sapiens left Africa approximately:

Options
  • 1.8 mya
  • 500 kya
  • 60–70 kya
  • 5 kya
Answer

C

Explanation

The recent out-of-Africa expansion ~60–70 kya peopled the globe.

7.

Cultural evolution differs from biological evolution in being:

Options
  • Slower
  • Genetically inherited only
  • Cumulative and transmissible within a lifetime
  • Random
Answer

C

Explanation

Language and technology pass between individuals within generations — far faster than genes.
