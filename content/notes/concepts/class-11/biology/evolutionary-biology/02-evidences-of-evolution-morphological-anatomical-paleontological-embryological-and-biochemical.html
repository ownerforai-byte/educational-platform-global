Enriched Content

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Case for Evolution

    Evolution is not believed on authority; it is inferred from evidence — anatomical, fossil, embryological and biochemical. Each line of evidence is an independent witness, and they all agree: life shares common ancestry and has changed through time. NEB has asked this topic three years running (2021, 2022, 2024).

  • 2. The Five Lines of Evidence

    2.1 Morphological and anatomical evidences (NEB 2021, 2022, 2024)

    EvidenceDefinitionExampleWhat it shows
    Homologous organsSame basic structure and origin, different functionsForelimbs of human (grasping), whale (swimming), bat (flying), horse (running) — same bones: humerus, radius-ulna, carpals, digitsDivergent evolution from a common ancestor
    Analogous organsDifferent structures and origins, same functionWings of butterfly (chitin membrane) vs bird (feathered bone-limb); eyes of octopus vs mammalConvergent evolution — similar selection pressures
    Vestigial organsReduced, functionless remnants of useful ancestral organsHuman appendix, coccyx, wisdom teeth, nictitating membrane; whale pelvic bonesAncestors used them; structures persist as history
    Connecting linksOrganisms bridging two groupsArchaeopteryx (reptile–bird), Peripatus (annelid–arthropod), Euglena (plant–animal)Transition between groups

    Which is better evidence — NEB 2021: homologous organs are stronger evidence because shared structural plans (same bones, same embryonic origin) point to common ancestry — the core claim of evolution. Analogous organs show only that similar environments produce similar solutions (convergence), not descent.

    2.2 Paleontological evidence (NEB 2022, 2024)

    AspectDetail
    FossilAny preserved remains or traces of past life (bones, shells, impressions, footprints, amber, petrification)
    DatingRelative (stratigraphy — deeper is older); absolute (radioactive dating — C-14 for <50,000 yr; U-Pb, K-Ar for older rocks)
    SequenceSimple prokaryotes (3.5 bya) → eukaryotes → invertebrates → fish → amphibians → reptiles → birds/mammals → humans: complexity increases with time
    Transitional fossilsArchaeopteryx (reptile→bird: feathers + teeth/tail), Tiktaalik (fish→tetrapod), whale series (Pakicetus → Basilosaurus)
    SignificanceFossils are the direct historical record — they show life changing through time in the exact order evolution predicts

    2.3 Embryological evidence

    Early embryos of fish, salamander, chick and human are strikingly similar — gill pouches (pharyngeal arches), tail, notochord — diverging later (von Baer). Haeckel's "ontogeny recapitulates phylogeny" is overstated, but shared embryonic stages still reveal common ancestry (human gill-pouch homologs become jaw, ear bones, throat structures).

    2.4 Biochemical (molecular) evidence

    ComparisonFinding
    Universal moleculesSame DNA code, ATP, 20 amino acids, same central dogma in all life
    Protein/DNA similarityHuman vs chimpanzee cytochrome c: identical; human vs rhesus: 1 difference; human vs yeast: many — the molecular tree matches the fossil tree
    MeaningThe closer two species, the more alike their molecules — common ancestry written in sequences

    2.5 Biogeographical evidence

    Isolated regions evolve distinctive fauna/flora: marsupials radiated in Australia; Galápagos finches differ island by island (Darwin's observation); Nepal's altitude zones mirror latitude. Species are distributed by ancestry plus geography, not by environmental fit alone.

    4.2 Model answers

    Q1 (3m, 2021) — Homologous vs analogous + which is better evidence. Answer: Homologous organs share the same basic structure and embryonic origin but perform different functions — e.g. the forelimbs of a human, whale, bat and horse all contain humerus, radius–ulna and digits despite grasping, swimming, flying and running. Analogous organs have different structures and origins but perform the same function — e.g. the wings of a butterfly (chitinous membrane) and a bird (feathered forelimb) both fly. Homologous organs are the better evidence of evolution because their shared structural plan can only be explained by common ancestry (divergent evolution); analogous organs merely show that similar environments independently produce similar adaptations (convergent evolution).

    Q2 (3m, 2022 & 2024) — Evidences with one morphological and one paleontological example. Answer: Evolution is supported by morphological/anatomical evidence — homologous organs (forelimbs of human, whale, bat, horse: same bones, different functions → common ancestry), analogous organs, vestigial organs (human appendix) and connecting links — and by paleontological evidence — fossils, the preserved remains of past life, dated by stratigraphy and radioactive methods, showing simple prokaryotes before eukaryotes and invertebrates before vertebrates, with transitional forms such as Archaeopteryx (reptilian teeth and tail plus bird feathers) bridging reptiles and birds. Both lines independently confirm that life has descended with modification.

    Q3 (2m, 2024) — Homologous vs analogous with examples. Answer: Homologous — same structure and origin, different function (human arm vs whale flipper — same bones); evidence of divergent evolution from a common ancestor. Analogous — different structure and origin, same function (butterfly wing vs bird wing); evidence of convergent evolution under similar selection.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Calling analogous organs evidence of common ancestry — they show convergence, not descent.
    2. Swapping the definitions (homologous = same function) — check: homo = same structure.
    3. Naming vestigial organs that are actually functional (e.g. "tonsils" is debatable) — use appendix, coccyx, wisdom teeth.
    4. Giving fossils without the sequence logic — the point is order through time.
    5. Forgetting Archaeopteryx as the connecting-link example examiners expect.
Confusion
  • Homologous vs analogous: homologous = same bones, different jobs (divergence); analogous = different parts, same job (convergence). Memory: "Homo = same anatomy; Analog = same action."
  • Vestigial vs atavistic: vestigial = permanently reduced remnant (appendix); atavistic = rare reappearance of an ancestral trait (human tail).

Practice Questions

Formulas
  • Homologous: same structure, different function, common origin (divergent evolution)
  • Analogous: different structure, same function, different origin (convergent evolution)
  • Vestigial: reduced remnant of an ancestral organ
  • Molecular: DNA/protein similarity inversely related to evolutionary distance
Key Points
  • Morphological: homologous (forelimb of human/whale/bat/horse — divergent, common ancestry), analogous (butterfly vs bird wing — convergent), vestigial (appendix, coccyx), connecting links (Archaeopteryx, Peripatus).
  • Paleontological: fossils dated by stratigraphy and radioisotopes; sequence simple→complex; transitional fossils (Archaeopteryx, Tiktaalik).
  • Embryological: shared early embryos (gill pouches, tail). Biochemical: same genetic code; cytochrome c similarity tracks relatedness. Biogeographical: marsupials of Australia, Galápagos finches.
Summary

Evolution is inferred, not assumed. Morphology shows homologous organs — the same bones serving different lives in human, whale, bat and horse — proving divergent evolution from common ancestry; analogous wings of butterfly and bird show convergence instead; vestigial appendix and coccyx are inherited footnotes; Archaeopteryx bridges reptile and bird. Paleontology supplies the direct record: fossils dated by strata and radioisotopes appear in exactly the order evolution predicts, from stromatolites to Tiktaalik. Embryology shows fish, chick and human sharing gill pouches and tails; biochemistry finds the same genetic code everywhere, with cytochrome c diverging exactly as relatedness requires; biogeography scatters marsupials and finches along ancestral lines. Five witnesses, one verdict: descent with modification.

Original Content

Notes
  • Evidence of evolution: morphological, anatomical, paleontological (fossil), embryological, and biochemical/molecular.
  • Homologous organs (divergent evolution): similar structure, different function, common ancestry — e.g. forelimbs of whale, bat, human, horse.
  • Analogous organs (convergent evolution): similar function, different structure/origin — e.g. wings of bird vs insect; eyes of octopus vs vertebrate.
  • Vestigial organs: reduced, non-functional remnants of ancestral organs — e.g. appendix, coccyx, wings of flightless birds, pelvic bones in whales.
  • Fossil evidence: progressive increase in complexity over time; transitional fossils (e.g. Archaeopteryx: reptilian + bird features) link groups.
  • Embryological evidence: early embryos of different vertebrates are very similar (pharyngeal slits, tail), then diverge.
  • Biochemical evidence: universal genetic code, similar proteins (cytochrome c) across species; closer species share more similar DNA/proteins.
  • Molecular clock: rate of genetic mutation can estimate divergence time between species.
Confusion
  • Homologous = same origin, different function (divergent); Analogous = different origin, same function (convergent).
  • Vestigial organs are evidence of common ancestry, not of "uselessness" in an absolute sense.
  • Fossils are not just "old bones"; they include traces (footprints, burrows), impressions, and molecular fossils.
  • Archaeopteryx shows both reptilian (teeth, long bony tail, claws) and avian (feathers, wing) features — a transitional form.
  • Embryological similarity reflects common ancestry; the differences develop later as embryos mature.
  • A "universal genetic code" (same codons → same amino acids) across all life is strong evidence of common descent.

Universal Facts
  • Archaeopteryx lithographica (1861, Germany) is the classic bird-reptile transitional fossil.
  • Humans and chimpanzees share ~98.8% of their DNA.
  • The universal genetic code is nearly identical in all known organisms (a few exceptions exist).
  • Cytochrome c, a respiratory protein, is very similar in all aerobes, reflecting common ancestry.
Examples
  • Homologous: forelimbs of human (arm), bat (wing), whale (flipper), horse (leg) — same bone pattern, different use.
  • Analogous: wings of bird (feather, bone) vs insect (chitin, no bone) — both fly, different origin.
  • Vestigial: human appendix, coccyx (tailbone), wisdom teeth; whale pelvic bones.
  • Transitional: Archaeopteryx; Tiktaalik (fish-amphibian link); Australopithecus (human ancestor).
  • Molecular: cytochrome c in horse vs human differs by only a few amino acids.

  • Vestigial: reduced remnant of an ancestral organ

Key Points
  • Five types of evidence: morphological, anatomical, fossil, embryological, biochemical.
  • Homologous (divergent) vs analogous (convergent) organs.
  • Vestigial organs = reduced ancestral remnants.
  • Fossils show progressive complexity; transitional forms link groups.
  • Embryos of vertebrates are similar early, diverge later.
  • Universal genetic code + similar proteins (cytochrome c) = molecular evidence.
  • Molecular clock estimates divergence time.
Summary

Evolution is supported by multiple independent lines of evidence. Homologous organs (common origin, divergent function), analogous organs (convergent evolution), and vestigial structures reveal common ancestry. Fossils document the progressive increase in complexity and transitional forms like Archaeopteryx. Embryological similarity among vertebrates and molecular evidence (universal genetic code, similar proteins such as cytochrome c) provide the strongest modern confirmation that all life shares a common origin.

Practice

  • Why does the universal genetic code argue for a single origin of life?

    Answer. All life — bacteria, archaea, plants, animals — uses the same DNA code, the same 20 amino acids, ATP as the energy currency and the same central dogma. If life had originated independently several times, each lineage would be expected to use a different code; the chance of every origin adopting one identical code is astronomically small. The shared code is therefore a homology — an inheritance from one common ancestor (LUCA) — biochemical evidence that all life descends from a single origin.

They are historical evidence of descent with modification: the structure persists in reduced form because its function was lost.

Summary

Evolution is inferred, not assumed. Morphology shows homologous organs — the same bones serving different lives in human, whale, bat and horse — proving divergent evolution from common ancestry; analogous wings of butterfly and bird show convergence instead; vestigial appendix and coccyx are inherited footnotes; Archaeopteryx bridges reptile and bird. Paleontology supplies the direct record: fossils dated by strata and radioisotopes appear in exactly the order evolution predicts, from stromatolites to Tiktaalik. Embryology shows fish, chick and human sharing gill pouches and tails; biochemistry finds the same genetic code everywhere, with cytochrome c diverging exactly as relatedness requires; biogeography scatters marsupials and finches along ancestral lines. Five witnesses, one verdict: descent with modification.

Important Concepts

  • Convergent vs divergent evolution: homologous structures diverge from one ancestor; analogous structures converge from different ancestors — the same two words explain both sides of the comparison question.

Exercises

1.

Two species have very similar cytochrome c (differ by 0 amino acids). What does this suggest about their evolutionary relationship?

ID

1

Steps
  • Cytochrome c is a conserved respiratory protein
  • 0 differences = nearly identical sequence
  • Suggests very close (recent) common ancestry
Answer

Very close evolutionary relationship (recent common ancestor)
