Enriched Content

Notes
  • Evidence of evolution: morphological, anatomical, paleontological (fossil), embryological, and biochemical/molecular.
  • Homologous organs (divergent evolution): similar structure, different function, common ancestry — e.g. forelimbs of whale, bat, human, horse.
  • Analogous organs (convergent evolution): similar function, different structure/origin — e.g. wings of bird vs insect; eyes of octopus vs vertebrate.
  • Vestigial organs: reduced, non-functional remnants of ancestral organs — e.g. appendix, coccyx, wings of flightless birds, pelvic bones in whales.
  • Fossil evidence: progressive increase in complexity over time; transitional fossils (e.g. Archaeopteryx: reptilian + bird features) link groups.
  • Embryological evidence: early embryos of different vertebrates are very similar (pharyngeal slits, tail), then diverge.
  • Biochemical evidence: universal genetic code, similar proteins (cytochrome c) across species; closer species share more similar DNA/proteins.
  • Molecular clock: rate of genetic mutation can estimate divergence time between species.
Confusion
  • Homologous = same origin, different function (divergent); Analogous = different origin, same function (convergent).
  • Vestigial organs are evidence of common ancestry, not of "uselessness" in an absolute sense.
  • Fossils are not just "old bones"; they include traces (footprints, burrows), impressions, and molecular fossils.
  • Archaeopteryx shows both reptilian (teeth, long bony tail, claws) and avian (feathers, wing) features — a transitional form.
  • Embryological similarity reflects common ancestry; the differences develop later as embryos mature.
  • A "universal genetic code" (same codons → same amino acids) across all life is strong evidence of common descent.

  • Describe the embryological evidence for evolution in vertebrates.
  • Explain how protein/DNA similarity (cytochrome c) supports evolution.
Universal Facts
  • Archaeopteryx lithographica (1861, Germany) is the classic bird-reptile transitional fossil.
  • Humans and chimpanzees share ~98.8% of their DNA.
  • The universal genetic code is nearly identical in all known organisms (a few exceptions exist).
  • Cytochrome c, a respiratory protein, is very similar in all aerobes, reflecting common ancestry.
Examples
  • Homologous: forelimbs of human (arm), bat (wing), whale (flipper), horse (leg) — same bone pattern, different use.
  • Analogous: wings of bird (feather, bone) vs insect (chitin, no bone) — both fly, different origin.
  • Vestigial: human appendix, coccyx (tailbone), wisdom teeth; whale pelvic bones.
  • Transitional: Archaeopteryx; Tiktaalik (fish-amphibian link); Australopithecus (human ancestor).
  • Molecular: cytochrome c in horse vs human differs by only a few amino acids.

  • How does the molecular/cytoplasmic evidence (DNA, proteins) support evolution?
Formulas
  • Homologous: same structure, different function, common origin (divergent evolution)
  • Analogous: different structure, same function, different origin (convergent evolution)
  • Vestigial: reduced remnant of an ancestral organ
  • Molecular: DNA/protein similarity inversely related to evolutionary distance
Key Points
  • Five types of evidence: morphological, anatomical, fossil, embryological, biochemical.
  • Homologous (divergent) vs analogous (convergent) organs.
  • Vestigial organs = reduced ancestral remnants.
  • Fossils show progressive complexity; transitional forms link groups.
  • Embryos of vertebrates are similar early, diverge later.
  • Universal genetic code + similar proteins (cytochrome c) = molecular evidence.
  • Molecular clock estimates divergence time.
Summary

Evolution is supported by multiple independent lines of evidence. Homologous organs (common origin, divergent function), analogous organs (convergent evolution), and vestigial structures reveal common ancestry. Fossils document the progressive increase in complexity and transitional forms like Archaeopteryx. Embryological similarity among vertebrates and molecular evidence (universal genetic code, similar proteins such as cytochrome c) provide the strongest modern confirmation that all life shares a common origin.

Special Notes
  • NEB frequently asks for homologous vs analogous organs with examples.
  • Vestigial organs in humans (appendix, coccyx) are a common short-answer.
  • Archaeopteryx as a transitional fossil is a very frequent exam topic.
  • Molecular evidence (DNA similarity, cytochrome c) is increasingly tested.
Important Statements
  • Homologous structures indicate common descent with divergent evolution.
  • Analogous structures result from convergent evolution under similar selection pressures.
  • Vestigial organs are remnants of structures that were functional in ancestors.
  • The universal genetic code is powerful evidence for a single common ancestor of all life.
Important Notes
  • Memorise 2 homologous and 2 analogous examples.
  • List 3 vestigial organs in humans.
  • Learn the features of Archaeopteryx (reptilian + avian).
  • Explain the embryological similarity of vertebrates.
  • Describe molecular evidence (cytochrome c, DNA similarity).
Exam Short Tricks
  • Homo = same origin; Ana = same function.
  • Vestigial = "Vest" (a leftover vest of evolution).
  • Archaeopteryx = Archae (ancient) + pteryx (wing) = ancient bird.
  • DNA similarity: closer species = more similar DNA.
