  • 1. Topic Introduction — Reading the Primate Family Album

    The primate order is a family album: prosimians, two monkey lineages separated by 40 million years of ocean, the tailless apes, and us. Comparing new world monkeys, old world monkeys, apes and man sharpens the differences that define each grade — and is a listed syllabus topic in its own right.

  • 5. Exam Orientation

    5.1 Previous year questions

    No new-world/old-world monkey question exists in the authored bank. Questions below are NEB-style practice, clearly labelled.

    5.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Distinguish new world from old world monkeys. Answer: (1) Distribution: new world monkeys live in Central/South America; old world in Africa/Asia. (2) Nose: flat, wide-septum side-facing nostrils (platyrrhine) vs narrow downward nostrils (catarrhine). (3) Tail: long and often prehensile (spider monkey) vs shorter and never prehensile, with ischial callosities and cheek pouches in old world forms. (4) Thumb: less opposable in new world; fully opposable in old world monkeys.

    Q2 (2m) — Two differences between apes and man. Answer: (1) Man is fully erect and bipedal with legs longer than arms; apes are semi-erect knuckle-walkers with arms longer than legs. (2) Man's brain (~1400 cc) and speech far exceed the ape's (~400–500 cc, calls only).

    Q3 (2m) — Why is the gibbon called a lesser ape? Answer: Gibbons (Hylobatidae) are small, tailless, expert brachiators, but differ from great apes (Hominidae — orangutan, gorilla, chimpanzee) in size, posture and lack of knuckle-walking; hence "lesser ape".

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Giving apes tails — apes have no tails; that is the monkey–ape divider.
    2. Saying new world monkeys lack tails — they have the most specialised (prehensile) tails.
    3. Confusing platyrrhine/catarrhine noses.
    4. Placing gibbons among great apes.

Confusion

  • Prehensile vs non-prehensile tail: prehensile tails grasp branches (new world spiders/howlers); old world tails only balance.
  • Monkey vs ape: the surest single test is the tail — monkeys have one, apes do not.

Key Points

  • New world (platyrrhine): S. America, flat side nostrils, prehensile tail, less opposable thumb, no cheek pouches — spider, howler, marmoset.
  • Old world (catarrhine): Africa/Asia, downward nostrils, non-prehensile tail, opposable thumb, cheek pouches, ischial callosities — macaque, langur, baboon.
  • Apes: tailless, arms > legs, knuckle-walk, 400–500 cc — gibbon (lesser), orangutan, gorilla, chimp (great).
  • Man: bipedal, legs > arms, ~1400 cc, speech, chin, culture.

Important Statements

  • "From sideways nostrils to downward, from grasping tail to grasping thumb, from knuckles to feet — the primate ladder."

Examples

  • Spider monkey — new world prehensile-tail specialist.
  • Rhesus macaque — old world monkey; the classical lab primate.

Universal Facts

  • Old world monkeys and apes share the catarrhine nose — humans inherited it too.

Formulas

  • New World: sideways nostrils + 2.1.3.3 + tail often prehensile.
  • Old World monkey: downward nostrils + 2.1.2.3 + tail not prehensile + ischial callosities.
  • Ape: no tail + long arms. Human: no tail + bipedal skeleton.

Practice

  • Why did prehensile tails evolve in America but not Africa?

    Answer. New World monkeys (platyrrhines) reached South America early — probably by rafting across the Atlantic ~35–40 mya — into dense arboreal forest with little competition. There a grasping tail compensated for their only partially opposable thumb, becoming a fifth hand for spider and howler monkeys. Old World anthropoids evolved fully opposable thumbs, larger bodies and semi-terrestrial habits instead, which made a grasping tail unnecessary; the catarrhine line therefore kept a non-prehensile (or reduced) tail and invested in precision grip and ground living.

Practice Questions

  • Q. Name the four great apes.

    Answer. Orangutan (Asia), gorilla (Africa), chimpanzee (Africa) and bonobo (Africa) — all family Hominidae, our closest living relatives. The gibbon is a lesser ape, not a great ape.

Exercises

1.

Identify a spider monkey, a rhesus macaque, a chimpanzee and a human from one character each.

Steps
  • The spider monkey has sideways nostrils and a grasping tail, so it is a New World monkey.
  • The rhesus macaque has downward nostrils, cheek pouches and sitting pads, so it is an Old World monkey.
  • The chimpanzee has no external tail and arms longer than its legs, so it is an ape.
  • The human has no tail and a bipedal skeleton, with the foramen magnum under the skull.
Answer

Spider monkey New World, rhesus Old World, chimpanzee ape, human bipedal.

Summary

The primate album sorts into grades. New world monkeys — spider, howler, marmoset — live in the Americas with flat sideways nostrils and, uniquely, prehensile tails. Old world monkeys — macaque, langur, baboon — have downward nostrils, opposable thumbs, cheek pouches and sitting pads, but never grasping tails. Apes lose the tail entirely: the small brachiating gibbon (lesser ape) and the great apes — orangutan, gorilla, chimpanzee — with long arms and knuckle-walking. Man completes the grade: legs longer than arms, fully erect bipedalism, a 1400 cc brain, speech and cumulative culture. Tail, nose, thumb and posture — four characters tell the whole story.
