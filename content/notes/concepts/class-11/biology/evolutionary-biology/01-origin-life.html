Options

  • Cell division
  • Spontaneous generation
Answer

B

Visual Type

origin-of-life-experiment

Enriched Content

Notes
  • Origin of life: life arose from non-living matter by chemical evolution (abiogenesis) in a warm dilution (primordial ocean).
  • Oparin-Haldane theory (1924/29): the early reducing atmosphere (CH4, NH3, H2O, H2, no O2) allowed formation of organic molecules from inorganic ones.
  • Stages: (1) formation of simple organics (amino acids, nucleotides) → (2) polymers (proteins, nucleic acids) → (3) coacervates/microspheres (proto-cells) → (4) self-replicating systems.
  • Miller-Urey experiment (1953): simulated early Earth (electric sparks in CH4, NH3, H2, H2O vapour); produced amino acids (glycine, alanine) and other organics → supports chemical evolution.
  • RNA world hypothesis: self-replicating RNA (ribozymes) may have preceded DNA-protein life.
  • Evidence for chemical evolution: laboratory synthesis of organics; presence of organics in meteorites (Murchison); deep-sea hydrothermal vents as energy sources.
  • Significance: explains the transition from chemistry to biology; basis of origin-of-life studies.
Confusion
  • Abiogenesis ≠ spontaneous generation (spontaneous generation is the disproved idea that life arises from life daily; abiogenesis is the origin of life from non-life in primitive conditions).
  • Miller-Urey proved organics CAN form from inorganics under early-Earth-like conditions; it did NOT prove life formed.
  • The early atmosphere was reducing (no free O2); free oxygen came later from photosynthetic cyanobacteria.
  • Oparin (Russia) and Haldane (UK) independently proposed similar chemical-evolution theories.
  • Coacervates (Oparin) and proteinoid microspheres (Fox) are models of proto-cells, not proven living cells.
  • The RNA world is a hypothesis; it explains how self-replication and catalysis could coexist before DNA and proteins.
Practice
  • Describe the Oparin-Haldane theory of the origin of life.
  • Explain the Miller-Urey experiment (setup, conditions, results, significance).
  • List the stages of chemical evolution.
  • Explain the RNA world hypothesis.
  • Differentiate abiogenesis from spontaneous generation.
Universal Facts
  • Life on Earth is estimated to be ~3.5–4 billion years old.
  • The Miller-Urey apparatus produced amino acids in just one week.
  • The Murchison meteorite (1969, Australia) contained over 70 amino acids, some not found on Earth.
  • Deep-sea hydrothermal vents may have provided the energy and chemistry for early life.
Examples
  • Miller-Urey (1953): electric discharge in CH4/NH3/H2O/H2 → glycine, alanine, aspartic acid.
  • Oparin's coacervates: protein solutions form droplets that can take up substances from the environment.
  • RNA ribozymes (e.g., self-splicing introns) demonstrate RNA can catalyse its own replication.
  • Hydrothermal vent microbes (chemoautotrophs) suggest life's earliest energy source may have been chemical, not solar.
Practice Questions
  • Explain the Oparin-Haldane theory of the origin of life.
  • Describe the Miller-Urey experiment and its significance.
  • What is the RNA world hypothesis?
  • Differentiate abiogenesis from spontaneous generation.
  • List three lines of evidence supporting chemical evolution.
Formulas
  • Chemical evolution: Inorganic (CH4, NH3, H2O, H2) → Simple organics (amino acids, sugars, bases) → Polymers → Proto-cells → Life
  • Miller-Urey: electric sparks (lightning) + reducing atmosphere → amino acids
Key Points
  • Oparin-Haldane: reducing early atmosphere allowed chemical evolution.
  • Stages: simple organics → polymers → coacervates → self-replicating systems.
  • Miller-Urey (1953): produced amino acids from inorganic precursors.
  • RNA world: self-replicating, catalytic RNA may predate DNA/protein life.
  • Abiogenesis (origin of life) ≠ spontaneous generation (disproved).
  • Evidence: lab synthesis, meteorites, hydrothermal vents.
Summary

The origin of life is explained by chemical evolution (abiogenesis). The Oparin-Haldane theory proposed that a reducing early atmosphere allowed inorganic molecules to form simple organics, then polymers, then proto-cells, then self-replicating life. The Miller-Urey experiment (1953) demonstrated that amino acids can form from inorganic precursors under early-Earth conditions. The RNA world hypothesis suggests self-replicating RNA preceded the DNA-protein world. Evidence from meteorites and hydrothermal vents supports chemical evolution.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects the Miller-Urey experiment (setup + results + significance) as a standard question.
  • The Oparin-Haldane theory and its 4 stages are a frequent long-answer.
  • Differentiate abiogenesis from spontaneous generation — a common "trick" question.
  • Mention the RNA world as a modern hypothesis for early life.
Important Statements
  • Life arose from non-living matter through gradual chemical evolution.
  • The Miller-Urey experiment provided experimental support for chemical evolution.
  • The early Earth atmosphere was reducing and oxygen-free.
  • Self-replicating molecules (likely RNA) were the key step toward life.
Important Notes
  • Memorise the 4 stages of chemical evolution.
  • Learn the Miller-Urey experiment setup and results.
  • Explain the Oparin-Haldane theory.
  • Differentiate abiogenesis from spontaneous generation.
  • State the RNA world hypothesis with one example.
Exam Short Tricks
  • Oparin-Haldane = O-H (Organics from Heat).
  • Miller-Urey = M-U (Made Urea... no — Made amino acids from Urey's setup).
  • Reducing atmosphere = no O2 = CH4, NH3, H2, H2O.
  • RNA world = RNA did BOTH replication AND catalysis.
Exam Notes
  • Explain the Oparin-Haldane theory with 4 stages.
  • Describe the Miller-Urey experiment (setup, conditions, results).
  • What is the RNA world hypothesis?
  • Differentiate abiogenesis from spontaneous generation.
  • List evidence supporting chemical evolution.
