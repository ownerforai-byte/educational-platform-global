Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — How the First Cell Arose

    Life did not appear fully formed: chemical evolution on the early Earth assembled molecules, then self-replicating systems, then cells. The Oparin–Haldane hypothesis predicted the recipe; the Miller–Urey experiment (NEB 2022) cooked it in glass — producing amino acids from a simulated primordial atmosphere in a week.

  • 2. The Early Earth and the Setting for Life

    ConditionEarly Earth (~4.0–3.5 bya)
    AtmosphereReducing — CH₄, NH₃, H₂, H₂O vapour (little or no free O₂)
    Energy sourcesUV radiation, lightning, volcanic heat, cosmic rays
    TemperatureHot; extensive water vapour condensing into oceans
    Oceans"Primordial soup" — accumulating organic molecules

  • 3. The Oparin–Haldane Hypothesis (1920s)

    A. I. Oparin (Russia) and J. B. S. Haldane (Britain) independently proposed chemical evolution:

    1. Simple inorganic molecules (CH₄, NH₃, H₂O, H₂) in the reducing atmosphere reacted under UV and lightning.
    2. Simple organic molecules formed — amino acids, sugars, fatty acids, nitrogen bases — accumulating in the oceans ("hot dilute soup" / primordial soup).
    3. Macromolecules — proteins, nucleic acids — polymerised (possibly on clay surfaces).
    4. Coacervates/protobionts: protein-lipid droplets with internal chemistry, bounded from the environment — precursors of cells.
    5. First self-replicating systems: RNA-first world (RNA can store information and catalyse — ribozymes), then DNA–protein systems.
    6. First cells: membrane-bounded, anaerobic heterotrophs feeding on the soup; later photosynthesis (cyanobacteria) added O₂ — transforming the atmosphere and enabling aerobic life.

    Why the reducing atmosphere mattered: without free O₂, organic molecules would not be immediately oxidised — they could accumulate for millions of years.

  • 4. The Miller–Urey Experiment (1953) — NEB 2022

    AspectDetail
    ApparatusSealed glass loop: lower flask (ocean, water heated) → upper flask (atmosphere) → electrodes (lightning) → condenser (rain) → back to ocean flask
    Gases usedCH₄, NH₃, H₂ and water vapour (H₂O) — the Oparin reducing atmosphere
    EnergyContinuous electric discharge between electrodes (simulated lightning)
    DurationAbout one week
    Products obtainedAmino acids (glycine, alanine, aspartic, glutamic acid), plus hydroxy acids, formic/acetic acid, and other organic compounds — sugars and nucleotide precursors in later variants
    SignificanceFirst laboratory demonstration that life's building blocks form abiotically under plausible early-Earth conditions — direct experimental support for Oparin–Haldane chemical evolution

    Limitations: did not create life; only simple organics; modern models argue for a more neutral early atmosphere (CO₂, N₂) — later Miller-type runs with such mixtures still yield amino acids, though less abundantly. Alternative sites (deep-sea hydrothermal vents, meteorite delivery — amino acids found in carbonaceous meteorites) supplement the picture.

    7.2 Model answer

    Q (3m) — Miller–Urey experiment: chemicals, products, significance. Answer: In 1953 Stanley Miller, guided by Harold Urey, circulated methane (CH₄), ammonia (NH₃), hydrogen (H₂) and water vapour — a model of the early reducing atmosphere — in a sealed apparatus, subjecting the gases to continuous electric discharge (simulated lightning) for about a week, with condensation returning the "rain" to the boiling "ocean". Products: the water analysis revealed amino acids (glycine, alanine, aspartic and glutamic acids) together with several simple organic acids — the building blocks of proteins. Significance: the experiment provided the first laboratory evidence that the organic molecules of life can form abiotically under plausible early-Earth conditions, directly supporting the Oparin–Haldane hypothesis of chemical evolution.

    7.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Writing oxygen in the gas mixture — the early atmosphere was reducing (no free O₂).
    2. Saying the experiment "created life" — it created organic building blocks, not cells.
    3. Omitting the electric discharge or the condenser (rain) in the apparatus description.
    4. Naming only "amino acids" without examples — glycine and alanine earn precision marks.
    5. Attributing the experiment to Oparin or Haldane — they proposed the hypothesis; Miller and Urey tested it.
Confusion
  • Coacervate vs cell: coacervate = protein-lipid droplet with some internal chemistry but no true heredity; a cell has a genome and division.
  • Chemical vs biological evolution: chemical evolution built the first molecules/cells; biological evolution (natural selection) acts on replicating organisms afterwards.

Summary

Life's origin is a chemistry story. Oparin and Haldane proposed that a reducing early atmosphere — methane, ammonia, hydrogen and water vapour — energised by lightning and ultraviolet light, cooked simple organics into the oceans' primordial soup, where macromolecules, coacervate droplets and finally self-replicating RNA systems gave rise to the first anaerobic cells. In 1953 Miller and Urey tested the idea: circulating those exact gases past electric sparks for a week produced glycine, alanine and other amino acids — the first laboratory proof that life's building blocks arise without life. Cyanobacteria then oxygenated the air, and biological evolution took over from chemical evolution.

Notes
  • Origin of life: life arose from non-living matter by chemical evolution (abiogenesis) in a warm dilution (primordial ocean).
  • Oparin-Haldane theory (1924/29): the early reducing atmosphere (CH4, NH3, H2O, H2, no O2) allowed formation of organic molecules from inorganic ones.

Confusion
  • Abiogenesis ≠ spontaneous generation (spontaneous generation is the disproved idea that life arises from life daily; abiogenesis is the origin of life from non-life in primitive conditions).
  • Miller-Urey proved organics CAN form from inorganics under early-Earth-like conditions; it did NOT prove life formed.
  • The early atmosphere was reducing (no free O2); free oxygen came later from photosynthetic cyanobacteria.
  • Oparin (Russia) and Haldane (UK) independently proposed similar chemical-evolution theories.
  • Coacervates (Oparin) and proteinoid microspheres (Fox) are models of proto-cells, not proven living cells.
  • The RNA world is a hypothesis; it explains how self-replication and catalysis could coexist before DNA and proteins.

  • Why would an oxygen-rich atmosphere have prevented the primordial soup?

    Answer. Oxygen is strongly oxidising: it would attack and break down reduced organic molecules (amino acids, sugars) about as fast as they formed, so nothing could accumulate. Free O₂ appeared only later, after cyanobacterial photosynthesis drove the Great Oxidation Event (~2.5 bya).

Oparin and Haldane proposed that a reducing early atmosphere — methane, ammonia, hydrogen and water vapour — energised by lightning and ultraviolet light, cooked simple organics into the oceans' primordial soup, where macromolecules, coacervate droplets and finally self-replicating RNA systems gave rise to the first anaerobic cells. In 1953 Miller and Urey tested the idea: circulating those exact gases past electric sparks for a week produced glycine, alanine and other amino acids — the first laboratory proof that life's building blocks arise without life. Cyanobacteria then oxygenated the air, and biological evolution took over from chemical evolution.

Why was a reducing (oxygen-free) atmosphere important for the formation of organic molecules?

ID

1

Steps

  • It would break down newly formed organics
  • Reducing conditions allow stable organics to form

Oxygen would oxidise and destroy organic molecules; reducing conditions allowed them to accumulate
