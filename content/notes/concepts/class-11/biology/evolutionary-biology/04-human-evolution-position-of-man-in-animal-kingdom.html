  • 1. Topic Introduction — Where Do We Sit in the Tree of Life?

    Humans are animals — mammals, primates — distinguished not by separate creation but by a few decisive evolutionary acquisitions: upright walking, free hands, a large brain, language and culture. This topic places Homo sapiens in the animal kingdom and traces the hominid line from ape ancestors.

    6.2 Model answer

    Q (3m) — Stages of human evolution from Dryopithecus to Homo sapiens. Answer: (1) Dryopithecus (~20–14 mya) — the earliest ape-like ancestor of the human line, a forest quadruped; (2) Ramapithecus (~14–10 mya) — heavy jaws and teeth, earliest proposed hominid; (3) Australopithecus (~4–1 mya, "Lucy") — the first habitual biped with a small brain (~450–500 cc) and simple tools; (4) Homo habilis (~2.4–1.4 mya) — the "handy man", first stone tools, brain ~650–750 cc; (5) Homo erectus (~1.8 mya–300 kya, Java and Peking man) — fully erect, brain ~900–1100 cc, controlled fire and hand-axes; (6) Homo neanderthalensis (~400–40 kya) — brain as large as ours, fine flake tools, burial of the dead; (7) Homo sapiens — modern humans with ~1400 cc brain, speech, art, agriculture and civilisation. The major trends: bipedalism first, then tools, then brain and culture.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Putting bipedalism after big brain — walking upright came first (Australopithecus).
    2. Writing Neanderthals as our direct ancestors — they are a sister lineage (we share ancestry, plus some interbreeding).
    3. Confusing Dryopithecus (ape ancestor) with Ramapithecus (first hominid candidate).
    4. Giving brain sizes backwards — brains grew through the sequence, not from the start.
    5. Omitting cultural milestones (fire, tools, agriculture) that examiners expect in stage answers.

Confusion

  • Hominid vs hominin: hominid = the great-ape family (gorillas, chimp, humans); hominin = humans and our ancestors after splitting from chimp lineage.
  • Australopithecus vs Homo: Australopithecus walked upright but had ape-sized brains and made no stone tools; Homo shows larger brain + tools.

Practice

  • Why did bipedalism evolve before large brains?

    Answer. The fossil sequence proves it: Australopithecus (4–1 mya) was already a habitual biped with a small ~450–500 cc brain, while large brains appear only with early Homo (~2 mya). Bipedalism came first because it paid immediately — it freed the hands for carrying food and infants, made savanna travel energy-efficient and raised predator vigilance. Free hands then allowed tool-making; tools and meat feeding improved nutrition; better nutrition and cooperative care permitted brain growth. The feedback loop runs posture → hands → tools → brain, never the reverse.

Universal Facts

  • Modern humans and Neanderthals interbred — most non-African humans carry 1–2% Neanderthal DNA.

Examples

  • Lucy (A. afarensis, 3.2 mya) — the famous bipedal skeleton proving walking came before big brains.

Practice Questions

  • Q. Give two differences between man and apes.

    Answer. 1. Posture and locomotion — man is fully erect and bipedal with an S-shaped spine; apes knuckle-walk with semi-erect posture.

    1. Brain and language — man's brain is ~1,350–1,450 cc with developed speech areas and true grammatical language; the ape brain is ~400–500 cc with only calls and gestures.

    (Also acceptable: free hands with a fully opposable thumb and precision grip in man; prognathous muzzle vs flat face with chin.)

Key Points

  • Position: Animalia → Chordata → Mammalia → Primates → Hominidae → Homo sapiens.
  • Stages: Dryopithecus (20–14 mya ape ancestor) → Ramapithecus (14–10 mya) → Australopithecus (4–1 mya, first biped, Lucy) → Homo habilis (tools, 650–750 cc) → Homo erectus (fire, 900–1100 cc) → Neanderthal (burial, ~1400 cc) → Homo sapiens (~1400 cc, culture).
  • Trends: bipedalism → free hands → tools → brain → language → cultural evolution.

Summary

Humans are primates among primates: Animalia, Chordata, Mammalia, order Primates, family Hominidae, species Homo sapiens. Our line left the apes through a sequence of fossils: Dryopithecus, the Miocene ape ancestor; Ramapithecus with heavy hominid jaws; Australopithecus — Lucy — walking upright with a small brain; Homo habilis with the first stone tools; Homo erectus taming fire; Neanderthals burying their dead with brains as large as ours; and Homo sapiens, whose ~1400 cc brain, language, art, agriculture and civilisation made culture the fastest-evolving organ of the species. The order matters: walking came first, tools second, big brains third — each freeing or rewarding the next.

Notes
  • Position of humans: Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Mammalia, Order Primates, Family Hominidae, Genus Homo, Species Homo sapiens.
  • Primates: grasping hands, forward-facing eyes, large brain; include prosimii (lemurs), monkeys, apes, and humans.
  • New World vs Old World monkeys: New World (Platyrrhini — flat nose, New World); Old World (Cercopithecinae — narrow nose, Old World).
  • Apes (no tail): gorilla, chimpanzee, orangutan, gibbon; humans are great apes (hominids).
  • Human evolution: Australopithecus (bipedal, small brain) → Homo habilis (tool use) → Homo erectus (fire, larger brain) → Homo neanderthalensis → Homo sapiens.
  • Key adaptations: bipedalism, enlarged brain, loss of body hair, development of language and culture.
  • Fossil evidence: Lucy (Australopithecus afarensis, Ethiopia, 1974); Homo habilis (Olduvai Gorge); Neanderthals (Europe/Asia).
  • Molecular evidence: human-chimpanzee DNA similarity (~98.8%) and shared mitochondrial Eve; both lineages diverged ~6–7 million years ago.
Confusion
  • Humans are NOT apes; we are hominids (great apes) within the family Hominidae, but a distinct lineage.
  • Australopithecus was bipedal but had a small brain (~400–500 mL); the brain enlarged later in Homo.
  • Homo habilis (the "handy man") made stone tools; Homo erectus (the "upright man") controlled fire and left Africa.
  • Neanderthals were NOT primitive; they made tools, buried dead, and had larger brains on average than modern humans.
  • Bipedalism (upright walking) evolved BEFORE the large brain — a key sequence in human evolution.
  • Humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor (~6–7 Ma); we did not evolve "from" chimpanzees.

  • Key adaptations: bipedalism, encephalisation, language, culture
Key Points
  • Humans: Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Mammalia, Order Primates, Family Hominidae.
  • Primates: grasping hands, forward eyes, large brain.
  • New World (Platyrrhini) vs Old World (Cercopithecinae) monkeys.
  • Apes (no tail): gorilla, chimpanzee, orangutan, gibbon, humans (hominids).
  • Human evolution: bipedalism → tool use → fire → brain enlargement → language.
  • Fossil evidence: Lucy (Australopithecus), Homo habilis, Homo erectus, Neanderthals.
  • Molecular: ~98.8% DNA similarity with chimpanzees; divergence ~6–7 Ma.
Summary

Humans (Homo sapiens) are primates in the family Hominidae. Evolution proceeded from bipedal Australopithecus through tool-using Homo habilis and fire-using Homo erectus to Neanderthals and modern Homo sapiens. Key adaptations include bipedalism, brain enlargement, loss of body hair, and the development of language and culture. Fossil evidence (Lucy, Homo habilis, Neanderthals) and molecular evidence (~98.8% DNA similarity with chimpanzees) confirm that humans share a common ancestor with other primates ~6–7 million years ago.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects the taxonomic position of humans and a clear evolutionary sequence.
  • Differentiate New World vs Old World monkeys and apes from monkeys (tail).
  • Bipedalism BEFORE encephalisation is a key exam point.
  • Neanderthals were NOT "primitive"; they had large brains and made tools.
Important Statements
  • Humans are primates in the family Hominidae.
  • Bipedalism evolved before the large brain in human ancestors.
  • The sequence Australopithecus → H. habilis → H. erectus → H. sapiens is well supported by fossils.
  • Humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor ~6–7 million years ago.
