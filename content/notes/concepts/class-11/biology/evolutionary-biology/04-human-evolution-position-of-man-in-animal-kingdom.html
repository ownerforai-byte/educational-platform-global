Notes

  • Position of humans: Kingdom Animalia, Phylum Chordata, Class Mammalia, Order Primates, Family Hominidae, Genus Homo, Species Homo sapiens.
  • Primates: grasping hands, forward-facing eyes, large brain; include prosimii (lemurs), monkeys, apes, and humans.
  • New World vs Old World monkeys: New World (Platyrrhini — flat nose, New World); Old World (Cercopithecinae — narrow nose, Old World).
  • Apes (no tail): gorilla, chimpanzee, orangutan, gibbon; humans are great apes (hominids).
  • Human evolution: Australopithecus (bipedal, small brain) → Homo habilis (tool use) → Homo erectus (fire, larger brain) → Homo neanderthalensis → Homo sapiens.

  • Fossil evidence: Lucy (Australopithecus afarensis, Ethiopia, 1974); Homo habilis (Olduvai Gorge); Neanderthals (Europe/Asia).
  • Molecular evidence: human-chimpanzee DNA similarity (~98.8%) and shared mitochondrial Eve; both lineages diverged ~6–7 million years ago.

Confusion

  • Humans are NOT apes; we are hominids (great apes) within the family Hominidae, but a distinct lineage.
  • Australopithecus was bipedal but had a small brain (~400–500 mL); the brain enlarged later in Homo.
  • Homo habilis (the "handy man") made stone tools; Homo erectus (the "upright man") controlled fire and left Africa.
  • Neanderthals were NOT primitive; they made tools, buried dead, and had larger brains on average than modern humans.
  • Bipedalism (upright walking) evolved BEFORE the large brain — a key sequence in human evolution.
  • Humans and chimpanzees share a common ancestor (~6–7 Ma); we did not evolve "from" chimpanzees.

Summary

Humans (Homo sapiens) are primates in the family Hominidae. Evolution proceeded from bipedal Australopithecus through tool-using Homo habilis and fire-using Homo erectus to Neanderthals and modern Homo sapiens. Key adaptations include bipedalism, brain enlargement, loss of body hair, and the development of language and culture. Fossil evidence (Lucy, Homo habilis, Neanderthals) and molecular evidence (~98.8% DNA similarity with chimpanzees) confirm that humans share a common ancestor with other primates ~6–7 million years ago.
