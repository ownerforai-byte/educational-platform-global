Enriched Content

Notes
  • Lamarckism (Lamarck, 1809): "use and disuse" of organs + "inheritance of acquired characteristics"; organisms change during life and pass on acquired traits. Mostly rejected (no mechanism for heredity).
  • Darwinism (Darwin, 1859): natural selection; variation exists in populations; overproduction leads to struggle for existence; organisms better adapted survive and reproduce (survival of the fittest).
  • Neo-Darwinism (synthetic theory, early 20th c.): integrates Mendelian genetics with natural selection; evolution = change in allele frequencies in a population; mechanisms: mutation, recombination, natural selection, genetic drift, gene flow.
  • Population genetics: Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (p² + 2pq + q² = 1); evolution occurs when allele frequencies change.
  • Evidences supporting modern synthesis: observed selection (peppered moth, antibiotic resistance), genetics (mutations, recombination), and molecular data.
  • Key concept: evolution acts on populations, not individuals; individuals adapt within a generation, but evolution is change across generations.
Confusion
  • Lamarck's "inheritance of acquired characteristics" is largely rejected: a muscle built by exercise is NOT passed to offspring.
  • Darwin did NOT know about genes; his mechanism (natural selection) was later explained by genetics (Neo-Darwinism).
  • Natural selection acts on VARIATION in a population; it does not create new traits (mutations create variation).
  • Genetic drift is a random change in allele frequency (important in small populations); natural selection is NON-random (directed by fitness).
  • Neo-Darwinism = modern synthesis = genetics + natural selection + drift + gene flow + recombination.
  • Evolution is NOT goal-oriented; there is no "progress" toward a goal, only adaptation to current environments.
Practice
  • Explain Lamarck's theory with an example and state why it is rejected.
  • Describe Darwin's theory of natural selection (postulates).
  • Explain the modern synthesis (Neo-Darwinism).
  • Differentiate natural selection from genetic drift.
  • State the Hardy-Weinberg equation and its significance.
Universal Facts
  • Darwin's 1859 book "On the Origin of Species" laid out natural selection.
  • Lamarck published "Philosophie Zoologique" (1809), proposing inheritance of acquired traits.
  • The "modern synthesis" (Neo-Darwinism) was consolidated in the 1930s–40s.
  • Antibiotic resistance in bacteria is a direct, observable example of natural selection.
Examples
  • Lamarck: giraffes stretched necks → passed longer necks (rejected).
  • Darwin: peppered moth (Biston betularia) — industrial melanism (dark form favoured post-industrial).
  • Antibiotic resistance: a few resistant bacteria survive → become the population (selection).
  • Genetic drift: founder effect in island populations (random allele change).
  • Neo-Darwinism: changes in allele frequencies (e.g., sickle-cell trait in malaria regions).
Practice Questions
  • Explain Lamarck's theory and why it is largely rejected.
  • Describe the postulates of Darwin's theory of natural selection.
  • What is Neo-Darwinism (the modern synthesis)?
  • Differentiate natural selection from genetic drift.
  • State the Hardy-Weinberg principle and the conditions for equilibrium.
Formulas
  • Hardy-Weinberg: p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (p = dominant allele freq, q = recessive)
  • Natural selection: variation → struggle → survival of fittest → adaptation
  • Evolution (modern): change in allele frequencies in a population over generations
Key Points
  • Lamarckism: use/disuse + inheritance of acquired traits (rejected).
  • Darwinism: natural selection; variation, overproduction, survival of fittest.
  • Neo-Darwinism: genetics + selection + drift + gene flow + recombination.
  • Evolution = change in allele frequencies in a population.
  • Natural selection is non-random; genetic drift is random.
  • Hardy-Weinberg: p² + 2pq + q² = 1 (equilibrium when no evolution).
Summary

Theories of evolution progressed from Lamarck's inheritance of acquired traits (rejected) to Darwin's natural selection (variation, struggle, survival of the fittest) to Neo-Darwinism (modern synthesis), which integrates Mendelian genetics with selection, genetic drift, gene flow, and recombination. In modern terms, evolution is the change in allele frequencies in a population, governed by the Hardy-Weinberg principle. Observable examples (peppered moth, antibiotic resistance) confirm these mechanisms.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects a clear Lamarck vs Darwin comparison.
  • The modern synthesis (Neo-Darwinism) is the "correct" current theory; know its components.
  • Natural selection vs genetic drift is a frequent short-answer.
  • Use antibiotic resistance as a real-world example of selection.
Important Statements
  • Lamarck's inheritance of acquired characteristics lacks a hereditary mechanism and is largely rejected.
  • Darwin's natural selection explains adaptation through variation and differential survival.
  • Neo-Darwinism (modern synthesis) explains evolution as change in allele frequencies.
  • Natural selection is a non-random, directional process; genetic drift is random.
Important Notes
  • Memorise Lamarck's two ideas (use/disuse, inherited traits) and why rejected.
  • Learn Darwin's postulates (variation, overproduction, struggle, survival of fittest).
  • List the components of Neo-Darwinism.
  • Differentiate natural selection from genetic drift.
  • State the Hardy-Weinberg equation and its 5 conditions.
Exam Short Tricks
  • Lamarck = L-A-M (Learned, Acquired, Modified) traits — inherited (wrong).
  • Darwin = D-N-S (Difference, Nature, Selection) — survival of the fittest.
  • Neo = genetics + selection + drift + flow + recombination.
  • Natural selection = non-random; Drift = random (small pop).
Exam Notes

  • Describe the modern synthesis (Neo-Darwinism).
  • Differentiate natural selection from genetic drift.
  • State the Hardy-Weinberg principle and conditions.
