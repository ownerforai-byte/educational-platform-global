Notes

  • Nepal occupies under 0.1% of the world's land area but hosts about 2% of its flowering plants — over 7,000 vascular plant species have been recorded from the country.
  • Altitude drives Nepal's floral belts: tropical sal and chir pine forest in the Terai, oak–rhododendron and temperate conifer belts in the hills, birch–juniper scrub and alpine meadows above the tree line.
  • Endemism is high for a country of its size: roughly 3% of Nepal's flowering plants are endemic, concentrated in the central Himalaya (for example Himalayan hemlock and spiny berryl).
  • World pattern: tropical rainforests cover under 10% of the earth's land but hold about two-thirds of all plant species; species richness falls steadily with increasing latitude and altitude.
  • Threats to flora include habitat loss, over-harvesting, invasive species and climate change; conservation uses in-situ methods (protected areas) together with ex-situ methods (seed banks, botanical gardens).
  • The timberline, near 4000 m, is where trees stop. The snowline is higher. Alpine herbs live between them, so a sentence that puts Sal above the timberline has left the tropical belt.
