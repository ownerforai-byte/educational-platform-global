Enriched Content

Notes
  • DETAILED GYMNOSPERM (Pinus) MORPHOLOGY AND REPRODUCTION.
  • Plant body: woody stem (trunk) + branch system; needle-like (Pinus) or scale-like (Deodar) leaves; no true flowers; reproduction via cones (strobili).
  • Male cone (microstrobilus): small, clustered at the base of new shoots; microsporangia → microspores (n) → pollen grains (male gametophyte) with an air-sac for wind dispersal.
  • Female cone (megasporangium / seed cone): larger, at the tip of shoots; megasporangia → megaspores (n) → female gametophyte (archegonia) on the ovule; the ovule is exposed on the cone scale (naked seed).
  • Pollination: wind (anemophilous); pollen lands on the resinous ovule; pollen tube grows to the archegonium; fertilisation (no water needed).
  • Seed: embryo + endosperm (n) + seed coat; no fruit (naked); dispersed by wind.
  • Life cycle: SPOROPHYTE (2n) dominant; gametophyte reduced, non-photosynthetic, and dependent on the sporophyte.
  • Key differences from angiosperms: no flowers, no fruits, no double fertilisation; seeds exposed on cone scales.
  • Economic importance: timber (construction, paper), resin (turpentine from Pinus), ornamental (conifers in gardens), soil stabilisation in alpine regions; Deodar is a sacred tree in Nepali tradition.
  • Other gymnosperms: Ginkgo (living fossil, deciduous), Cycas (cycad, palm-like), Gnetum (angiosperm-like).
  • NEB focus: detailed Pinus male + female cone structure, pollen, ovule, seed, and the life cycle; economic importance in Nepal.
Confusion
  • Gymnosperms produce SEEDS but NOT fruits; angiosperms produce both.
  • The male cone produces pollen (male gametophyte); the female cone bears the ovule → seed.
  • Pinus is wind-pollinated (anemophilous); no flowers, no nectar, no insects involved.
  • Gymnosperms do NOT undergo double fertilisation (angiosperm feature).
  • The gametophyte in gymnosperms is reduced and non-photosynthetic (unlike bryophytes where the gametophyte is dominant).
  • Deodar (Cedrus deodara) and Pine (Pinus) are both in the family Pinaceae; they differ in leaf form and cone structure.

Universal Facts
  • Gymnosperms appeared ~300 Ma (Carboniferous); they dominated the Mesozoic era.
  • A single Pinus male cone can release millions of pollen grains for wind dispersal.
  • The Deodar (Cedrus deodara) is a sacred tree in Nepali Hindu and Buddhist tradition.
  • Gymnosperms include over 1,000 species; conifers are the most common in temperate regions.
Examples

  • Seed: embryo + endosperm + seed coat; no fruit.
  • Economic: timber (Deodar, Pine), resin (turpentine), ornamental, sacred (Deodar), soil stabilisation.

Key Points
  • Pinus: woody, needle-like leaves, cones; no flowers or fruits.
  • Male cone: microsporangium → pollen (wind-dispersed).
  • Female cone: ovule on the cone scale → seed (naked).
  • Pollen: body + air-sac; two-celled; wind dispersal.
  • Life cycle: sporophyte (2n) dominant; gametophyte reduced.

  • Economic: timber, resin, ornamental, sacred (Deodar), soil stabilisation.
Summary

A detailed study of Pinus (gymnosperm) reveals a woody plant with needle-like leaves and cones. The male cone bears microsporangia that produce pollen grains (each with a body and an air-sac for wind dispersal). The female cone bears the ovule on the cone scale; pollination is anemophilous (wind), and fertilisation does not require water. The resulting seed is "naked" (exposed on the cone scale, no fruit). The life cycle has a dominant sporophyte and a reduced, non-photosynthetic gametophyte. Gymnosperms are economically important in Nepal as timber (Deodar, Pine), resin sources, ornamentals, and sacred trees. NEB focuses on the detailed cone structures, pollen, ovule, seed, and the life cycle.

  • No double fertilisation — a contrast with angiosperms.
  • Economic importance in Nepal: timber, resin, ornamental, sacred (Deodar).

Gymnosperms are economically important in Nepal as timber (Deodar, Pine), resin sources, ornamentals, and sacred trees.
