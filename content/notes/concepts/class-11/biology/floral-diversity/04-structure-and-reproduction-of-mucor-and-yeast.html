Notes

  • Mucor thallus. Mucor is a saprophytic mould with branched, aseptate (coenocytic) hyphae that grow on decaying organic matter such as bread and fruit.
  • Mucor reproduction. Asexually it forms sporangiospores inside a globose sporangium; sexually, compatible hyphae fuse to produce a thick-walled zygospore.
  • Yeast structure. Saccharomyces cerevisiae is a unicellular, oval eukaryote with a chitinous wall, a large vacuole and a single nucleus — it has no mycelium.
  • Yeast reproduction. Yeast multiplies by budding and fission; under sexual conditions it forms ascospores inside an ascus, so it is classified with the ascomycetes.
  • Economic importance. Yeast ferments sugars to ethyl alcohol and carbon dioxide (baking, brewing) and is a source of vitamin B and single-cell protein.
  • The columella inside a Mucor sporangium is sterile. Spores are cut from the cytoplasm around it. Plus and minus strains are both required for the zygospore in the usual heterothallic account.

Name the asexual container of Mucor and the sexual container of yeast.
