Enriched Content

Notes
  • Pteridophytes: seedless vascular plants (true xylem and phloem); include ferns, horsetails, clubmosses.
  • Life cycle: alternation of generations; the sporophyte (2n) is the dominant, visible generation; the gametophyte (n) is small, free-living, and short-lived.
  • Ferns: frond (leaf-like), rhizome (horizontal stem), stipe (leaf stalk), pinnae; spores in sori on the underside of fronds.
  • Dryopteris (model fern): rhizome, fronds, sori with sporangia; spores (n) germinate → prothallus (heart-shaped gametophyte) → antheridia and archegonia → fertilisation (requires water) → young sporophyte.
  • Reproduction: asexual (spores) and sexual (flagellated sperm + egg; requires water).
  • Economic/ecological importance: medicinal (Asteraceae ferns used in traditional medicine), ornamental (garden ferns), soil stabilisation, indicators of humidity; fossils of pteridophytes form coal deposits.
  • Key difference from bryophytes: pteridophytes have TRUE vascular tissue (xylem + phloem) and a dominant sporophyte generation.
Confusion
  • Pteridophytes have TRUE vascular tissue (xylem and phloem); bryophytes do NOT. This is the key distinction.
  • The SPOROPHYTE is the dominant generation in pteridophytes (unlike bryophytes where the gametophyte dominates).
  • The prothallus (gametophyte) of a fern is small, heart-shaped, and free-living — NOT the main plant body.
  • Sori are clusters of sporangia on the underside of fern fronds; they are NOT the frond itself.
  • Fertilisation still requires WATER in pteridophytes (flagellated sperm), even though the sporophyte is dominant.
  • Pteridophytes do NOT produce seeds; they reproduce by spores. Gymnosperms and angiosperms produce seeds.
Practice
  • Draw and label a Dryopteris frond (stipe, blade, pinnae, sori, sporangia).
  • Describe the life cycle of a fern (Dryopteris) with a labelled diagram.
  • Differentiate pteridophytes from bryophytes (4 points).
  • Explain the economic importance of pteridophytes (5 points).
  • Draw the prothallus of a fern and label the antheridia and archegonia.
Universal Facts
  • Ferns first appeared in the Carboniferous period (~300 million years ago); their fossils form coal deposits.
  • The largest fern is the tree fern (Cyathea), which can reach 20 m in height in tropical rainforests.
  • In pteridophytes, the sporophyte generation is dominant, marking the evolutionary transition toward seed plants.
  • Dryopteris is the standard model fern for NEB Class 11 Biology.
Examples
  • Dryopteris filix-medis — common fern, studied for NEB; fronds with sori on the underside.
  • Equisetum hyemale — horsetail, jointed stem with silica deposits, used in traditional medicine.
  • Lycopodium (clubmoss) — cone-like strobili, used as dusting powder in cosmetics.
  • Cyathea medullaris — tree fern, found in New Zealand; one of the largest ferns.

Formulas
  • Life cycle: Spore (n) → Prothallus (n, gametophyte) → Sperm + Egg → Zygote (2n) → Young sporophyte (2n, dominant) → Sori → Spores (n)
  • Sori: clusters of sporangia on the underside of fronds
  • Fertilisation: Sperm (flagellated) + Egg (in archegonium on prothallus) → Zygote (requires water)
Key Points
  • Pteridophytes: seedless, VASCULAR plants (true xylem + phloem).
  • Sporophyte (2n) is the dominant, visible generation.
  • Prothallus (n) is the small, free-living gametophyte.
  • Reproduction: spores (asexual) + flagellated sperm (sexual, requires water).
  • Fern frond: stipe + blade; sori on underside with sporangia.
  • Economic: medicinal, ornamental, soil, fossil coal deposits.
Summary

Pteridophytes are seedless vascular plants that represent the evolutionary transition between non-vascular bryophytes and seed-bearing plants. The sporophyte (2n) is the dominant generation; the gametophyte (prothallus) is small and free-living. Ferns like Dryopteris have fronds with sori (spore clusters). Fertilisation still requires water. Pteridophytes are economically important as medicinal herbs, ornamentals, soil stabilisers, and the source of coal deposits.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects a detailed diagram of the Dryopteris frond (stipe, blade, pinnae, sori, sporangia, indusium).
  • The life cycle of a fern (alternation of generations) is a very frequent long-answer question.
  • Differentiate pteridophytes from bryophytes: key difference is the presence of TRUE vascular tissue and dominant sporophyte.
  • Note that fertilisation still requires water in pteridophytes — a key exam point.
Important Statements
  • Pteridophytes were the first plants to evolve true vascular tissue.
  • The dominant generation in pteridophytes is the sporophyte (2n).
  • Fern fossils from the Carboniferous period form the world's coal deposits.
  • The prothallus is the free-living gametophyte generation in ferns.
Important Notes

  • Learn the fern life cycle (alternation of generations) with a diagram.
  • Differentiate pteridophytes vs bryophytes (vascular tissue, dominant generation).
