Visual Type

algae-types-diagram

Enriched Content

Notes
  • Algae: photosynthetic, eukaryotic (mostly), aquatic or damp-environment organisms; no true roots, stems, or leaves.
  • Major groups: Chrysophyceae (diatoms, golden algae — silica cell walls), Pyrrophyceae (dinoflagellates — bioluminescence, red tides), Chlorophyceae (green algae — Chlorophyll a + b, starch storage).
  • Reproduction: asexual (spores, fragmentation) and sexual (isogamous, anisogamous, oogamous).
  • Ecological importance: primary producers in aquatic ecosystems; produce ~50% of Earth's oxygen; base of marine food chains.
  • Economic importance: food (agar, carrageenan, spirulina), industry (alginates, pigments), agriculture (biofertilisers — cyanobacteria in rice paddies).
  • In NEB syllabus: algae are studied as non-vascular, photosynthetic organisms before bryophytes.
Confusion
  • Algae are NOT plants in the strict sense: they lack true tissues, roots, and seeds. They are placed in Protista (or Plantae in some systems) depending on the classification used.
  • Cyanobacteria (blue-green algae) are prokaryotes, not true algae; they are in the domain Bacteria.
  • Dinoflagellates (Pyrrophyceae) can cause harmful algal blooms (red tides); most algae are harmless.
  • Chlorophyll content determines the group: green algae have chlorophyll a + b; brown algae (Phaeophyceae) have a + c + fucoxanthin.
  • Algal "stems" and "leaves" are not true vascular tissues; they are structural modifications.
Practice
  • Tabulate the three algal groups: pigment, storage product, example.
  • Explain the ecological importance of algae (5 points).
  • Differentiate asexual and sexual reproduction in green algae.
  • Describe the structure of a diatom cell.
  • List three economic uses of algae.
Universal Facts
  • Algae contribute approximately 50% of global primary oxygen production via photosynthesis.
  • Diatoms form the base of the oceanic food web; their silica shells accumulate as diatomaceous earth.
  • Spirulina, a blue-green alga (cyanobacterium), is a high-protein food supplement used in space missions.
  • Algal blooms can deplete oxygen in water bodies, causing mass fish kills (hypoxic events).
Examples
  • Chlorella pyrenoidosa — green alga, used in bioremediation and food supplements.
  • Laminaria (kelp) — brown alga, source of alginates and iodine.
  • Porifera-like: Ulva (sea lettuce) — green alga, food in some tropical regions.
  • Diatom: Navicula — silica-walled, forms diatomaceous earth used in filtration.
Practice Questions
  • Why are algae considered the primary producers of aquatic ecosystems?
  • Differentiate Chrysophyceae from Pyrrophyceae (3 points).
  • Explain the ecological problem of algal blooms.
  • What is the storage product in green, brown, and red algae?
  • Describe the structure and reproduction of a diatom.
Formulas
  • Green algae: Chlorophyll a + b, store starch
  • Brown algae: Chlorophyll a + c + fucoxanthin, store laminarin
  • Red algae: Chlorophyll a + d + phycobilins, store floridean starch
  • Diatom cell wall: SiO₂ (silica)
Key Points
  • Algae are photosynthetic, mostly aquatic, lack true vascular tissues.
  • Three major groups: Chrysophyceae, Pyrrophyceae, Chlorophyceae.
  • Ecological role: ~50% of Earth's O₂; base of aquatic food chains.
  • Reproduction: asexual (spores, fragmentation) + sexual (isogamy, anisogamy, oogamy).
  • Economic uses: food, industry (agar, alginates), biofertilisers.
  • Cyanobacteria ≠ true algae (prokaryotic vs eukaryotic).
Summary

Algae are photosynthetic, mostly aquatic organisms that lack true roots, stems, and leaves. They are classified into Chrysophyceae (diatoms, golden algae), Pyrrophyceae (dinoflagellates), and Chlorophyceae (green algae). As primary producers, algae generate about half of the world's oxygen and form the base of aquatic food webs. Their economic importance spans food, industry, and agriculture. NEB examinations focus on algal classification, ecological role, and economic uses.

Special Notes
  • NEB asks for a tabulation of algal groups with pigments, storage products, and examples.
  • The ecological importance of algae (O₂ production, food web base) is a frequent long-answer topic.
  • Algal blooms (eutrophication) are an environmental application question.
  • Differentiate cyanobacteria (prokaryotic) from true algae (eukaryotic).
Important Statements
  • Algae are the primary producers of most aquatic ecosystems.
  • They contribute approximately 50% of global oxygen via photosynthesis.
  • Diatoms have silica cell walls and form the base of the marine food web.
  • Algal blooms, caused by nutrient pollution, can lead to hypoxic dead zones.
Important Notes
  • Memorise pigments and storage products for green, brown, and red algae.
  • Learn the ecological role of algae (O₂, food web, biogeochemical cycles).
  • Draw a diatom cell and a green alga (Chlamydomonas or Ulothrix).
  • List 3 economic uses and 1 environmental problem of algae.
Exam Short Tricks
  • Algae = A-O-A (Aquatic, Oxygen, Aquatic food base).
  • Green algae = starch; Brown = laminarin; Red = floridean starch.
  • Diatom = silica shell (SiO₂).
  • Cyanobacteria = blue-green = prokaryotic, NOT true algae.
Exam Notes
  • Tabulate algal groups (pigment, storage, example, reproduction).
  • Explain the ecological importance of algae (5 points).
  • Describe algal blooms and their environmental impact.
  • Differentiate asexual and sexual reproduction in algae.
  • List economic uses of algae (food, industry, agriculture).

1.

A pond turns green and fish die. What is the likely cause and explain the mechanism.

ID

1

Steps
  • Excess nutrients (N, P) from agriculture/sewage
  • Algal bloom (eutrophication)
  • Algae die → decomposers use O₂
  • O₂ depletion → fish die (hypoxia)
Answer

Eutrophication: algal bloom → O₂ depletion → fish death
