Enriched Content

Notes
  • Bryophytes: non-vascular land plants (no true xylem or phloem); include mosses, liverworts, and hornworts.
  • Life cycle: alternation of generations with the gametophyte (n) as the dominant, visible generation; the sporophyte (2n) is dependent on the gametophyte.
  • Mosses (Bryophyta): e.g., Polytrichum, Funaria; stem-like (cauloid), leaf-like (phylloid) structures; no true roots (rhizoids instead).
  • Liverworts (Marchantiophyta): thalloid (flat, ribbon-like) or leafy; Marchantia polymorpha is the model; gemmae cups for asexual reproduction.
  • Hornworts (Anthocerotophyta): thalloid; the sporophyte is horn-like and elongates from the base.
  • Reproduction: asexual (fragmentation, gemmae) and sexual (flagellated sperm swim to egg in the archegonium; fertilisation requires water).
  • Economic/ecological importance: pioneer species on bare rock, soil formation, moisture retention, indicators of air pollution (sensitive to SO₂), ornamental use.
  • NEB focus: Marchantia structure (thalus, rhizoids, gemma cups, antheridia, archegonia) and life cycle.
Confusion
  • Bryophytes lack TRUE roots, stems, and leaves; they have rhizoids, cauloids, and phylloids instead.
  • The gametophyte is the DOMINANT generation in bryophytes (unlike seed plants where the sporophyte dominates).
  • The sporophyte is dependent on the gametophyte for nutrition (it does not photosynthesise independently in most mosses).
  • Fertilisation in bryophytes requires WATER for sperm to swim to the egg; this limits bryophytes to moist habitats.
  • Gemmae are asexual propagules in gemma cups; they are NOT spores.
  • Liverworts are NOT mosses: liverworts are thalloid (Marchantia) or leafy; mosses are cauloid (stem + leaf).
Practice
  • Draw and label a Marchantia thallus (rhizoids, gemma cups, antheridia, archegonia).
  • Describe the life cycle of a moss (e.g., Funaria) with a labelled diagram.
  • Differentiate bryophytes from pteridophytes (4 points).
  • Explain the ecological importance of bryophytes (5 points).
  • Draw a gemma cup and label the gemmae.
Universal Facts
  • Bryophytes were the first plants to colonise land (Devonian period, ~400 million years ago).
  • Marchantia polymorpha is the most commonly studied liverwort in school laboratories.
  • Mosses and liverworts are sensitive bio-indicators of air pollution, especially SO₂.
  • In bryophytes, the sporophyte (2n) is short-lived and nutritionally dependent on the gametophyte (n).
Examples
  • Marchantia polymorpha — liverwort, thalloid, gemma cups for asexual reproduction.
  • Funaria hygrometrica — moss, cauloid, capsule opens with elaters in dry weather.
  • Polytrichum commune — moss, elongated capsule, common in temperate regions.
  • Anthoceros — hornwort, horn-like elongating sporophyte.

  • Differentiate the gametophyte and sporophyte generations in bryophytes.
  • Describe the structure of a Marchantia thallus with a labelled diagram.
  • Explain the role of water in bryophyte reproduction.
  • State the ecological importance of bryophytes (5 points).
Formulas
  • Life cycle: Spore (n) → Gametophyte (n, dominant) → Gametes → Zygote (2n) → Sporophyte (2n, dependent) → Spores (n)
  • Gemmae: asexual, multicellular, in gemma cups
  • Fertilisation: Sperm (flagellated) + Egg (in archegonium) → Zygote
Key Points
  • Bryophytes: non-vascular (no xylem/phloem), rhizoids instead of roots.
  • Gametophyte (n) is the dominant, visible generation.
  • Sporophyte (2n) is dependent on the gametophyte for nutrition.
  • Three groups: mosses (Bryophyta), liverworts (Marchantiophyta), hornworts (Anthocerotophyta).
  • Reproduction requires water (flagellated sperm); gemmae for asexual reproduction.
  • Ecological role: pioneers, soil formation, moisture, air pollution indicators.
Summary

Bryophytes are the simplest land plants: non-vascular, with the gametophyte as the dominant generation. They include mosses, liverworts, and hornworts. Marchantia (liverwort) and Funaria (moss) are the model organisms studied in NEB. Reproduction requires water for fertilisation; asexual reproduction occurs via gemmae and fragmentation. Bryophytes are ecologically important as pioneer species, soil builders, moisture retainers, and air-pollution indicators.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects a labelled diagram of the Marchantia thallus (upper/lower epidermis, air chambers, rhizoids, gemma cups, antheridia, archegonia).
  • The "amphibians of the plant kingdom" analogy is a favourite exam phrase: bryophytes live on land but need water to reproduce.
  • Differentiate bryophytes from pteridophytes: bryophytes lack vascular tissue; pteridophytes have true xylem and phloem.
  • Gemmae ≠ spores: gemmae are asexual, multicellular, in gemma cups; spores are produced meiotically in the sporangium.
Important Statements
  • Bryophytes are the first group of plants to colonise terrestrial habitats.
  • The gametophyte generation is dominant in bryophytes, unlike seed plants.
  • Water is essential for bryophyte fertilisation (sperm must swim to the egg).
  • Bryophytes are sensitive bio-indicators of air pollution, particularly SO₂.
Important Notes
  • Memorise the Marchantia thallus structure (all labelled parts).
  • Learn the bryophyte life cycle (alternation of generations).
  • Differentiate gametophyte vs sporophyte in bryophytes.
  • List ecological and economic importance of bryophytes.
  • Know why bryophytes are called "amphibians of the plant kingdom".
Exam Short Tricks
  • Bryophyte = no VASCULAR tissue = "Bry" (as in "no BR" in bryophyte).
  • Gametophyte DOMINANT in bryophytes; Sporophyte DOMINANT in seed plants.
  • Gemma cups = asexual; Archegonium = sexual.
  • Water needed for sperm to swim → bryophytes need moist habitat.
