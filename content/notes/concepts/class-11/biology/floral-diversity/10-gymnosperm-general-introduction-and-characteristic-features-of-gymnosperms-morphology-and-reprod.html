Notes

  • Naked seeds. Gymnosperms bear seeds that are not enclosed in a fruit; ovules lie exposed on the surface of megasporophylls, usually in cones.
  • Plant body. They are mostly evergreen, woody trees and shrubs with well-developed roots (often with mycorrhizae) and needle-like or scale-like leaves.
  • Reproduction. Male cones release pollen carried by wind to female cones; the ovule contains the female gametophyte and fertilisation gives a seed with a stored food supply.
  • Examples. Cycas, Pinus, Cedrus (deodar), Ginkgo and Ephedra are familiar gymnosperms; Cycas retains fern-like leaflets and coralloid roots with nitrogen-fixing cyanobacteria.
  • Economic importance. Gymnosperms give timber, resins, turpentine, ephedrine (medicine) and ornamentals; their pollen can also cause allergies.
  • Pinus is monoecious: male cones and female cones on one tree. Pollen is winged and carried by wind. Pollination and fertilisation are separated by many months. The seed is winged.

Exercises

1.

Why is a pine cone not called a fruit, and how does the pollen travel?

Steps
  • A fruit is a ripened ovary. Gymnosperms have no ovary closed around the seed.
  • The ovule sits exposed on a cone scale.
  • Pollen is blown by wind.
  • The seed that later forms is winged.
Answer

No closed ovary, so no fruit. Wind carries the pollen.
