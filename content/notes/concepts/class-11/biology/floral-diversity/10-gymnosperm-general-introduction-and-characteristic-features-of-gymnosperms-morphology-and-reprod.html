  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Naked-Seed Plants

    Gymnosperms (Greek gymnos = naked, sperma = seed) are the plants whose seeds lie naked on the surface of megasporophylls, not enclosed in an ovary or fruit. They solved the land plant's final pre-flowering problems: no external water for fertilisation, and a seed that carries its own lunch and shelter. The syllabus covers their general features, the morphology and reproduction of Pinus, and their economic importance.

  • 3. Pinus — Morphology and Reproduction

    3.1 Morphology (the sporophyte)

    • Tree: tall conifer; long shoots and dwarf shoots — the dwarf shoots bear needle-like leaves in fascicles (bundles of 1–5, e.g. Pinus roxburghii has 3) surrounded by scale leaves; a dwarf shoot + needles = a "spur".
    • Roots: tap root with mycorrhizal associations (fungus partner essential for mineral uptake).
    • Stem: branched, resinous wood (resin canals).

    3.2 Reproduction — two kinds of cones on the same tree (monoecious)

    Male cone (microsporangiate strobilus):

    1. Clusters of microsporophylls, each bearing two microsporangia.
    2. Meiosis produces winged pollen grains (microspores) — the wings aid wind dispersal.
    3. Each pollen grain contains the male gametophyte (a few cells, including two male gametes).

    Female cone (megasporangiate strobilus):

    1. Woody megasporophylls in spirals; each carries two ovules on its upper surface — naked seeds on the scale, the defining gymnosperm image.
    2. Inside the ovule, one megaspore mother cell → meiosis → 4 megaspores → one functional → female gametophyte (endosperm, haploid) with 2–3 archegonia.

    Pollination and fertilisation:

    1. Wind carries pollen to the ovule's micropylar end; a pollination drop traps it.
    2. The pollen grain germinates into a pollen tube that grows through the nucellus toward the archegonia — siphonogamy (no external water needed).
    3. One male gamete fuses with the egg → zygote.
    4. The zygote develops into an embryo (with several cotyledons — polycotyledonous) inside the female gametophyte (its stored food).
    5. The ovule matures into the naked seed; the cone scales woody and open to release winged seeds.

    Life cycle: sporophyte dominant; both gametophytes microscopic and retained — the seed habit complete except for the missing ovary.

    7.2 Model answer

    Q (3m) — Distinguish between gymnosperms and angiosperms with two examples of each. Answer: (1) Seed: gymnosperm seeds lie naked on megasporophylls; angiosperm seeds are enclosed within the ovary, which matures into a fruit. (2) Flowers: gymnosperms bear cones; angiosperms bear true flowers. (3) Xylem: gymnosperms lack vessels (tracheids only); angiosperms have vessels. (4) Fertilisation/endosperm: gymnosperms show simple fertilisation with haploid endosperm and archegonia; angiosperms show double fertilisation with triploid endosperm. Examples: gymnosperm — Pinus, Cycas; angiosperm — mango (Mangifera indica), mustard (Brassica).

    7.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying gymnosperms have fruits — no ovary, no fruit; the "pine cone" is a strobilus.
    2. Giving gymnosperm endosperm as triploid — it is haploid (pre-fertilisation female gametophyte).
    3. Drawing ovules enclosed in an ovary — draw them naked on the scale.
    4. Forgetting winged pollen and the pollen tube (siphonogamy) in Pinus reproduction.
    5. Writing that Pinus is dioecious — it is monoecious (both cones on one tree).

Confusion

  • Naked seed vs fruit: the gymnosperm seed sits exposed on the cone scale; only angiosperms form fruits from ovaries.
  • Endosperm type: gymnosperm endosperm = haploid female gametophyte made BEFORE fertilisation; angiosperm endosperm = triploid, formed AFTER double fertilisation.
  • Long shoot vs dwarf shoot: long shoots elongate the branch; dwarf shoots bear the needle fascicles and are shed with them.
  • Male gametes: motile (flagellated) in Cycas; non-motile, delivered by the pollen tube in Pinus — siphonogamy.

Practice

  • Why are gymnosperm leaves typically needle-shaped?

    Answer. The needle is a drought-and-cold package:

    1. Tiny surface area cuts transpiration to a minimum — essential where winter freezes the soil and roots cannot replace lost water.
    2. Thick cuticle and sunken stomata further seal the surface.
    3. The narrow profile sheds snow instead of carrying its weight, and resists wind tearing.
    4. Evergreen needles keep photosynthesising whenever conditions allow, instead of rebuilding a whole new canopy each year — a huge energy saving in short growing seasons.

    The cost (low total leaf area) is offset by keeping the leaves for years. It is the same logic as the xerophyte spine: survive the inhospitable season, don't chase maximum display.

Universal Facts

  • Gymnosperms dominated the Mesozoic ("Age of Conifers") before flowering plants radiated.

Examples

  • Pinus roxburghii — chir pine of Nepal's mid-hills; resin and timber.
  • Cedrus deodara — deodar; timber and temple wood.
  • Taxus baccata/brevifolia — yew; source of taxol.
  • Cycas revoluta — sago cycad; ornamental; motile sperm.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What is siphonogamy?

    Answer. Fertilisation by pollen tube: the male gametophyte germinates into a tube that grows through the ovule tissue and delivers the male gametes directly to the egg inside the archegonium. Gymnosperms were the first plants to achieve it — reproduction no longer depends on external water, the great step beyond ferns and bryophytes.

  • Short answer (2m): Why are Pinus roots mycorrhizal?

    Answer. Conifer-forest soils are poor, acidic and slow to release minerals, especially phosphorus. Pinus roots depend on an associated fungus wrapped around them: the fungal threads forage far beyond the root hair zone and hand over water and minerals (P, N) in exchange for sugars. Seedlings fail to establish without the fungus — the partnership is near-obligatory, and is why nursery pines are inoculated with forest soil.

Key Points

  • Gymnosperms: naked seeds on megasporophylls, cones not flowers, tracheid-only xylem, no companion cells, wind pollination, pollen-tube fertilisation (siphonogamy), haploid endosperm, polycotyledonous embryo, sporophyte dominant.
  • Pinus: long + dwarf shoots with needle fascicles; mycorrhizal roots; monoecious; male cones with winged pollen; female cones with two naked ovules per scale; pollen tube delivers non-motile male gamete; winged seeds.
  • Economic: timber (Cedrus, Pinus), resin/turpentine, paper pulp, chilgoza nuts, Taxol from Taxus, ornamentals (Cycas, Thuja).

Summary

Gymnosperms are the naked-seed plants: evergreen trees with tracheid-only xylem, needle leaves and cones instead of flowers. Pinus bears long and dwarf shoots, mycorrhizal roots, male cones releasing winged pollen and female cones carrying two naked ovules per scale; the pollen tube (siphonogamy) delivers a non-motile gamete to archegonia of the haploid female gametophyte, and the maturing ovule becomes a winged naked seed with a polycotyledonous embryo. Compared with angiosperms they lack vessels, fruits, double fertilisation and triploid endosperm. They supply timber, resin, turpentine, chilgoza nuts and the anticancer drug taxol — the seed habit's first great success.

Formulas

  • Pinus is monoecious: male cones and female cones on one tree. Pollen is winged and carried by wind. Pollination and fertilisation are separated by many months. The seed is winged.

  • Short answer (2m): What is siphonogamy? — Fertilisation achieved by a pollen tube carrying male gametes to the egg inside the ovule — reproduction freed from the need for external water (first achieved by gymnosperms).
  • Short answer (2m): Why are Pinus roots mycorrhizal? — The roots depend on an associated fungus to absorb water and minerals (especially phosphorus) from poor conifer-forest soils; without the fungus the seedling fails.
Summary

Gymnosperms are the naked-seed plants: evergreen trees with tracheid-only xylem, needle leaves and cones instead of flowers. Pinus bears long and dwarf shoots, mycorrhizal roots, male cones releasing winged pollen and female cones carrying two naked ovules per scale; the pollen tube (siphonogamy) delivers a non-motile gamete to archegonia of the haploid female gametophyte, and the maturing ovule becomes a winged naked seed with a polycotyledonous embryo. Compared with angiosperms they lack vessels, fruits, double fertilisation and triploid endosperm. They supply timber, resin, turpentine, chilgoza nuts and the anticancer drug taxol — the seed habit's first great success.

1.

Gymnosperm seeds are called naked because they:

Options
  • Lack stored food
  • Are not enclosed in an ovary/fruit
  • Have no embryo
  • Are green
Answer

B

Explanation

The ovule sits exposed on the megasporophyll; no ovary wall forms a fruit around it.

2.

Gymnosperm xylem lacks:

Options
  • Tracheids
  • Vessels
  • Fibres
  • Lignin
Answer

B

Explanation

Gymnosperm wood is tracheid-only — no vessels; vessels appear in angiosperms.

3.

Pinus pollen grains are winged to aid:

Options
  • Insect attraction
  • Wind dispersal
  • Water floatation
  • Germination
Answer

B

Explanation

The two air sacs increase buoyancy in air — anemophily (wind pollination).

4.

The endosperm of a gymnosperm is:

Options
  • Triploid, formed after fertilisation
  • Haploid female gametophyte formed before fertilisation
  • Diploid maternal tissue
  • Absent
Answer

B

Explanation

It is the retained female gametophyte (n) that feeds the embryo; angiosperm endosperm is triploid after double fertilisation.

5.

Fertilisation in Pinus is achieved by:

Options
  • Swimming sperm in a water film
  • A pollen tube (siphonogamy)
  • Wind-borne male gametes
  • Insect vectors
Answer

B

Explanation

The pollen tube grows through the nucellus and releases non-motile male gametes near the archegonia.

6.

Taxol, an anticancer drug, is obtained from:

Options
  • Pinus
  • Taxus
  • Cycas
  • Ginkgo
Answer

B

Explanation

Taxus (yew) bark yields taxol, used against ovarian and breast cancers.

7.

Pinus is:

Options
  • Dioecious
  • Monoecious with both cones on one tree
  • Sterile
  • Hermaphrodite-flowered
Answer

B

Explanation

Male and female cones occur on the same tree (monoecious).

8.

Which is NOT a gymnosperm?

Options
  • Ginkgo
  • Cycas
  • Ephedra
  • Mangifera

D

Explanation

Mangifera (mango) is an angiosperm; Ginkgo, Cycas and Ephedra are gymnosperms.
