Enriched Content

Notes
  • DETAILED BRYOPHYTE (Marchantia) STRUCTURE AND LIFE CYCLE.
  • Thallus: flat, ribbon-like, dichotomously branched; upper surface with air cavities (photorespiration) + photosynthetic chambers; lower surface with rhizoids (anchorage + absorption) + scales.
  • Gemma cups: dome-shaped, on the upper surface; contain gemmae (asexual, multicellular) that splash out and grow into new thalli.
  • Sexual organs: antheridia (male, flask-shaped, at the base of the antheridiophore) produce biflagellated sperm; archegonia (female, neck + venter, at the base of the archegoniophore) produce a single egg.
  • Fertilisation: sperm swim through water to the egg in the archegonium → zygote (2n) in the venter.
  • Sporophyte: dependent on the thallus; foot (attached) + seta (stalk) + capsule (sporangium) with elaters; meiosis in the spore mother cell → spores (n).
  • Spores germinate → new protonema → thallus (gametophyte) — the DOMINANT generation.
  • Life cycle: alternation of generations with gametophyte (n) dominant; sporophyte (2n) short-lived and nutritionally dependent.
  • Economic/ecological: pioneer on bare rock, soil formation, moisture retention, SO2 bio-indicator, ornamental; peat (from Sphagnum moss) used as fuel + soil conditioner.
  • Other bryophytes: mosses (Funaria, Polytrichum — cauloid with stem + leaf), liverworts (Marchantia — thalloid), hornworts (Anthoceros — horn-like elongating sporophyte).
  • NEB focus: detailed Marchantia structure, asexual (gemmae) + sexual (antheridia/archegonia) reproduction, and the life cycle.
Confusion
  • The thallus is NOT a true leaf or stem; it is a flattened gametophyte body.
  • Rhizoids are single-celled (in liverworts) or multicellular (in mosses) filaments; they are NOT true roots (no xylem/phloem).
  • Gemmae are asexual propagules; spores are meiotic products — they are different.
  • The sporophyte is dependent on the gametophyte (absorbs nutrients through the foot); it does NOT photosynthesise independently in Marchantia.
  • Fertilisation requires WATER for the flagellated sperm to swim — a key limitation of bryophytes.
  • The gametophyte (n) is the dominant, visible generation; the sporophyte (2n) is small and short-lived.

Universal Facts
  • Marchantia polymorpha is the most commonly studied liverwort in NEB laboratories.
  • Mosses and liverworts are sensitive bio-indicators of air pollution, especially SO2.
  • Peat (from Sphagnum moss) forms most of the world's peat bogs and is used as fuel and soil conditioner.
  • The bryophyte gametophyte is the dominant generation — the reverse of seed plants.

Summary

A detailed study of Marchantia (liverwort) reveals a flat, dichotomously branched thallus with upper air cavities and lower rhizoids. Asexual reproduction occurs through gemma cups that release gemmae. Sexual reproduction involves antheridia (sperm) and archegonia (egg) at the base of the stalked reproductive bodies; fertilisation requires water. The dependent sporophyte (foot + seta + capsule with elaters) produces spores by meiosis, which germinate into the dominant gametophyte generation. Bryophytes are ecologically important as pioneers, soil builders, moisture retainers, and SO2 bio-indicators; Sphagnum moss forms peat. NEB focuses on the detailed Marchantia structure, both reproduction modes, and the life cycle.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects a fully labelled Marchantia thallus (air cavities, rhizoids, gemma cups, antheridia, archegonia).
  • Gemmae (asexual) vs spores (meiotic) — a common distinction question.
  • The dependent sporophyte (foot + seta + capsule + elaters) is a key structural point.
  • Fertilisation requires water — the "amphibian of the plant kingdom" link.
  • Economic: peat (Sphagnum), SO2 bio-indicator, soil formation.

Summary

A detailed study of Marchantia (liverwort) reveals a flat, dichotomously branched thallus with upper air cavities and lower rhizoids. Asexual reproduction occurs through gemma cups that release gemmae. Sexual reproduction involves antheridia (sperm) and archegonia (egg) at the base of the stalked reproductive bodies; fertilisation requires water. The dependent sporophyte (foot + seta + capsule with elaters) produces spores by meiosis, which germinate into the dominant gametophyte generation. Bryophytes are ecologically important as pioneers, soil builders, moisture retainers, and SO2 bio-indicators; Sphagnum moss forms peat. NEB focuses on the detailed Marchantia structure, both reproduction modes, and the life cycle. Bryophytes are the simplest land plants: non-vascular, with the gametophyte as the dominant generation. They include mosses, liverworts, and hornworts. Marchantia (liverwort) and Funaria (moss) are the model organisms studied in NEB. Reproduction requires water for fertilisation; asexual reproduction occurs via gemmae and fragmentation. Bryophytes are ecologically important as pioneer species, soil builders, moisture retainers, and air-pollution indicators.
