Notes

  • Amphibians of the plant kingdom. Bryophytes live in damp places and need water for fertilisation because the male gametes swim to the egg, yet they lack true vascular tissue.
  • Gametophyte dominance. The leafy or thalloid gametophyte is the persistent phase; the sporophyte is small, short-lived and remains attached to it.
  • Sex organs. Gametes are produced in multicellular antheridia (sperm) and archegonia (egg), both embedded in the gametophyte tissue.
  • Examples. Marchantia is a thalloid liverwort, Anthoceros a hornwort, and Funaria a moss with a leafy gametophyte and a seta–capsule sporophyte.
  • Importance. Sphagnum peat is used as fuel and packing material, bryophytes hold moisture and prevent soil erosion, and they indicate humid habitats.
  • Marchantia is a flat liverwort with gemma cups for asexual reproduction and separate male and female stalks. The sporophyte, foot, seta and capsule, stays attached to the gametophyte and does not live free.
