  • 1. Topic Introduction — The First Land Plants

    Bryophytes are the simplest and earliest land plants — small, soft, green, and permanently tied to water. They stand exactly halfway between algae and vascular plants: they live on land but must swim to reproduce. The syllabus covers the three groups (liverworts, hornworts, mosses), the structure and reproduction of Marchantia, and the economic importance of bryophytes.

  • 3. Marchantia — Structure and Reproduction (the syllabus specimen)

    3.1 Structure

    • Thallus: flat, dichotomously branched, dorsiventral ribbon; dorsal surface shows diamond-shaped air-chamber polygons with a midrib and a growing apex in each branch notch; ventral surface carries scales and smooth + pegged rhizoids.
    • Internal: upper photosynthetic zone with air chambers and pores; lower storage zone.
    • Gemma cups: cup-shaped notches on the upper surface holding lens-shaped gemmae — multicellular vegetative propagules splashed out by rain.

    3.2 Reproduction

    (A) Vegetative: death and decay of older thallus parts (fragmentation); gemmae from gemma cups.

    (B) Sexual — heterothallic (separate male and female plants):

    StructureMale plantFemale plant
    ReceptacleAntheridiophore — stalked, disc-shaped, star-lobedArchegoniophore — stalked, umbrella with radiating lobes hanging downward
    Sex organsAntheridia embedded in the disc; produce biflagellate antherozoidsArchegonia (flask-shaped: venter + neck) hang from the lobes

    Fertilisation: rain splashes antherozoids onto the archegoniophore; they swim down the neck canal; one fuses with the egg → zygote (2n).

    Sporophyte and spore formation:

    1. The zygote develops within the archegonium into the sporophyte: foot + seta + capsule.
    2. Inside the capsule, spore mother cells undergo meiosis → haploid spores; sterile elaters help disperse them.
    3. The capsule opens; spores germinate into male or female gametophytes — completing the alternation of generations.

    6.2 Model answers

    Q1 (2m) — Why are bryophytes called the amphibians of the plant kingdom? Answer: Like amphibians, bryophytes live on land but remain dependent on water — they inhabit moist habitats, lack a cuticle/vascular system to resist drying, and their motile antherozoids must swim through a water film to reach the archegonium for fertilisation. Without water their life cycle cannot complete.

    Q2/Q3 (3m) — General characteristics of Bryophyta + two economic importances. Answer: Bryophytes are small, green, non-vascular land plants: (1) body is a thallus or leafy shoot without true roots, stem, leaves or vascular tissue, attached by rhizoids; (2) they show alternation of generations with a dominant, photosynthetic gametophyte and a dependent sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule); (3) sex organs are multicellular and jacketed — antheridium and archegonium; (4) fertilisation needs water (motile antherozoids); (5) the capsule produces haploid spores by meiosis. Economic importance: (1) Sphagnum — peat fuel and antiseptic wound dressing/nursery packing; (2) moss carpets bind soil, prevent erosion and aid soil formation on bare rock.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying bryophytes have roots — they have rhizoids; roots begin with ferns.
    2. Calling the sporophyte the dominant plant — the gametophyte dominates in bryophytes (reverse of higher plants).
    3. Writing that Marchantia is monoecious — it is heterothallic (separate male/female plants with antheridiophores/archegoniophores).
    4. Forgetting elaters in the capsule.
    5. Omitting water dependence when listing characteristics — it is the defining theme.

Confusion

  • Rhizoid vs root: rhizoids are single cells or filaments for anchorage/absorption; roots are vascular organs — bryophytes have no roots.
  • Protonema vs thallus: mosses germinate first into a filamentous protonema (alga-like) before budding the leafy shoot; liverworts have no protonema.
  • Antheridiophore vs archegoniophore: male receptacle (disc, star-lobed) vs female receptacle (umbrella with hanging archegonia) — Marchantia is heterothallic.
  • Gemmae vs spores: gemmae are multicellular diploid (gametophyte) vegetative propagules; spores are single-celled haploid products of meiosis.

Practice

  • Why can't bryophytes grow tall like ferns?

    Answer. Two missing inventions:

    1. No vascular tissue (xylem/phloem) — there is no pipeline to lift water from the ground to any height, and no strong conducting tissue to hold the plant up; every cell must be within diffusion range of the surface film of water.
    2. No lignified support tissue — nothing stiffens a stem against gravity.

    So the thallus stays flat and low, absorbing water over its whole surface like a sponge. Ferns solved both problems with true vascular bundles and lignin, and immediately grew tall. Bryophytes also still need external water for fertilisation — another reason they remain the 'amphibians' of the plant kingdom, hugging wet ground.

Universal Facts

  • Bryophytes were the first plants to live on land (~450 million years ago).

Examples

  • Marchantia polymorpha — liverwort with gemma cups and stalked receptacles.
  • Anthoceros — hornwort with horn-like sporophyte.
  • Funaria — common moss with peristome-teeth capsule.
  • Sphagnum — peat moss; fuel and antiseptic packing.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What are gemmae?

    Answer. Multicellular, lens-shaped green buds produced inside gemma cups on the Marchantia thallus. Raindrops splash them out; each gemma lands, grows and becomes a new thallus — a vegetative reproduction that also spreads the thallus's sex (each gemma carries the parent's sex).

  • Short answer (2m): What is an archegonium?

    Answer. The flask-shaped female sex organ of bryophytes (also pteridophytes and gymnosperms): a swollen venter holding the single egg, topped by a neck canal that opens to let swimming antherozoids through. Its jacket protects the egg and, later, the developing embryo — the origin of the protected embryo that defines land plants.

  • Long answer (5m): Describe the structure and reproduction of Marchantia.

    Answer. Structure: the thallus is dichotomously branched, flat and ribbon-like, with a midrib and a dorsal surface pitted with air chambers and pores for gas exchange; the ventral side carries scales and smooth + pegged rhizoids for anchorage and absorption. Internally: upper photosynthetic zone with air chambers, lower storage zone. No vascular tissue — that is its size limit.

    Vegetative reproduction:

    1. Fragmentation of the branching thallus (the older middle decays, branches separate);
    2. Gemmae — lens-shaped buds in gemma cups, splashed out by rain.

    Sexual reproduction (heterothallic):

    1. Male plants grow upright antheridiophores bearing antheridia; female plants grow archegoniophores with flask-shaped archegonia hanging beneath the rays.
    2. Rain splashes biflagellate antherozoids from the antheridia to the archegonial neck; one fertilises the egg — water-dependent fertilisation.
    3. The zygote develops in the archegonium into the sporophyte: foot + seta + capsule.
    4. Inside the capsule meiosis produces haploid spores mixed with elaters (hygroscopic, help disperse spores).
    5. Spores germinate into new gametophytes.

    Alternation of generations: dominant, independent gametophyte (the thallus) alternates with a short-lived, dependent sporophyte nourished by the gametophyte.

Key Points

  • Bryophytes: amphibians of the plant kingdom; thalloid/leafy, no vascular tissue, rhizoids; alternation of generations with dominant gametophyte; antheridium + archegonium; water-dependent fertilisation; capsule spores with elaters.
  • Three groups: liverworts (Marchantia, Riccia), hornworts (Anthoceros), mosses (Funaria, Sphagnum).
  • Marchantia: dichotomously branched thallus, gemma cups; antheridiophore/archegoniophore; sporophyte = foot + seta + capsule.
  • Economic: Sphagnum peat fuel/wound dressing/nursery packing; soil binding and formation; research models.

Summary

Bryophytes — liverworts, hornworts and mosses — are the amphibians of the plant kingdom: small, non-vascular, rhizoid-anchored plants of moist habitats whose motile antherozoids still need water to fertilise the archegonium. Their life cycle alternates a dominant green gametophyte with a dependent sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule) that yields meiotic spores. Marchantia shows the liverwort plan: a dichotomous thallus with gemma cups for vegetative spread and separate male and female plants bearing antheridiophores and archegoniophores. Economically, Sphagnum gives peat fuel, antiseptic dressing and nursery packing, while moss carpets bind soil, start soil formation and mark the first step of plant life onto land.

  • Economic: Sphagnum peat fuel/wound dressing/nursery packing; soil binding and formation; research models.
Important Concepts
  • Alternation of generations: gametophyte (n) → gametes → fertilisation → sporophyte (2n) → meiosis → spores → gametophyte; bryophytes keep the gametophyte dominant, all higher plants flip it.
Examples
  • Marchantia polymorpha — liverwort with gemma cups and stalked receptacles.
  • Anthoceros — hornwort with horn-like sporophyte.
  • Funaria — common moss with peristome-teeth capsule.
  • Sphagnum — peat moss; fuel and antiseptic packing.
Universal Facts
  • Bryophytes were the first plants to live on land (~450 million years ago).
Important Statements
  • "Bryophytes are land plants still swimming in their past."
Important Notes
  • The bryophyte characteristics question repeated in 2022 and 2024 — memorise: thallus, no vascular tissue, alternation with dominant gametophyte, archegonium, water-dependent fertilisation + two named economic uses.
Exam Short Tricks
  • "Gametophyte first in bryo, sporophyte first in ferns and above."
Exam Notes
  • Most-tested: amphibian reasoning (2021), characteristics + economic importance (2022, 2024).
  • Diagram-worthy: Marchantia thallus with gemma cup, antheridiophore vs archegoniophore, moss capsule with peristome.
Practice
  • Why can't bryophytes grow tall like ferns?
Practice Questions
  • Short answer (2m): What are gemmae? — Multicellular, lens-shaped green buds produced in gemma cups on the Marchantia thallus; rain splashes them out and each grows into a new thallus (vegetative reproduction).
  • Short answer (2m): What is an archegonium? — The flask-shaped female sex organ of bryophytes (and pteridophytes/gymnosperms), with a neck and a venter holding the egg; the jacket protects the developing embryo.
  • Long answer (5m): Describe the structure and reproduction of Marchantia. — Thallus structure (dichotomous, air chambers, scales, rhizoids) → vegetative reproduction (fragmentation, gemmae) → heterothallic sexual reproduction (antheridiophore/archegoniophore, antherozoids, water-splashed fertilisation) → sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule; meiosis; elaters) → alternation of generations.
Summary

Bryophytes — liverworts, hornworts and mosses — are the amphibians of the plant kingdom: small, non-vascular, rhizoid-anchored plants of moist habitats whose motile antherozoids still need water to fertilise the archegonium. Their life cycle alternates a dominant green gametophyte with a dependent sporophyte (foot, seta, capsule) that yields meiotic spores. Marchantia shows the liverwort plan: a dichotomous thallus with gemma cups for vegetative spread and separate male and female plants bearing antheridiophores and archegoniophores. Economically, Sphagnum gives peat fuel, antiseptic dressing and nursery packing, while moss carpets bind soil, start soil formation and mark the first step of plant life onto land.

Mcs
1.

Bryophytes are called amphibians of the plant kingdom because they:

Options
  • Live in water and on land equally
  • Live on land but need water for fertilisation
  • Have vascular tissue
  • Grow in deserts
Answer

B

Explanation

They are terrestrial but their motile antherozoids require a water film to reach the archegonium.

2.

The dominant phase of the bryophyte life cycle is the:

Options
  • Sporophyte
  • Gametophyte
  • Zygote
  • Protonema
Answer

B

Explanation

The green, photosynthetic haploid gametophyte is the visible plant; the sporophyte is attached and dependent.

3.

Marchantia reproduces vegetatively by:

Options
  • Conidia
  • Gemmae in gemma cups
  • Zoospores
  • Soredia
Answer

B

Explanation

Lens-shaped gemmae in dorsal gemma cups are splashed by rain into new thalli.

4.

The Marchantia sporophyte consists of:

Options
  • Foot, seta, capsule
  • Rhizoid, seta, capsule
  • Foot, rhizoid, capsule
  • Stem, seta, capsule
Answer

A

Explanation

Foot (anchorage), seta (stalk) and capsule (spore production) — the standard bryophyte sporophyte plan.

5.

Elaters in the liverwort capsule help in:

Options
  • Fertilisation
  • Spore dispersal
  • Photosynthesis
  • Anchorage
Answer

B

Explanation

Hygroscopic elaters twist with humidity changes, loosening and dispersing spores.

6.

Peat used as fuel is formed from:

Options
  • Funaria
  • Sphagnum
  • Marchantia
  • Anthoceros
Answer

B

Explanation

Compressed Sphagnum (bog moss) forms peat — also used historically as antiseptic wound dressing.

7.

Which structure is ABSENT in bryophytes?

Options
  • Archegonium
  • Xylem and phloem
  • Antheridium
  • Chlorophyll
Answer

B

Explanation

Bryophytes lack vascular tissue — the reason they stay small and need moist habitats.

8.

Male and female Marchantia plants bear respectively:

Options
  • Antheridiophore and archegoniophore
  • Archegoniophore and antheridiophore
  • Gemma cups on both
  • Sori on both
Answer

A

Explanation

Marchantia is heterothallic — separate male (antheridiophore) and female (archegoniophore) plants.
