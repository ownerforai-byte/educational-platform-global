Notes

  • Nepal's diverse geography (tropical to alpine) supports rich biodiversity, but many species are threatened by habitat loss, poaching, and human-wildlife conflict.
  • One-horned rhinoceros (Rhinoceros unicornis) — Vulnerable; found in Chitwan and Bardia National Parks; recovered from ~100 individuals in 1960s to ~750 today.
  • Bengal tiger (Panthera tigris) — Endangered; population ~355 in Nepal (2022 census), concentrated in Terai protected areas.
  • Snow leopard (Panthera uncia) — Vulnerable; inhabits high Himalayas; threatened by poaching and retaliatory killing.
  • Red panda (Ailurus fulgens) — Endangered; found in eastern and central Himalayan forests; threatened by habitat fragmentation.
  • Gharial (Gavialis gangeticus) — Critically Endangered; freshwater crocodile of river systems; fewer than 200 in Nepal.
  • Himalayan musk deer (Moschus chrysogaster) — Endangered; hunted for musk pod; found in high-altitude forests.
  • Wild water buffalo (Bubalus arnee) — Endangered; restricted to Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve.
  • Conservation efforts: protected areas, anti-poaching units, community forestry, transboundary cooperation (India, China), and captive breeding programs.
  • Nepal's National Biodiversity Strategy and Action Plan (NBSAP) guides species conservation priorities.

Key Points

  • Nepal hosts flagship endangered species: rhino, tiger, snow leopard, red panda
  • Rhino recovered from ~100 (1960s) to ~750
  • Tiger population ~355 (2022)
  • Gharial is Critically Endangered
  • Conservation: protected areas, anti-poaching, community forestry, transboundary cooperation

Confusion

  • ❌ All endangered species live in national parks. ✅ Some live in community forests and buffer zones.
  • ❌ Tiger and leopard are the same species. ✅ They are distinct species with different habitats.
  • ❌ Red panda is a bear. ✅ It belongs to its own family Ailuridae, not Ursidae.
  • ❌ Gharial and mugger crocodile are identical. ✅ Gharial has a long narrow snout; mugger has a broad snout.

Examples

  • Chitwan National Park — rhino and tiger stronghold
  • Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve — wild water buffalo and gharial
  • Langtang National Park — red panda habitat
  • Shey-Phoksundo National Park — snow leopard range

Universal Facts

  • Nepal has 212 mammal species, 886 bird species
  • Nepal's forest cover ~44.7%
  • National Parks and Wildlife Conservation Act 1973 protects wildlife
  • Nepal doubled its tiger population by 2022 (TX2 goal)

Summary

Nepal's endangered species — including rhino, tiger, snow leopard, red panda, and gharial — reflect both severe threats and successful recovery efforts. Protected areas, anti-poaching, community forestry, and international cooperation have driven notable conservation successes.

Important Statements

  • The greater one-horned rhino is protected in Chitwan and Bardia. Its global rank is Vulnerable, after a recovery from a very small population.
  • The Bengal tiger is Endangered. Nepal's Terai parks hold the breeding population.
  • The snow leopard is Vulnerable and lives above the tree line. Retaliatory killing and poaching are the pressures.

Formulas

Name the site for wild water buffalo and the habitat of the red panda.

Steps
  • Wild water buffalo, Bubalus arnee, is the Koshi Tappu animal.
  • The red panda lives in temperate forest, not in that reserve's grassland.
  • Their ranks are both serious, and they are not the same sentence as extinct.
  • The rhino's parks are Chitwan and Bardia.
Answer

Koshi Tappu for the wild water buffalo. Temperate forest for the red panda.
