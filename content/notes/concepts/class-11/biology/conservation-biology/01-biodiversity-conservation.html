Enriched Content

Notes
  • Biodiversity: the variety of life at three levels — genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity.
  • Genetic diversity: variation in genes within a species; important for adaptation and survival.
  • Species diversity: number and abundance of species in an area; higher in tropics.
  • Ecosystem diversity: variety of habitats, biotic communities, and ecological processes in a region.
  • Biodiversity conservation: protecting and managing biological diversity; in-situ (in natural habitat) and ex-situ (outside habitat).
  • In-situ conservation: protected areas (national parks, wildlife reserves, conservation areas), community forests, sacred groves.
  • Ex-situ conservation: zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, gene banks, cryopreservation.
  • Why conserve? Food, medicine, industry, ecological services (pollination, water, climate), aesthetic, ethical, and intrinsic value.
  • Threats: habitat loss/fragmentation, overexploitation, pollution, invasive species, climate change, illegal trade (poaching).
  • Nepal: global biodiversity hotspot (Himalaya); 64% of forests; community forestry (22,000+ groups manage ~36% of forest land); national parks and conservation areas.
  • NEB focus: concept of biodiversity, its conservation, and in-situ vs ex-situ strategies.
Confusion
  • Biodiversity has THREE levels: genetic, species, ecosystem (not just "number of species").
  • In-situ conservation protects species IN their natural habitat; ex-situ conserves them OUTSIDE (zoos, seed banks).
  • Protected areas (in-situ) are the most effective large-scale conservation strategy.
  • Community forestry (in-situ) gives local communities rights and responsibility; it is Nepal's success story.
  • A biodiversity HOTSPOT has high endemic species AND severe habitat loss (both criteria).
  • Conservation is not just "protecting animals"; it includes genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity.
Practice
  • Define biodiversity and its three levels (genetic, species, ecosystem).
  • Explain in-situ conservation with Nepali examples.
  • Explain ex-situ conservation (zoos, seed banks, botanical gardens).
  • List the threats to biodiversity.
  • Explain the importance of community forestry in Nepal.
Universal Facts
  • About 64% of Nepal's total area is under forest; ~40% is under some protection.
  • Community forest groups (22,000+) manage ~36% of Nepal's forest land.
  • The Himalaya is one of the world's 36 biodiversity hotspots (high endemism + habitat loss).
  • Over 20,000 plant species, 1,000+ mammal/bird species, and many endemic species occur in Nepal.
Examples
  • Genetic diversity: different rice varieties in Terai and hills; different rhododendron species.
  • Species diversity: Chitwan (Bengal tiger, one-horned rhino), Annapurna (Himalayan flora).
  • Ecosystem diversity: Terai grassland, riverine forest, temperate oak-rhododendron, alpine meadow, nival zone.
  • In-situ: Chitwan National Park, community forests, sacred groves (deval-ghari).
  • Ex-situ: Nepal National Herbarium, seed bank, Kathmandu Zoological Park, botanical gardens.
