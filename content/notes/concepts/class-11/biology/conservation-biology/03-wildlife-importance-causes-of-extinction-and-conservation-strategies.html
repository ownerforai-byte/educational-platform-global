Notes

  • Wildlife includes all non-domesticated plants, animals, and microorganisms living in natural habitats, forming essential components of ecosystems.
  • Wildlife provides ecosystem services: pollination, seed dispersal, nutrient cycling, pest control, and maintenance of food webs.
  • Wildlife has economic value through ecotourism, medicinal resources, genetic diversity for agriculture, and sustainable harvest of timber and non-timber products.
  • Wildlife holds cultural, aesthetic, and ethical value, supporting indigenous knowledge and spiritual traditions.
  • Major causes of extinction: habitat destruction and fragmentation, overexploitation (hunting, poaching), invasive species, pollution, climate change, and disease.
  • Habitat loss is the leading cause of species extinction, driven by deforestation, agriculture, urbanization, and infrastructure development.
  • Overexploitation occurs when harvesting exceeds reproduction rates, as seen in ivory poaching and overfishing.
  • Invasive alien species outcompete native species for resources and alter ecosystem dynamics (e.g., Lantana camara in Nepal).
  • Conservation strategies include legal protection (CITES, national laws), protected areas, captive breeding, habitat restoration, community-based conservation, and international cooperation.
  • CITES (Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species) regulates cross-border trade in threatened species through Appendix I, II, and III listings.

Key Points

  • Wildlife = non-domesticated organisms in natural habitats
  • Ecosystem services: pollination, seed dispersal, nutrient cycling
  • Leading extinction cause: habitat destruction
  • Other causes: overexploitation, invasive species, pollution, climate change
  • Strategies: legal protection, protected areas, captive breeding, restoration
  • CITES regulates international wildlife trade

Confusion

  • ❌ Extinction and endangerment are the same. ✅ Extinct means no living individuals remain; endangered means at high risk of extinction.
  • ❌ Poaching and hunting are identical. ✅ Hunting may be legal and regulated; poaching is illegal.
  • ❌ Invasive species always increase biodiversity. ✅ They usually reduce native biodiversity.
  • ❌ Conservation only means protecting animals. ✅ It includes plants, fungi, and ecosystems.

Examples

  • One-horned rhinoceros — recovered from near-extinction in Chitwan through anti-poaching and habitat protection
  • Bengal tiger — protected under CITES Appendix I
  • Red panda — endangered due to habitat fragmentation in eastern Nepal
  • Snow leopard — threatened by poaching and climate change in Himalayas

Universal Facts

  • Over 1 million species are currently threatened with extinction (IPBES)
  • Habitat loss affects ~85% of threatened species
  • CITES has 184 member countries
  • Nepal's wildlife is protected under National Parks and Wildlife Conservation Act 1973

Summary

Wildlife underpins ecosystem function and human well-being, yet faces severe threats from habitat loss, overexploitation, invasive species, and climate change. Effective conservation combines legal frameworks, protected areas, captive breeding, and community engagement.

Important Statements

  • Wildlife means undomesticated organisms, not only large mammals.
  • It matters for pollination, seed dispersal, soil, tourism and for its own sake.
  • The main causes of loss are habitat clearing, poaching, pollution, invasive species and a changing climate.

Exercises

1.

Give two causes of extinction and two responses that are not the same tool.

Steps
  • Habitat loss and poaching are two causes.
  • A protected area answers habitat loss by keeping land.
  • Anti-poaching patrols and the law answer hunting.
  • A buffer zone and community forestry answer the fact that people live beside the habitat.
Answer

Habitat loss and poaching. Responses: a park, and patrols plus a buffer zone.
