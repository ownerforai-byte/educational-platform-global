Notes

  • The IUCN Red List is the world's most comprehensive inventory of the global conservation status of biological species, maintained by the International Union for Conservation of Nature.
  • Extinct (EX): no reasonable doubt that the last individual has died; no surveys can find it.
  • Extinct in the Wild (EW): survives only in captivity or cultivation, with no natural population.
  • Critically Endangered (CR): extremely high risk of extinction in the wild; e.g., population < 50 mature individuals or rapid decline.
  • Endangered (EN): very high risk of extinction in the wild; smaller populations or steeper declines than CR threshold.
  • Vulnerable (VU): high risk of extinction in the wild; larger populations than EN but still threatened.
  • Near Threatened (NT): close to qualifying for a threatened category.
  • Least Concern (LC): widespread and abundant; lowest risk of extinction.
  • Data Deficient (DD): insufficient information to assess extinction risk.
  • Not Evaluated (NE): not yet assessed against the criteria.

  • IUCN categories of threatened species: meaning of extinct, endangered, vulnerable, rare and threatened species

Key Points

  • IUCN Red List = global species conservation status inventory
  • EX: no living individuals remain
  • EW: survives only in captivity/cultivation
  • CR, EN, VU = threatened categories (in decreasing severity)
  • NT: close to threatened
  • LC: lowest risk
  • DD: insufficient data; NE: not evaluated

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Threatened' is a single category. ✅ 'Threatened' collectively refers to CR, EN, and VU.
  • ❌ Rare species are always endangered. ✅ Rarity alone doesn't imply endangerment; population trend matters.
  • ❌ Extinct in the wild means extinct. ✅ EW species still survive in captivity.
  • ❌ Data deficient means safe. ✅ DD means unknown risk, not low risk.

Examples

  • Tiger — Endangered (EN)
  • One-horned rhinoceros — Vulnerable (VU)
  • Red panda — Endangered (EN)
  • Snow leopard — Vulnerable (VU)
  • Dodo — Extinct (EX)

Universal Facts

  • IUCN Red List founded in 1964
  • Over 150,000 species assessed
  • More than 42,000 species threatened with extinction
  • Nepal has 27 mammal species listed as threatened

Summary

The IUCN Red List uses standardized categories (EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC, DD, NE) to assess extinction risk. Understanding these categories is essential for prioritizing conservation action and policy.

Important Statements

  • Extinct means no reasonable doubt that the last individual has died.
  • Endangered means a very high risk of extinction in the wild in the near future.
  • Vulnerable means a high risk, but not as immediate as endangered.

Exercises

1.

Place extinct, endangered, vulnerable and rare from the syllabus words, and say what threatened covers.

Steps
  • Extinct: none left.
  • Endangered: very high risk in the wild.
  • Vulnerable: high risk, less immediate than endangered.
  • Rare: small population, not always in fast decline. Threatened covers the risk categories, including endangered and vulnerable.
Answer

Extinct, then endangered, then vulnerable. Rare is a small population. Threatened is the risk group.
