  • Causes of extinction: habitat loss, poaching, pollution, climate change, invasive species, overexploitation.
  • Conservation strategies: protected areas, community forestry, anti-poaching laws (NTNC), ex-situ (zoos, seed banks), awareness, international agreements (CITES).

  • Biodiversity conservation involves safeguarding and scientifically managing diverse life forms to sustain their levels and provide lasting benefits for current and future generations.
  • It protects plant, animal, microbial, and genetic resources crucial for food production, agriculture, and ecosystem functions like soil fertilization, nutrient recycling, pest control, erosion prevention, and crop and tree pollination.
  • Conservation practices aim to manage nature and resources for present and future generations, ensuring the sustained well-being of humanity.
  • There are two basic strategies of biodiversity conservation: a.
  • In-Situ Conservation (within natural habitat): This method protects organisms in their natural habitats like national parks and other protected areas.
  • For instance, in Chitwan National Park, we have centers for Elephant Breeding, Gharial Conservation, and Vulture Conservation.
  • Ex-Situ Conservation (outside natural habitat): This type of conservation strategy includes protection of animals outside their natural habitats such as botanical or zoological gardens, cryopreservation, seed gene banks etc.

  • Protected areas: designated areas for conservation; in Nepal: 10 national parks, 6 conservation areas, 1 hunting reserve, and Ramsar wetlands.
  • National parks: Chitwan (1973, Bengal tiger + one-horned rhino), Bardia, Banke, Shuklaphanta, Koshi Tappu, Parsa, Rara, Langtang (1976), Sagarmatha (1976), Annapurna (1986), Manaslu (1986), Makalu (1992), Kanchenjunga (1989). NEB commonly cites 10 national parks; several sources list 11–14 depending on inclusion of the newer parks.
  • Conservation areas (6): Kanchenjunga, Manaslu, Shey-Phoksundo, Khoratadi, Khaptad, and Barun.
  • Ramsar sites: Koshi Tappu, Rara, Chitwan (Terai wetlands) — internationally important wetlands.
  • IUCN Red List categories: Extinct (EX), Extinct in the Wild (EW), Critically Endangered (CR), Endangered (EN), Vulnerable (VU), Near Threatened (NT), Least Concern (LC).
  • Threatened species: CR, EN, VU (at risk of extinction); Near Threatened; Rare; Extinct.
  • Endangered species in Nepal: Bengal tiger (EN), one-horned rhino (VU), snow leopard (VU), Asian elephant (EN), goral, musk deer, rhesus macaque, red panda (VU), Himalayan tahr (NT), kingfisher (some CR).
  • Causes of extinction: habitat loss, poaching, pollution, climate change, invasive species, overexploitation.
  • Conservation strategies: protected areas, community forestry, anti-poaching laws (NTNC), ex-situ (zoos, seed banks), awareness, international agreements (CITES).
  • NEB focus: national parks, wildlife reserves, conservation areas, Ramsar sites, IUCN categories, endangered species in Nepal, causes of extinction, and conservation strategies.
  • Wildlife: importance (ecological services, economy, culture) and causes of extinction.
Confusion
  • Chitwan National Park (1973) is the FIRST and most famous; it is home to the Bengal tiger and one-horned rhino.
  • Sagarmatha National Park (1976) includes Mount Everest; it is a UNESCO World Heritage Site.
  • IUCN "CR" = Critically Endangered (highest risk); "EN" = Endangered; "VU" = Vulnerable.
  • A national park is more strictly protected (no human habitation) than a conservation area (allows local people inside).
  • Ramsar sites are internationally important WETLANDS (not the same as national parks).
  • Community forests (in-situ) and national parks are BOTH conservation strategies; they differ in management (community vs government).
  • The one-horned rhinoceros (Rhinoceros unicornis) is VU (Vulnerable) globally; the Bengal tiger is EN (Endangered).

Summary

Nepal protects its biodiversity through 10 national parks (Chitwan, Bardia, Banke, Shuklaphanta, Koshi Tappu, Parsa, Ranipurna, Sagarmatha, Langtang, Annapurna, Makalu), 6 conservation areas (Kanchenjunga, Manaslu, etc.), 1 hunting reserve, and Ramsar wetlands. Chitwan (1973) and Sagarmatha (1976) are UNESCO World Heritage Sites. The IUCN Red List (EX, EW, CR, EN, VU, NT, LC) tracks species risk; in Nepal, the Bengal tiger (EN), one-horned rhino (VU), snow leopard (VU), and Asian elephant (EN) are key endangered species. Threats include habitat loss, poaching, pollution, and climate change. Conservation strategies (in-situ, ex-situ, community forestry, anti-poaching, CITES) are essential to prevent extinction. NEB focuses on protected areas, IUCN categories, endangered species, causes of extinction, and conservation strategies.
