Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Nepal as a Vertical Garden

    Nepal compresses five climatic zones from the Terai plains (below 300 m) to the nival zone above 5000 m — a vertical gradient that packs vegetation from sal forests to alpine meadows within 200 km. This altitudinal zonation is the syllabus's core: know each zone's altitude range, dominant species and character.

  • 2. Vegetation Zones of Nepal by Altitude (NEB 2023 & 2024)

    ZoneAltitudeVegetationTypical species
    1. Tropical zoneBelow ~1000 m (Terai, Siwaliks, Bhabar, low valleys)Tropical deciduous and evergreen forest**Sal (Shorea robusta)**, sissoo (Dalbergia sissoo), khair (Acacia catechu), simal (Bombax ceiba), Terminalia; grasslands (saccharum) of Terai
    2. Subtropical zone~1000–2000 m (Mahabharat, mid-hills)Subtropical broad-leaved and pine forest**Chir pine (Pinus roxburghii)**, Schima wallichii (chilaune), Castanopsis (katus), alder
    3. Temperate zone~2000–3000 mTemperate coniferous and broad-leaved forest**Oak (Quercus), blue pine (Pinus wallichiana)**, fir (Abies), hemlock (Tsuga), rhododendron (Rhododendron arboreum — lali gurans), deodar (Cedrus deodara) in dry west
    4. Subalpine zone~3000–4000 mSubalpine forest and shrublandBirch (Betula utilis — bhojpatra), fir, juniper, rhododendron shrubs
    5. Alpine zone~4000–5000 mAlpine meadows (bugyals), cushion plants, shrubsKobresia meadows, Primula, Gentiana, Rhododendron anthopogon, Juniperus
    6. Nival zoneAbove ~5000 mSnowline — no true vegetation; lichens and mosses on rocksCrustose lichens, Androsace

    2.1 Key contrasts (NEB 2021 & 2024)

    BasisTropical deciduous (sal forest)Temperate evergreen (oak–conifer)
    AltitudeBelow ~1000 m~2000–3000 m
    ClimateHot summer, monsoon rain; dry winterCool, moist, snowy winter
    Dominant speciesShorea robusta (sal), sissoo, khairOak (Quercus), blue pine, fir, hemlock, rhododendron
    Leaf habitDeciduous — shed leaves in dry season to save waterEvergreen — needle/broad leaves retained year-round
    Commercial valueSal timber — construction; most exploited zoneTimber, resin, fuel; slower growth

    2.2 Terai and Bhabar vegetation (NEB 2022)

    The Terai (alluvial plain) carries tall sal forests, riverine sissoo–khair forests and the famous tall grasslands (Saccharum, Phragmites, Themeda) — habitat of rhino, tiger and swamp deer. The Bhabar (boulder-strewn porous belt below the Siwaliks) carries drier sal and khair–sissoo forest with deep water tables. Four important species: sal, sissoo, khair, simal (plus elephant grass).

  • 3. Factors Controlling This Zonation

    FactorGradientEffect

    Rule of thumb: climbing 1000 m in Nepal ≈ travelling 1000 km toward the poles — the reason a day's walk crosses climate zones that span a continent.

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • Topic 24 (succession) → here: each zone's forest is a climax community determined by its climate.
    • Here → Topic 25 (adaptation): alpine cushion plants are cold-xerophytes; Terai sal is a drought-deciduous strategy.
    • Here → Topic 28–29: conservation of these zones happens in protected areas and through community forestry.
    • Here → geography/economics: timber (sal), resin (chir pine), tourism (rhododendron trails) tie vegetation to livelihoods.

    5.2 Model answers

    Q1 (5m/3m) — Major vegetation zones of Nepal by altitude. Answer: Nepal's vegetation rises through six zones: (1) Tropical (below ~1000 m) — sal (Shorea robusta) and sissoo forests with Terai grasslands; (2) Subtropical (1000–2000 m) — chir pine (Pinus roxburghii) with Schima and Castanopsis; (3) Temperate (2000–3000 m) — oak, blue pine, fir, hemlock and rhododendron (deodar in the dry west); (4) Subalpine (3000–4000 m) — birch (Betula utilis), fir and juniper shrubland; (5) Alpine (4000–5000 m) — Kobresia meadows, primulas and cushion plants; (6) Nival (above 5000 m) — snowline with only lichens and mosses. Temperature falling ~6 °C per 1000 m drives this zonation.

    Q2 (3m) — Tropical deciduous vs temperate evergreen. Answer: Altitude/climate: tropical deciduous lies below ~1000 m in hot monsoon climate with dry winters; temperate evergreen occupies 2000–3000 m with cool moist climate and snowy winters. Dominants: sal, sissoo, khair vs oak, blue pine, fir and rhododendron. Leaf habit: deciduous trees shed leaves in the dry season to conserve water; temperate evergreens retain needle/broad leaves year-round.

    Q3 (2m) — Sal's zone and two economic uses. Answer: Sal (Shorea robusta) dominates the tropical deciduous forest zone (Terai, Siwaliks, below ~1000 m). Uses: (1) hard, durable timber for construction, doors and railway sleepers; (2) resin/damar and sal seed oil (soap, candles) — also excellent fuel and furniture wood.

    Q4 (3m) — Terai and Bhabar vegetation with four species. Answer: The Terai alluvial plain carries tall sal forests, riverine sissoo–khair forests and extensive tall grasslands (Saccharum, Phragmites) that shelter rhino and tiger. The Bhabar, a porous boulder belt below the Siwaliks with a deep water table, carries drier sal and khair–sissoo open forest. Four important species: sal (Shorea robusta), sissoo (Dalbergia sissoo), khair (Acacia catechu) and simal (Bombax ceiba).

    Q5 (2m) — Temperate coniferous vs broad-leaved temperate forest. Answer: Coniferous: cone-bearing needle-leaved trees (blue pine, fir, hemlock, deodar), evergreen, adapted to cold and snow. Broad-leaved: flat-leaved trees such as oak (Quercus) and rhododendron, often evergreen but broad-canopied; both occur at 2000–3000 m but differ in leaf form, reproduction and snow tolerance.

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Placing chir pine in the temperate zone — it is subtropical (Pinus wallichiana is the temperate pine).
    2. Missing altitude ranges — every zone answer needs numbers.
    3. Calling alpine meadows forests — the alpine zone is treeless.
    4. Confusing Bhabar (porous boulders) with Terai (alluvial plain).
    5. Giving sal's uses as "oxygen" — write concrete products (timber, resin, seed oil).
Confusion
  • Chir pine vs blue pine: Pinus roxburghii = subtropical (1000–2000 m); Pinus wallichiana = temperate (2000–3000 m+).
  • Bhabar vs Terai: Bhabar = porous boulder belt with deep water table; Terai = fertile alluvial plain with sal and grasslands.
  • Alpine vs nival: alpine (4000–5000 m) has meadows and cushion plants; nival (5000 m+) is essentially beyond plants.

Summary

Nepal's vegetation stacks six climate zones up its slopes: tropical sal and sissoo forests below 1000 m; subtropical chir pine with Schima and Castanopsis from 1000–2000 m; temperate oak, blue pine, fir, hemlock and rhododendron from 2000–3000 m; subalpine birch and juniper to 4000 m; alpine Kobresia meadows and cushion plants to 5000 m; and the nival snowline beyond, where only lichens persist. Temperature falling 6 °C per 1000 m drives it all — altitude written as latitude. Sal dominates the tropical deciduous zone and supplies timber, resin and seed oil; the Terai and Bhabar carry sal, sissoo, khair and simal with the great grasslands that shelter Nepal's megafauna. To know Nepal's plants is to read altitude as climate.

Original Content

Notes
  • Vegetation introduction: the natural plant cover of a region; determined by climate, altitude, soil, and human activity.
  • Types of vegetation in Nepal: Terai subtropical (sal, grasslands), sub-montane (mixed deciduous), temperate (oak, rhododendron, chestnut), sub-alpine (pine, fir, juniper), alpine (meadows, dwarf shrubs, lichens), nival (bare rock, snow).
  • In-situ conservation: protecting vegetation in its natural location — protected areas, community forests, sacred groves.
  • Ex-situ conservation: conserving plant material outside the natural habitat — botanical gardens, seed banks, tissue culture, herbaria.
  • Natural environment-vegetation: the interdependence of climate, soil, water, and plants; vegetation regulates the water cycle, prevents erosion, stabilises the climate, and supports biodiversity.
  • Human activities affecting vegetation: deforestation (firewood, jhum/shifting agriculture, grazing, over-exploitation, illegal logging), habitat loss, climate change, invasive species, pollution.
  • Jhum (shifting/slash-and-burn) agriculture on hillsides causes severe erosion, landslides, and forest loss.
  • Over-grazing degrades alpine meadows; firewood collection degrades riparian forests.
  • Conservation strategies: protected areas, community forestry, anti-logging laws, reforestation, awareness, international agreements.
  • NEB focus: introduction to vegetation, types in Nepal, in-situ and ex-situ conservation, and the natural environment-vegetation relationship with human activities.
  • Importance of vegetation: oxygen, climate regulation, soil conservation, water, biodiversity, food, medicine, culture, and livelihood.
  • Nepal: ~64% of the country is forested; community forestry (22,000+ groups) manages ~36% of forest land.
Confusion
  • Vegetation is the NATURAL plant cover; plantations and cultivated crops are human-made, not natural vegetation.
  • In-situ conservation protects vegetation IN its natural habitat; ex-situ conserves plant material OUTSIDE.
  • Jhum (shifting) agriculture is a major cause of hill deforestation; it is different from permanent agriculture.
  • Over-grazing degrades alpine meadows; it is a separate threat from jhum.
  • Deforestation disrupts the water cycle and causes erosion, landslides, and climate change — not just loss of trees.
  • Community forests (in-situ) are community-managed; national parks are government-managed.

Summary

Vegetation is the natural plant cover of a region, determined by climate, altitude, soil, and human activity. Nepal's vegetation is zoned by altitude: Terai (sal, grasslands), sub-montane, temperate (oak, rhododendron), sub-alpine (pine, fir), alpine (meadows, lichens), and nival (bare rock). Conservation includes in-situ (protected areas, community forests, sacred groves) and ex-situ (botanical gardens, seed banks, tissue culture, herbaria) strategies. Vegetation is vital to the natural environment — it regulates the water cycle, prevents erosion, stabilises the climate, and supports biodiversity. Human activities (jhum, grazing, firewood, logging, climate change) threaten vegetation. NEB focuses on vegetation introduction, types in Nepal, in-situ and ex-situ conservation, and the natural environment-vegetation relationship with human activities.

    5.2 Model answers

    Q1 (5m/3m) — Major vegetation zones of Nepal by altitude. Answer: Nepal's vegetation rises through six zones: (1) Tropical (below ~1000 m) — sal (Shorea robusta) and sissoo forests with Terai grasslands; (2) Subtropical (1000–2000 m) — chir pine (Pinus roxburghii) with Schima and Castanopsis; (3) Temperate (2000–3000 m) — oak, blue pine, fir, hemlock and rhododendron (deodar in the dry west); (4) Subalpine (3000–4000 m) — birch (Betula utilis), fir and juniper shrubland; (5) Alpine (4000–5000 m) — Kobresia meadows, primulas and cushion plants; (6) Nival (above 5000 m) — snowline with only lichens and mosses. Temperature falling ~6 °C per 1000 m drives this zonation.

    Q2 (3m) — Tropical deciduous vs temperate evergreen. Answer: Altitude/climate: tropical deciduous lies below ~1000 m in hot monsoon climate with dry winters; temperate evergreen occupies 2000–3000 m with cool moist climate and snowy winters. Dominants: sal, sissoo, khair vs oak, blue pine, fir and rhododendron. Leaf habit: deciduous trees shed leaves in the dry season to conserve water; temperate evergreens retain needle/broad leaves year-round.

    Q3 (2m) — Sal's zone and two economic uses. Answer: Sal (Shorea robusta) dominates the tropical deciduous forest zone (Terai, Siwaliks, below ~1000 m). Uses: (1) hard, durable timber for construction, doors and railway sleepers; (2) resin/damar and sal seed oil (soap, candles) — also excellent fuel and furniture wood.

    Q4 (3m) — Terai and Bhabar vegetation with four species. Answer: The Terai alluvial plain carries tall sal forests, riverine sissoo–khair forests and extensive tall grasslands (Saccharum, Phragmites) that shelter rhino and tiger. The Bhabar, a porous boulder belt below the Siwaliks with a deep water table, carries drier sal and khair–sissoo open forest. Four important species: sal (Shorea robusta), sissoo (Dalbergia sissoo), khair (Acacia catechu) and simal (Bombax ceiba).

    Q5 (2m) — Temperate coniferous vs broad-leaved temperate forest. Answer: Coniferous: cone-bearing needle-leaved trees (blue pine, fir, hemlock, deodar), evergreen, adapted to cold and snow. Broad-leaved: flat-leaved trees such as oak (Quercus) and rhododendron, often evergreen but broad-canopied; both occur at 2000–3000 m but differ in leaf form, reproduction and snow tolerance.

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Placing chir pine in the temperate zone — it is subtropical (Pinus wallichiana is the temperate pine).
    2. Missing altitude ranges — every zone answer needs numbers.
    3. Calling alpine meadows forests — the alpine zone is treeless.
    4. Confusing Bhabar (porous boulders) with Terai (alluvial plain).
    5. Giving sal's uses as "oxygen" — write concrete products (timber, resin, seed oil).

Confusion

  • Chir pine vs blue pine: Pinus roxburghii = subtropical (1000–2000 m); Pinus wallichiana = temperate (2000–3000 m+).
  • Bhabar vs Terai: Bhabar = porous boulder belt with deep water table; Terai = fertile alluvial plain with sal and grasslands.
  • Alpine vs nival: alpine (4000–5000 m) has meadows and cushion plants; nival (5000 m+) is essentially beyond plants.

Summary

Nepal's vegetation stacks six climate zones up its slopes: tropical sal and sissoo forests below 1000 m; subtropical chir pine with Schima and Castanopsis from 1000–2000 m; temperate oak, blue pine, fir, hemlock and rhododendron from 2000–3000 m; subalpine birch and juniper to 4000 m; alpine Kobresia meadows and cushion plants to 5000 m; and the nival snowline beyond, where only lichens persist. Temperature falling 6 °C per 1000 m drives it all — altitude written as latitude. Sal dominates the tropical deciduous zone and supplies timber, resin and seed oil; the Terai and Bhabar carry sal, sissoo, khair and simal with the great grasslands that shelter Nepal's megafauna. To know Nepal's plants is to read altitude as climate.
