Enriched Content

Notes
  • Vegetation introduction: the natural plant cover of a region; determined by climate, altitude, soil, and human activity.
  • Types of vegetation in Nepal: Terai subtropical (sal, grasslands), sub-montane (mixed deciduous), temperate (oak, rhododendron, chestnut), sub-alpine (pine, fir, juniper), alpine (meadows, dwarf shrubs, lichens), nival (bare rock, snow).
  • In-situ conservation: protecting vegetation in its natural location — protected areas, community forests, sacred groves.
  • Ex-situ conservation: conserving plant material outside the natural habitat — botanical gardens, seed banks, tissue culture, herbaria.
  • Natural environment-vegetation: the interdependence of climate, soil, water, and plants; vegetation regulates the water cycle, prevents erosion, stabilises the climate, and supports biodiversity.
  • Human activities affecting vegetation: deforestation (firewood, jhum/shifting agriculture, grazing, over-exploitation, illegal logging), habitat loss, climate change, invasive species, pollution.
  • Jhum (shifting/slash-and-burn) agriculture on hillsides causes severe erosion, landslides, and forest loss.
  • Over-grazing degrades alpine meadows; firewood collection degrades riparian forests.
  • Conservation strategies: protected areas, community forestry, anti-logging laws, reforestation, awareness, international agreements.
  • NEB focus: introduction to vegetation, types in Nepal, in-situ and ex-situ conservation, and the natural environment-vegetation relationship with human activities.
  • Importance of vegetation: oxygen, climate regulation, soil conservation, water, biodiversity, food, medicine, culture, and livelihood.
  • Nepal: ~64% of the country is forested; community forestry (22,000+ groups) manages ~36% of forest land.
Confusion
  • Vegetation is the NATURAL plant cover; plantations and cultivated crops are human-made, not natural vegetation.
  • In-situ conservation protects vegetation IN its natural habitat; ex-situ conserves plant material OUTSIDE.
  • Jhum (shifting) agriculture is a major cause of hill deforestation; it is different from permanent agriculture.
  • Over-grazing degrades alpine meadows; it is a separate threat from jhum.
  • Deforestation disrupts the water cycle and causes erosion, landslides, and climate change — not just loss of trees.
  • Community forests (in-situ) are community-managed; national parks are government-managed.

Summary

Vegetation is the natural plant cover of a region, determined by climate, altitude, soil, and human activity. Nepal's vegetation is zoned by altitude: Terai (sal, grasslands), sub-montane, temperate (oak, rhododendron), sub-alpine (pine, fir), alpine (meadows, lichens), and nival (bare rock). Conservation includes in-situ (protected areas, community forests, sacred groves) and ex-situ (botanical gardens, seed banks, tissue culture, herbaria) strategies. Vegetation is vital to the natural environment — it regulates the water cycle, prevents erosion, stabilises the climate, and supports biodiversity. Human activities (jhum, grazing, firewood, logging, climate change) threaten vegetation. NEB focuses on vegetation introduction, types in Nepal, in-situ and ex-situ conservation, and the natural environment-vegetation relationship with human activities.

Human activities (jhum, grazing, firewood, logging, climate change) threaten vegetation.
