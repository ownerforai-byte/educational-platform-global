  • 1. Topic Introduction — Keeping Species Alive

    Conservation biology distinguishes two grand strategies: in-situ conservation protects species where they live; ex-situ conservation protects them elsewhere — in gardens, banks and cages. Nepal, a biodiversity hotspot with 2% of the world's flowering plants and flagship megafauna, uses both.

    6.2 Model answer

    Q (3m) — Distinguish in-situ and ex-situ conservation with one Nepalese example each. Answer: In-situ conservation protects species within their natural habitat, preserving ecosystems, interactions and ongoing evolution — e.g. Chitwan National Park, which conserves the one-horned rhinoceros and tiger in their Terai home (protected areas: national parks, reserves, conservation areas). Ex-situ conservation protects species outside their habitat through botanical gardens, zoos, seed banks, tissue culture and cryopreservation — e.g. the National Botanical Garden, Godawari, which maintains living plant collections (or the Khumaltar genebank storing crop seeds). In-situ is ecosystem-wide but vulnerable to habitat loss; ex-situ is species-specific but enables research and reintroduction.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Giving a foreign example when Nepal is asked — use Chitwan, Godawari, Khumaltar, Central Zoo.
    2. Calling seed banks in-situ — they are ex-situ.
    3. Confusing national park (strict) with conservation area (community multiple-use).
    4. Omitting the "why" (evolution continues vs paused) — the conceptual point earns marks.

Confusion

  • National park vs conservation area: park = strict state protection, no settlement; conservation area = community-managed sustainable use (Annapurna, ACAP).
  • Genebank vs botanical garden: genebank stores dormant seeds/cryo material; a garden grows living labelled plants.

Practice

  • Can a species conserved only ex-situ ever be truly saved?

    Answer. Only partly. Ex-situ methods — zoos, botanical gardens, seed banks, tissue culture, cryopreservation — preserve the genes and the individuals, but they pause evolution: no natural selection, no habitat, no ecological role, and adaptations to captivity or storage creep in. A species is truly saved only when a self-sustaining wild population is restored in its habitat (in-situ), as with Nepal's gharial and vulture captive-breeding programmes that end with release into protected rivers and forests. Ex-situ is the bridge; in-situ is the destination.

Universal Facts

  • Nepal doubled its tiger population between 2009 and 2022 — one of the few countries to meet the TX2 goal, entirely through in-situ conservation.

Examples

  • Chitwan National Park (1973) — Nepal's first park; rhino and tiger stronghold.
  • Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve — last refuge of the wild water buffalo (arna); Ramsar site.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What is a biodiversity hotspot?

    Answer. A region with exceptionally high endemic species richness undergoing severe habitat loss — the two criteria together. Nepal falls inside the Himalaya hotspot. Hotspots are where in-situ conservation investment buys the most species saved per rupee.

Key Points

  • In-situ: species in habitat — national parks (Chitwan 1973, Shey-Phoksundo largest), wildlife reserves (Koshi Tappu — arna), conservation areas (Annapurna ACAP), hunting reserve (Dhorpatan), Ramsar wetlands, Himalaya hotspot.
  • Ex-situ: outside habitat — botanical garden (Godawari), zoo (Central Zoo Jawalakhel), seed bank (Khumaltar genebank), tissue culture, cryopreservation, captive breeding (gharial, vulture).
  • In-situ preserves evolution; ex-situ pauses it.

Summary

Conservation runs on two strategies. In-situ keeps species where they live and evolve: Nepal's national parks (Chitwan, first in 1973; Shey-Phoksundo, largest), wildlife reserves (Koshi Tappu's wild buffalo), community-managed conservation areas (Annapurna), Dhorpatan hunting reserve and Ramsar wetlands protect whole ecosystems at scale. Ex-situ keeps species when habitat cannot: Godawari's botanical garden, the Central Zoo, Khumaltar's seed genebank, tissue culture, cryopreservation and captive breeding of gharial and vultures. In-situ preserves evolution itself but is vulnerable to poaching and climate shift; ex-situ pauses evolution but rescues the nearly-lost. The 2024 paper asked for one Nepali example of each — Chitwan and Godawari — and the concept behind them: the lifeboat versus the ocean.

1.

Nepal's first national park, established in 1973, is:

Options
  • Sagarmatha
  • Chitwan
  • Bardiya
  • Langtang
Answer

B

Explanation

Chitwan National Park (1973) began Nepal's protected-area system.

2.

The largest protected area of Nepal is:

Options
  • Chitwan
  • Annapurna Conservation Area
  • Shey-Phoksundo National Park
  • Koshi Tappu
Answer

C

Explanation

Shey-Phoksundo NP is the largest national park; Annapurna CA is the largest conservation area overall.

3.

Storing seeds at low temperature in a genebank is:

Options
  • In-situ conservation
  • Ex-situ conservation
  • Restoration
  • Succession
Answer

B

Explanation

Seed banks preserve genetic material outside the habitat — ex-situ.

4.

Koshi Tappu Wildlife Reserve is famous for:

Options
  • Snow leopard
  • Wild water buffalo (arna)
  • One-horned rhino only
  • Red panda
Answer

B

Explanation

Koshi Tappu holds Nepal's last wild water buffalo population; it is also a Ramsar wetland.

5.

Which is a community-managed conservation area?

Options
  • Chitwan NP
  • Annapurna Conservation Area
  • Parsa Reserve
  • Dhorpatan
Answer

B

Explanation

ACAP involves local communities in multiple-use management — the model of participatory conservation.

6.

A key advantage of in-situ conservation is that:

Options
  • It is cheap per species
  • Evolution and interactions continue naturally
  • It requires no land
  • It works without habitat
Answer

B

Explanation

Only in-situ preserves the whole ecological and evolutionary context.

7.

The National Botanical Garden of Nepal is located at:

Options
  • Khumaltar
  • Godawari
  • Sauraha
  • Koshi
Answer

B

Explanation

Godawari (Lalitpur) hosts the National Botanical Garden — Nepal's flagship ex-situ plant collection.

8.

Nepal lies within which biodiversity hotspot?

Options
  • Western Ghats
  • Indo-Burma
  • Himalaya
  • Sundaland
Answer

C

Explanation

The Himalaya hotspot — high endemism under heavy habitat pressure.

Important Statements

  • "Ex-situ is the lifeboat; in-situ is the ocean."

Formulas

  • In-situ conservation protects a species in the place where it already lives, so its partners and its climate stay with it.
  • National parks, wildlife reserves, conservation areas and buffer zones are the in-situ tools. Chitwan is the first national park. Annapurna is a conservation area where villages remain.
