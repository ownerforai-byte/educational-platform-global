Notes

  • [Remember] Vegetation is the natural plant cover shaped by climate, altitude, soil and water; Nepal's steep altitudinal gradient packs tropical to alpine zones within 200 km.
  • [Remember] Altitudinal zones: tropical Terai sal forest (<1000 m) → subtropical Schima–Castanopsis (1000–2000 m) → temperate oak–rhododendron (2000–3000 m) → subalpine fir–birch → alpine meadows → nival lichens.
  • [Understand] Human activities that degrade vegetation: shifting cultivation, fuelwood cutting, overgrazing, forest fire, encroachment and road building; Terai deforestation and Chure degradation as classic cases.
  • [Understand] Consequences: soil erosion and landslides, loss of biodiversity and wildlife habitat, drying springs, heavier floods in the Terai plains.
  • [Apply] Protection table — in-situ (national parks, community forests, buffer zones) vs ex-situ (botanical gardens, seed banks) with two Nepali examples of each.
  • [Analyze] Community forestry of Nepal as a world-cited success: degraded mid-hill slopes restored by handing management to local user groups.

Confusion

  • Vegetation is natural plant cover; a planted crop field or garden is NOT natural vegetation.
  • Sal dominates the tropical Terai; rhododendron–oak dominates the temperate hills — do not swap them.
  • The tree line ends tall trees, not all plant life; alpine meadows and lichens grow above it.
  • Community forestry protects through local management; it is not a ban on all forest use.

Universal Facts

  • Altitude mimics latitude: 1000 m of ascent resembles hundreds of km toward the poles.
  • Sal–Schima–oak–rhododendron–fir is the uphill dominant-species chant examiners expect.
  • Community forestry is Nepal's globally cited conservation success story.

Examples

  • Chitwan sal forest felled for settlement, then flooding worsens downstream each monsoon.
  • Mid-hill community forest restoring a bare slope within a decade of local management.
  • Chure sand–boulder mining drying village springs and destabilising the Siwalik range.

Name one pressure, one consequence, and the management that restored hill forest.

Answer
