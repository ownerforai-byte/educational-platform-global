Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Two-Way Street Between People and Plants

    Vegetation shapes human life — timber, fuel, fodder, medicine, water regulation — and human life reshapes vegetation, mostly destructively. Nepal's story is dramatic: from ~35% forest cover in the 1960s to under 30% by the 1990s, then a remarkable recovery to ~45% by the 2010s driven by community forestry. This topic covers the interaction and its lessons.

    3.1 Major causes of deforestation in Nepal (NEB 2024)

    1. Expansion of agriculture and settlements into forest land (Terai conversion).
    2. Fuelwood and fodder collection exceeding regeneration.
    3. Illegal and commercial logging (including the 1990s Terai timber scandal era).
    4. Overgrazing preventing sapling establishment.
    5. Infrastructure development — roads, dams, quarrying.
    6. Fires and encroachment, plus weak governance historically.

    3.2 Consequences

    Soil erosion and landslides on fragile slopes; springs drying; siltation of rivers and hydropower reservoirs; biodiversity loss (habitat specialists first); local climate drying; carbon release feeding climate change (Topic 26).

  • 4. Community Forestry — Nepal's Conservation Success Story (NEB 2024)

    Definition: a programme in which the government hands forest management to local Community Forest User Groups (CFUGs), which protect, regulate and use the forest under an approved operational plan, keeping most of the income.

    History: launched after the 1993 Forest Act (pilot in 1970s–80s); today >22,000 CFUGs manage ~2.3 million hectares, involving over a third of Nepali households.

    Roles in vegetation conservation:

    1. Protection and regeneration: local patrols end open access; degraded forests recover (biodiversity and canopy cover rose nationally).
    2. Regulated sustainable use: planned thinning, lopping and deadwood harvest replace uncontrolled extraction.
    3. Income for reinvestment: sale of timber, NTFPs (non-timber forest products) funds schools, nurseries and plantations.
    4. Local stewardship: communities monitor, plant and protect — enforcement cost drops dramatically.
    5. Equity and women's participation: many CFUGs led by women; fuelwood plantations near villages cut drudgery.

    Challenges: elite capture of benefits, uneven quality of plans, remote forest under-management, conflict over distribution.

    7.2 Model answers

    Q1 (2m) — Major causes of deforestation in Nepal. Answer: (1) expansion of agriculture and human settlement into forest land; (2) excessive fuelwood and fodder collection beyond regeneration; (3) illegal and commercial logging of valuable species (sal, khair); (4) overgrazing that prevents sapling survival; (5) infrastructure development (roads, dams, quarrying) and uncontrolled fires. (Any four with brief explanation.)

    Q2 (3m) — What is community forestry? Explain its role in vegetation conservation. Answer: Community forestry hands the management of a national forest to a local Community Forest User Group (CFUG), which protects and sustainably uses it under an approved operational plan and keeps most of the income. Roles: (1) local protection ends open access — degraded forests regenerate, raising canopy and biodiversity; (2) planned harvest replaces destructive extraction; (3) forest income funds nurseries, plantations and community development; (4) local stewardship and monitoring make enforcement cheap and effective; (5) participation (including women) builds long-term ownership. With over 22,000 CFUGs managing about 2.3 million hectares, community forestry is the main reason Nepal's forest cover has increased since the 1990s.

    7.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Writing only "cutting trees" as the cause of deforestation — list the distinct named causes.
    2. Describing community forestry as "government plantation" — the defining feature is community ownership and management.
    3. Omitting numbers (22,000 CFUGs, 2.3 million ha) that anchor the answer.
    4. Ignoring challenges — a balanced answer notes elite capture and distribution conflicts.

Confusion

  • Community forestry vs protected areas: community forestry = local user groups managing usable forest; protected areas = state-strict conservation with limited use.
  • Afforestation vs reforestation: afforestation plants trees where none grew recently; reforestation restores recently forested land.

Practice

  • Why might community forestry underperform in remote, sparsely populated forests?

    Answer. Community forestry runs on local people's labour and self-interest: households must patrol, prepare and follow the operational plan, and reinvest the income. In remote, thinly settled forests:

    1. too few users share the work, so protection falls to a handful of families;
    2. monitoring against outside loggers costs more than the forest earns;
    3. weak local institutions and poor markets mean little income to reinvest.

    Where the user group is small and distant from the resource, the incentive engine that makes Nepal's 22,000+ CFUGs work simply has too little fuel.

Universal Facts

  • Nepal's forest cover rose from about 26% (1990s assessments) to roughly 45% — among the fastest national recoveries, credited mainly to community forestry.

Examples

  • CFUG operational plan — the legal instrument giving a community both the right and the duty to manage its forest.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What are NTFPs?

    Answer. Non-timber forest products — goods harvested from forests other than timber: medicinal herbs (chiraito, yarsagumba), wild foods, resins, fibres and fodder. Sustainable NTFP enterprises give communities an income that keeps the forest standing rather than felled, aligning livelihoods with conservation.

Key Points

  • Forest services: provisioning (timber, fuel, fodder, medicine), regulating (water, flood, climate, carbon), supporting (soil, cycles), cultural.
  • Deforestation causes: agricultural expansion, fuelwood/fodder overharvest, illegal logging, overgrazing, infrastructure, fire.
  • Community forestry: CFUGs manage under operational plans, keep income; >22,000 groups, ~2.3 million ha; roles — protection, sustainable harvest, income reinvestment, local stewardship, participation.

Summary

Vegetation and people shape each other. Forests provision timber, fuel, fodder and medicine; regulate water, floods and climate; build soil; and carry cultural meaning. Human pressure — agricultural expansion, fuelwood and fodder harvest, illegal logging, overgrazing, infrastructure and fire — drove Nepal's deforestation, bringing erosion, landslides, drying springs and biodiversity loss. Nepal's answer became a world model: community forestry hands forests to local user groups who protect, sustainably harvest and profit from them under operational plans — more than 22,000 CFUGs now steward about 2.3 million hectares, and national forest cover has climbed since the 1990s. Conservation succeeds where the guardian and the beneficiary are the same household.

Overgrazing damages forest regeneration because:

Options

  • Livestock eat seedlings and compact soil

Grazing removes saplings and compacts ground — the next generation of trees never establishes.
