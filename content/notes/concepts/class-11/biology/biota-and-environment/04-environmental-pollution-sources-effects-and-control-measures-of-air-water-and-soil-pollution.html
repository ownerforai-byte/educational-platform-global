Notes

  • Pollution is the addition of substances or energy to air, water or soil at a rate the environment cannot make harmless. A pollutant is harmful because of its amount, its toxicity, or both.
  • Air pollutants include sulphur dioxide and nitrogen oxides from burning fuel, carbon monoxide from incomplete burning, smoke and dust, and ground-level ozone formed in sunlight from vehicle exhaust. They irritate lungs, damage leaves, and form acid rain when sulphur and nitrogen oxides dissolve in cloud water.
  • Water is polluted by untreated sewage, factory effluent, fertiliser washed off fields, and oil. Sewage and fertiliser add nutrients. Algae then bloom, die and are decomposed, and the decomposers use up dissolved oxygen. Fish die in that oxygen-poor water. This is eutrophication.
  • Soil is polluted by persistent pesticides, plastics and heavy metals from industry and dumps. DDT and similar fat-soluble poisons increase in concentration along a food chain, so top predators carry the highest dose. That increase is biomagnification.
  • Control follows the source. Use cleaner fuel and maintain engines for air. Treat sewage before it enters a river. Match fertiliser to the crop so the surplus does not run off. Prefer pesticides that break down, and do not burn plastic on fields.
  • Laws and local habits both matter. A treatment plant that is bypassed, or a pesticide that is sprayed on the day of harvest, undoes the technical control. Monitoring of river oxygen and of air smoke tells whether the control is working.

Confusion

  • Eutrophication is caused by too many nutrients, not by a poison in the ordinary sense. The fish die from lack of oxygen after the algal bloom collapses.
  • Biomagnification is not the same as bioaccumulation. Accumulation is the build-up inside one organism. Magnification is the rise in concentration from prey to predator along the chain.
  • Carbon dioxide from ordinary breathing is not listed with the classical air pollutants such as sulphur dioxide, nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide and particulates.
  • Biodegradable sewage is still a pollutant when there is more of it than the river can decompose without losing its oxygen.

Key Points

  • Air: SO2, NOx, CO, particulates; acid rain and lung damage.
  • Water: sewage and fertiliser cause eutrophication and oxygen loss.
  • Soil: pesticides, plastics, heavy metals.
  • Biomagnification: fat-soluble poisons such as DDT rise along the food chain.
  • Control means treating the source: fuel, sewage, fertiliser dose and pesticide choice.

Important Statements

  • A pollutant is defined by harm at the concentration present, not only by being unnatural.
  • Eutrophication kills fish by oxygen depletion after an algal bloom, not by the fertiliser burning them.
  • Biomagnification makes a dilute pesticide dangerous to top predators.

Examples

  • Sulphur dioxide from coal and diesel contributes to acid rain that damages leaves and acidifies lakes.
  • Fertiliser runoff into a lake produces green algal scum, then a fish kill.
  • DDT residues have been highest in fish-eating birds, a textbook case of biomagnification.

Universal Facts

  • Dissolved oxygen falls when bacteria decompose a large mass of dead algae.
  • Fat-soluble pesticides persist in body fat and are passed on when one animal eats another.

Formulas

  • Eutrophication sequence: nutrients → algal bloom → death and decay → oxygen used up → fish death.
  • Biomagnification: concentration rises at each step of the food chain for persistent fat-soluble substances.

Exercises

1.

A lake receives fertiliser from fields. Algae cover the surface, then die. Fish are found dead although the fertiliser itself is not a nerve poison. Explain the chain.

Steps
  • Extra nutrients enter the lake with the runoff.
  • Algae multiply into a bloom because nutrients were the limit on their growth.
  • The bloom dies and bacteria decompose that mass of organic matter.
  • Decomposition consumes dissolved oxygen, and fish die of oxygen shortage.
Answer

The fish die from oxygen loss after the nutrient-driven algal bloom collapses and decays. This is eutrophication.

Summary

Air, water and soil are polluted when harmful substances arrive faster than they can be made safe. Sulphur and nitrogen oxides acidify rain. Sewage and fertiliser drive eutrophication and oxygen loss. Persistent pesticides such as DDT biomagnify up food chains. Control works at the source: cleaner burning, sewage treatment, careful fertiliser use and pesticides that do not persist.
