Notes

  • A reflex is a rapid, automatic response to a stimulus. The path is a reflex arc: receptor, sensory neuron, a coordinator in the spinal cord or brain, motor neuron, and effector. The knee-jerk arc is handled in the spinal cord, so the leg extends before the brain has finished registering the tap.
  • Taxes are directed movements toward or away from a stimulus. Positive phototaxis is movement toward light. Negative phototaxis is movement away from it. Chemotaxis follows a chemical, geotaxis follows gravity, and thigmotaxis follows touch.
  • Kinesis is a change in speed or turning rate when a stimulus changes, without a fixed direction. An animal that simply moves faster in a dry patch, and slows down in a moist patch, shows kinesis. It is not a taxis, because it is not aimed.
  • Innate behavior is present without being taught, as in a reflex or a taxis. Learned behavior changes with experience. Most real behavior mixes the two: the ability to learn is innate, the particular lesson is not.
  • Dominance is a social ranking in which some individuals get priority to food, mates or resting sites. A pecking order in birds is a linear dominance hierarchy. Leadership is the tendency of a group to follow one individual when moving or fleeing. The leader is not always the most dominant animal.
  • These patterns matter in the wild because they decide who feeds, who breeds and how a herd escapes a predator. A reflex protects the body immediately. A taxis places the animal in a better spot. A hierarchy reduces constant fighting inside the group.

Confusion

  • A taxis is directed. A kinesis is not. Calling every movement a taxis loses the distinction.
  • A reflex does not require a conscious decision. The brain may be informed afterwards, but the knee-jerk does not wait for that.
  • The dominant animal is not automatically the leader of every movement. Dominance is about priority. Leadership is about being followed.
  • Negative phototaxis is not a failure to respond to light. It is an active movement away from light.

Key Points

  • Reflex arc: receptor, sensory neuron, coordinator, motor neuron, effector.
  • Taxis: directed movement, named by the stimulus and by the sign (positive or negative).
  • Kinesis: non-directional change in activity.
  • Dominance is a ranking for priority. Leadership is being followed during movement.
  • The knee-jerk coordinator is the spinal cord.

Important Statements

  • A reflex is fast because the arc is short and does not depend on conscious thought.
  • Taxes orient an animal in a gradient. Kinesis only changes how much the animal moves.
  • Social rank reduces repeated fights by making the outcome of a contest predictable.

Examples

  • The knee-jerk: a tap on the tendon stretches a receptor, and the leg extends.
  • Maggots often move away from light, a negative phototaxis.
  • Woodlice move more in dry air and slow down in moist air, a kinesis that leaves them in the moist place.

Universal Facts

  • The same reflex arc parts appear in vertebrates: a sensor, an incoming nerve, a central connection, an outgoing nerve and a muscle or gland.
  • Pecking order is the classic name for a linear dominance hierarchy, first described in flocks of hens.

Formulas

  • Reflex arc order: receptor → sensory neuron → coordinator → motor neuron → effector.
  • Sign of a taxis: positive = toward the stimulus, negative = away from it.

Practice

  • List the five parts of a reflex arc in order.
  • Classify knee-jerk, movement toward food odour, and speeding up in bright light as reflex, taxis or kinesis.
  • Distinguish dominance from leadership with one example.

Practice Questions

  • Why can a knee-jerk happen before you feel the tap?
  • How would you tell a taxis from a kinesis in a simple experiment?
  • What advantage does a stable hierarchy give a group?

Exam Notes

  • Draw the reflex arc and label all five parts. An unlabelled list is weaker.
  • Give the stimulus name and the sign for any taxis you mention, for example negative phototaxis.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Taxis has a direction. Kinesis has only a speed.
  • Knee-jerk lives in the spinal cord, not the knee and not the thinking part of the brain.

Special Notes

  • Name the stimulus in the taxis: photo, chemo, geo or thigmo.
  • Do not call a reflex a taxis. One is a body response through a nerve arc. The other is locomotion aimed at a stimulus.

Important Notes

  • The order of the arc is the usual lost-credit point. Write it in one direction only.
  • Add a social example, pecking order or a moving herd, if the question mentions dominance or leadership.

Exercises

1.

Woodlice walk quickly in dry bright air and almost stop under a damp stone. Is this best called a taxis or a kinesis, and why?

Steps
  • A taxis would be movement aimed toward or away from a stimulus, such as walking straight to the dark.
  • Here the animal changes its rate of movement as conditions change.
  • It slows where it is damp, so it spends more time there without steering along a compass direction.
  • A change in speed or turning, without a set direction, is kinesis.
Answer

It is a kinesis: activity falls in the damp place, so the animal accumulates there without a directed path.

Summary

Animal behaviour at this level covers the reflex arc, directed taxes, undirected kinesis, and social dominance and leadership. Reflexes protect the body through a short nerve path. Taxes and kineses place the animal in a better environment. Rank and leadership organise the group.
