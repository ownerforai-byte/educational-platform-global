Enriched Content

Notes
  • Animal behavior: the actions and responses of animals to stimuli; includes innate (fixed) and learned behaviours.
  • Reflex action: an automatic, involuntary response to a stimulus, mediated by a reflex arc (receptor → sensory neuron → CNS → motor neuron → effector).
  • Taxes: orientation responses (movement toward or away from a stimulus). Phototaxis (light), chemotaxis (chemical), thigmotaxis (touch), geotaxis (gravity).
  • Taxis vs Kinesis: taxis is DIRECTIONAL movement toward/away from a stimulus; kinesis is NON-directional (random) movement in response to a stimulus.
  • Dominance and leadership: social hierarchies in group-living animals (primates, wolves, fish); the dominant individual has priority access to food, mates, and territory.
  • Fish migration: seasonal movement to feeding or breeding grounds; e.g., salmon (upstream spawning), mackerel (Nepal Terai rivers).
  • Bird migration: long-distance seasonal movement (e.g., Himalayan birds migrate to lower altitudes in winter; storks migrate to the Terai).
  • NEB focus: reflex action, taxes, dominance and leadership, fish and bird migration.
  • Migration is a behavioural adaptation; it is seasonal and genetically programmed.
Confusion
  • A reflex arc is SHORT (spinal level); it does NOT involve the brain (faster response).
  • Taxis is DIRECTIONAL (toward/away from a stimulus); kinesis is NON-DIRECTIONAL (random movement).
  • Phototaxis: movement in response to light (positive = toward, negative = away).
  • Dominance hierarchies are ESTABLISHED through social interactions; they are not fixed by birth.
  • Fish migration is often seasonal (breeding, feeding); it is not random movement.
  • Bird migration is a BEHAVIOURAL adaptation; it is genetically programmed and often learned by first-year migrants.
Practice
  • Explain a reflex action with a reflex arc diagram.
  • Differentiate taxis from kinesis with examples.
  • List the types of taxes (phototaxis, chemotaxis, thigmotaxis, geotaxis).
  • Explain dominance and leadership in a social group.
  • Describe fish and bird migration with Nepali examples.
Universal Facts
  • The fastest reflex is the knee-jerk (patellar) reflex, with a latency of ~30–50 ms.
  • Some salmon (e.g., Atlantic salmon) migrate thousands of km upstream to spawn in the stream where they were born.
  • Honeybees use a "waggle dance" to communicate the direction and distance of a food source to nestmates.
  • Migration is one of the most spectacular animal behaviours; some birds fly >10,000 km per year.
Examples
  • Reflex: touching a hot object → hand withdraws (reflex arc: skin receptor → sensory neuron → spinal cord → motor neuron → muscle).
  • Phototaxis: earthworms move AWAY from light (negative phototaxis).
  • Chemotaxis: bacteria move toward a nutrient source (positive chemotaxis).
  • Dominance: a dominant male lion has priority access to females in the pride.
  • Fish migration: mackerel in the Kosi and Gandaki rivers migrate seasonally to feed and breed.
  • Bird migration: Himalayan birds (e.g., bar-headed goose) migrate to the Terai in winter; storks migrate to lowland lakes.

Summary

Animal behaviour includes reflex actions (automatic, spinal-level responses via a reflex arc), taxes (directional movements: photo-, chemo-, thigmo-, geotaxis), and kinesis (non-directional movement).
