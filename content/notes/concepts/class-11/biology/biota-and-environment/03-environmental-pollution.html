Enriched Content

Notes
  • Environmental pollution: the addition of substances or energy to the environment at a rate that exceeds its capacity to dilute, degrade, or absorb them.
  • Air pollution: SO2 (coal), NOx (vehicles), CO (incomplete combustion), PM2.5/PM10 (particulates), ozone (ground-level), CFCs; sources: industry, vehicles, power plants, biomass burning.
  • Effects of air pollution: respiratory diseases (asthma, bronchitis), acid rain, smog, global warming, ozone depletion.
  • Kathmandu Valley: PM2.5 exceeds WHO limits ~10× in winter (stove smoke, vehicles, dust).
  • Water pollution: domestic waste, industrial effluent, agricultural runoff (pesticides, fertilisers), oil spills, thermal pollution; effects: eutrophication, oxygen depletion, disease (cholera, typhoid).
  • Soil pollution: heavy metals, pesticides, plastics, industrial waste; effects: reduced fertility, contamination of food, leaching to groundwater.
  • Pesticides: chemicals to control pests; types: insecticides, herbicides, fungicides, rodenticides; effects: biomagnification, resistance, non-target species death, soil/water contamination.
  • Control measures: reduce emissions, renewable energy, tree planting, waste treatment, organic farming, international agreements (Montreal, Paris).
  • NEB focus: sources, effects, and control measures of air, water, and soil pollution; pesticides and their effects.
  • Nepal: vulnerable to air (Kathmandu), water (river pollution), and soil (pesticide) pollution; deforestation and urbanisation are major contributors.
Confusion
  • Acid rain (pH < 5.6) is caused by SO2 and NOx, NOT by CO2 alone.
  • Ozone is beneficial in the STRATOSPHERE (blocks UV) but harmful at ground level (smog, air pollutant).
  • Biomagnification: toxin concentration INCREASES at higher trophic levels (e.g., DDT in fish-eating birds).
  • Pesticide resistance: pests evolve resistance over time; higher doses are needed.
  • Eutrophication: excess nutrients (N, P) → algal bloom → O2 depletion → fish kill.
  • PM2.5 (fine particulates) are MORE harmful than PM10 because they penetrate deeper into the lungs.
Practice
  • List the sources, effects, and control measures of air pollution.
  • Explain the sources and effects of water pollution.
  • Describe the effects of soil pollution on the environment and health.
  • Explain pesticides (types, effects, biomagnification, resistance).
  • List control measures for all types of pollution.
Universal Facts
  • WHO guideline for PM2.5 is 10 µg/m³ (annual mean); Kathmandu Valley often exceeds 100 µg/m³ in winter.
  • DDT (banned in many countries) biomagnifies up food chains; it caused eggshell thinning in raptors (eagles, falcons).
  • Eutrophication is the #1 cause of "dead zones" (hypoxic zones) in lakes and coastal waters.
  • Nepal's community forestry program manages ~36% of the country's forest land.
Examples
  • Air pollution: SO2 from coal plants, NOx from vehicles, PM2.5 from biomass stoves and dust in Kathmandu.
  • Water pollution: domestic sewage in Bagmati and Karnali rivers; agricultural runoff (fertilisers, pesticides).
  • Soil pollution: heavy metals from industrial waste; pesticide residues in farmland.
  • Pesticides: DDT (insecticide) biomagnifies in the food chain; non-target species (bees, fish) die.
  • Control: tree planting, renewable energy, waste treatment, organic farming, international agreements.

Summary

Environmental pollution — air, water, and soil — is a major human-induced threat. Air pollution (SO2, NOx, CO, PM2.5, O3, CFCs) causes respiratory disease, acid rain, smog, and global warming; Kathmandu Valley's PM2.5 exceeds WHO limits ~10× in winter. Water pollution (domestic, industrial, agricultural) leads to eutrophication, O2 depletion, and disease. Soil pollution (heavy metals, pesticides, plastics) reduces fertility and contaminates food. Pesticides biomagnify up food chains, cause resistance, and harm non-target species. Control measures include reducing emissions, renewable energy, tree planting, waste treatment, organic farming, and international agreements.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects clear sources, effects, and control measures for air, water, and soil pollution.
  • Pesticides: types, effects, biomagnification, and resistance are a very common long-answer.
  • Kathmandu Valley air pollution (PM2.5) is a key Nepali application question.
  • Acid rain (SO2 + NOx) and ozone depletion (CFCs) are different processes.
  • Biomagnification: give a DDT example (raptor eggshell thinning).
  • Control measures: list 5+ for each pollution type.
Important Statements
  • Air pollution causes respiratory disease, acid rain, and global warming.
  • Water pollution leads to eutrophication, O2 depletion, and disease.
  • Pesticides biomagnify up food chains and cause resistance.
  • Control measures include reducing emissions, renewable energy, and organic farming.

  • Control = reduce + renewable + trees + treatment + organic.
