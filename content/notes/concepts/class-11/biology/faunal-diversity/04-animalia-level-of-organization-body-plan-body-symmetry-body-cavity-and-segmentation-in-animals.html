Desc

Organisation from cellular to organ-system, then the three kinds of body cavity, with symmetry and metamerism underneath.

Notes
  • Animalia is sorted, before any phylum name is used, by a short list of body characters: how the cells are organised, the body plan, the symmetry, the number of germ layers, the body cavity, and whether the body is segmented. Two animals that look nothing alike can still sit near each other if those characters match. Two animals that both live in water can sit far apart if they do not.
  • Level of organisation runs cellular, tissue, organ, then organ system. Porifera stop at the cellular level: the cells divide labour, but they do not form true tissues. Cnidaria reach the tissue level. Platyhelminthes reach organs, such as a pharynx and flame cells, without packing those organs into systems the way a worm with a closed blood system does. From Aschelminthes onward the body is built as organ systems: a complete gut, a body cavity, and a more concentrated nervous system.
  • Symmetry is the way a body can be cut into matching halves. Most sponges are asymmetrical. Cnidaria are radial: several planes through the oral-aboral axis give similar halves, which suits a life of waiting for food to arrive from any side. From flatworms onward the body is bilateral. One plane only divides it into left and right, and the front end gathers sense organs. That gathering is cephalisation. Adult echinoderms are the exception that returns to radial symmetry; their larvae are still bilateral.
  • Body plan names the layout of the gut. Sponges are a cell aggregate with canals, not a gut. Cnidaria and flatworms have a blind sac: one opening both takes food in and puts waste out. From roundworms onward the plan is a tube within a tube. The mouth and the anus are separate, so food can move one way while the animal keeps feeding.
  • Germ layers and the coelom decide how much space the organs have. Diploblastic animals, the cnidarians, have ectoderm and endoderm with jelly between them. Triploblastic animals add mesoderm. Flatworms are acoelomate: mesoderm fills the space as a solid parenchyma, and there is no body cavity. Roundworms are pseudocoelomate: a cavity is present, but mesoderm lines only the body wall, not the gut. Annelids and the phyla after them are eucoelomate. Mesoderm lines both the body wall and the gut. That true coelom lets organs move, grow, and be supported by fluid, which is why an earthworm can push through soil by changing the shape of its segments.
  • Segmentation, or metamerism, is the repetition of body units that each carry a share of the same organs. It is the annelid character, and it continues in arthropods and chordates. A tapeworm's proglottides are not this kind of segment: they are reproductive packets budded from a neck, and the animal is still an unsegmented flatworm. Bilateral symmetry, a true coelom, and metamerism together are the body of an earthworm, which is why that animal is the type used for the next notes.

Confusion

  • A blind sac and a tube-within-a-tube are different plans. Hydra and a liver fluke have one opening. Ascaris and an earthworm have a mouth and an anus.
  • A pseudocoelom is not a true coelom. In Ascaris the cavity is not lined by mesoderm on the gut side.
  • Adult starfish look radial, but they are eucoelomate and their larvae are bilateral. Radial symmetry does not put them with Hydra.
  • Proglottides of a tapeworm are not metameric segments. Metamerism, as used here, starts in Annelida.

Key Points

  • Cellular (Porifera), tissue (Cnidaria), organ (flatworms), organ system (roundworms onward).
  • Asymmetry, radial symmetry, bilateral symmetry with cephalisation.
  • Blind sac versus tube-within-a-tube.
  • Acoelomate, pseudocoelomate, eucoelomate.
  • Metamerism is serial segmentation, clearest in the earthworm.

Important Statements

  • A true coelom is lined with mesoderm on both the body wall and the gut.
  • Bilateral animals have one plane of symmetry and a front end where sense organs collect.
  • Level of organisation, symmetry, coelom and segmentation are decided before the phylum is named.

Examples

  • Sycon is cellular and irregular in shape, with a canal system rather than a gut.
  • Hydra is radial, diploblastic, and a blind sac.
  • Pheretima is bilateral, triploblastic, eucoelomate and metameric, with a mouth and an anus.

Universal Facts

  • Echinoderm larvae are bilateral even though the adult is radial.
  • The coelomic fluid of an earthworm is a hydrostatic skeleton: muscles squeeze it, and the segment changes shape.

Formulas

  • Cellular, tissue, organ, organ system.
  • Acoelomate flatworms, pseudocoelomate roundworms, eucoelomate annelids onward.
  • Blind sac: one opening. Tube within a tube: mouth and anus.

Exam Notes

  • A five-mark answer is a table: animal, level, symmetry, coelom, segmentation. One row each is enough.
  • Define the coelom before you use the word eucoelomate. The lining is the whole definition.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Flat none, round false, ringed true: acoelom, pseudocoelom, eucoelom.
  • One hole is a blind sac. Two holes is a tube.

Special Notes

  • Diploblastic stops at Cnidaria in this course. Every phylum from flatworms on is triploblastic.
  • Sponges are left asymmetrical. Do not force a symmetry on Sycon.

Important Notes

  • Organ level and organ-system level are not the same grade. Flatworms have organs. A complete gut plus a body cavity is the organ-system grade.
  • Radial symmetry in Cnidaria is a primary condition. Radial symmetry in an adult starfish is secondary.

Exercises

1.

Place Sycon, Hydra, Fasciola, Ascaris and Pheretima by organisation, symmetry and coelom.

Steps
  • Sycon is cellular, asymmetrical, and has no coelom. Its body is a canal system.
  • Hydra is tissue grade, radial and diploblastic, with a blind-sac gut and no coelom.
  • Fasciola is organ grade, bilateral and triploblastic, and acoelomate.
  • Ascaris is organ-system grade, bilateral, and pseudocoelomate, with a mouth and an anus.
  • Pheretima is organ-system grade, bilateral, eucoelomate and metameric.
Answer

Sycon cellular and asymmetrical; Hydra radial with no coelom; Fasciola acoelomate; Ascaris pseudocoelomate; Pheretima eucoelomate and segmented.

Bounds

  • This note stops at the body characters. The classes inside each phylum are the next note.
  • Germ-layer words here are ectoderm, endoderm and mesoderm only.

Summary

Animals are compared by organisation, symmetry, body plan, germ layers, coelom and segmentation. Sponges are cellular and asymmetrical. Cnidaria are radial, diploblastic and blind-sac. Flatworms are bilateral and acoelomate. Roundworms add a pseudocoelom and a complete gut. Annelids add a true coelom, lined by mesoderm on both sides, and metameric segments. Those characters identify an earthworm before its genus is named.
