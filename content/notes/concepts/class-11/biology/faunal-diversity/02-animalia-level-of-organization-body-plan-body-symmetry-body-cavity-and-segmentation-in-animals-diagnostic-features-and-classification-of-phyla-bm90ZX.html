MCQS

1.

Sponges are at which level of organization?

Options
  • Protoplasmic
  • Cellular
  • Tissue
  • Organ
Answer

B

Explanation

Porifera are multicellular with little coordination and no true tissues.

2.

Cnidaria are diploblastic, meaning they have:

Options
  • Two body cavities
  • Ectoderm and endoderm with mesoglea
  • Three germ layers
  • No germ layers
Answer

B

Explanation

Diploblastic = ectoderm + endoderm separated by mesoglea.

3.

Flatworms are acoelomate, meaning:

Options
  • They have no gut
  • Mesoderm fills the space between gut and body wall
  • Their cavity is unlined
  • They lack mesoderm
Answer

B

Explanation

No body cavity exists; mesoderm fills the space — limiting body thickness.

4.

In deuterostomes the blastopore becomes the:

Options
  • Mouth
  • Anus
  • Coelom
  • Notochord
Answer

B

Explanation

Deuterostomes: blastopore becomes the anus; the mouth forms secondarily.

5.

Adult echinoderms are radially symmetrical, but this is:

Options
  • Primitive radial symmetry
  • Secondary radial symmetry — larvae are bilateral
  • Actually asymmetry
  • A mistake in classification
Answer

B

Explanation

Echinoderm larvae are bilateral; adult radial symmetry is secondarily derived.

6.

True metamerism (internal and external segmentation aligned) is seen in:

Options
  • Nematoda
  • Annelida
  • Platyhelminthes
  • Porifera
Answer

B

Explanation

Annelids show true metamerism — each segment repeats nerves, vessels, nephridia and muscles.

7.

A pseudocoelom differs from a true coelom in that it is:

Options
  • Larger
  • Not fully lined by mesoderm
  • Filled with blood
  • Absent in adults
Answer

B

Explanation

The pseudocoelom is a blastocoel remnant not fully lined by mesoderm.

8.

In protostomes, cleavage is:

Options
  • Radial and indeterminate
  • Spiral and determinate
  • Absent
  • Radial and determinate
Answer

B

Explanation

Protostomes: spiral determinate cleavage; deuterostomes: radial indeterminate cleavage.

Enriched Content

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Body Plans That Sort the Animal Kingdom

    Animals differ first in how their bodies are built: how many cells cooperate, how the cells are organised, whether the body is symmetrical and how, whether a body cavity exists, and how the body is segmented. These characters — level of organization, body plan, symmetry, body cavity, segmentation — are the diagnostic framework used to classify the animal phyla up to class.

  • 2. Levels of Organization

    LevelDescriptionPhyla
    Protoplasmic (protoplasmic grade)Unicellular; all functions in one cellProtozoa (Protista)
    CellularMulticellular but little coordination; no true tissuesPorifera (sponges)
    TissueCells organised into tissuesCnidaria (Coelenterata)
    OrganTissues grouped into organsPlatyhelminthes onward
    Organ-systemOrgans form systems (digestive, nervous…)Annelida onward

  • 3. Body Symmetry

    TypeDescriptionPhyla
    AsymmetricalNo plane divides the body into mirror halvesMost Porifera
    RadialAny plane through the central axis gives mirror halves; suited to sessile or drifting lifeCnidaria, adult Echinodermata
    BilateralOnly one plane (sagittal) divides into mirror halves; head-tail axis; suited to directional movementPlatyhelminthes onward (most animals)

    Echinoderm exception: larvae are bilateral, adults secondarily radial — radial symmetry in echinoderms is secondary.

  • 4. Body Plans: Germ Layers and Body Cavity

    4.1 Germ layers

    PlanLayersPhyla
    DiploblasticEctoderm + endoderm (with mesoglea between)Cnidaria
    TriploblasticEctoderm + mesoderm + endodermPlatyhelminthes onward

    4.2 Body cavity

    PlanCavityPhylaConsequence
    AcoelomateNo cavity; mesoderm fills the space between gut and wallPlatyhelminthesDiffusion-limited, flat body
    PseudocoelomateBody cavity not fully lined by mesoderm (blastocoel remnant)Aschelminthes (Nematoda)Cavity for organs, but unlined
    Eucoelomate (true coelom)Cavity fully lined by mesoderm (peritoneum)Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, ChordataRoom for organs, hydrostatic skeleton, independent gut movement

    4.3 Protostomes vs deuterostomes (coelomates)

    BasisProtostomiaDeuterostomia
    Blastopore fateMouthAnus (mouth forms secondarily)
    CleavageSpiral, determinateRadial, indeterminate
    Coelom originSchizocoely (splitting of mesoderm)Enterocoely (outpocketing of archenteron)
    PhylaAnnelida, Arthropoda, MolluscaEchinodermata, Chordata

  • 5. Segmentation (Metamerism)

    Definition: repetition of body units along the length, each with similar nerve, blood vessel, excretory and muscle arrangements.

    PhylumSegmentation
    AnnelidaTrue metamerism — internal and external segmentation aligned (earthworm)
    ArthropodaExternal segmentation with jointed appendages; tagmatization (head, thorax, abdomen)
    ChordataSegmentation of vertebrae and muscles (internal)

    Segmentation allows regional specialisation (tagmatization) and redundancy — damage to one segment does not disable the animal.

  • 7. Exam Orientation

    7.1 Previous year questions

    No organization/body-plan question exists in the authored bank (the chordates vs non-chordates question of 2024 belongs to Topic 42). Questions below are NEB-style practice, clearly labelled.

    7.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Distinguish acoelomate, pseudocoelomate and coelomate body plans with one phylum each. Answer: (1) Acoelomate — no body cavity; mesoderm fills the space between gut and body wall; Platyhelminthes (flatworms) — flat bodies because diffusion limits thickness. (2) Pseudocoelomate — a body cavity not fully lined by mesoderm; Aschelminthes (Nematoda). (3) Coelomate — a true cavity fully lined by mesoderm (peritoneum); Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata, Chordata — allowing organ suspension and a hydrostatic skeleton.

    Q2 (2m) — Distinguish protostomes from deuterostomes. Answer: In protostomes the blastopore becomes the mouth, cleavage is spiral and determinate, and the coelom forms by schizocoely (Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca). In deuterostomes the blastopore becomes the anus, cleavage is radial and indeterminate, and the coelom forms by enterocoely (Echinodermata, Chordata).

    Q3 (2m) — Why are cnidarians called diploblastic and radial? Answer: Their body has only two epithelial layers — ectoderm and endoderm — separated by mesoglea (diploblastic), and any plane through the central axis yields mirror halves (radial symmetry), suiting their sessile or drifting life.

    7.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Calling sponges tissue-grade — Porifera are cellular grade with no true tissues.
    2. Treating adult echinoderm radial symmetry as primitive — it is secondary; larvae are bilateral.
    3. Calling the pseudocoelom a true coelom — it is not fully mesoderm-lined.
    4. Reversing blastopore fates — protostome = mouth first; deuterostome = anus first.
Confusion
  • Acoelomates have NO body cavity; pseudocoelomates have a cavity not fully lined by mesoderm; coelomates have a fully mesoderm-lined cavity.
  • Cnidaria are radial (adult) but their larvae may be bilateral; echinoderms are radial in adults but bilateral in larvae (secondary radial symmetry).
  • Segmentation (metamerism) is seen in annelids, arthropods, and chordates — NOT in sponges or cnidarians.
  • Porifera (sponges) have a cellular level of organisation (no true tissues); Cnidaria have tissue-level organisation.
  • Nematodes (roundworms) are pseudocoelomates; Annelida are coelomates.
  • All chordates have a notochord at some stage; in vertebrates it is replaced by the vertebral column.
Practice
  • Why can acoelomates not grow large and thick-bodied?
Universal Facts
  • Bilateral symmetry with a head-tail axis is the body plan of the vast majority of animal species.
Examples
  • Peripatus — connecting link between annelids and arthropods.
  • Earthworm — true coelom and true metamerism.
Practice Questions
  • Short answer (2m): Distinguish diploblastic and triploblastic animals. — Diploblastic animals (Cnidaria) have two germ layers, ectoderm and endoderm, separated by mesoglea; triploblastic animals have a third layer, mesoderm, which forms muscles, the coelom lining and most organs.
Formulas
  • Body cavity: Acoelom (no cavity) / Pseudocoelom (partly lined) / Coelom (fully mesoderm-lined)
  • Symmetry: Asymmetry (Porifera) / Radial (Cnidaria, Echinodermata) / Bilateral (Platyhelminthes onward)
  • Chordate features: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, post-anal tail
Key Points
  • Levels: protoplasmic (Protozoa) → cellular (Porifera) → tissue (Cnidaria) → organ (Platyhelminthes) → organ-system (Annelida onward).
  • Symmetry: asymmetrical (Porifera), radial (Cnidaria; secondary in adult Echinodermata), bilateral (Platyhelminthes onward).
  • Germ layers: diploblastic (Cnidaria) vs triploblastic (onward). Body cavity: acoelomate (Platyhelminthes), pseudocoelomate (Aschelminthes), eucoelomate (Annelida onward).
  • Protostomes (mouth from blastopore; spiral cleavage; schizocoely) vs deuterostomes (anus from blastopore; radial cleavage; enterocoely).
  • Segmentation: true metamerism (Annelida), external with tagmatization (Arthropoda), vertebral (Chordata).
Summary

Animals are sorted by how their bodies are built. Levels of organization climb from the protoplasmic grade of Protozoa through the cellular grade of sponges and the tissue grade of cnidarians to organ and organ-system grades. Symmetry runs from asymmetrical sponges through the radial cnidarians (secondarily radial adult echinoderms) to the bilateral plan of most animals. Germ layers split diploblastic cnidarians from triploblasts, and the triploblasts divide again by body cavity — acoelomate flatworms, pseudocoelomate roundworms, and true coelomates — and by blastopore fate, protostomes versus deuterostomes. Segmentation, true in annelids, external with tagmatization in arthropods and vertebral in chordates, allows specialisation and redundancy. These characters are the diagnostic framework for classifying every animal phylum.

  • Arthropoda is the most diverse animal phylum (insects, crustaceans, arachnids, myriapods).

Examples

  • Annelida: Pheretima (earthworm) — coelomate, segmented.
  • Arthropoda: Insecta (cockroach), Crustacea (prawn), Arachnida (scorpion).

  • Echinodermata: starfish — radial symmetry (adult), water-vascular system.
  • Chordata: Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia.

Summary

Animals are classified by level of organisation (cellular to organ system), body symmetry (asymmetry, radial, bilateral), body cavity (acoelom, pseudocoelom, coelom), and segmentation. The animal kingdom spans 9 major phyla from Porifera (cellular level) to Chordata (organ system level). Chordates are defined by a notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, and a post-anal tail; they include the 5 vertebrate classes. NEB examinations focus on phylum classification up to class and the chordate diagnostic features.

    1. Calling sponges tissue-grade — Porifera are cellular grade with no true tissues.
    2. Treating adult echinoderm radial symmetry as primitive — it is secondary; larvae are bilateral.
    3. Calling the pseudocoelom a true coelom — it is not fully mesoderm-lined.
    4. Reversing blastopore fates — protostome = mouth first; deuterostome = anus first.

Confusion

  • Acoelomates have NO body cavity; pseudocoelomates have a cavity not fully lined by mesoderm; coelomates have a fully mesoderm-lined cavity.
  • Cnidaria are radial (adult) but their larvae may be bilateral; echinoderms are radial in adults but bilateral in larvae (secondary radial symmetry).
  • Segmentation (metamerism) is seen in annelids, arthropods, and chordates — NOT in sponges or cnidarians.
  • Porifera (sponges) have a cellular level of organisation (no true tissues); Cnidaria have tissue-level organisation.
  • Nematodes (roundworms) are pseudocoelomates; Annelida are coelomates.
  • All chordates have a notochord at some stage; in vertebrates it is replaced by the vertebral column.

Universal Facts

  • Bilateral symmetry with a head-tail axis is the body plan of the vast majority of animal species.

Examples

  • Peripatus — connecting link between annelids and arthropods.
  • Earthworm — true coelom and true metamerism.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): Distinguish diploblastic and triploblastic animals. — Diploblastic animals (Cnidaria) have two germ layers, ectoderm and endoderm, separated by mesoglea; triploblastic animals have a third layer, mesoderm, which forms muscles, the coelom lining and most organs.

Formulas

  • Body cavity: Acoelom (no cavity) / Pseudocoelom (partly lined) / Coelom (fully mesoderm-lined)
  • Symmetry: Asymmetry (Porifera) / Radial (Cnidaria, Echinodermata) / Bilateral (Platyhelminthes onward)
  • Chordate features: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, post-anal tail

Key Points

  • Levels: protoplasmic (Protozoa) → cellular (Porifera) → tissue (Cnidaria) → organ (Platyhelminthes) → organ-system (Annelida onward).
  • Symmetry: asymmetrical (Porifera), radial (Cnidaria; secondary in adult Echinodermata), bilateral (Platyhelminthes onward).
  • Germ layers: diploblastic (Cnidaria) vs triploblastic (onward). Body cavity: acoelomate (Platyhelminthes), pseudocoelomate (Aschelminthes), eucoelomate (Annelida onward).
  • Protostomes (mouth from blastopore; spiral cleavage; schizocoely) vs deuterostomes (anus from blastopore; radial cleavage; enterocoely).
  • Segmentation: true metamerism (Annelida), external with tagmatization (Arthropoda), vertebral (Chordata).

Summary

Animals are sorted by how their bodies are built. Levels of organization climb from the protoplasmic grade of Protozoa through the cellular grade of sponges and the tissue grade of cnidarians to organ and organ-system grades. Symmetry runs from asymmetrical sponges through the radial cnidarians (secondarily radial adult echinoderms) to the bilateral plan of most animals. Germ layers split diploblastic cnidarians from triploblasts, and the triploblasts divide again by body cavity — acoelomate flatworms, pseudocoelomate roundworms, and true coelomates — and by blastopore fate, protostomes versus deuterostomes. Segmentation, true in annelids, external with tagmatization in arthropods and vertebral in chordates, allows specialisation and redundancy. These characters are the diagnostic framework for classifying every animal phylum.
