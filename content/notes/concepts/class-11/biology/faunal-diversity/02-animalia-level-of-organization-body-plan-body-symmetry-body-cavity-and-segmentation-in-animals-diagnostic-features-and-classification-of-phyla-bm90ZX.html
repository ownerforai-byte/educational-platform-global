Enriched Content

Notes
  • Levels of organisation: cellular (sponges) → tissue → organ system (most animals).
  • Body symmetry: asymmetry (sponges), radial (cnidarians, echinoderms), bilateral (most animals).
  • Body cavity: acoelom (no cavity, flatworms), pseudocoelom (partly lined, nematodes), coelom (fully lined, most higher animals).
  • Segmentation: annelids, arthropods, chordates (metamerism).
  • Phylum classification (up to class): Porifera (sponges), Cnidaria (coelenterates — jellyfish, Hydra), Platyhelminthes (flatworms), Nematoda (roundworms), Annelida (earthworms, leeches), Arthropoda (insects, crustaceans, arachnids), Mollusca (snails, octopus, clams), Echinodermata (starfish), Chordata (vertebrates + tunicates).
  • Chordata diagnostic features: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, post-anal tail (present at some stage).
  • Vertebrate classes: Pisces (fishes), Amphibia (frogs), Reptilia (reptiles), Aves (birds), Mammalia (mammals).
  • NEB focus: level of organisation, symmetry, body cavity, segmentation, and phylum classification up to class.
Confusion
  • Acoelomates have NO body cavity; pseudocoelomates have a cavity not fully lined by mesoderm; coelomates have a fully mesoderm-lined cavity.
  • Cnidaria are radial (adult) but their larvae may be bilateral; echinoderms are radial in adults but bilateral in larvae (secondary radial symmetry).
  • Segmentation (metamerism) is seen in annelids, arthropods, and chordates — NOT in sponges or cnidarians.
  • Porifera (sponges) have a cellular level of organisation (no true tissues); Cnidaria have tissue-level organisation.
  • Nematodes (roundworms) are pseudocoelomates; Annelida are coelomates.
  • All chordates have a notochord at some stage; in vertebrates it is replaced by the vertebral column.

Universal Facts
  • Arthropoda is the most diverse animal phylum (insects, crustaceans, arachnids, myriapods).
  • Porifera are the simplest animals with a cellular (no tissue) level of organisation.
  • Echinoderms (starfish, sea urchins) have a water-vascular system and radial symmetry in adults.
  • Chordata includes all vertebrates plus tunicates (Urochordata) and lancelets (Cephalochordata).
Examples
  • Porifera (sponges): Sycon, Spongaria — cellular organisation, radial/asymmetric.
  • Cnidaria: Hydra, Aurelia (jellyfish) — radial symmetry, tissue level, cnidocytes.
  • Platyhelminthes: Planaria (flatworm) — acoelomate, bilateral, dorso-ventrally flattened.
  • Nematoda: Ascaris (roundworm) — pseudocoelomate, cylindrical.
  • Annelida: Pheretima (earthworm) — coelomate, segmented.
  • Arthropoda: Insecta (cockroach), Crustacea (prawn), Arachnida (scorpion).
  • Mollusca: snail, octopus, clam — mantle + foot.
  • Echinodermata: starfish — radial symmetry (adult), water-vascular system.
  • Chordata: Pisces, Amphibia, Reptilia, Aves, Mammalia.

Summary

Animals are classified by level of organisation (cellular to organ system), body symmetry (asymmetry, radial, bilateral), body cavity (acoelom, pseudocoelom, coelom), and segmentation. The animal kingdom spans 9 major phyla from Porifera (cellular level) to Chordata (organ system level). Chordates are defined by a notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal slits, and a post-anal tail; they include the 5 vertebrate classes. NEB examinations focus on phylum classification up to class and the chordate diagnostic features.

  • Chordate = C-D-P-P (Chordate, Dorsal nerve, Pharyngeal slits, post-anal tail).
  • Body cavity: A=none, P=part, C=complete.
  • Radial = jelly/star; Bilateral = flatworm onward.
