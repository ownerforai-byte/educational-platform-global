Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Two Protozoans, Two Life Strategies

    Paramecium caudatum, a free-living ciliate, and Plasmodium vivax, the malarial parasite, show the two extremes of protozoan life: an independent predator-scavenger with elaborate organelles, and an obligate parasite with a life cycle woven into two hosts. Both are unicellular eukaryotes — the differences lie in how they feed, move and reproduce.

    3. Reproduction of Paramecium

    • Binary fission (asexual): transverse fission across the slipper's width; micronucleus divides by mitosis, macronucleus by amitosis; two identical individuals.
    • Conjugation (sexual): two compatible mating types join at the oral groove; micronuclei undergo meiosis; haploid nuclei are exchanged; the exconjugants divide. Conjugation rejuvenates the clone — after many asexual divisions, conjugation restores vigour. Macronucleus degenerates and is rebuilt from fusion products.

  • 4. Plasmodium vivax — Structure and Life Cycle

    Plasmodium vivax is a sporozoan causing benign tertian malaria; the life cycle needs two hosts: the female Anopheles mosquito (definitive host — sexual phase) and the human (intermediate host — asexual phase).

    In the human (asexual cycle):

    1. Inoculation: sporozoites are injected with the mosquito's saliva; they enter the bloodstream.
    2. Liver (pre-erythrocytic) schizogony: sporozoites enter liver cells and multiply asexually into thousands of merozoites; liver cells rupture, merozoites enter the blood.
    3. Erythrocytic schizogony: merozoites invade red blood cells, becoming ring-form trophozoites → schizonts → more merozoites; red cells rupture every 48 hours → the tertian fever chills of P. vivax.
    4. Gametocytes: some merozoites develop into male and female gametocytes — taken up by a mosquito bite.

    In the mosquito (sexual cycle): gametocytes → gametes; fertilisation in the mosquito gut; zygote → ookinete → oocyst on gut wall → thousands of sporozoites migrate to the salivary glands — ready to infect the next human.

Confusion

  • Conjugation in Paramecium does not double the population. Transverse binary fission does.
  • Contractile vacuoles are for water balance. Food vacuoles are for digestion.
  • The definitive host of Plasmodium is the mosquito, not the person, because sex happens in the mosquito.
  • P. vivax can relapse from hypnozoites in the liver. Treating only the blood stages misses that.

Practice

  • Why does conjugation restore vigour to Paramecium clones?

    Answer. After many asexual fissions a clone accumulates deleterious mutations and macronuclear wear — the lineage ages. Conjugation is a sexual reset: the micronuclei undergo meiosis and the two partners exchange haploid nuclei, so each exconjugant receives new gene combinations, replacing damaged recessive alleles with functional copies from the partner and re-shuffling variation. The macronucleus is rebuilt from the refreshed micronucleus. The result is restored fertility, growth rate and stress tolerance — sex bought at the cost of a pause in multiplication.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What is schizogony?

    Answer. Multiple asexual fission inside a host cell: the parasite's nucleus divides repeatedly before the cytoplasm splits, so one infected cell (a liver cell or red blood cell of Plasmodium) releases many merozoites at once — the step that multiplies the parasite explosively and produces the fever spikes.

  • Long answer (5m): Describe the structure of Paramecium and compare its reproduction with that of Plasmodium.

    Answer. **Structure of Paramecium: a slipper-shaped ciliate (~0.25 mm) bounded by a firm pellicle beneath the cilia that row it through water. The oral groove leads to the cytostome (cell mouth) for feeding; two contractile vacuoles with radiating canals pump out excess water; trichocysts discharge defensive threads. Inside are food vacuoles circulating in the cytoplasm and two nuclei — a large polyploid macronucleus (everyday functions) and a small diploid micronucleus** (inheritance).

    **Reproduction — Paramecium: asexual transverse binary fission across the middle; occasionally conjugation**, in which two individuals exchange haploid micronuclear products to rejuvenate the clone.

    **Reproduction — Plasmodium: a parasite with two hosts. In the human (intermediate host) it multiplies asexually by schizogony** — liver phase, then erythrocytic schizogony every 48 h that produces the tertian fever — and finally forms gametocytes. In the female Anopheles (definitive host) the gametes fuse, and sporozoites develop to be injected at the next blood meal.

    Contrast: Paramecium is free-living, feeding and swimming for itself, with simple fission plus optional sex; Plasmodium is an obligate parasite with no locomotor organelles, whose life cycle is a chain of multiplication phases alternating between two hosts and ending in sexual reproduction only in the mosquito.

Summary

Paramecium caudatum and Plasmodium vivax are two faces of the protozoan cell. Paramecium, a free-living ciliate, swims on coordinated cilia, feeds through an oral groove into food vacuoles, osmoregulates with two contractile vacuoles, and divides transversely — conjugation exchanging micronuclei to rejuvenate the clone. Plasmodium vivax, an obligate sporozoan, lives as a parasite with two hosts: schizogony in human liver and red blood cells every 48 hours produces the tertian fever, gametocytes pass to the female Anopheles — the definitive host — where sexual recombination yields sporozoites for the next human. One protozoan thrives by feeding; the other by hiding — both are single eukaryotic cells.
