Enriched Content

Notes
  • Male reproductive system: testes (2, kidney-shaped, along the dorsal wall) → vasa deferentia → seminal vesicles → cloaca; no copulatory organ.
  • Female reproductive system: ovaries (2, lobed, along the dorsal wall) → oviducts (funnel-shaped opening near the kidney) → uterus → cloaca.
  • Eggs: unfertilised ova are released into the oviducts; the oviduct adds a jelly coat; eggs are deposited in water.
  • Fertilisation: EXTERNAL; the male mounts the female (amplexus), releases sperm over the eggs as the female releases eggs; fertilisation in water.
  • Amplexus: male grasps the female behind the ribs (axillary amplexus in Rana) to ensure sperm is released near the eggs.
  • Development: fertilised egg → zygote → blastula → gastrula → tadpole (larva with gills + tail) → metamorphosis → adult frog.
  • Economic/ecological importance: pest control (eats insects), indicator of water quality (sensitive to pollution), food in some cultures.
  • NEB focus: male and female reproductive organs; external fertilisation; amplexus; development.
Confusion
  • Frogs have EXTERNAL fertilisation (in water), NOT internal.
  • The male does NOT have a copulatory organ; he releases sperm over the eggs during amplexus.
  • Amplexus: male grasps the female (axillary in Rana, inguinal in some frogs) to coordinate egg and sperm release.
  • The oviduct adds a jelly coat to the eggs; the jelly protects the eggs in water.
  • Tadpoles have gills + tail; adults have lungs + no tail (metamorphosis).
  • The testes are kidney-shaped and lie along the dorsal body wall; the ovaries are lobed and also on the dorsal wall.

Universal Facts
  • Frogs lay 5,000–20,000 eggs at a time; most do not survive, but external fertilisation ensures high numbers.
  • Amplexus (the male's grip on the female) is unique to most frogs; it is absent in most salamanders.
  • The tadpole-to-adult metamorphosis takes 2–3 months in most species.
  • Frogs are sensitive bio-indicators: they absorb pollutants through their skin, making them early warning species for water quality.

  • Amplexus: male (axillary) grasps female; egg + sperm release in water.
  • Development: zygote → blastula → gastrula → tadpole (gills + tail) → froglet → adult (lungs, no tail).

Summary

The frog's reproductive system is adapted for external fertilisation in water. The male has two kidney-shaped testes, vasa deferentia, seminal vesicles, and a cloaca (no copulatory organ). The female has two lobed ovaries, oviducts that add a jelly coat, a uterus, and a cloaca. During amplexus, the male grasps the female and both release gametes into the water, where external fertilisation occurs. Development proceeds from the zygote to the tadpole (gills + tail) to the adult frog (lungs, no tail) via metamorphosis. Frogs are ecologically important as pest controllers and water-quality bio-indicators.
