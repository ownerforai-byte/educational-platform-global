  • 1. Topic Introduction — One Parasite, Enormous Consequences

    Plasmodium falciparum is the most virulent human malarial parasite — the agent of malignant tertian malaria and most malaria deaths. Its "economic importance" is overwhelmingly negative: it drains health systems, depresses labour productivity, deters investment and tourism, and reshapes demography in endemic regions. A small part of its importance is scientific.

  • Deep dive — why falciparum is the expensive one. P. falciparum infects RBCs of ALL ages, so parasitaemia rises steeply; it causes capillary plugging (cerebral malaria) and kills within days if untreated. Every case avoided saves not just a treatment course but the cascade: hospitalisation, blood transfusions, lost workdays, orphaned households. That is why bed nets and one-cent drugs have the highest return-on-investment of almost any public-health measure — economics, not just medicine, argues for eradication.

    6.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    **Q1 (3m) — Explain the economic importance of Plasmodium falciparum.** Answer: P. falciparum causes malignant tertian malaria, the deadliest human malaria. Its economic importance is overwhelmingly negative: (1) heavy mortality — hundreds of thousands of deaths yearly, mostly young children; (2) lost labour productivity from fever episodes and chronic anaemia; (3) large health-system costs for diagnosis and artemisinin-based treatment, inflated by drug resistance; (4) deterred investment and tourism in endemic regions; (5) recurring vector-control expenditure on bed nets and spraying. A minor scientific importance: it is a model organism for host–parasite interaction and vaccine research.

    **Q2 (2m) — Distinguish P. falciparum from P. vivax malaria.** Answer: P. falciparum infects red cells of all ages, giving high parasitaemia, a 36–48-hour malignant tertian fever and complications such as cerebral malaria; P. vivax prefers reticulocytes, gives lower parasitaemia with a 48-hour benign tertian fever and rarely kills.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Listing only "causes malaria" — the topic demands the economic consequences, not just the disease.
    2. Confusing the fever periodicity (falciparum: 36–48 h malignant tertian; vivax: 48 h benign tertian).
    3. Ignoring indirect costs (investment, tourism, demography) — economic importance includes them.
    4. Forgetting the small positive scientific importance.

Confusion

  • Falciparum malaria shows NO relapse from liver hypnozoites (unlike vivax) — do not claim relapse for falciparum.
  • Crescent (banana-shaped) gametocytes point to falciparum; round gametocytes with Schüffner's dots point to vivax.
  • 'Economic importance' here is mostly harmful (disease burden) plus diagnostic and control value — state both sides.
  • Mosquito is the vector and second host, not a passive carrier; sporogony happens inside it.

Practice

  • Why does malaria control target the mosquito rather than only the parasite?

    Answer. Cutting the vector breaks transmission for every parasite strain at once:

    1. The mosquito is the bottleneck — the parasite must pass through the female Anopheles to spread; no mosquito, no new cases, however many parasites exist.
    2. Drugs attack one parasite species at a time and resistance evolves quickly (chloroquine resistance is widespread); vector tools — IRS insecticides, insecticide-treated nets, larval source management, draining breeding water — protect whole communities whether or not people are infected.
    3. The mosquito can be attacked in open-air, predictable stages (larvae in water, adults indoors), whereas parasites hide inside cells.

    Drugs still cure patients, but without vector control every cure is quickly followed by re-infection.

Universal Facts

  • The malaria belt coincides broadly with the tropical belt — climate controls both the vector and the parasite.

Examples

  • P. falciparum — malignant tertian malaria; most severe human malaria.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): Why is P. falciparum more dangerous than P. vivax?

    Answer. P. falciparum invades red cells of all ages, so parasitaemia climbs to enormous levels; its 36–48 h cycle produces malignant tertian fever, and clogged capillaries plus massive red-cell destruction cause cerebral malaria, severe anaemia and death. P. vivax invades only young reticulocytes, stays at low parasitaemia and is rarely fatal — debilitating tertian fever instead.

Key Points

  • P. falciparum: sporozoan; female Anopheles vector; malignant tertian malaria (36–48 h); infects all red cell ages → high parasitaemia; cerebral malaria, severe anaemia, death.
  • Economic costs: mortality (children under five), lost labour productivity, health-system and drug-resistance costs, deterred investment/tourism, vector-control spending.
  • Small scientific importance: model for host–parasite studies, vaccine research (RTS,S), parasite genomics.

Summary

Plasmodium falciparum, a sporozoan transmitted by the female Anopheles, causes malignant tertian malaria — the deadliest human malaria, with high parasitaemia, cerebral malaria and severe anaemia. Its economic importance is overwhelmingly negative: hundreds of thousands of deaths a year, lost labour productivity, heavy health-system and drug-resistance costs, deterred investment and tourism, and relentless vector-control spending. Its small scientific importance — a model for host–parasite interaction, vaccine research and parasite genomics — is the one positive entry in an otherwise costly ledger. Control targets both hosts: bed nets and spraying for the mosquito, artemisinin therapy for the parasite.

MCQS

1.

The most virulent human malarial parasite is:

Options
  • P. vivax
  • P. falciparum
  • P. malariae
  • P. ovale
Answer

B

Explanation

P. falciparum causes malignant tertian malaria and most malaria deaths.

2.

P. falciparum differs from P. vivax in infecting:

Options
  • Only reticulocytes
  • Red cells of all ages
  • Only liver cells
  • Only platelets
Answer

B

Explanation

Infecting red cells of all ages produces high parasitaemia and severe disease.

3.

The vector of P. falciparum is:

Options
  • Aedes aegypti
  • Female Anopheles
  • Culex
  • Sandfly
Answer

B

Explanation

The female Anopheles mosquito is the definitive host and vector of all human malaria.

4.

Cerebral malaria, a lethal complication, is caused mainly by:

Options
  • P. vivax
  • P. falciparum
  • P. malariae
  • P. ovale
Answer

B

Explanation

High falciparum parasitaemia obstructs brain capillaries — cerebral malaria.

5.

First-line treatment of falciparum malaria is:

Options
  • Quinine monotherapy
  • Artemisinin combination therapy
  • Chloroquine alone
  • Penicillin
Answer

B

Explanation

Artemisinin combination therapy is standard; resistance is closely monitored.

6.

Which is an indirect economic cost of falciparum malaria?

Options
  • Drug prices only
  • Deterred investment and tourism in endemic regions
  • Bed-net prices
  • Hospital food
Answer

B

Explanation

Endemic malaria deters investment and tourism — an indirect but large economic cost.
