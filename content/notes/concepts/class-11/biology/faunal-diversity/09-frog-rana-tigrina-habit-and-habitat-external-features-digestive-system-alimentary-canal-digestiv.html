Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Frog as a Vertebrate Type Study

    Rana tigrina (the Indian bullfrog) is the syllabus's chordate type specimen: an amphibian whose body plan — alimentary canal, heart, lungs, kidneys — is the template for vertebrate anatomy. This topic covers habit and habitat, external features and the digestive system; circulatory, respiratory and reproductive systems follow.

  • 2. Habit and Habitat

    • Amphibious: lives in fresh water and on moist land; hibernates in winter and aestivates in summer drought, buried in mud.
    • Nocturnal; carnivorous, catching insects with a protrusible bifid tongue.
    • Skin moist and glandular — cutaneous respiration; must stay near water for breeding.
    • Cold-blooded (poikilothermic): body temperature follows the environment.

  • 3. External Features

    FeatureDetail
    BodyDivided into head and trunk (no neck); streamlined; ~10–15 cm
    SkinSmooth, moist, glandular (mucus + poison glands); no scales
    HeadWide mouth; external nostrils; tympanum (eardrum) behind each eye; bulging eyes with nictitating membrane (protects under water)
    ForelimbsShort; 4 fingers (thumb absent in females; male has a nuptial pad on the first finger)
    Hind limbsLong, muscular, webbed toes — swimming and jumping
    CloacaCommon opening for digestive, urinary and reproductive tracts
    Sexual dimorphismMale: nuptial pad on first finger, vocal sacs (amplify croaking), thicker forearm

  • 4. Digestive System

    4.1 Alimentary canal

    PartStructure and function
    Buccopharyngeal cavityWide mouth on elastic ligaments; vomerine teeth (upper jaw) and maxillary teeth hold prey; protrusible bifid tongue flips out to catch insects; internal nostrils and Eustachian tubes open here
    OesophagusShort, wide tube to the stomach
    StomachCurved, muscular; gastric glands secrete HCl and pepsin; pyloric sphincter at exit
    Small intestineDuodenum + ileum; liver (large, bilobed) with gall bladder and pancreas pour bile and pancreatic juice in; digestion and absorption complete
    Large intestine / rectumWater absorption; opens into the cloaca
    CloacaCommon chamber for digestive, urinary and reproductive openings → cloacal aperture

    4.2 Physiology of digestion

    Frog is carnivorous: the tongue flips out, prey is seized and held by the teeth; in the stomach, HCl activates pepsin for protein digestion; in the intestine, bile emulsifies fat, pancreatic juice (trypsin, amylase, lipase) and intestinal juice complete digestion; villi absorb the products into the hepatic portal vein to the liver.

    6.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Describe the external features of Rana tigrina. Answer: The body divides into head and trunk with no neck. The head carries a wide mouth, external nostrils, bulging eyes with a nictitating membrane, and a tympanum behind each eye. The skin is smooth, moist and glandular. Forelimbs are short with four fingers (the male bears a nuptial pad on the first finger); hind limbs are long, muscular and webbed for swimming and jumping. The trunk ends in a cloaca. The male has vocal sacs and a nuptial pad — sexual dimorphism.

    Q2 (3m) — Describe the alimentary canal of the frog. Answer: The wide buccopharyngeal cavity bears vomerine and maxillary teeth and a protrusible bifid tongue for catching insects; a short oesophagus leads to the curved muscular stomach (HCl + pepsin); the small intestine (duodenum and ileum) receives bile from the bilobed liver and pancreatic juice from the pancreas, where digestion is completed and absorbed; the large intestine absorbs water and opens into the cloaca, the common exit for digestive, urinary and reproductive systems.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Giving the frog five fingers — the forelimb has four fingers (the male's thumb carries the nuptial pad).
    2. Placing the tympanum on the eye — it is a separate circular membrane behind the eye.
    3. Saying the frog is warm-blooded — it is poikilothermic.
    4. Forgetting the nictitating membrane or vocal sacs as male characters.
    5. Calling the frog's teeth for chewing — they only hold prey; there is no chewing.

Confusion

  • The frog chews with nothing. Maxillary and vomerine teeth only grip. The lower jaw has no teeth.
  • The tongue is hinged at the front, not at the back. A diagram with the root at the throat is a mammal's tongue.
  • The cloaca is not an anus. Faeces, urine and gametes share it.
  • Hibernation and aestivation are both dormancy. One answers cold, the other answers heat and drought.

Practice

  • Why must a frog's skin stay moist?

    Answer. Because the skin is a respiratory organ: gas exchange happens only by diffusion across a wet surface — oxygen dissolves in the film of moisture before it can cross into the capillaries, and CO₂ leaves the same way (cutaneous respiration dominates in water and works even at rest on land). A dry skin shuts this down, and with simple sac lungs as the only backup the frog would suffocate. Moisture also keeps the skin's mucus and defensive functions working — hence the frog's life around water and its drying-out risk on land.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What is the cloaca?

    Answer. A common posterior chamber into which the intestine, the urinary ducts and the reproductive ducts all open; it exits to the exterior through the cloacal aperture. Frog, like other amphibians, retains this single exit for faeces, urine and gametes.

Exercises

1.

A frog swallows an insect. State what happens to the insect's proteins and fats, in order of organs, and name the exit.

Steps
  • The mouth grips the insect with maxillary and vomerine teeth and swallows it. No enzyme acts there.
  • In the stomach, hydrochloric acid and pepsin begin protein digestion.
  • In the duodenum, bile from the liver emulsifies the fat and neutralises the acid. Pancreatic lipase then splits the fat. Pancreatic protease continues the proteins.
  • Intestinal enzymes finish both to absorbable products. Amino acids and the fat products are absorbed in the ileum.
  • Undigested remains pass through the rectum and leave by the cloaca.
Answer

Proteins start in the stomach and finish in the intestine. Fats are emulsified by bile and split by lipase in the duodenum. The exit is the cloaca.

Bounds

  • Heart and respiration are the next note. Reproductive organs of the frog are a later topic and are not written here.
  • Enzyme names are limited to pepsin, pancreatic protease, amylase and lipase, plus bile as a non-enzyme.

Summary

Rana tigrina, the amphibian type specimen, is a nocturnal, poikilothermic carnivore of water and moist land that hibernates and aestivates in mud. Its head and trunk carry bulging eyes with nictitating membranes, a tympanum behind each eye, and a wide mouth with vomerine and maxillary teeth and a bifid protrusible tongue; the moist glandular skin breathes cutaneously. Forelimbs bear four fingers (males add a nuptial pad), hind limbs are webbed for swimming, and the male adds vocal sacs. The alimentary canal runs from the buccopharyngeal cavity through a pepsin-secreting stomach, an intestine fed by liver and pancreas, and a water-absorbing large intestine into the cloaca — the common exit of the vertebrate body plan.
