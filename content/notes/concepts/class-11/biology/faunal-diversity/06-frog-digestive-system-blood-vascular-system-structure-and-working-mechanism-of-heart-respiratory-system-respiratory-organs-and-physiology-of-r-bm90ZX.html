Enriched Content

Notes
  • Digestive system: mouth (tongue) → buccal cavity → oesophagus → stomach → intestine (small + large) → rectum → cloaca; liver and gall bladder; pancreas; bile for fat digestion.
  • Circulatory system: 3-chambered heart (2 atria + 1 ventricle) → double circulation; oxygenated + deoxygenated blood partially mixed in the ventricle; systemic + pulmonary circuits.
  • Heart structure: right atrium (deoxygenated from body), left atrium (oxygenated from lungs/skin), single ventricle (partially mixed blood); ventricle has a spongy myocardium (crista ventriculi) that reduces mixing.
  • Respiratory system: lungs (pulmonary), moist skin (cutaneous), buccal cavity (buccal); tadpoles use gills.
  • Physiology of respiration: inhalation (buccal floor lowers → air enters through nostrils), exhalation (buccal floor rises → air pushed into lungs); skin respiration is significant in water and at night.
  • Working mechanism of the heart: atria contract first (fill ventricle), then ventricle contracts (pump blood to lungs + body); valves (atrioventricular, conus valves) prevent backflow.
  • NEB focus: digestive system, blood vascular system (heart structure + working), respiratory system (organs + physiology).
Confusion
  • The frog heart is 3-chambered (2 atria + 1 ventricle), NOT 4-chambered like mammals.
  • Blood is PARTIALLY MIXED in the frog ventricle (unlike the 4-chambered heart of birds and mammals where it is fully separated).
  • The spongy myocardium (crista ventriculi) in the frog ventricle helps reduce mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood.
  • Frogs use THREE respiratory surfaces: lungs, moist skin (cutaneous), and the buccal cavity; tadpoles use gills.
  • The cloaca is the common exit for digestive, excretory, and reproductive systems.
  • Bile (from the liver, stored in the gall bladder) emulsifies fat in the small intestine; it is not a digestive enzyme.

Universal Facts
  • The frog heart has a single ventricle with a spongy myocardium (crista ventriculi) that reduces blood mixing.
  • Frogs can lose up to ~25% of their water through the skin; cutaneous respiration is most active in water and at night.
  • The 3-chambered heart of the frog is an intermediate between the fish (2 chambers) and the mammal (4 chambers).
  • Bile from the gall bladder emulsifies fat in the small intestine; it contains no digestive enzymes.
Examples
  • Heart: right atrium (body blood) + left atrium (lung blood) + single ventricle (mixed).
  • Digestive: liver + gall bladder (bile) + pancreas (digestive enzymes).
  • Respiratory: lungs + moist skin + buccal cavity; tadpole gills.
  • Cloaca: common opening for digestion, excretion, and reproduction.

  • Working: atria contract → fill ventricle → ventricle contracts → blood to lungs + body
  • Respiration: buccal floor down (inhale) → nostrils close, mouth floor up (exhale to lungs); skin respiration
Key Points

  • Cloaca: common exit for digestive, excretory, reproductive.
  • Bile emulsifies fat; no enzyme in bile.
Summary

The frog's digestive system runs from the mouth (with a sticky tongue) to the cloaca, with the liver, gall bladder, and pancreas aiding digestion. The 3-chambered heart (2 atria + 1 ventricle) provides a partially mixed blood flow; the spongy myocardium reduces mixing. Respiration uses three surfaces: lungs, moist skin (cutaneous), and the buccal cavity; tadpoles use gills. The cloaca is the common exit for digestive, excretory, and reproductive systems. NEB focuses on heart structure and working, the digestive pathway, and the physiology of respiration.

1.

Why is the frog heart called "partially mixed"? Explain the role of the spongy myocardium.

ID

1

Steps
  • One ventricle receives both oxygenated (left atrium) and deoxygenated (right atrium) blood
  • Blood mixes in the single ventricle
  • Spongy myocardium (crista ventriculi) forms ridges that direct blood, reducing mixing
Answer

Because one ventricle mixes both types of blood; the spongy myocardium reduces (but does not eliminate) the mixing
