Desc

Four protozoan classes by locomotion: pseudopodia, flagella, cilia, or none, with one example each.

Notes
  • Protista is the kingdom of eukaryotic organisms that are mostly unicellular and do not sit cleanly inside plants, animals or fungi. An outline of the kingdom, as used in this course, takes in the photosynthetic and fungus-like groups as well as the animal-like ones: chrysophytes such as diatoms, dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds, and the protozoa. The rest of this note is the protozoa, because that is the group classified to class.
  • Protozoa are unicellular eukaryotes, microscopic, without a cellulose cell wall. A pellicle or a naked membrane bounds the cell. They live in fresh water, in the sea, in damp soil, or inside a host. Nutrition is holozoic, by engulfing food, or saprozoic, by absorbing dissolved food, or parasitic. A few, such as Euglena, are also photosynthetic in the light. Reproduction is asexual by binary fission or multiple fission, and some groups add a sexual process such as conjugation or the fusion of gametes.
  • The classes are separated by how the cell moves, or by the absence of any locomotor organ. Class Rhizopoda, also called Sarcodina, moves and feeds with pseudopodia. Amoeba is the example. The body shape changes. There is no fixed mouth.
  • Class Flagellata, or Mastigophora, moves with one or more flagella. Euglena has a flagellum and can photosynthesise. Trypanosoma, the sleeping-sickness parasite, is a flagellate with no photosynthesis. Having a flagellum is the class character. Being green is not.
  • Class Ciliata moves with many short cilia, has a definite shape, and usually has two kinds of nucleus: a large macronucleus for day-to-day control and a small micronucleus for sexual exchange. Paramecium caudatum is the example. The cilia also sweep food into a cell mouth.
  • Class Sporozoa has no locomotor organelle in the adult stage. Members are parasites and they reproduce by spores, often with multiple fission. Plasmodium, the malaria parasite, is the example. It is placed here because it lacks cilia, flagella and pseudopodia, not because it causes a famous disease. The disease is a consequence of that parasitic life.

Confusion

  • Euglena is a flagellate even though it is green. Photosynthesis does not move it into the plants for this classification.
  • Paramecium is not a flagellate. Its organelles are cilia, and it has two nuclei.
  • Plasmodium is a sporozoan. It does not crawl on pseudopodia inside the blood.
  • Protista is not only protozoa. Diatoms, dinoflagellates and slime moulds are in the kingdom outline too.

Key Points

  • Protozoa: unicellular eukaryotes, no cell wall, locomotion or parasitism defines the class.
  • Rhizopoda: pseudopodia, Amoeba.
  • Flagellata: flagella, Euglena and Trypanosoma.
  • Ciliata: cilia and two nuclei, Paramecium.
  • Sporozoa: no locomotor organ, parasitic, spores, Plasmodium.

Important Statements

  • Locomotion is the classification character for the first three classes.
  • Sporozoa are defined by what they lack and by their spore-forming parasitism.
  • A macronucleus plus a micronucleus is the nuclear pattern to associate with ciliates.

Examples

  • Amoeba engulfs a bacterium with a pseudopodium.
  • Trypanosoma swims in blood with a flagellum.
  • Plasmodium multiplies inside liver cells and red blood cells without cilia or flagella.

Universal Facts

  • Binary fission in Paramecium is transverse. In Euglena it is longitudinal.
  • Euglena is mixotrophic: photosynthetic in light, and able to take organic food in the dark.

Formulas

  • Rhizopoda pseudopodia, Flagellata flagella, Ciliata cilia, Sporozoa none.
  • Ciliate nuclei: macronucleus for vegetative control, micronucleus for genetic exchange.

Practice

  • Place Amoeba, Euglena, Paramecium and Plasmodium in their classes and name the locomotor structure.
  • Give three diagnostic features shared by protozoa.
  • Say why Euglena is not removed from Flagellata for being green.

Practice Questions

  • What nuclear feature separates a ciliate from an amoeba?
  • Why is Plasmodium not put with the flagellates?
  • Name two protist groups that are not protozoa.

Exam Notes

  • Write the four classes as a table: class, locomotion, one example. That is the whole classification question.
  • Add 'no cell wall, unicellular, eukaryotic' when the question says diagnostic features.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Amoeba feet, Euglena whip, Paramecium hairs, Plasmodium none.
  • Two nuclei means ciliate.

Special Notes

  • Class names have older synonyms: Sarcodina for Rhizopoda, Mastigophora for Flagellata. Either pair is acceptable if the example matches.
  • Do not invent extra classes. These four cover the course.

Important Notes

  • Slime moulds and diatoms belong in the kingdom outline and not in the four protozoan classes.
  • Multiple fission is the asexual pattern to mention for Plasmodium. Binary fission is the pattern for Amoeba and Paramecium.

Exercises

1.

Assign Amoeba, Euglena, Paramecium and Plasmodium to classes.

Steps
  • Amoeba moves by pseudopodia, so it is Rhizopoda.
  • Euglena moves by a flagellum, so it is Flagellata, whether or not it is green.
  • Paramecium moves by cilia and has two kinds of nucleus, so it is Ciliata.
  • Plasmodium has no locomotor organelle and is a spore-forming parasite, so it is Sporozoa.
Answer

Rhizopoda, Flagellata, Ciliata, Sporozoa, in that organism order.

Summary

Protista includes unicellular eukaryotes such as diatoms, dinoflagellates, euglenoids, slime moulds and protozoa. Protozoa have no cell wall and are classified to class by locomotion: Rhizopoda by pseudopodia (Amoeba), Flagellata by flagella (Euglena, Trypanosoma), Ciliata by cilia and two nuclei (Paramecium), and Sporozoa by the lack of those organs and a spore-forming parasitic life (Plasmodium).
