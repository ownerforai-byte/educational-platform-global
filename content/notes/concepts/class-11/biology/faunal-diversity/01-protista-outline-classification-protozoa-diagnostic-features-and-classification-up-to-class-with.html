Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Single-Celled Kingdom

    Protista gathers eukaryotes that are not plants, animals or fungi: mostly single-celled organisms living in water or moist environments. The syllabus focuses on Protozoa — the animal-like protists — and their classification up to class with examples.

  • 2. General Characteristics of Protista

    FeatureDetail
    Cell typeEukaryotic, mostly unicellular; some colonial
    HabitatAquatic, moist soil; parasites inside hosts
    NutritionAutotrophic (algae), heterotrophic (protozoa), or mixotrophic (euglenoids)
    OrganellesTrue nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; specialized organelles replace organs (contractile vacuole, pellicle)
    ReproductionAsexual (binary fission, multiple fission) and sexual (conjugation, syngamy)

    2.1 Classification of Protozoa up to class

    Class (subphylum Sarcomastigophora etc.)Locomotor organExample
    Rhizopoda (Sarcodina)Pseudopodia ("false feet")Amoeba, Entamoeba
    Mastigophora (Zoomastigina/Flagellata)FlagellaTrypanosoma, Giardia
    Ciliophora (Ciliata)Cilia; two nuclei (macro- and micronucleus)Paramecium, Vorticella
    Sporozoa (Apicomplexa)No locomotor organelle in adult; parasiticPlasmodium, Eimeria

    All four classes are unicellular eukaryotes; they differ chiefly in locomotion and nutrition. Sporozoa are entirely parasitic with a complex life cycle involving alternating sexual and asexual phases in a host.

    4.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Classify Protozoa up to class with one example of each. Answer: (1) Rhizopoda (Sarcodina) — move by pseudopodia; Amoeba. (2) Mastigophora (Flagellata) — move by flagella; Trypanosoma. (3) Ciliophora — move by cilia, with macro- and micronuclei; Paramecium. (4) Sporozoa — non-motile adult parasites with complex life cycles; Plasmodium.

    **Q2 (2m) — Why is Euglena placed in Protista and not in Plantae?** Answer: Euglena is unicellular, lacks a cell wall of cellulose, and though it has chloroplasts it can also feed heterotrophically in darkness — a mix of plant-like and animal-like characters that fits neither Plantae nor Animalia, so it is placed in Protista.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Calling protozoa prokaryotes — they are eukaryotes (contrast with Monera).
    2. Assigning flagella to Sporozoa — adult sporozoans are non-motile; locomotion by gliding/flagellated gametes at most.
    3. Forgetting the two nuclei of Ciliophora (macro- and micronucleus).
    4. Writing Paramecium as a sporozoan — it is a ciliate.
Confusion
  • Pseudopodia vs cilia vs flagella: pseudopodia are flowing cell extensions (Rhizopoda); cilia are many short oars (Ciliophora); flagella are few long whips (Mastigophora).
  • Protista vs Monera: protists are eukaryotic with a true nucleus; Monera are prokaryotic.

Practice

  • Why are sporozoans the only protozoan class without locomotor organelles?

    Answer. Because they live an entirely internal parasitic life: they are carried passively — in blood, in the mosquito's salivary flow, through gut walls — and never need to seek food or mates on their own. Locomotion costs energy and builds structure; a sporozoan like Plasmodium invests everything in invasion and multiplication inside host cells. Free-living classes (Rhizopoda, Mastigophora, Ciliophora) must move to feed, so each evolved pseudopodia, flagella or cilia; the sporozoan's environment made movement pointless.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What is a contractile vacuole?

    Answer. An organelle of freshwater protozoa (Paramecium, Amoeba) that continuously collects the water that osmotically floods in and expels it to the exterior, osmoregulating the cell so it does not swell and burst. Marine protozoa, whose body fluids match the sea, generally lack them.

    Answer. Phylum Protozoa — unicellular eukaryotes, classified chiefly by the locomotor organelle:

Exercises

1.

Assign Amoeba, Euglena, Paramecium and Plasmodium to classes.

Steps
  • Amoeba moves by pseudopodia, so it is Rhizopoda.
  • Euglena moves by a flagellum, so it is Flagellata, whether or not it is green.
  • Paramecium moves by cilia and has two kinds of nucleus, so it is Ciliata.
  • Plasmodium has no locomotor organelle and is a spore-forming parasite, so it is Sporozoa.
Answer

Rhizopoda, Flagellata, Ciliata, Sporozoa, in that organism order.

Summary

Protista holds the eukaryotes that are neither plants, animals nor fungi — mostly single-celled aquatic organisms. Protozoa, their animal-like members, are classified by locomotion: Rhizopoda creep on pseudopodia (Amoeba), Mastigophora swim on flagella (Trypanosoma), Ciliophora row on cilia with two nuclei (Paramecium), and Sporozoa glide as non-motile obligate parasites with complex life cycles (Plasmodium). All are eukaryotic cells whose organelles do the work of organs — contractile vacuoles osmoregulate, pellicles shape, pseudopodia feed. The four classes supply the syllabus specimens Paramecium and Plasmodium and most of the protozoal diseases of humans.

  • Ciliophora
  • Sporozoa
  • Mastigophora
  • Rhizopoda
Answer

B

Plasmodium is a non-motile obligate parasite with a complex life cycle — Sporozoa.

Ciliophora are characterised by:

Options
  • Pseudopodia only
  • Two types of nuclei
  • Flagella only
  • No nucleus
Answer

B

Explanation

Ciliates have both a macronucleus and one or more micronuclei, plus cilia.

5.

Protozoa differ from bacteria in that protozoa:

Options
  • Lack a cell membrane
  • Are eukaryotic
  • Reproduce only sexually
  • Have cell walls of peptidoglycan
Answer

B

Explanation

Protozoa are eukaryotic; bacteria are prokaryotic with 70S ribosomes and peptidoglycan walls.

Options
  • Pseudopodia
  • Cilia
  • Flagella
  • Gliding only as an adult
Answer

C

Explanation

Trypanosoma is a mastigophoran (flagellate) that swims with a flagellum.

Which protozoan class is entirely parasitic?

  • Rhizopoda
  • Mastigophora
  • Ciliophora
  • Sporozoa

D

Sporozoa (Apicomplexa) are obligate parasites with complex life cycles.
