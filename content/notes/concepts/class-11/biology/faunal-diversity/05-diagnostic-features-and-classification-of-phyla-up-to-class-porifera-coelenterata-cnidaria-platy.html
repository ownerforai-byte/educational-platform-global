Desc

Nine phyla, each reduced to the one character that identifies it: pores, cnidocytes, no coelom, pseudocoelom, segments, jointed legs, mantle, water vascular system, notochord.

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Nine Phyla Up to Class

    The syllabus requires the diagnostic features and classification up to class of the major phyla: Porifera, Coelenterata (Cnidaria), Platyhelminthes, Aschelminthes, Annelida, Arthropoda, Mollusca, Echinodermata and Chordata. Each phylum is recognised by its body plan (Topic 41) plus a few diagnostic characters.

  • 2. Phylum-by-Phylum Diagnostic Features and Classes

    2.1 Porifera (sponges)

    • Cellular grade; asymmetrical; pore-bearing body with canal system (ascon, sycon, leucon); choanocytes (collar cells) create water currents; skeleton of spicules/spongin; larvae free-swimming.
    • Classes: Calcarea (calcium carbonate spicules — Sycon), Hexactinellida (six-rayed siliceous spicules — glass sponges), Demospongiae (spongin/siliceous spicules — Spongia, bath sponge).

    2.2 Coelenterata / Cnidaria

    • Diploblastic, radially symmetrical; cnidoblasts (stinging cells); tissue grade; gastrovascular cavity; polyp and medusa forms; alternation of generations.
    • Classes: Hydrozoa (both polyp and medusa; Hydra, Obelia), Scyphozoa (medusa dominant — jellyfish, Aurelia), Anthozoa (polyp only; sea anemones, corals).

    2.3 Platyhelminthes (flatworms)

    • Triploblastic acoelomate; bilaterally symmetrical, dorsoventrally flattened; flame cells (protonephridia); most hermaphrodite.
    • Classes: Turbellaria (free-living, Planaria), Trematoda (flukes, Fasciola — liver fluke), Cestoda (tape-worms, Taenia).

    2.4 Aschelminthes / Nematoda (roundworms)

    • Triploblastic pseudocoelomate; cylindrical body with cuticle; complete alimentary canal (mouth + anus); sexes usually separate.
    • Classes (as commonly treated): Nematoda (Ascaris, Wuchereria), Rotifera (wheel-bearer cilia).

    2.5 Annelida (segmented worms)

    • Triploblastic coelomate; true metamerism; nephridia; closed circulatory system; cutaneous respiration.
    • Classes: Polychaeta (marine, parapodia with setae, dioecious — Nereis), Oligochaeta (few setae, hermaphrodite — earthworm), Hirudinea (leeches — suckers, no setae).

    2.6 Arthropoda (NEB 2023 & 2024)

    • Triploblastic coelomate; jointed appendages; chitinous exoskeleton moulted by ecdysis; open circulatory system; segmented body tagmatized; compound eyes.
    • Classes: Crustacea (2 pairs antennae, biramous appendages — prawn, crab), Myriapoda (many legs — centipede, millipede), Insecta (3 pairs legs, 1 pair antennae, wings — butterfly, housefly), Arachnida (4 pairs legs, no antennae — spider, scorpion).

    2.7 Mollusca (NEB 2024)

    • Triploblastic coelomate; unsegmented soft body; muscular foot, visceral hump, mantle (secretes shell), mantle cavity; open circulatory system (mostly); rasping radula.
    • Classes: Gastropoda (one shell or none, torsion — Pila, snail), Bivalvia/Pelecypoda (two shells, filter feeders — Unio, oyster), Cephalopoda (foot into arms/tentacles; jet propulsion — Octopus, Sepia).

    2.8 Echinodermata

    • Marine; secondary radial (pentamerous) adult symmetry; water vascular system with tube feet; endoskeleton of calcareous ossicles; larvae bilateral.
    • Classes: Asteroidea (starfish — arms not distinct from disc), Ophiuroidea (brittle stars — arms distinct), Echinoidea (sea urchins — no arms), Holothuroidea (sea cucumbers — elongated), Crinoidea (feather stars).

    Water vascular system (NEB 2024): a hydraulic system of canals (ring canal, radial canals, tube feet with ampullae) unique to Echinodermata; it drives locomotion, feeding, respiration and attachment — tube feet extend by hydraulic pressure and grip the substrate.

    2.9 Chordata

    • Diagnostic features (at least in some stage): notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits, post-anal tail; ventral heart.
    • Chordates vs non-chordates (NEB 2024): chordates have a notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits and post-anal tail; non-chordates (all other phyla) lack the notochord and dorsal hollow nerve cord (nerve cord, when present, is ventral and solid).
    • Classes (of subphylum Vertebrata): Pisces (fish — gills, fins), Amphibia (frog — aquatic larvae, moist skin), Reptilia (scales, amniote egg), Aves (feathers, flight, four-chambered heart), Mammalia (hair, mammary glands).

    7.2 Model answers

    Q1 (3m/5m, 2023 & 2024) — Diagnostic features of Arthropoda (+ classes). Answer: Arthropods are triploblastic coelomates with (1) a jointed, chitinous exoskeleton moulted by ecdysis; (2) jointed paired appendages modified for walking, feeding or swimming; (3) a segmented body tagmatized into head, thorax and abdomen; (4) an open circulatory system with a dorsal heart and haemocoel; (5) a well-developed nervous system with compound eyes. Classes: Crustacea — two pairs of antennae, biramous appendages (prawn, crab); Myriapoda — many legs, one pair of antennae (centipede, millipede); Insecta — three pairs of legs, one pair of antennae, usually wings (butterfly, housefly); Arachnida — four pairs of legs, no antennae (spider, scorpion).

    Q2 (3m, 2024) — General characters of Mollusca with two examples. Answer: Molluscs are triploblastic coelomates with an unsegmented soft body: (1) a muscular foot for locomotion; (2) a visceral hump containing the organs, covered by a mantle that secretes the shell; (3) a mantle cavity housing gills; (4) a rasping radula for feeding (except bivalves); (5) mostly an open circulatory system. Examples: Pila (apple snail — Gastropoda), Unio (freshwater mussel — Bivalvia), Octopus (Cephalopoda).

    Q3 (3m, 2024) — Chordates vs non-chordates. Answer: Chordates possess, at least in some stage of life: (1) a notochord — a rod-like skeletal support; (2) a dorsal, hollow nerve cord; (3) pharyngeal gill slits; (4) a post-anal tail; the heart is ventral. Non-chordates (Porifera to Echinodermata) lack the notochord and dorsal hollow nerve cord; where a nerve cord exists it is ventral and solid, and there is no post-anal tail.

    Q4 (2m, 2024) — General characters of Annelida with one example. Answer: Annelids are triploblastic coelomates showing (1) true metamerism — the body divided into similar segments; (2) a closed circulatory system; (3) nephridia for excretion; (4) cutaneous respiration; setae or parapodia for locomotion. Example: Pheretima (earthworm) — Oligochaeta.

    Q5 (3m, 2024) — Water vascular system: phylum and functions. Answer: The water vascular system is a hydraulic canal system — madreporite, stone canal, ring canal, radial canals and tube feet with ampullae — found uniquely in the phylum Echinodermata. Water pressure extends the tube feet; it functions in locomotion, feeding (gripping and handling prey), respiration (gas exchange across tube feet) and attachment to the substrate.

    7.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Giving Arthropoda a closed circulatory system — it is open.
    2. Assigning two pairs of antennae to insects — Insecta has one pair; Crustacea has two.
    3. Calling Mollusca segmented — the body is unsegmented (unlike Annelida/Arthropoda).
    4. Placing the water vascular system in Echinodermata but describing chordate features.
    5. Writing that all chordates have backbones — the notochord is the defining feature; vertebrates replace it with a vertebral column.

Confusion

  • Spongilla is a sponge that lives in fresh water. Marine life is not the diagnostic character of Porifera. Pores and choanocytes are.
  • Hydra is a cnidarian even though it lives in fresh water and has no medusa. Anthozoa also have no medusa. The class still stands.
  • A leech is an annelid without setae. Losing setae does not move it into the flatworms. It still has segments, a coelom and suckers.
  • Octopus is a mollusc. The shell is reduced. The mantle, the arms made from the foot, and the radula keep it in Cephalopoda.

Practice

  • Why do arthropods need to moult while annelids do not?

    Answer. The arthropod's chitinous exoskeleton is a rigid armour — excellent for protection and muscle leverage, but it cannot grow. Once filled, the animal must escape it: ecdysis dissolves the inner layer, the body swells and a new, larger cuticle hardens. The annelid's body wall is a flexible cuticle over muscle, which simply stretches as the worm grows — no size ceiling, no moult. The price arthropods pay for jointed-armour locomotion is a lifetime of dangerous, soft-bodied moults.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): What is a connecting link?

    Answer. An organism that shows characters of two taxonomic groups, marking an evolutionary transition between them — e.g. Peripatus (annelid–arthropod: worm-like body with unjointed legs, tracheae and jaws), Archaeopteryx (reptile–bird: feathers plus teeth and a long tail), Euglena (plant–animal: chloroplasts but no cell wall, ingests food).

  • Asterias is Echinodermata, class Asteroidea. Rana is Chordata, subphylum Vertebrata, class Amphibia.
Answer

Calcarea, Hydrozoa, Cestoda, Nematoda, Oligochaeta, Insecta, Bivalvia, Asteroidea, Amphibia, in that organism order.

Summary

Nine phyla, nine body plans. Sponges filter water through choanocyte-lined canals at the cellular grade; cnidarians sting with cnidoblasts as diploblastic radials; flatworms flatten as acoelomates; roundworms round out as pseudocoelomates with a complete gut; annelids segment with true metamerism and nephridia; arthropods joint their chitinous armour and tagmatize into Crustacea, Myriapoda, Insecta and Arachnida; molluscs wrap a muscular foot and visceral hump in a mantle, from snail to octopus; echinoderms pump water through tube feet in pentamerous adults; and chordates carry a notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, gill slits and post-anal tail, radiating from fish to mammals. Each phylum's diagnostics — asked repeatedly in 2023 and 2024 — are the filing system of the animal kingdom.

Collar cells create the water currents that feed sponges.

Cnidoblasts (stinging cells) are diagnostic of:

Options
  • Porifera
  • Cnidaria
  • Platyhelminthes
  • Mollusca
Answer

B

Explanation

Cnidoblasts discharge nematocysts — the signature of Cnidaria.

3.

Flame cells are excretory organs of:

Options
  • Annelida
  • Platyhelminthes
  • Arthropoda
  • Echinodermata
Answer

B

Explanation

Flame cells (protonephridia) excrete in flatworms; annelids use nephridia.

4.

The body cavity of Aschelminthes is a:

Options
  • True coelom
  • Pseudocoelom
  • Haemocoel
  • Acoelom
Answer

B

Explanation

Aschelminthes (Nematoda) are pseudocoelomate — the cavity is not fully mesoderm-lined.

5.

Which arthropod class has two pairs of antennae?

Options
  • Insecta
  • Crustacea
  • Arachnida
  • Myriapoda
Answer

B

Explanation

Crustacea has two pairs of antennae and biramous appendages (prawn, crab).

6.

Arachnida differ from Insecta in having:

Options
  • Three pairs of legs
  • Four pairs of legs and no antennae
  • Two pairs of antennae
  • Wings
Answer

B

Explanation

Arachnids have four pairs of legs and no antennae; insects have three pairs of legs and one pair of antennae.

7.

The mantle of a mollusc functions to:

Options
  • Excrete
  • Secrete the shell
  • Digest food
  • Pump water
Answer

B

Explanation

The mantle secretes the shell and lines the mantle cavity with gills.

8.

The water vascular system functions in:

Options
  • Circulation of blood
  • Locomotion and feeding via tube feet
  • Nerve conduction
  • Reproduction
Answer

B

Explanation

Hydraulic pressure extends tube feet for locomotion, feeding, respiration and attachment (Echinodermata).

9.

Which is a defining chordate feature?

Options
  • Ventral solid nerve cord
  • Dorsal hollow nerve cord
  • Open circulation
  • Segmented exoskeleton
Answer

B

Explanation

Chordates have a dorsal hollow nerve cord, notochord, pharyngeal slits and post-anal tail.

10.

Ecdysis (moulting) is necessary in arthropods because:

Options
  • The exoskeleton is chitinous and non-expandable
  • Blood must be renewed
  • Segments must fuse
  • Eyes must be replaced
Answer

A

Explanation

The rigid chitinous exoskeleton cannot grow, so it is periodically moulted.
