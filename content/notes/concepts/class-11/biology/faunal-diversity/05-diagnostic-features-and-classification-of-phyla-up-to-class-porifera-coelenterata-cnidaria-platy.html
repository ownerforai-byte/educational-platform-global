Desc

Nine phyla, each reduced to the one character that identifies it: pores, cnidocytes, no coelom, pseudocoelom, segments, jointed legs, mantle, water vascular system, notochord.

Notes
  • Each phylum below is identified by one diagnostic character, then taken only as far as class, with one example. The body-cavity words from the previous note are the sorting key: no cavity, a false cavity, or a true cavity.
  • Porifera are pore-bearing, cellular, and mostly marine, with a canal system, choanocytes, and a skeleton of spicules or spongin. Water enters by ostia and leaves by the osculum. Classes: Calcarea (Sycon, calcareous spicules), Hexactinellida (Euplectella, six-rayed silica spicules), Demospongiae (Spongilla, Euspongia). Spongilla is the freshwater example, so 'all sponges are marine' is false. Cnidaria have cnidocytes, radial symmetry, a diploblastic body and a gastrovascular cavity, and they alternate polyp and medusa or emphasise one of them. Classes: Hydrozoa (Hydra, Obelia), Scyphozoa (Aurelia), Anthozoa (sea anemone, coral, polyp only).
  • Platyhelminthes are dorsoventrally flat, bilateral, triploblastic and acoelomate, with flame cells and a blind gut, except the tapeworms, which have lost the gut. Classes: Turbellaria (Planaria, free living), Trematoda (Fasciola, the liver fluke), Cestoda (Taenia). Aschelminthes are cylindrical, pseudocoelomate, unsegmented, with a complete gut and separate sexes. In this course the class is Nematoda. Ascaris lives in the intestine. Wuchereria lives in lymph vessels and is carried by mosquitoes.
  • Annelida have metameric segments, a true coelom, a closed blood system, nephridia and setae or parapodia. Classes: Polychaeta (Nereis, many setae on parapodia), Oligochaeta (Pheretima, a few setae per segment in a ring), Hirudinea (Hirudinaria, the leech, no setae, anterior and posterior suckers). Arthropoda have jointed appendages, a chitinous exoskeleton, and an open blood system in a haemocoel. Classes used here: Crustacea (Palaemon, prawn), Insecta (Periplaneta, Apis, three pairs of legs), Arachnida (spider and scorpion, four pairs of legs), Chilopoda (Scolopendra, one pair of legs per segment), Diplopoda (Julus, two pairs per segment).
  • Mollusca have a soft unsegmented body, a mantle that may secrete a shell, a muscular foot, and, except in bivalves, a radula. Classes: Gastropoda (Pila, a coiled shell and a creeping foot), Pelecypoda or Bivalvia (Unio, two valves, no radula), Cephalopoda (Octopus, Sepia, the foot drawn out as arms, a reduced or lost shell). Echinodermata are marine, spiny-skinned, with a water vascular system and tube feet, and a calcite endoskeleton. The adult is radial and the larva is bilateral. Classes: Asteroidea (starfish), Ophiuroidea (brittle star), Echinoidea (sea urchin), Holothuroidea (sea cucumber), Crinoidea (feather star).
  • Chordata, at some stage of life, have a notochord, a dorsal hollow nerve cord, pharyngeal gill slits and a post-anal tail. The subphyla are Urochordata (Herdmania, notochord in the tail of the larva), Cephalochordata (Branchiostoma, notochord to the front of the head) and Vertebrata (notochord replaced by a vertebral column). Vertebrate classes: Cyclostomata (Petromyzon, jawless), Chondrichthyes (Scoliodon, cartilaginous), Osteichthyes (Labeo, bony), Amphibia (Rana), Reptilia (Calotes), Aves (Columba) and Mammalia. Hair and mammary glands mark the last class.

Confusion

  • Spongilla is a sponge that lives in fresh water. Marine life is not the diagnostic character of Porifera. Pores and choanocytes are.
  • Hydra is a cnidarian even though it lives in fresh water and has no medusa. Anthozoa also have no medusa. The class still stands.
  • A leech is an annelid without setae. Losing setae does not move it into the flatworms. It still has segments, a coelom and suckers.
  • Octopus is a mollusc. The shell is reduced. The mantle, the arms made from the foot, and the radula keep it in Cephalopoda.

Key Points

  • Porifera: ostia, osculum, choanocytes. Classes Calcarea, Hexactinellida, Demospongiae.
  • Cnidaria: cnidocytes. Hydrozoa, Scyphozoa, Anthozoa.
  • Flatworms acoelomate with flame cells. Roundworms pseudocoelomate, class Nematoda.
  • Annelida segmented and coelomate. Arthropoda jointed, chitin, open circulation.
  • Mollusca mantle and foot. Echinodermata water vascular system. Chordata notochord.

Important Statements

  • Cnidocytes define Cnidaria. A polyp or a medusa is the body form, not the phylum character.
  • The notochord has to be present at some stage. In an adult frog it has already been replaced by vertebrae.
  • Bivalves have no radula. Every other molluscan class in this note has one.

Examples

  • Euplectella is a glass sponge, Hexactinellida, with six-rayed silica spicules.
  • Fasciola is a trematode. Taenia is a cestode and has no gut.
  • Nereis has parapodia. Pheretima has a clitellum and setae in rings. Hirudinaria has suckers.

Universal Facts

  • No echinoderm lives in fresh water. The phylum is entirely marine.
  • Insects have three pairs of walking legs. Spiders and scorpions have four pairs. That pair-count is the class split inside Arthropoda.

Formulas

  • Pores, stings, flat, round, rings, joints, shell, tube feet, notochord.
  • Annelid classes: many bristles, few bristles, leech.
  • Chordate quartet: notochord, dorsal hollow nerve cord, gill slits, post-anal tail.

Exam Notes

  • Write phylum, one diagnostic line, classes, one example. Do not write a life cycle unless the question asks.
  • For Chordata, give the four characters first, then the three subphyla, then the vertebrate classes if the question says 'up to class'.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Choanocyte means sponge. Cnidocyte means Cnidaria. Flame cell means flatworm.
  • Tube feet mean echinoderm. A notochord means chordate, even if the adult no longer shows it.

Special Notes

  • Aschelminthes is the syllabus name. Nematoda is the class name used for Ascaris and Wuchereria.
  • Pelecypoda and Bivalvia are the same class. Either label is acceptable with Unio as the example.

Important Notes

  • Taenia has no alimentary canal. Do not draw a blind gut for it. Absorption is through the body surface.
  • Branchiostoma keeps a notochord as an adult. Herdmania has it in the larva. A frog replaces it with a vertebral column.

Exercises

1.

Assign Sycon, Hydra, Taenia, Ascaris, Pheretima, Apis, Unio, Asterias and Rana to phylum and class.

Steps
  • Sycon is Porifera, class Calcarea, because the spicules are calcareous.
  • Hydra is Cnidaria, class Hydrozoa. Taenia is Platyhelminthes, class Cestoda.
  • Ascaris is Aschelminthes, class Nematoda. Pheretima is Annelida, class Oligochaeta.
  • Apis is Arthropoda, class Insecta. Unio is Mollusca, class Bivalvia.
  • Asterias is Echinodermata, class Asteroidea. Rana is Chordata, subphylum Vertebrata, class Amphibia.
Answer

Calcarea, Hydrozoa, Cestoda, Nematoda, Oligochaeta, Insecta, Bivalvia, Asteroidea, Amphibia, in that organism order.

Bounds

  • Life cycles of Plasmodium, earthworm and frog are separate notes. This note is the classification grid only.
  • Orders and families are out of scope. Stop at class.

Summary

Nine phyla are recognised by pores, cnidocytes, a flat acoelomate body, a pseudocoelom, metameric segments, jointed chitinous appendages, a mantle, a water vascular system, and a notochord. Each is classified only to class: three sponge classes, three cnidarian classes, three flatworm classes, three annelid classes, five arthropod classes, three molluscan classes, five echinoderm classes, and the vertebrate classes under Chordata. Spongilla, Hydra and the leech are the usual traps, because habitat or a missing seta does not move them out of the phylum.
