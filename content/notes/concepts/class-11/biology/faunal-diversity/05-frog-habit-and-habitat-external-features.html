Enriched Content

Notes
  • Rana tigrina (tiger frog / Indian frog): amphibian; ~5–8 cm long; model organism for NEB.
  • Habit and habitat: lives near water (ponds, streams, wetlands); spends most of the time on land but returns to water for breeding.
  • External features: moist, glandular, scaleless skin (cutaneous respiration); four limbs (forelimbs 4 digits, hindlimbs 5 digits, webbed); tympanum (eardrum); nictitating membrane (third eyelid); lateral line absent in adults.
  • Body plan: distinct head, neck, trunk, and tail (lost in adults); tail present in tadpole (larval) stage.
  • Adaptations: webbed hind feet for swimming; sticky pads on forelimbs for climbing; moist skin for cutaneous respiration; tympanum for hearing.
  • Taxonomy: Animalia > Chordata > Vertebrata > Amphibia > Anura (tailless frogs) > Rana.
  • NEB focus: habit and habitat, external features, and general body plan of Rana tigrina.
Confusion
  • Frogs are amphibians, NOT reptiles; they need moist skin and return to water to breed.
  • Adult frogs have NO tail; tadpoles (larvae) have a tail (metamorphosis).
  • Forelimbs have 4 digits; hindlimbs have 5 digits (webbed) — a key exam point.
  • The tympanum is the external ear (eardrum); the nictitating membrane is a transparent third eyelid.
  • Frogs do NOT have a lateral line in adulthood (it is present in tadpoles).
  • Cutaneous respiration (skin) is major in frogs; lungs are also present but the skin contributes significantly to gas exchange.

Universal Facts
  • Rana tigrina has 4 forelimb digits and 5 webbed hindlimb digits.
  • The frog tadpole has a tail and gills; the adult loses the tail and gains lungs.
  • Frogs can lose up to ~25% of their body weight in water through the skin.
  • Metamorphosis (tadpole → frog) takes 2–3 months in most species.
Examples
  • Webbed hind feet: for swimming (amphibious life).
  • Sticky pads on forelimbs: for climbing surfaces.
  • Moist, glandular skin: for cutaneous respiration and protection.
  • Tympanum (eardrum): for hearing; nictitating membrane: for eye protection underwater.

Formulas
  • External: head (eyes, tympanum, nostrils) + neck + trunk + 4 limbs (forelimb 4 digits, hindlimb 5 digits, webbed)
  • Adaptations: moist skin, webbed feet, sticky pads, tympanum, nictitating membrane
  • Metamorphosis: tadpole (tail + gills) → adult frog (no tail + lungs)
Key Points
  • Rana tigrina: amphibian, ~5–8 cm, lives near water.
  • External: moist glandular skin, 4 forelimb digits, 5 webbed hindlimb digits.
  • Tympanum (eardrum) + nictitating membrane (third eyelid).

  • Adaptations: webbed feet (swimming), sticky pads (climbing), moist skin (respiration).
  • Taxonomy: Amphibia > Anura > Rana.
  • Cutaneous respiration is significant in addition to lungs.
Summary

Rana tigrina is the model amphibian for NEB. It lives near water, spends most of the time on land, and returns to water to breed. External features include moist, glandular, scaleless skin; a tympanum; a nictitating membrane; 4-digit forelimbs; and 5-digit webbed hindlimbs. The tadpole has a tail and gills; the adult loses the tail and gains lungs. Adaptations (webbed feet, sticky pads, moist skin) reflect its amphibious lifestyle. NEB focuses on external anatomy, habitat, and adaptations.

Special Notes

  • The digit count (4 fore, 5 hind) is a very common exam detail.
  • Metamorphosis (tadpole → frog) is a frequent topic.
  • Adaptations to amphibious life (skin, feet, tympanum) are a favourite application question.
