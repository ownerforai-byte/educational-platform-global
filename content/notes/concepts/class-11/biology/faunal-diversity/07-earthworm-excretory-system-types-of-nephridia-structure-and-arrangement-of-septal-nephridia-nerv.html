Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Worm's Working Systems

    Completing the earthworm anatomy: the excretory system of nephridia (with the septal nephridia in detail), the central nervous system of a double ventral nerve cord with ganglia, and the reproductive system of a simultaneous hermaphrodite that still cross-fertilises.

  • 2. Excretory System — Types of Nephridia

    Nephridia are the excretory organs; Pheretima has three types:

    TypeLocationOpeningStructure
    Septal nephridiaAttached to the posterior faces of septa of segments 15 to the lastOpen into the alimentary canal (enteronephric) — via the septal excretory ducts into the intestineNephrostome (ciliated funnel) → neck → body of lobe (short straight lobe + long twisted lobe with 4 loops) → duct
    Integumentary nephridiaAttached to the body wall, in all segments except the first twoOpen to the exterior at nephridiopores (exonephric)Small, "v" shaped, without nephrostome
    Pharyngeal nephridiaThree paired tufts in segments 4, 5 and 6Ducts open into the buccal cavity/pharynx (enteronephric)Tufted, without nephrostome

    2.1 Structure and arrangement of septal nephridia (in detail)

    Each septal nephridium hangs from the posterior face of an intersegmental septum (from segment 15 onward), 40–50 per segment, opening into the alimentary canal:

    1. Nephrostome: ciliated funnel opening into the coelom, collects coelomic fluid.
    2. Neck: short tube leading from the nephrostome.
    3. Body of the nephridium: a short straight lobe and a long twisted lobe whose internal loop is coiled four times, with a ciliated inner canal.
    4. Septal excretory duct: receives the nephridium's efferent duct and opens into the intestine.

    Function: collect coelomic fluid and waste from the blood, convert to hypotonic urine (conserving salts), excrete urea and ammonia; enteronephric types conserve water by excreting into the gut.

  • 3. Nervous System

    • Central nervous system: a ventral nerve cord (double) running from segment 4 to the last, with a ganglion in each segment; anteriorly it divides around the pharynx into a circumpharyngeal ring with a dorsal "brain" (supra-pharyngeal ganglia) in segment 3 and sub-pharyngeal ganglia in segment 4.
    • Peripheral nervous system: nerves from the ganglia to body wall, gut, and organs.
    • Sympathetic nervous system: plexus in the gut wall.
    • Sense organs: photoreceptors (dorsal epidermis), tactile and chemoreceptor cells — no eyes.

    Reflex action is mediated segmentally; the ventral cord permits coordinated peristaltic locomotion.

  • 4. Reproductive System

    Pheretima is a simultaneous hermaphrodite but practises cross-fertilisation by copulation.

    Male organs:

    • Two testes in segments 10 and 11, with testis sacs.
    • Two seminal vesicles in segments 9 and 12 — store and mature sperm.
    • Two vasa deferentia run to segment 18, opening at the male genital pores.
    • Prostate glands (one pair, large) with accessory gland masses also open at segment 18.

    Female organs:

    • One pair of ovaries in segment 13 (attached to the septum 12/13).
    • Oviducal funnels with ciliated oviducts opening at the female pore on segment 14.
    • Four pairs of spermathecae in segments 6–9 — receive and store the partner's sperm during copulation.

    Copulation and cocoon formation:

    1. Two worms exchange sperm through the spermathecal pores during copulation (ventral surfaces apposed, heads at opposite ends).
    2. The clitellum secretes a mucus band that hardens into a cocoon; as it slides forward it receives ova from the female pore (segment 14) and stored sperm from the spermathecae.
    3. Fertilisation occurs in the cocoon; each cocoon yields 1–20 young (often 2); development is direct — no larva.

    6.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Describe the types of nephridia in Pheretima. Answer: (1) Septal nephridia — attached to the posterior faces of septa from segment 15 onward, 40–50 per segment; open into the alimentary canal (enteronephric); each has a ciliated nephrostome, neck, a body of short straight and long twisted lobes, and a septal excretory duct into the intestine. (2) Integumentary nephridia — small, on the body wall of all segments except the first two; open to the exterior (exonephric). (3) Pharyngeal nephridia — three paired tufts in segments 4, 5 and 6; ducts open into the pharynx region (enteronephric).

    Q2 (3m) — Describe the arrangement of septal nephridia. Answer: Each septal nephridium hangs from the posterior face of an intersegmental septum from segment 15 onward, 40–50 per segment. It consists of a ciliated nephrostome opening into the coelom, a neck, and a body composed of a short straight lobe and a long twisted lobe whose internal loop is coiled four times; its efferent duct joins the septal excretory duct, which opens into the intestine. They excrete into the gut (enteronephric), conserving water.

    Q3 (3m) — Describe the reproductive system of Pheretima and how the cocoon is formed. Answer: Pheretima is a simultaneous hermaphrodite. Male: two testes in testis sacs (segments 10, 11), two seminal vesicles (9, 12), two vasa deferentia and prostate glands opening at the male pores (segment 18). Female: one pair of ovaries (segment 13), oviducts opening at segment 14, and four pairs of spermathecae (segments 6–9) storing the partner's sperm. Cocoon: after cross-copulation, the clitellum secretes a mucus band that hardens into a cocoon; as it slides forward it collects ova (segment 14) and stored sperm; fertilisation occurs in the cocoon, which yields 1–20 young by direct development.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying septal nephridia open to the exterior — they open into the alimentary canal (enteronephric).
    2. Placing septal nephridia from segment 5 — they begin at segment 15.
    3. Saying Pheretima self-fertilises — it is hermaphrodite but cross-fertilises by copulation.
    4. Giving two pairs of spermathecae — there are four pairs (segments 6–9).
    5. Placing the ventral nerve cord dorsally — the "brain" is dorsal, the cord is ventral.

Confusion

  • Enteronephric ducts open into the gut. Exonephric ducts open on the skin. Septal nephridia are enteronephric. Integumentary nephridia are exonephric.
  • Integumentary and pharyngeal nephridia have no nephrostome. Only septal nephridia collect coelomic fluid through a ciliated funnel.
  • The earthworm's nerve cord is ventral and solid. The frog's is dorsal and hollow. 'Nerve cord' alone is not a shared structure.
  • The brain is the supra-pharyngeal ganglia in segment 3, not a swelling of the ventral cord in the clitellum.

Practice

  • Why does a hermaphrodite animal still cross-fertilise?

    Answer. Because self-fertilisation is genetic suicide in the long run: mating with another individual recombines genes, restores heterozygosity, masks deleterious recessives and keeps the population adaptable. In Pheretima the anatomy even enforces outcrossing — the male pores (segment 18) and the spermathecal pores (segments 6–9) sit so far apart that a worm's own sperm cannot reach its own spermathecae during copulation; each worm receives the partner's sperm and uses it later. Both animals leave the encounter fertilised — the hermaphrodite's advantage — but with fresh genes.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): How many spermathecae does Pheretima have and where?

    Answer. Four pairs, located in segments 6–9 (i.e. in the intersegmental grooves of 5/6, 6/7, 7/8, 8/9). Each spermatheca stores the partner's sperm received during copulation, releasing it later to fertilise the ova as the cocoon is formed.

Exercises

1.

Sort these into septal, integumentary or pharyngeal: a ciliated funnel on the septum of segment 20; a duct opening on the skin of segment 30; a tuft in segment 5 opening into the pharynx.

Steps
  • A funnel on a septum behind segment 15 is a septal nephridium. Its duct system ends in the intestine, so it is enteronephric.
  • A duct that opens on the skin, with no funnel, is an integumentary nephridium. It is exonephric.
  • A tuft in segment 5 that opens into the pharynx is a pharyngeal nephridium. Segments 4, 5 and 6 are its only range.
  • Only the first of the three has a nephrostome.
Answer

Septal, integumentary, pharyngeal, in that order. Only the septal nephridium has a nephrostome.

Summary

The earthworm's working systems complete its anatomy. Excretion runs on three types of nephridia — septal nephridia hanging from septa from segment 15, 40–50 per segment, each with a ciliated nephrostome, neck, straight and four-looped twisted lobes, discharging into the intestine; integumentary nephridia on the body wall opening to the exterior; and pharyngeal tufts in segments 4–6 — together excreting hypotonic urine that conserves salts. The nervous system is a double ventral nerve cord with segmental ganglia, a dorsal brain and sub-pharyngeal ganglia, plus photoreceptors without eyes. The hermaphrodite reproductive system — testes in 10 and 11, seminal vesicles, prostates at 18, ovaries in 13, four pairs of spermathecae — still cross-fertilises: clitellar mucus forms a cocoon in which fertilisation and direct development occur.
