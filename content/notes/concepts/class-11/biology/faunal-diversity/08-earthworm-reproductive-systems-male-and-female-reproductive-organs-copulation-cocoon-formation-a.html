Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Male and Female Systems, Copulation and the Cocoon

    This topic expands the earthworm's reproductive biology: the male and female organs in detail, the mechanism of copulation, and the formation of the cocoon with its direct development. Pheretima is a simultaneous hermaphrodite — each worm has complete male and female systems — yet cross-fertilisation is obligatory.

  • 4. Copulation, Cocoon Formation and Development

    1. Copulation: two worms lie with ventral surfaces apposed, heads pointing in opposite directions; each worm's male pores (18) align with the other's spermathecal pores (5/6–8/9); sperm are exchanged and stored in the spermathecae; accessory glands provide the sticky secretion.
    2. Cocoon formation: the clitellum (14–16) secretes a mucus band and albumen; the worm backs out of the hardened band, which becomes a cocoon; as it slides forward over segments 14 and 9–6 it receives ova from the female pore and sperm from the spermathecae.
    3. Fertilisation: occurs inside the cocoon — stored sperm fertilise the ova.
    4. Development: direct — no larval stage; each cocoon yields 1–20 young worms (commonly 2); young worms resemble adults.

    6.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Describe the male reproductive system of Pheretima. Answer: Two testes lie in testis sacs in segments 10 and 11; sperm pass into the two seminal vesicles in segments 9 and 12, where they mature and are stored; two vasa deferentia conduct mature sperm to the male genital pores on segment 18, where they open alongside the ducts of the large prostate glands (segments 17–19) whose secretion forms the seminal fluid; accessory glands at segment 18 secrete the sticky fluid used in copulation.

    Q2 (3m) — Describe copulation and cocoon formation in Pheretima. Answer: During copulation two worms align with ventral surfaces apposed and heads in opposite directions; each worm's male pores (segment 18) lie against the other's spermathecal pores (grooves 5/6–8/9), and sperm are exchanged into the four pairs of spermathecae. In cocoon formation the clitellum (14–16) secretes a mucus band that hardens; as the worm backs out, the band slides forward, receiving ova at the female pore (14) and stored sperm from the spermathecae; fertilisation occurs in the cocoon, which yields 1–20 young by direct development.

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying self-fertilisation occurs — Pheretima is hermaphrodite but obligately cross-fertilises.
    2. Giving two pairs of spermathecae — four pairs in segments 6–9.
    3. Placing fertilisation in the spermatheca during copulation — it occurs in the cocoon.
    4. Saying development includes a larva — development is direct.

Confusion

  • Hermaphrodite does not mean self-fertilising. Pheretima exchanges sperm with another worm.
  • Fertilisation is not in the oviduct. It is in the cocoon, after both eggs and stored sperm have been shed into it.
  • Spermathecae store the partner's sperm. They are not the ovaries, and they are not the seminal vesicles.
  • The clitellum secretes the cocoon. It is not itself the cocoon, and it is not shed as a permanent organ.

Practice

  • Why is cross-fertilisation preferable to self-fertilisation even for a hermaphrodite?

    Answer. Self-fertilisation is inbreeding at its extreme: recessive deleterious alleles meet themselves, heterozygosity collapses and the line loses the variation it needs to track disease and climate. Cross-fertilisation recombines two genomes, masks harmful recessives, produces new gene combinations and keeps evolutionary options open. That is why Pheretima — despite carrying both sexes — goes to the trouble of mutual copulation, and even places its pores so that selfing is mechanically impossible. Hermaphroditism buys the certainty of mating; crossing buys the quality of the offspring.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): Where does fertilisation occur in Pheretima and how many young hatch per cocoon?

    Answer. Inside the clitellar cocoon, not in the body: as the clitellum's mucus band slides forward it collects ova from the female pore (14) and sperm from the spermathecae (6–9), and fertilisation happens within the secreted cocoon. Each cocoon yields 1–20 young, commonly 2, by direct development — no larva.

Exercises

1.

Put these events in order and say where each happens: fertilisation, storage of received sperm, secretion of the cocoon, copulation, hatching.

Steps
  • Copulation comes first. Two worms exchange sperm on the ventral surface. Sperm enters the spermathecae of segments 6 to 9.
  • The received sperm is stored in the spermathecae. Eggs are still in segment 13.
  • The clitellum, segments 14 to 16, secretes the cocoon girdle with albumen.
  • As the worm slips backward, eggs enter from segment 14 and sperm enters from the spermathecae. The cocoon seals.
  • Fertilisation occurs in the cocoon, in the soil. Hatching releases a small worm. There is no larva.
Answer

Copulation, storage in spermathecae, cocoon secretion, fertilisation in the cocoon, direct hatching.

Summary

Pheretima's reproductive system is complete in both sexes in one body. The male side: testes in testis sacs at segments 10–11, sperm maturing in seminal vesicles at 9 and 12, conducted by vasa deferentia with prostatic secretions to the male pores at segment 18. The female side: ovaries at segment 13, oviducts to the female pore at 14, and four pairs of spermathecae at 6–9 to store the partner's sperm. During copulation, worms appose ventral surfaces with heads opposed and exchange sperm; the clitellar band hardens into a cocoon that receives ova and stored sperm, and fertilisation inside it yields 1–20 young by direct development — hermaphroditism with obligatory cross-fertilisation.
