Desc

Clitellum on segments 14 to 16, the gland that secretes the cocoon. Male pores lie just behind it, on segment 18.

Notes
  • Pheretima is hermaphrodite. Each worm has a full male system and a full female system. Self-fertilisation is not the normal event. Two worms copulate, exchange sperm, and separate. Fertilisation happens later, outside the body, inside a cocoon. That sequence is the whole reproductive story.
  • The male organs are crowded into a few front segments. Two pairs of testes lie in segments 10 and 11, inside testis sacs. Sperm mature in two pairs of seminal vesicles in segments 11 and 12. Ciliated spermiducal funnels, also in segments 10 and 11, collect the sperm into vasa deferentia. Each side's duct runs back to segment 18, where it joins a prostate gland. The prostate secretion is the fluid in which the sperm leave. The male genital pores open on segment 18. Accessory glands on segments 17 and 19 serve the genital papillae that help the worms hold together.
  • The female organs are smaller. One pair of ovaries lies in segment 13. An ovarian funnel under each ovary leads into an oviduct, and the two oviducts meet to open at the single female genital pore on segment 14. Eggs are not stored for a long time. They are shed when a cocoon passes that segment. Four pairs of spermathecae, in segments 6, 7, 8 and 9, receive the partner's sperm during copulation. Each spermatheca has a larger ampulla and a narrow diverticulum, and the received sperm stays there until the cocoon is formed.
  • During copulation the two worms lie head to tail, ventral surface against ventral surface, so that the male pores of each are opposite the spermathecal pores of the other. Mucus and the setae hold them. Sperm and prostatic fluid pass into the spermathecae. No eggs move across. The worms then separate. A worm that has just copulated is carrying another worm's sperm, not embryos.
  • The cocoon is a product of the clitellum. Glands in segments 14 to 16 secrete a girdle of membrane and albumen. The worm wriggles backward and slips out of the girdle. As the girdle passes segment 14, eggs enter it. As it passes the spermathecal openings, the stored sperm enters. The ends of the girdle close, and the sealed cocoon is left in damp soil. Fertilisation, the actual fusion, takes place in the albumen inside that cocoon. Development is direct. A small worm emerges. There is no trochophore and no other larva.
  • The same animal that reproduces this way is useful in soil. Burrows let air and water deeper into the ground. Castings return mineral nutrients to the surface in a form roots can use, and they improve the crumb of the soil. Earthworms are the workforce of vermicompost. They are also bait and food for birds. They are not a crop pest in the sense of an insect that eats leaves. Their economic importance is the soil they leave behind them.

Confusion

  • Hermaphrodite does not mean self-fertilising. Pheretima exchanges sperm with another worm.
  • Fertilisation is not in the oviduct. It is in the cocoon, after both eggs and stored sperm have been shed into it.
  • Spermathecae store the partner's sperm. They are not the ovaries, and they are not the seminal vesicles.
  • The clitellum secretes the cocoon. It is not itself the cocoon, and it is not shed as a permanent organ.

Key Points

  • Hermaphrodite, with cross-fertilisation in a cocoon.
  • Testes in segments 10 and 11. Seminal vesicles in 11 and 12. Male pores on 18.
  • Ovaries in segment 13. Female pore on 14.
  • Four pairs of spermathecae in segments 6 to 9, each with ampulla and diverticulum.
  • Direct development. Economic value is soil structure, castings and vermicompost.

Important Statements

  • Copulation exchanges sperm. It does not fertilise the eggs.
  • The cocoon receives eggs at segment 14 and sperm at the spermathecal pores.
  • There is no larval stage. The hatchling is a small worm.

Examples

  • After copulation, sperm from the partner sits in the spermathecae of segments 6 to 9 until a cocoon is secreted.
  • A cocoon in damp soil yields a juvenile worm, not a larva that must metamorphose.
  • A vermicompost bed uses the same casting habit that fertilises a field.

Universal Facts

  • The prostate gland opens at the male pore and adds fluid to the sperm. It is not a sperm store. The seminal vesicles are the store.
  • Accessory glands of segments 17 and 19 are not prostates. The prostate is the larger gland opening on segment 18.

Formulas

  • Testes 10 and 11. Vesicles 11 and 12. Ovaries 13. Female pore 14. Male pores 18.
  • Spermathecae in 6, 7, 8 and 9.
  • Copulation, then cocoon, then fertilisation, then direct development.

Special Notes

  • Do not invent a larval stage. Direct development is a standard point against the marine annelid larva.
  • Prostate, accessory gland and spermatheca are three different structures. A labelled diagram that swaps them loses the segment numbers.

Important Notes

  • Seminal vesicles store the worm's own sperm. Spermathecae store the partner's sperm. The names are not interchangeable.
  • Economic importance in this course is soil fertility. Do not claim they fix nitrogen the way Rhizobium does.

Exercises

1.

Put these events in order and say where each happens: fertilisation, storage of received sperm, secretion of the cocoon, copulation, hatching.

Steps
  • Copulation comes first. Two worms exchange sperm on the ventral surface. Sperm enters the spermathecae of segments 6 to 9.
  • The received sperm is stored in the spermathecae. Eggs are still in segment 13.
  • The clitellum, segments 14 to 16, secretes the cocoon girdle with albumen.
  • As the worm slips backward, eggs enter from segment 14 and sperm enters from the spermathecae. The cocoon seals.
  • Fertilisation occurs in the cocoon, in the soil. Hatching releases a small worm. There is no larva.
Answer

Copulation, storage in spermathecae, cocoon secretion, fertilisation in the cocoon, direct hatching.

Bounds

  • Nephridia and the nerve cord are the previous note. Digestion is the one before that.
  • Numbers are for Pheretima posthuma, not for every oligochaete.

Summary

Each Pheretima has testes in segments 10 and 11, seminal vesicles in 11 and 12, male pores on 18, ovaries in 13, a female pore on 14, and four pairs of spermathecae in segments 6 to 9. Copulation exchanges sperm into those spermathecae. The clitellum then secretes a cocoon that picks up eggs and the stored sperm as the worm wriggles out of it. Fertilisation is in the cocoon, and development is direct. Burrows and castings are the economic return: air, water and mineral crumbs in the soil.
