Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Ecosystem as a Working Machine

    An ecosystem is the functional unit of nature: a community of organisms interacting with each other and with their physical environment, with energy flowing one way and matter cycling. The syllabus demands the structure of an ecosystem with a diagram (5 marks in both 2023 and 2024) using the pond and forest as standard examples.

  • 2. Definition and Structure of an Ecosystem

    Definition: an ecosystem is a self-sustaining unit of nature comprising all the organisms (biotic community) of an area interacting with their physical environment (abiotic components) through energy flow and nutrient cycling (Tansley, 1935).

    2.1 Structural components (the answer skeleton for the 5m question)

    A. Abiotic components

    1. Inorganic substances — C, N, P, water, CO₂, O₂ (matter pool for cycles)
    2. Organic substances — humus, proteins, carbohydrates (dead-matter pool)
    3. Climate — light, temperature, rainfall, humidity (energy regime)

    B. Biotic components

    1. Producers (autotrophs) — green plants/algae fix solar energy into chemical energy
    2. Consumers (macro/micro) — herbivores (T2), carnivores (T3, T4), omnivores
    3. Decomposers (saprotrophs) — bacteria and fungi breaking dead matter into inorganic nutrients, closing the cycle

    C. Functional components

    1. Energy flow — unidirectional: sun → producers → consumers → heat loss
    2. Nutrient cycling — biogeochemical cycles return matter to producers
    3. Homeostasis — self-regulation balancing populations (predation, competition)

    2.2 The pond ecosystem (standard diagram example)

    ZoneDepthInhabitants
    LittoralShallow edgesRooted plants, snails, insect larvae, frogs
    LimneticOpen water to light depthPhytoplankton (algae), zooplankton, fish
    Profundal/benthicBelow light penetrationDecomposers, bottom dwellers, anaerobic bacteria

    Pond flow: phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → large fish (grazing chain) plus a detritus chain at the bottom. Sunlight drives photosynthesis; nutrients recycled from the benthic zone.

    2.3 The forest ecosystem

    LayerInhabitants
    Canopy (upper)Birds, monkeys, epiphytes, lianas
    UnderstoreyShade-tolerant shrubs, small mammals, insects
    Forest floorLeaf litter, earthworms, fungi, bacteria — the detritus engine

    Forest flow: trees → herbivores (deer, insects) → carnivores (leopard, birds of prey), with the detritus food chain dominating (most forest production ends as litter). Forests store huge biomass (high NPP) and regulate water and climate.

    2.4 Types of ecosystems

    • Natural: terrestrial (forest, grassland, desert, tundra) and aquatic (freshwater — pond, lake, river; marine — ocean, estuary).
    • Artificial (man-made): crop fields, aquariums, dams — require human energy input.

  • 3. Functional Aspects — How the Machine Runs

    1. Energy flow: solar radiation → ~1% fixed by producers → NPP = GPP − R feeds consumers → heat lost at every transfer (unidirectional, no recycling of energy).
    2. Nutrient cycling: decomposers return C, N, P to the abiotic pool; producers reabsorb — matter is recycled indefinitely.
    3. Food relations: grazing and detritus chains interweave into webs.
    4. Succession: ecosystems change directionally over time toward a stable climax.
    5. Homeostasis: feedback loops (predator-prey, resource-consumer) keep populations within limits.

  • Deep dive — why energy flow is one-way but nutrients cycle. Every transfer loses about 90% of the energy as heat (the second law of thermodynamics); heat cannot be recycled by plants, so energy must be resupplied by the sun forever. Matter, however, is atoms — carbon, nitrogen, phosphorus — and atoms are neither created nor destroyed, so decomposers return them to the producers indefinitely. One arrow in, one arrow out; one loop closed. Keep those two shapes separate and every ecology answer becomes clearer.

Practice

  • Why does an aquarium need frequent cleaning while a pond does not?

    Answer. A pond is a complete, self-sustaining ecosystem: enough producers, consumers and — crucially — decomposers to cycle nutrients and keep oxygen balanced (ecosystem homeostasis). An aquarium is an incomplete artificial ecosystem: its producer and decomposer populations are too small for the fish biomass, so waste accumulates, ammonia rises and oxygen falls. The owner must repeatedly act as the missing decomposer service, removing waste and renewing water.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): Name the zones of a pond ecosystem.

    Answer. Littoral — shallow edges, rooted plants, snails, insect larvae, frogs. Limnetic — open water down to light depth, phytoplankton, zooplankton, fish. Profundal/benthic — deep, dark bottom, decomposers and anaerobic bacteria.

1.

Producers in a forest capture 10,000 kJ. Using the ten percent law, how much energy reaches the tertiary consumers?

Steps
  • Primary consumers receive about 0.1 × 10,000 = 1,000 kJ.
  • Secondary consumers receive about 0.1 × 1,000 = 100 kJ.
  • Tertiary consumers receive about 0.1 × 100 = 10 kJ.
Answer

About 10 kJ.

Summary

An ecosystem (Tansley, 1935) is nature's self-sustaining unit: a biotic community running on its abiotic environment through one-way energy flow and cyclic nutrients. Its structure has three layers — abiotic components (inorganic nutrients, organic detritus, climate), biotic components (producers, consumers, decomposers) and functional links (energy flow, nutrient cycling, homeostasis). The pond shows it in cross-section: littoral rooted plants, limnetic plankton and fish, benthic decomposers feeding a grazing chain back with nutrients. The forest shows it in layers: canopy, understorey and a litter floor where the detritus chain dominates. Asked in both 2023 and 2024 for 5 marks, this structure-with-diagram answer is the single most valuable paragraph in ecology.
