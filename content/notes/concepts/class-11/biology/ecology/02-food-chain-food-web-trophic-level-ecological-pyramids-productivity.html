Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — How Energy Moves Through Life

    Every ecosystem runs on one currency: energy, entering as sunlight and leaving as heat, passing from plants to herbivores to carnivores. Food chains and webs trace that flow; pyramids depict it; productivity measures it. This topic carries more NEB questions than any other in ecology — including a numerical.

  • 2. Food Chain, Food Web and Trophic Levels

    2.1 Food chain — the linear energy path

    Grazing food chain (starts with living plants): Grass → Grasshopper → Frog → Snake → Hawk (Producer → Primary consumer → Secondary → Tertiary → Top carnivore)

    Detritus food chain (starts with dead matter): Dead leaves → Detritivores (earthworm, woodlice) → Decomposer bacteria/fungi → Small carnivores → Top carnivore

    TermMeaning
    Trophic levelPosition in the chain (T1 producers, T2 herbivores, T3 carnivores…)
    Standing cropTotal biomass at a trophic level at a time
    10% law (Lindeman, 1942)Only ~10% of energy transfers to the next level; the rest is lost as heat, respiration, excretion

    Why chains are short: with only ~10% passing on, after 4–5 transfers too little energy remains to support another level — hence top carnivores are rare.

    2.2 Food web vs food chain (NEB 2024)

    BasisFood chainFood web
    DefinitionSingle linear sequence of who-eats-whomInterconnected network of many food chains
    StructureOne pathway of energyMany interlocking pathways
    StabilityFragile — remove one link and the chain breaksStable — alternative pathways buffer loss
    RealismTheoretical simplificationWhat nature actually contains
    ExampleGrass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawkGrassland web: grass eaten by insects, rabbits, cattle; each preyed on by several predators

    2.3 The ten percent law applied

    If producers fix 10,000 kJ: herbivores get ~1,000 kJ, small carnivores ~100 kJ, top carnivores ~10 kJ. This explains (1) short chains, (2) small top-predator populations, (3) large human grain consumption being more efficient than meat-heavy diets.

  • 3. Ecological Pyramids (NEB 2024 + 2023)

    Definition: graphical representation of a trophic parameter (number, biomass, energy) with producers at the base and top carnivores at the apex.

    PyramidBase → apexUsually upright?Can invert?
    Pyramid of numbersCount of individualsUsuallyYes — one big tree supports thousands of insects (parasitic food chain); a banyan → herbivores → parasites inverts it
    Pyramid of biomassDry weight (g/m²)Usually in terrestrial systemsYes in aquatic systems — a small standing crop of phytoplankton supports a larger zooplankton mass at a moment in time (fast turnover)
    Pyramid of energyEnergy (kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹)Always upright — never invertedNever

    Why the energy pyramid is always upright (NEB 2024): energy flows one way — at each transfer ~90% is lost as heat (respiration, excretion, egestion), and heat cannot be recycled back. So each successive level must hold less energy than the one below; a higher level can never contain more energy than its food source. Numbers and biomass are snapshots (invertible); energy is a rate measured over time (never invertible).

  • 4. Productivity

    TermDefinitionUnit
    GPP (Gross Primary Productivity)Total solar energy fixed by producerskcal m⁻² yr⁻¹
    NPP (Net Primary Productivity)GPP − plant respiration (R); the energy actually stored, available to consumerskcal m⁻² yr⁻¹
    Secondary productivityEnergy stored by consumerskcal m⁻² yr⁻¹

    NPP = GPP − R — the most-tested formula in ecology.

    4.1 Worked example (NEB 2022)

    Grassland: GPP = 4800 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹; plant respiration = 1200 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹. NPP = 4800 − 1200 = 3600 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹. If herbivores consume 20% of NPP: energy reaching herbivores = 0.20 × 3600 = 720 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹.

    Open oceanLow per m², but huge total areaNutrient-limited
    DesertsVery lowWater-limited

    6.2 Model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Food chain vs food web + ten percent law. Answer: A food chain is a single linear sequence of energy transfer (grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk), while a food web is the interconnected network of many food chains in an ecosystem, giving alternative pathways and stability. The ten percent law (Lindeman) states that only about 10% of the energy at one trophic level is transferred to the next; ~90% is lost as heat through respiration, excretion and egestion — which is why chains rarely exceed 4–5 levels.

    Q2 (3m) — Ecological pyramids; why energy pyramid is always upright. Answer: Ecological pyramids graph trophic parameters — numbers, biomass or energy — with producers at the base. The pyramid of energy is always upright because energy flows unidirectionally: at each transfer about 90% is dissipated as heat, and heat cannot be recycled, so each higher level necessarily contains less energy than the one below it. Numbers and biomass are momentary snapshots and can invert (one tree → many insects; phytoplankton → zooplankton), but energy is a rate over time and can never increase upward.

    Q3 (2m) — Pyramid of numbers vs biomass + inversion cases. Answer: The pyramid of numbers counts individuals per level (usually upright; inverted when one large tree supports many herbivorous insects and their parasites). The pyramid of biomass uses dry weight per level (usually upright on land; inverted in open ocean where a small standing crop of phytoplankton supports a larger zooplankton biomass because phytoplankton reproduce rapidly).

    Q4 (3m) — NPP numerical. Answer: NPP = GPP − R = 4800 − 1200 = 3600 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹. (If asked: energy to herbivores at 20% consumption = 720 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹; at 10% transfer, secondary consumers receive ~72 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹.)

    6.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Inverting the energy pyramid "like biomass" — never; give the one-way heat-loss reason.
    2. Writing NPP = GPP + R — it is minus.
    3. Confusing trophic level numbering (producers are T1, not T0).
    4. Forgetting units (kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹) in numericals — units carry marks.
    5. Describing the food web as "a long food chain" — it is a network, not a chain.

Confusion

  • GPP vs NPP: GPP = total fixed; NPP = GPP − respiration = what's actually stored and available.
  • Standing crop vs productivity: standing crop = biomass at an instant (snapshot); productivity = rate of production over time (movie).
  • Grazing vs detritus chain: grazing starts with living plants; detritus starts with dead organic matter — in forests the detritus chain dominates.

Practice

  • Why is a meat-heavy human diet ecologically expensive?

    Answer. By the 10% law, only about one-tenth of the energy at each trophic level reaches the next. Eating grain directly puts a person at T2 and harvests ~10× more energy per hectare than eating grain-fed animals, which puts the person at T3. A meat-heavy diet therefore demands far more land, water and primary production to deliver the same calories — it supports fewer people per hectare and leaves a larger ecological footprint.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (2m): Define trophic level.

    Answer. The position an organism occupies in a food chain, decided by its source of energy: T1 producers, T2 primary consumers (herbivores), T3 secondary consumers (carnivores), T4 tertiary consumers/top carnivores. Because only ~10% of energy passes upward (Lindeman's 10% law), chains rarely exceed four or five levels.

Formulas

  • Food chain: Producer (TL1) → Primary consumer (TL2) → Secondary consumer (TL3) → Tertiary consumer (TL4)
  • 10% rule: only ~10% of energy transfers between trophic levels
  • GPP = total photosynthesis; NPP = GPP − plant respiration (R)
  • Pyramid of energy: always upright; each level < the one below

Key Points

  • Food chain: linear energy path; grazing vs detritus; trophic levels T1–T5; 10% law (Lindeman) → chains short, top predators rare.
  • Food web: interlocking chains — stable, realistic.
  • Pyramids: numbers (invert: tree-insects), biomass (invert: ocean phytoplankton), energy (always upright — heat lost one-way).
  • NPP = GPP − R; units kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹. 2022 numerical: 4800 − 1200 = 3600.

Summary

Energy moves through ecosystems in food chains — grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk — or, more realistically, in food webs of interlocking chains that buffer species loss. Lindeman's ten percent law governs every step: ~90% of energy is lost as heat, so chains stay short and top predators rare. Ecological pyramids depict this: numbers can invert (one banyan, countless insects), biomass can invert (ocean plankton), but the pyramid of energy is always upright because heat cannot flow back. Productivity quantifies the flow — GPP minus plant respiration gives NPP (4800 − 1200 = 3600 kcal m⁻² yr⁻¹ in the 2022 numerical) — the energy budget on which every consumer, including us, draws.

Original Content

Notes
  • Food chain: a linear sequence of organisms where each is eaten by the next; transfers energy and matter (sun → producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer).
  • Food web: a network of interconnected food chains; more realistic than a single chain; shows multiple feeding relationships.
  • Trophic levels: TL1 producers (autotrophs), TL2 primary consumers (herbivores), TL3 secondary consumers (carnivores), TL4+ tertiary consumers.
  • Ecological pyramids: number (pyramid of numbers), biomass (pyramid of biomass), energy (pyramid of energy).
  • Pyramid of energy: always upright; each level has less energy than the one below (10% rule).
  • Pyramid of numbers: usually upright; can be inverted (e.g., one tree supports many insects).
  • Pyramid of biomass: usually upright; can be inverted in aquatic ecosystems (phytoplankton turn over fast).
  • Productivity: gross primary productivity (GPP = total photosynthesis), net primary productivity (NPP = GPP − plant respiration).
  • Consumer productivity: energy assimilated by consumers per unit time.
  • NEB focus: food chain, food web, trophic levels, ecological pyramids, and productivity.
Confusion
  • A food chain is LINEAR; a food web is a NETWORK of chains (more realistic).
  • Producers (plants, phytoplankton) are at trophic level 1; herbivores at level 2; carnivores at level 3+.
  • The pyramid of ENERGY is always upright; the pyramids of numbers and biomass CAN be inverted.
  • An inverted pyramid of numbers occurs when one large organism supports many small ones (e.g., a tree with many insects).
  • An inverted pyramid of biomass occurs in aquatic ecosystems where phytoplankton (producers) have low standing biomass but high turnover.
  • The 10% rule: only ~10% of energy transfers from one trophic level to the next; ~90% is lost as heat.

Universal Facts
  • The 10% rule (Lindeman, 1942) explains why food chains are usually 4–5 trophic levels long.
  • A pyramid of energy is ALWAYS upright because energy is lost at each transfer (heat, respiration).
  • Phytoplankton in the ocean produce ~50% of Earth's oxygen (GPP of aquatic ecosystems).
  • NPP is the energy available to consumers (herbivores) in an ecosystem.
Examples
  • Grassland food chain: grass → rabbit → fox → eagle (4 trophic levels).
  • Pond food web: algae → mosquito larva → frog → snake → eagle; also algae → zooplankton → small fish → big fish.
  • Inverted pyramid of numbers: one oak tree (TL1) → many aphids (TL2) → a few ladybirds (TL3).
  • Inverted pyramid of biomass: phytoplankton (TL1) → zooplankton (TL2) → small fish (TL3) in the ocean.
  • Productivity: GPP = 1000 gC/m²/yr; plant respiration = 500 gC/m²/yr → NPP = 500 gC/m²/yr.

Formulas
  • Food chain: Producer (TL1) → Primary consumer (TL2) → Secondary consumer (TL3) → Tertiary consumer (TL4)
  • 10% rule: only ~10% of energy transfers between trophic levels
  • GPP = total photosynthesis; NPP = GPP − plant respiration (R)
  • Pyramid of energy: always upright; each level < the one below
Key Points
  • Food chain = linear; food web = network of chains.
  • Trophic levels: TL1 producers, TL2 herbivores, TL3 carnivores, TL4+ top carnivores.
  • Three pyramids: number, biomass, energy.
  • Pyramid of energy is ALWAYS upright; number and biomass can be inverted.
  • 10% rule: ~10% energy transfers between levels.
  • Productivity: GPP (total), NPP (GPP − respiration).
  • NPP is the energy available to herbivores.
Summary

Energy and matter flow through ecosystems in food chains (linear) and food webs (networks). Trophic levels (producers → primary consumers → secondary → tertiary) structure these. Ecological pyramids (number, biomass, energy) visualise trophic structure; the pyramid of energy is always upright because ~90% of energy is lost at each transfer (10% rule). Productivity (GPP, NPP) measures the rate of energy capture and availability to consumers. NEB focuses on food chains, food webs, trophic levels, the three pyramids, and productivity calculations.
