Notes

  • Detailed notes on 02 food chain food web trophic level ecological pyramids productivity within the ecology unit.
  • Food chain: a linear sequence of organisms where each is eaten by the next; transfers energy and matter (sun → producer → primary consumer → secondary consumer → tertiary consumer).
  • Food web: a network of interconnected food chains; more realistic than a single chain; shows multiple feeding relationships.
  • Trophic levels: TL1 producers (autotrophs), TL2 primary consumers (herbivores), TL3 secondary consumers (carnivores), TL4+ tertiary consumers.
  • Ecological pyramids: number (pyramid of numbers), biomass (pyramid of biomass), energy (pyramid of energy).
  • Pyramid of energy: always upright; each level has less energy than the one below (10% rule).
  • Pyramid of numbers: usually upright; can be inverted (e.g., one tree supports many insects).
  • Pyramid of biomass: usually upright; can be inverted in aquatic ecosystems (phytoplankton turn over fast).
  • Productivity: gross primary productivity (GPP = total photosynthesis), net primary productivity (NPP = GPP − plant respiration).
  • Consumer productivity: energy assimilated by consumers per unit time.
  • NEB focus: food chain, food web, trophic levels, ecological pyramids, and productivity.
  • In an ecosystem one can observe the transfer or flow of energy from one trophic level to other trophic level by the process of eating and being eaten.
  • It forms a linear chain called food chain.
  • There are basically three types of food chain in nature: 1.
  • Grazing food chain: The food chain that starts from the green plants and ends to carnivores (top consumer) by passing though herbivores.
  • Detritus food chain: The organic wastes and dead matter derived from grazing food chain are called as detritus.
  • The food chain that starts with detritus is called detritus food chain.
  • Parasitic food chain: This food chain starts either from producers or consumers.
  • It goes from large organisms to smaller ones without killing.

Confusion

  • ❌ Misconception about 02 food chain food web trophic level ecological pyramids productivity. ✅ Scientific explanation.
  • A food chain is LINEAR; a food web is a NETWORK of chains (more realistic).
  • Producers (plants, phytoplankton) are at trophic level 1; herbivores at level 2; carnivores at level 3+.
  • The pyramid of ENERGY is always upright; the pyramids of numbers and biomass CAN be inverted.
  • An inverted pyramid of numbers occurs when one large organism supports many small ones (e.g., a tree with many insects).
  • An inverted pyramid of biomass occurs in aquatic ecosystems where phytoplankton (producers) have low standing biomass but high turnover.
  • The 10% rule: only ~10% of energy transfers from one trophic level to the next; ~90% is lost as heat.
