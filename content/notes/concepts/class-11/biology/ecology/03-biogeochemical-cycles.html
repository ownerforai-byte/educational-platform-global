Enriched Content

Notes
  • Biogeochemical cycles: the movement of elements (C, N, P, H, O) through biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) compartments.
  • Carbon cycle: atmospheric CO2 → photosynthesis (plants) → respiration (animals + plants) → decomposition → soil → oceans; combustion (fossil fuels) releases CO2; carbonate rocks (limestone) store carbon long-term.
  • Nitrogen cycle: atmospheric N2 (78%) → nitrogen fixation (Rhizobium, Azotobacter, lightning) → ammonia (NH3) → nitrification (Nitrosomonas → nitrite; Nitrobacter → nitrate) → assimilation (plants) → ammonification (decomposers) → denitrification (Pseudomonas → N2).
  • Phosphorus cycle: rock (apatite) → weathering → soil → plants → animals → decomposition → sediment; no gaseous phase (unlike C, N).
  • Water cycle: evaporation → condensation → precipitation → runoff → oceans.
  • Succession: the process by which an ecosystem changes over time; primary (on bare rock, no soil) vs secondary (on existing soil).
  • Primary succession: bare rock → lichens/mosses → grasses → shrubs → forest (climax).
  • Secondary succession: after a disturbance (fire, flood, logging) → grasses → shrubs → forest (faster than primary).
  • Climax community: stable, mature community in equilibrium with the environment (e.g., mature oak-rhododendron forest in Nepal).
  • NEB focus: carbon and nitrogen cycles; concept of succession.
Confusion
  • Nitrogen fixation converts N2 to ammonia (NH3/NH4+); nitrification converts ammonia to nitrite then nitrate; denitrification converts nitrate back to N2.
  • Rhizobium (symbiotic, in legume root nodules) and Azotobacter (free-living, in soil) are both nitrogen-fixing bacteria.
  • The carbon cycle has a gaseous phase (CO2); the phosphorus cycle does NOT have a gaseous phase.
  • Primary succession starts on BARE ROCK (no soil); secondary succession starts on EXISTING SOIL (after disturbance).
  • Succession is a PREDICTABLE, directional process toward a stable climax community.
  • Decomposition releases nutrients (C, N, P) back into the soil; without it, cycles would stop.

  • Draw and explain the nitrogen cycle (all major steps).
  • Differentiate primary from secondary succession.
  • Define climax community and give a Nepali example.
  • Explain the role of bacteria in the nitrogen cycle.
Universal Facts
  • Atmospheric nitrogen (N2) makes up ~78% of the air but is unusable by most organisms until fixed.
  • Carbon dioxide is the main gaseous form of carbon in the carbon cycle.
  • The phosphorus cycle has no gaseous phase; it is the slowest of the major biogeochemical cycles.
  • Climax forests in Nepal: mature oak-rhododendron (temperate) or sal (Terai) forests.
Examples
  • Carbon cycle: forest (photosynthesis absorbs CO2) → animals (respiration releases CO2) → decomposers (release CO2) → atmosphere → ocean (dissolved CO2) → limestone (long-term storage).
  • Nitrogen cycle: Rhizobium in pea root nodules fixes N2 → plants assimilate nitrate → animals eat plants → decomposers ammonify → denitrifying bacteria release N2.
  • Primary succession: bare rock after a volcanic eruption → lichens → mosses → grasses → shrubs → forest.
  • Secondary succession: after a forest fire in Nepal → regrowth of grasses → shrubs → trees (faster than primary).
  • Climax community: a mature, stable oak-rhododendron forest on the Nepal hills.

Formulas
  • Carbon cycle: CO2 ↔ organic C (photosynthesis/respiration/decomposition); fossil fuel combustion → CO2
  • Nitrogen cycle: N2 → (fixation) NH3 → (nitrification) NO2- → NO3- → (assimilation) plants → (ammonification) NH3 → (denitrification) N2
  • Succession: primary (bare rock) / secondary (existing soil) → climax community
Key Points
  • Biogeochemical cycles: C, N, P, H, O move through biotic + abiotic compartments.
  • Carbon cycle: gaseous (CO2); photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion.
  • Nitrogen cycle: N2 → NH3 → NO2- → NO3- → assimilation → ammonification → N2.
  • Bacteria: Rhizobium (fixation), Nitrosomonas (nitrification), Pseudomonas (denitrification).
  • Phosphorus cycle: no gaseous phase; slowest cycle.
  • Succession: primary (bare rock) vs secondary (existing soil); climax community is stable.
  • Climax: mature, stable community in equilibrium with the environment.
Summary

Biogeochemical cycles move essential elements through living and non-living compartments. The carbon cycle (gaseous CO2) and the nitrogen cycle (N2 → NH3 → NO2- → NO3- → N2) are the most important; bacteria (Rhizobium, Nitrosomonas, Pseudomonas) drive key steps. The phosphorus cycle has no gaseous phase and is the slowest. Succession is the directional change of an ecosystem toward a stable climax community: primary succession starts on bare rock, secondary on existing soil. NEB focuses on the carbon and nitrogen cycles and the concept of succession.

Special Notes
  • NEB expects detailed, labelled diagrams of the carbon and nitrogen cycles.
  • The role of bacteria (Rhizobium, Nitrosomonas, Pseudomonas) in the nitrogen cycle is a very common exam point.
  • Primary vs secondary succession: key difference is the starting point (bare rock vs existing soil).
  • Climax community: give a Nepali example (mature oak-rhododendron forest).
  • Note that the phosphorus cycle has no gaseous phase.
Important Statements
  • Biogeochemical cycles recycle essential elements through biotic and abiotic compartments.
  • Nitrogen fixation converts atmospheric N2 to usable ammonia.
  • Primary succession begins on bare rock; secondary succession on existing soil.
  • A climax community is a stable, mature ecosystem in equilibrium with its environment.

Exercises
1.

In the nitrogen cycle, a plant absorbs nitrate (NO3-). What does the plant use it for, and how does the nitrogen eventually return to the atmosphere?

ID

1

Steps
  • Plant uses NO3- to make amino acids/proteins (assimilation)
  • Animal eats plant; N moves to animal proteins
  • Decomposers break down dead tissue → ammonia (ammonification)
  • Nitrosomonas → nitrite; Nitrobacter → nitrate
  • Pseudomonas (denitrifying bacteria) convert NO3- to N2 gas
Answer

The plant uses nitrate to build proteins; nitrogen returns to the atmosphere via denitrifying bacteria (Pseudomonas) converting nitrate to N2
