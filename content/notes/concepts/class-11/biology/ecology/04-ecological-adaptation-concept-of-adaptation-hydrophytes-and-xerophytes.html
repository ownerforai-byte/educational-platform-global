Notes

  • Detailed notes on 04 ecological adaptation concept of adaptation hydrophytes and xerophytes within the ecology unit.
  • Ecological adaptation: the structural, physiological, and behavioural features that allow an organism to survive in a particular environment.
  • Adaptation types: structural (body form), physiological (internal processes), behavioural (activities).
  • Hydrophytes (water plants): adapted to aquatic life; examples: water hyacinth (Nymphaea lotus), lotus, Vallisneria.
  • Hydrophyte adaptations: aerenchyma (air spaces in tissues for gas exchange), floating leaves, reduced root system, stomata on upper surface, no cuticle or thin cuticle.
  • Xerophytes (dry-land plants): adapted to arid conditions; examples: cacti, Casuarina, Prosopis.
  • Xerophyte adaptations: thick cuticle, reduced leaves (spines in cacti), deep or extensive root system, succulent stem, CAM photosynthesis, stomata in pits.
  • Mesophytes: plants of moderate moisture (most temperate plants); intermediate adaptations.
  • NEB focus: concept of adaptation, hydrophytes, and xerophytes with their adaptations.
  • Adaptation is the result of natural selection over generations; it is not "intentional" or "goal-directed".
  • Any factor, abiotic or biotic, that influences the living beings are called as ecological factors or environmental factors.
  • These factors either singly or in combination influence or determine the presence or absence, vigour or weakness and relative success or failure of various plant communities in a particular habitat.
  • Ecological factors are classified into two groups: 4.

  • Abiotic Factors: The non-living things of the environment are known as abiotic factors.
  • The abiotic factors that influence the living beings are grouped into three main group: climatic factors, topographic factor and Edaphic factor.

Confusion

  • ❌ Misconception about 04 ecological adaptation concept of adaptation hydrophytes and xerophytes. ✅ Scientific explanation.
  • Hydrophytes have AERENCHYMA (air spaces) for gas exchange; xerophytes have reduced surface area to conserve water.
  • Cacti's "spines" are modified LEAVES (to reduce water loss); the stem is the photosynthetic organ (succulent).
  • Xerophytes often use CAM photosynthesis (open stomata at night to reduce water loss); hydrophytes use the C3 pathway with stomata on the upper surface.
  • Hydrophytes may have REDUCED roots (they float; roots are for anchorage, not water uptake).
  • Adaptation is a POPULATION-LEVEL feature (evolution over generations), NOT an individual changing during its life.
  • Acclimatisation (short-term, individual adjustment) is different from adaptation (long-term, evolutionary).

Summary

Ecological adaptation refers to the structural, physiological, and behavioural features that allow an organism to survive in its environment. Hydrophytes (water plants) have aerenchyma, floating leaves, reduced roots, and thin cuticles for aquatic life. Xerophytes (dry-land plants) have thick cuticles, spines (modified leaves), succulent stems, deep roots, and CAM photosynthesis for arid conditions. Mesophytes of moderate moisture have intermediate adaptations. Adaptation is an evolutionary (population-level) process, distinct from short-term acclimatisation. NEB focuses on the concept of adaptation, hydrophytes, and xerophytes with their specific adaptations.
