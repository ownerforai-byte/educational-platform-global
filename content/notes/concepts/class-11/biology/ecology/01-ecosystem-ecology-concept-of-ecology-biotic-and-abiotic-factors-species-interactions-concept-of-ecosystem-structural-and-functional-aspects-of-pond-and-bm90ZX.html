Notes

  • Detailed notes on 01 ecosystem ecology concept of ecology biotic and abiotic factors species interactions concept of ecosystem structural and functional aspects of pond and forest ecosystem within the ecology unit.
  • Ecology: the study of the interactions between organisms and their environment (biotic + abiotic).
  • Biotic factors: living components (plants, animals, microorganisms); Abiotic factors: non-living (light, temperature, water, soil, pH, gases).
  • Species interactions: mutualism (+/+), commensalism (+/0), parasitism (+/-), predation (+/-), competition (-/-), amensalism (-/0), neutralism (0/0).
  • Ecosystem: a functional unit of biotic + abiotic components interacting; types: aquatic (pond, lake, ocean) and terrestrial (grassland, forest, desert).
  • Structural aspects: producers (autotrophs), consumers (herbivores, carnivores, omnivores), decomposers (bacteria, fungi); trophic levels.
  • Functional aspects: energy flow (unidirectional, sun → producers → consumers → heat), nutrient cycling (biogeochemical), and food chains/webs.
  • Pond and forest ecosystems: producers (phytoplankton / trees), consumers (zooplankton / herbivores / carnivores), decomposers; abiotic factors (light, temperature, water, soil).
  • Nepal: Terai, hills, and mountains each have distinct ecosystems (e.g., Chitwan Terai, temperate oak-rhododendron hills, alpine zone).
  • NEB focus: concept of ecology, biotic/abiotic factors, species interactions, and structural + functional aspects of pond and forest ecosystems.
  • In nature an organism does not exist in isolation, it is associated with organisms of its own and of different kinds as well as with the physical environment.
  • If the organism is plant, it depends for growth on the aerial environment for light, temperature and carbon dioxide, on the soil environment for inorganic nutrients and water.
  • If it is an animal it depends on the plants for food and shelter.
  • If it is a microorganism it depend on the dead organic matter from plants and animals for its food, and releases the nutrients tied in the organic matter for the reuse of plants.
  • Thus, there are continuous interactions among the organisms and between the organisms and the habitat resulting into an integrated, functional unit.
  • This functional unit is known as ecosystem.
  • It is the structural and functional unit of ecology.
  • Thus, ecosystem is defined as ‘the structural and functional unit of ecology where the living organisms interact with each other and the surrounding environment’.

Confusion

  • ❌ Misconception about 01 ecosystem ecology concept of ecology biotic and abiotic factors species interactions concept of ecosystem structural and functional aspects of pond and forest ecosystem. ✅ Scientific explanation.
  • Predation (+/-) and parasitism (+/-) both harm one partner; predation usually KILLS the prey quickly, parasitism is long-term and usually does not kill the host immediately.
  • Mutualism (+/+) benefits both; commensalism (+/0) benefits one, affects neither; parasitism (+/-) benefits one, harms the other.
  • Competition (-/-) harms BOTH species (interspecific); intra-specific competition is within the same species.
  • An ecosystem is a FUNCTIONAL unit (energy flow + nutrient cycling); a community is just the assemblage of species.
  • Energy flows ONEDIRECTIONALLY through an ecosystem (sun → producers → consumers → heat); it is NOT cycled.
  • Nutrients (C, N, P) ARE cycled (biogeochemical); energy is not cycled.

Important Statements
  • An ecosystem is a functional unit of biotic and abiotic components.
  • Energy flows unidirectionally through an ecosystem; nutrients cycle.
  • Species interact through mutualism, commensalism, parasitism, predation, and competition.
  • Producers, consumers, and decomposers are the structural components of every ecosystem.

In a pond, a mosquito larva feeds on algae, and a dragonfly larva feeds on the mosquito larva. Identify the trophic levels of each.

ID

1

Steps
  • Algae = producer (trophic level 1)
  • Mosquito larva (herbivore) = primary consumer (TL2)
  • Dragonfly larva (carnivore) = secondary consumer (TL3)
Answer

Algae (TL1 producer), mosquito larva (TL2 primary consumer), dragonfly larva (TL3 secondary consumer)
