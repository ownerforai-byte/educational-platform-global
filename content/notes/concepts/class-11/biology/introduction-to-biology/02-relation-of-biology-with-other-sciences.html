  • 1. Topic Introduction — Biology as a Bridge Science

    Biology sits between the physical sciences and the human world: it borrows chemistry's molecules, physics's instruments and mathematics's models, and lends its findings to medicine, agriculture and society.   • 3. The Biological Method of Investigation (NEB 2022, 2024)

    Science advances by a cycle: observe → question → hypothesise → predict → experiment → analyse → conclude → communicate.

    StepWhat happensExample: "Why do leaves of sal fall in winter?"
    1. ObservationNotice a phenomenon with senses/instrumentsSal trees shed leaves in the dry season
    2. QuestionConvert the observation into a testable questionDoes water stress trigger leaf fall?
    3. HypothesisAn educated, testable, falsifiable tentative answer"Sal sheds leaves to reduce water loss when soil moisture falls"
    4. Prediction (deduction)If the hypothesis is true, then X should happenIf watered regularly, sal trees should retain leaves
    5. ExperimentTest with control vs experimental groups, varying one factor (independent variable), measuring another (dependent variable)Two groups of saplings: watered (control) vs unwatered (experimental); measure leaf retention
    6. Data analysisOrganise, graph, apply statisticsCompare % leaf retention between groups
    7. ConclusionAccept, reject or modify the hypothesisWatered trees retain leaves → hypothesis supported
    8. Publication/verificationReport methods and results so others can repeatJournal, replication by other labs

    Key principles:

    • Controlled experiment: only one variable differs between groups; everything else constant.
    • Falsifiability: a hypothesis must be capable of being proven wrong — that is what makes it scientific.
    • Theory: a hypothesis confirmed repeatedly by many independent tests (e.g. cell theory, evolution) — not a "guess".
    • Verification and repetition: results must be reproducible by others.

    3.1 A classic worked example — Pasteur and spontaneous generation

    Observation: broth left open "grows" microbes. Question: do microbes arise spontaneously? Hypothesis: microbes come from the air, not from the broth. Prediction: broth kept air-free stays sterile. Experiment: swan-neck flasks — broth sterilised, necks curved so dust and microbes settle in the bend but air enters. Result: broth stayed sterile for months; neck broken → broth spoiled. Conclusion: life comes from existing life (biogenesis) — a hypothesis tested, confirmed and repeated, becoming a principle.

    Q1 (5m) — Steps of the biological method with a suitable example. Answer: The biological method is the logical cycle by which biology converts curiosity into knowledge: (1) Observation — e.g. broth left open soon swarms with microbes; (2) Question — do microbes arise spontaneously from the broth?; (3) Hypothesis — a testable tentative answer: microbes enter from airborne dust, not from the broth itself; (4) Prediction — if true, sterilised broth protected from dust should stay sterile while remaining exposed to air; (5) Experiment — Pasteur's swan-neck flasks: broth sterilised; curved necks admit air but trap dust (control vs experimental when a neck is broken); (6) Data analysis — compare sterility over weeks; (7) Conclusion — broth stays sterile until the neck is broken: microbes come from existing life (biogenesis); the hypothesis is supported; (8) Publication and verification — results published and repeated worldwide, becoming a foundation principle of biology.     Q2 (2m) — Steps of the scientific method. Answer: Observation → question → hypothesis (testable, falsifiable) → prediction → controlled experiment → data analysis → conclusion (accept/reject/modify) → publication and verification. (List the steps with one-line meanings for full marks.)

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Calling a hypothesis "a theory" — a theory is a repeatedly verified explanation.
    2. Omitting the control group in the experiment step.
    3. Writing an unfalsifiable hypothesis ("God made it") — not scientific.
    4. Forgetting the example — the 5m question demands one (Pasteur, photosynthesis, leaf-fall all work).
    5. Skipping publication/verification — science is communal and repeatable.
Confusion
  • Hypothesis vs theory: hypothesis = a single testable tentative explanation; theory = a hypothesis (or set) verified repeatedly across many independent tests.
  • Control vs experimental group: control = normal/baseline condition; experimental = the one factor changed — the difference isolates the variable's effect.

Summary

Original Content

Notes
  • Biology is interconnected with other sciences: it relies on and contributes to Chemistry (biochemistry), Physics (biophysics), Mathematics (biostatistics), and Earth Science (geobiology).
  • Biochemistry: the chemistry of life — metabolism, enzymes, DNA/RNA/protein structures.
  • Biophysics: physics applied to biology — biophysics of the cell, nerve conduction, muscle mechanics, imaging (MRI, X-ray).
  • Biostatistics: mathematics used in biology — genetics (Punnett), population models, experimental design.
  • Geobiology: interaction of life with Earth — evolution (fossils, climate), ecology (biogeochemical cycles), palaeontology.
  • Interdisciplinary fields: medicine (biology + chemistry + physics), agriculture (biology + ecology), conservation biology (biology + policy), biotechnology (biology + engineering).
  • NEB focus: how biology connects with chemistry, physics, mathematics, and earth science.
  • Biology uses scientific tools from other disciplines: microscopes (physics), spectrometers (chemistry), statistical software (mathematics).
Confusion
  • Biochemistry is NOT the same as organic chemistry; it focuses on the chemistry of living systems (metabolism, enzymes, macromolecules).
  • Biophysics applies physics to biological problems; it is not "physics of life" in a vague sense.
  • Biostatistics is a branch of statistics, not a branch of biology; it is used BY biologists.
  • Geobiology (biology + earth science) is different from ecology (biology + environment).
  • Interdisciplinary fields combine multiple sciences; they are NOT a new "fourth" science.
  • Biology uses tools from other sciences (microscopes, spectrometers) but has its own methods (experiments, observations, classifications).

Practice

  • Why must a control group exist for an experiment to mean anything?

    Answer. The control group is identical to the experimental group in every factor except the one variable being tested. It provides the baseline against which the treatment is compared. Without it, any difference could come from weather, soil, time, handling or chance — not from the factor under study. The control is what makes the one-variable-at-a-time logic of the scientific method possible and the conclusion attributable.

Practice Questions

  • Q. What makes a hypothesis scientific?

    Answer. A hypothesis is scientific when it is:

    1. Testable — it makes predictions that observation or experiment can check.
    2. Falsifiable — it states clearly what result would disprove it.
    3. Precise and repeatable — other workers can repeat the test and get the same result.

    A claim that no possible observation could disprove (for example, "plants grow because of an unknowable life force") lies outside science.

Biology as a hub science — borrowing methods from every other discipline

Name

Shared physical and chemical basis

Points
  • All life obeys the same physics and chemistry: diffusion, osmosis, thermodynamics, bonding
  • Water's polarity and hydrogen bonding explain almost every transport and solvent property in cells
  • Carbon's tetravalency underwrites the whole diversity of organic molecules

Topic

Level 2 — Core: the interface map

Subtopics
1.
Name

Biology with physical sciences

Points
  • Biochemistry: reaction pathways, enzyme kinetics, energetics of ATP
  • Biophysics: membrane potentials, muscle mechanics, X-ray crystallography of DNA and proteins
  • Physical chemistry explains pH, buffers, colligative properties and osmosis in living systems
2.
Name

Biology with formal sciences

Points
  • Biomathematics: population growth models, epidemic curves, genetic ratios, statistical tests
  • Bioinformatics: sequence alignment, phylogenetics, database mining of genomes
  • Statistics is essential because biological variation is the norm, not the exception
3.
Name

Biology with social and earth sciences

Points
  • Geography and geology: biogeography, fossil record, soil formation, plate tectonics and species distribution
  • Psychology and neuroscience: behaviour, learning, reflex pathways
  • Economics and ethics: agriculture, conservation policy, bioethics of gene editing

Name

From reduction to systems

Points
  • Reductionism explains parts; systems biology explains how parts interact as networks
  • Omics layers stack: genome -> transcriptome -> proteome -> metabolome -> phenotype
  • Emergent properties (consciousness, ecosystem stability) cannot be read off a single molecule

Ethical boundary
