Notes

  • A population is the members of one species in a place. A community is the populations living together. An ecosystem adds the physical surroundings.
  • A niche is the role: what the organism eats, when it is active, and which conditions it tolerates. Two species do not keep identical niches for long.
  • Biotic factors are the living influences. Abiotic factors are light, temperature, water, soil and salinity.
  • A food chain moves energy from a producer to consumers. A food web is many chains joined. Decomposers return matter to the soil.
  • Only about ten percent of the energy at one level is available at the next. The rest is lost as heat and as work of staying alive. Chains therefore stay short.
  • A pond and a forest are both ecosystems. The pond's producers are algae and water plants. The forest's are trees. The structure differs. The energy rule does not.

Important Concepts

  • Food webs: interconnected feeding relationships between producers, consumers, and decomposers
  • Energy flow: energy transfers through trophic levels (producers → herbivores → carnivores)
  • Biomass: total mass of living organisms in an ecosystem
  • Carrying capacity: maximum population size an environment can sustain
  • Succession: gradual change in species composition over time (primary/secondary succession)

Important Statements

  • Energy flows through ecosystems but matter cycles continuously.
  • Ecological succession describes how communities change over time after disturbance.
  • Keystone species have disproportionately large effects on ecosystem structure.

Key Points

  • Food chains and food webs illustrate energy transfer and nutrient cycling.
  • Competition, predation, and mutualism shape species interactions.
  • Ecosystems can be classified by location (terrestrial, aquatic, marine) or by productivity level.
  • Population, community, ecosystem.
  • Niche versus habitat.
  • Food chain and web.
  • About 10 percent energy transfer.

Special Notes

  • A keystone predator (e.g., sea otters) maintains balanced predator-prey dynamics.
  • Primary succession begins on bare rock or volcanic ash; secondary succession follows disturbances like fire or flooding.
  • Ecosystem services include pollination, water purification, and climate regulation.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Food web = complex network of feeding relationships.
  • Carrying capacity = max population sustainable by the environment.
  • Keystone species = has large impact relative to abundance.

Exam Notes

  • Distinguish between biotic (living) and abiotic (non-living) factors.
  • Understand the difference between population density and carrying capacity.
  • Learn about ecological succession stages (primary vs secondary).

Practice Questions

  • What is a food web?
  • Define carrying capacity and give an example.
  • How do keystone species influence ecosystem structure?
  • Explain primary vs secondary ecological succession.

Confusion

  • A community does not include the climate. An ecosystem does.
  • Ten percent transfer is a loss, not a gain, at each step.
  • A niche is not a habitat. The habitat is the address. The niche is the job.
  • Decomposers are not producers.

Exercises

1.

Why are food chains short?

Steps
  • Each transfer passes on about a tenth of the energy.
  • The rest is lost as heat and maintenance.
  • After a few steps too little energy remains for another level.
  • A web does not cancel that loss. It only joins chains.
Answer

Because about nine tenths of the energy is lost at each step.

Summary

Ecology scales from organism to population, community and ecosystem. A niche is a role, a habitat an address. Food chains pass on about ten percent of the energy at each step, which is why they stay short. Ponds and forests differ in their producers and share that energy rule.
