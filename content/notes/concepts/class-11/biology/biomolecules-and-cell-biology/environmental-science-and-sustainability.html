Notes

  • A sustainable use takes no more of a resource, and dumps no more waste, than the system can replace or absorb.
  • Climate pressure, polluted air and water, loss of habitat and the using-up of freshwater and fertile soil are the four pressures named in this note.
  • Renewable resources renew if the harvest stays below the renewal. A forest cut faster than it grows is being used as if it were a mine.
  • Non-renewable resources, such as coal and oil, are not replaced on a human timescale. Sustainability then means using less and wasting less.
  • Reduce, reuse and recycle cut the demand and the rubbish. Community forestry in Nepal is a worked case of a renewable resource brought back under local rules.
  • A practice fails the test if it solves today's need by destroying the stock the next harvest depends on. That is the whole standard.

Important Concepts

  • Carbon cycle: exchange of carbon between atmosphere, oceans, and living organisms
  • Water cycle: evaporation, condensation, precipitation, runoff
  • Ecosystem services: provisioning, regulating, cultural, and supporting services
  • Sustainable development: meeting present needs without compromising future generations

Important Statements

  • Climate change is driven primarily by greenhouse gas emissions (CO2, methane, nitrous oxide).
  • Sustainability requires balancing economic growth, social equity, and environmental protection.
  • Renewable energy sources (solar, wind, hydro) reduce reliance on fossil fuels.

Key Points

  • Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere, causing global warming.
  • Deforestation reduces carbon sequestration capacity and disrupts water cycles.
  • Circular economy principles aim to minimize waste and maximize resource reuse.
  • Use no faster than renewal.
  • Forest, water, soil, fuel.
  • Reduce, reuse, recycle.
  • Community forestry as a case.

Special Notes

  • The Paris Agreement (2015) sets targets to limit global warming to well below 2°C.
  • Biodiversity hotspots (e.g., Amazon rainforest, coral reefs) are critical for ecosystem resilience.
  • Sustainable agriculture practices (crop rotation, cover cropping) reduce environmental impact.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Climate change = greenhouse gases + global warming.
  • Sustainability = economic + social + environmental balance.
  • Ecosystem services = benefits humans derive from nature.

Exam Notes

  • Connect environmental science with policy (e.g., carbon taxes, renewable energy mandates).
  • Understand the difference between renewable and non-renewable resources.
  • Consider the socio-economic dimensions of sustainability (equity, justice).

Practice Questions

  • What is the primary driver of climate change?
  • Define sustainable development and its three pillars.
  • How does deforestation affect the carbon cycle?
  • Explain the concept of ecosystem services.

Confusion

  • Renewable does not mean infinite. A forest can be overcut.
  • Recycling is not a licence to waste more.
  • Community forestry is management, not a new species.
  • Pollution is a waste problem, not a separate planet from resource use.

Exercises

1.

Why can a renewable forest still be used unsustainably?

Steps
  • Renewal has a rate.
  • If the cut exceeds the growth, the stock falls.
  • The resource is renewable only when the harvest respects that rate.
  • Community forestry is one way of keeping the cut inside the growth.
Answer

Because harvest can exceed regrowth.

Summary

Sustainability keeps harvest and waste inside what the system can renew and absorb. Forests, water and soil are renewable only within a rate. Coal and oil are not renewed on a human timescale. Using less, reusing and recycling, as in a well-run community forest, is the practical test.
