Enriched Content

Notes
  • DETAILED ORGANELLE ANALYSIS (plant + animal cell).
  • Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer (heads out, tails in) + integral proteins (channels, pumps, receptors) + peripheral proteins + cholesterol + glycocalyx (cell recognition). Fluid-mosaic model (Singer & Nicolson, 1972).
  • Mitochondrion: double membrane; outer smooth, inner folded into cristae; matrix with Krebs enzymes; own DNA + 70S ribosomes (endosymbiosis); aerobic respiration: glycolysis (cytoplasm) → Krebs (matrix) → ETS (cristae) → ~34 ATP.
  • Chloroplast: double membrane; thylakoids stacked into grana; stroma with Calvin-cycle enzymes; chlorophyll a + b; own DNA + 70S ribosomes; light reactions (grana → ATP, NADPH, O2) + Calvin cycle (stroma → glucose).
  • Nucleus: nuclear envelope (4 nm, two membranes, pores); nucleoplasm; chromatin (DNA + histone proteins); nucleolus (rRNA + ribosome assembly).
  • Rough ER (RER): ribosomes on outer surface → protein synthesis (secretory + membrane proteins); continuous with nuclear envelope.
  • Smooth ER (SER): lipid synthesis, carbohydrate metabolism, detoxification (liver), Ca2+ storage (muscle — sarcoplasmic reticulum).
  • Golgi apparatus: cisternae + vesicles; modifies (glycosylation), packages, sorts, and secretes proteins; forms lysosomes + cell plate (plants).
  • Lysosomes: 8–12 nm vesicle, hydrolytic enzymes (acidic pH); digest food particles, worn organelles (autophagy); "suicide bags" when damaged.
  • Vacuole: plant — large central (turgor, storage); animal — small (excretion, osmoregulation, contractile in protozoa).
  • Cytoskeleton: microfilaments (actin), intermediate filaments, microtubules (tubulin); shape, movement, intracellular transport, division (spindle).
  • Centrioles (animal): microtubule rings; form the spindle during mitosis; absent in most plants.
  • Cell inclusions: storage granules (starch, fat, glycogen), pigments (leuco-, chro-, heterochromoplasts), crystalloids.
  • Cell wall (plant): cellulose microfibrils (primary wall) + pectin; secondary wall with lignin; fully permeable; provides rigidity + protection.
Confusion
  • RER makes PROTEIN; SER makes LIPID. A cell secreting lots of protein has abundant RER + Golgi (e.g., pancreatic acinar cell).
  • The Golgi does NOT synthesise proteins; it MODIFIES, SORTS, and PACKAGES what the RER made.
  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their OWN DNA + 70S ribosomes — the key endosymbiosis evidence.
  • Lysosomes are animal-cell organelles; plants use the large vacuole for hydrolytic digestion.
  • Cilia and flagella share the 9+2 axonemal microtubule structure; centrioles (9+0) are related but lack the central pair.
  • The nucleus is NOT the only place with DNA: mitochondria + chloroplasts carry their own genomes.
Practice
  • Draw a fully labelled plant cell (all organelles + wall + chloroplast + large vacuole).
  • Differentiate RER and SER (structure + function, 4 points).
  • Explain the endosymbiotic theory with the 4 lines of evidence.
  • Trace the pathway of a secreted protein (ribosome → RER → Golgi → vesicle → membrane).
  • State the functions of the nucleolus and the nuclear envelope pores.
Universal Facts
  • The liver cell contains over a million ribosomes.
  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts reproduce by binary fission, like bacteria.
  • The nuclear envelope is continuous with the outer membrane of the RER.
  • A typical human cell has ~500 mitochondria, varying by energy demand.
Examples
  • Secretory protein pathway: ribosome (free) → RER → Golgi → transport vesicle → plasma membrane (exocytosis).
  • Mitochondrial respiration: glucose → pyruvate (cytoplasm) → Krebs (matrix) → ETS (cristae) → ATP.
  • Chloroplast photosynthesis: light reactions in grana → ATP + NADPH; Calvin cycle in stroma → glucose.
  • Lysosome autophagy: damaged mitochondrion → autophagosome → lysosomal digestion → recycled.

  • Why are mitochondria and chloroplasts called semi-autonomous organelles?
  • Differentiate RER and SER (4 points).
  • Describe the protein-secretion pathway in a cell.
  • Explain the endosymbiotic theory with evidence.
  • State the role of the nucleolus.
Formulas
  • Fluid-mosaic: phospholipid bilayer + integral/peripheral protein + cholesterol + glycocalyx
  • Protein secretion: ribosome → RER → Golgi → vesicle → exocytosis
  • Mitochondrial respiration: glycolysis → Krebs → ETS → ~34 ATP
  • 9+2 axoneme (cilia/flagella); 9+0 centriole
Key Points
  • Each organelle has a distinct, examinable function (mitochondrion→ATP, chloroplast→photosynthesis, RER→protein, SER→lipid, Golgi→packaging, lysosome→digestion, nucleus→inheritance).
  • RER = rough = ribosomes = protein; SER = smooth = no ribosomes = lipid.
  • Mitochondria + chloroplast: own DNA, 70S ribosomes, double membrane, binary fission (endosymbiosis).
  • Protein-secretion pathway: ribosome → RER → Golgi → vesicle → membrane.
  • Plant cell: wall + chloroplast + large central vacuole; animal cell: centrioles + lysosomes + small vacuoles.
  • Cytoskeleton: microfilaments, intermediate filaments, microtubules.
  • Nucleus: envelope + nucleolus + chromatin; chromatin condenses to chromosomes in division.
Summary

A detailed examination of the eukaryotic cell shows how each membrane-bound organelle specialises a metabolic function. The fluid-mosaic plasma membrane controls transport; mitochondria and chloroplasts convert energy (and carry their own DNA, evidence of endosymbiosis); RER makes protein while SER makes lipid; the Golgi modifies and packages; lysosomes digest; the nucleus and nucleolus control heredity; and the cytoskeleton (microfilaments, microtubules) maintains shape and drives division. The protein-secretion pathway (ribosome → RER → Golgi → vesicle → membrane) and the plant/animal cell contrast are core NEB written-answer topics.

Special Notes
  • NEB written answers expect a labelled organelle diagram + a one-line function per organelle.
  • The endosymbiotic theory (4 evidence lines: own DNA, 70S ribosomes, double membrane, binary fission) is a favourite 4-mark question.
  • Trace the secreted-protein pathway step by step.
  • Contrast plant vs animal cell (wall, chloroplast, large vacuole vs centrioles, lysosomes).
  • RER/SER distinction is a very common short-answer.
Important Statements
  • The plasma membrane is the selectively permeable boundary of every cell.
  • Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration; chloroplasts of photosynthesis.
  • The Golgi apparatus modifies, sorts, and packages proteins and lipids.
  • Cilia, flagella, and centrioles are built from microtubules.
Important Notes
  • Memorise organelle → function pairs.
  • Draw a plant cell with all labels in one pass.
  • Learn the 9+2 axonemal structure of cilia/flagella and the 9+0 centriole.
  • Note plant-specific organelles (wall, chloroplast, central vacuole).
  • Trace the protein-secretion pathway from ribosome to membrane.
Exam Short Tricks
  • RER = rough = protein; SER = smooth = lipid.
  • Mito + Chloro = own DNA + 70S ribosomes = endosymbiosis.
  • Golgi = G-O-L-G-I = packages + SECREtes.
  • Plant = Wall + Chloroplast + big Vacuole; Animal = Centrioles + Lysosomes + small vacuoles.
  • Secretion: Ribosome → RER → Golgi → Vesicle → Membrane.
Exam Notes

  • Differentiate RER and SER.
  • Explain the endosymbiotic theory with 4 evidence lines.
  • Trace the protein-secretion pathway.
  • Contrast plant and animal cells (two-column table).
  • State the functions of the nucleolus and the Golgi.

Exercises
1.

Trace the pathway of a protein made by a ribosome that is to be secreted from the cell.

ID

1

Steps
  • Ribosome on RER synthesises the protein
  • Protein enters the RER lumen and is folded
  • Transport vesicle carries it to the Golgi
  • Golgi modifies (glycosylates) and packages it
  • Secretory vesicle moves to the plasma membrane
  • Exocytosis releases the protein outside
Answer

Ribosome (RER) → RER → Golgi → secretory vesicle → plasma membrane → exocytosis

1.

Trace the pathway of a protein made by a ribosome that is to be secreted from the cell.

ID

1

Steps
  • Ribosome on RER synthesises the protein
  • Protein enters the RER lumen and is folded
  • Transport vesicle carries it to the Golgi
  • Golgi modifies (glycosylates) and packages it
  • Secretory vesicle moves to the plasma membrane
  • Exocytosis releases the protein outside
Answer

Ribosome (RER) → RER → Golgi → secretory vesicle → plasma membrane → exocytosis
