Visual Type

organelle-functions

Enriched Content

Notes
  • Cell wall (plant: cellulose; bacteria: peptidoglycan; fungi: chitin) — rigid, fully permeable outer support; in plants it can secondarily thicken (lignin).
  • Plasma membrane: phospholipid bilayer + integral/protein (fluid-mosaic model, Singer & Nicolson 1972); selectively permeable; controls transport (diffusion, osmosis, active transport).
  • Mitochondrion: site of aerobic respiration (Krebs + ETS → ATP); double membrane; cristae; "powerhouse of the cell".
  • Chloroplast (plastid): photosynthesis; double membrane; thylakoid grana + stroma; green pigment chlorophyll a & b.
  • Endoplasmic reticulum: rough (RER, ribosome-studded, protein synthesis) and smooth (SER, lipid synthesis, detoxification).
  • Golgi apparatus: modifies, packages and secretes proteins and forms lysosomes and cell plate.
  • Lysosome: hydrolytic "digestive" enzymes; self-digestion = autophagy.
  • Ribosome: 80S in eukaryotes (60S+40S subunits) — protein synthesis; free or attached to RER.
  • Nucleus: genetic control centre; nuclear envelope, nucleolus, chromatin/chromosomes.
  • Cilia and flagella: locomotion/transport; composed of 9+2 microtubules (axoneme).
  • Cell inclusions: storage granules and pigments (e.g. oil droplets, glycogen granules, crystal inclusions).
Confusion
  • The cell wall is fully permeable; the plasma membrane is selectively permeable.
  • RER is rough because of ribosomes; it makes PROTEINS. SER makes LIPIDS and detoxifies — it has no ribosomes.
  • The Golgi packages proteins and lipids; it does not synthesise them.
  • Lysosomes digest the cell's own material when damaged (autophagy); they are not plant cells' organelles (plants use vacuoles).
  • Cilia and flagella both have a 9+2 microtubule axoneme; flagella are longer; in prokaryotes the flagellum has a different (no microtubule) structure.
  • Chromatin (diffuse, interphase) and chromosomes (condensed, division) are the SAME genetic material in two forms.
Practice
  • Label all organelles on a plant cell diagram.
  • Match each organelle with its main function in a table.
  • Differentiate rough and smooth endoplasmic reticulum (4 points).
  • Draw and explain the fluid-mosaic model of the plasma membrane.
  • State the functions of the nucleus, nucleolus and chromatin.
Universal Facts
  • Mitochondria and chloroplasts have their own DNA and 70S ribosomes, suggesting an endosymbiotic origin.
  • A single human liver cell may contain over one million ribosomes.
  • The nuclear envelope is continuous with the outer membrane of the endoplasmic reticulum.
Examples
  • Oxidative phosphorylation in the mitochondrial cristae produces the bulk of ATP.
  • Secretory cells (e.g. pancreatic) have abundant RER and Golgi for protein export.
  • Central vacuole in a plant cell stores water, pigments and maintains turgor.
Practice Questions
  • Why are the mitochondria and chloroplast called "semi-autonomous" organelles?
  • Differentiate rough and smooth endoplasmic reticulum.
  • Describe the functions of the Golgi body in a plant cell.
  • What is the significance of cristae in mitochondria?
  • State the role of the nucleolus.
Formulas
  • Fluid-mosaic model: phospholipid bilayer + protein mosaic + cholesterol + glycocalyx
  • 9+2 axonemal structure of cilia/flagella (9 doublets + central pair)
Key Points
  • Each organelle has a distinct, frequently-examined function (mitochondrion→ATP, chloroplast→photosynthesis).
  • RER makes protein; SER makes lipid; Golgi packages; lysosome digests.
  • Plasma membrane is selectively permeable fluid mosaic.
  • Nucleus + nucleolus + chromatin = genetic control; chromatin condenses into chromosomes.
  • Plant-specific: cell wall, chloroplast, large central vacuole.
  • Mitochondria and chloroplast carry their own DNA (endosymbiosis evidence).
Summary

The internal architecture of a eukaryotic cell is defined by membrane-bound organelles. Each has a specialised role: the plasma membrane controls transport; mitochondria and chloroplasts convert energy; RER/SER synthesise proteins and lipids; the Golgi, lysosomes and vacuoles process and store material; the nucleus controls inheritance; cilia, flagella and inclusions complete the picture. The endosymbiotic origin of the two double-membrane organelles is a common exam application.

Special Notes
  • NEB written answers expect a labelled organelle diagram plus a one-line function per organelle.
  • The endosymbiotic theory (Mitochondria/chloroplast from engulfed prokaryotes) is a favourite 4-mark question.
  • Contrast plant vs animal cells (wall, chloroplast, large vacuole vs centrioles).
Important Statements
  • The plasma membrane is the selectively permeable boundary of every cell.
  • Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration; chloroplasts of photosynthesis.
  • The Golgi apparatus modifies and packages proteins and lipids.
  • Cilia and flagella use a 9+2 microtubule arrangement for locomotion.
Important Notes
  • Memorise organelle → function pairs (mitochondrion→ATP, ER→synthesis, Golgi→packaging, lysosome→digestion).
  • Draw a plant cell with all labels in one pass.
  • Learn the 9+2 axonemal structure of cilia and flagella.
  • Note plant-specific organelles (cell wall, chloroplast, central vacuole).
Exam Short Tricks
  • POWER: P=power (mitochondrion), O=oil & lipid (SER), W=wall (plant), E=envelope, R=respiration, power house = mitochondrion.
  • MITOCHONDRIUM = MITOchondria make ENERGY (powerhouse).
  • Golgi = Golgi packages & SECREtes.
  • Plant = Wall + Chloroplast + big Vacuole; Animal = Centrioles + Lysosomes + small vacuoles.
Exam Notes
  • Organelle label-and-function questions are very common.
  • Draw the fluid-mosaic model with a labelled membrane.
  • Explain endosymbiotic theory with evidence (own DNA, 70S ribosomes, double membrane).
  • Compare plant and animal cells in a two-column table.
