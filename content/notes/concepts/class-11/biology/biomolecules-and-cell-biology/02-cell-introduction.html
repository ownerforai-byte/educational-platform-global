Enriched Content

Notes
  • Cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow): all organisms are made of one or more cells; the cell is the basic structural and functional unit; every cell arises from a pre-existing cell.
  • Cell = the smallest unit of life; bounded by the plasma membrane; size typically 1-100 µm.
  • Prokaryotic cell: no membrane-bound nucleus or organelles; 70S ribosomes; e.g. bacteria, cyanobacteria; smaller (1-10 µm).
  • Eukaryotic cell: true nucleus with a nuclear envelope, membrane-bound organelles, 80S ribosomes; e.g. plant and animal cells.
  • Key differences: nucleus, organelles, chromatin vs naked DNA, cell size, ribosome size, cytoskeleton.
  • Basic cell components: plasma membrane, cytoplasm (cytosol + organelles), genetic material, and (in prokaryotes) cell wall, capsule, pili, flagella.
Confusion
  • A virus is not a cell and is not prokaryotic or eukaryotic; it is an acellular, obligate intracellular parasite.
  • Prokaryotes lack a nuclear membrane but still carry DNA (usually a single circular chromosome).
  • Mycoplasma has no cell wall and is among the smallest known free-living cells (~0.2–0.4 µm).
  • A eukaryotic nucleus is a true membrane-bound organelle; the nucleoid of a bacterium is not.
  • Cell walls are found in plants, fungi and most bacteria; they are ABSENT in animal cells.
Practice
  • Tabulate 6 differences between a prokaryotic and a eukaryotic cell.
  • Label a simple bacterium: capsule, cell wall, plasma membrane, cytoplasm, nucleoid, ribosomes, pili, flagellum.
  • Explain why the cell is the smallest unit of life.
  • State the three postulates of cell theory and their originators.
  • Distinguish the nucleoid from a true nucleus.
Universal Facts
  • The largest known cell is the unfertilized ostrich ovum; mycoplasmas are among the smallest known free-living cells (~0.2–0.4 µm).
  • Human ova (~0.12 mm) are visible to the naked eye; the sperm head is ~5 µm long.
  • A human body contains roughly 3.7×10^13 cells (published estimate; counts vary with body size).
Examples
  • E. coli is a classic prokaryotic model organism.
  • The onion root-tip cell is the standard school example of a plant eukaryotic cell.
  • Amoeba is a single eukaryotic cell that carries out all life functions.
Practice Questions
  • Why are prokaryotes called "lower" organisms?
  • State two features present only in eukaryotes.
  • If a specimen has a cell wall, chloroplasts and a nucleus, is it plant, animal or fungal? Justify.
  • Explain the significance of the plasma membrane being selectively permeable.
  • Compare the 70S and 80S ribosomes.
Formulas
  • General size: prokaryote ~ 1-10 µm; eukaryote ~ 10-100 µm
  • Magnification = image size ÷ real size
Key Points
  • Cell = smallest structural + functional unit of life.
  • Prokaryote: no nucleus, 70S ribosomes, circular DNA, cell wall usually peptidoglycan.
  • Eukaryote: membrane-bound nucleus, 80S ribosomes, linear chromosomes, organelles.
  • Three postulates of cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow).
  • Cell wall: plants (cellulose), fungi (chitin), bacteria (peptidoglycan); absent in animals.
  • Viruses are acellular; they are NOT classified as cells.
Summary

The cell is the basic structural and functional unit of life. Cell theory (Schleiden, Schwann, Virchow) unifies all organisms. Cells are grouped into prokaryotes (no true nucleus; bacteria, cyanobacteria) and eukaryotes (true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles; plants, animals). Mastering their structural differences — nucleus, ribosome type, cell wall, organelles — is a core NEB examination point.

Special Notes
  • NEB often asks a two-column prokaryote/eukaryote table; always include ribosome size (70S vs 80S).
  • Mention Mycoplasma (smallest cell) and the ostrich egg (largest) to add marks.
  • Stress that a "cell" must carry out metabolism to be considered the unit of life.
Important Statements
  • All known living organisms are cellular.
  • A prokaryote is the simplest true cell; a eukaryote is the most complex.
  • The plasma membrane is selectively permeable and controls the entry of materials.
  • Cell theory has three postulates and is the foundation of modern biology.
Important Notes
  • Memorise cell-theory postulates and the three scientists.
  • Learn the ribosome size distinction (70S prokaryotes, 80S eukaryotes).
  • Draw and label a bacterium and an animal/plant cell.
  • Keep a two-column prokaryote vs eukaryote table for quick revision.
Exam Short Tricks
  • PRO = small, NO nucleus; EU = nucleus + organelles.
  • Ribosome 7-0 (seventy) = prokaryote; 8-0 = eukaryote.
  • Cell wall: Plant (cellulose), Fungi (chitin), Bacteria (peptidoglycan), Animal (none).
  • Largest cell = ostrich egg; smallest living cell = Mycoplasma.
Exam Notes
  • Prokaryote/eukaryote comparison table is a very common short-answer.
  • State cell theory fully (3 postulates + names).
  • Describe the structure of a typical bacterium with a labelled diagram.
  • Explain why viruses are not considered living cells.
