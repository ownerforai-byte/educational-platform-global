Sequence and series: arithmetic, geometric, harmonic sequences

Topic SLUG

Relevance

100

Notes

  • 1. Sequence Definition

  • An ordered list of numbers following a pattern: a1, a2, a3, ..., an, ...

  • 2. Arithmetic Progression (AP)

    • Common difference: d = an - a(n-1)
    • nth term: an = a + (n-1)d
    • Sum of n terms: Sn = n/2[2a + (n-1)d] = n/2(a + l)
    • Arithmetic Mean: A = (a+b)/2

  • 3. Geometric Progression (GP)

    • Common ratio: r = an/a(n-1)
    • nth term: an = ar^(n-1)
    • Sum of n terms: Sn = a(r^n - 1)/(r-1) for r != 1
    • Infinite GP (|r| < 1): S_infinity = a/(1-r)
    • Geometric Mean: G = sqrt(ab)

  • 4. Harmonic Progression (HP)

  • Reciprocals of an AP: 1/a, 1/(a+d), 1/(a+2d), ...
    • Harmonic Mean: H = 2ab/(a+b)
    • Relation: A x H = G^2 (AM x HM = GM^2)

  • 5. Important Relations

    • For AP: an - a(n-1) = d (constant)
    • For GP: an/a(n-1) = r (constant)
    • AM >= GM >= HM for positive numbers

  • 6. Entrance Exam Focus

  • MCQs: Find nth term, sum, GM/HM; Word problems on AP/GP; relation between AM, GM, HM.

Confusion

  • x HP has a direct sum formula. x Take reciprocals, use AP sum.
  • x r can be any value in infinite GP. x Must have |r| < 1 for convergence.
  • x AM = GM always. x AM >= GM, equality only when all terms equal.

Practice

  • Find the 20th term and sum of first 20 terms of AP: 3, 7, 11, ...
  • Find the GP: 2, 6, 18, ... and its 8th term.
  • If AM = 13 and GM = 12, find the two numbers.
  • Sum of infinite GP: 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + ...

Formulas

  • an = a + (n-1)d (AP)
  • Sn = n/2[2a + (n-1)d] (AP)
  • an = ar^(n-1) (GP)
  • Sn = a(r^n - 1)/(r-1) (GP)
  • S_infinity = a/(1-r), |r| < 1 (GP)
  • G = sqrt(ab), H = 2ab/(a+b)

Summary

A sequence is an ordered list a1, a2, a3, ... following a pattern, and a series is the sum of its terms. In an arithmetic progression the difference d = a_n - a_(n-1) is constant, giving a_n = a + (n-1)d and S_n = n/2[2a + (n-1)d] = n/2(a + l); in a geometric progression the ratio r = a_n/a_(n-1) is constant, giving a_n = a r^(n-1) and S_n = a(r^n - 1)/(r - 1), which reduces to a/(1 - r) only when |r| < 1. A harmonic progression is the reciprocal of an AP, and for two positive numbers the means satisfy A >= G >= H with A x H = G^2.

Important Concepts

  • Arithmetic progression: the successive differences a_n - a_(n-1) = d are constant, so terms rise by equal jumps.
  • Geometric progression: the successive ratios a_n/a_(n-1) = r are constant, so each term is a fixed multiple of the previous one.
  • Harmonic progression: the reciprocals 1/a, 1/(a+d), 1/(a+2d) form an arithmetic progression, so it has no direct sum formula.
  • Means of two positive numbers a and b: A = (a+b)/2, G = sqrt(ab) and H = 2ab/(a+b), with A >= G >= H.
  • An infinite GP converges to a/(1 - r) only when |r| < 1; for r = 1 the terms stay constant and for r > 1 they grow.
  • The last term l of an AP is l = a + (n-1)d, which lets S_n be written in the short form n/2(a + l).

Important Statements

  • The nth term of an AP with first term a and common difference d is a_n = a + (n-1)d, and the sum of n terms is S_n = n/2[2a + (n-1)d] = n/2(a + l).
  • The nth term of a GP with first term a and common ratio r is a_n = a r^(n-1), and for r != 1 the sum of n terms is S_n = a(r^n - 1)/(r - 1).
  • An infinite geometric series 1 + r + r^2 + ... sums to S_infinity = a/(1 - r) if and only if |r| < 1.
  • For two positive numbers the geometric mean is G = sqrt(ab) and the harmonic mean is H = 2ab/(a+b), and these satisfy A x H = G^2.
  • For positive numbers AM >= GM >= HM, with equality in any of the three comparisons only when a = b.

Important Tasks

  • Find the nth term and the sum of the first n terms of a given AP, having first identified a and d, or the last term l, from the listed terms.
  • Find the nth term and the sum to n terms of a GP, having first identified the common ratio by dividing any term by the one before it.
  • Sum an infinite geometric series, checking |r| < 1 before quoting the formula a/(1 - r).
  • Recover two numbers from their AM, GM or HM by setting x + y = 2A and xy = G^2 = A x H and solving the resulting quadratic.

Key Points

  • A difference signals an AP and a ratio signals a GP, so subtract a(n-1) to get d and divide by a(n-1) to get r.
  • S_n = n/2(a + l) is often the quickest AP sum, because l is usually given directly in the question.
  • For a GP with r < 1, write S_n as a(1 - r^n)/(1 - r) so that the bracket stays positive.
  • Never use S_infinity = a/(1 - r) without first confirming that |r| < 1.
  • AM, GM and HM are means of two numbers; they are not obtained by averaging the terms of a progression.

Special Notes

  • A harmonic progression has no sum formula of its own: take reciprocals, sum the resulting AP, and the sum of the HP is the sum of the reciprocals of those partial sums.
  • If r = 1 the GP is constant with S_n = na, and the formula carrying (r - 1) in the denominator is undefined.
  • Negative or zero r still give a valid finite sum formula, but the series diverges to infinity unless |r| < 1.
  • AM = GM = HM only when every number is equal, so a strict inequality A > G > H is the signal that the numbers are not identical.
  • Arithmetic mean is always defined, but GM needs non-negative numbers and HM needs non-zero numbers, since the means are taken over reciprocals.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Read the first three terms: a constant second difference means AP, while a constant ratio means GP.
  • For the sum of n terms of an AP use S_n = n/2(a + l) when the last term is given, and n/2[2a + (n-1)d] otherwise.
  • To find two numbers from their AM and GM, set x + y = 2A and xy = G^2, then solve t^2 - 2At + G^2 = 0.
  • An infinite series with |r| >= 1 has no finite sum, so if the options are all rational numbers |r| < 1 is the intended case.

Exam Notes

  • MCQs ask for a single missing term, the common difference or ratio, or the nature of a given series, all in about 1 mark.
  • 5-mark questions typically ask for the nth term and S_n of an AP or a GP, and 10-mark questions add an infinite sum or a word problem.
  • Word problems on salary, instalments or interest are AP and GP wordings, so convert them to a_n = a + (n-1)d or a_n = a r^(n-1) before any algebra.
  • The chain A >= G >= H is a frequent 1-mark MCQ, and recovering the two numbers from A and H is a standard 5-mark question.

Practice Questions

  • Find the 20th term and the sum of the first 20 terms of the AP 3, 7, 11, ...
  • Find the common ratio and the 8th term of the GP 2, 6, 18, ..., and state the formula for S_n.
  • If the AM of two positive numbers is 13 and their GM is 12, find the two numbers.
  • Find the sum to infinity of 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + ... and state the condition on the common ratio.
  • The first term of a GP is 5 and the sum to infinity is 10; find the common ratio and hence the third term.

Mcs

1.

The 20th term of the AP 3, 7, 11, ... is

Options
  • 83
  • 79
  • 80
  • 77
Answer

B

Explanation

Here a = 3 and d = 4, so a_20 = a + (20 - 1)d = 3 + 19 x 4 = 79. Reaching the 20th term needs 19 jumps, not 20.

2.

The sum to infinity of the series 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + ... is

Options
  • 3/2
  • 1/2
  • 2/3
  • 4/3
Answer

A

Explanation

Here a = 1 and r = 1/3 with |r| < 1, so the sum is a/(1 - r) = 1/(1 - 1/3) = 1/(2/3) = 3/2.

3.

The 8th term of the GP 2, 6, 18, ... is

Options
  • 4374
  • 2187
  • 1458
  • 8748
Answer

A

Explanation

The common ratio is r = 6/2 = 3, so a_8 = a r^7 = 2 x 3^7 = 2 x 2187 = 4374. The exponent is 7 because a r^0 is the first term.

4.

An infinite geometric series with common ratio r converges to a finite sum only when

Options
  • r > 1
  • r = 1
  • |r| < 1
  • r < -1
Answer

C

Explanation

The terms must tend to zero, which requires |r| < 1. For r = 1 the sum is na and diverges, and for |r| > 1 the terms grow without bound.

5.

The AM and GM of two positive numbers are 13 and 12 respectively. The numbers are

Options
  • 8 and 18
  • 9 and 17
  • 12 and 14
  • 6 and 20
Answer

A

Explanation

AM = 13 gives x + y = 26 and GM = 12 gives xy = 144, so t^2 - 26t + 144 = 0 = (t - 8)(t - 18). Hence the numbers are 8 and 18.
