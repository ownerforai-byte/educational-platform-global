Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Progress With a Pattern

    A sequence is an ordered list with a rule; a series is its sum. The theorems: the AP nth-term and sum formulas, the GP nth-term, sum and sum to infinity, the AM–GM–HM ordering, and the special sums Σn, Σn², Σn³.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Arithmetic progression (AP)

    • Constant difference d: aₙ = a + (n−1)d; sum Sₙ = n/2[2a + (n−1)d] = n/2(a + l) (l = last term).
    • Arithmetic mean: the middle term of any three-term AP block: b = (a + c)/2.

    2.2 Geometric progression (GP)

    • Constant ratio r: aₙ = arⁿ⁻¹; sum Sₙ = a(rⁿ − 1)/(r − 1) (r ≠ 1).
    • Sum to infinity (theorem): converges iff |r| < 1, and then S∞ = a/(1 − r). Proof idea: Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) and rⁿ → 0 exactly when |r| < 1.
    • Geometric mean: b² = ac for three-term GP blocks (positive terms).

    2.3 Harmonic progression and the means

    • HP: reciprocals form an AP; nth term = 1/(a + (n−1)d)′... i.e. 1/(Aₙ of the reciprocal AP).
    • AM–GM–HM inequality (theorem): for positive a, b: AM ≥ GM ≥ HM, with equality iff a = b; AM·HM = GM².
    • AM = (a+b)/2, GM = √(ab), HM = 2ab/(a+b).

    2.4 Special sums (theorems)

    • Σi = n(n+1)/2; Σi² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6; Σi³ = [n(n+1)/2]² — the cube sum is the square of the triangular number.
    • Σ(arᵏ) for k = 0…n−1 is the GP sum; arithmetico-geometric series use term-by-term subtraction (the "multiply by r and shift" trick).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why the AP sum formula works

    • Write S forwards and backwards and add: each column sums to a + l, and there are n columns — Gauss's pairing argument gives Sₙ = n(a + l)/2.

    4.2 Why |r| < 1 is the convergence cliff

    • Partial sums Sₙ = a(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r): the fate of the series hangs on rⁿ. It dies to 0 iff |r| < 1; otherwise it oscillates or explodes — no middle ground.

    4.3 Reading the means geometrically

    • For a right triangle's altitude to the hypotenuse: GM appears as the altitude, AM as the half-hypotenuse — the inequality AM ≥ GM becomes a length comparison.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    S∞ needs |r| < 1: a/(1 − r) for r = 2 gives a negative "sum" — nonsense; the formula is licensed only inside the convergence cliff.

  • n vs n−1 in the nth term: aₙ = a + (n−1)d — the first term uses 0 steps.
  • GM of negatives: √(ab) with a, b < 0 is defined via |ab| but the AM–GM theorem needs positive terms.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): The sum of the first 10 terms of an AP is 175 and the first term is 5. Find the common difference and the 10th term. — S₁₀ = 10/2[2(5) + 9d] = 5(10 + 9d) = 175 ⟹ 10 + 9d = 35 ⟹ d = 25/9. Then a₁₀ = 5 + 9(25/9) = 5 + 25 = 30. (Check: S₁₀ = 10/2(5 + 30) = 5 × 35 = 175 ✓.)

  • NEB-style (3m): Find the sum to infinity of the GP 8 − 4 + 2 − 1 + ⋯ — Ratio r = −4/8 = −1/2, and |r| = ½ < 1 ⟹ convergent. S∞ = a/(1 − r) = 8/(1 − (−½)) = 8/(3/2) = 16/3 ≈ 5.33. Partial-sum check: S₄ = 8 − 4 + 2 − 1 = 5, already close to 5.33 ✓.
  • NEB-style (2m): Insert 3 arithmetic means between 4 and 16. — The block 4, A₁, A₂, A₃, 16 is an AP with a = 4, l = 16, n = 5 terms: 16 = 4 + 4d ⟹ d = 3. Means: 7, 10, 13. (Geometric means between them would instead solve 16 = 4r⁴ ⟹ r = √2, giving 4√2, 8, 8√2.)
  • NEB-style (2m): Prove AM ≥ GM for positive a, b. — (a − b)² ≥ 0 ⟹ a² − 2ab + b² ≥ 0 ⟹ a² + 2ab + b² ≥ 4ab ⟹ (a + b)² ≥ 4ab ⟹ (a + b)/2 ≥ √(ab) (both sides positive). AM ≥ GM, equality iff a = b ∎.

Confusion

  • x HP has a direct sum formula. x Take reciprocals, use AP sum.
  • x r can be any value in infinite GP. x Must have |r| < 1 for convergence.
  • x AM = GM always. x AM >= GM, equality only when all terms equal.

Practice

  • In an AP, S₁₀ = 175 and a = 5; find d and a₁₀.
  • Find the sum to infinity of 8 − 4 + 2 − 1 + ⋯
  • Prove AM ≥ GM for positive a, b and state the equality case.

Summary

Sequences impose patterns; series sum them. AP (constant difference): aₙ = a + (n−1)d and Sₙ = n/2(a + l) — Gauss's forward–backward pairing proves it in one line. GP (constant ratio): aₙ = arⁿ⁻¹, Sₙ = a(rⁿ−1)/(r−1), and the sum to infinity S∞ = a/(1 − r) exists iff |r| < 1 — the fate of rⁿ is the whole story (8 − 4 + 2 − 1 + ⋯ with r = −½ sums to 16/3). HP hides an AP in the reciprocals. The means order: AM = (a+b)/2 ≥ GM = √(ab) ≥ HM = 2ab/(a+b), equality iff a = b, proved by expanding (a − b)² ≥ 0. Insertion problems reduce to counting terms and solving for d or r (4→16 with three arithmetic means: d = 3, giving 7, 10, 13). The special sums Σi = n(n+1)/2, Σi² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6, Σi³ = [n(n+1)/2]² — the cube sum squaring the triangular number — close the toolkit.

Important Concepts

  • Convergence hangs on r^n: |r| < 1 kills it, and only then does a/(1 - r) mean anything - the formula is licensed by the limit, not the other way round.

Important Statements

  • "S_inf = a/(1 - r) iff |r| < 1; AM >= GM >= HM with equality iff a = b; sum i^3 = [n(n+1)/2]^2."

Important Tasks

  • Find the nth term and the sum of the first n terms of a given AP, having first identified a and d, or the last term l, from the listed terms.
  • Find the nth term and the sum to n terms of a GP, having first identified the common ratio by dividing any term by the one before it.
  • Sum an infinite geometric series, checking |r| < 1 before quoting the formula a/(1 - r).
  • Recover two numbers from their AM, GM or HM by setting x + y = 2A and xy = G^2 = A x H and solving the resulting quadratic.

Key Points

  • AP: a_n = a + (n-1)d; S_n = n/2[2a + (n-1)d] = n/2(a + l); arithmetic mean b = (a + c)/2. GP: a_n = ar^(n-1); S_n = a(r^n - 1)/(r - 1); sum to infinity S_inf = a/(1 - r) iff |r| < 1; geometric mean b^2 = ac. HP: reciprocals form an AP. AM-GM-HM: AM = (a+b)/2 >= GM = sqrt(ab) >= HM = 2ab/(a+b), equality iff a = b; AM x HM = GM^2. Special sums: sum i = n(n+1)/2; sum i^2 = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6; sum i^3 = [n(n+1)/2]^2. Gauss pairing proves the AP sum; the fate of r^n decides GP convergence.

Special Notes

  • A harmonic progression has no sum formula of its own: take reciprocals, sum the resulting AP, and the sum of the HP is the sum of the reciprocals of those partial sums.
  • If r = 1 the GP is constant with S_n = na, and the formula carrying (r - 1) in the denominator is undefined.
  • Negative or zero r still give a valid finite sum formula, but the series diverges to infinity unless |r| < 1.
  • AM = GM = HM only when every number is equal, so a strict inequality A > G > H is the signal that the numbers are not identical.
  • Arithmetic mean is always defined, but GM needs non-negative numbers and HM needs non-zero numbers, since the means are taken over reciprocals.

Exam Short Tricks

  • AP sum: pair first and last. GP S_inf: check |r| < 1 first. Means insertion: count terms then solve for d or r. AM-GM: expand (a - b)^2 >= 0.

Exam Notes

  • For S_inf questions, write the convergence check |r| < 1 as a separate line - it is a mark even when the arithmetic is trivial.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): The sum of the first 10 terms of an AP is 175 and its first term is 5. Find the common difference and the 10th term. — Sum formula: S₁₀ = n/2[2a + (n−1)d] = (10/2)[2(5) + 9d] = 5(10 + 9d). Set equal: 5(10 + 9d) = 175 ⟹ 10 + 9d = 35 ⟹ d = 25/9. Tenth term: a₁₀ = a + 9d = 5 + 9(25/9) = 5 + 25 = 30. Verification via the paired formula: S₁₀ = n/2(a + l) = (10/2)(5 + 30) = 5 × 35 = 175 ✓ — the two sum formulas agree, confirming both answers.
  • NEB-style (3m): Find the sum to infinity of the geometric series 8 − 4 + 2 − 1 + ⋯ — Step 1 — ratio: r = (−4)/8 = −1/2. Step 2 — convergence check: |r| = 1/2 < 1 ✓ — the necessary licence for using S∞; without it the formula is meaningless. Step 3 — formula: S∞ = a/(1 − r) = 8/(1 − (−½)) = 8/(3/2) = 16/3 ≈ 5.33. Partial-sum sanity: S₄ = 8 − 4 + 2 − 1 = 5, S₅ = 5.5, oscillating ever closer to 5.33 — the alternating signs make the partial sums bracket the limit from both sides.
  • NEB-style (2m): Insert three arithmetic means between 4 and 16, and verify the block. — The sequence 4, A₁, A₂, A₃, 16 must be an AP with first term a = 4, fifth term 16, so 5 terms total. Solve for d: a₅ = a + 4d ⟹ 16 = 4 + 4d ⟹ d = 3. Means: A₁ = 4 + 3 = 7, A₂ = 10, A₃ = 13. Verification: the block 4, 7, 10, 13, 16 has constant difference 3 ✓ and 4 + 7 + 10 + 13 + 16 = 50 = n/2(a + l) = (5/2)(20) ✓. (The geometric counterpart would use 16 = 4r⁴ ⟹ r = 2^{1/2}, giving means 4√2, 8, 8√2 — same endpoints, multiplicative spacing.)

1.

The 20th term of the AP 3, 7, 11, ... is

Options
  • 83
  • 79
  • 80
  • 77
Answer

B

Explanation

Here a = 3 and d = 4, so a_20 = a + (20 - 1)d = 3 + 19 x 4 = 79. Reaching the 20th term needs 19 jumps, not 20.

2.

The sum to infinity of the series 1 + 1/3 + 1/9 + ... is

Options
  • 3/2
  • 1/2
  • 2/3
  • 4/3
Answer

A

Explanation

Here a = 1 and r = 1/3 with |r| < 1, so the sum is a/(1 - r) = 1/(1 - 1/3) = 1/(2/3) = 3/2.

3.

The 8th term of the GP 2, 6, 18, ... is

Options
  • 4374
  • 2187
  • 1458
  • 8748
Answer

A

Explanation

The common ratio is r = 6/2 = 3, so a_8 = a r^7 = 2 x 3^7 = 2 x 2187 = 4374. The exponent is 7 because a r^0 is the first term.

4.

An infinite geometric series with common ratio r converges to a finite sum only when

Options
  • r > 1
  • r = 1
  • |r| < 1
  • r < -1
Answer

C

Explanation

The terms must tend to zero, which requires |r| < 1. For r = 1 the sum is na and diverges, and for |r| > 1 the terms grow without bound.

5.

The AM and GM of two positive numbers are 13 and 12 respectively. The numbers are

Options
  • 8 and 18
  • 9 and 17
  • 12 and 14
  • 6 and 20
Answer

A

Explanation

AM = 13 gives x + y = 26 and GM = 12 gives xy = 144, so t^2 - 26t + 144 = 0 = (t - 8)(t - 18). Hence the numbers are 8 and 18.
