  • 1. Topic Introduction — Series With Structure

    Beyond AP/GP lie the special sums (Σi, Σi², Σi³), telescoping series, and arithmetico-geometric series. Each carries a theorem-grade derivation — induction, telescoping collapse, or the shift trick — and each is a standard NEB computation.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The special sums (theorems with proofs)

    • Σᵢ₌₁ⁿ i = n(n+1)/2 — Gauss pairing: S forwards + S backwards = n columns of (n+1).
    • Σi² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 — telescoping via (k+1)³ − k³ = 3k² + 3k + 1 summed over k.
    • Σi³ = [n(n+1)/2]² — same telescoping with (k² − (k−1)²)² or induction; the cube sum is the square of the triangular sum.
    • Σk³ telescoping proof: (k+1)³ − k³ = 3k² + 3k + 1. Summing k = 1…n: (n+1)³ − 1 = 3Σk² + 3Σk + n — this derives Σk²; the companion identity (k² − (k−1)²)² = ... gives Σk³.

    2.2 Telescoping series

    • Definition: a series whose partial sums collapse: Σ(bₖ − bₖ₊₁) = b₁ − bₙ₊₁ → b₁ − lim bₙ₊₁.
    • Theorem: a telescoping series converges iff the tail sequence bₙ converges; the sum is b₁ − lim bₙ.
    • Method: partial fractions often manufacture the telescope (e.g. 1/(k(k+1)) = 1/k − 1/(k+1), sum = 1 − 1/(n+1) → 1).

    2.3 Arithmetico-geometric series

    • Terms like (a + (n−1)d)rⁿ⁻¹: multiply the partial sum by r, subtract (the shift trick), and the AP part collapses to a GP — S − rS isolates the sum.
    • Σ n xⁿ⁻¹ = 1/(1−x)² for |x| < 1 — the derivative of the GP sum, the calculus twin of the shift trick.

    2.4 Properties of summation

    • Linearity: Σ(caₖ + dbₖ) = cΣaₖ + dΣbₖ; index shifts change nothing if applied consistently — the working grammar of all series manipulation.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Telescoping as cancellation engineering

    • Each term cancels its neighbour's counterpart, leaving only the first and last — the series "telescopes" like a collapsing spyglass. Partial fractions are the standard factory for telescopes.

    4.2 The shift trick's algebra

    • For S = Σₖ₌₁ⁿ k·2ᵏ⁻¹: 2S = Σ k·2ᵏ. Subtracting aligned indices turns the coefficient k into a constant 1 inside a pure GP — the arithmetic part is annihilated.

    4.3 Induction as the auditor

    • Every closed form here can be verified by induction: check n = 1, assume n, prove n+1. Derivation shows why; induction confirms that.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Telescoping needs the limit: the sum is b₁ − lim bₙ₊₁, not b₁ − bₙ₊₁ — forgetting the limit leaves a variable in the "answer".

  • Shift-trick alignment: subtract S from rS with indices aligned (rS = Σₖ₌₂ⁿ⁺¹ (k−1)2ᵏ⁻¹) — misalignment produces nonsense.
  • Σi³ vs (Σi)²: they are equal — that is a theorem, not a definition; students who "simplify" it away lose the identity.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Find the sum of 1·2 + 2·3 + 3·4 + ⋯ + n(n+1). — k(k+1) = k² + k, so S = Σk² + Σk = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 + n(n+1)/2 = n(n+1)[(2n+1) + 3]/6 = n(n+1)(n+2)/3. Check n = 3: 2 + 6 + 12 = 20 and 3·4·5/3 = 20 ✓.

  • NEB-style (3m): Sum to infinity: 1/(1·2) + 1/(2·3) + 1/(3·4) + ⋯ — Partial fractions: 1/(k(k+1)) = 1/k − 1/(k+1). Telescoping partial sum: Sₙ = (1 − ½) + (½ − ⅓) + ⋯ + (1/n − 1/(n+1)) = 1 − 1/(n+1) — every middle term cancels. Limit: S = lim Sₙ = 1 − 0 = 1. The tail 1/(n+1) → 0 is the convergence licence.
  • NEB-style (3m): Find the sum Sₙ = 1 + 2x + 3x² + ⋯ + nxⁿ⁻¹ for x ≠ 1. — Shift trick: xS = x + 2x² + ⋯ + (n−1)xⁿ⁻¹ + nxⁿ. Subtract: S − xS = 1 + x + x² + ⋯ + xⁿ⁻¹ − nxⁿ = (1 − xⁿ)/(1 − x) − nxⁿ. Hence S = (1 − xⁿ)/(1 − x)² − nxⁿ/(1 − x). For |x| < 1 the limit is 1/(1−x)² — the closed form quoted in the theorem.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Find the sum of the series 1·2 + 2·3 + 3·4 + ⋯ + n(n+1). — Split by linearity: k(k+1) = k² + k, so Sₙ = Σk² + Σk. Apply the special sums: Σk² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 and Σk = n(n+1)/2. Combine: Sₙ = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 + n(n+1)/2 = n(n+1)[(2n+1) + 3]/6 = n(n+1)(n+2)/3. Verification (n = 3): direct sum 1·2 + 2·3 + 3·4 = 2 + 6 + 12 = 20; formula 3·4·5/3 = 20 ✓. (Bonus structure: n(n+1)(n+2)/3 = 2·C(n+2, 3) — the sum counts twice the triples chosen from n+2 objects.)
  • NEB-style (3m): Find the sum to infinity of the series 1/(1·2) + 1/(2·3) + 1/(3·4) + ⋯ — Step 1 — partial fractions: 1/(k(k+1)) = 1/k − 1/(k+1) (check: (k+1) − k = 1 over k(k+1) ✓). Step 2 — telescoping partial sum: Sₙ = (1 − ½) + (½ − ⅓) + (⅓ − ¼) + ⋯ + (1/n − 1/(n+1)). Every interior term appears once positive and once negative — all cancel, leaving Sₙ = 1 − 1/(n+1). Step 3 — take the limit: S = limₙ→∞ Sₙ = 1 − 0 = 1. The convergence licence is exactly the vanishing tail 1/(n+1) → 0; without that limit the collapse is incomplete and the "sum" would still contain n.
  • NEB-style (3m): Sum the series S = 1 + 2x + 3x² + ⋯ + nxⁿ⁻¹ (x ≠ 1) and find its limit for |x| < 1. — Shift trick: form xS = x + 2x² + ⋯ + (n−1)xⁿ⁻¹ + nxⁿ. Subtract aligned: S − xS = 1 + (2−1)x + (3−2)x² + ⋯ + (n−(n−1))xⁿ⁻¹ − nxⁿ = 1 + x + x² + ⋯ + xⁿ⁻¹ − nxⁿ. The coefficient arithmetic collapsed into a pure GP: (1 − x)S = (1 − xⁿ)/(1 − x) − nxⁿ, hence S = (1 − xⁿ)/(1 − x)² − nxⁿ/(1 − x). Limit for |x| < 1: xⁿ → 0 and nxⁿ → 0 (exponential beats linear), so S → 1/(1 − x)² — the closed form; e.g. 1 + 2(½) + 3(¼) + ⋯ = 4. The shift trick is the algebraic twin of differentiating the GP sum 1/(1−x).

Summary

Structured series yield to three tools. The special sums: Σi = n(n+1)/2 by Gauss pairing, Σi² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 and Σi³ = [n(n+1)/2]² by telescoping (k+1)³ − k³ = 3k² + 3k + 1 — the cube sum literally squares the triangular sum. Telescoping series collapse to their endpoints: partial fractions manufacture them (1/(k(k+1)) = 1/k − 1/(k+1)) and the sum to infinity is b₁ − lim bₙ — for 1/(1·2) + 1/(2·3) + ⋯ exactly 1. Arithmetico-geometric series (n·xⁿ⁻¹ forms) surrender to the shift trick: multiply by x, subtract aligned, watch the arithmetic part collapse to a GP — S = (1 − xⁿ)/(1−x)² − nxⁿ/(1−x), tending to 1/(1−x)² as |x| < 1. Split-and-shift-and-cancel: the three verbs of every computation here.
