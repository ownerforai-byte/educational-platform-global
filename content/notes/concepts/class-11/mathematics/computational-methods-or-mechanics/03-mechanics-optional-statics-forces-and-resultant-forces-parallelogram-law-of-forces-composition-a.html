Practice Questions

  • Q1. NEB-style (5m): Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles on a particle. Find the resultant's magnitude and direction. Solution. Magnitude: R = √(32 + 42) = √25 = 5 N (perpendicular case of the parallelogram law: cos θ = 0). Direction: tan α = 4/3 ⟹ α = 36.87° from the 3 N force toward the 4 N force. Deep point: the 3-4-5 right triangle is the parallelogram law's cleanest case - when θ = 90° the general formula collapses to Pythagoras, and the direction is a single tangent away.
  • Q2. NEB-style (6m): State the parallelogram law of forces and use it to find the resultant of two equal forces of 5 N with 60° between them. Solution. Law: if two forces acting at a point are represented in magnitude and direction by the adjacent sides of a parallelogram, the resultant is represented by the diagonal through that point; R = √(P2 + Q2 + 2PQ cos θ). Compute: R = √(25 + 25 + 2·25·cos 60°) = √(50 + 25) = √75 = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 N, along the bisector (equal forces ⟹ symmetric diagonal). Deep point: equal forces make the parallelogram a rhombus whose diagonal bisects the angle - the resultant's direction is read from symmetry before any tangent formula.
  • Q3. NEB-style (6m): Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant of forces 7 N and 5 N acting at 120° to each other. Solution. Magnitude: R = √(49 + 25 + 2·35·cos 120°) = √(74 - 35) = √39 ≈ 6.245 N. Direction: tan α = (Q sin θ)/(P + Q cos θ) = (5·(√3/2))/(7 + 5·(-1/2)) = 4.330/4.5 = 0.9623 ⟹ α ≈ 43.9° from the 7 N force. Check: the resultant must lie inside the angle between the forces and nearer the larger force - 43.9° < 60° ✓. Deep point: the negative cos θ (obtuse angle) subtracts from P2 + Q2 - large-angle pairs give small resultants; the direction check (nearer the bigger force) catches sign slips in the tangent formula.
  • Q4. NEB-style (5m): Resolve a force of 10 N acting at 30° to the horizontal into rectangular components. Solution. Horizontal: F cos 30° = 10·(√3/2) = 5√3 ≈ 8.66 N. Vertical: F sin 30° = 10·(1/2) = 5 N. Check: √(75 + 25) = √100 = 10 ✓ - the components recombine to the original. Deep point: resolution and composition are inverse operations through the same right triangle - cos picks the adjacent projection, sin the opposite; the check √(x2 + y2) = F is free and always worth the line.
  • Q5. NEB-style (6m): A particle is in equilibrium under three forces: 6 N and 8 N at right angles, and a third force R. Find R and verify with Lami's theorem. Solution. Resultant of the pair: √(36 + 64) = 10 N; equilibrium requires the third force to balance it ⟹ R = 10 N opposite the pair's resultant. Lami's theorem (three concurrent forces in equilibrium): each force is proportional to the sine of the angle between the other two. The 6 N and 8 N forces are 90° apart; R is opposite the pair's resultant, i.e. at angle (180° - φ) from each, where tan φ = 8/6 ⟹ φ = 53.13°. Check: 6/sin(angle between 8 and R) = 8/sin(angle between 6 and R) = 10/sin 90° = 10. Angle between 8 N and R: 180° - 36.87° = 143.13°, sin = 0.6 ⟹ 10 × 0.6 = 6 ✓; angle between 6 N and R: 180° - 53.13° = 126.87°, sin = 0.8 ⟹ 10 × 0.8 = 8 ✓. Deep point: Lami's theorem is the triangle law with the sine rule inside - equilibrium means the three force vectors close a triangle, and the triangle's sine rule is the theorem.
  • Q6. NEB-style (6m): Two forces of 5 N and 3 N act at a point. Between what limits can their resultant lie? Find the angle between them if the resultant is 4 N. Solution. Limits: maximum when θ = 0°: R = 5 + 3 = 8 N; minimum when θ = 180°: R = 5 - 3 = 2 N ⟹ resultant lies in [2, 8] N. For R = 4: 16 = 25 + 9 + 2·15·cos θ ⟹ 30 cos θ = -18 ⟹ cos θ = -0.6 ⟹ θ ≈ 126.87°. Check: the obtuse angle shrinks the resultant below √(25+9) = √34 ≈ 5.83 toward the minimum - 4 sits plausibly in range ✓. Deep point: every intermediate magnitude is attainable exactly once as θ sweeps 0° to 180° - cos θ is strictly decreasing, so the resultant traces [2, 8] monotonically; the question is the parallelogram law read backwards.
  • Q7. NEB-style (6m): Derive the direction formula tan α = (Q sin θ)/(P + Q cos θ) for the resultant of P and Q, and evaluate it for P = 4 N, Q = 3 N at 90°. Solution. Resolve Q along and perpendicular to P: components Q cos θ (along P) and Q sin θ (perpendicular). The resultant's perpendicular component is Q sin θ; its component along P is P + Q cos θ. Hence tan α = (Q sin θ)/(P + Q cos θ) where α is the angle between R and P. Evaluation: θ = 90° ⟹ tan α = (3·1)/(4 + 0) = 3/4 ⟹ α = 36.87°, and R = √(16 + 9) = 5 N. Deep point: the formula is two resolutions and one tangent - the same component bookkeeping that powers every statics problem; deriving it once from components is worth more than memorising it, because the derivation survives every variant the exam can invent.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Resultant of two forces: R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cos θ); direction tan α = Q sin θ/(P + Q cos θ) — two formulas cover every statics numerical.
  • Trick 2: Equilibrium of three forces → Lami's theorem: P/sin α = Q/sin β = R/sin γ — use when three concurrent forces balance.
  • Trick 3: Resolution trick: choose axes along and perpendicular to the likely resultant; ΣFx and ΣFy then give R and θ directly.
  • Trick 4: Minimum force to move... on a smooth plane: resolve weight along the plane mg sin θ — the standard free-body setup earns the method mark.
