Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Continuity You Can See

    Intuitive continuity: a function is continuous where its graph can be drawn without lifting the pen. This topic formalises the picture into the three-part test and surveys how it fails — the map of discontinuities.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The pen test

    • Continuous at a ⟺ small changes in x produce small changes in f(x) — no jumps, holes or tears at a. Globally continuous on an interval ⟹ drawable in one stroke there.

    2.2 The three-part test (the theorem)

    • f is continuous at a ⟺ (1) f(a) exists, (2) lim_{x→a} f(x) exists, (3) f(a) = lim_{x→a} f(x).
    • Each condition can fail independently — and each failure has a name (rows 36–38): missing value, missing limit, mismatched value.

    2.3 Continuous families

    • Theorems: polynomials are continuous on ℝ; rational functions are continuous wherever the denominator ≠ 0; roots on their domains; sin, cos everywhere; sums, products, quotients (non-zero denominator) and compositions of continuous functions are continuous.
    • Composition theorem: g continuous at a and f continuous at g(a) ⟹ f∘g continuous at a — the chain rule's continuity shadow.

    2.4 Continuity on intervals

    • Continuous on [a, b] means continuous on (a, b) plus one-sided continuity at the endpoints — the hypothesis of the Extreme Value and Intermediate Value theorems that calculus will invoke.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Small change, small response

    • Continuity is a stability contract: inputs perturbed slightly yield outputs perturbed slightly. Jumps violate it; holes violate it mildly (fixable); blow-ups violate it violently.

    4.2 Building continuity from parts

    • The closure theorems mean you rarely test continuity from scratch: build expressions from continuous families and quote the closure rules — the continuity analogue of "well-formed formulas stay well-formed".

    4.3 Why endpoints get one-sided treatment

    • At a domain edge only one neighbourhood exists; demanding two-sided agreement there would disqualify every endpoint. One-sided continuity is the honest localisation.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Existence of f(a) alone is not continuity: the limit must also exist and match — three boxes, all needed.

  • Continuous ≠ differentiable: |x| is continuous everywhere but not differentiable at 0 — continuity is the weaker contract.
  • Composition preserves continuity, not necessarily differentiability at kinks — the same distinction one level up.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m): Show that f(x) = x² sin(1/x) (x ≠ 0), f(0) = 0 is continuous at 0. — Limit: |x² sin(1/x)| ≤ x²·1 = x² → 0 (squeeze: sin(1/x) is trapped in [−1, 1]), so lim_{x→0} f(x) = 0. Value: f(0) = 0. Match: 0 = 0 ⟹ continuous at 0 — the composition/closure theorems fail here (sin(1/x) is discontinuous at 0), but the x² damping saves the day: continuity can be earned by squeeze, not only by closure.

  • NEB-style (2m): State where f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x + 1) is continuous, and classify any break. — Rational function: continuous wherever x + 1 ≠ 0, i.e. on ℝ \ {−1}. At x = −1: f undefined (condition 1 fails) ⟹ discontinuous; but the limit exists — (x²−1)/(x+1) = x − 1 for x ≠ −1 gives lim = −2 ⟹ removable discontinuity (a hole at (−1, −2), repairable by defining f(−1) = −2).
  • NEB-style (2m): Prove that the composition of continuous functions is continuous at a. — Let g be continuous at a and f continuous at g(a). Take any sequence... (ε–δ sketch): given ε > 0, continuity of f at g(a) supplies δ₁ with |f(u) − f(g(a))| < ε whenever |u − g(a)| < δ₁; continuity of g at a supplies δ with |g(x) − g(a)| < δ₁ whenever |x − a| < δ. Chaining: |x − a| < δ ⟹ |f(g(x)) − f(g(a))| < ε. Hence f∘g is continuous at a ∎ — the neighbourhoods nest, which is all the proof needs.

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — The Pen Test as a Physical Model

    • Intuitive continuity models unbroken processes: temperature over an afternoon, water level while filling a tank, position along a walk. The pen never lifts because the quantity never teleports — intermediate values are physically occupied on the way between any two.
    • This physical reading powers the IVT intuition: if the temperature went from 15° to 25°, it was every temperature between at some moment — no continuous process skips a value. Discontinuities are exactly the events where the model breaks: an instant switch, a jump cut, a step.
    • The limits of the intuition: the pen test catches jumps and holes but is silent about finer structure (it cannot distinguish differentiable from non-differentiable — a corner is still drawable in one stroke). Intuition certifies continuity, not smoothness.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Continuity as Composition Insurance

    • The composition theorem — lim f(g(x)) = f(lim g(x)) when f is continuous at the inner limit — is continuity's purpose: continuous functions commute with limits. That commutation is what lets you substitute inside composed expressions.
    • Chain of custody: a limit's value passes through each continuous function unchanged. Every "evaluate by substitution" step in a composite limit is an appeal to this licence; the licence is granted precisely by continuity.
    • Where the licence expires: at f's discontinuity points the limit and the value can part ways — f(lim g) may differ from lim f(g). Locating a function's discontinuities is therefore locating exactly where substitution reasoning must stop and one-sided analysis must begin.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — Continuous Functions Preserve Connectedness and Compactness

    • A continuous image of an interval is an interval: the function cannot tear the line apart. This is the Intermediate Value Theorem's deep content — f([a,b]) contains every value between f(a) and f(b).
    • A continuous image of a closed bounded interval is closed and bounded: the Extreme Value Theorem's content — maxima and minima are actually attained, not merely approached.
    • Why these theorems matter before calculus: they guarantee solutions exist (roots, extrema) before any technique finds them — continuity is the hypothesis that converts "should exist" into "does exist," and the derivative-based machinery later only locates what continuity already promised.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (2m): Show that f(x) = x² sin(1/x) for x ≠ 0, with f(0) = 0, is continuous at x = 0. — The obstacle: sin(1/x) has no limit at 0 (oscillation), so no closure theorem applies — continuity must be earned directly. Squeeze argument: −1 ≤ sin(1/x) ≤ 1 for every x ≠ 0, so multiplying by the non-negative x²: −x² ≤ x² sin(1/x) ≤ x². Both bounds → 0 as x → 0, so by the squeeze theorem lim_{x→0} x² sin(1/x) = 0. Three-part test: (1) f(0) = 0 exists ✓; (2) the limit exists and equals 0 ✓; (3) f(0) = 0 = limit ✓ ⟹ f is continuous at 0. The moral: damping factors can tame oscillation — continuity by squeeze where composition fails.
  • NEB-style (2m): Determine where f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x + 1) is continuous and classify the discontinuity. — Closure route: f is a rational function, continuous wherever its denominator is non-zero: continuous on ℝ \ {−1}. At x = −1: condition 1 of the three-part test fails — f(−1) is undefined (0/0). Limit check: factoring, (x² − 1)/(x + 1) = x − 1 for x ≠ −1, so lim_{x→−1} f(x) = −2 — the limit exists. Classification: missing value with existing limit ⟹ removable discontinuity: a hole at the point (−1, −2); defining f(−1) = −2 would make f continuous on all of ℝ. The factor-and-cancel pattern is the signature of every removable rational break.
  • NEB-style (2m): Prove that if g is continuous at a and f is continuous at g(a), then f∘g is continuous at a. — Proof (ε–δ chaining). Let ε > 0 be given. Since f is continuous at g(a), there is δ₁ > 0 such that |u − g(a)| < δ₁ ⟹ |f(u) − f(g(a))| < ε. Since g is continuous at a, there is δ > 0 such that |x − a| < δ ⟹ |g(x) − g(a)| < δ₁. Chain the implications: for |x − a| < δ, set u = g(x); then |u − g(a)| < δ₁, so |f(g(x)) − f(g(a))| < ε. This is precisely the ε–δ statement that f∘g is continuous at a. ∎ Reading: the neighbourhoods nest — f's tolerance δ₁ becomes g's target — which is the entire mechanism; the theorem is why sin(x²), e^{sin x} and √(1 + x²) inherit continuity for free.
  • NEB-style (6m): Use the Intermediate Value Theorem to show that x³ + x − 3 = 0 has a root between 1 and 2, and explain what continuity contributes. — Setup: let f(x) = x³ + x − 3, continuous everywhere (polynomial). Evaluate the endpoints: f(1) = 1 + 1 − 3 = −1 (negative); f(2) = 8 + 2 − 3 = 7 (positive). Apply the IVT: f is continuous on [1, 2] and 0 lies between f(1) = −1 and f(2) = 7 ⟹ there exists c ∈ (1, 2) with f(c) = 0 — a root exists in (1, 2). What continuity contributes: the IVT is false without it — a discontinuous function can step over 0 between its endpoint values (e.g. a function that is −1 just below 1.5 and +7 just above, never 0). Continuity is the hypothesis that the graph, in travelling from −1 to 7, must pass through every intermediate height. Deep point: the IVT certifies existence, not location or uniqueness — pinning c tighter needs more endpoint bisections or monotonicity, but the guarantee itself cost only two evaluations and one continuity check.
  • NEB-style (5m): The function f(x) = (x² − 4)/(x − 2) for x ≠ 2, f(2) = 3. Is f continuous on [0, 4]? Justify with the three-part test at the suspect point. — Where continuity can fail: f is a rational function — continuous at every point of its domain — so the only suspect is the excluded point x = 2. Part 1: f(2) = 3 exists ✓. Part 2: lim_{x→2} f(x) — factoring gives x + 2 for x ≠ 2 ⟹ limit = 4 ✓. Part 3: f(2) = 3 ≠ 4 ✗ — the test fails. Verdict: f is continuous on [0, 4] except at x = 2; the discontinuity is removable (redefine f(2) = 4). Deep point: the triage is domain-first — rational functions are continuous wherever defined, so all scrutiny concentrates on the excluded point; the three-part test then localises the failure to the value (Part 3), which is what makes the break repairable.
  • NEB-style (5m): Explain, with a sketch-reasoned example, why the pen test alone cannot decide differentiability, using f(x) = |x| at 0. — Pen test verdict: |x| is continuous at 0 — the V-shape is drawn in one stroke, no lift, no jump; the pen test passes. Differentiability test: the one-sided slopes disagree — from the right the slope is +1, from the left −1 ⟹ f′(0) does not exist; the graph has a corner. The gap between the tests: continuity asks whether the values connect; differentiability asks whether the slopes connect. A corner connects values while breaking slopes, so it passes the pen test and fails the derivative test. Deep point: the pen test certifies the coarsest level of smoothness only; each finer level (differentiable, continuously differentiable) needs its own test, and |x| is the canonical witness that drawable-without-lifting is strictly weaker than having a tangent line.
  • NEB-style (6m): A tank's water level V(t) in litres is V(t) = 100 − 4t for 0 ≤ t ≤ 10, V(t) = 120 − 6t for 10 < t ≤ 20. Is V continuous at t = 10, and what does the answer mean physically? — LHL (t → 10⁻, first piece): 100 − 4(10) = 60. RHL (t → 10⁺, second piece): 120 − 6(10) = 60. Existence: LHL = RHL = 60 ⟹ lim_{t→10} V(t) = 60. Value: V(10) = 100 − 40 = 60 (the ≤ condition puts 10 in the first piece). Verdict: value = limit ⟹ V is continuous at t = 10. Physical meaning: the level passes through 60 L at the switch — the outflow rate changed (4 L/min → 6 L/min) but the amount did not jump; water cannot teleport, so continuity is the physically mandatory answer, while the rate change is a corner in the graph (the slope drops... steepens from −4 to −6). Deep point: continuity models conserved quantities (amount, position, temperature); rates may jump even when amounts cannot — the graph is continuous with a kink, which is exactly the pen-test-passes, derivative-fails signature.

Formulas

    • continuity at a: f(a) = lim_{x→a} f(x), both existing
    • closure: f, g continuous ⟹ f ± g, f·g, f/g (g ≠ 0), f∘g continuous
    • rational p/q continuous on {q ≠ 0}; |x|, sin, cos, eˣ continuous on ℝ

Key Points

  • Pen test: continuous where the graph draws without lifting. Three-part test: f(a) exists, lim exists, f(a) = lim - each failure independently named. Continuous families: polynomials (all R), rationals (denominator != 0), roots (domain), sin/cos (all R); sums, products, quotients, compositions of continuous functions are continuous (closure theorems). Composition theorem: g continuous at a and f at g(a) => f o g continuous at a. Continuity on [a, b]: interior two-sided + endpoint one-sided - the EVT/IVT hypothesis. Squeeze can certify continuity where closure fails (x^2 sin(1/x) at 0). Continuous does not imply differentiable (|x| at 0).

Summary

Intuitive continuity is the pen test — drawable without lifting — and its formal face is the three-part theorem: f(a) exists, lim_{x→a} f(x) exists, and they are equal. Each part can fail alone, and the failure names the discontinuity (removable hole, jump, infinite). Closure theorems make continuity cheap: polynomials, rationals (off denominator zeros), roots, sin, cos are continuous, and sums, products, quotients and compositions inherit it — f∘g continuous by neighbourhood-nesting ε–δ chaining. Where closure fails, the squeeze can still certify continuity: x² sin(1/x) with f(0) = 0 is continuous at 0 because x² damps the oscillation to 0. Continuity on [a, b] adds one-sided endpoint tests — the hypothesis the Extreme Value and Intermediate Value theorems will demand. And continuity ≠ differentiability: |x| bends without breaking. Three boxes, closure shortcuts, squeeze rescues — the whole continuity toolkit.

Justify with the three-part test at the suspect point. — Where continuity can fail: f is a rational function — continuous at every point of its domain — so the only suspect is the excluded point x = 2. Part 1: f(2) = 3 exists ✓. Part 2: lim_{x→2} f(x) — factoring gives x + 2 for x ≠ 2 ⟹ limit = 4 ✓. Part 3: f(2) = 3 ≠ 4 ✗ — the test fails. Verdict: f is continuous on [0, 4] except at x = 2; the discontinuity is removable (redefine f(2) = 4). Deep point: the triage is domain-first — rational functions are continuous wherever defined, so all scrutiny concentrates on the excluded point; the three-part test then localises the failure to the value (Part 3), which is what makes the break repairable.
  • NEB-style (5m): Explain, with a sketch-reasoned example, why the pen test alone cannot decide differentiability, using f(x) = |x| at 0. — Pen test verdict: |x| is continuous at 0 — the V-shape is drawn in one stroke, no lift, no jump; the pen test passes. Differentiability test: the one-sided slopes disagree — from the right the slope is +1, from the left −1 ⟹ f′(0) does not exist; the graph has a corner. The gap between the tests: continuity asks whether the values connect; differentiability asks whether the slopes connect. A corner connects values while breaking slopes, so it passes the pen test and fails the derivative test. Deep point: the pen test certifies the coarsest level of smoothness only; each finer level (differentiable, continuously differentiable) needs its own test, and |x| is the canonical witness that drawable-without-lifting is strictly weaker than having a tangent line.
  • NEB-style (6m): A tank's water level V(t) in litres is V(t) = 100 − 4t for 0 ≤ t ≤ 10, V(t) = 120 − 6t for 10 < t ≤ 20. Is V continuous at t = 10, and what does the answer mean physically? — LHL (t → 10⁻, first piece): 100 − 4(10) = 60. RHL (t → 10⁺, second piece): 120 − 6(10) = 60. Existence: LHL = RHL = 60 ⟹ lim_{t→10} V(t) = 60. Value: V(10) = 100 − 40 = 60 (the ≤ condition puts 10 in the first piece). Verdict: value = limit ⟹ V is continuous at t = 10. Physical meaning: the level passes through 60 L at the switch — the outflow rate changed (4 L/min → 6 L/min) but the amount did not jump; water cannot teleport, so continuity is the physically mandatory answer, while the rate change is a corner in the graph (the slope drops... steepens from −4 to −6). Deep point: continuity models conserved quantities (amount, position, temperature); rates may jump even when amounts cannot — the graph is continuous with a kink, which is exactly the pen-test-passes, derivative-fails signature.
