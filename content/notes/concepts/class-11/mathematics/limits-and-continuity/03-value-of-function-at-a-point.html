Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Substitution and Its Failure Modes

    "Value of a function at a point" is direct evaluation — and the diagnosis of when it fails. The topic classifies the outcomes: clean substitution, indeterminate 0/0, and blow-up — the three doors every limit question opens.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Direct substitution

    • Theorem (continuity route): if f is continuous at a, then lim_{x→a} f(x) = f(a) — substitution is not just a trick but a theorem-licensed method.
    • Polynomials, |x|, sin/cos, eˣ are continuous everywhere: substitution always works for them.

    2.2 The 0/0 door

    • f(a) = 0/0 ⟹ indeterminate: the limit may be any number, ±∞, or nonexistent — the form alone decides nothing. Transform: factor, rationalise, standard limits, or (later) L'Hôpital.

    2.3 The blow-up door

    • Denominator → 0 with numerator → c ≠ 0 ⟹ |f(x)| → ∞: the limit is ±∞ or does not exist (sides may disagree in sign). Example: 1/(x−2) near 2 → +∞ from the right, −∞ from the left.

    2.4 Evaluation of defined functions

    • When f(a) exists, f(a) is just a number: compute it. The subtlety is only in knowing which function value the question wants — f(a) vs the limit.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Substitution as the default

    • Always substitute first. Three outcomes: a number (done — cite continuity), 0/0 (transform), c/0 (blow-up analysis). The substitution is the diagnosis, not merely a guess.

    4.2 Why 0/0 is rich

    • Both numerator and denominator vanish together — their rates matter, which is precisely what the transformed expression measures. 0/0 is the doorway to derivatives.

    4.3 Sign analysis at blow-ups

    • Near a denominator zero, the sign of the denominator flips across a (unless the zero has even multiplicity): left and right limits may be −∞ and +∞ — then the two-sided limit does not exist even though each side is infinite.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    0/0 ≠ 1: the form is indeterminate; "anything over itself is 1" fails when both are approaching 0 rather than being 0.

  • ∞ is not a limit value: writing "= ∞" describes behaviour; the two-sided limit may still not exist if the sides disagree.
  • f(a) existing doesn't end the story: the limit question may still need side analysis if nearby behaviour is wild (e.g. piecewise joints).

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (3x² + 2x − 4). — Polynomial ⟹ continuous ⟹ substitution licensed: 3 + 2 − 4 = 1.

  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1/x). — Substitution gives 1/0 — numerator 1 ≠ 0 ⟹ blow-up door. Side analysis: x → 0⁺ ⟹ 1/x → +∞; x → 0⁻ ⟹ 1/x → −∞. Sides disagree ⟹ the two-sided limit does not exist.
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→4} (√x − 2)/(x − 4). — Substitution: (2 − 2)/0 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Rationalise: multiply by (√x + 2)/(√x + 2): numerator becomes x − 4, cancelling the denominator: 1/(√x + 2) for x ≠ 4. Limit: 1/(2 + 2) = 1/4. (The derivative of √x at 4 in disguise.)

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — Substitution Is a Theorem, Not a Habit

    • Direct substitution works at a because polynomials are continuous there: lim P(x) = P(a) is the continuity of P restated. The habit "just plug in" is really "check the function is continuous at this point, then plug in."
    • The failure modes are the syllabus map: denominator zero (→ 0/0 or c/0), even-root boundary (→ one-sided domain edge), piecewise joint (→ compare sides), log of non-positive (→ domain edge). Each failure mode has its own dedicated technique — substitution failing is diagnostic information, not a dead end.
    • The c/0 family deserves its own reading: c/0⁺ → +∞, c/0⁻ → −∞ (sign of c matters), and the two sides disagreeing means no limit — substitution's failure at c/0 is loud (blow-up), while its failure at 0/0 is quiet (needs work). Learning which failure you have is the triage step.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Value, Limit, and the Three-Way Possibility Space

    • At any point a, three quantities can each exist or not: f(a), LHL, RHL. The full pattern of which exist and which match is a classification system — it generates continuity, removable, jump, and one-sided-only cases from one framework.
    • Value and limit are causally independent: f(a) is set by the definition at the point; the limit by the neighbourhood. Changing f(a) never moves the limit; changing the neighbourhood never moves f(a). Continuity is the coincidence of two independent quantities.
    • This independence is why repair is possible: when only the value is wrong (limit exists), a redefinition fixes it — you are editing one independent quantity to match the other. When the sides disagree, no value edit can help; the break is in the neighbourhood itself.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — Evaluation Errors as Limit Errors in Disguise

    • Most algebraic limit mistakes are evaluation at the wrong object: plugging into the unsimplified form (getting 0/0 and stopping), evaluating a piecewise function with the wrong piece's condition, or substituting into a cancelled expression at the cancelled point.
    • The discipline: substitute first (to diagnose), transform only if needed, substitute again on the simplified form (to finish). The two substitutions bookend the work; skipping the first loses the diagnosis, skipping the second loses the answer.
    • Cross-check culture: verify a limit answer by a second route (graph reasoning, a nearby numeric value like f(1.99) and f(2.01), or the factoring identity) — the value of f at points near a previews the limit and catches sign slips.

Examples

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (3x² + 2x − 4). — Substitution: 3(1)² + 2(1) − 4 = 3 + 2 − 4 = 1. Justification: the function is a polynomial, continuous at every real point, so the limit theorem lim_{x→a} f(x) = f(a) applies — substitution is a licensed method, not a guess. No transformation needed; the first substitution already landed on a number, closing the question.
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1/x). — Substitution: 1/0 with numerator 1 ≠ 0 ⟹ the blow-up door — not indeterminate. Side analysis: as x → 0⁺, 1/x is large positive ⟹ +∞; as x → 0⁻, 1/x is large negative ⟹ −∞. Verdict: the one-sided limits are both infinite but disagree in sign, so the two-sided limit does not exist. (Contrast 1/x², whose sides agree at +∞ — even multiplicity in the denominator unifies the blow-up; odd behaviour like 1/x splits it.)
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→4} (√x − 2)/(x − 4). — Substitution: (2 − 2)/(4 − 4) = 0/0 — indeterminate ⟹ transform. Rationalise: multiply by the conjugate (√x + 2)/(√x + 2): numerator (√x − 2)(√x + 2) = x − 4, which cancels the denominator (licensed since x ≠ 4): the expression becomes 1/(√x + 2) for x ≠ 4. Substitute: 1/(2 + 2) = 1/4. (Recognition: this limit is exactly the derivative of √x at x = 4 — the difference quotient in root form; the conjugate trick is the standard key for every √-difference over a linear denominator.)
  • NEB-style (6m): For the piecewise function f(x) = 3x − 1 for x < 2, f(2) = 4, f(x) = x² + 1 for x > 2: evaluate f(2), LHL and RHL at 2, and determine lim_{x→2} f(x) if it exists. — Value: f(2) = 4 — the middle rule fires exactly at x = 2; the strict inequalities in the other pieces exclude it. LHL (x → 2⁻, first piece): lim (3x − 1) = 3(2) − 1 = 5. RHL (x → 2⁺, third piece): lim (x² + 1) = 4 + 1 = 5. Existence: LHL = RHL = 5 ⟹ lim_{x→2} f(x) = 5 — the limit exists even though f(2) = 4. Continuity verdict: limit 5 ≠ value 4 ⟹ discontinuous at 2, and since the limit exists the break is removable — redefining f(2) = 5 would repair it. Deep point: all three quantities are independent: the value came from the middle rule, the limit from the agreement of the side pieces, and the mismatch between them is exactly the three-part test failing at step three.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→5} (√(x) − √5)/(x − 5) by first diagnosing the substitution result, then transforming. — Diagnose: substitute x = 5: (0 − 0)/0 = 0/0 — indeterminate, so transformation is required. Transform (conjugate / difference of roots): √x − √5 = (x − 5)/(√x + √5) — multiply top and bottom by the conjugate √x + √5. Substitute the identity: the expression becomes [(x − 5)/(√x + √5)]/(x − 5) = 1/(√x + √5) for x ≠ 5. Finish: substitute again — 1/(√5 + √5) = 1/(2√5). Cross-check: the star (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ at n = ½ gives (1/2)·5^{−1/2} = 1/(2√5) ✓. Deep point: the 0/0 diagnosis predicted a shared root (x = 5 kills both √x − √5 and x − 5), and the conjugate was the tool that exposed it — diagnose with substitution, cure with the identity that matches the form.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} 1/x and lim_{x→0⁻} 1/x, and explain why lim_{x→0} 1/x does not exist. — Right side: for x → 0⁺, x is a small positive number, so 1/x is a large positive number ⟹ lim_{x→0⁺} 1/x = +∞ (unbounded above — not a real number, a blow-up). Left side: for x → 0⁻, x is small and negative, so 1/x is large and negative ⟹ lim_{x→0⁻} 1/x = −∞. Existence: the two sides go to opposite infinities ⟹ they do not agree, so lim_{x→0} 1/x does not exist. Contrast: for 1/x², both sides give +∞ (x² > 0 from both sides) — still no (finite) limit, but the graph has one unified wall instead of two opposing ones. Deep point: c/0 forms are read sign by side; the sign of c and the side of approach jointly decide +∞ or −∞, and disagreement of the sides is the existence theorem failing at infinite scale.
  • NEB-style (6m): The function f(x) = (x³ − 8)/(x − 2) for x ≠ 2, f(2) = 11. Find the limit at 2, compare with f(2), classify any discontinuity, and give the repair. — Diagnose: substitute x = 2: (8 − 8)/0 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Factor (difference of cubes): x³ − 8 = (x − 2)(x² + 2x + 4) ⟹ f(x) = x² + 2x + 4 for x ≠ 2. Limit: substitute again: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Compare: f(2) = 11 ≠ 12 ⟹ discontinuous at 2. Classify: the limit exists and the failure is only the point's value ⟹ removable discontinuity. Repair: redefine f(2) = 12 ⟹ all three continuity parts pass. Deep point: the difference-of-cubes identity is the transformation tool for cubic-over-linear 0/0 forms, exactly as difference of squares serves quadratics — each power's identity exposes the shared root, and after cancellation the answer is a plain substitution.

Key Points

  • Substitute first. Three outcomes: (1) a number - f continuous at a, lim = f(a), done; (2) 0/0 - indeterminate, transform by factoring, rationalising or standard limits; (3) c/0 with c != 0 - blow-up: |f| -> inf, and the two-sided limit DNE if the sides' signs disagree (1/x at 0: +inf right, -inf left). Polynomials, |x|, sin, cos, e^x continuous everywhere - substitution always licensed. Rationalising trick: multiply by the conjugate to expose the common factor. 0/0 richness: the rates of vanishing decide, the doorway to derivatives.

Summary

Evaluating f at a point is the first move of every limit question, and its outcome routes the rest. Clean number ⟹ f is continuous there and lim f(x) = f(a) — a theorem, valid for all polynomials, |x|, sin, cos, eˣ (3x² + 2x − 4 at 1 gives 1). 0/0 ⟹ indeterminate, demanding transformation: factor, or rationalise root-differences via the conjugate — (√x − 2)/(x − 4) becomes 1/(√x + 2), limiting to 1/4 (the derivative of √x in disguise). c/0 with c ≠ 0 ⟹ blow-up: |f| → ∞, and the two-sided limit does not exist when the sides' signs disagree (1/x: +∞ right, −∞ left; 1/x² would agree). Substitution is the diagnosis — done, transform, or side-analysis — and naming the outcome is part of the answer.

Find the limit at 2, compare with f(2), classify any discontinuity, and give the repair. — Diagnose: substitute x = 2: (8 − 8)/0 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Factor (difference of cubes): x³ − 8 = (x − 2)(x² + 2x + 4) ⟹ f(x) = x² + 2x + 4 for x ≠ 2. Limit: substitute again: 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Compare: f(2) = 11 ≠ 12 ⟹ discontinuous at 2. Classify: the limit exists and the failure is only the point's value ⟹ removable discontinuity. Repair: redefine f(2) = 12 ⟹ all three continuity parts pass. Deep point: the difference-of-cubes identity is the transformation tool for cubic-over-linear 0/0 forms, exactly as difference of squares serves quadratics — each power's identity exposes the shared root, and after cancellation the answer is a plain substitution.
