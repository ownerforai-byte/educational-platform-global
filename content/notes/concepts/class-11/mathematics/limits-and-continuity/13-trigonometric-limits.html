Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Trigonometry Meets 0/0

    Trigonometric limits deploy the sin x/x star and its relatives: sin kx/x, tan kx/x, (1 − cos x)/x², and compound-angle constructions. The craft is steering every unknown 0/0 into a standard form.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The core star and its rescalings

    • lim_{u→0} sin u/u = 1 (radians). Rescaling theorem: lim_{x→0} sin(kx)/x = k, since sin(kx)/x = k·sin(kx)/(kx) with kx → 0.
    • lim_{x→0} sin(kx)/(mx) = k/m — the general ratio form.
    • lim_{x→0} tan(kx)/x = k (tan u/u → 1 via sin/cos).

    2.2 The cosine-squared family

    • lim_{x→0} (1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2 — via 1 − cos x = 2sin²(x/2): the expression becomes 2sin²(x/2)/x² = ½·[sin(x/2)/(x/2)]² → ½·1 = ½.
    • lim_{x→0} (1 − cos kx)/x² = k²/2 — same route with the rescaling.

    2.3 Compound-angle steering

    • Expressions like sin 3x/sin 5x, (1 − cos 2x)/x sin x, sin(x + a) − sin a: rewrite with identities (sin A − sin B = 2 cos((A+B)/2) sin((A−B)/2)), split into standard ratios, and multiply by 1 (form kx/kx factors).

    2.4 The squeeze safety net

    • When steering stalls, squeeze: |sin u| ≤ |u| bounds the expression. The sandwich proof of sin x/x → 1 is the template: (cos x)·... sin x/x is trapped between cos x and 1.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why radians are non-negotiable

    • sin x/x → 1 equates arc and chord asymptotics; degrees carry π/180 in every derivative and limit. Convert first, compute second.

    4.2 The kx/kx insertion

    • The universal move: multiply and divide by the argument's coefficient. sin 5x/sin 3x = [5x·sin5x/(5x)]/[3x·sin3x/(3x)] → (5·1)/(3·1) = 5/3 — steering by bookkeeping.

    4.3 Identities as reducers

    • Compound angles and half-angles convert unfamiliar trig expressions into products of standard ratios. Keep the identity sheet beside every trig limit.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Degrees again: every trig limit in this topic assumes radians — state it. %% Half-angle factor:** (1 − cos x)/x² is ½, not 1 — the 2sin²(x/2) identity carries the ½.

  • sin kx/x vs sin x/x: the coefficient k survives as a factor — forgetting it halves marks on rescaled forms.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} sin 5x/sin 3x. — Insert the rescalings: sin 5x/sin 3x = [5x·(sin 5x/5x)]/[3x·(sin 3x/3x)] = (5/3)·[sin 5x/5x]/[sin 3x/3x]. As x → 0 both bracketed stars → 1 ⟹ limit = 5/3. (General rule: sin kx/sin mx → k/m.)

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 − cos 4x)/x². — Half-angle: 1 − cos 4x = 2sin²(2x). Expression = 2sin²(2x)/x² = 2·[sin 2x/(2x)]²·4 = 8·[sin 2x/2x]² → 8·1 = 8 — matching the formula k²/2 = 16/2 = 8 ✓.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. — Direct forms stall; use the cubic refinement x − sin x ~ x³/6 (from sin x = x − x³/6 + ⋯): the limit is 1/6. Derivation without series: x − sin x = x(1 − sin x/x), and 1 − sin x/x = [x − sin x]/x — circular; the honest elementary route applies the standard limits twice via sin x − sin y identities or accepts the known refinement — quote it as a standard result.

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — The Sine Star as a Geometry Theorem

    • sin u/u → 1 is not algebra — it is the squeeze theorem applied to geometry: for small positive u, the areas/lengths give sin u < u < tan u, and dividing by sin u pins 1 < u/sin u < 1/cos u → 1. The star is a squeeze result wearing a trig costume.
    • Why radians are structural: the inequality sin u < u compares a chord-vertical to an arc, and arcs are measured in radians. In degrees the star reads sin u°/(u°·π/180) — the π/180 factor is the residue of the wrong unit; radians are the unit where geometry and limits coincide.
    • The star's corollaries cascade: (1 − cos u)/u → 0 (Pythagorean identity), tan u/u → 1 (quotient of stars), (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2 (second-order refinement). One geometric fact powers the whole trig-limit table.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Orders of Vanishing: The Trig Hierarchy

    • Near 0, the trig functions die at different speeds: sin u ~ u (order 1), 1 − cos u ~ u²/2 (order 2), u − sin u ~ u³/6 (order 3), tan u − u ~ u³/3 (order 3). The order is the exponent of the leading term — the function's "vanishing exponent."
    • Reading races by order: in a quotient, same-order contestants leave a nonzero constant (sin 3x/tan 3x → 3x/3x = 1); a higher-order numerator dies faster ⟹ limit 0; a higher-order denominator ⟹ ±∞. The order comparison decides the limit before any computation.
    • Why the hierarchy matters for L'Hôpital: each order of vanishing is one derivative pass — sin u/u resolves in one pass, (1−cos u)/u² in two, (u − sin u)/u³ in three. The hierarchy is the map of how much work each 0/0 form will cost.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — Substitution Depth: Nesting Trig Stars

    • The stars compose: with u = g(x) → 0, sin(g(x)) ~ g(x) — so sin(x²) ~ x², sin(tan x) ~ tan x ~ x. Each nesting adds a layer but the leading-order behaviour is governed by the innermost argument's approach to 0.
    • The classic trap — sin of something not → 0: sin u/u → 1 needs u → 0. For sin(1/x)/x-type expressions the star does not apply; the squeeze theorem or damping is needed instead. Always annotate what u is and why it → 0.
    • Steering with trig identities: when the expression resists direct star-matching, identities (double-angle, Pythagorean, sum-to-product) reshape it into star form — e.g. 1 − cos 2x = 2sin²x converts a second-order problem into two first-order stars squared.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} sin 5x/sin 3x. — Steering by insertion: multiply and divide each sine by its own argument: sin 5x/sin 3x = [5x · (sin 5x)/(5x)] / [3x · (sin 3x)/(3x)] = (5/3) · [(sin 5x)/(5x)] / [(sin 3x)/(3x)]. Apply the star: as x → 0, both 5x → 0 and 3x → 0, so (sin 5x)/(5x) → 1 and (sin 3x)/(3x) → 1 (radians throughout). Result: (5/3)·(1/1) = 5/3. General rule harvested: lim_{x→0} sin(kx)/sin(mx) = k/m — the double insertion turns any sine ratio into a coefficient ratio.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 − cos 4x)/x². — Half-angle identity: 1 − cos θ = 2sin²(θ/2), so 1 − cos 4x = 2sin²(2x). Rewrite: (1 − cos 4x)/x² = 2sin²(2x)/x² = 2 · [sin 2x/(2x)]² · (2x)²/x² = 2 · [sin 2x/(2x)]² · 4 = 8·[sin 2x/(2x)]². Apply the star: sin 2x/(2x) → 1 as x → 0 ⟹ the limit is 8·1² = 8. Formula check: the general result (1 − cos kx)/x² → k²/2 gives 4²/2 = 16/2 = 8 ✓ — the half-angle identity is exactly where the ½ and the k² come from.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. — Why steering stalls: both x − sin x and x³ vanish to third order — the first-order standard limits cancel out, exposing the cubic layer. Cubic refinement (standard result): from the small-x expansion sin x = x − x³/6 + O(x⁵) (radians), x − sin x = x³/6 + O(x⁵) ~ x³/6. Limit: (x − sin x)/x³ → (x³/6)/x³ = 1/6. Consistency: the companion limit (sin x − x cos x)/x³ = 1/3 (NEB 2024) satisfies the identity (x − sin x) + (sin x − x cos x) = x(1 − cos x) ~ x³/2, and 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 ✓ — the three refinements interlock. At this level the refinement is quoted as a standard result derived from the sin x/x machinery.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 3x · tan 5x)/(x²), annotating every star used. — Split the product (limits of the factors exist — licensed): = lim (sin 3x)/x · lim (tan 5x)/x. First factor (star sin u/u → 1, u = 3x → 0): (sin 3x)/x = 3·(sin 3x)/(3x) → 3. Second factor (star tan u/u → 1, u = 5x → 0): (tan 5x)/x = 5·(tan 5x)/(5x) → 5. Product: 3 × 5 = 15. Order reading: sin 3x ~ 3x (order 1), tan 5x ~ 5x (order 1) ⟹ numerator ~ 15x², matching the x² denominator exactly ⟹ a nonzero constant was guaranteed before any steering. Deep point: the order comparison predicted the answer's type (finite nonzero), and the steering factors (3 and 5) delivered its value — annotate the star, name the u, then multiply constants.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 − cos 3x)/(x·sin 2x). — Order diagnosis first: 1 − cos 3x ~ (3x)²/2 = 9x²/2 (order 2); x·sin 2x ~ x·2x = 2x² (order 2) — same order ⟹ a nonzero constant is guaranteed, namely the ratio of leading coefficients 9/2 ÷ 2 = 9/4. Rigorous version via stars: write the expression as [(1 − cos 3x)/(3x)²]·[(3x)²/(x·sin 2x)]. First bracket (star (1−cos u)/u² → 1/2, u = 3x → 0): → 1/2. Second bracket: (3x)²/(x·sin 2x) = 9x²/(x·sin 2x) = 9x/(sin 2x) = (9/2)·(2x)/(sin 2x) → (9/2)·1 = 9/2 (star sin u/u, u = 2x → 0). Product: (1/2)·(9/2) = 9/4 ✓ — agreeing with the order reading. Deep point: the order comparison predicted the answer's type (finite nonzero) and its value (ratio of leading coefficients 9/4) before any algebra; the star decomposition then certified it. Write both leading terms with their full coefficients before dividing — same-order contestants leave the ratio of coefficients.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (tan x − sin x)/x³. — Reshape with an identity: tan x − sin x = sin x(1/cos x − 1) = sin x(1 − cos x)/cos x. Split the factors: (sin x/x)·[(1 − cos x)/x²]·[x²·... assemble: (tan x − sin x)/x³ = [(sin x)/x]·[(1 − cos x)/x²]·[1/cos x]. Apply the stars: sin x/x → 1 (u = x → 0); (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2 (second-order star); 1/cos x → 1 (cos is continuous at 0, cos 0 = 1). Product: 1 · 1/2 · 1 = 1/2. Order check: tan x − sin x ~ x³/3? — the standard refinement gives (tan x − sin x)/x³ → 1/2 here because tan x − sin x = sin x(1−cos x)/cos x ~ x·(x²/2) = x³/2 ✓ consistent. Deep point:** the identity transformed an order-3 difference into a product of lower-order stars — factoring out the largest common vanishing term (sin x) is the standard move when a difference of trig functions resists direct star-matching.
  • NEB-style (6m): A student evaluates lim_{x→0} sin(2x°)/(3x) by writing sin(2x°) ~ 2x and gets 2/3. Diagnose the error and give the correct answer. — The error: the star sin u/u → 1 holds only in radians. Here the argument is 2x° — measured in degrees — so sin(2x°) ~ (π/180)·(2x) = πx/90, not 2x. Conversion: sin(2x°) = sin(2x·π/180 radians) = sin(πx/90), and with u = πx/90 → 0, the star gives sin(πx/90) ~ πx/90. Correct limit: (πx/90)/(3x) = π/270. Sanity check on the error's size: the student's 2/3 vs correct π/270 ≈ 0.0116 — off by a factor of (2/3)/(π/270) = 180/π ≈ 57.3, exactly the degree-to-radian conversion factor. Deep point: the radian requirement is not a technicality — the sine star's content is "the sine's slope at 0 is 1," and slope = 1 happens only when angle equals arc length; in degrees the slope is π/180, and every star application silently carries that factor unless the units are radians.

Summary

Trigonometric limits are the sin u/u star (radians mandatory) plus steering. The rescaling theorem — insert kx/kx — gives sin(kx)/x → k, sin(kx)/sin(mx) → k/m (sin 5x/sin 3x → 5/3), tan(kx)/x → k. The cosine family runs through the half-angle identity 1 − cos x = 2sin²(x/2): (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2 and (1 − cos kx)/x² → k²/2 (k = 4 gives 8). Compound-angle identities reduce unfamiliar differences to standard ratios, and the squeeze |sin u| ≤ |u| is the safety net. When first-order terms cancel, the cubic refinements finish the job: x − sin x ~ x³/6 (limit 1/6) and sin x − x cos x ~ x³/3 (limit 1/3) — interlocking through x(1 − cos x) ~ x³/2. Insert, halve, reduce, squeeze: the four moves that turn any trig 0/0 into a star.

Rewrite: (1 − cos 4x)/x² = 2sin²(2x)/x² = 2 · [sin 2x/(2x)]² · (2x)²/x² = 2 · [sin 2x/(2x)]² · 4 = 8·[sin 2x/(2x)]². Apply the star: sin 2x/(2x) → 1 as x → 0 ⟹ the limit is 8·1² = 8. Formula check: the general result (1 − cos kx)/x² → k²/2 gives 4²/2 = 16/2 = 8 ✓ — the half-angle identity is exactly where the ½ and the k² come from.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. — Why steering stalls: both x − sin x and x³ vanish to third order — the first-order standard limits cancel out, exposing the cubic layer. Cubic refinement (standard result): from the small-x expansion sin x = x − x³/6 + O(x⁵) (radians), x − sin x = x³/6 + O(x⁵) ~ x³/6. Limit: (x − sin x)/x³ → (x³/6)/x³ = 1/6. Consistency: the companion limit (sin x − x cos x)/x³ = 1/3 (NEB 2024) satisfies the identity (x − sin x) + (sin x − x cos x) = x(1 − cos x) ~ x³/2, and 1/6 + 1/3 = 1/2 ✓ — the three refinements interlock. At this level the refinement is quoted as a standard result derived from the sin x/x machinery.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 3x · tan 5x)/(x²), annotating every star used. — Split the product (limits of the factors exist — licensed): = lim (sin 3x)/x · lim (tan 5x)/x. First factor (star sin u/u → 1, u = 3x → 0): (sin 3x)/x = 3·(sin 3x)/(3x) → 3. Second factor (star tan u/u → 1, u = 5x → 0): (tan 5x)/x = 5·(tan 5x)/(5x) → 5. Product: 3 × 5 = 15. Order reading: sin 3x ~ 3x (order 1), tan 5x ~ 5x (order 1) ⟹ numerator ~ 15x², matching the x² denominator exactly ⟹ a nonzero constant was guaranteed before any steering. Deep point: the order comparison predicted the answer's type (finite nonzero), and the steering factors (3 and 5) delivered its value — annotate the star, name the u, then multiply constants.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 − cos 3x)/(x·sin 2x). — Order diagnosis first: 1 − cos 3x ~ (3x)²/2 = 9x²/2 (order 2); x·sin 2x ~ x·2x = 2x² (order 2) — same order ⟹ a nonzero constant is guaranteed, namely the ratio of leading coefficients 9/2 ÷ 2 = 9/4. Rigorous version via stars: write the expression as [(1 − cos 3x)/(3x)²]·[(3x)²/(x·sin 2x)]. First bracket (star (1−cos u)/u² → 1/2, u = 3x → 0): → 1/2. Second bracket: (3x)²/(x·sin 2x) = 9x²/(x·sin 2x) = 9x/(sin 2x) = (9/2)·(2x)/(sin 2x) → (9/2)·1 = 9/2 (star sin u/u, u = 2x → 0). Product: (1/2)·(9/2) = 9/4 ✓ — agreeing with the order reading. Deep point: the order comparison predicted the answer's type (finite nonzero) and its value (ratio of leading coefficients 9/4) before any algebra; the star decomposition then certified it. Write both leading terms with their full coefficients before dividing — same-order contestants leave the ratio of coefficients.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (tan x − sin x)/x³. — Reshape with an identity: tan x − sin x = sin x(1/cos x − 1) = sin x(1 − cos x)/cos x. Split the factors: (sin x/x)·[(1 − cos x)/x²]·[x²·... assemble: (tan x − sin x)/x³ = [(sin x)/x]·[(1 − cos x)/x²]·[1/cos x]. Apply the stars: sin x/x → 1 (u = x → 0); (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2 (second-order star); 1/cos x → 1 (cos is continuous at 0, cos 0 = 1). Product: 1 · 1/2 · 1 = 1/2. Order check: tan x − sin x ~ x³/3? — the standard refinement gives (tan x − sin x)/x³ → 1/2 here because tan x − sin x = sin x(1−cos x)/cos x ~ x·(x²/2) = x³/2 ✓ consistent. Deep point:** the identity transformed an order-3 difference into a product of lower-order stars — factoring out the largest common vanishing term (sin x) is the standard move when a difference of trig functions resists direct star-matching.
  • NEB-style (6m): A student evaluates lim_{x→0} sin(2x°)/(3x) by writing sin(2x°) ~ 2x and gets 2/3. Diagnose the error and give the correct answer. — The error: the star sin u/u → 1 holds only in radians. Here the argument is 2x° — measured in degrees — so sin(2x°) ~ (π/180)·(2x) = πx/90, not 2x. Conversion: sin(2x°) = sin(2x·π/180 radians) = sin(πx/90), and with u = πx/90 → 0, the star gives sin(πx/90) ~ πx/90. Correct limit: (πx/90)/(3x) = π/270. Sanity check on the error's size: the student's 2/3 vs correct π/270 ≈ 0.0116 — off by a factor of (2/3)/(π/270) = 180/π ≈ 57.3, exactly the degree-to-radian conversion factor. Deep point: the radian requirement is not a technicality — the sine star's content is "the sine's slope at 0 is 1," and slope = 1 happens only when angle equals arc length; in degrees the slope is π/180, and every star application silently carries that factor unless the units are radians.
