Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Drill Gallery 15.1

    Exercise 15.1 is the foundation drill: direct-substitution limits, polynomial and rational evaluation, and the first 0/0 factoring exercises. This canonical packages the exercise's method types with worked exemplars.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Method types in 15.1

    • Type A — direct substitution: polynomials, continuous functions: lim = value (cite continuity).
    • Type B — factoring 0/0: quadratics/cubics over linear denominators: factor, cancel (x ≠ a), substitute.
    • Type C — rationalising 0/0: root differences over linear terms: multiply by the conjugate.
    • Type D — limits at infinity of rationals: compare degrees (row 41's cousin; appears as extension).

    2.2 Type B template

    • lim_{x→a} (p(x))/(x − a) with p(a) = 0 ⟹ (x − a) divides p ⟹ factor, cancel, substitute. The factor theorem guarantees the factor exists whenever substitution gave 0/0.

    2.3 Type C template

    • lim_{x→a} (√g(x) − √g(a))/(x − a): multiply by the conjugate; numerator becomes g(x) − g(a), which carries the factor (x − a).

    2.4 Answer discipline

    • State the form (number / 0/0 / c/0), the transformation, the cancellation licence, then the value — the four-line rhythm every limit answer follows.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why 15.1 matters

    • The exercise drills the diagnosis reflex: substitute, classify, act. Every later technique (standard limits, L'Hôpital-style reasoning) assumes this triage is automatic.

    4.2 The factor theorem as a promise

    • 0/0 at a means the numerator must contain (x − a) — the factor theorem turns an inconvenience into a guarantee: the cancellation always exists; finding it is the only work.

    4.3 Conjugates and hidden factors

    • (√g(x) − √g(a))² expansions reveal (x − a) inside radical expressions — the algebraic identity doing what factoring does for polynomials.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Skipping the classification line: jumping to algebra without naming 0/0 loses the recognition mark.

  • Cancelling without the licence: state x ≠ a — the cancellation is a punctured-neighbourhood statement.
  • Type C sign slips: (√u − √v)(√u + √v) = u − v — mind which square difference appears.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→3} (x² − 9)/(x² − 5x + 6). — 0/0 ⟹ factor both: (x−3)(x+3)/[(x−3)(x−2)] = (x+3)/(x−2) for x ≠ 3. Limit: 6/1 = 6. Both polynomials shared the factor (x − 3) — the factor theorem at work on numerator and denominator simultaneously.

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (√(x + 9) − 3)/x. — 0/0 ⟹ conjugate: [(√(x+9) − 3)(√(x+9) + 3)]/[x(√(x+9) + 3)] = (x + 9 − 9)/[x(√(x+9)+3)] = 1/(√(x+9) + 3) for x ≠ 0. Limit: 1/6. (The derivative of √(x+9) at 0.)
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (x³ − 8)/(x − 2). — 0/0 ⟹ factor: x³ − 8 = (x − 2)(x² + 2x + 4) ⟹ limit = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. (Cross-check: the (xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a) star gives 3·2² = 12 ✓.)

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — The Four-Line Rhythm as Proof Discipline

    • Every algebraic limit solution is the same four lines: form (substitute, name 0/0 or c/0), transformation (factor, conjugate, or compare degrees), licence (state x ≠ a so cancellation is legal), value (substitute the simplified form). The rhythm is not style — each line is a theorem being invoked.
    • Why the licence line is graded: cancelling (x − a) changes the expression at a only; writing "for x ≠ a" is what makes the cancelled and original expressions limit-equivalent. Omitting it leaves a logical gap an examiner can mark.
    • The factor theorem as a promise: 0/0 at a guarantees (x − a) divides the numerator — so type B never dead-ends. If factoring stalls, the diagnosis was wrong: recheck whether the form is really 0/0.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Double Factoring and the Shape of Rational 0/0

    • When numerator and denominator both vanish at a, both carry (x − a): the limit is the ratio of the remaining factors evaluated at a — e.g. (x²−5x+6)/(x²−4) at 2 = (x−3)(x−2)/[(x−2)(x+2)] → (−1)/4. One cancellation, one substitution.
    • Higher multiplicity: if (x − a)² survives in the denominator after one cancellation, the form re-collapses to c/0 — a wall. After each cancellation, re-substitute to re-diagnose: 0/0 again means factor again; c/0 means classify the wall.
    • Conjugate work is factoring in disguise: √g(x) − √g(a) = [g(x) − g(a)]/[√g(x) + √g(a)] — the conjugate converts a radical difference into a polynomial difference, feeding it back into the type-B machinery.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — Infinity Limits as Degree Arithmetic

    • For rational functions at ∞, only degrees and leading coefficients matter: divide by the highest power present and every lower term dies. The answer is determined by three numbers — top degree, bottom degree, leading ratio.
    • The three cases: top > bottom ⟹ ±∞ (sign from leading coefficients and parity); top = bottom ⟹ leading ratio; top < bottom ⟹ 0. Everything else in the polynomials is decoration that vanishes in the race.
    • Why this is deep: it is the growth ladder applied inside one family — polynomials grow by degree, so comparing degrees is comparing rungs. The same referee later ranks exponentials, logs, and powers; degree comparison is the ladder's first and most-used instance.

Examples

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→3} (x² − 9)/(x² − 5x + 6). — Classify: (9 − 9)/(9 − 15 + 6) = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Factor both: numerator (x − 3)(x + 3); denominator x² − 5x + 6 = (x − 3)(x − 2) — the factor theorem delivered (x − 3) to both polynomials. Cancel (licensed for x ≠ 3): (x + 3)/(x − 2). Substitute: 6/1 = 6. The double-factor pattern is common in 15.1: when numerator and denominator both vanish at a, both carry the factor, and one cancellation serves both.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (√(x + 9) − 3)/x. — Classify: (3 − 3)/0 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Rationalise: multiply by the conjugate (√(x+9) + 3)/(√(x+9) + 3): numerator = (√(x+9))² − 3² = (x + 9) − 9 = x, which cancels the denominator (x ≠ 0 licensed): the expression becomes 1/(√(x+9) + 3). Substitute: 1/(3 + 3) = 1/6. Recognition: this is the derivative of √(x+9) at x = 0 — the Type C conjugate route is the derivative computation before calculus names it.
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (x³ − 8)/(x − 2) by two methods. — Method 1 — factoring: x³ − 8 = (x − 2)(x² + 2x + 4) (difference of cubes); cancelling (x ≠ 2) leaves x² + 2x + 4 ⟹ limit = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12. Method 2 — the algebraic star: lim_{x→a} (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹ with n = 3, a = 2 gives 3·2² = 12 ✓. Moral: the star formula is the factoring theorem compressed — both routes must agree, and the agreement is a built-in error check for every polynomial 0/0.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (x³ − 8)/(x² − 4), with the four-line rhythm. — Form: substitute x = 2: (8 − 8)/(4 − 4) = 0/0 — indeterminate. Transformation (double factoring): x³ − 8 = (x − 2)(x² + 2x + 4); x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2) — both carry (x − 2), as the factor theorem promised. Licence: for x ≠ 2, the expression = (x² + 2x + 4)/(x + 2). Value: substitute — (4 + 4 + 4)/(4) = 3. Cross-check with the star: (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ applied to the numerator alone gives 3·4 = 12, and the denominator's star gives 2·2 = 4 ⟹ 12/4 = 3 ✓. Deep point: double factoring is the workhorse of rational 0/0 — cancel the shared root once, then the surviving expression is continuous at a, so the final substitution is licensed by continuity, not by further algebra.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (√(1 + x) − √(1 − x))/x. — Form: (1 − 1)/0 = 0/0 ✓. Transformation (double conjugate): multiply by (√(1+x) + √(1−x))/(same): numerator = (1 + x) − (1 − x) = 2x; the expression = 2x/[x(√(1+x) + √(1−x))] = 2/(√(1+x) + √(1−x)) for x ≠ 0. Licence: the cancellation of x is valid for x ≠ 0 — exactly the punctured neighbourhood the limit uses. Value: substitute x = 0: 2/(1 + 1) = 1. Cross-check: each root's star (√(1+u) − 1)/u → 1/2 gives (1/2) − (−1/2) = 1 ✓. Deep point: when two radicals straddle the point, one conjugate clears both at once because their difference of squares is linear in x — the conjugate's job is always to convert the radical difference into a difference the factor theorem can handle.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (2x³ − x + 5)/(5x³ + 4x²) and lim_{x→∞} (2x³ − x + 5)/(5x⁴ + 1), stating the degree rule in each. — First limit (degrees tie, 3 = 3): divide numerator and denominator by x³: (2 − 1/x² + 5/x³)/(5 + 4/x) → (2 − 0 + 0)/(5 + 0) = 2/5 — the leading-coefficient ratio. Second limit (top < bottom, 3 < 4): divide by x⁴: (2/x − 1/x³ + 5/x⁴)/(5 + 1/x⁴) → 0/5 = 0 — the denominator's higher degree wins the race to infinity. Rule stated: at ∞, top > bottom ⟹ ±∞; tie ⟹ leading ratio; top < bottom ⟹ 0. Deep point: dividing by the highest power present is the mechanical realisation of the growth ladder inside the polynomial family — every lower term's contribution dies like 1/xᵏ, leaving only the leading coefficients to compete.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (x² − 3x + 2)/(x² − 1) and classify the discontinuity of f(x) = that expression with f(1) defined as 1/2. — Form: (1 − 3 + 2)/(1 − 1) = 0/0 ✓. Transformation (double factoring): x² − 3x + 2 = (x − 1)(x − 2); x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1) ⟹ for x ≠ 1 the expression = (x − 2)/(x + 1). Licence: cancellation valid for x ≠ 1. Value: (1 − 2)/(1 + 1) = −1/2. Classification: the limit exists (−1/2); f(1) = 1/2 ≠ −1/2 ⟹ removable discontinuity at 1 — a detached dot at (1, 1/2) over the hole at (1, −1/2). Repair: f(1) = −1/2 makes f continuous. Deep point: the exercise's two halves are one computation — the limit (type B drill) and the classification (continuity drill) both flow from the same factorisation; the assigned value 1/2 is the examiner's handle on a point the algebra otherwise erases.

Formulas

    • Type B: p(a) = 0 ⟹ p(x) = (x − a)q(x)
    • Type C: (√g(x) − √g(a))/(x − a) = 1/(√g(x) + √g(a))
    • star cross-check: lim (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = naⁿ⁻¹

Key Points

  • Exercise 15.1 method types: A direct substitution (continuous functions); B factor-and-cancel 0/0 (factor theorem guarantees (x - a) divides the numerator); C conjugate rationalisation for radical differences; D degree comparison for infinity limits. Four-line answer rhythm: form, transformation, cancellation licence, value. Type B double factoring: numerator and denominator may both carry (x - a). Type C: (sqrt g(x) - sqrt g(a))(sqrt g(x) + sqrt g(a)) = g(x) - g(a). Cross-check with the (x^n - a^n)/(x - a) star: n a^(n-1).

Summary

Exercise 15.1 is the triage drill: substitute first, classify the outcome, act. Type A — continuous functions surrender to direct substitution (cite continuity). Type B — polynomial 0/0 surrenders to the factor theorem: p(a) = 0 guarantees the factor (x − a), so factor, cancel (x ≠ a licensed), substitute ((x²−9)/(x²−5x+6) → 6 with the factor shared by both polynomials; x³ − 8 over x − 2 → 12, cross-checked by the star naⁿ⁻¹ = 3·2²). Type C — radical 0/0 surrenders to the conjugate: (√(x+9) − 3)/x becomes 1/(√(x+9)+3) → 1/6, the derivative of √(x+9) in disguise. Type D — ∞/∞ rationals surrender to degree comparison. The four-line rhythm — form, transformation, licence, value — is both the method and the marking scheme; the factor theorem's promise (the cancellation always exists) is the topic's deepest comfort.

Second limit (top < bottom, 3 < 4): divide by x⁴: (2/x − 1/x³ + 5/x⁴)/(5 + 1/x⁴) → 0/5 = 0 — the denominator's higher degree wins the race to infinity. Rule stated: at ∞, top > bottom ⟹ ±∞; tie ⟹ leading ratio; top < bottom ⟹ 0. Deep point: dividing by the highest power present is the mechanical realisation of the growth ladder inside the polynomial family — every lower term's contribution dies like 1/xᵏ, leaving only the leading coefficients to compete.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (x² − 3x + 2)/(x² − 1) and classify the discontinuity of f(x) = that expression with f(1) defined as 1/2. — Form: (1 − 3 + 2)/(1 − 1) = 0/0 ✓. Transformation (double factoring): x² − 3x + 2 = (x − 1)(x − 2); x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1) ⟹ for x ≠ 1 the expression = (x − 2)/(x + 1). Licence: cancellation valid for x ≠ 1. Value: (1 − 2)/(1 + 1) = −1/2. Classification: the limit exists (−1/2); f(1) = 1/2 ≠ −1/2 ⟹ removable discontinuity at 1 — a detached dot at (1, 1/2) over the hole at (1, −1/2). Repair: f(1) = −1/2 makes f continuous. Deep point: the exercise's two halves are one computation — the limit (type B drill) and the classification (continuity drill) both flow from the same factorisation; the assigned value 1/2 is the examiner's handle on a point the algebra otherwise erases.
