Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Limit Hall of Fame

    Standard limits are the pre-computed results every other limit reduces to: lim sin x/x = 1, lim (1+x)^{1/x} = e, lim (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = naⁿ⁻¹, and their kin. Memorise the statements; understand the reductions.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The trigonometric star

    • lim_{x→0} sin x/x = 1 (x in radians — the radian is what makes it true).
    • Companion: lim_{x→0} tan x/x = 1; lim_{x→0} (1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2.

    2.2 The exponential star

    • lim_{x→0} (1 + x)^{1/x} = e and the twin lim_{x→∞} (1 + 1/x)^x = e.
    • lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x = 1 — the exponential's rate at 0, the seed of its derivative.
    • lim_{x→0} (aˣ − 1)/x = ln a (general base).
    • lim_{x→0} ln(1 + x)/x = 1.

    2.3 The algebraic star

    • lim_{x→a} (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n·aⁿ⁻¹ — valid for all rational n; the factorisation theorem wearing a limit.
    • lim_{x→0} (1 + x)ᵏ... ((1+x)ⁿ − 1)/x = n — the binomial baby case.

    2.4 Reduction discipline

    • Any 0/0 candidate: try to rewrite into a standard form via substitution (u = g(x) → 0), algebraic identity, or multiplication by 1. The standard limits are the targets of every transformation.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why radians

    • sin x/x → 1 is a geometry statement: arc length ≈ chord length for tiny angles — true only when x measures arc in radius units. Degrees smuggle in a factor π/180.

    4.2 The (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) theorem as a bridge

    • For integer n it is factoring; for general rational n it is the derivative of xⁿ at a in disguise — the standard limit that calculus will generalise.

    4.3 e's double definition

    • (1 + x)^{1/x} at 0 and (1 + 1/x)^x at ∞ are the same limit seen from the two ends of u = 1/x — compound interest's continuous limit.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Degrees break sin x/x: always check the angle unit; convert degrees to radians first.

  • (1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2, not 1: the half-angle identity sin²(x/2) = (1 − cos x)/2 supplies the factor.
  • (aˣ − 1)/x → ln a: specialising a = e gives 1; the general base keeps the ln a factor.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x equals: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) e (d) ∞ (1m) — This is the standard limit: lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x = 1 → option (b). It is the definition of eˣ's instantaneous rate at 0 — the fact that makes (eˣ)′ = eˣ true.

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate (i) lim_{x→0} (sin x − x cos x)/x³, (ii) lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. (4m) — (i) Write (sin x − x cos x)/x³ = [sin x/x − cos x]/x². Now sin x/x → 1 and cos x → 1, giving 0/0 again — refine: sin x − x cos x = x[sin x/x − cos x]; using the standard-limit toolkit (sin x = x − x³/6 + ⋯, cos x = 1 − x²/2 + ⋯): sin x − x cos x = (x − x³/6) − x(1 − x²/2) = x − x³/6 − x + x³/2 = x³/3. (i) = 1/3. (ii) x − sin x = x − (x − x³/6) = x³/6 ⟹ (ii) = 1/6. (Series route is the clean one; equivalently apply the standard limits twice with the compound-angle identity sin x − x cos x = x²·(sin x/x)′-type reductions.)
  • NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (√(7+x) − 3)/(x − 2). (3m) — 0/0 ⟹ rationalise: multiply by (√(7+x) + 3)/(√(7+x) + 3): numerator = (7 + x) − 9 = x − 2, cancelling the denominator (x ≠ 2): 1/(√(7+x) + 3). Limit: 1/(√9 + 3) = 1/6. (Standard-limit thinking: the conjugate reduction is the root version of the (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) theorem with n = 1/2, a = 2: (1/2)·2^{−1/2} = 1/(2√2)... here the shifted form gives 1/6 — the rationalisation is the honest working.)

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — Stars Are Tangent Approximations in Disguise

    • Each standard limit is a statement that a complicated function agrees with a simpler one to first order: sin u ~ u says the sine curve hugs the line y = u near 0; eᵘ − 1 ~ u says the exponential hugs y = 1 + u; ln(1+u) ~ u says the log hugs y = u. The stars are a table of local linearisations.
    • Why radians are non-negotiable: sin u ~ u is only true when u measures arc length — in degrees the mismatch factor π/180 breaks the star. The radian is the angle unit that makes the sine's slope at 0 equal 1; the star is that geometric fact written as a limit.
    • Composability: stars compose with the substitution u = g(x) → 0 — sin(3x) ~ 3x, ln(1 + 5x) ~ 5x, e^{−2x} − 1 ~ −2x. One table, unlimited composites; the only care needed is that u genuinely → 0 at the point in question.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Steering: Manufacturing the Star's Denominator

    • The stars have fixed denominators (u, u², u³...), but exam expressions arrive with arbitrary denominators. Steering is the craft of multiplying by 1 — rewriting A/B as (A·k)/(B·k) to make the denominator match a star's form, then compensating with the constant.
    • The canonical steering: to use sin u/u, force the denominator to be exactly the argument of the sine: (sin 5x)/x = 5·(sin 5x)/(5x) → 5·1 = 5. The inserted factor 5x is the star's demanded denominator; the outside 5 pays for it.
    • Higher-order stars decide close races: when two contestants vanish at the same order (both ~ u), the ratio is a nonzero constant; when one is faster (u² vs u), the ratio is 0 or ∞. Knowing the order of each star — sin u ~ u (order 1), 1 − cos u ~ u²/2 (order 2), tan u − u ~ u³/3 (order 3) — tells you which tool resolves which expression before any algebra.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — The Hall of Fame as a Proof Network

    • The stars are not independent facts: sin u/u → 1 plus the Pythagorean identity yields (1 − cos u)/u → 0; (eᵘ − 1)/u → 1 plus continuity yields ln(1+u)/u → 1 (inverse function); the compound star ((1+u)^{1/u} → e) is the definition of e wearing limit notation.
    • One star, many faces: lim_{u→0} (1+u)^{1/u} = e is simultaneously the definition of e, the compound-interest limit, and the engine of every 1^∞ power form. Memorising it as three separate facts triples the memorisation and quarters the understanding.
    • Usage discipline: every star application should be annotated — which star, what u is, why u → 0 (or → ∞ for the e forms). The annotation is the proof; the answer is just its last line.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x equals: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) e (d) ∞ (1m) — Recognition: this is the exponential standard limit: lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x = 1 — option (b). Why it is true: eˣ = 1 + x + x²/2 + ⋯, so eˣ − 1 = x + x²/2 + ⋯ = x(1 + x/2 + ⋯), and dividing by x leaves 1 + x/2 + ⋯ → 1. Significance: this limit is the statement that eˣ has instantaneous rate 1 at x = 0 — the seed of (eˣ)′ = eˣ, the defining perk of the natural base. (For general base a, (aˣ − 1)/x → ln a; a = e gives ln e = 1.)
  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate (i) lim_{x→0} (sin x − x cos x)/x³ and (ii) lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. (4m) — Series route (small-x expansions, radians): sin x = x − x³/6 + O(x⁵) and cos x = 1 − x²/2 + O(x⁴). (i): sin x − x cos x = (x − x³/6) − x(1 − x²/2) = x − x³/6 − x + x³/2 = x³(1/2 − 1/6) = x³/3 ⟹ the limit is 1/3. (ii): x − sin x = x − (x − x³/6) = x³/6 ⟹ the limit is 1/6. Standard-limit cross-check for (ii): x − sin x = x(1 − sin x/x); with sin x/x = 1 − x²/6 + ⋯ (from the half-angle identity 1 − sin x/x... equivalently the cubic term), 1 − sin x/x ~ x²/6, so x(1 − sin x/x)/x³ → 1/6 ✓. Both are higher-order refinements of sin x/x → 1: the leading x-terms cancel, exposing the cubic layer.
  • NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (√(7+x) − 3)/(x − 2). (3m) — Substitution: (√9 − 3)/0 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Rationalise: multiply by the conjugate (√(7+x) + 3)/(√(7+x) + 3): numerator = (7 + x) − 9 = x − 2, which cancels the denominator (licensed for x ≠ 2): the expression becomes 1/(√(7+x) + 3). Substitute: 1/(√9 + 3) = 1/6. Connection: this is the (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) standard limit with a fractional power — equivalently the derivative of √(7+x) at x = 2, which the chain rule will later confirm as 1/(2√9) = 1/6 ✓. The conjugate is the honest algebraic route before calculus tools are available.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 5x − tan 3x)/(2x) using standard limits, annotating each star used. — Split the quotient (both pieces' limits exist — licensed): = lim (sin 5x)/(2x) − lim (tan 3x)/(2x). First piece (star: sin u/u → 1, u = 5x → 0): (sin 5x)/(2x) = (5/2)·(sin 5x)/(5x) → (5/2)·1 = 5/2 — the steering factor 5x was manufactured in the denominator, paid for by the outside 5/2. Second piece (star: tan u/u → 1, u = 3x → 0): (tan 3x)/(2x) = (3/2)·(tan 3x)/(3x) → (3/2)·1 = 3/2. Combine: 5/2 − 3/2 = 1. Deep point: every term was steered to its star's exact denominator and the constant carried outside — the annotation (which star, which u) is the working; the arithmetic is bookkeeping. Note the answer is a clean difference of first-order coefficients, which is the general pattern for same-order contestants.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 − cos 4x)/(x²) using the standard limit (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2. — Match the star's form: the star wants u² under (1 − cos u); here u = 4x, so u² = 16x². Steer: (1 − cos 4x)/(x²) = 16·(1 − cos 4x)/(16x²) = 16·(1 − cos 4x)/(4x)². Apply the star (u = 4x → 0): (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2 ⟹ the limit = 16·(1/2) = 8. Cross-check via the first-order star: (1 − cos 4x)/(4x) → 0 shows the numerator vanishes faster than x — consistent with the contest being decided at second order, where the u²/2 star operates ✓. Deep point: the steering factor is the square of the argument's coefficient (16 = 4²) because the star's denominator is quadratic — matching orders of vanishing means matching powers of the steering factor.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (1 + 3/x)^{x}, citing the compound star. — Recognise the form: base → 1, exponent → ∞ — the 1^∞ species, home of the compound star. Reshape to the star's exact form: (1 + 3/x)^{x} = [(1 + 3/x)^{x/3}]³ — the inner bracket is (1 + u)^{1/u} with u = 3/x → 0 as x → ∞. Apply the star: (1 + u)^{1/u} → e ⟹ the limit = e³. Deep point: the star (1+u)^{1/u} → e is the definition of e; every 1^∞ question is a disguise of it, and the answer is always e^{(coefficient ratio)} — here the exponent's coefficient 1 against the base's displacement 3 gives e³. The reshape-then-exponentiate move generalises: steer the base to (1 + u) with u → 0, then read the exponent as 1/u times a constant.
  • NEB-style (6m): Using standard limits, evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{3x} − 1)/(ln(1 + 2x)). — Form check: substitute x = 0: (1 − 1)/ln 1 = 0/0 — indeterminate. Split into stars: numerator e^{3x} − 1 ~ 3x (star: (eᵘ−1)/u → 1, u = 3x → 0); denominator ln(1 + 2x) ~ 2x (star: ln(1+u)/u → 1, u = 2x → 0). Take the ratio: (e^{3x} − 1)/ln(1+2x) ≈ (3x)/(2x) = 3/2 — more rigorously, write the expression as [(e^{3x}−1)/(3x)]·[2x/ln(1+2x)]·(3/2): the first bracket → 1, the second → 1, leaving 3/2. Deep point: when both contestants vanish at first order, the limit is just the ratio of their first-order coefficients — the stars convert each function into its linear approximation, and the linear ratio survives. This is L'Hôpital's answer computed without differentiating: same theory, star-engine instead of derivative-engine.

Key Points

  • Standard limits (x in radians): sin x/x -> 1; tan x/x -> 1; (1 - cos x)/x^2 -> 1/2; (e^x - 1)/x -> 1; (a^x - 1)/x -> ln a; ln(1+x)/x -> 1; (x^n - a^n)/(x - a) -> n a^(n-1) as x -> a; (1 + x)^(1/x) -> e as x -> 0; (1 + 1/x)^x -> e as x -> inf; ((1+x)^n - 1)/x -> n. Summary

Standard limits are the pre-proved stars every 0/0 reduces to. The trigonometric shelf (radians mandatory): sin x/x → 1, tan x/x → 1, (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2. The exponential shelf: (eˣ − 1)/x → 1 (NEB 2022's MCQ — the seed of (eˣ)′ = eˣ), (aˣ − 1)/x → ln a, ln(1+x)/x → 1, and the double definition of e: (1+x)^{1/x} → 0⁺... → e as x → 0 and (1 + 1/x)^x → e as x → ∞. The algebraic star: (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ — factoring for integers, a derivative in disguise for fractional powers (√(7+x) − 3 over x − 2 rationalises to 1/6, NEB 2021). Reduction discipline: substitute, factor, multiply by 1 — steer the unknown into a star. When cancellations run deep, series finish the job: x − sin x ~ x³/6 and sin x − x cos x ~ x³/3 (NEB 2024's paired 1/6 and 1/3). Memorise the shelf; master the steering.

  • The radian contract - sin x/x -> 1 is arc-versus-chord geometry, valid only in radians; degrees smuggle a pi/180 factor into every trig limit.
Universal Facts
  • Compound interest converging to continuous compounding is (1 + 1/n)^n -> e - the exponential star discovered by bankers.
Important Statements
  • "sin x/x -> 1; (e^x - 1)/x -> 1; (x^n - a^n)/(x - a) -> n a^(n-1); (1 + x)^(1/x) -> e."
Important Notes
  • Verified PYQs: NEB 2022 ((e^x - 1)/x MCQ), NEB 2024 (sin x - x cos x and x - sin x over x^3), NEB 2021 (sqrt(7+x) - 3 over x - 2) - assigned to this row.
Exam Short Tricks
  • 0/0: hunt for a standard form. Roots: conjugate. Trig: force sin u/u. Exponentials: force (e^u - 1)/u. Series fallback: x - sin x ~ x^3/6, sin x - x cos x ~ x^3/3.
Exam Notes
  • Quote the standard limit by name when using it - the citation is the method mark; keep radians explicit in trig limits.
Formulas
    • lim_{x→0} sin x/x = 1; tan x/x = 1; (1 − cos x)/x² = 1/2

Practice Questions
  • NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x equals: (a) 0 (b) 1 (c) e (d) ∞ (1m) — Recognition: this is the exponential standard limit: lim_{x→0} (eˣ − 1)/x = 1 — option (b). Why it is true: eˣ = 1 + x + x²/2 + ⋯, so eˣ − 1 = x + x²/2 + ⋯ = x(1 + x/2 + ⋯), and dividing by x leaves 1 + x/2 + ⋯ → 1. Significance: this limit is the statement that eˣ has instantaneous rate 1 at x = 0 — the seed of (eˣ)′ = eˣ, the defining perk of the natural base. (For general base a, (aˣ − 1)/x → ln a; a = e gives ln e = 1.)
  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate (i) lim_{x→0} (sin x − x cos x)/x³ and (ii) lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³. (4m) — Series route (small-x expansions, radians): sin x = x − x³/6 + O(x⁵) and cos x = 1 − x²/2 + O(x⁴). (i): sin x − x cos x = (x − x³/6) − x(1 − x²/2) = x − x³/6 − x + x³/2 = x³(1/2 − 1/6) = x³/3 ⟹ the limit is 1/3. (ii): x − sin x = x − (x − x³/6) = x³/6 ⟹ the limit is 1/6. Standard-limit cross-check for (ii): x − sin x = x(1 − sin x/x); with sin x/x = 1 − x²/6 + ⋯ (from the half-angle identity 1 − sin x/x... equivalently the cubic term), 1 − sin x/x ~ x²/6, so x(1 − sin x/x)/x³ → 1/6 ✓. Both are higher-order refinements of sin x/x → 1: the leading x-terms cancel, exposing the cubic layer.
  • NEB 2021 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→2} (√(7+x) − 3)/(x − 2). (3m) — Substitution: (√9 − 3)/0 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Rationalise: multiply by the conjugate (√(7+x) + 3)/(√(7+x) + 3): numerator = (7 + x) − 9 = x − 2, which cancels the denominator (licensed for x ≠ 2): the expression becomes 1/(√(7+x) + 3). Substitute: 1/(√9 + 3) = 1/6. Connection: this is the (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) standard limit with a fractional power — equivalently the derivative of √(7+x) at x = 2, which the chain rule will later confirm as 1/(2√9) = 1/6 ✓. The conjugate is the honest algebraic route before calculus tools are available.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 5x − tan 3x)/(2x) using standard limits, annotating each star used. — Split the quotient (both pieces' limits exist — licensed): = lim (sin 5x)/(2x) − lim (tan 3x)/(2x). First piece (star: sin u/u → 1, u = 5x → 0): (sin 5x)/(2x) = (5/2)·(sin 5x)/(5x) → (5/2)·1 = 5/2 — the steering factor 5x was manufactured in the denominator, paid for by the outside 5/2. Second piece (star: tan u/u → 1, u = 3x → 0): (tan 3x)/(2x) = (3/2)·(tan 3x)/(3x) → (3/2)·1 = 3/2. Combine: 5/2 − 3/2 = 1. Deep point: every term was steered to its star's exact denominator and the constant carried outside — the annotation (which star, which u) is the working; the arithmetic is bookkeeping. Note the answer is a clean difference of first-order coefficients, which is the general pattern for same-order contestants.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 − cos 4x)/(x²) using the standard limit (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2. — Match the star's form: the star wants u² under (1 − cos u); here u = 4x, so u² = 16x². Steer: (1 − cos 4x)/(x²) = 16·(1 − cos 4x)/(16x²) = 16·(1 − cos 4x)/(4x)². Apply the star (u = 4x → 0): (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2 ⟹ the limit = 16·(1/2) = 8. Cross-check via the first-order star: (1 − cos 4x)/(4x) → 0 shows the numerator vanishes faster than x — consistent with the contest being decided at second order, where the u²/2 star operates ✓. Deep point: the steering factor is the square of the argument's coefficient (16 = 4²) because the star's denominator is quadratic — matching orders of vanishing means matching powers of the steering factor.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (1 + 3/x)^{x}, citing the compound star. — Recognise the form: base → 1, exponent → ∞ — the 1^∞ species, home of the compound star. Reshape to the star's exact form: (1 + 3/x)^{x} = [(1 + 3/x)^{x/3}]³ — the inner bracket is (1 + u)^{1/u} with u = 3/x → 0 as x → ∞. Apply the star: (1 + u)^{1/u} → e ⟹ the limit = e³. Deep point: the star (1+u)^{1/u} → e is the definition of e; every 1^∞ question is a disguise of it, and the answer is always e^{(coefficient ratio)} — here the exponent's coefficient 1 against the base's displacement 3 gives e³. The reshape-then-exponentiate move generalises: steer the base to (1 + u) with u → 0, then read the exponent as 1/u times a constant.
  • NEB-style (6m): Using standard limits, evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{3x} − 1)/(ln(1 + 2x)). — Form check: substitute x = 0: (1 − 1)/ln 1 = 0/0 — indeterminate. Split into stars: numerator e^{3x} − 1 ~ 3x (star: (eᵘ−1)/u → 1, u = 3x → 0); denominator ln(1 + 2x) ~ 2x (star: ln(1+u)/u → 1, u = 2x → 0). Take the ratio: (e^{3x} − 1)/ln(1+2x) ≈ (3x)/(2x) = 3/2 — more rigorously, write the expression as [(e^{3x}−1)/(3x)]·[2x/ln(1+2x)]·(3/2): the first bracket → 1, the second → 1, leaving 3/2. Deep point: when both contestants vanish at first order, the limit is just the ratio of their first-order coefficients — the stars convert each function into its linear approximation, and the linear ratio survives. This is L'Hôpital's answer computed without differentiating: same theory, star-engine instead of derivative-engine.
