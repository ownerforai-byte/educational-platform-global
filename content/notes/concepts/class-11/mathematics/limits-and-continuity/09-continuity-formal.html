Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Continuity With Precision

    The formal (ε–δ-free at this level: sequential/interval) definition of continuity replaces the pen picture with quantified statements. This topic states the precise definition, works the algebra of continuity, and frames the Intermediate and Extreme Value theorems.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The precise statement

    • f is continuous at a ⟺ for every ε > 0 there exists δ > 0 such that |x − a| < δ ⟹ |f(x) − f(a)| < ε (x in the domain).
    • Reading: however small a tolerance ε you demand of the output, a window δ of the input delivers it — the stability contract with numbers attached.

    2.2 Sequential characterisation

    • Theorem: f continuous at a ⟺ for every sequence xₙ → a (in the domain), f(xₙ) → f(a).
    • This converts continuity into sequence bookkeeping — and gives the fastest discontinuity proofs: find one sequence xₙ → a with f(xₙ) escaping f(a).

    2.3 Algebra of continuity

    • Sums, products, quotients (non-zero denominator), compositions: continuous — the closure theorems proved once, used everywhere. Polynomials continuous everywhere; |f| continuous when f is.

    2.4 The big interval theorems (statements)

    • Intermediate Value Theorem (IVT): f continuous on [a, b], u between f(a) and f(b) ⟹ some c ∈ (a, b) has f(c) = u — continuous functions cannot skip values.
    • Extreme Value Theorem (EVT): f continuous on a closed bounded [a, b] attains a maximum and a minimum.
    • Both need closed + bounded + continuous — drop any and counterexamples appear.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 What ε–δ buys

    • The pen picture cannot say how continuous; ε–δ quantifies it. Uniform continuity, approximation rates and numerical analysis all live downstream of this precision.

    4.2 The IVT as a root-finding licence

    • If f(a) and f(b) have opposite signs, u = 0 lies between ⟹ a root exists in (a, b). Bisection methods are IVT turned into an algorithm.

    4.3 Why EVT needs closed and bounded

    • On (0, 1], f(x) = 1/x has no maximum; on [0, 1], f(x) = x for x < 1, f(1) = 0 (discontinuous) has no maximum. Closed + bounded + continuous is exactly the insurance EVT demands.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    IVT needs continuity on the whole interval: a single break can let f skip the target value.

  • EVT fails on open intervals: (0, 1) with f(x) = x has sup 1 but no maximum — "attained" is the operative word.
  • Sequential discontinuity proofs need only one bad sequence: a single xₙ → a with f(xₙ) ↛ f(a) kills continuity.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Show that f(x) = x³ − 4x + 1 has a root between 0 and 1. — f is a polynomial ⟹ continuous on [0, 1]. f(0) = 1 > 0; f(1) = 1 − 4 + 1 = −2 < 0. Since 0 lies between f(1) = −2 and f(0) = 1, the IVT supplies c ∈ (0, 1) with f(c) = 0 ∎ — sign change plus continuity equals existence of a root.

  • NEB-style (2m): Prove f(x) = 1/x is discontinuous at 0 using sequences. — Take xₙ = 1/n → 0 (each xₙ in the domain's punctured neighbourhood). Then f(xₙ) = n → ∞, so f(xₙ) does not converge to any finite value, let alone f(0) (undefined). By the sequential characterisation, f is not continuous at 0 — one escaping sequence suffices.
  • NEB-style (2m): State the Extreme Value Theorem and give a counterexample when the interval is open. — EVT: a function continuous on a closed, bounded interval [a, b] attains an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum on it. Counterexample on (0, 1): f(x) = x is continuous on the open interval but attains neither 0 nor 1 — for every x ∈ (0, 1) there are larger and smaller values; the sup 1 and inf 0 are never reached. Closedness is the hypothesis that turns "sup" into "max".

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — Epsilon as a Challenge, Delta as the Answer

    • The definition is a game: the skeptic names any tolerance ε > 0 (however tiny); the prover must produce a window δ > 0 so that staying within δ of a keeps f within ε of L. Continuity at a = winning this game for every ε.
    • Why "for every ε" matters: one ε is an accident, finitely many is a lucky stretch — only all-of-them-at-once pins the approach. The order of quantifiers ( ∀ε ∃δ ) is the definition's engine; swapping it ( ∃ε ∀δ ) describes a function stuck at a distance — a jump.
    • Delta may depend on epsilon: smaller ε demands smaller δ — the working recipe (preliminary window, then refine) exists precisely because δ is chosen after seeing ε. A single δ working for all ε would force f to be constant near a.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — The Algebra of Continuity Is Inherited, Not Re-proved

    • Once the base functions are certified continuous (polynomials by limit laws, sin/cos by the stars, roots on their domains), the closure theorems do the rest: sums, products, quotients (where defined), and compositions of continuous functions are continuous — no new ε–δ proofs needed.
    • Why composition transmits continuity: f continuous at b, g continuous at b's preimage a ⟹ x near a sends g(x) near b, which sends f(g(x)) near f(b). Continuity chains through machines — this is the licence behind every "evaluate the inner limit, apply the outer function" step.
    • Rational functions: continuous on their whole domain — the domain's holes are exclusions, not discontinuities. 1/x is continuous on ℝ \ {0}; saying "1/x is discontinuous at 0" confuses "not defined" with "defined but broken."

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — Formal Continuity Locates the Theorems' Power

    • The EVT and IVT are stated for continuous functions on closed bounded intervals — the ε–δ definition is what makes "continuous" precise enough for their proofs, and compactness (closed + bounded) is what makes the guarantees unconditional.
    • Continuity is local, theorems are global: the definition speaks of a single point; the big theorems harvest whole intervals of points. The bridge is that continuity at every point of [a, b] — a local property held universally — yields global conclusions (boundedness, attained extrema, intermediate values).
    • Failure modes map to missing hypotheses: drop closedness (f = 1/x on (0,1]) and boundedness fails; drop continuity (a step function) and IVT fails. Each theorem's hypotheses are exactly the guards against a named counterexample.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Show that the equation x³ − 4x + 1 = 0 has a root between 0 and 1. — Hypothesis check: f(x) = x³ − 4x + 1 is a polynomial, hence continuous on the closed interval [0, 1] — the IVT's standing requirement. Endpoint values: f(0) = 0 − 0 + 1 = 1 > 0; f(1) = 1 − 4 + 1 = −2 < 0. Sign change: 0 lies between f(1) = −2 and f(0) = 1. Conclusion (IVT): there exists c ∈ (0, 1) with f(c) = 0 — the equation has a root between 0 and 1. ∎ (Bisection would localise it: f(0.25) ≈ 0.016 > 0, f(0.26) ≈ −0.058 < 0 — the root is near 0.254, but existence needed only the two endpoint signs.)
  • NEB-style (2m): Using sequences, prove that f(x) = 1/x is discontinuous at x = 0. — Sequential characterisation: f is continuous at a iff every sequence xₙ → a (with xₙ in the domain) satisfies f(xₙ) → f(a); one counterexample sequence disproves continuity. The sequence: xₙ = 1/n → 0, and every xₙ ≠ 0 (so each is in the domain). Its images: f(xₙ) = 1/(1/n) = n → ∞ — the image sequence diverges to infinity, hence converges to no real number, and in particular cannot converge to f(0) (which does not even exist). Conclusion: f is not continuous at 0 ∎. (Note the pattern: to disprove continuity, one escaping sequence is enough; to prove it, all sequences must cooperate — asymmetry worth remembering.)
  • NEB-style (2m): State the Extreme Value Theorem and show it fails on the open interval (0, 1). — Theorem (EVT): if f is continuous on a closed and bounded interval [a, b], then f attains an absolute maximum value and an absolute minimum value at points of [a, b]. Counterexample on (0, 1): take f(x) = x, continuous on the open interval (0, 1). For any x ∈ (0, 1), the value (x + 1)/2 ∈ (0, 1) satisfies f((x+1)/2) > f(x) — so no point achieves a maximum; similarly (x/2) beats every candidate for the minimum downward. The supremum 1 and infimum 0 exist as bounds but are never attained. Moral: closedness is exactly the hypothesis that converts "least upper bound" into "maximum" — drop it and EVT collapses.
  • NEB-style (6m): Prove that f(x) = 3x + 1 is continuous at x = 2 using the epsilon–delta definition. — Target: for every ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 2| < δ ⟹ |f(x) − 5| < ε (note L = f(2) = 7? — compute: f(2) = 7, so target |f(x) − 7| < ε). Compute the gap: |f(x) − 7| = |3x + 1 − 7| = |3x − 6| = 3|x − 2| — the gap is a constant multiple of the input distance. Choose δ: require 3|x − 2| < ε, i.e. |x − 2| < ε/3 ⟹ δ = ε/3 works. Verify: if |x − 2| < δ = ε/3, then |f(x) − 7| = 3|x − 2| < 3(ε/3) = ε ✓ — for every ε, a δ exists ⟹ continuous at 2. Deep point: the proof's whole structure is visible in the gap computation — for linear functions the gap factors into (slope)·(input distance), so δ = ε/|slope| always; the ε–δ game is solved by expressing the output distance in terms of the input distance and reversing the inequality.
  • NEB-style (6m): Prove that f(x) = x² is continuous at x = 2 using the epsilon–delta definition. — Target: for every ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 2| < δ ⟹ |x² − 4| < ε. Factor the gap: |x² − 4| = |x − 2||x + 2| — the |x + 2| factor is the complication (it depends on x). Preliminary window: first restrict |x − 2| < 1, so 1 < x < 3 ⟹ 3 < x + 2 < 5 ⟹ |x + 2| < 5. Bound the gap inside the window: |x² − 4| < 5|x − 2|. Choose δ: require 5|x − 2| < ε ⟹ δ = min(1, ε/5). Verify: if |x − 2| < δ, then |x + 2| < 5 (window holds) and |x² − 4| = |x−2||x+2| < 5·(ε/5) = ε ✓. Deep point: the preliminary window trick — cap δ by a constant first to bound the awkward factor, then shrink δ further for the ε demand — is the universal recipe for nonlinear gaps; δ = min(1, ε/5) encodes both constraints in one choice.
  • NEB-style (5m): Using the formal definition's logic, show that f(x) = 1/x is continuous at x = 3, identifying the gap factor and the δ choice. — Target: for every ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 3| < δ ⟹ |1/x − 1/3| < ε. Compute the gap: |1/x − 1/3| = |(3 − x)/(3x)| = |x − 3|/(3|x|). Preliminary window: restrict |x − 3| < 1 ⟹ 2 < x < 4 ⟹ |x| > 2 ⟹ 1/(3|x|) < 1/6. Bound: |1/x − 1/3| < (1/6)|x − 3|. Choose δ = min(1, 6ε): then |1/x − 1/3| < (1/6)(6ε) = ε ✓. Deep points: (1) the gap factored into (input distance) × (a bounded factor), reproducing the linear-function recipe once the window tamed 1/|x|; (2) the continuity holds at x = 3 only because x ≠ 0 nearby — the same function at 0 has no value and no continuity claim; the domain exclusion is what keeps the preliminary window's lower bound |x| > 2 possible.
  • NEB-style (6m): For f(x) = x² for x ≤ 1, f(x) = ax + b for x > 1, find a and b so that f is continuous at 1, and justify via the three-part test. — Left side: the first piece is a polynomial ⟹ LHL = 1² = 1, and f(1) = 1 (≤ puts 1 in the first piece). Right side: RHL = lim_{x→1⁺} (ax + b) = a + b (polynomial ⟹ substitution). Continuity demands: RHL = LHL = f(1) ⟹ a + b = 1. One equation, two unknowns: continuity at this single joint constrains only the sum — e.g. a = 2, b = −1; or a = 0, b = 1 (flat right piece); or a = 3, b = −2. Verify one choice (a = 2, b = −1): RHL = 2 − 1 = 1 = LHL = f(1) ✓ — all three parts pass. Deep point: one joint imposes one equation; the family of continuous weldings is a line in (a, b)-space. Extra demands (differentiability at the joint, another joint's continuity) add equations — continuity conditions are always linear constraints on the parameters, which is why "find the constants" questions are systems of equations in disguise.

Formulas

    • ∀ε > 0 ∃δ > 0: |x − a| < δ ⟹ |f(x) − f(a)| < ε
    • IVT: f ∈ C[a,b], u ∈ [f(a), f(b)] ⟹ ∃c: f(c) = u
    • EVT: f ∈ C[a,b] ⟹ max and min attained
    • sequential: xₙ → a ⟹ f(xₙ) → f(a)

Key Points

  • Precise continuity: for every eps > 0 there is delta > 0 with |x - a| < delta => |f(x) - f(a)| < eps. Sequential theorem: f continuous at a iff x_n -> a implies f(x_n) -> f(a); one escaping sequence disproves continuity. Closure theorems: sums, products, quotients (denominator != 0), compositions continuous. IVT: f continuous on [a, b], u between f(a) and f(b) => f(c) = u for some c in (a, b) - sign change gives roots. EVT: continuous on closed bounded [a, b] attains max and min; open intervals or discontinuities break it (x on (0,1), 1/x blow-up).

Summary

Formal continuity quantifies the pen test: for every ε > 0 there is δ > 0 with |x − a| < δ ⟹ |f(x) − f(a)| < ε — a stability contract with numbers. The sequential characterisation (xₙ → a ⟹ f(xₙ) → f(a)) turns checks into bookkeeping and gives the fastest refutations: one escaping sequence (1/n for 1/x at 0) kills continuity. Closure theorems — sums, products, quotients, compositions — build continuous expressions from continuous parts. The interval theorems need closed + bounded + continuous: the IVT guarantees every intermediate value (sign change ⟹ root — the bisection engine; x³ − 4x + 1 crosses zero in (0, 1)), and the EVT guarantees attained max and min (f(x) = x on (0, 1) reaches neither 0 nor 1 — openness breaks it). Precision, sequences, closure, IVT, EVT: continuity's full formal wardrobe.

The supremum 1 and infimum 0 exist as bounds but are never attained. Moral: closedness is exactly the hypothesis that converts "least upper bound" into "maximum" — drop it and EVT collapses.
  • NEB-style (6m): Prove that f(x) = 3x + 1 is continuous at x = 2 using the epsilon–delta definition. — Target: for every ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 2| < δ ⟹ |f(x) − 5| < ε (note L = f(2) = 7? — compute: f(2) = 7, so target |f(x) − 7| < ε). Compute the gap: |f(x) − 7| = |3x + 1 − 7| = |3x − 6| = 3|x − 2| — the gap is a constant multiple of the input distance. Choose δ: require 3|x − 2| < ε, i.e. |x − 2| < ε/3 ⟹ δ = ε/3 works. Verify: if |x − 2| < δ = ε/3, then |f(x) − 7| = 3|x − 2| < 3(ε/3) = ε ✓ — for every ε, a δ exists ⟹ continuous at 2. Deep point: the proof's whole structure is visible in the gap computation — for linear functions the gap factors into (slope)·(input distance), so δ = ε/|slope| always; the ε–δ game is solved by expressing the output distance in terms of the input distance and reversing the inequality.
  • NEB-style (6m): Prove that f(x) = x² is continuous at x = 2 using the epsilon–delta definition. — Target: for every ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 2| < δ ⟹ |x² − 4| < ε. Factor the gap: |x² − 4| = |x − 2||x + 2| — the |x + 2| factor is the complication (it depends on x). Preliminary window: first restrict |x − 2| < 1, so 1 < x < 3 ⟹ 3 < x + 2 < 5 ⟹ |x + 2| < 5. Bound the gap inside the window: |x² − 4| < 5|x − 2|. Choose δ: require 5|x − 2| < ε ⟹ δ = min(1, ε/5). Verify: if |x − 2| < δ, then |x + 2| < 5 (window holds) and |x² − 4| = |x−2||x+2| < 5·(ε/5) = ε ✓. Deep point: the preliminary window trick — cap δ by a constant first to bound the awkward factor, then shrink δ further for the ε demand — is the universal recipe for nonlinear gaps; δ = min(1, ε/5) encodes both constraints in one choice.
  • NEB-style (5m): Using the formal definition's logic, show that f(x) = 1/x is continuous at x = 3, identifying the gap factor and the δ choice. — Target: for every ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 3| < δ ⟹ |1/x − 1/3| < ε. Compute the gap: |1/x − 1/3| = |(3 − x)/(3x)| = |x − 3|/(3|x|). Preliminary window: restrict |x − 3| < 1 ⟹ 2 < x < 4 ⟹ |x| > 2 ⟹ 1/(3|x|) < 1/6. Bound: |1/x − 1/3| < (1/6)|x − 3|. Choose δ = min(1, 6ε): then |1/x − 1/3| < (1/6)(6ε) = ε ✓. Deep points: (1) the gap factored into (input distance) × (a bounded factor), reproducing the linear-function recipe once the window tamed 1/|x|; (2) the continuity holds at x = 3 only because x ≠ 0 nearby — the same function at 0 has no value and no continuity claim; the domain exclusion is what keeps the preliminary window's lower bound |x| > 2 possible.
  • NEB-style (6m): For f(x) = x² for x ≤ 1, f(x) = ax + b for x > 1, find a and b so that f is continuous at 1, and justify via the three-part test. — Left side: the first piece is a polynomial ⟹ LHL = 1² = 1, and f(1) = 1 (≤ puts 1 in the first piece). Right side: RHL = lim_{x→1⁺} (ax + b) = a + b (polynomial ⟹ substitution). Continuity demands: RHL = LHL = f(1) ⟹ a + b = 1. One equation, two unknowns: continuity at this single joint constrains only the sum — e.g. a = 2, b = −1; or a = 0, b = 1 (flat right piece); or a = 3, b = −2. Verify one choice (a = 2, b = −1): RHL = 2 − 1 = 1 = LHL = f(1) ✓ — all three parts pass. Deep point: one joint imposes one equation; the family of continuous weldings is a line in (a, b)-space. Extra demands (differentiability at the joint, another joint's continuity) add equations — continuity conditions are always linear constraints on the parameters, which is why "find the constants" questions are systems of equations in disguise.
