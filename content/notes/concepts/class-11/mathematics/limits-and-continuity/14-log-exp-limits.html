Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — e, ln and the Exponential Star

    Logarithmic and exponential limits revolve around e: (1 + x)^{1/x} → e, (eˣ − 1)/x → 1, ln(1+x)/x → 1, and the 1^∞ form resolved by the exponential bridge lim f^g = e^{lim g·ln f}.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The e-limits

    • lim_{x→0} (1 + x)^{1/x} = e; twin: lim_{x→∞} (1 + 1/x)^x = e (u = 1/x bridges them).
    • General power: lim_{x→0} (1 + kx)^{1/x} = eᵏ — insert kx/kx.
    • lim_{x→0} (aˣ − 1)/x = ln a; a = e gives 1. lim_{x→0} ln(1 + x)/x = 1.

    2.2 The 1^∞ form and the bridge

    • 1^∞ is indeterminate (not 1!): f → 1, g → ∞ makes f^g undecided.
    • Bridge theorem: lim f(x)^{g(x)} = e^{lim g(x)·ln f(x)} whenever the exponent limit exists — because f^g = e^{g ln f} identically.
    • Standard resolution: for lim (1 + u)^{v} with u → 0, v → ∞: the limit is e^{lim uv}.

    2.3 Growth comparisons (theorems)

    • ln x ≪ xᵃ ≪ aˣ ≪ x! ≪ xˣ as x → ∞: logarithms lose to powers, powers to exponentials. Consequences: lim ln x/x = 0, lim xᵏ/eˣ = 0, lim x^{1/x} = 1.

    2.4 Reduction discipline

    • Rewrite exponentials via e and logs via ln (natural), then steer into the three stars: (eᵘ − 1)/u, ln(1+u)/u, (1+u)^{1/u}.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why (1+x)^{1/x} defines e

    • Compound interest compounded ever more finely: (1 + 1/n)ⁿ climbs to e ≈ 2.71828. The limit is the definition — e is the answer to continuous compounding.

    4.2 The bridge in action

    • Any f^g with f → 1, g → ∞: write f = 1 + u, so f^g = [(1+u)^{1/u}]^{uv} → e^{lim uv}. The whole 1^∞ technology is one exponent bookkeeping.

    4.3 The growth ladder as a limit generator

    • The ordering ln x ≪ xᵃ ≪ aˣ is a theorem (comparative growth rates); every "which dominates" limit is read off it: ln x/x → 0, x¹⁰⁰/eˣ → 0, x^{1/x} → 1.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    1^∞ ≠ 1: the form is indeterminate — (1 + 1/n)ⁿ → e ≈ 2.718, not 1.

  • (aˣ − 1)/x keeps ln a: only a = e kills the factor; general bases keep it.
  • Bridge needs natural logs: f^g = e^{g ln f} uses ln — converting log bases first is mandatory.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 + 3x)^{1/x}. — 1^∞ form (base → 1, exponent → ∞). Bridge: = e^{lim (1/x)·ln(1+3x)}; ln(1+3x) ~ 3x ⟹ exponent limit = 3x·(1/x)... lim (ln(1+3x)/x) = 3. Answer: e³. (Shortcut: (1 + kx)^{1/x} → eᵏ with k = 3.)

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (x + 1/x)^x... lim_{x→∞} ((x+1)/(x−1))^x. — Base → 1, exponent → ∞ ⟹ 1^∞. Write base = 1 + 2/(x−1); then u = 2/(x−1) → 0 and exponent x·u = 2x/(x−1) → 2. Limit = e². (Bridge: e^{lim x·ln((x+1)/(x−1))} with ln(1+u) ~ u gives the same e².)
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} x^{1/x}. — Bridge: x^{1/x} = e^{(ln x)/x}; by the growth ladder ln x/x → 0 ⟹ limit = e⁰ = 1. Powers beat logs even at the 1/x power.

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — e as the Fixed Point of the Rate Map

    • The exponential star (eᵘ − 1)/u → 1 says: at u = 0, the exponential's rate equals its value. Every other base a pays the correction factor ln a — (aᵘ − 1)/u → ln a — so e is the unique base whose growth rate is its own size. That self-replication is why e appears wherever rates compound naturally.
    • The log star is the mirror: ln(1+u)/u → 1 says the log's rate at 1 is 1 — the inverse of the exponential's property. The two stars are one fact read from both sides of the inverse pair.
    • The compound star closes the loop: (1+u)^{1/u} → e is the definition of e as the limit of compounding — continuously compounded growth. All three stars are the same constant seen as value, rate, and process.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Growth Races and the Hierarchy at Infinity

    • The ladder: ln x grows slowest, then xᵖ (any fixed power), then aˣ (any a > 1), then xˣ fastest. Each rung dominates the one below: aˣ/xⁿ → ∞ and ln x/xⁿ → 0 for every n, however large.
    • Why the ladder decides limits: in any quotient of unlike types at infinity, the higher rung wins and the limit is 0 or ∞ regardless of coefficients — x¹⁰⁰/eˣ → 0, ln(x¹⁰⁰)/x → 0. No algebra needed; only the rungs matter.
    • The mechanism: exponentials compound multiplicatively (each unit of x multiplies by a), powers additively (each unit adds a fixed increment) — multiplicative growth outruns additive growth eventually, always. Logs grow by diminishing increments, which is why they lose to everything.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — The Log as the Great Lineariser

    • ln converts multiplication to addition: ln(ab) = ln a + ln b. In limits this turns products of many factors into sums (where degree comparison works) and turns powers u^v into products v·ln u (where the 0·∞ machinery works).
    • Every power-form limit is a log limit: 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ all surrender to f^g = e^{g ln f}. The exponential bridge is not a trick but an isomorphism — it transports the question to addition-land, solves it there, and transports the answer back.
    • Domain discipline: logs demand positive arguments — one-sided limits at 0 (ln x → −∞ as x → 0⁺) and absolute values inside (ln|f| for f′/f patterns) are the standard repairs. A negative argument is not a small answer; it is no answer.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 + 3x)^{1/x}. — Form recognition: the base → 1 and the exponent 1/x → ∞ — the 1^∞ indeterminate form (not 1!). Bridge route: (1 + 3x)^{1/x} = e^{(1/x)·ln(1+3x)} identically, so the limit is e^L with L = lim_{x→0} ln(1+3x)/x. Compute L: ln(1 + u) ~ u with u = 3x gives ln(1+3x) ~ 3x, so L = lim 3x/x = 3 (equivalently by the standard limit ln(1+u)/u → 1 with the kx/kx insertion). Answer: e³. Shortcut confirmed: the general theorem (1 + kx)^{1/x} → eᵏ with k = 3 — the bridge and the shortcut agree.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} ((x + 1)/(x − 1))^x. — Form: base (x+1)/(x−1) = 1 + 2/(x−1) → 1 and exponent x → ∞ ⟹ 1^∞. Bridge: with u = 2/(x−1) → 0, the expression is (1 + u)^x, and the theorem (1+u)^v → e^{lim uv} applies with v = x: compute lim x·u = lim 2x/(x−1) = 2. Answer: e². Cross-check by the full bridge: x·ln((x+1)/(x−1)) = x·ln(1 + 2/(x−1)) ~ x·2/(x−1) → 2, so e² ✓. The entire 1^∞ technology reduces to exponent bookkeeping: find lim uv.
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} x^{1/x}. — Bridge: x^{1/x} = e^{(ln x)/x} identically — the exponent is the contest between log and power. Growth ladder: ln x ≪ xᵃ for any a > 0; in particular ln x/x → 0 as x → ∞ (logs lose to powers). Conclusion: the exponent → 0, so the limit is e⁰ = 1. Reading: even the tiniest positive power of x outgrows the logarithm, so taking the 1/x-th power of x flattens it to 1 — the growth ladder settles infinity contests without any ε-work.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{5x} − 1)/(ln(1 + 3x)), annotating the stars. — Form check: substitute x = 0: (1 − 1)/ln 1 = 0/0 ✓. Numerator (star (eᵘ−1)/u → 1, u = 5x → 0): e^{5x} − 1 ~ 5x ⟹ (e^{5x} − 1)/(5x) → 1. Denominator (star ln(1+u)/u → 1, u = 3x → 0): ln(1 + 3x) ~ 3x ⟹ ln(1+3x)/(3x) → 1. Assemble: the expression = [(e^{5x}−1)/(5x)]·[(3x)/ln(1+3x)]·(5/3) → 1·1·(5/3) = 5/3. Order reading: both contestants vanish at order 1 with coefficients 5 and 3 ⟹ the ratio of coefficients 5/3 was predictable before the stars. Deep point: the exponential and logarithmic stars are inverse-pair twins — each converts its function to its linear approximation at the anchor point, and same-order ratios reduce to coefficient arithmetic.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (eˣ + 3x²)/(2eˣ − x). — Identify the race: exponential (top rung) vs polynomial — the ladder says eˣ dominates any fixed power ⟹ the eˣ terms decide. Divide by eˣ (the dominant term): (eˣ + 3x²)/(2eˣ − x) = (1 + 3x²/eˣ)/(2 − x/eˣ). Apply the ladder: x²/eˣ → 0 (exponential beats any power) and x/eˣ → 0. Limit: (1 + 0)/(2 − 0) = 1/2. Cross-check by ladder logic alone: numerator ~ eˣ (leading term), denominator ~ 2eˣ ⟹ ratio ~ 1/2 ✓. Deep point: dividing by the dominant term is the standard move when rungs differ — it converts the race into (tiny)/(constant) bookkeeping; the coefficients 1 and 2 on the eˣ terms are the only survivors, exactly as the ladder predicts.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} x·ln x, and use the result to find lim_{x→0⁺} x^{x}. — Part 1 — the 0·∞ form: x·ln x is indeterminate (0 times −∞). Flip to a quotient: x ln x = ln x/(1/x) — now −∞/∞ ⟹ L'Hôpital applies (both → ∞ in magnitude): differentiate separately — (1/x)/(−1/x²) = −x → 0. So lim_{x→0⁺} x ln x = 0 — the log's slow dive loses to x's collapse. Part 2 — the power form: xˣ = e^{x ln x} ⟹ lim xˣ = e^{lim x ln x} = e⁰ = 1. Deep points: (1) the flip choice matters — writing x/(1/ln x) instead would differentiate to a worse form; flip the factor that differentiates toward simplicity (ln x → 1/x is the friendly derivative); (2) the growth ladder at a point: even at 0⁺, ln's sluggishness loses the race, and the exponential bridge converts that race's verdict into the power's limit.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (ln(3x² + 1))/(ln(x⁴ + 2)). — Extract leading behaviour: ln(3x² + 1) = ln(x²(3 + 1/x²)) = 2 ln x + ln(3 + 1/x²) → 2 ln x + ln 3 (the correction → ln 3, bounded). Similarly ln(x⁴ + 2) = 4 ln x + ln(1 + 2/x⁴) → 4 ln x + 0. Form the ratio: (2 ln x + ln 3)/(4 ln x + ln(1+2/x⁴)) = [2 + ln 3/ln x]/[4 + ln(1+2/x⁴)/ln x] → (2 + 0)/(4 + 0) = 1/2 as ln x → ∞. Shortcut via degrees: inside logs, only the degree of the polynomial matters — ln(f) ~ deg(f)·ln x ⟹ the limit is 2/4 = 1/2 ✓. Deep point: the log's linearity on powers (ln xᵏ = k ln x) makes log-of-polynomial limits pure degree arithmetic — the coefficients and lower terms vanish because ln x → ∞ drowns every constant, the mirror image of the polynomial-at-infinity degree rule.

Summary

Exponential and logarithmic limits orbit e. The e-limits: (1+x)^{1/x} → e (x → 0), its twin (1 + 1/x)^x → e (x → ∞), the rescaled (1 + kx)^{1/x} → eᵏ (NEB-style: k = 3 gives e³), (eˣ − 1)/x → 1, (aˣ − 1)/x → ln a, ln(1+x)/x → 1. The 1^∞ form is indeterminate — and the bridge theorem f^g = e^{g ln f} resolves it: for (1+u)^v with u → 0, v → ∞ the limit is e^{lim uv} (((x+1)/(x−1))^x → e² since 2x/(x−1) → 2). The growth ladder ln x ≪ xᵃ ≪ aˣ ≪ x! ≪ xˣ reads off every dominance contest: ln x/x → 0, xᵏ/eˣ → 0, and x^{1/x} → 1 (the log loses even at fractional power). Convert to natural exp and log, steer into the stars, bridge the powers: the whole topic is e's biography.

Shortcut confirmed: the general theorem (1 + kx)^{1/x} → eᵏ with k = 3 — the bridge and the shortcut agree.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} ((x + 1)/(x − 1))^x. — Form: base (x+1)/(x−1) = 1 + 2/(x−1) → 1 and exponent x → ∞ ⟹ 1^∞. Bridge: with u = 2/(x−1) → 0, the expression is (1 + u)^x, and the theorem (1+u)^v → e^{lim uv} applies with v = x: compute lim x·u = lim 2x/(x−1) = 2. Answer: e². Cross-check by the full bridge: x·ln((x+1)/(x−1)) = x·ln(1 + 2/(x−1)) ~ x·2/(x−1) → 2, so e² ✓. The entire 1^∞ technology reduces to exponent bookkeeping: find lim uv.
  • NEB-style (2m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} x^{1/x}. — Bridge: x^{1/x} = e^{(ln x)/x} identically — the exponent is the contest between log and power. Growth ladder: ln x ≪ xᵃ for any a > 0; in particular ln x/x → 0 as x → ∞ (logs lose to powers). Conclusion: the exponent → 0, so the limit is e⁰ = 1. Reading: even the tiniest positive power of x outgrows the logarithm, so taking the 1/x-th power of x flattens it to 1 — the growth ladder settles infinity contests without any ε-work.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{5x} − 1)/(ln(1 + 3x)), annotating the stars. — Form check: substitute x = 0: (1 − 1)/ln 1 = 0/0 ✓. Numerator (star (eᵘ−1)/u → 1, u = 5x → 0): e^{5x} − 1 ~ 5x ⟹ (e^{5x} − 1)/(5x) → 1. Denominator (star ln(1+u)/u → 1, u = 3x → 0): ln(1 + 3x) ~ 3x ⟹ ln(1+3x)/(3x) → 1. Assemble: the expression = [(e^{5x}−1)/(5x)]·[(3x)/ln(1+3x)]·(5/3) → 1·1·(5/3) = 5/3. Order reading: both contestants vanish at order 1 with coefficients 5 and 3 ⟹ the ratio of coefficients 5/3 was predictable before the stars. Deep point: the exponential and logarithmic stars are inverse-pair twins — each converts its function to its linear approximation at the anchor point, and same-order ratios reduce to coefficient arithmetic.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (eˣ + 3x²)/(2eˣ − x). — Identify the race: exponential (top rung) vs polynomial — the ladder says eˣ dominates any fixed power ⟹ the eˣ terms decide. Divide by eˣ (the dominant term): (eˣ + 3x²)/(2eˣ − x) = (1 + 3x²/eˣ)/(2 − x/eˣ). Apply the ladder: x²/eˣ → 0 (exponential beats any power) and x/eˣ → 0. Limit: (1 + 0)/(2 − 0) = 1/2. Cross-check by ladder logic alone: numerator ~ eˣ (leading term), denominator ~ 2eˣ ⟹ ratio ~ 1/2 ✓. Deep point: dividing by the dominant term is the standard move when rungs differ — it converts the race into (tiny)/(constant) bookkeeping; the coefficients 1 and 2 on the eˣ terms are the only survivors, exactly as the ladder predicts.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} x·ln x, and use the result to find lim_{x→0⁺} x^{x}. — Part 1 — the 0·∞ form: x·ln x is indeterminate (0 times −∞). Flip to a quotient: x ln x = ln x/(1/x) — now −∞/∞ ⟹ L'Hôpital applies (both → ∞ in magnitude): differentiate separately — (1/x)/(−1/x²) = −x → 0. So lim_{x→0⁺} x ln x = 0 — the log's slow dive loses to x's collapse. Part 2 — the power form: xˣ = e^{x ln x} ⟹ lim xˣ = e^{lim x ln x} = e⁰ = 1. Deep points: (1) the flip choice matters — writing x/(1/ln x) instead would differentiate to a worse form; flip the factor that differentiates toward simplicity (ln x → 1/x is the friendly derivative); (2) the growth ladder at a point: even at 0⁺, ln's sluggishness loses the race, and the exponential bridge converts that race's verdict into the power's limit.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (ln(3x² + 1))/(ln(x⁴ + 2)). — Extract leading behaviour: ln(3x² + 1) = ln(x²(3 + 1/x²)) = 2 ln x + ln(3 + 1/x²) → 2 ln x + ln 3 (the correction → ln 3, bounded). Similarly ln(x⁴ + 2) = 4 ln x + ln(1 + 2/x⁴) → 4 ln x + 0. Form the ratio: (2 ln x + ln 3)/(4 ln x + ln(1+2/x⁴)) = [2 + ln 3/ln x]/[4 + ln(1+2/x⁴)/ln x] → (2 + 0)/(4 + 0) = 1/2 as ln x → ∞. Shortcut via degrees: inside logs, only the degree of the polynomial matters — ln(f) ~ deg(f)·ln x ⟹ the limit is 2/4 = 1/2 ✓. Deep point: the log's linearity on powers (ln xᵏ = k ln x) makes log-of-polynomial limits pure degree arithmetic — the coefficients and lower terms vanish because ln x → ∞ drowns every constant, the mirror image of the polynomial-at-infinity degree rule.
