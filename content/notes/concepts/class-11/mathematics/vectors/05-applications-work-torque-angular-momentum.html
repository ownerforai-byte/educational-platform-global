Key Points

Torque: tau = r x F; magnitude r F sin th = moment arm x force; axial vector by right-hand rule; unit N.m; zero when force line passes through the pivot. Angular momentum: L = r x p; conserved when net torque is zero (tau = dL/dt). Parallelogram law of forces: resultant magnitude sqrt(F1^2 + F2^2 + 2F1F2 cos alpha). Component route: torque from determinant |i j k; rx ry rz; Fx Fy Fz|.

Summary

Vectors run mechanics. Work is the dot product W = F·d = Fd cos θ — a signed scalar, zero for perpendicular force, measured in joules (10 N at 60° over 5 m gives 25 J). Torque is the cross product τ = r × F, magnitude rF sin θ = moment arm × force, an axial vector in N·m, zero when the force's line passes through the pivot (the hinge principle); computed from components as a determinant (3î+4ĵ N at 2î+ĵ gives 5k̂ N·m). Angular momentum L = r × p is conserved whenever net torque vanishes — the theorem behind Kepler's second law. The parallelogram law √(F₁² + F₂² + 2F₁F₂cos α) closes the statics bridge. Dot for along, cross for across: the whole topic in six words.

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Vectors at Work (Literally)

    Physics runs on the vector products: work W = F·d (dot), torque τ = r × F (cross), angular momentum L = r × p (cross). Each formula is a theorem about components — and each has a syllabus-standard numerical type.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Work — the dot product

    • Theorem: W = F·d = |F||d|cos θ = F d cos θ, where θ is the angle between force and displacement.
    • Only the force component along the displacement earns work: W = (F cos θ)d. Perpendicular force does zero work (θ = 90°).
    • SI unit: joule (J = N·m). Sign: positive (assisting), zero (perpendicular), negative (opposing — friction).

    2.2 Torque — the cross product

    • Theorem: τ = r × F, magnitude τ = r F sin θ = (moment arm) × force; direction by the right-hand rule (along the rotation axis).
    • Moment arm: the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force's line of action — d = r sin θ. Force along the line through the pivot (θ = 0) gives zero torque.
    • SI unit: newton-metre (N·m) — dimensionally a joule, but never called one: torque is a vector about an axis.

    2.3 Angular momentum — the cross product

    • Theorem: L = r × p = r p sin θ n̂, for position r and momentum p = mv.
    • Conservation theorem: if the net torque is zero, L is constant (τ = dL/dt) — planetary orbits and spinning skaters live here.

    2.4 The parallelogram law of forces (statics bridge)

    • Two forces at a point have resultant R = F₁ + F₂ with magnitude √(F₁² + F₂² + 2F₁F₂cos α), α the angle between them — the vector triangle in magnitudes.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why dot for work, cross for torque

    • Work asks "how much force along the motion" — a projection (cos θ, dot). Torque asks "how much force across the lever" — a perpendicular measure (sin θ, cross). The two products split physics between them.

    4.2 The zero cases are the examiners' favourites

    • Force perpendicular to displacement: zero work (circular motion's centripetal force). Force through the pivot: zero torque (door pushed at the hinge). Both are direct theorem readings.

    4.3 Sign and direction discipline

    • Work is a scalar with sign; torque and angular momentum are axial vectors — their direction (into/out of the page) is chosen by the right-hand rule and matters in stated answers.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    N·m for torque is not a joule: same dimensions, different quantity — torque is a vector about an axis, energy a scalar.

  • Moment arm is perpendicular distance: r sin θ, not r — the full radius only works at θ = 90°.
  • Negative work is real: friction doing negative work removes kinetic energy; the sign is information.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): A force of 10 N acts at 60° to a displacement of 5 m. Find the work done. — W = F d cos θ = 10 × 5 × cos 60° = 10 × 5 × ½ = 25 J. Only the component F cos θ = 5 N along the displacement contributes; the perpendicular 5√3 N component does nothing.

  • NEB-style (3m): A force F = 3î + 4ĵ N acts at the point r = 2î + ĵ m relative to a pivot. Find the torque about the pivot. — τ = r × F = |î ĵ k̂; 2 1 0; 3 4 0| = k̂[(2)(4) − (1)(3)] = 5k̂ N·m — magnitude 5 N·m, direction out of the plane (right-hand rule). The z-zero components make the cross product a pure k̂ vector: the rotation is about the z-axis.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why does a door open easiest when pushed at the edge farthest from the hinge? — Torque τ = r F sin θ: for a fixed force applied perpendicular to the door (sin θ = 1), τ is proportional to r, the distance from the hinge. The far edge maximises r, hence the torque — the moment-arm theorem in everyday mechanics.

Universal Facts

  • A planet keeps orbiting because the Sun's force points along r: zero torque, conserved angular momentum - Kepler's second law in one line.

Examples

  • The hinge theorem — a door's handle is far from the hinge because torque scales with r: same push, more turn.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): A force of 10 N acts at 60° to a displacement of 5 m. Find the work done. — Formula: W = F·d = F d cos θ, the dot product of force and displacement. Substitute: W = 10 × 5 × cos 60° = 50 × ½ = 25 J. Reading: the force component along the displacement is F cos θ = 10 cos 60° = 5 N; only this component earns energy, while the perpendicular component F sin θ = 5√3 N does zero work (θ = 90° case of the theorem). The answer is positive because the force assists the motion.
  • NEB-style (3m): A force F = 3î + 4ĵ N acts at a point with position vector r = 2î + ĵ m from the pivot. Find the torque about the pivot. — Formula: τ = r × F. Determinant: |î ĵ k̂; 2 1 0; 3 4 0| = î[(1)(0) − (0)(4)] − ĵ[(2)(0) − (0)(3)] + k̂[(2)(4) − (1)(3)] = 0î − 0ĵ + 5k̂. Answer: τ = 5k̂ N·m — magnitude 5 N·m directed along the positive z-axis (out of the plane), by the right-hand rule. Since all r and F vectors lie in the xy-plane, the torque is purely axial about z — the determinant's î and ĵ components vanish exactly because rz = Fz = 0.
  • NEB-style (2m): Explain why a force directed along the line joining the pivot to its point of application produces no torque. — Torque is τ = r × F with magnitude rF sin θ, where θ is the angle between the position vector r and the force F. If F points along the line through the pivot, then θ = 0° or 180° and sin θ = 0, so |τ| = 0 — the force's line of action passes through the pivot and its moment arm (perpendicular distance r sin θ) is zero. Physically: pushing a door directly at the hinge cannot rotate it, no matter how hard you push. The same theorem explains why central forces (gravity along r) give planets zero torque and conserved angular momentum.
