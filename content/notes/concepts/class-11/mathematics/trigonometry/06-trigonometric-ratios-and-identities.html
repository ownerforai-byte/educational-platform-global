Notes

  • 1. Basic Trigonometric Ratios

  • In a right triangle with angle theta:
    • sin theta = opposite/hypotenuse
    • cos theta = adjacent/hypotenuse
    • tan theta = opposite/adjacent = sin theta/cos theta
    • cosec theta = 1/sin theta, sec theta = 1/cos theta, cot theta = 1/tan theta

  • 2. Key Values

  • | theta | 0 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 90 |
  • | sin theta | 0 | 1/2 | 1/sqrt(2) | sqrt(3)/2 | 1 |
  • | cos theta | 1 | sqrt(3)/2 | 1/sqrt(2) | 1/2 | 0 |
  • | tan theta | 0 | 1/sqrt(3) | 1 | sqrt(3) | undefined |

  • 3. Fundamental Identities

    • sin^2 theta + cos^2 theta = 1
    • 1 + tan^2 theta = sec^2 theta
    • 1 + cot^2 theta = cosec^2 theta

  • 4. Compound Angle Formulas

    • sin(A +/- B) = sin A cos B +/- cos A sin B
    • cos(A +/- B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
    • tan(A +/- B) = (tan A +/- tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B)

  • 5. Double Angle Formulas

    • sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
    • cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A = 2cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2sin^2 A
    • tan 2A = 2tan A/(1 - tan^2 A)

  • 6. Entrance Exam Focus

  • Prove identities; Solve trig equations; Use compound/double angle formulas.

Summary

The six trigonometric ratios come from a right triangle: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and tan θ = sin θ/cos θ, with cosec, sec and cot their reciprocals, and their special values at 0°, 30°, 45°, 60° and 90° are tabulated. Three fundamental identities tie the ratios together: sin^2 θ + cos^2 θ = 1, 1 + tan^2 θ = sec^2 θ and 1 + cot^2 θ = cosec^2 θ. From these come the compound angle formulas sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B, cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B and tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B), and the double angle formulas sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A and cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A.

Important Concepts

  • Ratios in a right triangle: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and tan θ = opposite/adjacent = sin θ/cos θ.
  • Reciprocal ratios: cosec θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ and cot θ = 1/tan θ = cos θ/sin θ.
  • The identity sin^2 θ + cos^2 θ = 1 and its two rearrangements 1 + tan^2 θ = sec^2 θ and 1 + cot^2 θ = cosec^2 θ.
  • Compound angles: sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B and cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B, where the two signs are always opposite for cosine.
  • Double angles as the case B = A: sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A, cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A = 2cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2sin^2 A, and tan 2A = 2 tan A/(1 - tan^2 A).
  • Standard values: sin 30° = 1/2, sin 45° = 1/sqrt(2), sin 60° = sqrt(3)/2, with cosine running in the reverse order and tan 45° = 1, tan 30° = 1/sqrt(3), tan 60° = sqrt(3).

Important Statements

  • In a right triangle sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse and tan θ = opposite/adjacent, while cosec θ, sec θ and cot θ are the reciprocals of sin, cos and tan.
  • The fundamental identities are sin^2 θ + cos^2 θ = 1, 1 + tan^2 θ = sec^2 θ and 1 + cot^2 θ = cosec^2 θ.
  • The compound angle formulas are sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B, cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B and tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B).
  • The double angle formulas are sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A, cos 2A = cos^2 A - sin^2 A = 2cos^2 A - 1 = 1 - 2sin^2 A and tan 2A = 2 tan A/(1 - tan^2 A).
  • If sin θ = 3/5 for an acute angle then cos θ = 4/5 and tan θ = 3/4, so a single known ratio and the quadrant determine all the others.

Important Tasks

  • Given one trigonometric ratio and the quadrant of the angle, find all the other ratios by applying the relevant identity and the signs of that quadrant.
  • Prove an identity by starting from one side and transforming it, line by line, into the other side using the fundamental identities.
  • Evaluate sin(A + B), cos 2A or tan 2A when the ratios of A and B are given, choosing the form of the formula that needs the least work.
  • Solve a trigonometric equation in a stated interval, first reducing it to sin x = c, cos x = c or tan x = c and then listing every solution in 0° <= x <= 360°.

Key Points

  • sin(A + B) is not sin A + sin B; the compound angle formula must be used instead.
  • In the cosine formula the signs are opposite, so cos(A + B) = cos A cos B - sin A sin B while cos(A - B) = cos A cos B + sin A sin B.
  • cos 2A has three forms, and the useful one is the one matching what you are given: 2cos^2 A - 1 if you know cos A, or 1 - 2sin^2 A if you know sin A.
  • tan 2A = 2 tan A/(1 - tan^2 A), not 2 tan A, and it is undefined when tan A = ±1.
  • tan θ = sin θ/cos θ requires cos θ != 0, which is why tan 90° is undefined while sin 90° = 1 and cos 90° = 0.

Special Notes

  • sec 90° = 1/cos 90° and tan 90° = sin 90°/cos 90° are undefined because cos 90° = 0, and cosec 0° is undefined because sin 0° = 0.
  • The double angle formulas are just the compound angle formulas with B = A, so a proof of one family gives the other.
  • tan(A + B) needs 1 - tan A tan B != 0; when it is zero the sum is 90° (mod 180°) and the tangent is undefined even though sin and cos are well defined.
  • The identity sin(A + B) sin(A - B) = sin^2 A - sin^2 B is a product-to-sum result often asked as a proof, with the right side reached by expanding both factors.
  • Because the standard values are exact surds, exact answers should be left as fractions with surds rather than rounded decimals.

Exam Short Tricks

  • To get all the ratios from sin θ = 3/5, build the right triangle with hypotenuse 5, opposite 3 and adjacent 4, then read off cos = 4/5 and tan = 3/4.
  • For cos 2A choose 2cos^2 A - 1 or 1 - 2sin^2 A, since using cos^2 A - sin^2 A forces you to find the other ratio first.
  • When solving in 0° <= x <= 360°, remember that sin x = c and cos x = c give two angles each while tan x = c gives only one per period.
  • Fix the signs from the quadrant, where sin is positive in I and II, cos in I and IV, and tan in I and III, which is faster than calculation.

Exam Notes

  • Finding the remaining ratios from one given ratio is the most common 2-mark question, and the quadrant must be stated.
  • Proving an identity is a standard 5-mark question, with marks for starting from one side, for each valid step, and for reaching the other side.
  • Evaluating sin(A + B) or cos 2A from given ratios is a 5-mark question, and the answer is left as an exact fraction with a surd.
  • MCQs give a value from the table of standard angles and ask for the ratio of another angle, or give an equation and ask for its solutions in a stated interval.

Practice Questions

  • Prove that sin^2 θ + cos^2 θ = 1.
  • If sin θ = 3/5 and θ is acute, find cos θ, tan θ, sec θ, cosec θ and cot θ.
  • Prove that sin(A + B) sin(A - B) = sin^2 A - sin^2 B.
  • Solve 2sin^2 x - sin x - 1 = 0 for 0° <= x <= 360°.
  • If cos A = 4/5 and sin B = 3/5 with A and B acute, find sin(A + B) and cos 2A.

Mcs

1.

If sin theta = 3/5 for an acute angle theta, then cos theta is

Options
  • 3/4
  • 4/5
  • 5/4
  • 4/3
Answer

B

Explanation

The identity sin^2 theta + cos^2 theta = 1 gives cos^2 theta = 1 - 9/25 = 16/25, and cosine is positive in the first quadrant, so cos theta = 4/5. The 3/4 option is tan theta, not cos theta.

2.

The solutions of 2sin^2 x - sin x - 1 = 0 for 0 <= x <= 360 degrees are

Options
  • x = 90° only
  • x = 30°, 150°, 210° and 330°
  • x = 90°, 210° and 330°
  • x = 210° and 330° only
Answer

C

Explanation

Factorising gives (2 sin x + 1)(sin x - 1) = 0, so sin x = -1/2 or sin x = 1. In the given interval sin x = 1 gives x = 90° and sin x = -1/2 gives x = 210° and x = 330°.

3.

If sin A = 3/5 and sin B = 4/5, then sin(A + B) sin(A - B) equals

Options
  • 7/25
  • -7/25
  • 24/25
  • 0
Answer

B

Explanation

The identity gives sin(A + B) sin(A - B) = sin^2 A - sin^2 B = 9/25 - 16/25 = -7/25. The value 24/25 comes from wrongly adding the two squares.

4.

If cos A = 4/5, then cos 2A equals

Options
  • 7/25
  • -7/25
  • 9/25
  • 24/25
Answer

A

Explanation

Use cos 2A = 2cos^2 A - 1 = 2(16/25) - 1 = 32/25 - 25/25 = 7/25. Using cos 2A = 1 - 2sin^2 A with sin A = 3/5 gives the same result.

5.

tan 2A is equal to

Options
  • 2 tan A
  • 2 tan A/(1 - tan^2 A)
  • tan A/2
  • 2 tan A/(1 + tan^2 A)
Answer

B

Explanation

The double angle formula is tan 2A = 2 tan A/(1 - tan^2 A), which comes from the compound angle formula. It is undefined when tan A = ±1, and the first option is the common error.
