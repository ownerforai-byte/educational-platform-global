Practice Questions

  • Q1. NEB-style (5m): Show that 2x2 + 5xy + 3y2 = 0 represents a pair of real straight lines and find them. Solution. Condition: h2−ab=425​−6=41​>0 ✓ real pair. Factor: 2x2+5xy+3y2=(2x+3y)(x+y) ⟹ lines 2x+3y=0 and x+y=0, both through the origin. Check by expansion: 2x2+2xy+3xy+3y2 ✓. Deep point: a homogeneous second-degree equation always passes through the origin - the pair's vertex is fixed there; the discriminant condition h2≥ab decides real versus imaginary lines.
  • Q2. NEB-style (5m): For which values of k does x2 - kxy + 9y2 = 0 represent a pair of real lines? Solution. Here a=1, h=−2k​, b=9; condition h2≥ab: 4k2​≥9 ⟹ ∣k∣≥6, i.e. k≤−6 or k≥6. Boundary: k=±6 gives h2=ab - coincident lines (a perfect square: (x∓3y)2). Deep point: the inequality's boundary is the coincidence case - the pair degenerates to a double line exactly when the discriminant vanishes, the quadratic-form analogue of a repeated root.
  • Q3. NEB-style (6m): Find the angle between the pair of lines 3x2 - 8xy - 3y2 = 0. Solution. a=3, h=−4, b=−3: h2−ab=16+9=25, h2−ab​=5. tanθ=∣a+b∣2h2−ab​​=∣0∣10​ ⟹ denominator zero ⟹ θ=90° - the lines are perpendicular. Confirm by factoring: 3x2−8xy−3y2=(3x+y)(x−3y) ⟹ slopes −3 and 31​ ⟹ product −1 ✓. Deep point: perpendicularity of a pair is a+b=0 - the tangent formula's infinite value is the vertical-angle case read through the formula's failure mode.
  • Q4. NEB-style (5m): Factor 6x2 - xy - y2 = 0 into its pair of lines and state their slopes. Solution. 6x2−xy−y2=(3x+y)(2x−y) ⟹ lines 3x+y=0 (slope −3) and 2x−y=0 (slope 2). Slope check via m1​+m2​=−b2h​=−11​=−1 and m1​m2​=ba​=−16​=−6: −3+2=−1 ✓, (−3)(2)=−6 ✓. Deep point: the slope relations ∑m=−2h/b, ∏m=a/b (from substituting y=mx) verify a factoring without re-expanding - two one-line checks against two expansion-prone multiplications.
  • Q5. NEB-style (6m): Find the equations of the bisectors of the angles between the pair 2x2 + 5xy + 3y2 = 0. Solution. The pair's lines: 2x+3y=0, x+y=0; standardise: −2x−3y=0 and −x−y=0 (constants 0 - homogeneous lines need no constant fix). Normalisers 13​ and 2​. Bisectors: 13​−2x−3y​=±2​−x−y​; rationalise: 2​(−2x−3y)=±13​(x+y) ⟹ (−22​∓13​)x+(−32​∓13​)y=0 - two bisectors through the origin, perpendicular as required. Deep point: homogeneous pairs have bisectors through the origin (the pair's vertex); irrational coefficients are normal here - the bisector formula does not promise integers.
  • Q6. NEB-style (5m): Determine whether the point (2, 2) lies on either line of the pair x2 - 3xy + 2y2 = 0. Solution. Factor: (x−y)(x−2y)=0 ⟹ lines x−y=0 and x−2y=0. At (2, 2): x−y=0 ✓ - the point lies on the first line (and on the second? 2−4=−2=0, no). Deep point: a point lies on the pair iff it lies on at least one member line - substituting into the combined quadratic gives 0 for points on either line, but locating which line needs the factorisation.
  • Q7. NEB-style (6m): The equation 6x2 + 5xy + y2 + 7x + ... represents a pair of lines. Explain the role of the condition h2−ab versus the full determinant condition abc + 2fgh - af2 - bg2 - ch2 = 0. Solution. For the homogeneous part 6x2+5xy+y2: h2−ab=425​−6=41​>0 - the quadratic part factors, but a general second-degree equation (with x and y terms and constant) represents a real pair only if the full determinant condition holds AND h2>ab (non-parallel) or h2=ab with further conditions (parallel). The homogeneous condition alone governs only the direction pair; the linear terms shift the pair's vertex away from the origin. Deep point: two conditions, two jobs - h2≥ab reads the directions (real or imaginary), the determinant reads the whole conic (pair, point, or nothing); confusing them misclassifies every non-homogeneous equation.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: ax² + 2hxy + by² = 0 through the origin → factorise by treating as quadratic in (y/x); slopes m1m2 = a/b, m1 + m2 = −2h/b.
  • Trick 2: General second-degree represents a pair of lines when abc + 2fgh − af² − bg² − ch² = 0 — memorise as 'abc + 2fgh − af² − bg² − ch²'.
  • Trick 3: Angle between the lines: tan θ = 2√(h² − ab)/(a + b); perpendicular when a + b = 0; parallel when h² = ab.
  • Trick 4: For ax² + 2hxy + by² = 0: perpendicular ⇔ a + b = 0 — the fastest 1-mark test.
