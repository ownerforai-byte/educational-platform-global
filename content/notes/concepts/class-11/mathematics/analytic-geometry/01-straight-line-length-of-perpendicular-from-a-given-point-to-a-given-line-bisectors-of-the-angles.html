Practice Questions

  • Q1. NEB-style (5m): Find the perpendicular distance from the point (3, 4) to the line 3x + 4y - 5 = 0. Solution. d=32+42​∣3(3)+4(4)−5∣​=5∣9+16−5∣​=520​=4 units. Deep point: the denominator is the normaliser that converts the raw expression into signed distance - the numerator is the signed distance times exactly that normaliser, which is why the formula has the shape it has.
  • Q2. NEB-style (6m): Find the equations of the bisectors of the angles between x + 2y - 5 = 0 and 4x + 2y + 9 = 0, labelling the acute one. Solution. Standardise: L1​ as −x−2y+5=0; L2​ has c=9>0. Normalisers 5​ and 25​; multiply by 25​: 2(−x−2y+5)=±(4x+2y+9). (+): 6x+6y−1=0; (-): 2x−2y+19=0. Rule: a1​a2​+b1​b2​=(−1)(4)+(−2)(2)=−8<0 ⟹ (+) is acute. Check: slopes −1 and 1 ⟹ perpendicular. Deep point: the lcm multiplication by 25​ is where the factor 2 enters - dropping it produces lines that are not bisectors.
  • Q3. NEB-style (5m): Find all values of k for which the point (2, k) lies at distance 3 from the line 4x - 3y + 1 = 0. Solution. 5∣8−3k+1∣​=3 ⟹ ∣9−3k∣=15 ⟹ 9−3k=±15 ⟹ k=−2 or k=8. Deep point: the absolute value splits into two linear equations - distance-3 points form a pair of lines parallel to the given one, one on each side; both solutions live one on each side.
  • Q4. NEB-style (6m): Find the direction cosines of the line joining P(1, 2, 3) and Q(4, 6, 15), and verify the fundamental identity. Solution. Direction ratios (3, 4, 12); magnitude 9+16+144​=13. Cosines: l=±133​, m=±134​, n=±1312​ (signs together). Identity: 1699+16+144​=1 ✓. Deep point: the cosines are the unit vector's components - the identity is ∣u^∣=1 restated, and the ± pair is the line's two directions.
  • Q5. NEB-style (6m): Find the foot of the perpendicular from P(2, 3) to the line x + 2y - 4 = 0. Solution. Signed distance numerator: D=2+6−4=4; a2+b2=5. Foot: (2−51⋅4​, 3−52⋅4​)=(56​,57​). Verify on the line: 56​+514​−4=0 ✓. Deep point: the foot formula subtracts the signed-distance vector a2+b2D​(a,b) - the normal direction scaled to reach the line; the on-line check certifies it.
  • Q6. NEB-style (5m): Find the distance between the parallel lines 3x + 4y + 5 = 0 and 3x + 4y - 10 = 0. Solution. Shortcut: a2+b2​∣c1​−c2​∣​=5∣5−(−10)∣​=515​=3 units - valid because the two equations share identical (a, b), hence the same normaliser and orientation. Direct check: take (−35​,0) on the first line; distance to the second: 5∣3(−35​)+0+10∣​=5∣−5+10∣​=3 ✓. Deep point: the parallel-lines shortcut is the point-substitution route compressed - identical (a, b) is its licence, and without it the general point-on-line-1 method is the honest fallback.
  • Q7. NEB-style (6m): Find the bisector of the angle between x - 2y + 1 = 0 and 2x + y - 3 = 0 that bisects the angle containing the origin. Solution. Standardise: L1​ has c=1>0; L2​ has c=−3<0 ⟹ rewrite as −2x−y+3=0. Normalisers both 5​. (+) bisector: x−2y+1=−2x−y+3 ⟹ 3x−y−2=0. Origin's wedge: plugging (0,0) into both standardised forms gives +5​1​ and +5​3​ - both positive, the same-signed region ⟹ the (+) bisector 3x−y−2=0 bisects the origin's angle. Deep point: with constants positive, the origin sits on the positive side of both lines, so the same-signed (+) equation is automatically the origin's bisector - the convention does the selection.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Distance formula: d = |ax1 + by1 + c|/√(a² + b²) — memorise the denominator once and every distance problem is a substitution.
  • Trick 2: Bisectors: (a1x + b1y + c1)/√(a1²+b1²) = ±(a2x + b2y + c2)/√(a2²+b2²); choose the sign by substituting a test point (origin) — the sign that gives the bisector of the angle containing the origin is the one with equal signs.
  • Trick 3: Foot of perpendicular: use d along the normal direction (a, b) — foot = (x1 − a·t, y1 − b·t) with t = (ax1+by1+c)/(a²+b²).

Bisectors of two lines

Points
  • For lines a1x+b1y+c1=0 and a2x+b2y+c2=0, the bisectors are (a1x+b1y+c1)/sqrt(a1^2+b1^2) = ±(a2x+b2y+c2)/sqrt(a2^2+b2^2)
  • The two signs give the two bisectors, which are perpendicular to each other
  • The bisector of the acute angle is the one nearer the origin for lines with positive constants
