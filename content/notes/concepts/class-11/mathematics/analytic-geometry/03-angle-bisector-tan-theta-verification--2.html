Angle Bisector — Verifying with the tan θ Method

Class 11MathematicsAnalytic Geometry

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The tan θ Verification II (Axis-Parallel Bisectors)

    This canonical runs the tangent verification on the second worked pair — the lines 2x + y − 7 = 0 and 2x − y + 1 = 0 with bisectors 2x − 3 = 0 (acute) and y = 4 (obtuse). The vertical bisector exercises the axis-angle fallback where slopes fail, completing the verification toolkit.

  • 2. Theorem Mirror (as used here)

    • Angle between two lines: tan θ = |(m₂ − m₁)/(1 + m₁m₂)| — valid for finite slopes.
    • Axis-angle fallback: a line's inclination α = atan(m) (or 90° for vertical); the angle between two lines is the difference of inclinations, taken acutely.
    • The threshold test: tan θ < 1 ⟹ the candidate bisects the acute angle; tan θ > 1 ⟹ the obtuse one.
    • The doubling check: the lines' full angle, computed independently, must equal twice the verified half-angle.

  • 3. Deep Conceptual — Where Slopes Fail, Angles Take Over

    • The vertical bisector 2x − 3 = 0 has no slope — the tangent formula cannot even be written for it. The axis-angle reading (a line's direction is its inclination α; the angle between lines is |α₂ − α₁| taken acutely) is the universal fallback, and it handles vertical lines as naturally as any other.
    • The verification's full anatomy on this pair: L₂'s inclination is atan 2 ≈ 63.43°, the vertical bisector's is 90° ⟹ half-angle 26.57° < 45° ⟹ acute ✓; the horizontal bisector against L₁ (inclination atan(−2), i.e. −63.43° ≡ 116.57°) gives 26.57°... measured to the horizontal: |0 − (−63.43°)| = 63.43° > 45° ⟹ obtuse ✓. Both labels certified, one by slope tangent, one by axis angles — the toolkit's two modes in one example.
    • The doubling closure: tan(angle between lines) = 4/3 ⟹ θ ≈ 53.13°; twice the verified half-angle 26.57° gives 53.13° ✓ — the half-angle story and the full-angle fact close the loop.

  • 4. Deep Conceptual — Threshold Comparisons as Fraction Arithmetic

    • Every verdict in this canonical is a comparison with 1, never an angle computation: tan = 2 > 1 (obtuse), tan = 1/2 < 1 (acute). Keeping the arithmetic in fractions preserves exactness and speed; converting to decimals or degrees too early invites rounding doubt.
    • The reciprocal signature: the acute and obtuse half-angle tangents (1/2 and 2 here) are reciprocals because the half-angles are complementary — a quick internal consistency check that costs one glance.

  • 5. Worked Example Types

    • Type A — horizontal bisector audit: plain slope tangent, threshold comparison.
    • Type B — vertical bisector audit: axis-angle fallback, same threshold.
    • Type C — doubling closure: full angle = 2 × half-angle, all in fractions.
    • Type D — reciprocal signature: the two half-angle tangents multiply to 1.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "Vertical lines break the method": they break the slope formula, not the method — axis angles are the fallback. "63° > 45° needs a calculator": tan 63.43° = 2 exactly here — the fractions carry the comparison.

Practice Questions
  • NEB-style (6m): Using the tangent formula, verify that y = 4 is the obtuse angle bisector of the lines 2x + y − 7 = 0 and 2x − y + 1 = 0. — Slopes: bisector y = 4 ⟹ m_b = 0; L₁: y = 7 − 2x ⟹ m₁ = −2. Tangent: tan θ = |(0 − (−2))/(1 + (0)(−2))| = 2. Verdict: tan θ = 2 > 1 ⟹ θ ≈ 63.43° > 45° ⟹ y = 4 splits the obtuse angle ✓. Consistency: the lines' obtuse angle is 180° − 53.13° = 126.87°, half is 63.43° ✓ — the half-angle lands exactly on the tangent's prediction. Deep point: the verdict never left fractions — tan = 2 was compared with 1 directly; the degree values in the narration are commentary, not computation.
  • NEB-style (6m): Using axis angles (the vertical-line fallback), verify that 2x − 3 = 0 is the acute angle bisector of 2x + y − 7 = 0 and 2x − y + 1 = 0. — Inclinations: L₂ (2x − y + 1 = 0 ⟹ y = 2x + 1) has α₂ = atan 2 ≈ 63.43°; the vertical bisector has α_b = 90°. Angle between: |90° − 63.43°| = 26.57°. Verdict: 26.57° < 45° ⟹ the vertical bisector splits the acute angle ✓ — consistent with the a₁a₂ + b₁b₂ = −3 < 0 rule's verdict. Fraction form: tan(26.57°) = 1/2 exactly (from the 3-4-5 reading of the full angle), and 1/2 < 1 is the same verdict in tangent language. Deep point: the slope formula cannot even be written for the vertical bisector (no slope exists), but inclinations cover every line — the fallback is not an exception handler but the more general tool, with slopes as the special finite case.
  • NEB-style (5m): Compute the full angle between 2x + y − 7 = 0 and 2x − y + 1 = 0, and verify the doubling closure against the verified half-angle. — Full angle: tan θ = |(2 − (−2))/(1 + (−2)(2))| = 4/|−3| = 4/3 ⟹ θ ≈ 53.13° (acute angle between the lines). Half-angle from verification: the acute bisector made 26.57° with L₂ ⟹ doubled: 2 × 26.57° = 53.13° ✓ — the closure holds. Exact chain: tan(θ/2) = sin θ/(1 + cos θ) with the 3-4-5 reading (sin = 4/5, cos = 3/5) gives tan(θ/2) = (4/5)/(8/5) = 1/2, and atan(1/2) ≈ 26.57° ✓. Deep point: the doubling closure is the verification's final seal — half-angle (from the bisector), full angle (from the lines), and the factor 2 connecting them are three independent computations that must agree; when they do, the whole bisector story is internally consistent.
  • NEB-style (6m): The half-angle tangents for this pair are 1/2 (acute side) and 2 (obtuse side). Explain their reciprocal relationship and verify it via the complementary-angle identity. — The relationship: the acute half-angle (26.57°) and the obtuse half-angle (63.43°) are complementary (sum 90°) because the two bisectors are perpendicular — each bisector's half-angle lives on opposite sides of the right angle. Identity: tan(90° − φ) = cot φ = 1/tan φ ⟹ tan(63.43°) = 1/tan(26.57°) = 1/(1/2) = 2 ✓. Geometric reading: the two half-angles fill the right angle between the bisectors — their complementarity is the perpendicularity of the bisector pair, expressed in tangent arithmetic. Deep point: reciprocal half-angle tangents are a one-glance consistency check for any bisector pair: compute both, multiply, expect 1; a product ≠ 1 signals that the two "half-angles" do not belong to the same bisector system.

Summary

The second verification canonical completes the toolkit: the horizontal bisector's label falls to the plain slope tangent (tan = 2 > 1, obtuse), the vertical bisector's to the axis-angle fallback (26.57° < 45°, acute), and the doubling closure ties both to the lines' full angle tan = 4/3. Algebra labels the bisectors; geometry — in fractions — certifies the labels.
