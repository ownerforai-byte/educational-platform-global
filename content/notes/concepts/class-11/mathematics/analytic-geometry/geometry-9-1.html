  • <h2>Question 5 (a)</h2>
  • <p><b>Problem:</b> Find the equations of the bisectors of the angles between the lines containing the origin in each of the following cases:<br>i) 4x−3y+1=0 and 12x−5y+7=0<br>ii) 7x−y+11=0 and x+y−15=0<br>Prove that the bisectors of the angles are at right angles to each other.</p>
  • <p><b>Formula:</b> The equations of the bisectors of the angles between two lines a1​x+b1​y+c1​=0 and a2​x+b2​y+c2​=0 are given by:</p>
  • a12​+b12​​a1​x+b1​y+c1​​=±a22​+b22​​a2​x+b2​y+c2​​
  • <p>To find the bisector containing the origin, first make the constant terms c1​ and c2​ positive. The bisector containing the origin takes the positive (+) sign.</p>
  • <h3>Solution for i):</h3>
  • <p>Both constant terms (+1 and +7) are already positive.</p>
  • 42+(−3)2​4x−3y+1​=±122+(−5)2​12x−5y+7​
  • 54x−3y+1​=±1312x−5y+7​
  • 13(4x−3y+1)=±5(12x−5y+7)
  • <p>Taking the positive sign (bisector containing the origin):</p>
  • 13(4x−3y+1)=5(12x−5y+7)
  • 52x−39y+13=60x−25y+35
  • 8x+14y+22=0⟹4x+7y+11=0
  • <p>The other bisector (not containing the origin) uses the negative sign:</p>
  • 13(4x−3y+1)=−5(12x−5y+7)
  • 52x−39y+13=−60x+25y−35
  • 112x−64y+48=0⟹7x−4y+3=0
  • <p><b>Perpendicularity Proof:</b></p>
  • <p>Slope of first bisector (m1​)=−74​</p>
  • <p>Slope of second bisector (m2​)=−−47​=47​</p>
  • m1​×m2​=(−74​)×(47​)=−1 (Hence, they are at right angles).
  • <h3>Solution for ii):</h3>
  • <p>First, rewrite the second equation to make the constant positive: −x−y+15=0.</p>
  • 72+(−1)2​7x−y+11​=(−1)2+(−1)2​−x−y+15​
  • 50​7x−y+11​=2​−x−y+15​
  • <p>Since 50​=52​:</p>
  • 52​7x−y+11​=2​−x−y+15​
  • 7x−y+11=5(−x−y+15)
  • 7x−y+11=−5x−5y+75
  • 12x+4y−64=0⟹3x+y−16=0
  • <p>The other bisector (using the negative sign):</p>
  • 7x−y+11=−5(−x−y+15)⟹7x−y+11=5x+5y−75
  • 2x−6y+86=0⟹x−3y+43=0
  • <p><b>Perpendicularity Proof:</b> Slope of 3x+y−16=0 is −3. Slope of x−3y+43=0 is 31​.</p>
  • (−3)×31​=−1 (Hence, they are at right angles).
  • <h2>Question 5 (b)</h2>
  • <p><b>Problem:</b> Find the equations of the bisectors of the acute angle between each of the following pairs of lines:<br>i) y=x and y=7x+4<br>ii) x+2y−5=0 and 4x+2y+9=0</p>
  • <h3>Solution for i):</h3>
  • <p>Rearrange equations to x−y=0 and 7x−y+4=0. Make constants positive (already positive or zero).</p>

Practice Questions

  • Q1. NEB-style (5m): Find the equations of the bisectors of the angles between the coordinate axes x = 0 and y = 0. Solution. Standardised forms: x=0 (i.e. 1⋅x+0⋅y+0=0) and y=0; normalisers both 1. Bisectors: x=±y ⟹ y=x and y=−x - the two 45-degree lines through the origin, perpendicular as required. Deep point: the coordinate axes are the bisector machinery's simplest input - equal normalisers, zero constants, and the answer's symmetry is visible before any algebra.
  • Q2. NEB-style (6m): Find the bisector of the angle between 4x + 3y - 12 = 0 and 3x + 4y - 6 = 0 that contains the origin. Solution. Standardise: L1​ as −4x−3y+12=0, L2​ as −3x−4y+6=0 (both constants positive). Normalisers both 5. (+) bisector: −4x−3y+12=−3x−4y+6 ⟹ −x+y+6=0, i.e. y=x−6. Origin's wedge: (0,0) gives +512​ and +56​ - both positive, the same-signed region ⟹ the (+) bisector y=x−6 bisects the origin's angle. Deep point: with constants positive the origin is automatically on the (+) side of both lines - the selection is the convention's corollary, not a new computation.
  • Q3. NEB-style (6m): Verify that the point (3, -3) on the bisector y = -x is equidistant from the axes x = 0 and y = 0. Solution. Distance to x = 0 (the y-axis): ∣3∣=3. Distance to y = 0 (the x-axis): ∣−3∣=3 ✓ equal. The point lies on y=−x: −3=−3 ✓. Deep point: equidistance from the axes is exactly ∣x∣=∣y∣ - the bisector equations y=±x are that condition written as lines; the definition and the equations are the same statement.
  • Q4. NEB-style (5m): Show that the bisectors y = x and y = -x of the coordinate axes are perpendicular. Solution. Slopes 1 and −1 ⟹ product −1 ⟹ perpendicular ✓. In general: the two bisectors of any pair of intersecting lines are perpendicular - algebraically the normalised coefficients' cross terms cancel in the dot product; geometrically the two half-angles (acute and obtuse sides) are complementary. Deep point: the perpendicularity is the bisector system's invariant - it holds for every pair, which is what makes it a falsification test for computed bisectors.
  • Q5. NEB-style (6m): For the lines x + 2y - 5 = 0 and 4x + 2y + 9 = 0, determine which bisector is acute without computing the bisectors, then verify with the tangent test on 6x + 6y - 1 = 0. Solution. Standardise L1​ as −x−2y+5=0; rule: a1​a2​+b1​b2​=(−1)(4)+(−2)(2)=−8<0 ⟹ the (+) bisector is acute. Compute (+): 5​−x−2y+5​=25​4x+2y+9​ ⟹ 2(−x−2y+5)=4x+2y+9 ⟹ 6x+6y−1=0. Tangent test: bisector slope −1, L1​ slope −21​: tanθ=1+21​∣−1+21​∣​=31​<1 ⟹ acute ✓ - algebra and geometry agree. Deep point: the rule classifies cheaply, the tangent test certifies independently - dual-channel agreement is the standard of proof.
  • Q6. NEB-style (6m): Find the bisectors of the lines 3x + y = 7 and x - 3y = 1, and explain the shape of the answer. Solution. Standardise: L1​: −3x−y+7=0; L2​: −x+3y−1=0 has c=−1<0 ⟹ rewrite x−3y+1=0. Normalisers 10​ and 10​ - equal. Skeleton: −3x−y+7=±(x−3y+1). (+): −3x−y+7=x−3y+1 ⟹ −4x+2y+6=0 ⟹ 2x−y−3=0. (-): −3x−y+7=−x+3y−1 ⟹ −2x−4y+8=0 ⟹ x+2y−4=0. Shape: slopes 2 and −21​ ⟹ product −1 ✓ perpendicular; the lines' slopes −3 and 31​ are negative reciprocals - the original pair is perpendicular, so its bisectors sit at 45 degrees to each line. Deep point: when the original lines are perpendicular (a1​a2​+b1​b2​=0), the acute/obtuse distinction dissolves and both bisectors bisect right angles - the classification step retires.
  • Q7. NEB-style (6m): A circle is inscribed in the angle formed by 3x + 4y - 5 = 0 and 4x - 3y + 10 = 0 with centre on the internal bisector through the origin's side. Find the bisector line the centre lies on. Solution. Standardise: L1​: −3x−4y+5=0; L2​: 4x−3y+10=0 (c=10>0). Normalisers 5 and 5. (+) bisector: −3x−4y+5=4x−3y+10 ⟹ −7x−y+5=0 ⟹ 7x+y−5=0. Origin check: (0,0) gives +55​ and +510​ - both positive ⟹ the (+) bisector 7x+y−5=0 is the origin-side (internal, for this wedge) bisector; an inscribed circle's centre lies on it. Deep point: the in-centre application is the bisector's purpose - equal distances to both arms is exactly the circle's tangency condition, which is why bisector loci and incircles are the same geometry.

Summary

Angle bisectors of pairs of straight lines: the equal-distance locus made algebraic. Normalise each line into a signed distance, equate up to plus/minus, and the two perpendicular bisector equations fall out. The constants-positive convention plus the a1a2 + b1b2 dot-product rule classify acute versus obtuse and select the origin's wedge; the tangent test (compare with 1) certifies the label geometrically. Perpendicularity of the bisector pair is an identity - the built-in falsification test for every computation.
