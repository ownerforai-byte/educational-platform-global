Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to Indeterminate Forms

    This canonical is the short-form doorway to the seven indeterminate forms: what each shape means, why it demands work, and which cure fits. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the forms, adds the race-reading, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

  • 2. Theorem Mirror

    • The seven: 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ — shapes, never values.
    • Cures by form: 0/0 ⟹ factor, conjugate, or steer to stars (or L'Hôpital); ∞/∞ ⟹ degree comparison or divide by the dominant term; 0·∞ ⟹ flip one factor into a quotient; ∞ − ∞ ⟹ merge fractions or rationalise; 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ ⟹ the log bridge f^g = e^{g ln f}.
    • Determinate neighbours: c/0 (wall), 0/c (zero), ∞ + c (∞) — answers, not work orders.

  • 3. Deep Conceptual — Forms as Races Between Rates

    • Every indeterminate form is a race whose outcome is not yet decided: 0/0 is two vanishing rates competing; ∞/∞ two growth rates; 0·∞ a collapse against an explosion; 1^∞ a displacement fighting an exponent. The limit is the race's verdict, and the cure is the tool that times the contestants.
    • Orders decide races: contestants vanishing at the same order leave coefficient ratios (sin 3x/tan 5x → 3/5); a faster vanisher loses (→ 0); a faster grower wins (→ ∞). The hierarchy sin u ~ u, 1 − cos u ~ u²/2, u − sin u ~ u³/6 is the race schedule.
    • The log bridge is an isomorphism: powers have no algebra, but logs turn them into products — every power form surrenders to f^g = e^{g ln f} plus one flip into 0/0 or ∞/∞.

  • 4. Deep Conceptual — Form Checks Are Hypothesis Tests

    • Before every technique, name the form: L'Hôpital needs 0/0 or ∞/∞; degree comparison needs genuine ∞/∞; the stars need u → 0. Applying a tool outside its form manufactures wrong answers (L'Hôpital on c/0 "gives" 0 at a wall).
    • Forms can reappear mid-solution: after one L'Hôpital pass the new ratio may be indeterminate again — re-check each pass; termination is when substitution decides.
    • ∞ − ∞ must be merged first: the algebra (common denominator, conjugate) creates the quotient the machinery needs; no rule operates on a difference of infinities directly.

  • 5. Worked Example Types

    • Type A — 0/0 by factoring: shared root exposed, cancelled, substituted.
    • Type B — 0·∞ flip: x ln x → ln x/(1/x) ⟹ L'Hôpital ⟹ 0.
    • Type C — ∞ − ∞ merge: sec x − tan x = (1 − sin x)/cos x ⟹ 0/0 ⟹ resolve.
    • Type D — power form: xˣ → e^{lim x ln x} = e⁰ = 1.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "0/0 = 1": no value exists — transform. "1^∞ = 1": the base's small displacement times a huge exponent can leave any positive constant — e^c is the answer shape.

Confusion

  • Reading 0/0 as the answer — it means substitution failed, and the limit may be anything.
  • Applying L'Hôpital to 0⋅∞ or ∞−∞ without first rewriting as a quotient.
  • Differentiating only the numerator and forgetting the denominator.

Universal Facts

  • The seven forms exhaust the ways two competing rates can obscure a limit.

Summary

The seven indeterminate forms are races between rates whose outcomes the tools must time: 0/0 and ∞/∞ by factoring, degrees, or L'Hôpital; 0·∞ by flipping a factor; ∞ − ∞ by merging; the power trio by the log bridge. Orders of vanishing are the race schedule, form checks are hypothesis tests, and the flip that simplifies the derivatives is the winning move. Name the form, name the cure, verify the verdict — the forms are the subject's work orders, never its answers.

Special Notes

  • L'Hôpital requires the derivative quotient's limit to EXIST; if it does not, the rule is silent and another technique is needed.
  • Some 1∞ forms have no limit, so writing the form down never licenses the exponential answer.

Practice

  • Classify and evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{4x} − 1)/(tan 2x).
  • Evaluate lim_{x→∞} x·e^{−x}.
  • Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (√(x² + x) − x).

Examples

  • The flip choice - x ln x: flip ln x into the numerator (ln x/(1/x)) not x into the log - the derivative that simplifies wins.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (6m): Classify the form and evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{4x} − 1)/(tan 2x). — Form check: (1 − 1)/0 = 0/0 ✓. Cure — coefficient ratio: e^{4x} − 1 ~ 4x (order 1, coefficient 4); tan 2x ~ 2x (order 1, coefficient 2) ⟹ same order ⟹ limit = 4/2 = 2. Rigorous version: [(e^{4x} − 1)/(4x)]·[(2x)/(tan 2x)]·2 → 1·1·2 = 2 ✓. Deep point: the form check selected the cure (same-order 0/0 ⟹ coefficient ratio); the stars certified it — diagnosis, cure, verification in three named moves.
  • NEB-style (5m): Classify the form and evaluate lim_{x→∞} x·e^{−x}. — Form: ∞ · 0 — the 0·∞ species (x → ∞, e^{−x} → 0). Flip: x·e^{−x} = x/eˣ — now ∞/∞ ✓. Ladder verdict: exponential dominates any power ⟹ 0. L'Hôpital check: x/eˣ → 1/eˣ → 0 ✓. Deep point: the flip converted a product race into a quotient race where the ladder's rungs decide; flipping the polynomial down (rather than the exponential up) keeps the derivatives friendly — flip toward simplicity.
  • NEB-style (6m): Classify the form and evaluate lim_{x→∞} (√(x² + x) − x). — Form: ∞ − ∞ — merge or rationalise. Conjugate: multiply by (√(x²+x) + x)/(same): numerator = (x² + x) − x² = x ⟹ expression = x/(√(x²+x) + x). Divide by x: = 1/(√(1 + 1/x) + 1) → 1/(1 + 1) = 1/2. Deep point: the conjugate exposed the race's fine structure — √(x² + x) is x·√(1 + 1/x), which is x plus a half-unit correction; the limit is that correction's coefficient, exactly the "hidden half-step" the ∞ − ∞ form was hiding.
  • NEB-style (6m): Classify the form and evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} (sin x)^{tan x}. — Form: base → 0⁺, exponent → 0 ⟹ 0⁰. Log bridge: the limit is e^L with L = lim tan x·ln(sin x) — a 0·(−∞) form. Flip: L = lim ln(sin x)/(cot x) — now −∞/∞ ✓. L'Hôpital: d/dx ln(sin x) = cos x/sin x = cot x; d/dx cot x = −cosec²x ⟹ ratio = cot x/(−cosec²x) = −sin x·cos x → 0. Limit: e⁰ = 1. Deep point: the flip choice mattered — flipping tan x into cot x in the denominator made both derivatives trigonometric and the product collapsed to −sin x cos x; power forms are log races, and the flip that simplifies the derivatives wins.
