Notes

  • What 'indeterminate' means: a form like 0/0 describes the SHAPE the expression takes as x approaches the point, never its value. The same form can give 0, 1, 7, ∞, or no limit at all. xx2​→0, x2x2​→1 and x2x​→∞ are all 0/0.
  • The seven forms: 0/0, ∞/∞, 0⋅∞, ∞−∞, ∞0, 00, 1∞. Everything else is determinate — 50​=0 and 05​=∞ need no further work. 0⋅∞ is the classic trap because each factor is a LIMIT rather than a value.
  • Technique 1 — factorise and cancel: find the common vanishing factor. limx→2​x−2x2−4​=limx−2(x−2)(x+2)​=4. For powers use an−bn=(a−b)(an−1+⋯+bn−1).
  • Technique 2 — rationalise with the conjugate: limx→0​x21−cosx​=limx2(1+cosx)1−cos2x​=limx2(1+cosx)sin2x​=21​. The conjugate is chosen so a difference of squares appears.
  • Technique 3 — L'Hôpital: for 0/0 or ∞/∞, differentiate numerator and denominator SEPARATELY and take the limit of the ratio, provided that limit exists. x−2x2−4​ becomes 12x​→4.
  • Technique 4 — logs for 1∞: with y=(1+f)g and f→0, take logs and use limfln(1+f)​=1, giving limy=elimgln(1+f). Hence limn→∞​(1+n1​)n=e.

Confusion

  • Reading 0/0 as the answer — it means substitution failed, and the limit may be anything.
  • Applying L'Hôpital to 0⋅∞ or ∞−∞ without first rewriting as a quotient.
  • Differentiating only the numerator and forgetting the denominator.

Universal Facts

  • Euler's number is defined as a limit, e=limn→∞​(1+n1​)n — the compound-interest form of the 1∞ case.
  • Numerical differentiation avoids f(x+h)−f(x) over h precisely because at tiny h this 0/0 suffers catastrophic cancellation.

Key Points

  • An indeterminate form is a shape, not a value.
  • Factorise, rationalise, or use L'Hôpital — never substitute and stop.
  • L'Hôpital applies only to 0/0 and ∞/∞.
  • For 1∞, take logarithms to reduce to a 0/0 quotient.

Summary

An indeterminate form describes the shape an expression takes near a point, never its value — the same 0/0 may resolve to 0, 1, or nothing at all. Four techniques cover essentially every case: factorise and cancel, rationalise with a conjugate, apply L'Hôpital to 0/0 and ∞/∞, and take logarithms for 1∞.

Special Notes

  • L'Hôpital requires the derivative quotient's limit to EXIST; if it does not, the rule is silent and another technique is needed.
  • Some 1∞ forms have no limit, so writing the form down never licenses the exponential answer.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: 0/0 and ∞/∞ are indeterminate, but 5/0 and 0/5 are determinate.
  • Statement 2: L'Hôpital's rule may be applied only to 0/0 and ∞/∞ forms.
  • Statement 3: Differentiating both parts is only part of L'Hôpital — the resulting limit must exist.
  • Statement 4: 0⋅∞ and ∞−∞ must be rewritten as a quotient first.
  • Statement 5: limn→∞​(1+nk​)n=ek.

Important Notes

  • Topic anchor: Technique 4 — logs for 1infty with y=(1+f)g and fto0, take logs and use limfracln(1+f)f=1, giving limy=elimgln(1+f).

Exam Short Tricks

  • Match technique to form: 0/0 → factorise, rationalise or L'Hôpital; 1∞ → logs; 0⋅∞ and ∞−∞ → rewrite as a quotient.
  • If the expression contains a root, reach for the conjugate first.

Exam Notes

  • Naming the correct technique for a given form is a frequent 2-mark question in the limits block.
