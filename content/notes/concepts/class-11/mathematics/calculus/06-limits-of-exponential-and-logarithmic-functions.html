Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Limit Menagerie by Family

    Evaluating limits of algebraic, trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions: each family has its reduction grammar — factoring for polynomials, standard stars for trig, e-limits for exp/log.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Algebraic limits

    • Polynomials: direct substitution (continuity). Rationals at a: substitute; 0/0 ⟹ factor-and-cancel or conjugate; c/0 ⟹ ±∞ by side-sign. Rationals at ∞: degree rule.
    • Surds: conjugate multiplication; (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹ for fractional n too.

    2.2 Trigonometric limits

    • Everything reduces to the stars: sin u/u → 1, tan u/u → 1, (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2, all in radians. Rescaling sin(kx)/x → k; compound-angle identities as reducers; squeeze as safety net.

    2.3 Exponential and logarithmic limits

    • (eᵘ − 1)/u → 1; (aᵘ − 1)/u → ln a; ln(1+u)/u → 1; (1+u)^{1/u} → e; 1^∞ via the bridge e^{lim uv}. Growth ladder for contests at ∞: ln x ≪ xᵃ ≪ aˣ.

    2.4 The universal recipe

    • Substitute → classify the form → pick the family's grammar → transform to a standard form → answer. The families differ only in their standard forms; the recipe is one.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why families need different grammars

    • Each family's singularities have different structure: polynomial zeros factor; trig singularities oscillate; exp/log singularities curve. The standard limits encode each structure once, so every member reduces to its family's stars.

    4.2 Mixed-family expressions

    • Split into factors (when limits exist) and evaluate each by its own grammar: (e^{2x} − 1)/ln(1+4x) is an exp star over a log star via the x-bridge → 2/4 = 1/2. Mixed forms are the exam's favourite compound.

    4.3 The radian and base disciplines

    • Two silent assumptions break trig and log limits: degrees (sin x/x fails) and non-natural logs (ln(1+u)/u → 1 only for natural log). State them; convert when needed.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Mixing grammars: a trig expression needs trig stars, not factoring — (1 − cos x)/x² is ½ by half-angle, not by "cancelling cos".

  • Forgetting the coefficient: sin 3x/x → 3 (the a survives); (e^{2x}−1)/x → 2.
  • Log base slips: ln(1+x)/x → 1 is natural-log only; log₁₀(1+x)/x → 1/ln 10.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 3x)/x. (2m) — Rescaling: sin 3x/x = 3·[sin 3x/(3x)]; as x → 0, 3x → 0 and the star gives sin 3x/(3x) → 1 ⟹ limit = 3. (General: sin(kx)/x → k — the coefficient survives, the argument dies.)

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{5x} − 1)/(sin 2x). — Split via stars: = [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)]·(5x)/[(sin 2x/(2x))·(2x)] — insert the argument factors: = (5/2)·[e^{5x}−1)/(5x)]/[sin 2x/2x] → (5/2)·(1/1) = 5/2. Exponential star over trig star, coefficient ratio deciding.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (x − 1)/ln x. — Substitute: 0/0. Steer: set u = x − 1 → 0; ln x = ln(1 + u) ⟹ expression = u/ln(1+u) = 1/[ln(1+u)/u] → 1/1 = 1. The log star resolves the classic log-versus-linear race at 1.

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — Why Each Family Has Its Own Singularity Structure

    • Polynomial zeros are algebraic: they factor cleanly, so 0/0 in rational functions always surrenders to factoring — the factor theorem guarantees the cancellation exists.
    • Trigonometric singularities are geometric: sin x vanishes like x (its first-order tangent), cos x vanishes like a square near π/2 (1 − cos u ~ u²/2). The standard stars encode these orders of vanishing: sin u ~ u, 1 − cos u ~ u²/2, tan u ~ u.
    • Exponential/logarithmic singularities are self-referential: eᵘ − 1 ~ u (the base's own rate) and ln(1+u) ~ u (the log's tangent). The exponential's vanishing is the number e; the log's vanishing is the natural base's inverse.
    • The deep unity: every standard limit is a first-order Taylor approximation in disguise — sin u ≈ u, eᵘ ≈ 1 + u, ln(1+u) ≈ u. The families differ because their functions' tangents differ; the recipe is one because tangents are always linear.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Orders of Vanishing and the Hierarchy of Small

    • As u → 0: u ≫ u² ≫ u³ ≫ … — higher powers vanish faster. When a limit's numerator and denominator vanish at the same order, the ratio survives as a constant; when the numerator vanishes faster, the limit is 0; slower, ±∞.
    • Worked hierarchy: sin u ~ u (order 1), 1 − cos u ~ u²/2 (order 2), u − sin u ~ u³/6 (order 3), tan u − sin u ~ u³/2 (order 3). These orders predict limits before any computation: (tan u − sin u)/u³ must be a nonzero constant because both sides are order 3.
    • Exam power: before computing, estimate the order of every factor. If numerator order = denominator order ⟹ finite nonzero; unequal ⟹ 0 or ∞. The computation then merely finds the constant.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — The x-Bridge as Separation of Rates

    • Mixed-family ratios like (e^{5x} − 1)/sin 2x are resolved by inserting x/x — which is secretly dividing both contestants by the common yardstick x and comparing each to it. Each star measures "how fast this function vanishes relative to x", and the answer is the ratio of those relative rates.
    • This is the exact structure of comparing via a reference standard: the star sin u/u → 1 says "sin u behaves like u"; the bridge says "so sin 2x behaves like 2x and e^{5x} − 1 behaves like 5x; their ratio behaves like 5x/2x = 5/2".
    • Generalisation preview: this is L'Hôpital's rule in embryo — comparing two vanishing functions via their linear approximations. The x-bridge is the elementary special case where the approximations are known stars.

Confusion

  • lnx is only defined for x>0. Never attempt ln(0) or ln(−1).
  • The limit limx→0​(ex−1)/x=1 requires the exponent to be exactly x. For e2x, adjust: limx→0​(e2x−1)/x=2.
  • ex grows much faster than any polynomial xn as x→∞, and lnx grows much slower than any positive power xn.
  • The limit limx→0​(sinx)/x=1 only works when x is in RADIANS. In degrees, the limit would be π/180.
  • Do not confuse limx→0​(sinx)/x=1 with limx→∞​(sinx)/x=0 — these are completely different situations.
  • limx→0​xsinx​=1 does NOT mean sinx=x. It means they are approximately equal for very small x.
  • The quotient rule requires the denominator limit to be non-zero. If both numerator and denominator approach zero, you have a 0/0 indeterminate form and must simplify first.
  • These properties hold only when the individual limits on the right side exist (except for the quotient rule, which also requires a non-zero denominator limit).
  • 0/0 is indeterminate, but k/0 (where k=0) is NOT indeterminate — the limit is ±∞ (does not exist as a finite number).
  • ∞/∞ is indeterminate, but k/∞=0 for any finite constant k — this IS determinate.
  • For rational functions where both numerator and denominator approach 0 at x=a, do NOT simply say the limit is the ratio of leading coefficients — you must factor and simplify first.
  • At infinity, only the highest-degree terms matter. Lower-degree terms become negligible.
  • The limit limx→a​f(x) does NOT require f(a) to exist. The limit is about the behaviour of f(x) near a, not at a.
  • Do not substitute x=a directly as a first step — many limits yield 0/0 which requires algebraic simplification before substitution.

Practice Questions
  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin 3x)/x. (2m) — Rescaling insertion: multiply and divide by 3: sin 3x/x = 3·[sin 3x/(3x)]. Star: as x → 0, the argument 3x → 0, so by the fundamental star (radians) sin 3x/(3x) → 1. Result: 3·1 = 3. General theorem harvested: lim_{x→0} sin(kx)/x = k — the coefficient survives, the argument dies into the star. This is the 15.2/45 reduction grammar in its purest one-line form.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{5x} − 1)/(sin 2x). — Form: 0/0 mixing two families — exponential over trigonometric. Double insertion: (e^{5x} − 1)/(sin 2x) = [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)] · (5x) / { [sin 2x/(2x)] · (2x) } = (5/2) · [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)] / [sin 2x/(2x)]. Stars: e^{5x} − 1 over 5x → 1 (exponential star, u = 5x → 0); sin 2x/(2x) → 1 (trigonometric star, radians). Result: (5/2)·(1/1) = 5/2. The x-bridge converts a mixed-family 0/0 into a ratio of coefficients — the standard move whenever exp meets trig in one fraction.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (x − 1)/ln x. — Substitute: (1 − 1)/ln 1 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Steer into the log star: set u = x − 1, so u → 0 as x → 1 and ln x = ln(1 + u) (composition theorem licenses the rewrite). The expression becomes u/ln(1+u) = 1/[ln(1+u)/u]. Star: ln(1+u)/u → 1 as u → 0 (natural log mandatory). Result: 1/1 = 1. Reading: near x = 1 the logarithm grows exactly as fast as the linear term — ln x ≈ x − 1 is the first-order tangent approximation, and this limit is its certificate.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin x − tan x)/x³ and explain the sign. — Factor the difference: sin x − tan x = sin x(1 − 1/cos x) = sin x·(cos x − 1)/cos x = −sin x·(1 − cos x)/cos x. Split into stars (all factor limits exist): = −[sin x/x]·[(1 − cos x)/x²]·[1/cos x]. Evaluate each: sin x/x → 1 (order 1 star); (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2 (order 2 star, half-angle); 1/cos x → 1. Product: −(1)(1/2)(1) = −1/2. Why negative: tan x > sin x for small positive x (tan = sin/cos with cos < 1 inflates it), so the difference is negative — the algebraic sign and the function comparison agree. Order check: sin x (order 1) times 1 − cos x (order 2) = order 3, matching x³ — a finite nonzero constant was guaranteed before any computation.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (√(x² + 3x) − x). — Form: ∞ − ∞ — an indeterminate difference of two diverging quantities. Conjugate: multiply by (√(x²+3x) + x)/(√(x²+3x) + x): numerator = (x² + 3x) − x² = 3x, giving 3x/(√(x²+3x) + x). Divide by x (x > 0 so √(x²)/x = 1): = 3/(√(1 + 3/x) + 1). Limit: 3/x → 0 ⟹ 3/(1 + 1) = 3/2. Deep conceptual point: the two contestants are nearly equal — √(x²+3x) ≈ x√(1+3/x) ≈ x(1 + 3/(2x)) = x + 3/2 — the conjugate exposes the constant offset between the two giants. ∞ − ∞ questions always hide a finite difference between infinite quantities; the conjugate (or a common denominator) is how it is excavated.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 + sin x)^{2/x} using the exponential bridge, stating the form first. — Form check: base 1 + sin x → 1, exponent 2/x → ∞ ⟹ the 1^∞ indeterminate form — not 1. Bridge: (1 + sin x)^{2/x} = e^{(2/x)·ln(1+sin x)} identically, so the limit is e^L with L = lim_{x→0} (2/x)·ln(1 + sin x). Steer L into stars: ln(1 + sin x) ~ sin x (log star with u = sin x → 0), and sin x ~ x (trig star) ⟹ ln(1 + sin x) ~ x. Hence L = lim (2/x)·x = 2. Answer: e². Full bookkeeping shown via the chain: (2/x)ln(1+sin x) = 2·[ln(1+sin x)/sin x]·[sin x/x] → 2·1·1 = 2 ✓. Deep point: the 1^∞ battle is entirely in the exponent — the base's approach to 1 and the exponent's escape to ∞ fight to a draw whose score is lim (exponent × base's displacement), here 2/x × sin x → 2.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→π/2} (1 − sin x)/(π/2 − x)². — Substitution steering: set u = π/2 − x; as x → π/2, u → 0, and sin x = sin(π/2 − u) = cos u (compound angle). The limit becomes lim_{u→0} (1 − cos u)/u². Half-angle star: 1 − cos u = 2sin²(u/2) ⟹ (1 − cos u)/u² = 2·[sin(u/2)/u]² = 2·(1/4)·[sin(u/2)/(u/2)]² = (1/2)·[sin(u/2)/(u/2)]² → (1/2)·1² = 1/2. Deep points: (1) substitution converts a limit at a nonzero point into the standard at-0 theory — every trig limit away from 0 is one substitution away from home; (2) the answer confirms the order bookkeeping: 1 − sin x vanishes to order 2 at π/2 (its Taylor expansion there is (π/2−x)²/2), so the ratio against a quadratic must be a constant — and the constant is exactly the second-order coefficient 1/2.

Double insertion: (e^{5x} − 1)/(sin 2x) = [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)] · (5x) / { [sin 2x/(2x)] · (2x) } = (5/2) · [(e^{5x} − 1)/(5x)] / [sin 2x/(2x)]. Stars: e^{5x} − 1 over 5x → 1 (exponential star, u = 5x → 0); sin 2x/(2x) → 1 (trigonometric star, radians). Result: (5/2)·(1/1) = 5/2. The x-bridge converts a mixed-family 0/0 into a ratio of coefficients — the standard move whenever exp meets trig in one fraction.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→1} (x − 1)/ln x. — Substitute: (1 − 1)/ln 1 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Steer into the log star: set u = x − 1, so u → 0 as x → 1 and ln x = ln(1 + u) (composition theorem licenses the rewrite). The expression becomes u/ln(1+u) = 1/[ln(1+u)/u]. Star: ln(1+u)/u → 1 as u → 0 (natural log mandatory). Result: 1/1 = 1. Reading: near x = 1 the logarithm grows exactly as fast as the linear term — ln x ≈ x − 1 is the first-order tangent approximation, and this limit is its certificate.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (sin x − tan x)/x³ and explain the sign. — Factor the difference: sin x − tan x = sin x(1 − 1/cos x) = sin x·(cos x − 1)/cos x = −sin x·(1 − cos x)/cos x. Split into stars (all factor limits exist): = −[sin x/x]·[(1 − cos x)/x²]·[1/cos x]. Evaluate each: sin x/x → 1 (order 1 star); (1 − cos x)/x² → 1/2 (order 2 star, half-angle); 1/cos x → 1. Product: −(1)(1/2)(1) = −1/2. Why negative: tan x > sin x for small positive x (tan = sin/cos with cos < 1 inflates it), so the difference is negative — the algebraic sign and the function comparison agree. Order check: sin x (order 1) times 1 − cos x (order 2) = order 3, matching x³ — a finite nonzero constant was guaranteed before any computation.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (√(x² + 3x) − x). — Form: ∞ − ∞ — an indeterminate difference of two diverging quantities. Conjugate: multiply by (√(x²+3x) + x)/(√(x²+3x) + x): numerator = (x² + 3x) − x² = 3x, giving 3x/(√(x²+3x) + x). Divide by x (x > 0 so √(x²)/x = 1): = 3/(√(1 + 3/x) + 1). Limit: 3/x → 0 ⟹ 3/(1 + 1) = 3/2. Deep conceptual point: the two contestants are nearly equal — √(x²+3x) ≈ x√(1+3/x) ≈ x(1 + 3/(2x)) = x + 3/2 — the conjugate exposes the constant offset between the two giants. ∞ − ∞ questions always hide a finite difference between infinite quantities; the conjugate (or a common denominator) is how it is excavated.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (1 + sin x)^{2/x} using the exponential bridge, stating the form first. — Form check: base 1 + sin x → 1, exponent 2/x → ∞ ⟹ the 1^∞ indeterminate form — not 1. Bridge: (1 + sin x)^{2/x} = e^{(2/x)·ln(1+sin x)} identically, so the limit is e^L with L = lim_{x→0} (2/x)·ln(1 + sin x). Steer L into stars: ln(1 + sin x) ~ sin x (log star with u = sin x → 0), and sin x ~ x (trig star) ⟹ ln(1 + sin x) ~ x. Hence L = lim (2/x)·x = 2. Answer: e². Full bookkeeping shown via the chain: (2/x)ln(1+sin x) = 2·[ln(1+sin x)/sin x]·[sin x/x] → 2·1·1 = 2 ✓. Deep point: the 1^∞ battle is entirely in the exponent — the base's approach to 1 and the exponent's escape to ∞ fight to a draw whose score is lim (exponent × base's displacement), here 2/x × sin x → 2.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→π/2} (1 − sin x)/(π/2 − x)². — Substitution steering: set u = π/2 − x; as x → π/2, u → 0, and sin x = sin(π/2 − u) = cos u (compound angle). The limit becomes lim_{u→0} (1 − cos u)/u². Half-angle star: 1 − cos u = 2sin²(u/2) ⟹ (1 − cos u)/u² = 2·[sin(u/2)/u]² = 2·(1/4)·[sin(u/2)/(u/2)]² = (1/2)·[sin(u/2)/(u/2)]² → (1/2)·1² = 1/2. Deep points: (1) substitution converts a limit at a nonzero point into the standard at-0 theory — every trig limit away from 0 is one substitution away from home; (2) the answer confirms the order bookkeeping: 1 − sin x vanishes to order 2 at π/2 (its Taylor expansion there is (π/2−x)²/2), so the ratio against a quadratic must be a constant — and the constant is exactly the second-order coefficient 1/2.

Summary

Limit evaluation is one recipe with four grammars. Algebraic: substitute; 0/0 factors or rationalises ((xⁿ−aⁿ)/(x−a) → naⁿ⁻¹ even for fractional n); c/0 is determinately infinite by side-sign; ∞/∞ compares degrees. Trigonometric (radians only): everything steers into sin u/u → 1, tan u/u → 1, (1 − cos u)/u² → 1/2, with rescaling sin(kx)/x → k. Exponential: (eᵘ−1)/u → 1, (aᵘ−1)/u → ln a, the e-powers via (1+u)^{1/u} → e. Logarithmic: ln(1+u)/u → 1 (natural base mandatory). Mixed-family forms surrender to the x-bridge and legal splitting: (e^{5x}−1)/sin 2x → 5/2 (NEB-style), (x−1)/ln x → 1 (the tangent approximation of ln at 1). NEB 2024's sin 3x/x → 3 is the mantra made flesh: the coefficient survives, the argument dies.
