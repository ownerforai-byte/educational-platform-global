  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Capstone Limits Drill

    This canonical is the capstone limits drill: mixed problems spanning the whole limits toolkit — substitution, transformation, stars, squeeze, walls, and infinity — in exam format. It mirrors the parent limit-system canonical; the drill's discipline is route selection under time pressure.

  • 3. Deep Conceptual — Route Selection as the Exam Skill

    • Every limits problem has several working routes and one cheapest: the coefficient ratio beats the L'Hôpital passes; the ladder beats the dominant division; the squeeze beats any transformation of an oscillator. Capstone drills train the selection, which is where exam time is won.
    • The route table is a decision procedure, not a menu: read the symptom (form), take the row, execute. When a route stalls (form unchanged after a pass), the table says re-diagnose — persistence with a stalled route is the only real failure mode.
    • Cross-checks close the loop: star vs factoring, ladder vs L'Hôpital, squeeze vs damping — one confirming line turns a computed answer into a defended one.

  • 4. Deep Conceptual — Mixed Problems Test Architecture, Not Memory

    • A capstone item like (e^{2x} − 1 − 2x)/x² requires layered diagnosis: the 2x term cancels the exponential's first order, so the contest is at second order — the (eᵘ − 1 − u)/u² → 1/2 refinement. Mixed drills expose whether the student owns the order hierarchy or only the first-order stars.
    • Walls and squeeze in one problem: x²·sin(1/x) over sin x needs both the damping (numerator → 0 like x) and the star (denominator ~ x) — the ratio → 1. Architecture questions chain two theorems in one expression; recognising the chain is the skill.

  • 5. Worked Example Types

    • Type A — layered 0/0: first-order cancellation exposes second-order stars.
    • Type B — squeeze + star chains: damped oscillator over a vanishing denominator.
    • Type C — wall + classification: c/0 with side signs and the continuity verdict.
    • Type D — capstone infinity: mixed families with the ladder and a tie-case coefficient.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "Always L'Hôpital": the stars are cheaper and always legal where they apply. "The squeeze needs equal rails": it needs rails converging to the same value — boundedness alone proves nothing.

Confusion

  • Substituting the point and stopping — the limit asks what the function APPROACHES, not what it equals there.
  • Concluding the limit exists because both sides are 'big' — they must be the SAME FINITE number; 0/0 or −∞/+∞ is a jump.
  • Writing lim=∞ as a result — a divergent limit says the function is unbounded, not that it reached a value.

Universal Facts

  • Every limits problem in the syllabus falls to one row of the route table.

Examples

  • The layered race - (e^{2x}-1-2x)/x^2: the 2x cancels the exponential's first order, leaving the second-order star 1/2 - diagnosis in layers, not one glance.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{2x} − 1 − 2x)/x². — Layered diagnosis: e^{2x} − 1 ~ 2x (first order) — so the numerator's first-order part 2x cancels it exactly, and the contest moves to second order. Second-order star: (eᵘ − 1 − u)/u² → 1/2 as u → 0 (the exponential's parabolic correction). Apply with u = 2x: (e^{2x} − 1 − 2x)/x² = 4·(e^{2x} − 1 − 2x)/(2x)² → 4·(1/2) = 2. L'Hôpital double-check: pass 1 → (2e^{2x} − 2)/(2x) = (e^{2x} − 1)/x — still 0/0; pass 2 → 2e^{2x}/1 → 2 ✓. Deep point: the first-order cancellation was the diagnosis; the second-order star was the cure — layered forms are the hierarchy in action, and the two L'Hôpital passes are exactly the two orders climbed.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} [x²·sin(1/x)]/sin x. — Two theorems chained: numerator — the damped oscillator x²sin(1/x) → 0 by squeeze (amplitude x² vanishes); denominator — sin x ~ x by the first-order star. Assemble: the expression = [x²sin(1/x)/x]·[x/sin x] → [x·sin(1/x)]·[1] → 0 by squeeze (amplitude x). Direct version: |x²sin(1/x)/sin x| ≤ x²/|sin x| ~ x → 0 ✓. Deep point: the expression chains squeeze (numerator) and star (denominator) — capstone architecture; either theorem alone stalls, together they resolve in three lines.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→π/2⁻} tan x and lim_{x→π/2⁺} tan x, and classify the discontinuity of tan at π/2. — Left side: cos x → 0⁺ (x below π/2), sin x → 1 ⟹ tan x = sin/cos → +∞. Right side: cos x → 0⁻ ⟹ → −∞. Classification: infinite discontinuity — a splitting wall (the sides run to opposite infinities); tan is non-removable there, and the wall repeats at every π/2 + kπ. Deep point: the wall's geometry came from cos's sign change across π/2 — the denominator's simple zero with a sign-changing numerator neighbour; the side reading is the same c/0 machinery as rational walls, now in trigonometric clothing.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (x + sin x)/x, and explain why L'Hôpital's rule cannot certify it. — Route — split legally: (x + sin x)/x = 1 + sin x/x → 1 + 0 = 1 (the star sin x/x → 0 at infinity by squeeze: |sin x/x| ≤ 1/x → 0). Why L'Hôpital fails: the derivative ratio is (1 + cos x)/1, which oscillates in [0, 2] — it has no limit, so the rule's hypothesis ("lim f′/g′ exists") fails even though the original limit exists. The rule is sufficient, not necessary. Deep point: the squeeze certified sin x/x → 0 where differentiation could not — the capstone's lesson is that the route table outranks any single tool, and L'Hôpital's hypotheses are checks, not formalities.

Formulas

    • (eᵘ − 1 − u)/u² → 1/2 (second-order exponential star)
    • u − sin u ~ u³/6; tan u − u ~ u³/3

Key Points

  • Route table: substitution (continuity), 0/0 (factor/conjugate/steer/L'Hopital), c/0 (wall signs), damped oscillator (squeeze), joints (two-sided audit), infinity (degrees/ladder), power forms (log bridge). Selection is the exam skill - cheapest working route wins. Layered diagnosis: first-order cancellation exposes second-order stars, e.g. (e^u - 1 - u)/u^2 -> 1/2.

Summary

The capstone drill trains route selection: read the form, take the route table's row, execute the cheapest legal cure, and close with a cross-check. Layered forms (first-order cancellation exposing second-order stars), chained theorems (squeeze feeding stars), wall classifications, and L'Hôpital's hypothesis failures are the drill's four disciplines. The table always answers; persistence with a stalled route is the only way to lose.

Special Notes

  • The exclusion of x=a in the epsilon-delta definition is the key subtlety: it is what makes a limit independent of the function's value at the point.
  • limx→0​xsinx​=1 holds only in radians; in degrees the value is 180π​.
