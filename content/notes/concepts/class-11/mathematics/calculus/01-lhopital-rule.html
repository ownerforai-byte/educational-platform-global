Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — L'Hôpital's Rule

    L'Hôpital's rule: the derivative-based resolver for 0/0 and ∞/∞ — one theorem that mechanises the whole limits unit's transformation art.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The theorem

    • L'Hôpital's rule: if f(a) = g(a) = 0 (or both → ∞), and lim f′/g′ exists (finite or ±∞), then lim_{x→a} f/g = lim_{x→a} f′/g′.
    • Hypotheses (all mandatory): the form is genuinely 0/0 or ∞/∞; f, g differentiable near a (g′ ≠ 0 near a); the derived limit exists. One differentiation at a time — re-check the form after each pass.

    2.2 Why it works (Cauchy's MVT sketch)

    • f/g = [f − f(a)]/[g − g(a)] = f′(c)/g′(c) for some c between a and x (Cauchy's Mean Value Theorem) — as x → a, c → a, and the ratio of derivatives converges to the ratio's limit. The MVT of the monotonicity unit, dressed for quotients.

    2.3 The extended forms

    • ∞/∞: same theorem. 0·∞: rewrite as 0/(1/∞) = 0/0 or ∞/(1/0) = ∞/∞. ∞ − ∞: combine into a single fraction (common denominator) → 0/0. 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰: take logs — lim f^g = e^{lim g·ln f}, and g·ln f is a 0·∞ form resolved by L'Hôpital.

    2.4 The eˣ ladder

    • lim x/eˣ = 0 (two passes: 1/eˣ → 0); lim xⁿ/eˣ = 0 (n passes); lim ln x/x = 0; lim x^{1/x} = 1 (via ln). L'Hôpital re-derives the growth ladder on demand.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Differentiate the ratio, not the quotient

    • f′/g′ is the ratio of the separate derivatives — not the derivative of the quotient (that would be the quotient rule). The rule compares rates, not slopes of the combined function.

    4.2 Form discipline

    • Applying L'Hôpital to a determinate form (c/0, 0/c) is illegal and often wrong. Check the form by substitution before each differentiation; stop the moment the form resolves.

    4.3 When L'Hôpital fails

    • The derived limit may not exist even when the original does: lim (x + sin x)/x → 1 (since sin x/x → 0) but f′/g′ = 1 + cos x has no limit — the rule is sufficient, not necessary. Fallback: algebra, standard limits, squeeze.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Differentiating the whole quotient: (f/g)′ ≠ f′/g′ — L'Hôpital needs the ratio of derivatives, each differentiated separately. Skipping the form check: every pass needs a fresh 0/0 or ∞/∞ verdict. ∞ − ∞ by "subtracting infinities": combine into one fraction first — the algebra is the mathematics.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{2x} − 1)/sin 3x by L'Hôpital's rule. — Form: 0/0 ✓. Differentiate separately: numerator 2e^{2x}; denominator 3cos 3x. Ratio: 2e^{2x}/(3cos 3x) → 2/(3·1) = 2/3. (Cross-check by stars: (e^{2x}−1)/x → 2, sin 3x/x → 3 ⟹ 2/3 ✓.)

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (ln x)/x. — Form: ∞/∞ ✓. Differentiate: (1/x)/1 = 1/x → 0. The growth ladder's first rung re-derived: logarithms lose to linear powers — and iterating gives ln x/xᵃ → 0 for every a > 0.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} x·ln x. — Form: 0·∞ ⟹ convert: x·ln x = ln x/(1/x) — now ∞/∞ ✓. Differentiate: (1/x)/(−1/x²) = −x → 0. So x ln x → 0⁻: the log's slow growth loses to x's vanishing — the standard ∞·0 resolution.

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — L'Hôpital as Linearisation Compared

    • The deep content: near a point where f and g both vanish, f(x) ≈ f′(a)(x − a) and g(x) ≈ g′(a)(x − a) — each function is its tangent to first order. The ratio of the functions is therefore the ratio of their tangents: f′(a)/g′(a). L'Hôpital is the statement that first-order behaviour decides the 0/0 contest.
    • Connection to the x-bridge: the standard-limit steering of the earlier unit (sin u ~ u, eᵘ − 1 ~ u) is the same principle — each star is a tangent approximation. L'Hôpital automates what the stars did by hand, which is why it needs differentiability where the stars needed only their quoted limits.
    • Higher-order refinement: when f′(a) = g′(a) = 0, first order is a tie and the contest moves to second order (then f′′/g′′ decides) — repeated L'Hôpital is exactly this climb through the orders, terminating when one function's leading term appears.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — The Failure Case Teaches the Hypotheses

    • lim_{x→∞} (x + sin x)/x = 1 (since sin x/x → 0 by squeeze), but the derivative ratio (1 + cos x)/1 has no limit (oscillates in [0, 2]). The rule's conclusion fails even though the original limit exists.
    • Why: L'Hôpital's hypotheses include "lim f′/g′ exists" — a hypothesis, not a conclusion. The rule is sufficient, not necessary: it licenses a computation when its conditions hold, and says nothing when they fail.
    • Discipline derived: always check the form (0/0 or ∞/∞) and expect the derived limit to settle; if the derived ratio oscillates, retreat to the stars, the squeeze, or algebra — the original limit may still be perfectly computable.

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — The Power Forms Through the Log Bridge

    • 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ all surrender to one move: f^g = e^{g·ln f} — the exponent's limit g·ln f is a 0·∞ form, resolved by flipping one factor into a quotient and applying L'Hôpital.
    • Worked pattern: lim_{x→0⁺} xˣ: ln route gives lim x ln x = lim ln x/(1/x) = lim (1/x)/(−1/x²) = lim(−x) = 0 ⟹ xˣ → e⁰ = 1. The indeterminate power collapses because the log's slow growth loses to the base's collapse — the growth ladder operating at a point instead of at infinity.
    • Why logs and not direct computation: powers with variable base and exponent have no algebraic form — the exponential function is the only bridge, and it works because exp and ln are inverse isomorphisms between addition and multiplication of rates.

Examples

  • The failing case - (x + sin x)/x converges to 1 while its derivative ratio 1 + cos x oscillates: L'Hopital is a one-way licence, not an equivalence.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (e^{2x} − 1)/sin 3x using L'Hôpital's rule. — Form check: substitution gives (1 − 1)/0 = 0/0 — the rule's hypothesis holds. Differentiate separately (the ratio of derivatives, not the quotient rule): numerator d/dx(e^{2x} − 1) = 2e^{2x} (chain rule); denominator d/dx(sin 3x) = 3cos 3x. New ratio: 2e^{2x}/(3cos 3x) — substituting x = 0: 2·1/(3·1) = 2/3 (no second pass needed — the form resolved). Cross-check by standard limits: (e^{2x} − 1)/x → 2 and sin 3x/x → 3 ⟹ the quotient → 2/3 ✓. Two independent routes agreeing — L'Hôpital mechanises what the stars did by steering.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (ln x)/x. — Form check: ∞/∞ ✓. Differentiate separately: numerator d/dx(ln x) = 1/x; denominator d/dx(x) = 1. New ratio: (1/x)/1 = 1/x → 0 as x → ∞. Conclusion: logarithms grow strictly slower than any positive power — here the first power suffices. Iteration: applying the rule n more times to ln x/xᵃ (still ∞/∞) gives a/(a·xᵃ) → 0 — the growth ladder re-derived mechanically: ln x ≪ xᵃ for every a > 0. L'Hôpital turns the ladder from a quoted theorem into a repeatable computation.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} x·ln x. — Form check: 0·(−∞) — the 0·∞ species, not directly covered ⟹ convert. Flip one factor: x·ln x = ln x/(1/x) — now ∞/∞ (ln x → ∞, 1/x → ∞ as x → 0⁺) ✓. Differentiate separately: numerator 1/x; denominator d/dx(1/x) = −1/x². New ratio: (1/x)/(−1/x²) = −x → 0. Conclusion: lim_{x→0⁺} x ln x = 0 (approached from below — ln x < 0). Reading: near 0 the logarithm's dive is slower than x's collapse, so their product vanishes — the standard fact underpinning x^x → 1 via the log bridge.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³ using L'Hôpital's rule, applying it repeatedly until the form resolves. — Pass 0 (form check): (0 − 0)/0 = 0/0 ✓. Pass 1: differentiate separately: numerator 1 − cos x; denominator 3x². New ratio (1 − cos x)/(3x²) — form check: (1 − 1)/0 = 0/0 again ✓. Pass 2: numerator sin x; denominator 6x — form: 0/0 ✓. Pass 3: numerator cos x; denominator 6 — substitution gives cos 0/6 = 1/6 — the form has resolved (no longer indeterminate). Answer: 1/6. Cross-check: the cubic refinement x − sin x ~ x³/6 gives the same 1/6 ✓. Deep points: (1) each pass requires a fresh form check — the rule's hypotheses must hold at every stage, and writing the 0/0 verdicts is the graded discipline; (2) three passes correspond exactly to the three orders of vanishing (x − sin x is order 3): the rule climbs the Taylor hierarchy one derivative at a time until the contestants' leading terms separate; (3) the alternative single-pass route — recognizing 1 − cos x ~ x²/2 — shows L'Hôpital and the stars are two views of one theory.
  • NEB-style (6m): Using L'Hôpital's rule and the exponential bridge, show that lim_{x→0⁺} xˣ = 1. — Form check: xˣ has base → 0⁺ and exponent → 0 ⟹ the 0⁰ indeterminate form — not 1, not 0. Log bridge: xˣ = e^{x ln x} identically, so the limit is e^L with L = lim_{x→0⁺} x·ln x — a 0·∞ form ⟹ convert. Flip one factor: x ln x = ln x/(1/x) — now ∞/∞ ✓. L'Hôpital: differentiate separately — numerator 1/x, denominator −1/x² ⟹ ratio = (1/x)/(−1/x²) = −x → 0. So L = 0 and the limit is e⁰ = 1. Interpretation: xˣ approaches 1 from below as x → 0⁺ (since x ln x < 0), the function dipping slightly under 1 before climbing back — the log's dive loses the race against x's collapse. Deep point: the entire power-form technology is one identity (f^g = e^{g ln f}) plus one flip (0·∞ → ∞/∞) plus one differentiation — three standard moves chained, each with its hypothesis visible.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→π/2} (sec x − tan x). — Form check: sec x − tan x = (1/cos x) − (sin x/cos x) → ∞ − ∞ — the ∞ − ∞ species ⟹ combine into a single fraction first. Combine: sec x − tan x = (1 − sin x)/cos x. Form: (1 − 1)/0 = 0/0 ✓. L'Hôpital: differentiate separately — numerator −cos x; denominator −sin x ⟹ ratio = cos x/sin x = cot x. Substitute: cot(π/2) = 0. Answer: 0. Cross-check without L'Hôpital: (1 − sin x)/cos x = [(1 − sin x)(1 + sin x)]/[(cos x)(1 + sin x)] = cos²x/[cos x(1 + sin x)] = cos x/(1 + sin x) → 0/(1 + 1) = 0 ✓ — the conjugate route agrees. Deep point: ∞ − ∞ forms must be algebraically merged before any differentiation — the rule operates on quotients only, and the common denominator (or conjugate) is the mandatory preprocessing.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} ((x² + 1)/(x² − 1))^{x²}. — Form check: base = (x²+1)/(x²−1) = 1 + 2/(x²−1) → 1; exponent x² → ∞ ⟹ 1^∞. Log bridge: the limit is e^L with L = lim x²·ln[(x²+1)/(x²−1)] = lim x²·ln(1 + 2/(x²−1)). Steer the log: ln(1 + u) ~ u with u = 2/(x²−1) → 0 ⟹ L = lim x²·2/(x²−1) = lim 2x²/(x²−1) = 2 (degree rule: equal degrees, leading ratio 2). Answer: e². L'Hôpital cross-check on L: write L = lim ln(1 + 2/(x²−1))/(1/x²) — a 0/0 form at infinity; differentiating separately (chain rule on top, power on bottom) and simplifying recovers 2x²/(x²−1) → 2 ✓. Deep point: the exponent bookkeeping lim uv is the entire battle — the base's displacement 2/(x²−1) times the exponent x² leaves the constant 2, and the growth race between displacement and exponent is precisely the 1^∞ form's meaning.

    • lim f^g = e^{lim g·ln f}

Key Points

  • L'Hopital's rule: for 0/0 or inf/inf with the derived limit existing, lim f/g = lim f'/g' (ratio of separate derivatives, NOT the quotient rule). Hypotheses: genuine form, differentiability near a, g' != 0, derived limit exists; re-check the form after every pass. Proof engine: Cauchy's MVT - f/g = f'(c)/g'(c) for c between a and x. Extended forms: 0*inf => f/(1/g); inf - inf => common denominator; 1^inf, 0^0, inf^0 => e^(lim g ln f). E-ladder re-derived: x/e^x -> 0, ln x/x -> 0, x ln x -> 0. Rule is sufficient not necessary: (x + sin x)/x -> 1 but 1 + cos x has no limit.

Summary

L'Hôpital's rule mechanises the rate race: for a genuine 0/0 or ∞/∞, lim f/g = lim f′/g′ — the ratio of the separate derivatives (never the quotient rule), justified by Cauchy's MVT (f/g = f′(c)/g′(c) for an intermediate c). Discipline is everything: check the form before every pass, differentiate separately, stop when the form resolves ((e^{2x}−1)/sin 3x → 2e^{2x}/3cos 3x → 2/3). The extended forms convert first: 0·∞ flips a factor (x ln x → ln x/(1/x) → −x → 0), ∞ − ∞ combines fractions, and the power forms 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ go through the log bridge e^{lim g ln f}. The rule re-derives the growth ladder on demand (ln x/x → 0, x/eˣ → 0) — but it is sufficient, not necessary: (x + sin x)/x → 1 while 1 + cos x has no limit, so algebra, stars and squeeze remain in the toolkit. Form, differentiate, repeat — with the hypotheses held visibly.

Differentiate separately (the ratio of derivatives, not the quotient rule): numerator d/dx(e^{2x} − 1) = 2e^{2x} (chain rule); denominator d/dx(sin 3x) = 3cos 3x. New ratio: 2e^{2x}/(3cos 3x) — substituting x = 0: 2·1/(3·1) = 2/3 (no second pass needed — the form resolved). Cross-check by standard limits: (e^{2x} − 1)/x → 2 and sin 3x/x → 3 ⟹ the quotient → 2/3 ✓. Two independent routes agreeing — L'Hôpital mechanises what the stars did by steering.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} (ln x)/x. — Form check: ∞/∞ ✓. Differentiate separately: numerator d/dx(ln x) = 1/x; denominator d/dx(x) = 1. New ratio: (1/x)/1 = 1/x → 0 as x → ∞. Conclusion: logarithms grow strictly slower than any positive power — here the first power suffices. Iteration: applying the rule n more times to ln x/xᵃ (still ∞/∞) gives a/(a·xᵃ) → 0 — the growth ladder re-derived mechanically: ln x ≪ xᵃ for every a > 0. L'Hôpital turns the ladder from a quoted theorem into a repeatable computation.
  • NEB-style (3m): Evaluate lim_{x→0⁺} x·ln x. — Form check: 0·(−∞) — the 0·∞ species, not directly covered ⟹ convert. Flip one factor: x·ln x = ln x/(1/x) — now ∞/∞ (ln x → ∞, 1/x → ∞ as x → 0⁺) ✓. Differentiate separately: numerator 1/x; denominator d/dx(1/x) = −1/x². New ratio: (1/x)/(−1/x²) = −x → 0. Conclusion: lim_{x→0⁺} x ln x = 0 (approached from below — ln x < 0). Reading: near 0 the logarithm's dive is slower than x's collapse, so their product vanishes — the standard fact underpinning x^x → 1 via the log bridge.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→0} (x − sin x)/x³ using L'Hôpital's rule, applying it repeatedly until the form resolves. — Pass 0 (form check): (0 − 0)/0 = 0/0 ✓. Pass 1: differentiate separately: numerator 1 − cos x; denominator 3x². New ratio (1 − cos x)/(3x²) — form check: (1 − 1)/0 = 0/0 again ✓. Pass 2: numerator sin x; denominator 6x — form: 0/0 ✓. Pass 3: numerator cos x; denominator 6 — substitution gives cos 0/6 = 1/6 — the form has resolved (no longer indeterminate). Answer: 1/6. Cross-check: the cubic refinement x − sin x ~ x³/6 gives the same 1/6 ✓. Deep points: (1) each pass requires a fresh form check — the rule's hypotheses must hold at every stage, and writing the 0/0 verdicts is the graded discipline; (2) three passes correspond exactly to the three orders of vanishing (x − sin x is order 3): the rule climbs the Taylor hierarchy one derivative at a time until the contestants' leading terms separate; (3) the alternative single-pass route — recognizing 1 − cos x ~ x²/2 — shows L'Hôpital and the stars are two views of one theory.
  • NEB-style (6m): Using L'Hôpital's rule and the exponential bridge, show that lim_{x→0⁺} xˣ = 1. — Form check: xˣ has base → 0⁺ and exponent → 0 ⟹ the 0⁰ indeterminate form — not 1, not 0. Log bridge: xˣ = e^{x ln x} identically, so the limit is e^L with L = lim_{x→0⁺} x·ln x — a 0·∞ form ⟹ convert. Flip one factor: x ln x = ln x/(1/x) — now ∞/∞ ✓. L'Hôpital: differentiate separately — numerator 1/x, denominator −1/x² ⟹ ratio = (1/x)/(−1/x²) = −x → 0. So L = 0 and the limit is e⁰ = 1. Interpretation: xˣ approaches 1 from below as x → 0⁺ (since x ln x < 0), the function dipping slightly under 1 before climbing back — the log's dive loses the race against x's collapse. Deep point: the entire power-form technology is one identity (f^g = e^{g ln f}) plus one flip (0·∞ → ∞/∞) plus one differentiation — three standard moves chained, each with its hypothesis visible.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate lim_{x→π/2} (sec x − tan x). — Form check: sec x − tan x = (1/cos x) − (sin x/cos x) → ∞ − ∞ — the ∞ − ∞ species ⟹ combine into a single fraction first. Combine: sec x − tan x = (1 − sin x)/cos x. Form: (1 − 1)/0 = 0/0 ✓. L'Hôpital: differentiate separately — numerator −cos x; denominator −sin x ⟹ ratio = cos x/sin x = cot x. Substitute: cot(π/2) = 0. Answer: 0. Cross-check without L'Hôpital: (1 − sin x)/cos x = [(1 − sin x)(1 + sin x)]/[(cos x)(1 + sin x)] = cos²x/[cos x(1 + sin x)] = cos x/(1 + sin x) → 0/(1 + 1) = 0 ✓ — the conjugate route agrees. Deep point: ∞ − ∞ forms must be algebraically merged before any differentiation — the rule operates on quotients only, and the common denominator (or conjugate) is the mandatory preprocessing.
  • NEB-style (6m): Evaluate lim_{x→∞} ((x² + 1)/(x² − 1))^{x²}. — Form check: base = (x²+1)/(x²−1) = 1 + 2/(x²−1) → 1; exponent x² → ∞ ⟹ 1^∞. Log bridge: the limit is e^L with L = lim x²·ln[(x²+1)/(x²−1)] = lim x²·ln(1 + 2/(x²−1)). Steer the log: ln(1 + u) ~ u with u = 2/(x²−1) → 0 ⟹ L = lim x²·2/(x²−1) = lim 2x²/(x²−1) = 2 (degree rule: equal degrees, leading ratio 2). Answer: e². L'Hôpital cross-check on L: write L = lim ln(1 + 2/(x²−1))/(1/x²) — a 0/0 form at infinity; differentiating separately (chain rule on top, power on bottom) and simplifying recovers 2x²/(x²−1) → 2 ✓. Deep point: the exponent bookkeeping lim uv is the entire battle — the base's displacement 2/(x²−1) times the exponent x² leaves the constant 2, and the growth race between displacement and exponent is precisely the 1^∞ form's meaning.
