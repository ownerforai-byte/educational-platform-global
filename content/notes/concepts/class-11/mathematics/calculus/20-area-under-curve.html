Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to Area Under a Curve

    This canonical is the short-form doorway to area under a curve: the signed-area reading of the definite integral and the standard area computations in one page. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the theorems, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

  • 2. Theorem Mirror

    • Signed area: ∫ₐᵇf(x)dx counts area above the axis positively, below negatively — the integral is a net, not a total.
    • True (geometric) area: ∫ₐᵇ|f(x)|dx — split at the axis crossings, integrate each piece, add magnitudes.
    • Area between curve and axis: find the roots of f in [a, b], partition, integrate |f| piecewise.
    • Standard results: ∫₀ᵃ√(a² − x²)dx = πa²/4 (quarter circle); area of a triangle under a line = ½·base·height.

  • 3. Deep Conceptual — Signed vs Geometric: The Ledger and the Map

    • The definite integral is a ledger (credits above the axis, debits below — the net displacement reading); the geometric area is a map (every region counts positively — the total distance reading). One number, two questions: "where did I end up?" vs "how much ground did I cover?"
    • The split-at-roots protocol: the sign of f can only change at its zeros, so the roots partition [a, b] into constant-sign strips — integrate each strip and add |·|. Skipping the split is the classic error whenever the curve dips below the axis.
    • Velocity/position echo: ∫v dt is displacement (signed); ∫|v| dt is distance (geometric) — the ledger/map distinction is the same one physics uses, which is why the area reading is more than geometry.

  • 4. Deep Conceptual — Symmetry and Standard Shapes as Shortcuts

    • Even/odd symmetry: for even f, ∫₋ₐᵃf = 2∫₀ᵃf; for odd f, ∫₋ₐᵃf = 0 — the signed ledger cancels symmetric debits and credits. Half the work for even functions, zero work for odd ones.
    • Recognisable shapes: lines give triangles and trapezoids, circles give πa²/4 pieces — before integrating, ask whether the region is a standard shape; the integral is the general tool, geometry the shortcut.
    • The quarter-circle result: ∫₀ᵃ√(a²−x²)dx = πa²/4 comes from y² = a² − x² being the circle — recognising the integrand as a circle's upper half converts calculus to geometry.

  • 5. Worked Example Types

    • Type A — simple positive region: one anti-derivative, endpoints, done.
    • Type B — sign change mid-interval: split at the root, add magnitudes.
    • Type C — symmetry shortcut: even/odd halving or cancellation.
    • Type D — recognised shape: line (triangle) or circle (quarter).

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "The integral is the area": it is the signed area — split at roots for the geometric answer. "Always integrate": recognise triangles and circular quarters first.

Confusion

  • Reporting the signed integral as the area when the curve dips below the axis.
  • Forgetting to split at the roots before integrating.
  • Writing +C on an area calculation.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (5m): Find the area under y = x² from x = 0 to x = 3. — Sign check: x² ≥ 0 on [0, 3] ⟹ signed = geometric, no split needed. Evaluate: ∫₀³x²dx = [x³/3]₀³ = 9 square units. Shape note: the region is a parabolic segment — no standard shape, so the integral is the tool. Deep point: the sign check precedes the evaluation — on intervals where the curve stays on one side, the ledger and the map agree and one anti-derivative answers both questions.
  • NEB-style (6m): Find the geometric area between y = x² − 4 and the x-axis for 0 ≤ x ≤ 5. — Roots in [0, 5]: x² − 4 = 0 ⟹ x = 2 (and −2, outside). Sign strips: f < 0 on [0, 2) (dips below), f > 0 on (2, 5]. Piece 1: ∫₀²(4 − x²)dx = [4x − x³/3]₀² = 8 − 8/3 = 16/3. Piece 2: ∫₂⁵(x² − 4)dx = [x³/3 − 4x]₂⁵ = (125/3 − 20) − (8/3 − 8) = 65/3 − (−16/3) = 65/3 − 8/3 + 8 = 57/3 + 8 = 19 + 8 = 27. Geometric area: 16/3 + 27 = 16/3 + 81/3 = 97/3 ≈ 32.33. Signed comparison: ∫₀⁵(x²−4)dx = [x³/3 − 4x]₀⁵ = 125/3 − 20 = 65/3 ≈ 21.67 — smaller by exactly 2·(16/3), the double-counted dip. Deep point: the raw integral understates the map by twice the below-axis region — the ledger's debit is counted once as negative and should count once as positive; the difference 2·16/3 is the signature of the skipped split.
  • NEB-style (5m): Evaluate ∫₋₂²x³dx and ∫₋₂²x²dx, and explain the asymmetry. — Odd integrand: x³ is odd ⟹ ∫₋₂²x³dx = 0 — the credit on [0, 2] exactly cancels the debit on [−2, 0]. Even integrand: x² is even ⟹ ∫₋₂²x²dx = 2∫₀²x²dx = 2·(8/3) = 16/3. Interpretation: the odd function's signed ledger nets to zero while its geometric area is 2·∫₀²x³dx = 8 — symmetry statements are about the signed integral. Deep point: even/odd symmetry is a signed-area theorem: halving for even functions, cancellation for odd ones — and confusing the two readings (0 vs 8 for x³) is exactly the ledger/map distinction in action.
  • NEB-style (6m): Find the area enclosed by y = √(9 − x²) and the x-axis, and verify by geometry. — Recognise the shape: y² = 9 − x² ⟹ x² + y² = 9 — the upper semicircle of radius 3. Domain: −3 ≤ x ≤ 3; the integrand is nonnegative throughout ⟹ no split. Evaluate: area = ∫₋₃³√(9−x²)dx = 2∫₀³√(9−x²)dx = 2·(π·9/4) = 9π/2 — using the quarter-circle standard twice. Geometric verification: semicircle area = πr²/2 = 9π/2 ✓. Deep point: the integrand's form √(a² − x²) is the circle's signature — recognising it converts the computation to πa²/4 per quarter; the symmetry halving (even function) and the shape recognition are two shortcuts stacking on one integral.

Formulas

    • Geometric area = ∫ₐᵇ|f(x)|dx
    • ∫₀ᵃ√(a² − x²)dx = πa²/4; ∫₋ₐᵃf = 2∫₀ᵃf (even), 0 (odd)

Key Points

  • Signed area: above the axis positive, below negative - the integral is a net. Geometric area: integral of |f| - split at the roots, integrate piecewise, add magnitudes. Displacement vs distance is the same distinction. Symmetry: even f halves the work, odd f cancels to zero on [-a, a]. Recognise standard shapes first: lines give triangles/trapezoids, sqrt(a^2 - x^2) gives quarter circles (pi a^2/4).

Summary

Area under a curve begins with the ledger/map distinction: the definite integral is signed (net displacement), the geometric area is |f| split at the roots (total ground). Symmetry halves or cancels the signed work, and recognised shapes — triangles, trapezoids, quarter circles — replace anti-derivatives with geometry. Find the roots first, ask whether the region is standard, and state which area the question wants; the two answers differ by exactly twice the dipped region.

Special Notes

  • Where a formula is quoted it is valid only under the conditions stated; Reporting the signed integral as the area when the curve dips below the axis.
  • Every result here follows from the definitions, so a first-principles derivation is always a valid fallback in the exam.
