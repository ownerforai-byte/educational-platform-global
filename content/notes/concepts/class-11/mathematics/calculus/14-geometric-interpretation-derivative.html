Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Companion Doorway to the Derivative's Geometric Meaning

    This canonical is the short-form doorway to the derivative's geometric interpretation: tangent slope, increasing/decreasing, and the rate reading in one page. The parent canonical carries the full treatment; this companion mirrors the theorems, adds the deep readings, and drills four heavy-mark problems.

  • 2. Theorem Mirror

    • Tangent slope: f′(a) = slope of the tangent at (a, f(a)) — the limiting position of secants through (a, f(a)) and (a+h, f(a+h)).
    • Tangent line: y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a); normal line: slope −1/f′(a) (f′(a) ≠ 0); vertical tangent when f′ → ±∞.
    • Monotonicity reading: f′ > 0 on an interval ⟹ increasing; f′ < 0 ⟹ decreasing; f′ = 0 at a point ⟹ stationary (not necessarily extreme).
    • Rate reading: f′(t) = instantaneous rate of change of f with respect to t — velocity, growth rate, marginal anything.

  • 3. Deep Conceptual — Slope as the Limit of Secants

    • The tangent is not "the line touching at one point" — circles aside, that folk definition fails (cubics touch and cross). The precise definition is dynamic: the secant through two nearby points, with the second point sliding in. The limit of chords is the tangent; the limit of their slopes is f′(a).
    • One-sided tangents: where f′₊ ≠ f′₋ (a corner), two half-tangents exist and no single tangent does — the geometric face of the one-sided derivative test.
    • Vertical tangents: when the difference quotient blows up (f′ → ±∞) the tangent is vertical — x = a. The derivative's failure to be finite is itself geometric information.

  • 4. Deep Conceptual — The Sign of f′ Is a Global Statement

    • Local sign, global consequence: if f′ > 0 at every point of an interval, the function is strictly increasing on the whole interval — the Mean Value Theorem converts the local derivative data into a global monotonicity statement. This is the bridge that makes derivative sign tables into curve-sketching tools.
    • Rates are dimension-conscious: f′ carries the units of f per unit of x — m/s for position, °C/min for temperature. The geometric slope and the physical rate are the same number read in two languages; problems that mix them (related rates) are translations, not new mathematics.

  • 5. Worked Example Types

    • Type A — tangent/normal at a point: evaluate f and f′, write both lines.
    • Type B — corner geometry: one-sided slopes, two half-tangents, no tangent.
    • Type C — sign table sketching: f′ signs ⟹ increasing/decreasing intervals.
    • Type D — rate interpretation: read f′(t₀) as a physical rate with units.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "Tangent touches at exactly one point": the folk definition — the limit-of-secants definition is the real one. "f′ = 0 means a max or min": it means stationary; classification needs the second derivative or a sign study.

Confusion

  • Computing f′(a) and reporting it as the angle of inclination, forgetting to apply θ=tan−1(f′(a)).
  • Saying a horizontal tangent guarantees a maximum; it marks a candidate only.
  • Computing the normal's slope as −f′(a)1​ when f′(a)=0, which is division by zero — the normal is vertical instead.

Practice

  • Find the tangent to y = x² at (2, 4).
  • Find the tangent and normal to y = x³ − 2x at x = 1.
  • Interpret f′(3) = −5 for f(t) measuring temperature in °C at time t in minutes.

Universal Facts

  • The MVT is the bridge from local derivative signs to global monotonicity.

Examples

  • The touching test fails - y = x^3 at 0 touches the x-axis and crosses it; the limit-of-secants definition survives where the folk definition dies.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (5m): Find the tangent line to y = x² at (2, 4) and the normal line there. — Slope: y′ = 2x ⟹ at x = 2: m = 4. Tangent: y − 4 = 4(x − 2) ⟹ y = 4x − 4. Normal: slope −1/4 ⟹ y − 4 = −(1/4)(x − 2) ⟹ x + 4y = 18. Geometric check: the tangent passes through the origin? At x = 0: y = −4 — no; but the tangent's slope 4 equals the secant slope from (2,4) to (2+h, (2+h)²) in the limit: [(2+h)² − 4]/h = 4 + h → 4 ✓. Deep point: the normal's slope is the negative reciprocal because the two lines are perpendicular — the product of their slopes is −1; tangent-normal pairs are one computation and one reciprocal.
  • NEB-style (6m): Find the tangent and normal to y = x³ − 2x at x = 1, and determine where the tangent crosses the x-axis. — Point: f(1) = 1 − 2 = −1 ⟹ (1, −1). Slope: f′ = 3x² − 2 ⟹ f′(1) = 1. Tangent: y + 1 = 1(x − 1) ⟹ y = x − 2; it crosses the x-axis at x = 2. Normal: slope −1 ⟹ y + 1 = −(x − 1) ⟹ y = −x — passing through the origin. Deep point: the tangent crossing the axis at x = 2 while the curve itself crosses at x = √2 ≈ 1.414 shows the tangent is a local linearisation — it matches the curve's direction at the point and drifts elsewhere; the gap between 2 and √2 is the linearisation error in action.
  • NEB-style (5m): For f(x) = |x|, describe the tangent geometry at x = 0 and connect it to the derivative's failure. — Right side: slope +1 ⟹ half-tangent y = x for x ≥ 0. Left side: slope −1 ⟹ half-tangent y = −x for x ≤ 0. At 0: the two half-tangents meet at a 90° angle — no single tangent exists, matching f′(0)'s non-existence (RHD +1, LHD −1). Folk definition check: infinitely many lines touch the graph only at the origin, yet none is "the" tangent — the limit-of-secants definition, not the touching count, is what fails here. Deep point: the corner is where geometry and analysis agree perfectly: the picture's kink and the quotient's disagreement are the same fact in two languages.
  • NEB-style (6m): A body's position is s(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t (metres, seconds). Find the velocity at t = 2 and interpret its sign; find when the body momentarily stops. — Velocity: v(t) = s′(t) = 3t² − 12t + 9 = 3(t − 1)(t − 3). At t = 2: v = 3(1)(−1) = −3 m/s — moving in the negative direction at 3 m/s. Stops when: v = 0 ⟹ t = 1 and t = 3 — momentary rests. Direction timeline: v > 0 on (0, 1) (forward), v < 0 on (1, 3) (backward), v > 0 after 3 (forward again). Deep point: the derivative's sign is the physical direction — the sign table built for monotonicity is the same tool that reads a journey; position, velocity, acceleration are s, s′, s″, the geometric reading wearing a lab coat.

Formulas

    • Tangent: y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a); Normal: y − f(a) = −(1/f′(a))(x − a)
    • f′ > 0 on (a, b) ⟹ f strictly increasing on (a, b)

Key Points

  • Tangent slope = f'(a) = limit of secant slopes; tangent line y - f(a) = f'(a)(x - a); normal slope -1/f'(a); vertical tangent when f' -> +-inf. Corners have two half-tangents. f' > 0 on an interval implies increasing (MVT converts local signs to global monotonicity). f' = 0 means stationary, not necessarily extreme. The derivative is a rate with units - geometry and physics are the same number.

Summary

The derivative's geometric meaning is dynamic: the tangent is the limiting secant, its slope f′(a), the normal its perpendicular partner, and a vertical tangent is the derivative's finite failure. The sign of f′ is a local fact that the Mean Value Theorem promotes to global monotonicity, and the same number reads as a physical rate with units. Corners split the tangent in two, x³ touches and crosses, and every curve-sketching table is this interpretation put to work.

Special Notes

  • Where a formula is quoted it is valid only under the conditions stated; Computing f′(a) and reporting it as the angle of inclination, forgetting to apply θ=tan−1(f′(a)).
  • Every result here follows from the definitions, so a first-principles derivation is always a valid fallback in the exam.

  • f′=0 there
  • f′′=0 there

C

Explanation

Horizontal tangent means zero slope, i.e. f′=0 there. Whether that is an extremum needs a further test.

3.

The normal to y=x3 at the origin is:

Options
  • y=0
  • x=0
  • y=x
  • y=−x
Answer

B

Explanation

The tangent is horizontal, so the normal is vertical, i.e. x=0; the formula −f′1​=−01​ is undefined, which is exactly why.
