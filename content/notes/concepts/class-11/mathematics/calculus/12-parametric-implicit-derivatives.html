Notes

  • Parametric rule: if x=x(t) and y=y(t) then dxdy​=dx/dtdy/dt​, provided dtdx​=0. The parameter cancels, which is what allows a curve with no closed-form y=f(x) to be differentiated at all.
  • Why dtdx​=0 is special: it means dxdy​ is infinite, i.e. a vertical tangent. A circle x=cost, y=sint gives dxdy​=−sintcost​ and a vertical tangent at t=0, matching the geometry.
  • Implicit differentiation: differentiate F(x,y)=0 with respect to x, treating y as a function of x throughout, then collect the dxdy​ terms. For x2+y2=25 one gets 2x+2ydxdy​=0, hence dxdy​=−yx​.
  • Always state the assumption y=y(x) when differentiating implicitly; without it the method is a guess, and the resulting formula is only valid where ∂y∂F​=0.
  • Second derivatives from parametric form are found by differentiating dxdy​ with respect to t and dividing by dtdx​ again: dx2d2y​=dtd​(dx/dtdy/dt​)÷dtdx​.
  • Higher-order implicit derivatives substitute back the expression obtained for dxdy​ before differentiating again, so for a circle dx2d2y​=−y3r2​ once x2+y2=r2 is used.

Confusion

  • Inverting the ratio in the parametric rule, giving dtdx​/dtdy​ instead.
  • Treating y as a constant in implicit differentiation.
  • Forgetting the second division by dtdx​ when computing a parametric second derivative.

Universal Facts

  • Projectile motion is the standard parametric curve: eliminating t is clumsy, while dxdy​=vx​vy​​ gives the trajectory angle instantly.
  • A cycloid, the curve traced by a point on a rolling wheel, has no single-valued y(x) over a full arch, which is precisely why parametric differentiation is needed for it.

Formulas

  • Parametric: dxdy​=dx/dtdy/dt​ for dx/dt=0
  • Implicit: differentiate F(x,y)=0 w.r.t. x and solve for y′
  • Parametric 2nd: dx2d2y​=dtd​(dxdy​)÷dtdx​
  • Circle: dxdy​=−yx​, dx2d2y​=−y3r2​

Key Points

  • Parametric derivatives are a ratio of parameter derivatives, not a product.
  • dtdx​=0 corresponds to a vertical tangent.
  • Implicit differentiation presumes y is a differentiable function of x.

Summary

Parametric and implicit differentiation both exist because a curve need not be expressible as y=f(x). In the parametric case the parameter cancels, leaving a ratio of parameter derivatives, and a vanishing dtdx​ marks a vertical tangent. In the implicit case one differentiates an equation solved for neither variable, treating y as a function of x throughout, then isolates dxdy​.

Special Notes

  • Where a formula is quoted it is valid only under the conditions stated; Inverting the ratio in the parametric rule, giving dtdx​/dtdy​ instead.
  • Every result here follows from the definitions, so a first-principles derivation is always a valid fallback in the exam.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: dxdy​=dx/dtdy/dt​ for parametric equations when dx/dt=0.
  • Statement 2: dtdx​=0 indicates a vertical tangent.
  • Statement 3: Implicit differentiation requires y to be a differentiable function of x.
  • Statement 4: For x2+y2=r2, dxdy​=−yx​.
  • Statement 5: A parametric second derivative divides by dtdx​ a second time.

Important Notes

  • Topic anchor: Higher-order implicit derivatives substitute back the expression obtained for fracdydx before differentiating again, so for a circle fracd2ydx2=−fracr2y3 once x2+y2=r2 is used.

Exam Short Tricks

  • For the circle, always convert y to ±r2−x2​ before computing a second derivative — implicit is faster but needs the substitution step.
  • Draw the parametric curve for a few values of t if the shape is unclear; the vertical tangent becomes obvious.

Exam Notes

  • Slope of a parametric curve and slope of a circle by implicit differentiation are both standard 3-mark questions.
