  • 1. Topic Introduction — Limits as a System

    This canonical consolidates the limit of a function, the indeterminate forms, and the algebraic properties of limits — the operating system every later technique runs on.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The definition, restated

    • lim_{x→a} f(x) = L ⟺ f(x) can be made arbitrarily close to L by taking x sufficiently close to a (x ≠ a). The limit is about the neighbourhood, not the point.

    2.2 The indeterminate forms

    • 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ — seven forms where substitution fails and transformation is mandatory. Each is a shape, not a value: 0/0 candidates can converge to anything (x/x → 1; x²/x² → 1; x·0 forms vary).
    • Determinate outcomes: c/0⁺ → +∞, c/0⁻ → −∞ (c ≠ 0), 0/∞ → 0, ∞/c → ∞ — these need no transformation.

    2.3 The algebraic properties (theorems)

    • If lim f = L and lim g = M (both finite): sum: lim (f+g) = L + M; product: lim fg = LM; quotient: lim f/g = L/M (M ≠ 0); constant multiple: lim cf = cL; power: lim fⁿ = Lⁿ.
    • Root property: lim ⁿ√f = ⁿ√L (L ≥ 0 for even n).
    • Sandwich (squeeze) theorem: g ≤ f ≤ h with lim g = lim h = L ⟹ lim f = L.
    • Composition: lim_{x→a} f(g(x)) = f(lim_{x→a} g(x)) when f is continuous at the inner limit.

    2.4 The method ladder

    • Substitution → factor/conjugate (0/0) → degree comparison (∞/∞) → standard limits → squeeze. Properties justify every step: splitting a fraction into factors is the product theorem; steering through u is the composition theorem.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why indeterminate ≠ undefined

    • 0/0 is undetermined, not nonexistent: the neighbourhood values may conspire toward any number, ∞, or nothing. The form is a flag to investigate, not a verdict.

    4.2 The theorems as a licence system

    • Splitting (x²−4)/(x−2) into (x+2)·(x−2)/(x−2) uses the product theorem — but only after each factor's limit exists. The theorems have hypotheses; the licence metaphor keeps them visible.

    4.3 Squeeze as the endgame

    • When algebra cannot isolate the value (oscillating factors), bounding does. The squeeze theorem is the only property that works without an explicit formula for f.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    lim f = L does not need f(a) = L: the point is excluded — that is why 0/0 limits exist at all.

  • Splitting without existence: the product theorem fails if a factor's limit DNE — regroup before splitting.
  • c/0 is not indeterminate: it is determinately infinite (with sign from the side); only 0/0 needs work.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): Find lim_{x→2} (x² − 4)/(x − 2). (2m) — Substitute: (4 − 4)/0 = 0/0 ⟹ transform. Factor: x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2); cancel (x ≠ 2 licensed): x + 2. Substitute: 2 + 2 = 4. (Cross-check via the star (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) → naⁿ⁻¹: 2·2 = 4 ✓.)

  • NEB-style (2m): State the seven indeterminate forms and show that 0/0 has no fixed value. — The forms: 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰. No fixed value: (x² − 4)/(x − 2) → 4; (x³ − 8)/(x − 2) → 12; (x − 2)²/(x − 2) → 0; (x − 2)/(x − 2)² → ∞ — all are 0/0 at x = 2 yet converge differently ⟹ the form alone determines nothing; the specific functions decide.
  • NEB-style (3m): Prove the product rule for limits: if lim_{x→a} f = L and lim_{x→a} g = M (finite), then lim_{x→a} f·g = LM. — Write f = L + α, g = M + β where α, β → 0 (this is what the limits mean). Then fg = LM + Lβ + Mα + αβ, so |fg − LM| ≤ |L||β| + |M||α| + |α||β|. Given ε > 0, choose δ so that |α|, |β| < min(1, ε/(3(|L|+1)), ε/(3(|M|+1))); then |fg − LM| < ε. Hence lim fg = LM ∎ — the decomposition f = L + (f − L) is the standard trick that turns limit theorems into algebra.

  • 8. Deep Conceptual — The Theorems Do Not Reverse

    • The converses fail: lim (f + g) can exist while lim f and lim g do not — take f = |x|/x (sides ±1) and g = −|x|/x: the sum is 0 everywhere, yet neither piece has a limit at 0. Similarly lim fg can exist with neither factor converging (f = g = the Dirichlet-style oscillator: product of two wild functions can be tame).
    • Why this matters: the limit theorems are one-way licences — they certify the limit of a combination from the limits of the parts, never the reverse. Diagnosing "why can't I split this limit?" is precisely the skill of citing hypotheses.
    • Squeeze is the only non-algebraic property: it needs no formula for f, only bounds — which is why it is the tool of last resort for oscillating integrands.

  • 9. Deep Conceptual — Epsilon–Delta for the Working Student

    • Proving lim_{x→a} f(x) = L by ε–δ is a recipe: (1) simplify |f(x) − L| algebraically; (2) factor out |x − a|; (3) bound the leftover factor on a preliminary window (say |x − a| < 1); (4) choose δ = min(1, ε/bound).
    • The preliminary window is the whole trick: it converts an unknown factor into a constant, making δ computable. This is the same "restrict then divide" move used in every first-principles derivative proof.
    • Disproving a limit: exhibit one ε > 0 for which no δ works — typically by finding two sequences approaching a whose images converge to different values (the sequential test in ε-clothing).

  • 10. Deep Conceptual — Indeterminate Forms as Dimensional Analysis

    • Each form encodes a race between two rates: 0/0 is "which vanishes faster", ∞/∞ is "which grows faster", 0·∞ is "collapse versus explosion", 1^∞ is "approach to 1 versus unbounded exponent". The answer is always decided by the relative rates, never the individual behaviours.
    • This is why the same algebraic trick (factoring out the dominant term) resolves both 0/0 and ∞/∞: in both cases you isolate the race's deciding factor and let the rest collapse to 1 or 0.
    • Exam instinct: name the race, identify the two contestants, compare their orders (degree, exponent, logarithm) — the form's species tells you which comparison to run.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): Find lim_{x→2} (x² − 4)/(x − 2). (2m) — Substitute: (2² − 4)/(2 − 2) = 0/0 — indeterminate ⟹ transform. Factor: x² − 4 = (x − 2)(x + 2) (difference of squares), so (x² − 4)/(x − 2) = x + 2 for x ≠ 2 — the cancellation is licensed on the punctured neighbourhood. Substitute: 2 + 2 = 4. Cross-check: the algebraic star lim_{x→a} (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = naⁿ⁻¹ with n = 2, a = 2 gives 2·2¹ = 4 ✓. The two routes agree — the star is the factoring method compressed.
  • NEB-style (2m): State the seven indeterminate forms and demonstrate that the 0/0 form has no fixed value. — The seven forms: 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ — each names a shape of failure, not a value. Demonstration: at x = 2, all four of these are 0/0: (x² − 4)/(x − 2) → 4; (x³ − 8)/(x − 2) → 12; (x − 2)²/(x − 2) → 0; (x − 2)/(x − 2)² → ∞. Same form, four different outcomes ⟹ the form is a flag to transform, never an answer. Contrast: c/0 (c ≠ 0) is determinate — the quotient grows without bound, sign depending on the side — and needs no transformation, only a sign analysis.
  • NEB-style (3m): Prove the product rule: if lim_{x→a} f(x) = L and lim_{x→a} g(x) = M (both finite), then lim_{x→a} f(x)g(x) = LM. — Decomposition: write f = L + α and g = M + β, where α = f − L → 0 and β = g − M → 0 as x → a — this is precisely what the two limits assert. Expand: fg = (L + α)(M + β) = LM + Lβ + Mα + αβ, so |fg − LM| ≤ |L|·|β| + |M|·|α| + |α|·|β|. Epsilon bookkeeping: given ε > 0, choose δ so that for |x − a| < δ: |α| < 1 and |α| < ε/(3(|M|+1)), and |β| < ε/(3(|L|+1)). Then |fg − LM| ≤ |L|·ε/(3(|L|+1)) + |M|·ε/(3(|M|+1)) + 1·ε/3 < ε/3 + ε/3 + ε/3 = ε. Conclusion: lim fg = LM ∎. The decomposition f = L + (f − L) — "the limit plus a vanishing error" — is the master trick behind every limit theorem.
  • NEB-style (5m): Using the ε–δ definition, prove that lim_{x→3} (2x − 5) = 1. — Setup: given ε > 0, we must find δ > 0 such that 0 < |x − 3| < δ ⟹ |(2x − 5) − 1| < ε. Simplify the target: |(2x − 5) − 1| = |2x − 6| = 2|x − 3| — the error is exactly twice the input distance. Choose δ: take δ = ε/2. Then |x − 3| < δ ⟹ |2x − 6| = 2|x − 3| < 2·(ε/2) = ε ✓. Conclusion: lim_{x→3} (2x − 5) = 1 ∎. Conceptual reading: for linear functions the error is a constant multiple of the input distance, so δ = ε/slope works exactly — no preliminary window needed. The slope 2 is, of course, the derivative — ε–δ for linear functions is the derivative wearing a formal costume.
  • NEB-style (5m): Using the ε–δ definition, prove that lim_{x→2} x² = 4. — Setup: given ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 2| < δ ⟹ |x² − 4| < ε. Factor: |x² − 4| = |x + 2|·|x − 2| — the unwanted factor |x + 2| must be tamed. Preliminary window: restrict |x − 2| < 1, i.e. 1 < x < 3, so |x + 2| < 5. Then |x² − 4| < 5|x − 2|. Choose δ = min(1, ε/5): then |x − 2| < δ forces both the window and |x² − 4| < 5·(ε/5) = ε ✓. Conclusion: lim_{x→2} x² = 4 ∎. Deep point: the preliminary window converts the unknown factor |x + 2| into the constant 5 — the universal trick for every nonlinear ε–δ proof, and the reason δ is a min(), never a bare formula.
  • NEB-style (6m): For f(x) = x + 1 (x < 2), f(2) = 5, f(x) = 8 − x (x > 2): find LHL, RHL, decide whether lim_{x→2} f(x) exists, and discuss continuity. — Left-hand limit (left branch): lim_{x→2⁻} (x + 1) = 3. Right-hand limit (right branch): lim_{x→2⁺} (8 − x) = 6. Existence verdict: the two sides are finite but 3 ≠ 6 ⟹ the two-sided limit does not exist — f has a jump discontinuity at x = 2 with jump size 6 − 3 = 3. Continuity analysis: f(2) = 5 equals neither side ⟹ f is continuous at 2 from neither side; changing f(2) cannot help (it can match at most one branch). Deep conceptual point: the assigned value f(2) = 5 is a third independent claim — here it disagrees with both sides, producing a graph with a floating dot at (2, 5) suspended between the two rays. The existence of a limit is a property of the neighbourhoods; the value of f(a) is a property of the point — continuity demands all three agree, and this example breaks all three pairwise agreements at once.
  • NEB-style (5m): Use the squeeze theorem to evaluate lim_{x→0} x² cos(1/x²), and explain why no algebraic method can resolve it. — The obstacle: cos(1/x²) oscillates infinitely fast near 0 (its argument → ∞), so it has no limit and no standard-limit steering applies — algebra is powerless. The bounds: −1 ≤ cos(1/x²) ≤ 1 for every x ≠ 0; multiplying by the non-negative x²: −x² ≤ x² cos(1/x²) ≤ x². The squeeze: both bounds → 0 as x → 0, so by the squeeze theorem the limit is 0. Deep conceptual point: the squeeze theorem is the only property that works without an explicit formula for the limit — it replaces computation with bounding. The factor x² is a damping coefficient: wild oscillation squeezed between vanishing walls must itself vanish. The same pattern certifies continuity of x² sin(1/x) at 0 and underlies every "bounded times vanishing" argument in analysis.

Summary

Limits form a system. The definition is neighbourhoodal — x approaches, never arrives — which is exactly why 0/0 limits can exist. The seven indeterminate forms (0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰) are flags for transformation, provably value-free: four 0/0 examples at x = 2 land on 4, 12, 0, ∞. The algebraic properties — sum, product, quotient, constant multiple, power, root — are theorems with hypotheses (finite limits, non-zero denominators), proved by the master decomposition f = L + (f − L); the squeeze theorem handles what algebra cannot (oscillation), and the composition theorem licenses u-substitution. The method ladder — substitute, factor/conjugate, degree comparison, standard limits, squeeze — turns every limit into a diagnosis: classify the form, cite the licence, transform, answer. NEB 2023's (x²−4)/(x−2) → 4 is the ladder's first rung in action.

Cross-check: the algebraic star lim_{x→a} (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = naⁿ⁻¹ with n = 2, a = 2 gives 2·2¹ = 4 ✓. The two routes agree — the star is the factoring method compressed.
  • NEB-style (2m): State the seven indeterminate forms and demonstrate that the 0/0 form has no fixed value. — The seven forms: 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞ − ∞, 1^∞, 0⁰, ∞⁰ — each names a shape of failure, not a value. Demonstration: at x = 2, all four of these are 0/0: (x² − 4)/(x − 2) → 4; (x³ − 8)/(x − 2) → 12; (x − 2)²/(x − 2) → 0; (x − 2)/(x − 2)² → ∞. Same form, four different outcomes ⟹ the form is a flag to transform, never an answer. Contrast: c/0 (c ≠ 0) is determinate — the quotient grows without bound, sign depending on the side — and needs no transformation, only a sign analysis.
  • NEB-style (3m): Prove the product rule: if lim_{x→a} f(x) = L and lim_{x→a} g(x) = M (both finite), then lim_{x→a} f(x)g(x) = LM. — Decomposition: write f = L + α and g = M + β, where α = f − L → 0 and β = g − M → 0 as x → a — this is precisely what the two limits assert. Expand: fg = (L + α)(M + β) = LM + Lβ + Mα + αβ, so |fg − LM| ≤ |L|·|β| + |M|·|α| + |α|·|β|. Epsilon bookkeeping: given ε > 0, choose δ so that for |x − a| < δ: |α| < 1 and |α| < ε/(3(|M|+1)), and |β| < ε/(3(|L|+1)). Then |fg − LM| ≤ |L|·ε/(3(|L|+1)) + |M|·ε/(3(|M|+1)) + 1·ε/3 < ε/3 + ε/3 + ε/3 = ε. Conclusion: lim fg = LM ∎. The decomposition f = L + (f − L) — "the limit plus a vanishing error" — is the master trick behind every limit theorem.
  • NEB-style (5m): Using the ε–δ definition, prove that lim_{x→3} (2x − 5) = 1. — Setup: given ε > 0, we must find δ > 0 such that 0 < |x − 3| < δ ⟹ |(2x − 5) − 1| < ε. Simplify the target: |(2x − 5) − 1| = |2x − 6| = 2|x − 3| — the error is exactly twice the input distance. Choose δ: take δ = ε/2. Then |x − 3| < δ ⟹ |2x − 6| = 2|x − 3| < 2·(ε/2) = ε ✓. Conclusion: lim_{x→3} (2x − 5) = 1 ∎. Conceptual reading: for linear functions the error is a constant multiple of the input distance, so δ = ε/slope works exactly — no preliminary window needed. The slope 2 is, of course, the derivative — ε–δ for linear functions is the derivative wearing a formal costume.
  • NEB-style (5m): Using the ε–δ definition, prove that lim_{x→2} x² = 4. — Setup: given ε > 0, find δ > 0 with |x − 2| < δ ⟹ |x² − 4| < ε. Factor: |x² − 4| = |x + 2|·|x − 2| — the unwanted factor |x + 2| must be tamed. Preliminary window: restrict |x − 2| < 1, i.e. 1 < x < 3, so |x + 2| < 5. Then |x² − 4| < 5|x − 2|. Choose δ = min(1, ε/5): then |x − 2| < δ forces both the window and |x² − 4| < 5·(ε/5) = ε ✓. Conclusion: lim_{x→2} x² = 4 ∎. Deep point: the preliminary window converts the unknown factor |x + 2| into the constant 5 — the universal trick for every nonlinear ε–δ proof, and the reason δ is a min(), never a bare formula.
  • NEB-style (6m): For f(x) = x + 1 (x < 2), f(2) = 5, f(x) = 8 − x (x > 2): find LHL, RHL, decide whether lim_{x→2} f(x) exists, and discuss continuity. — Left-hand limit (left branch): lim_{x→2⁻} (x + 1) = 3. Right-hand limit (right branch): lim_{x→2⁺} (8 − x) = 6. Existence verdict: the two sides are finite but 3 ≠ 6 ⟹ the two-sided limit does not exist — f has a jump discontinuity at x = 2 with jump size 6 − 3 = 3. Continuity analysis: f(2) = 5 equals neither side ⟹ f is continuous at 2 from neither side; changing f(2) cannot help (it can match at most one branch). Deep conceptual point: the assigned value f(2) = 5 is a third independent claim — here it disagrees with both sides, producing a graph with a floating dot at (2, 5) suspended between the two rays. The existence of a limit is a property of the neighbourhoods; the value of f(a) is a property of the point — continuity demands all three agree, and this example breaks all three pairwise agreements at once.
  • NEB-style (5m): Use the squeeze theorem to evaluate lim_{x→0} x² cos(1/x²), and explain why no algebraic method can resolve it. — The obstacle: cos(1/x²) oscillates infinitely fast near 0 (its argument → ∞), so it has no limit and no standard-limit steering applies — algebra is powerless. The bounds: −1 ≤ cos(1/x²) ≤ 1 for every x ≠ 0; multiplying by the non-negative x²: −x² ≤ x² cos(1/x²) ≤ x². The squeeze: both bounds → 0 as x → 0, so by the squeeze theorem the limit is 0. Deep conceptual point: the squeeze theorem is the only property that works without an explicit formula for the limit — it replaces computation with bounding. The factor x² is a damping coefficient: wild oscillation squeezed between vanishing walls must itself vanish. The same pattern certifies continuity of x² sin(1/x) at 0 and underlies every "bounded times vanishing" argument in analysis.

Important Concepts

  • The limit theorems are a licence system: every algebraic step on limits (splitting, steering, quoting) must cite a theorem whose hypotheses actually hold.

Important Statements

  • "If lim f = L and lim g = M then lim(f+g) = L+M, lim(fg) = LM, lim(f/g) = L/M (M != 0); squeeze: bounded between equals shares their limit."
