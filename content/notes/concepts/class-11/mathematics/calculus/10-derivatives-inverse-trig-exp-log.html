Notes

  • Inverse trigonometric derivatives all carry a denominator: dxd​arcsinx=1−x2​1​, dxd​arccosx=−1−x2​1​, dxd​arctanx=1+x21​. Arccos carries a minus sign, which is the detail most often lost.
  • The remaining three follow the same shape: dxd​arccotx=−1+x21​, dxd​arcsecx=∣x∣x2−1​1​, dxd​arccscx=−∣x∣x2−1​1​. The absolute value on x is required, not optional.
  • Exponential: dxd​ex=ex is unique in being its own derivative. For ax use dxd​ax=axlna, so ax is its own derivative only when a=e.
  • Logarithms: dxd​lnx=x1​ and dxd​loga​x=xlna1​. The identity dxd​loga​u=ulnau′​ is what makes log a natural tool for exponential-growth problems, since dxd​ax contains exactly the factor lna that log removes.
  • Log of a product splits into a sum: ln(xy)=lnx+lny, so dxd​ln(xy)=y1​+x1​ — the chain rule applied to a product, which is a classic exam step.
  • The derivative of ln inverts the exponent: since ex is its own derivative, dxd​lnx=x1​ follows by inverse-function differentiation. The same logic gives dxd​arctanx once dxd​tanx=sec2x is known.

Confusion

  • Omitting the minus sign on the arccos derivative — it is the one inverse trig derivative that is negative among the three principal ones.
  • Writing dxd​ax=ax instead of axlna; only the natural exponential is self-derivative.
  • Dropping the absolute value in the arcsec and arccsc derivatives.

Universal Facts

  • Compound interest is the reason e matters: continuous growth is ert, and the derivative of the exponential is what makes that growth self-similar in time.
  • The curve y=lnx is the inverse of y=ex and shares its steepness there, because a function and its inverse are reflections in y=x and have reciprocal slopes.

Key Points

  • Arccos and arccot derivatives are negative; the others are positive.
  • Only ex is its own derivative — ax needs the lna factor.
  • The arcsec and arccsc derivatives require an absolute value on x.

Summary

Every inverse trigonometric derivative takes the form 'one over the derivative of its partner', with a sign that is negative for arccos and arccot. The exponential ex is uniquely self-derivative, while ax carries an extra lna factor — the same factor that differentiating a logarithm removes, which is why logs and exponentials are paired. Domains and absolute values are part of these formulas, not decorations.

Special Notes

  • Where a formula is quoted it is valid only under the conditions stated; Omitting the minus sign on the arccos derivative — it is the one inverse trig derivative that is negative among the three principal ones.
  • Every result here follows from the definitions, so a first-principles derivation is always a valid fallback in the exam.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: dxd​arccosx=−1−x2​1​.
  • Statement 2: dxd​arctanx=1+x21​.
  • Statement 3: dxd​ex=ex but dxd​ax=axlna.
  • Statement 4: dxd​lnx=x1​.
  • Statement 5: The arcsec and arccsc derivatives involve ∣x∣ in the denominator.

Important Notes

  • Topic anchor: The derivative of ln inverts the exponent since ex is its own derivative, fracddxlnx=frac1x follows by inverse-function differentiation.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Inverse trig derivative = 1 over the partner's derivative, then adjust the sign for the 'co' functions (cos, cot, csc, sec).
  • For ax, always write axlna — omitting lna is the common slip.

Exam Notes

  • Differentiating inverse trig and logarithmic expressions with the chain rule is a reliable 3-mark question.
