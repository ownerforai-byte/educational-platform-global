Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Measuring the Spread

    Central tendency says where data sits; dispersion says how widely it roams. The theorem set: range, mean deviation, variance and standard deviation (with the computational identity σ² = E(x²) − [E(x)]²), the coefficient of variation, and Karl Pearson's skewness coefficients.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Range and mean deviation

    • Range = max − min — crude but instant.
    • Mean deviation = (1/n)Σ|xᵢ − x̄| — average distance from the mean; about the median it is minimised (a theorem: the median minimises Σ|xᵢ − a|).

    2.2 Variance and standard deviation

    • Variance: σ² = (1/n)Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² — mean squared deviation.
    • Computational identity (theorem): σ² = (1/n)Σxᵢ² − x̄² — the "mean of squares minus square of mean". Proof: expand Σ(xᵢ − x̄)² = Σxᵢ² − 2x̄Σxᵢ + nx̄² and use Σxᵢ = nx̄.
    • Standard deviation: σ = √variance — same units as the data.
    • Theorem (shift invariance): adding a constant c to every datum leaves σ unchanged (xᵢ + c has the same spread); multiplying by c multiplies σ by |c|.

    2.3 Coefficient of variation

    • C.V. = (σ/x̄) × 100% — a relative, unit-free spread; lower C.V. = more consistent.
    • Used to compare variability across data sets with different units or means.

    2.4 Karl Pearson's skewness

    • Absolute skewness: Sk = mean − mode (or mean − median).
    • Pearson's first coefficient: Sk_p = (mean − mode)/σ; second coefficient: Sk_p = 3(mean − median)/σ.
    • Skewness sign: positive ⟹ right tail longer (mean > mode); negative ⟹ left tail longer; zero ⟹ symmetric.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why squares for variance

    • Absolute values are awkward analytically; squares are differentiable and additive. The price: units get squared — the √ in σ restores them.

    4.2 The computational identity at work

    • σ² = Σxᵢ²/n − x̄² lets you compute variance from running sums — no deviation table needed, and it is the identity examiners test.

    4.3 C.V. as the fair comparison

    • σ alone misleads: ₹100 spread on a ₹50 mean is huge, on a ₹10,000 mean trivial. Dividing by the mean normalises — that is the C.V.'s whole point.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Variance units are squared: report σ, not σ², when the question asks for spread in original units.

  • Lower C.V. = more consistent: students invert this — the smaller relative spread wins.
  • Skewness sign: mean > mode ⟹ positive (right) skew — the tail pulls the mean, not the mode.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Marks of 10 students: 12, 15, 18, 20, 22, 25, 28, 30, 35, 45. Calculate (i) the mean, (ii) the standard deviation. (4m) — Mean: x̄ = (12+15+18+20+22+25+28+30+35+45)/10 = 250/10 = 25. Squared deviations: (12−25)²=169, (15−25)²=100, (18−25)²=49, (20−25)²=25, (22−25)²=9, (25−25)²=0, (28−25)²=9, (30−25)²=25, (35−25)²=100, (45−25)²=400. Sum = 886. Variance: σ² = 886/10 = 88.6. Standard deviation: σ = √88.6 ≈ 9.41. (Equivalently via the identity: Σxᵢ²/n − x̄² = 7136/10 − 625 = 713.6 − 625 = 88.6 ✓.)

  • NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): The variance of the first 10 natural numbers is: (1m MCQ) — First 10 naturals: 1…10, mean 5.5. Σxᵢ² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 = 10·11·21/6 = 385. Variance = 385/10 − (5.5)² = 38.5 − 30.25 = 8.25 → option (a). The formula shortcut n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 turns this into a 30-second question.
  • NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): If P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.5, P(A∩B) = 0.3, then P(A∪B) is: (1m) — Addition law: P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B) = 0.6 + 0.5 − 0.3 = 0.8 → option (a). (The subtraction of the intersection prevents double-counting the overlap — the addition law is the topic's core theorem.)

Universal Facts

  • The C.V. is why a ±5 error on a 1000 g weighing is excellent and on a 10 g weighing is a disaster: relative, not absolute, spread tells the story.

Examples

  • Mean of squares minus square of mean — sigma^2 = sum(xi^2)/n - xbar^2: the one identity that turns variance into two running sums, no deviation table required.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): The marks obtained by 10 students in a test are 12, 15, 18, 20, 22, 25, 28, 30, 35 and 45. Calculate (i) the mean, and (ii) the standard deviation. (4m) — (i) Mean: x̄ = Σxᵢ/n = (12 + 15 + 18 + 20 + 22 + 25 + 28 + 30 + 35 + 45)/10 = 250/10 = 25. (ii) Standard deviation: deviations xᵢ − 25 are −13, −10, −7, −5, −3, 0, 3, 5, 10, 20; squares: 169, 100, 49, 25, 9, 0, 9, 25, 100, 400 — sum = 886. Variance σ² = 886/10 = 88.6; standard deviation σ = √88.6 ≈ 9.41 marks. Cross-check by the computational identity: Σxᵢ² = 144+225+324+400+484+625+784+900+1225+2025 = 7136; σ² = 7136/10 − 25² = 713.6 − 625 = 88.6 ✓ — both routes agree.
  • NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): The variance of the first 10 natural numbers is: (a) 8.25 (b) 9.25 (c) 10.25 (d) 11.25 (1m) — Data: 1, 2, …, 10 with mean (10+1)/2 = 5.5. Sum of squares formula: Σxᵢ² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6 = (10)(11)(21)/6 = 385. Variance via the identity: σ² = Σxᵢ²/n − x̄² = 385/10 − (5.5)² = 38.5 − 30.25 = 8.25 — option (a). (General fact worth memorising: the variance of the first n naturals is (n² − 1)/12, which for n = 10 gives 99/12 = 8.25 ✓.)
  • NEB-style (3m): Two machines fill packets with means 500 g and 505 g and standard deviations 4 g and 6 g respectively. Which is more consistent? — Coefficient of variation is the fair comparator: C.V.₁ = (4/500) × 100 = 0.8%; C.V.₂ = (6/505) × 100 ≈ 1.19%. The lower C.V. indicates greater consistency, so machine 1 is more consistent — its spread is smaller relative to its mean, even though its absolute σ (4 g) is being compared against a slightly different mean. The C.V. exists precisely because raw σ cannot be compared across different means.

    • Pearson skewness: (x̄ − mode)/σ or 3(x̄ − median)/σ
    • Σi² = n(n+1)(2n+1)/6; variance of first n naturals = (n²−1)/12

Summary

Dispersion quantifies the roam. Range is instant but crude; mean deviation averages |xᵢ − x̄| (minimised at the median); variance σ² = Σxᵢ²/n − x̄² squares the deviations — analytic but with squared units — and σ = √σ² restores them (NEB 2024: mean 25, σ ≈ 9.41 for the 10-mark set; NEB 2023: variance of 1…10 is 385/10 − 5.5² = 8.25). Adding a constant leaves σ unchanged; scaling scales it. The coefficient of variation σ/x̄ × 100% makes spread comparable across scales — lower C.V., more consistent. Karl Pearson's skewness (mean − mode)/σ (or 3(mean − median)/σ) signs the asymmetry: positive when the right tail drags the mean past the mode. The identity "mean of squares minus square of mean" is the topic's engine.
