  • 1. Topic Introduction — Chemistry Everywhere You Look

    Fertilisers that feed billions, medicines that cure, polymers that clothe, fuels that move — and the pollution that threatens. Chemistry's importance is double-edged: it creates solutions and creates problems, and understanding it is how we maximise the first and manage the second.

    2.2 Scope of chemistry

    The scope spans:

    • Pure research: discovering structures, reactions, new compounds.
    • Applied fields: engineering processes, quality control, forensic analysis.
    • Interdisciplinary frontiers: medicinal chemistry, environmental chemistry, materials science, computational chemistry, biochemistry.

    2.3 Harmful effects — the responsibility side

    Chemistry also brings: pollution (industrial effluents, smog), ozone depletion (CFCs), greenhouse gases (CO₂ from fossil-fuel combustion), toxic waste (heavy metals), and drug/narcotic misuse. Modern chemistry responds with green chemistry — designing processes that reduce or eliminate hazardous substances.

    2.4 Similarities and differences — benefits vs hazards of chemistry

    Similarities: both arise from the same chemical knowledge and reactions; both scale with industrial use; both are manageable through informed control. Differences: benefits are intended products (fertiliser, medicine) improving life; hazards are unintended by-products (effluents, emissions) harming life. Benefits are maximised by design; hazards are minimised by regulation, treatment and green chemistry.

Summary

Chemistry's importance spans agriculture (urea, NPK fertilisers), medicine (aspirin, anaesthetics), industry (H₂SO₄, NaOH, alloys), energy (fuels, batteries), daily life (soaps, polymers) and the environment (pollution control). Its scope runs from pure research to applied fields and interdisciplinary frontiers — medicinal, environmental, materials and computational chemistry. The same power harms: industrial pollution, CFC-driven ozone depletion, greenhouse gases and toxic waste. The modern answer is green chemistry — designing processes that prevent hazards at source rather than treating them after. Chemistry's story is therefore one of responsibility as much as capability.
