Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Weighing a Molecule on Paper

    Given a formula, you can compute exactly what fraction of its mass each element contributes — the percentage composition. It is the bridge between formulas and laboratory analysis, and the first step of every empirical-formula problem.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 The percentage composition formula

    % of element=molar mass of compoundmass of element in 1 mol of compound​×100

    The percentages of all elements sum to 100% — a built-in check.

    2.2 Worked numericals

    Q (2m, 2024): Calculate the percentage of nitrogen in urea, CO(NH₂)₂ (C = 12, O = 16, N = 14, H = 1).

    • Molar mass = 12 + 16 + 2(14 + 2×1) = 12 + 16 + 32 = 60 g/mol
    • Mass of N = 2 × 14 = 28 g

    %N=6028​×100≈46.7%​ — the highest nitrogen content of common fertilisers, which is why urea dominates the market.

    Q (NEB-style): Find the percentage composition of CaCO₃ (Ca = 40, C = 12, O = 16).

    • M = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100 g/mol
    • %Ca = 40%, %C = 12%, %O = 48% (sums to 100 ✓)

    Q (NEB-style): Which is the richer iron ore by mass — Fe₂O₃ or Fe₃O₄? (Fe = 56, O = 16)

    • Fe₂O₃: M = 160, %Fe = 112/160 = 70%
    • Fe₃O₄: M = 232, %Fe = 168/232 ≈ 72.4% → Fe₃O₄ is richer.

    2.3 Reverse direction — % composition to formula

    The same arithmetic runs backwards: assume 100 g of compound → convert each percentage to moles → divide by the smallest → simplest ratio = empirical formula → multiply by n = M/empirical-mass for the molecular formula (full treatment in Unit 2, Topic 12).

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 3: molecular formula is the input; empirical/molecular distinction matters.
    • To stoichiometry (Unit 2): every mole calculation begins with molar mass from the formula.
    • To chemistry of metals (Unit 10): ore richness comparisons use exactly this arithmetic.

Numericals

  • H₂SO₄: 2.04 / 32.65 / 65.31 (sum 100).
  • Urea: N 46.67, C 20, O 26.67, H 6.67.
  • NH₄NO₃: N 35% — poorer per kg than urea.
  • 40/6.7/53.3 → CH₂O; M_r 180 → C₆H₁₂O₆ (n=6); M_r 90 → C₃H₆O₃ (n=3).
  • 4.4 g → 8.8 g CO₂ + 3.6 g H₂O: C 54.55%, H 9.09% → C₂H₄O.
  • CaCO₃: Ca 40% — audit any limestone claim.
  • VD 45 → M_r 90; CH₂O (30) ×3 → C₃H₆O₃ (lactic acid).

Key Points

  • % element = (element mass per mol / molar mass) × 100. Urea: %N = 46.7%.

Summary

Percentage composition = (mass of the element in one mole ÷ molar mass) × 100. For urea CO(NH₂)₂: molar mass 60 g/mol, nitrogen mass 28 g → %N ≈ 46.7% (NEB 2024, 2m) — the highest of common fertilisers. For CaCO₃ the split is 40/12/48 = 40% Ca, 12% C, 48% O, always summing to 100%. Comparing ores: Fe₃O₄ (72.4% Fe) edges Fe₂O₃ (70%). Run in reverse, the same arithmetic converts percentages to moles to the empirical formula — the gateway to molecular-formula determination in Unit 2.
