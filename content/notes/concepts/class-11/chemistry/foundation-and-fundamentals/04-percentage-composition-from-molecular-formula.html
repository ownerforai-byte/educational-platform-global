  • Percentage composition from molecular formula

    1. Introduction

    A fertilizer bag shouts "46-0-0", a medicine strip lists milligrams per tablet, a steel specification demands 0.2% carbon — all are percentage composition: the mass share of each element in a compound. It is the most commercial calculation in chemistry, deciding what farmers pay for, what patients swallow, and what factories must guarantee.

    The formula is one line — (element mass ÷ molar mass) × 100 — but it runs both directions: forward from formula to percentages (is this urea really 46% N?), and backward from percentage analysis to empirical and molecular formulae (what is this unknown?). This topic closes the foundation unit by turning every earlier concept into numbers.

    What you will learn: (i) percentage composition from formula; (ii) the urea 46% derivation; (iii) composition → empirical formula; (iv) composition + molar mass → molecular formula; (v) applications in fertilizers, drugs and industry.

    Learning outcomes. You can: compute any % composition; prove urea's 46% N; derive empirical and molecular formulae from analysis data; and audit a label claim.

  • 2. Concepts and definitions

    Percentage composition (forward direction)

    1. Definition and formula. % of element E = (total mass of E in one mole ÷ molar mass) × 100. The percentages sum to 100 — the built-in answer check.
    2. Worked core. H₂SO₄ (M_r 98): %H = 2/98×100 = 2.04%; %S = 32/98×100 = 32.65%; %O = 64/98×100 = 65.31%. Sum: 100.00 ✓.
    3. Urea proof. CO(NH₂)₂, M_r = 60: %N = 28/60×100 = 46.67% — the "46-0-0" bag, derived not memorised. %C = 20%, %O = 26.67%, %H = 6.67%.
    4. Assumptions box. Formula assumed correct and pure; percentages are mass/mass (w/w); analytical data assumed dry and impurity-free unless stated.

    From analysis back to formula (reverse direction)

    1. Empirical from %. % → grams (per 100 g) → moles (÷ A_r) → simplest ratio (÷ smallest, clear decimals by ×2/×3). Example 40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O → 3.33 : 6.7 : 3.33 → CH₂O.
    2. Molecular from empirical. Need M_r (given, or from vapour density / mass spec): n = M_r/E_r; molecular = n × empirical. Glucose: E_r 30, M_r 180 → n = 6 → C₆H₁₂O₆.
    3. Vapour-density bridge. M_r = 2 × vapour density (H₂ = 2 scale) — the classic route by which NEB supplies M_r: VD 90 → M_r 180.
    4. Honesty check. Computed % must reproduce the analysis within experimental error; if not, the assumed formula is wrong — composition audits formulae, not just labels.

  • 3. Reaction and equation block — composition inside equations

    1. Combustion analysis. C_xH_y + (x + y/4)O₂ → xCO₂ + (y/2)H₂O: weighing CO₂ and H₂O gives %C and %H directly (C from CO₂: mass × 12/44; H from H₂O: mass × 2/18) — the laboratory source of % data.
    2. Urea manufacture (conditions). 2NH₃ + CO₂ →(150–200°C, high pressure) CO(NH₂)₂ + H₂O: the equation behind the 46% bag — conditions decide the economics.
    3. Lime audit. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂: %Ca in CaCO₃ = 40/100 = 40%; quicklime purity checked the same way — composition as quality control.
    4. Reading rule. Every stoichiometric mass in these equations is a % composition in disguise (56/100 CaO from CaCO₃ = 56% yield logic).

  • 5. Worked numericals (Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer with unit)

    1. H₂SO₄ shares. M_r 98: %H = 2/98×100 = 2.04%; %S = 32/98×100 = 32.65%; %O = 64/98×100 = 65.31%; sum 100.00 ✓.
    2. Urea proof. M_r 60: %N = 28/60×100 = 46.67% ≈ 46 — the bag verified; %C 20%, %O 26.67%, %H 6.67%.
    3. Ammonium nitrate check. NH₄NO₃, M_r 80: %N = 28/80×100 = 35% — poorer than urea per kilogram, the comparison farmers pay for.
    4. Empirical from analysis. 40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O → 40/12 = 3.33; 6.7/1 = 6.7; 53.3/16 = 3.33 → ÷3.33 → 1:2:1 → CH₂O.
    5. Molecular with M_r. Above + M_r 180 → E_r 30 → n = 6 → C₆H₁₂O₆. With M_r 90 → n = 3 → C₃H₆O₃ (lactic acid shares the ratio — empirical ≠ identity).
    6. Combustion analysis. 4.4 g organic → 8.8 g CO₂ + 3.6 g H₂O: C = 8.8×12/44 = 2.4 g (54.5%); H = 3.6×2/18 = 0.4 g (9.1%); O by difference 36.4% → C:H:O = 4.54:9.1:2.27 → ÷2.27 → 2:4:1 → C₂H₄O.

    6. Key terms, worked angles and traps

    • % composition = (element mass per mole ÷ M_r) × 100; bars must sum to 100.
    • Urea 46.67% — 28/60; the number behind "46-0-0".
    • Combustion factors — C: ×12/44 from CO₂; H: ×2/18 from H₂O; O by difference.
    • n = M_r/E_r — needs a measured M_r (vapour density ×2, mass spec); never assumed.
    • Traps: % is w/w mass share, not mole share · O by difference assumes C,H,O only · clearing decimals by rounding instead of ×2/×3 · same empirical (CH₂O) hides different molecules.

  • 7. Exam Zone

    NEB map. % composition computations are guaranteed numericals; empirical-from-% is the standard long derivation; molecular via vapour density bridges units. Urea 46%, H₂SO₄ shares and combustion-analysis chains recur yearly.

    Short tricks. "Sum to 100 or start over". "12/44 for C, 2/18 for H, O by difference". "M_r = 2×VD". "1:2:1 smells like carbohydrate; verify with M_r".

    8. Summary and self-check

    % composition = element mass ÷ molar mass × 100, summing to 100: H₂SO₄ (2.04/32.65/65.31), urea (N 46.67 — the bag proved), CaCO₃ (Ca 40). Backward: % → grams → moles → ratio → empirical (CH₂O); with M_r (often 2×VD): n = M_r/E_r → molecular (C₆H₁₂O₆). Combustion analysis (12/44, 2/18, O by difference) supplies % data; the balance (Fig. 4.3) weighs it and the mole (Fig. 4.4) counts it. Figures: composition bars, analysis flow, balance photo, mole reference.

    Self-check. (1) Prove urea's 46% and compare with NH₄NO₃'s 35%. (2) 8.8 g CO₂ from 4.4 g sample → %C? (3) From C₂H₄O data plus M_r 88, give the molecular formula. (4) Why must O come "by difference"? (5) Audit: a "40% Ca" limestone claim — show the true value.

    Common confusions. Mass % ≠ mole % (convert via A_r first) · empirical gives ratio, never identity (CH₂O ×n family) · vapour density needs the H₂ = 2 scale stated · balance precision (±0.1 mg) decides analysis quality.

Confusion

  • ❌ All atoms are identical in an element. ✅ Isotopes have same protons but different neutrons.
  • ❌ Atoms are indivisible. ✅ Atoms consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • ❌ Electrons orbit in fixed paths like planets. ✅ Electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals).
  • ❌ Heisenberg uncertainty means we cannot measure position and momentum precisely simultaneously — not that we lack good instruments.
  • ❌ de Broglie wavelength applies only to light. ✅ All matter has wave nature; detectable mainly for small masses like electrons.
  • ❌ Quantum number l can be any integer. ✅ l ranges from 0 to n−1.
  • ❌ Magnetic quantum number ml determines energy. ✅ ml determines orbital orientation, not energy (in absence of magnetic field).
  • ❌ Spin quantum number ms can be ±½ or any value. ✅ ms is always +½ or −½.
  • ❌ Aufbau principle means filling from bottom to top of the diagram. ✅ Fill lowest-energy orbitals first: 1s→2s→2p→3s→3p→4s→3d→4p→...
  • ❌ Pauli exclusion allows two electrons per orbital with any spin. ✅ Two electrons in same orbital must have opposite spins.
  • ❌ Hund's rule means pair electrons before spreading. ✅ Electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly first with parallel spins.
  • ❌ The mole is just a number like dozen. ✅ 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro constant) — a bridge between atomic and macroscopic scales.
  • ❌ Molar mass and molecular mass are the same thing. ✅ Molecular mass is in amu; molar mass is in g/mol — numerically equal but different units.
  • ❌ Limiting reagent is the reactant with smallest mass. ✅ Limiting reagent is the one that produces the least product (determined by moles, not mass).
  • ❌ % yield can exceed 100%. ✅ % yield >100% indicates impure product or incomplete drying.
  • ❌ Molarity and molality are the same. ✅ Molarity = mol/L solution (changes with T); Molality = mol/kg solvent (temperature-independent).
  • ❌ A strong acid completely ionizes in water. ✅ Strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) are ~100% ionized; weak acids partially ionize.
  • ❌ pH can be negative. ✅ pH < 0 is possible for concentrations >1M (e.g., 2M HCl has pH ≈ −0.3).
  • ❌ Kc changes when concentration changes. ✅ Kc is constant at constant temperature; only temperature changes K.
  • ❌ Adding catalyst shifts equilibrium position. ✅ Catalyst speeds both forward and backward reactions equally; no shift in equilibrium.
  • ❌ High pressure always favors products. ✅ High pressure favors side with fewer gas moles (Le Chatelier).
  • ❌ Exothermic reactions are spontaneous. ✅ Spontaneity depends on ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, not just ΔH.
  • ❌ Endothermic reactions cannot be spontaneous. ✅ Endothermic reactions can be spontaneous if TΔS > ΔH (e.g., ice melting above 0°C).
  • ❌ ΔH and ΔU are always the same. ✅ ΔH = ΔU + PΔV; they differ when gas moles change.
  • ❌ Heat (q) and work (w) are state functions. ✅ q and w are path functions; only ΔU, ΔH, ΔS, ΔG are state functions.
  • ❌ Oxidation and reduction happen separately. ✅ Oxidation and reduction always occur together (redox).
  • ❌ Oxidizing agent gets oxidized. ✅ Oxidizing agent accepts electrons and gets reduced.
  • ❌ Reducing agent gets reduced. ✅ Reducing agent donates electrons and gets oxidized.
  • ❌ Faraday's laws apply only to metals. ✅ Faraday's laws apply to any substance deposited/dissolved at electrodes.
  • ❌ Real gases obey PV=nRT at all conditions. ✅ Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • ❌ van der Waals constants a and b are same for all gases. ✅ a (attraction) and b (volume) are gas-specific.
  • ❌ Surface tension increases with temperature. ✅ Surface tension decreases as temperature increases.
  • ❌ Viscosity decreases with temperature for liquids. ✅ Viscosity of liquids decreases with T; gases increase with T.
  • ❌ Colligative properties depend on nature of solute. ✅ Colligative properties depend only on number of particles, not identity.
  • ❌ Raoult's law applies to all solutions. ✅ Raoult's law is exact for ideal solutions; approximate for dilute real solutions.
  • ❌ Osmotic pressure is a colligative property. ✅ Yes — π = CRT depends on solute particle concentration.
  • Mass % and mole % differ — convert with A_r before comparing.
  • O-by-difference assumes only C, H, O present; N/S need separate estimation.

  • 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ entities (Avogadro constant, Nₐ). Molar mass (M) is g·mol⁻¹; for a compound M = Σ(atomic masses).
  • n (mol) = mass/M = volume(ideal gas, STP)/22.4 L = N/Nₐ.
  • Limiting reactant is the one consumed first; % yield = (experimental/theoretical)×100.

Key Points

  • • Percentage composition from molecular formula
  • %E = (mass of E per mole ÷ M_r) × 100; bars sum to 100.
  • Urea N 46.67%; combustion factors 12/44 (C), 2/18 (H), O by difference.
  • Empirical from %; molecular = n × empirical via M_r (often 2×VD).

Summary

Percentage composition = element mass ÷ molar mass × 100, always summing to 100: H₂SO₄ (2.04/32.65/65.31), urea N 46.67% (the bag proved), NH₄NO₃ N 35%, CaCO₃ Ca 40%. Backward: % → grams → moles → ratio → empirical (CH₂O); with M_r (often 2× vapour density): n = M_r/E_r → molecular (C₆H₁₂O₆; M_r 90 → C₃H₆O₃). Combustion analysis gives %C (×12/44) and %H (×2/18), O by difference. Figures: composition bars, analysis-to-formula flow, analytical balance photo, carbon-12 mole reference.
