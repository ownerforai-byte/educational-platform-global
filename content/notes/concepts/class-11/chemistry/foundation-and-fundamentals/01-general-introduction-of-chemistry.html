  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Central Science

    Chemistry studies matter — its composition, structure, properties and the changes it undergoes. Sitting between physics and biology, it is called the central science: it explains materials, medicines, fuels, fertilisers and life's own molecules in one framework.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Definition and scope

    Chemistry is the branch of science that deals with the composition, structure, properties of matter and the changes (reactions) it undergoes, together with the energy changes accompanying them.

    2.2 Branches of chemistry — classification table

    BranchStudiesExample topic
    Physical chemistryLaws and principles governing chemical change; energy, rates, equilibriumGas laws, thermodynamics
    Inorganic chemistryAll elements and their compounds except most carbon compoundsAcids, salts, metals
    Organic chemistryCarbon compounds (hydrocarbons and derivatives)Alkanes, benzene
    Analytical chemistryIdentification and measurement of compositionTitration, tests
    BiochemistryChemistry of living systemsEnzymes, DNA
    Industrial/Applied chemistryLarge-scale manufactureFertilisers, acids

    2.3 Matter and its classification

    • Matter: anything that has mass and occupies space.
    • Classification by state: solid, liquid, gas (and plasma).
    • Classification by composition: pure substances (elements, compounds) vs mixtures (homogeneous solutions, heterogeneous suspensions).
    ElementCompoundMixture
    CompositionOne kind of atomTwo+ elements, fixed ratioVariable ratio
    SeparationCannot be broken chemicallyBy chemical meansBy physical means
    ExampleFe, O₂H₂O, CO₂Air, salt water

    2.4 Similarities and differences — element vs compound

    Similarities: both are pure substances; both have fixed, definite composition; both cannot be separated by physical methods. Differences: an element contains one kind of atom and cannot be decomposed chemically; a compound contains two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed ratio and can be decomposed into its elements.

Summary

Chemistry studies matter's composition, structure, properties and changes — the central science linking physics and biology. Its branches — physical, inorganic, organic, analytical, biochemical and applied — divide the field by what is studied and how. Matter itself classifies by state (solid, liquid, gas) and by composition: elements (one atom type), compounds (fixed ratio, chemical bonds, chemical separation only) and mixtures (variable composition, physical separation). Similarities between elements and compounds (pure, definite composition) hide their key difference: decomposability. Air vs water is the classic contrast pair.
