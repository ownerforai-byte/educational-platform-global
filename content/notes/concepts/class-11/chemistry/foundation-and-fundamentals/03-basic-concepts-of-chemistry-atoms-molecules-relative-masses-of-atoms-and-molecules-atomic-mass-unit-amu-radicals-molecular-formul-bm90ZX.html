    1. Topic Introduction

    Before chemistry can calculate anything — a fertilizer dose, a gas volume, a percentage — it must count particles it cannot see. The counting system is the mole, the weighing scale is the atomic mass unit, and the shorthand is the chemical formula. This topic builds that entire toolkit: atom vs molecule, relative masses, amu, radicals, and the two formulae (empirical and molecular) that compress analysis into symbols.

    A fertilizer bag labelled "46-0-0" is this topic compressed: 46% nitrogen by the percentage logic the next topic completes. A 50 kg urea sack holds a countable number of molecules only because the mole makes counting possible — 6.02214076×10²³ of them per mole, exact by the SI 2019 definition.

    Why it matters. Every subsequent Chemistry unit — Stoichiometry, Atomic Structure, Bonding, States of Matter, Equilibrium, Organic — relies on the language and conversions established here. Without the mole bridge (mass ↔ moles ↔ particles ↔ volume), quantitative chemistry is impossible.

    What you will learn: (i) atoms, molecules, ions and radicals; (ii) relative atomic/molecular masses and amu; (iii) the mole and Avogadro's number (exact since SI 2019); (iv) molar volume at STP (NEB convention); (v) empirical vs molecular formula with the n = M/E relation.

    Learning outcomes. You can: interconvert grams ↔ moles ↔ particles ↔ litres at STP; compute molecular masses and average atomic masses; and derive empirical and molecular formulae from composition data.

  • 2. Fundamental Conceptual Understanding

    What these concepts are

    • Atom: Smallest unit of an element retaining its chemical identity (H, O, Na).
    • Molecule: Two or more atoms bonded, acting as one unit — homoatomic (H₂, O₂, O₃) or heteroatomic (H₂O, CO₂, NH₃).
    • Ion: Charged atom or group (Na⁺, Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻).
    • Radical: Charged group of atoms with collective identity in reactions (NH₄⁺, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, OH⁻) — valency rides on radicals; salt formulae assemble from them.
    • Relative atomic mass (A_r): Average mass of an element's atoms relative to ¹²C = 12, weighted by natural abundance (e.g., Cl ≈ 35.5).
    • Relative molecular mass (M_r): Sum of A_r in the formula (H₂O = 18, H₂SO₄ = 98, CaCO₃ = 100, urea = 60).
    • Atomic mass unit (amu, u, dalton): 1/12th mass of a ¹²C atom ≈ 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg.
    • Mole: Amount containing exactly 6.02214076×10²³ entities (N_A, exact since SI 2019 redefinition).
    • Molar mass: Mass of one mole in g·mol⁻¹, numerically equal to M_r.
    • Molar volume: Volume of one mole of ideal gas at STP — NEB uses 22.4 L at 0°C, 1 atm.
    • Empirical formula: Simplest whole-number atom ratio (CH₂O for glucose).
    • Molecular formula: Actual atom numbers in a molecule (C₆H₁₂O₆ for glucose).

    Why these exist

    Atoms are too light for grams (H ≈ 1.67×10⁻²⁴ g), so we need relative masses (amu). Counting individual particles is impossible, so we need the mole (N_A entities). Chemical analysis gives percentages, so we need empirical/molecular formulae to identify substances. The whole system connects the atomic scale to the macroscopic scale.

    How they work

    The mole is the bridge: 1 mole = N_A particles = molar mass (g) = 22.4 L (ideal gas at STP). This "mole triangle" lets you convert any quantity to any other. Formula mass (M_r) lets you go from formula to mass %. Empirical formula from % data + M_r (often from vapour density) gives molecular formula.

    Where they occur

    Every chemical calculation: fertilizer bags (46% N), gas cylinders (volume at STP), drug dosages (mg per mole), titration (moles of titrant = moles of analyte), combustion analysis (CO₂/H₂O masses → %C/%H).

    What their components are

    Particles (atoms, molecules, ions), masses (amu, g), amounts (mol), volumes (L), ratios (empirical), actual counts (molecular).

    How components interact

    Atoms combine in whole-number ratios (Dalton) → molecules/ions → formulae → relative masses (A_r, M_r) → molar masses (g/mol) → moles (amount) → measurable mass (g) or volume (L at STP). The mole is the central hub.

    What makes them biologically important

    Biochemistry counts molecules: DNA base pairs, enzyme active sites, ATP molecules per glucose, hormone-receptor binding. Drug dosage is moles per kg body weight. Blood gas analysis uses partial pressures and molar volumes. The mole connects the molecular world to the clinical world.

    4.2 Relative masses and amu

    ConceptDefinitionExample
    amu (u)¹²C/12 ≈ 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg1 u = 1 Da
    A_r (relative atomic mass)Weighted average of isotopesCl = 0.7577(35) + 0.2423(37) ≈ 35.45

    Why decimals on periodic table: Natural isotope mixtures. Cl ≈ 35.5 because 75% ³⁵Cl + 25% ³⁷Cl. No single chlorine atom weighs 35.5 u — it's the average.

    4.3 The mole and its interconversions

    The Mole Triangle (central hub):

            mass (g)
         ↑  ↓
        /    \
   M (g/mol)  N_A
       /      \
      /        \
   moles (mol)  particles
       \        /
        \      /
         V_m (L/mol)
          ↓  ↑
       volume (L at STP)

    Conversion formulas (NEB STP: 0°C, 1 atm, V_m = 22.4 L·mol⁻¹):

    • n = m / M (mass → moles)
    • n = V / 22.4 (gas volume at STP → moles)
    • n = N / N_A (particles → moles)
    • m = n × M (moles → mass)
    • V = n × 22.4 (moles → gas volume at STP)
    • N = n × N_A (moles → particles)

    4.4 Empirical vs molecular formula

    AspectEmpirical formulaMolecular formula
    DefinitionSimplest whole-number ratioActual atom numbers
    From% composition dataEmpirical + M_r
    ExampleCH₂OC₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose), CH₂O (formaldehyde), C₂H₄O₂ (acetic acid)
    Ionic compoundsOnly empirical (NaCl, MgO)No discrete molecules
    Determination% → g (per 100g) → mol (÷A_r) → ratio (÷smallest, clear decimals ×2/×3)n = M_r/E_r; molecular = n × empirical
    Vapour density link—M_r = 2 × VD (H₂ = 2 scale)

    Worked example — glucose analysis:

    • Data: 40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O
    • Per 100 g: 40 g C, 6.7 g H, 53.3 g O
    • Moles: 40/12 = 3.33; 6.7/1 = 6.7; 53.3/16 = 3.33
    • Ratio ÷3.33: 1 : 2 : 1 → CH₂O (E_r = 30)
    • If M_r = 180 (given or 2×VD): n = 180/30 = 6 → C₆H₁₂O₆
    • If M_r = 90: n = 3 → C₃H₆O₃ (lactic acid — same ratio, different molecule!)

  • 6. Chemical Processes and Mechanisms

    Process: Mole interconversion (the universal bridge)

    1. Definition: Converting between mass, moles, particles, and gas volume using the mole as central hub.
    2. Location: Every stoichiometry calculation, gas law problem, titration, combustion analysis.
    3. Requirements: Balanced equation, molar masses, STP conditions (for gases), Avogadro's number.
    4. Components: Mass (g), amount (mol), number of entities (N), volume (L at STP).
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Identify given quantity and its unit; (ii) Convert to moles using appropriate formula; (iii) Use mole ratio from balanced equation; (iv) Convert moles to desired quantity.
    6. Result: Any measurable quantity interconverted — mass, volume, concentration, particle count.
    7. Significance: The single algorithm that solves all quantitative chemistry problems.

    Process: Empirical → molecular formula determination

    1. Definition: Identifying a compound's molecular formula from composition data and molar mass.
    2. Location: Organic analysis lab, structure elucidation, quality control.
    3. Requirements: % composition (from combustion analysis or elemental analyzer), M_r (from vapour density, mass spec, or colligative properties).
    4. Components: Elemental analyzer, mass spectrometer, vapour density apparatus, molar mass data.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Obtain % composition (C, H, O, N, S, halogens); (ii) Convert % → grams per 100 g sample; (iii) Convert grams → moles (÷ A_r); (iv) Find simplest mole ratio (÷ smallest, multiply to clear decimals); (v) Write empirical formula, compute E_r; (vi) Obtain M_r (given or measured); (vii) n = M_r/E_r; (viii) Molecular = n × empirical.
    6. Result: Unique molecular formula identifying the compound.
    7. Significance: Turns anonymous % data into specific molecular identity — essential for new drug/natural product characterization.

    Process: Combustion analysis (source of %C and %H)

    1. Definition: Burning organic sample in excess O₂; weighing CO₂ and H₂O produced to determine %C and %H.
    2. Location: Organic analysis laboratory.
    3. Requirements: Combustion train (CuO furnace, Mg(ClO₄)₂ for H₂O, KOH/Ascarite for CO₂), analytical balance (±0.1 mg).
    4. Components: Sample boat, O₂ flow, combustion tube (CuO, 800°C), H₂O absorber (Mg(ClO₄)₂), CO₂ absorber (KOH/Ascarite), balance.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Weigh empty absorbers; (ii) Weigh ~0.1 g sample; (iii) Burn in O₂ stream; (iv) Re-weigh absorbers; (v) Δmass H₂O absorber = mass H₂O; Δmass CO₂ absorber = mass CO₂; (vi) %C = mass CO₂ × (12/44) × 100 / sample mass; (vii) %H = mass H₂O × (2/18) × 100 / sample mass; (viii) %O = 100 − (%C + %H + %N + %S + ...).
    6. Result: %C, %H, (%O by difference) — the data for empirical formula.
    7. Significance: The classic experimental source of composition data for unknown organics.

  • 8. Conceptual Relationships

    Within this topic

    • Atoms/molecules/ions/radicals → valency crossing → formulae → A_r/M_r → molar mass → mole triangle (mass↔moles↔particles↔volume) → empirical formula from % → molecular formula from M_r.
    • Every conversion uses the mole as the central hub.

    To other units

    • Stoichiometry: Mole ratios from balanced equations, limiting reactant, % yield — all use the mole triangle.
    • Atomic Structure: Isotopes explain fractional A_r; electron configuration explains valency and bonding.
    • Chemical Bonding: Lewis structures, VSEPR shapes, hybridization — all start from valence electrons and formulae.
    • States of Matter: Gas laws (PV = nRT) use n = moles; molar volume at STP; kinetic theory counts particles.
    • All units: Empirical/molecular formula determination underpins organic analysis, qualitative analysis, combustion analysis.

    To real life

    • Fertilizer bag: 46% N = % composition from formula.
    • Gas cylinder: 47 kg LPG ≈ 1000 moles ≈ 22,400 L at STP.
    • Drug label: 500 mg paracetamol = 3.3 mmol; dose in moles per kg.
    • Blood test: glucose 5 mmol/L = 90 mg/dL; HbA1c % = moles glycated Hb / total Hb.
    • Air quality: PM2.5 μg/m³ → moles/m³ via M_r.

  • 10. Topic Mastery Check

    Important definitions

    1. Atom — smallest unit of an element retaining its chemical identity.
    2. Molecule — two or more atoms bonded, acting as one unit (homoatomic or heteroatomic).
    3. Ion — charged atom/group from electron loss/gain (cation +, anion −).
    4. Radical — charged group of atoms with collective identity in reactions (NH₄⁺, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, OH⁻).
    5. A_r — relative atomic mass; weighted average of isotopes relative to ¹²C = 12.
    6. M_r — relative molecular mass; sum of A_r in formula.
    7. amu (u, Da) — 1/12th mass of ¹²C atom ≈ 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg.
    8. Mole — amount containing exactly 6.02214076×10²³ entities (exact since SI 2019).
    9. Molar mass — mass of one mole in g·mol⁻¹, numerically = M_r.
    10. Molar volume — 22.4 L·mol⁻¹ at NEB STP (0°C, 1 atm).
    11. Empirical formula — simplest whole-number atom ratio.
    12. Molecular formula — actual atom numbers in one molecule.
    13. n = M_r/E_r — multiplier from empirical to molecular.

    Key concepts to remember

    • Atoms combine in whole-number ratios (Dalton) → molecules/ions/radicals.
    • Masses go relative: amu = ¹²C/12; A_r weighted (Cl 35.5); M_r summed (H₂SO₄ 98, urea 60, CaCO₃ 100).
    • The mole (exact N_A) with molar mass (g/mol) and 22.4 L/mol at NEB STP interconverts mass↔moles↔particles↔volume via the triangle.
    • Empirical (CH₂O) ×n = molecular (C₆H₁₂O₆), n = M_r/E_r; salts have empirical formulae only.
    • Combustion analysis: %C = mass CO₂ × 12/44; %H = mass H₂O × 2/18; %O by difference.

    Important classifications

    • Species: Atom (0 charge), Molecule (0 charge, 2+ atoms), Cation (+), Anion (−), Radical (±, group).
    • Formula type: Molecular (covalent) vs Empirical only (ionic).
    • Mass scale: amu (atomic) ↔ molar mass (macroscopic) — same number, different units.
    • M_r determination: Vapour density (M_r = 2×VD), mass spec, colligative, ideal gas law.

    Common conceptual confusions

    • amu is a mass (kg), mole is a count — never interchange.
    • M_r is unitless; molar mass carries g/mol — same digits, different quantities.
    • 22.4 L/mol needs all three: gas, 0°C, 1 atm (NEB STP).
    • % composition needs molar mass first (next topic completes the loop).
    • Empirical gives ratio, never identity (CH₂O ×n family: glucose, formaldehyde, lactic acid).
    • Ionic salts (NaCl) have no molecular formula — only empirical (simplest ratio).
    • Vapour density needs H₂ = 2 scale stated; M_r = 2×VD.
    • Limiting reactant is not the smallest mass — it's smallest (moles/coefficient).
    • % yield >100% means impure product or incomplete drying, not magic.

    Answers

    1. amu = 1/12th mass of ¹²C = 1.660539×10⁻²⁷ kg; mole = exactly 6.02214076×10²³ entities (SI 2019).
    2. Urea CO(NH₂)₂: 12+16+2(14+2) = 60; CaCO₃: 40+12+48 = 100.
    3. n = 49/98 = 0.5 mol; molecules = 0.5 × 6.022×10²³ = 3.011×10²³.
    4. 40/12 = 3.33; 6.7/1 = 6.7; 53.3/16 = 3.33 → ÷3.33 → 1:2:1 → CH₂O.
    5. Empirical = simplest ratio (CH₂O); Molecular = actual (C₆H₁₂O₆). NaCl empirical only; H₂O both same.
    6. VD = mass of gas volume / mass of same volume H₂; M_r = 2 × VD (H₂ = 2 scale).
    7. Limiting reactant = consumed first; identify by (moles/coefficient) smallest.
    8. Cl isotopes: 75.77% ³⁵Cl (35) + 24.23% ³⁷Cl (37) → 0.7577×35 + 0.2423×37 ≈ 35.45.
    9. M_r unitless (relative); molar mass = M_r g·mol⁻¹ (absolute, with units).
    10. n = 4.4/44 = 0.1 mol; V = 0.1 × 22.4 = 2.24 L.
    11. Balanced equations conserve atoms; mass ratios depend on M_r; moles give universal integer ratios.
    12. N_A now exact by definition — no uncertainty in mole-based calculations; previously measured with ~10⁻⁷ relative uncertainty.
    13. Ionic = giant lattice of alternating ions; no discrete molecules; formula = simplest ratio (NaCl).
    14. No — CH₂O family: glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆), formaldehyde (CH₂O), lactic acid (C₃H₆O₃). Need M_r to distinguish.
    15. C-12 = exactly 12 u by definition; O-16 was physical sample with isotopic variation. C-12 is universally reproducible.
    16. 11.2 L CO₂ at STP = 0.5 mol → 0.5 × 44 = 22 g.
    17. CH₂O; M_r 90 → n = 90/30 = 3 → C₃H₆O₃ (lactic acid).
    18. n = 1.204×10²³ / 6.022×10²³ = 0.2 mol → m = 0.2 × 18 = 3.6 g.
    19. M_r = 2×30 = 60; n = 60/14 = 4.29 not integer → empirical wrong or VD error. If empirical = CH₂ (E_r=14), n=60/14=4.29 — impossible. Valid: empirical = CH₂O (30), n=2 → C₂H₄O₂ (acetic acid).
    20. 50 kg urea = 50,000/60 = 833.33 mol → 833.33 × 6.022×10²³ = 5.02×10²⁶ molecules. Zn + H₂SO₄: 0.5 mol H₂SO₄ → 0.5 mol H₂ → 0.5 × 22.4 = 11.2 L H₂ at STP.

Confusion

  • M_r is a pure number; molar mass carries g/mol — same digits, different quantities.
  • 22.4 L/mol needs all three: gas, 0°C, 1 atm (NEB STP).
  • amu is a mass (kg), mole is a count — never interchange.
  • Empirical gives ratio, never identity (CH₂O ×n family).
  • Ionic salts (NaCl) have empirical formulae only — no molecular formula.
  • Vapour density needs H₂ = 2 scale stated; M_r = 2×VD.
  • Limiting reactant is not the smallest mass — it's smallest (moles/coefficient).
  • % yield >100% means impure product or incomplete drying, not magic.

Practice

—

Universal Facts

  • 1 mole = 6.02214076×10²³ entities (Avogadro constant, Nₐ, exact since SI 2019). Molar mass (M) is g·mol⁻¹; for a compound M = Σ(atomic masses).
  • n (mol) = mass/M = volume(ideal gas, STP)/22.4 L = N/Nₐ.
  • Limiting reactant is the one consumed first; % yield = (experimental/theoretical)×100.
  • M_r = 2 × vapour density (H₂ = 2 scale).
  • A_r is abundance-weighted average; Cl = 35.45 from 75.77% ³⁵Cl + 24.23% ³⁷Cl.

Examples

  • 50 kg urea sack holds countable moles only via N_A.
  • '46-0-0' bag label is percentage composition compressed.
  • CaCO₃ kiln: 100 g → 56 g + 44 g as mole recipe.
  • Glucose: CH₂O ×6 = C₆H₁₂O₆; lactic acid: CH₂O ×3 = C₃H₆O₃ (same ratio, different molecule).
  • Chlorine A_r 35.5 = 0.7577×35 + 0.2423×37 (isotope weighted average).

Formulas

  • n = m / M
  • n = V / 22.4 (g at STP)
  • n = N / Nₐ
  • M_r = Σ A_r
  • M_r = 2 × vapour density (H₂ = 2)
  • % yield = (actual / theoretical) × 100
  • n = M_r / E_r (empirical → molecular)

Numericals

  • M_r: H₂SO₄ 98, urea 60, CaCO₃ 100, glucose 180.
  • Cl average: 0.7577×35 + 0.2423×37 = 35.45.
  • 49 g H₂SO₄ = 0.5 mol = 3.011×10²³ molecules.
  • 4.4 g CO₂ = 0.1 mol = 2.24 L at STP.
  • 1.204×10²³ H₂O molecules = 0.2 mol = 3.6 g.
  • 40/6.7/53.3 → CH₂O; M_r 180 → C₆H₁₂O₆ (n=6); M_r 90 → C₃H₆O₃ (n=3).
  • 50 kg urea = 833.33 mol = 5.02×10²⁶ molecules.
  • VD 45 → M_r 90; CH₂O (30) ×3 → C₃H₆O₃.

Key Points

  • amu = ¹²C/12; A_r weighted (Cl 35.5); M_r summed (H₂SO₄ 98).
  • Mole exact N_A; triangle: n = m/M = V/22.4 = N/Nₐ.
  • Empirical simplest ratio; molecular = n × empirical, n = M_r/E_r.
  • Combustion analysis: C from CO₂ (×12/44), H from H₂O (×2/18), O by difference.
  • VD = 2 × M_r; limiting reactant = smallest (moles/coefficient).

Summary

Atoms, molecules, ions and radicals (valencies crossing into formulae) are counted by the mole (exact N_A = 6.02214076×10²³ since SI 2019) and weighed by amu (¹²C/12 ≈ 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg). A_r is abundance-weighted (Cl 35.5); M_r is summed (H₂SO₄ 98, urea 60, CaCO₃ 100). The triangle n = m/M = V/22.4 = N/N_A (22.4 L at NEB STP: 0°C, 1 atm) interconverts everything. Empirical (CH₂O) ×n = molecular (C₆H₁₂O₆), n = M_r/E_r; salts have empirical formulae only. Figures: Dalton-sphere builds, mole triangle, Dalton's 1808 plate, bent H₂O.
