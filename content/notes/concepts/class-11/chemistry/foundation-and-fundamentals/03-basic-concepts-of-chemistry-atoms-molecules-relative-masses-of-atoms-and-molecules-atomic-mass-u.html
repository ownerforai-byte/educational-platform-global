    1. Introduction

    Before chemistry can calculate anything — a fertilizer dose, a gas volume, a percentage — it must count particles it cannot see. The counting system is the mole, the weighing scale is the atomic mass unit, and the shorthand is the chemical formula. This topic builds that entire toolkit: atom vs molecule, relative masses, amu, radicals, and the two formulae (empirical and molecular) that compress analysis into symbols.

    A fertilizer bag labelled "46-0-0" is this topic compressed: 46% nitrogen by the percentage logic the next topic completes. A 50 kg urea sack holds a countable number of molecules only because the mole makes counting possible — 6.02214076×10²³ of them per mole, exact by the SI 2019 definition.

    What you will learn: (i) atoms, molecules, ions and radicals; (ii) relative atomic/molecular masses and amu; (iii) the mole and Avogadro's number; (iv) molar volume at STP; (v) empirical vs molecular formula with the n = M/E relation.

    Learning outcomes. You can: interconvert grams ↔ moles ↔ particles ↔ litres at STP; compute molecular masses and average atomic masses; and derive empirical and molecular formulae from data.

  • 2. Concepts and definitions

    Atoms, molecules, ions, radicals

    1. Atom. Smallest unit of an element keeping its identity (H, O, Na). Molecule. Two or more atoms bonded acting as one unit: homoatomic (H₂, O₂, O₃) or heteroatomic (H₂O, CO₂, NH₃). Ion. Charged atom/group (Na⁺, Cl⁻, SO₄²⁻). Radical. Charged group of atoms with collective identity in reactions (NH₄⁺, NO₃⁻, CO₃²⁻, OH⁻) — valency rides on radicals, and salt formulae assemble from them.
    2. Valency reminder. Combining capacity: H/Na/Ag = 1, O/Ca/Zn = 2, Al = 3, C = 4. Cation valency × anion valency cross to write formulae (Ca²⁺ + Cl⁻ → CaCl₂).
    3. Assumptions box. Relative masses use the C-12 scale; gases assumed ideal at STP for molar volume; Avogadro's number exact (defined, not measured) since SI 2019.

    Relative masses and amu

    1. Why relative. Atoms are too light for grams (H ≈ 1.67×10⁻²⁴ g), so masses are quoted relative to a standard: 1 amu (1 u, 1 dalton) = 1/12th the mass of one carbon-12 atom ≈ 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg.
    2. Relative atomic mass (A_r). Average mass of an element's atoms relative to C-12 = 12, weighted by natural abundance: chlorine = 0.75×35 + 0.25×37 ≈ 35.5 — the reason the periodic table shows decimals.
    3. Relative molecular mass (M_r). Sum of A_r in the formula: H₂O = 2(1) + 16 = 18; H₂SO₄ = 2 + 32 + 64 = 98; CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100; urea CO(NH₂)₂ = 12 + 16 + 2(14 + 2) = 60.
    4. Mole. Amount containing exactly 6.02214076×10²³ entities (N_A, exact since 2019). Molar mass = mass of one mole in grams = M_r in g·mol⁻¹ (18 g water, 98 g H₂SO₄ per mole).
    5. Molar volume. One mole of any ideal gas occupies 22.4 L at 0°C and 1 atm — the NEB STP convention used in every numerical here. (Validity note: IUPAC's current STP is 100 kPa, giving 22.7 L; NEB examinations use 22.4 L at 1 atm unless stated.)
    6. Empirical vs molecular. Empirical = simplest whole-number ratio (CH₂O for glucose); molecular = actual numbers (C₆H₁₂O₆); related by molecular = n × empirical with n = M_r/E_r. Ionic salts (NaCl) and giant structures have empirical formulae only.

  • 3. Reaction and equation block — the mole in action

    1. Formation with masses. C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g): 12 g + 32 g → 44 g — the equation as a weighing recipe (1 mol : 1 mol : 1 mol).
    2. Gas formation. Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g): 65.4 g Zn → 2 g H₂ → 22.4 L H₂ at STP — solid, solution and gas linked by one line.
    3. Decomposition (with condition). CaCO₃(s) →(Δ) CaO(s) + CO₂(g): 100 g → 56 g + 44 g — the kiln budget from the last topic, now in moles.
    4. Reading rule. Coefficients = particles = moles = (for gases at same T/P) volumes. One memorised line, four interchangeable readings.

  • 5. Worked numericals (Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer with unit)

    1. Molecular mass. M_r(H₂SO₄) = 2(1) + 32 + 4(16) = 98; M_r(urea) = 12 + 16 + 2(14+2) = 60; M_r(CaCO₃) = 40+12+48 = 100. Unitless — "98 amu per molecule, 98 g per mole".
    2. Average atomic mass. Chlorine: 75% ³⁵Cl + 25% ³⁷Cl → 0.75×35 + 0.25×37 = 26.25 + 9.25 = 35.5. Decimals on the table explained.
    3. Moles from mass. n = m/M: 49 g H₂SO₄ → 49/98 = 0.5 mol; contains 0.5×N_A = 3.011×10²³ molecules.
    4. Gas volume at STP. 4.4 g CO₂ → n = 4.4/44 = 0.1 mol → V = 0.1×22.4 = 2.24 L. (NEB STP: 0°C, 1 atm.)
    5. Particles to mass. 1.204×10²³ H₂O molecules → n = 1.204×10²³/6.022×10²³ = 0.2 mol → m = 0.2×18 = 3.6 g.
    6. Empirical formula. Compound: 40% C, 6.7% H, 53.3% O → masses per 100 g: 40/12 = 3.33, 6.7/1 = 6.7, 53.3/16 = 3.33 → ratio 1:2:1 → CH₂O. If M_r = 180, n = 180/30 = 6 → molecular C₆H₁₂O₆ (glucose).

    6. Key terms, worked angles and traps

    • amu (u, dalton) = 1/12th of C-12 ≈ 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg; the weighing scale of atoms.
    • A_r / M_r — relative atomic/molecular mass; weighted average / formula sum; unitless.
    • Mole — 6.02214076×10²³ entities (exact, SI 2019); molar mass in g·mol⁻¹ numerically equals M_r.
    • Molar volume — 22.4 L·mol⁻¹ at NEB STP (0°C, 1 atm); IUPAC 100 kPa gives 22.7 L.
    • Empirical (simplest ratio) vs molecular (actual) — molecular = n × empirical, n = M_r/E_r.
    • Traps: M_r unitless vs molar mass g/mol (same number, different meaning) · 22.4 L only for gases at STP · ionic salts have no molecular formula · abundance-weighting, not simple averages.

Summary

Atoms, molecules, ions and radicals (valencies crossing into formulae) are counted by the mole (exact N_A = 6.02214076×10²³ since SI 2019) and weighed by amu (C-12/12 ≈ 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg). A_r is abundance-weighted (Cl 35.5); M_r is summed (H₂SO₄ 98, urea 60, CaCO₃ 100). The triangle n = m/M = V/22.4 = N/N_A (22.4 L at NEB STP: 0°C, 1 atm) interconverts everything. Empirical (CH₂O) ×n = molecular (C₆H₁₂O₆), n = M_r/E_r; salts have empirical formulae only. Figures: Dalton-sphere builds, mole triangle, Dalton's 1808 plate, bent H₂O.
