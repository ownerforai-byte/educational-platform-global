Notes

  • Focus of this tab

  • The computational skill behind all the combination laws: converting raw percentage/mass data into whole-number ratios — the shared method of definite, multiple and reciprocal proportions, and of empirical-formula work.

  • The standard five-step method

  • (1) Take the percentage of each element as mass in 100 g (or use the given masses). (2) Divide each mass by the atomic mass of that element → moles. (3) Divide all values by the smallest. (4) If the ratios are still fractional (e.g. 1 : 1.5, 1 : 1.33, 1 : 1.67), multiply the whole set by 2, 3 or 4 respectively to clear fractions. (5) Interpret: the whole-number ratio is the atomic ratio in the compound.

  • Worked pattern 1 — a binary oxide

  • An oxide contains 60% metal and 40% oxygen (atomic mass of metal = 48). Metal = 60/48 = 1.25; O = 40/16 = 2.5. Divide by 1.25 → M : O = 1 : 2 → formula MO₂. (With 70% metal it would be 1.46 : 1.875 → 1 : 1.28… → careful: re-check data, since real ratios must resolve.)

  • Worked pattern 2 — two oxides checked against multiple proportions

  • Oxide A: 46.7% N; oxide B: 30.4% N. Per gram of nitrogen: A has 46.7/53.3 = 0.876 g O; B has 30.4/69.6 = 0.437 g O. Ratio 0.876 : 0.437 = 2 : 1 — a whole-number ratio, so the data obey the law of multiple proportions (NO₂-type and NO-type oxides).

  • Recognising the intended multiplier

  • • ×2 for halves: 1 : 1.5, 1 : 2.5.
  • • ×3 for thirds: 1 : 1.33, 1 : 1.67, 1 : 2.33.
  • • ×4 for quarters: 1 : 1.25, 1 : 1.75.

  • Error awareness (limitations of the method)

  • Real analytical data carry errors, so ratios like 1 : 1.48 may 'want' to be 1 : 1.5 while 1 : 1.35 may be measurement noise around 1 : 1.33. Judgement (and significant figures) decide; non-stoichiometric compounds (Fe₀.₉₃O) will never resolve exactly, and the method assumes the compound is a true formula compound.

Confusion

  • ❌ Any near-integer ratio may be rounded freely. ✅ Round only with analytical justification; 1 : 1.9 is not 1 : 2 in a berthollide.
  • ❌ The ratio is of masses. ✅ After dividing by atomic masses, the ratio is of atoms (moles), not grams.
  • ❌ Multiplying changes the formula. ✅ Scaling the whole ratio set preserves it (2 : 4 is the same as 1 : 2).

Practice

  • Describe the five-step method of converting percentage composition into a whole-number atomic ratio.
  • An oxide of a metal (atomic mass 56) contains 69.9% metal. Find its formula.
  • Two oxides of nitrogen contain 30.4% and 46.7% nitrogen. Verify the law of multiple proportions.
  • Which multipliers clear the ratios 1 : 1.33 and 1 : 1.25?

Universal Facts

  • This is exactly the arithmetic that produces empirical formulas from combustion data.
  • Cannizzaro used whole-number ratios of molecular weights to sort atomic masses in 1858.
  • The 'divide by smallest' step is the single most-omitted step in exam scripts.

Examples

  • Problem: A hydrocarbon gives C : H mass ratio 6 : 1. Find the empirical formula. Solution: C = 6/12 = 0.5; H = 1/1 = 1 → divide by 0.5 → C : H = 1 : 2 → CH₂.

Important Notes

  • Always divide by atomic masses before comparing — comparing raw masses is the classic wrong method.
  • State the multiplier used when clearing fractions (×2, ×3, ×4).
