Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Counting Without Weighing

    Avogadro's hypothesis (1811) — equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules — resolved the Dalton–Gay-Lussac conflict and gave chemists three deductions that turn gas volumes into molecular masses. It is the bridge between the gas laws and the mole.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Statement and meaning

    Equal volumes of all gases at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules. The law is strictly true for ideal gases and approximately true for real gases at low pressure.

    2.2 The three deductions (NEB's favourite 3m)

    DeductionResultUse
    Vapour density → molecular massM = 2 × VDWeigh a gas as vapour density, double it
    Molar volume1 mol gas = 22.4 L at STP (0 °C, 1 atm)Convert gas volume ↔ moles
    Equal moleculesH₂, O₂, N₂ etc. are diatomicExplains 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O volume ratios

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): The vapour density of a gas is 22. Find its molecular mass.

    • M = 2 × VD = 2 × 22 = 44 → the gas is CO₂ (C = 12 + O₂ = 32).

    Q (NEB-style): What volume does 8 g of O₂ occupy at STP? (O = 16)

    • n = 8/32 = 0.25 mol; V = 0.25 × 22.4 = 5.6 L.

    Q (NEB-style): 1 volume of N₂ combines with 3 volumes of H₂. How many volumes of NH₃ form?

    • N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃: 1 + 3 volumes of reactants → 2 volumes of product — molecule conservation, not volume conservation (4 → 2).

    2.4 Applications

    1. Determining molecular mass from vapour density.
    2. Determining the atomicity of gases (H₂, O₂, N₂, Cl₂ are diatomic).
    3. Deriving Gay-Lussac's law: equal volumes → equal molecules → simple volume ratios.
    4. Distinguishing atoms from molecules — Dalton's fatal confusion, Avogadro's fix.

    2.5 Similarities and differences — Avogadro's law vs Gay-Lussac's law

    Similarities: both concern gas volumes; both require the same temperature and pressure; both give simple whole-number ratios. Differences: Gay-Lussac is an experimental fact about reacting volumes; Avogadro is a hypothesis about molecules that explains the fact. Gay-Lussac compares reactants and products; Avogadro compares different gases in equal volumes.

    2.6 Similarities and differences — atom vs molecule

    Similarities: both are particles; both are electrically neutral; both are counted by the mole. Differences: an atom is the smallest particle of an element that takes part in a reaction; a molecule is the smallest particle of an element or compound that exists independently (H₂, H₂O). Hydrogen exists as H₂ molecules but reacts atom-wise inside compounds.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Gay-Lussac (Topic 2): Avogadro explains Gay-Lussac's volume ratios.
    • To the mole (Topic 4): 22.4 L at STP is the gas face of the mole.
    • To ideal gas laws (Unit 7): PV = nRT generalises 22.4 L to any T and P.

    4.1 Verified PYQs from the solved-paper bank

    No verified PYQ for this exact topic in the bank. Practise the deductions and applications as NEB-style practice.

    4.2 Likely question forms

    1. State Avogadro's law and give two deductions (3m).
    2. Vapour density numerical (2m).
    3. Gas volume stoichiometry (2–3m).

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Forgetting the ×2 in M = 2 × VD.
    2. Using 22.4 L at NTP or at room temperature — it holds at STP only.
    3. Saying volumes of products equal volumes of reactants — molecule numbers, not volumes, are conserved.

Confusion

  • Vapour density vs density: VD is a ratio relative to hydrogen (unitless); density is mass per volume (g/L). M = 2 × VD only because H₂ has M = 2.
  • STP vs NTP: STP = 0 °C, 1 atm (22.4 L/mol); NTP = 20 °C, 1 atm (24.0 L/mol) — do not mix them.

Practice

  • A gas has VD 16. What is its molecular mass, and which gas is it?

Formulas

    • M = 2 × VD (vapour density relative to H₂)
    • V = n × 22.4 L (gas at STP)
    • n = V/22.4 (gas at STP)

Universal Facts

  • Avogadro's hypothesis was ignored for 50 years (Cannizzaro revived it in 1860) — one of chemistry's greatest delayed recognitions.

Examples

  • VD of CO₂ = 22 — molecular mass 44: the fastest molecular-mass determination in chemistry.
