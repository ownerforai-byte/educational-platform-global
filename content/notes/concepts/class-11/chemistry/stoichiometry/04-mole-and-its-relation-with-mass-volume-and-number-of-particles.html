Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Chemistry's Currency Converter

    The mole is the SI unit for amount of substance: the amount containing as many elementary entities as there are atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12. One mole = 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number, Nₐ). The mole converts between the three currencies of chemistry: mass (g), volume (L of gas at STP) and number of particles.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The three faces of one mole

    MeasureQuantity in 1 molFormula
    MassMolar mass in grams (H₂O: 18 g)n = m/M
    Particles6.022 × 10²³ entitiesn = N/Nₐ
    Gas volume at STP22.4 Ln = V/22.4

    2.2 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB 2023, 3m): What is the mole concept? Calculate the number of molecules in 36 g of water.

    • Mole concept: the amount of substance containing as many particles as there are atoms in exactly 12 g of carbon-12; 1 mol = 6.022 × 10²³ particles.
    • M(H₂O) = 2(1) + 16 = 18 g/mol
    • n = 36/18 = 2 mol
    • N = 2 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules

    Q (NEB-style): How many atoms are in 0.5 mol of CO₂?

    • Molecules: 0.5 × 6.022 × 10²³ = 3.011 × 10²³
    • Atoms per molecule = 3 → total atoms = 3 × 3.011 × 10²³ = 9.033 × 10²³ atoms.

    Q (NEB-style): What is the mass of 5.6 L of O₂ at STP?

    • n = 5.6/22.4 = 0.25 mol; m = 0.25 × 32 = 8 g.

    2.3 Molar mass vs molecular mass — similarities and differences

    Similarities: both describe the mass of one molecule's worth of substance; both are computed from atomic masses; both are numerically equal. Differences: molecular mass is the mass of one molecule in u (amu) — relative, unitless in comparisons; molar mass is the mass of one mole in g/mol — an absolute laboratory quantity. H₂O: molecular mass 18 u; molar mass 18 g/mol.

    2.4 Mole vs molecule — similarities and differences

    Similarities: both count chemical particles; both connect to Avogadro's number. Differences: a molecule is one discrete particle (H₂O); a mole is a count — 6.022 × 10²³ of anything (molecules, atoms, ions, electrons). You can have half a mole but not half a molecule.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Avogadro (Topic 3): Nₐ and 22.4 L/mol come straight from his hypothesis.
    • To calculations (Topic 5): every mass–volume–particle conversion is a mole hop.
    • To concentration (Topic 5): molarity counts moles per litre of solution.

Summary

The mole is chemistry's counting unit: 6.022 × 10²³ particles (Avogadro's number), defined against exactly 12 g of carbon-12. One mole equals the molar mass in grams (n = m/M), contains Nₐ particles (N = nNₐ), and occupies 22.4 L as a gas at STP (n = V/22.4). Molar mass (g/mol) and molecular mass (u) are numerically equal but differ in meaning; a mole is a count, a molecule is a particle. 36 g of water = 2 mol = 1.204 × 10²⁴ molecules (NEB 2023).
