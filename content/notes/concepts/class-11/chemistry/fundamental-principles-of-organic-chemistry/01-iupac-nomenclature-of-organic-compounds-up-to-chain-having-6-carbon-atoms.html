Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — A Name That Encodes the Structure

    Before IUPAC, one compound wore many names (wood spirit, grain alcohol…). The IUPAC system builds every name from the structure itself: a word root for chain length, a suffix for the bond type/functional group, and prefixes for substituents. Master the rules for chains up to six carbons and you can name — or draw — any simple organic compound from its formula alone.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The three parts of an IUPAC name

    • Word root: chain length — meth (1), eth (2), prop (3), but (4), pent (5), hex (6).
    • Primary suffix: saturation — ane (single bonds), ene (C=C), yne (C≡C).
    • Prefix: substituents (methyl, ethyl, chloro…) written before the root in alphabetical order, each with a locant (position number). Functional-group suffixes (–ol, –al, –one, –oic acid) replace or follow the primary suffix with their own locant.

    2.2 The rules

    1. Select the longest continuous carbon chain as parent (must include the functional group or multiple bond if present).
    2. Number from the end that gives the lowest locants — to the functional group first, then multiple bonds, then substituents.
    3. Name substituents with locants; use di-, tri- for repeats; list prefixes alphabetically (di/tri ignored in alphabetising).
    4. Assemble: prefixes (alphabetical) + root + suffix (+ functional locant): e.g. butan-2-ol, 2-methylpropanoic acid.

    2.3 Worked examples

    • CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃ → butane. CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ → 2-methylbutane.
    • NEB 2024: CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ → 4-carbon chain, OH on C-2 → butan-2-ol.
    • CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH → butanoic acid; CH₃CHO → ethanal; HOCH₂CH₂OH → ethane-1,2-diol; CH₃CH(CH₃)COOH → 2-methylpropanoic acid.
    • NEB 2023 (harder): CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH(CH₂CH₃)CH₂OH → longest chain with OH = 6 carbons; number from the OH end → OH at C-1, ethyl at C-2, methyl at C-5 → 2-ethyl-5-methylhexan-1-ol (ethyl before methyl alphabetically).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why "longest chain" and "lowest locant" exist

    • One structure must yield one name — every chemist in the world must draw the same molecule from the same words. The longest-chain rule fixes the parent; the lowest-locant rule fixes the numbering; alphabetical order fixes the listing. Any other choice would give two names for one compound.

    4.2 Functional groups outrank substituents

    • When a functional group (OH, CHO, COOH) is present, numbering serves it first — butan-2-ol is numbered from the OH end even if a methyl then gets a higher number. Priority: COOH > CHO > C=O > OH > C=C/C≡C > alkyl.

    4.3 Common vs systematic names

    • Acetone = propanone; acetic acid = ethanoic acid; ethylene = ethene. Common names persist in industry and labs, but NEB answers should use IUPAC.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Alphabetical order ignores di/tri: "2-ethyl-5-methyl…" — ethyl precedes methyl regardless of locant; "dimethyl" alphabetises under "m".

  • The OH carbon counts in the chain: butan-2-ol is a 4-carbon chain with OH on C-2 — students who number from the wrong end write "3-methylbutan-1-ol" style errors.
  • Lowest locant set wins, not lowest first digit: 2,3,4 beats 2,4,5 even though both start with 2 — compare locant by locant.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Write the IUPAC name of CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃. — The longest chain contains four carbon atoms → root but-; only single bonds → -an-; the –OH group lies on C-2 when numbering from the nearer end → suffix -2-ol. IUPAC name: butan-2-ol.

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ, basic-concept bank): Give the IUPAC names of (a) CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH (b) CH₃CHO (c) HOCH₂CH₂OH (d) CH₃CH(CH₃)COOH. — (a) Four-carbon chain with the –COOH carbon as C-1 → butanoic acid. (b) Two-carbon chain with an –CHO group → ethanal. (c) Two carbons each bearing an OH → ethane-1,2-diol. (d) The longest chain including the carboxyl carbon has three carbons with a methyl on C-2 → 2-methylpropanoic acid.
  • NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): Give the IUPAC name of CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH(CH₂CH₃)CH₂OH. — The longest chain containing the –CH₂OH carbon has six carbons → root hex-, suffix -1-ol (numbering from the OH end). Substituents: ethyl on C-2 (the –CH₂CH₃ branch) and methyl on C-5. Listed alphabetically: 2-ethyl-5-methylhexan-1-ol.
  • NEB-style (2m): State any three rules of IUPAC nomenclature. — (i) The longest continuous carbon chain (including any functional group or multiple bond) is the parent. (ii) Numbering starts from the end giving the lowest locants, serving the functional group first. (iii) Substituents are named as prefixes with locants, repeats as di-/tri-, listed in alphabetical order before the root and suffix.

Confusion

  • ❌ All atoms are identical in an element. ✅ Isotopes have same protons but different neutrons.
  • ❌ Atoms are indivisible. ✅ Atoms consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • ❌ Electrons orbit in fixed paths like planets. ✅ Electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals).
  • ❌ Heisenberg uncertainty means we cannot measure position and momentum precisely simultaneously — not that we lack good instruments.
  • ❌ de Broglie wavelength applies only to light. ✅ All matter has wave nature; detectable mainly for small masses like electrons.
  • ❌ Quantum number l can be any integer. ✅ l ranges from 0 to n−1.
  • ❌ Magnetic quantum number ml determines energy. ✅ ml determines orbital orientation, not energy (in absence of magnetic field).
  • ❌ Spin quantum number ms can be ±½ or any value. ✅ ms is always +½ or −½.
  • ❌ Aufbau principle means filling from bottom to top of the diagram. ✅ Fill lowest-energy orbitals first: 1s→2s→2p→3s→3p→4s→3d→4p→...
  • ❌ Pauli exclusion allows two electrons per orbital with any spin. ✅ Two electrons in same orbital must have opposite spins.
  • ❌ Hund's rule means pair electrons before spreading. ✅ Electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly first with parallel spins.
  • ❌ The mole is just a number like dozen. ✅ 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro constant) — a bridge between atomic and macroscopic scales.
  • ❌ Molar mass and molecular mass are the same thing. ✅ Molecular mass is in amu; molar mass is in g/mol — numerically equal but different units.
  • ❌ Limiting reagent is the reactant with smallest mass. ✅ Limiting reagent is the one that produces the least product (determined by moles, not mass).
  • ❌ % yield can exceed 100%. ✅ % yield >100% indicates impure product or incomplete drying.
  • ❌ Molarity and molality are the same. ✅ Molarity = mol/L solution (changes with T); Molality = mol/kg solvent (temperature-independent).
  • ❌ A strong acid completely ionizes in water. ✅ Strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) are ~100% ionized; weak acids partially ionize.
  • ❌ pH can be negative. ✅ pH < 0 is possible for concentrations >1M (e.g., 2M HCl has pH ≈ −0.3).
  • ❌ Kc changes when concentration changes. ✅ Kc is constant at constant temperature; only temperature changes K.
  • ❌ Adding catalyst shifts equilibrium position. ✅ Catalyst speeds both forward and backward reactions equally; no shift in equilibrium.
  • ❌ High pressure always favors products. ✅ High pressure favors side with fewer gas moles (Le Chatelier).
  • ❌ Exothermic reactions are spontaneous. ✅ Spontaneity depends on ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, not just ΔH.
  • ❌ Endothermic reactions cannot be spontaneous. ✅ Endothermic reactions can be spontaneous if TΔS > ΔH (e.g., ice melting above 0°C).
  • ❌ ΔH and ΔU are always the same. ✅ ΔH = ΔU + PΔV; they differ when gas moles change.
  • ❌ Heat (q) and work (w) are state functions. ✅ q and w are path functions; only ΔU, ΔH, ΔS, ΔG are state functions.
  • ❌ Oxidation and reduction happen separately. ✅ Oxidation and reduction always occur together (redox).
  • ❌ Oxidizing agent gets oxidized. ✅ Oxidizing agent accepts electrons and gets reduced.
  • ❌ Reducing agent gets reduced. ✅ Reducing agent donates electrons and gets oxidized.
  • ❌ Faraday's laws apply only to metals. ✅ Faraday's laws apply to any substance deposited/dissolved at electrodes.
  • ❌ Real gases obey PV=nRT at all conditions. ✅ Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • ❌ van der Waals constants a and b are same for all gases. ✅ a (attraction) and b (volume) are gas-specific.
  • ❌ Surface tension increases with temperature. ✅ Surface tension decreases as temperature increases.
  • ❌ Viscosity decreases with temperature for liquids. ✅ Viscosity of liquids decreases with T; gases increase with T.
  • ❌ Colligative properties depend on nature of solute. ✅ Colligative properties depend only on number of particles, not identity.
  • ❌ Raoult's law applies to all solutions. ✅ Raoult's law is exact for ideal solutions; approximate for dilute real solutions.
  • ❌ Osmotic pressure is a colligative property. ✅ Yes — π = CRT depends on solute particle concentration.
  • ❌ All carbon compounds are organic. ✅ CO, CO₂, carbonates, and carbides are inorganic despite containing carbon.

Summary

The IUPAC system is a reversible code between structure and name. A name is assembled from a word root (meth → hex for C1 → C6), a primary suffix (-ane/-ene/-yne for single/double/triple bonds), functional suffixes with locants (-ol, -al, -one, -oic acid), and alphabetical prefixes with locants for substituents (di-/tri- ignored in alphabetising). Three rules make it unambiguous: the longest chain (including the functional group) is the parent; numbering gives the lowest locants with the functional group served first; prefixes are listed alphabetically. Worked through: CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ = butan-2-ol (NEB 2024); CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH = butanoic acid; CH₃CHO = ethanal; HOCH₂CH₂OH = ethane-1,2-diol; CH₃CH(CH₃)COOH = 2-methylpropanoic acid; and the six-carbon alcohol CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH(CH₂CH₃)CH₂OH = 2-ethyl-5-methylhexan-1-ol (NEB 2023).

Examples

  • The NEB 2023 name decoded — CH3CH(CH3)CH2CH2CH(CH2CH3)CH2OH looks fearsome until the rules run: longest chain holding the OH carbon = 6 (hexan-1-ol), ethyl at C-2, methyl at C-5, alphabetical order — 2-ethyl-5-methylhexan-1-ol.

Important Concepts

  • One structure must yield one name worldwide: longest chain fixes the parent, lowest locant fixes the numbering, alphabetical order fixes the listing — three rules, one unambiguous name.

(Numbering from the other end would wrongly put OH at C-3, violating the lowest-locant rule.)

(b) Two carbons ending in –CHO → ethanal. (c) Two carbons, each carrying an –OH → ethane-1,2-diol. (d) The longest chain containing the carboxyl carbon has three carbons (the methyl is a branch, not part of the parent) with the branch on C-2 → 2-methylpropanoic acid.
  • NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): Give the IUPAC name of the compound CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH(CH₂CH₃)CH₂OH. — Step 1 — parent chain: the longest chain containing the –CH₂OH carbon runs through six carbons → root hex-. Step 2 — numbering: from the OH end so the hydroxyl gets the lowest locant → hexan-1-ol. Step 3 — substituents: an ethyl group on C-2 (the –CH₂CH₃ branch) and a methyl group on C-5. Step 4 — assembly: prefixes in alphabetical order (ethyl before methyl) → 2-ethyl-5-methylhexan-1-ol.

(ii) Number the chain from the end giving the lowest locants, with the functional group taking priority, then multiple bonds, then substituents. (iii) Name substituents as prefixes with their locants, writing repeated groups as di-/tri- (ignored when alphabetising) and listing different prefixes in alphabetical order before the root and suffix.
