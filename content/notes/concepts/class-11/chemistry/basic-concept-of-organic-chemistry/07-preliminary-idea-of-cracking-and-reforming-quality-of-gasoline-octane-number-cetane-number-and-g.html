Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Making Petrol Behave

    Crude oil gives long-chain hydrocarbons; engines want branched ones. Cracking breaks big molecules into useful small ones, reforming reshapes straight chains into branched/aromatic ones, and the octane number measures the result. For diesel the logic reverses — the cetane number rewards straight chains. Additives (once TEL, now oxygenates) fine-tune the fuel.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Cracking

    • Cracking: decomposition of large hydrocarbon molecules into smaller, more useful ones by heat, with or without a catalyst.
    • Thermal cracking: high temperature (~500–700 °C) and pressure.
    • Catalytic cracking: lower temperature (~450–500 °C) with a zeolite/aluminosilicate catalyst — better yields of branched alkanes and aromatics; the main petroleum-refining workhorse.
    • Example: C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄ (decane → octane + ethene). Products: petrol-range alkanes + valuable alkenes for the chemical industry.

    2.2 Reforming

    • Reforming: conversion of straight-chain alkanes into branched-chain alkanes and aromatics (same carbon count, new skeleton) to raise octane number.
    • Catalytic reforming ("platforming"): Pt catalyst, ~500 °C, modest pressure. e.g. n-hexane → benzene + H₂; n-heptane → toluene + H₂.

    2.3 Octane number (rating)

    • Measures a petrol's resistance to knocking — premature, explosive auto-ignition that wastes power and damages engines.
    • Scale: iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane) = 100 (branched, burns smoothly); n-heptane = 0 (straight, knocks badly).
    • A petrol's octane number = % of iso-octane in an iso-octane/n-heptane mix that knocks identically. Branched and aromatic = high octane; straight = low.

    2.4 Cetane number (diesel's mirror)

    • Diesel engines ignite fuel by compression, so they want fuel that ignites readily — the opposite preference.
    • Scale: n-cetane (n-hexadecane, straight C₁₆) = 100 (ignites easily); α-methylnaphthalene = 0.
    • Straight-chain alkanes = high cetane (good diesel, bad petrol); branched/aromatic = high octane (good petrol, poor diesel).

    2.5 Gasoline additives

    • TEL (tetraethyl lead, Pb(C₂H₅)₄): historic anti-knock additive, raised octane at tiny doses — phased out worldwide because lead poisons people and catalysts.
    • Modern octane boosters: MTBE (being restricted for groundwater), ethanol (renewable blend, common today), plus detergents and antioxidants.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why branched fuels resist knocking

    • In a straight-chain alkane, compression and heat ignite the end gases before the spark — a pressure spike = knock. Branched molecules burn more slowly and uniformly, waiting for the flame front — high octane. This single idea explains cracking/reforming/additives alike: all three create or preserve branching.

    4.2 Cracking vs reforming — different jobs

    • Cracking changes molecular size (big → small; also makes alkenes as chemical feedstock). Reforming keeps size, changes shape (straight → branched/aromatic). Refineries run both: cracking supplies molecules, reforming upgrades them.

    4.3 The petrol/diesel seesaw

    • The same structural feature helps one fuel and hurts the other: branching raises octane (petrol) and lowers cetane (diesel). Straight-run kerosene/diesel fractions suit diesel engines; petrol needs the branched fraction.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Octane number is not the % of octane in the fuel: it is the % of iso-octane in the comparison mixture that matches the fuel's knocking — a fuel's own octane content is irrelevant to the rating.

  • Cracking and reforming are not synonyms: cracking cuts molecules apart; reforming rearranges them. Different chemistry, different catalysts, different products.
  • Higher cetane = easier ignition = good diesel; higher octane = harder ignition = good petrol: the two scales reward opposite behaviour — never mix them up.

    NEB-style (3m): What is cracking? Describe catalytic cracking with an example and state its importance. — Cracking is the decomposition of large hydrocarbon molecules into smaller, more useful ones by the action of heat, with or without a catalyst. In catalytic cracking the heavy fraction is heated to about 450–500 °C over a zeolite (aluminosilicate) catalyst, which lets the process run at lower temperature than thermal cracking and gives better yields of branched alkanes and aromatics. Example: C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄ (decane → octane + ethene). Importance: (i) converts surplus heavy fractions into petrol-range fuels; (ii) supplies alkenes (ethene, propene) — the feedstock of the polymer and chemical industries; (iii) raises the octane number of the product by producing branched chains.

  • NEB-style (3m): Define octane number. Why is iso-octane rated 100 and n-heptane 0? — The octane number of a petrol measures its resistance to knocking (premature auto-ignition), defined as the percentage of iso-octane (2,2,4-trimethylpentane) in a mixture with n-heptane that knocks at the same compression ratio as the petrol. Iso-octane = 100: its highly branched structure burns smoothly and uniformly, resisting premature ignition. n-Heptane = 0: its straight chain ignites spontaneously under compression, knocking violently. Thus branched/aromatic fuels have high octane numbers and straight-chain fuels low ones.
  • NEB-style (2m): What is reforming? Why does it improve petrol quality? — Reforming is the conversion of straight-chain alkanes into branched-chain alkanes and aromatic hydrocarbons of the same carbon number, catalysed by platinum at about 500 °C (platforming), e.g. n-hexane → benzene + H₂. It improves petrol because branched and aromatic structures resist knocking, so the octane number of the fuel rises without changing its boiling range.
  • NEB-style (2m): Distinguish between octane number and cetane number. — The octane number rates petrol's knock resistance: iso-octane = 100, n-heptane = 0; branched/aromatic fuels score high. The cetane number rates diesel's ignition readiness (compression ignition): n-cetane (straight C₁₆) = 100, α-methylnaphthalene = 0; straight-chain alkanes score high. The two scales are opposites — a structure that improves one rating worsens the other.

Summary

Refining chemistry reshapes molecules to suit engines. Cracking breaks large hydrocarbons into smaller useful ones — thermally (~500–700 °C) or catalytically (zeolite, ~450–500 °C), e.g. C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄ — supplying petrol-range alkanes and alkene feedstock. Reforming (Pt, ~500 °C) keeps the size and changes the shape: straight chains become branched alkanes and aromatics (n-hexane → benzene). Octane number measures petrol's knock resistance on the scale iso-octane = 100, n-heptane = 0 — branching and aromaticity score high because branched molecules burn smoothly instead of auto-igniting. Cetane number measures diesel's ignition readiness on the scale n-cetane = 100, α-methylnaphthalene = 0 — straight chains score high, the exact opposite preference. Additives fine-tune the fuel: TEL (Pb(C₂H₅)₄) once supplied cheap octane but is banned for lead poisoning, replaced by MTBE and ethanol blending.
