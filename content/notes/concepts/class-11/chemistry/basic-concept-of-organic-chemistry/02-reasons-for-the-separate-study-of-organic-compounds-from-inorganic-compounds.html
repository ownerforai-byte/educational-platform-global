Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Why Carbon Gets Its Own Shelf

    Organic chemistry is not studied separately by tradition but by necessity. Three facts force the separation: organic compounds are vastly more numerous, they behave differently (covalent, flammable, low-melting, isomer-rich), and they must be studied by different methods. This topic builds the full organic-vs-inorganic comparison and shows how Wöhler's urea blurred the border without erasing it.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Reason 1 — sheer numbers

    • More organic compounds are known than compounds of all the other elements combined (tens of millions).
    • The cause is carbon's self-linking (catenation) plus isomerism: one molecular formula can hide many compounds, and chains of any length can carry any functional group.

    2.2 Reason 2 — different properties

    • Organic compounds are covalent molecules: low melting/boiling points, volatile, flammable, non-conducting, soluble in organic solvents.
    • Inorganic compounds are frequently ionic lattices: crystalline, high-melting, non-flammable, conducting when molten or dissolved, water-soluble.
    • Organic reactions run between molecules, so they are slow and often need catalysts; ionic reactions meet in solution and finish in seconds.

    2.3 Reason 3 — different methods of study

    • Organic work demands its own toolkit: extraction, purification by crystallisation/distillation/chromatography, structure determination, and synthesis routes built step-by-step on the carbon skeleton.
    • Reaction mechanisms (bond breaking/formation in neutral molecules) differ fundamentally from ionic double-displacement chemistry.

    2.4 The border is conventional, not absolute

    • Wöhler's NH₄OCN → urea showed inorganic materials can build organic molecules; today urea, acetic acid and most organic families are made industrially from inorganic feedstocks (CO₂, NH₃, etc.).
    • The boundary that remains is about chemistry, not origin: compounds with the C–H, chain-building character are organic; carbonates, carbides, cyanides and carbon oxides behave as inorganic salts and oxides whatever their source.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 The full comparison table

    • Bonding, melting behaviour, flammability, conductivity, solubility, isomerism and reaction speed all move together — because they all follow from molecular covalent structure versus ionic lattice structure. Learn the table as one causal chain, not seven unrelated rows.

    4.2 Why the numbers differ so wildly

    • Every other element's chains top out quickly (Si–Si bonds are weak, so silanes stop at a few units). Carbon's C–C bond is strong enough for chains of unlimited length, and each carbon still has bonds left over for functional groups — combinatorial explosion.

    4.3 What Wöhler did and did not prove

    • He proved synthesis is possible — no vital force needed. He did not prove organic and inorganic chemistry are identical: the two families still differ in bonding and behaviour, which is exactly why they are still studied separately.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "Separate study" survives Wöhler: the vital force theory died, but the practical separation (different numbers, properties, methods) remains fully valid — do not answer "because organic compounds come from living things".

  • Slow reactions are not weak reactions: organic molecules react slowly because bonds must be broken in neutral molecules with a high activation barrier — the products can be perfectly stable.
  • Solubility follows polarity, not "organic-ness": small alcohols and acids (ethanol, acetic acid) dissolve in water because –OH/–COOH hydrogen-bond; the rule is like dissolves like.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Give reasons for the separate study of organic compounds from inorganic compounds. — (i) Number: organic compounds outnumber all inorganic compounds combined, because carbon catenates into chains and rings of unlimited length and each carbon carries functional groups — plus isomerism multiplies every formula. (ii) Properties: organic compounds are covalent molecules — low melting/boiling points, flammable, non-conducting, soluble in organic solvents — while inorganic compounds are largely ionic: crystalline, high-melting, conducting in solution, water-soluble. (iii) Methods: organic chemistry needs its own techniques — extraction, purification by distillation/crystallisation/chromatography, structural determination and stepwise synthesis — and its reactions are slow molecular processes with mechanisms, unlike fast ionic reactions. For these three reasons the two branches are studied as separate subjects.

  • NEB-style (2m): "Wöhler's urea synthesis removed the distinction between organic and inorganic chemistry." Comment. — The claim is only partly true. Wöhler's NH₄OCN → NH₂CONH₂ destroyed the vital force theory — organic compounds can indeed be synthesised from inorganic materials, so the origin distinction is gone. But the practical distinction remains: organic compounds are still far more numerous, covalent, low-melting, flammable and isomer-rich, and they are still studied by different methods. So the border moved from "where they come from" to "how they behave" — and on behaviour the separate study continues.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why do organic compounds generally have low melting and boiling points while inorganic compounds have high values? — Organic compounds exist as discrete covalent molecules held to each other only by weak intermolecular forces (van der Waals, dipole, hydrogen bonds), so little energy separates the molecules — low m.p./b.p. Inorganic compounds are usually ionic lattices held by strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions throughout the crystal, so large energy is needed to melt or boil them — high m.p./b.p.

Confusion

  • ❌ All atoms are identical in an element. ✅ Isotopes have same protons but different neutrons.
  • ❌ Atoms are indivisible. ✅ Atoms consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • ❌ Electrons orbit in fixed paths like planets. ✅ Electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals).
  • ❌ Heisenberg uncertainty means we cannot measure position and momentum precisely simultaneously — not that we lack good instruments.
  • ❌ de Broglie wavelength applies only to light. ✅ All matter has wave nature; detectable mainly for small masses like electrons.
  • ❌ Quantum number l can be any integer. ✅ l ranges from 0 to n−1.
  • ❌ Magnetic quantum number ml determines energy. ✅ ml determines orbital orientation, not energy (in absence of magnetic field).
  • ❌ Spin quantum number ms can be ±½ or any value. ✅ ms is always +½ or −½.
  • ❌ Aufbau principle means filling from bottom to top of the diagram. ✅ Fill lowest-energy orbitals first: 1s→2s→2p→3s→3p→4s→3d→4p→...
  • ❌ Pauli exclusion allows two electrons per orbital with any spin. ✅ Two electrons in same orbital must have opposite spins.
  • ❌ Hund's rule means pair electrons before spreading. ✅ Electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly first with parallel spins.
  • ❌ The mole is just a number like dozen. ✅ 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro constant) — a bridge between atomic and macroscopic scales.
  • ❌ Molar mass and molecular mass are the same thing. ✅ Molecular mass is in amu; molar mass is in g/mol — numerically equal but different units.
  • ❌ Limiting reagent is the reactant with smallest mass. ✅ Limiting reagent is the one that produces the least product (determined by moles, not mass).
  • ❌ % yield can exceed 100%. ✅ % yield >100% indicates impure product or incomplete drying.
  • ❌ Molarity and molality are the same. ✅ Molarity = mol/L solution (changes with T); Molality = mol/kg solvent (temperature-independent).
  • ❌ A strong acid completely ionizes in water. ✅ Strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) are ~100% ionized; weak acids partially ionize.
  • ❌ pH can be negative. ✅ pH < 0 is possible for concentrations >1M (e.g., 2M HCl has pH ≈ −0.3).
  • ❌ Kc changes when concentration changes. ✅ Kc is constant at constant temperature; only temperature changes K.
  • ❌ Adding catalyst shifts equilibrium position. ✅ Catalyst speeds both forward and backward reactions equally; no shift in equilibrium.
  • ❌ High pressure always favors products. ✅ High pressure favors side with fewer gas moles (Le Chatelier).
  • ❌ Exothermic reactions are spontaneous. ✅ Spontaneity depends on ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, not just ΔH.
  • ❌ Endothermic reactions cannot be spontaneous. ✅ Endothermic reactions can be spontaneous if TΔS > ΔH (e.g., ice melting above 0°C).
  • ❌ ΔH and ΔU are always the same. ✅ ΔH = ΔU + PΔV; they differ when gas moles change.
  • ❌ Heat (q) and work (w) are state functions. ✅ q and w are path functions; only ΔU, ΔH, ΔS, ΔG are state functions.
  • ❌ Oxidation and reduction happen separately. ✅ Oxidation and reduction always occur together (redox).
  • ❌ Oxidizing agent gets oxidized. ✅ Oxidizing agent accepts electrons and gets reduced.
  • ❌ Reducing agent gets reduced. ✅ Reducing agent donates electrons and gets oxidized.
  • ❌ Faraday's laws apply only to metals. ✅ Faraday's laws apply to any substance deposited/dissolved at electrodes.
  • ❌ Real gases obey PV=nRT at all conditions. ✅ Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • ❌ van der Waals constants a and b are same for all gases. ✅ a (attraction) and b (volume) are gas-specific.
  • ❌ Surface tension increases with temperature. ✅ Surface tension decreases as temperature increases.
  • ❌ Viscosity decreases with temperature for liquids. ✅ Viscosity of liquids decreases with T; gases increase with T.
  • ❌ Colligative properties depend on nature of solute. ✅ Colligative properties depend only on number of particles, not identity.
  • ❌ Raoult's law applies to all solutions. ✅ Raoult's law is exact for ideal solutions; approximate for dilute real solutions.
  • ❌ Osmotic pressure is a colligative property. ✅ Yes — π = CRT depends on solute particle concentration.
  • ❌ All carbon compounds are organic. ✅ CO, CO₂, carbonates, and carbides are inorganic despite containing carbon.

Summary

Carbon gets its own shelf for three reasons. Numbers: catenation plus isomerism has produced tens of millions of organic compounds — more than all other chemistry combined. Properties: organic compounds are covalent molecules — low m.p./b.p., flammable, non-conducting, organic-solvent soluble, isomer-rich, slow-reacting — while inorganic compounds are largely ionic lattices with the opposite profile in every row. Methods: extraction, crystallisation, distillation, chromatography, stepwise synthesis and mechanism study belong to the organic toolkit. Wöhler's NH₄OCN → urea (1828) killed the vital force theory and removed the distinction of origin — but the distinction of behaviour stands, and that is what still justifies the separate study.
