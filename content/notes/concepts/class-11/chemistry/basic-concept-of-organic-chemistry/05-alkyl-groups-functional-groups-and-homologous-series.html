  • 1. Topic Introduction — R–, the Reactive Patch, and the Family Ladder

    Three vocabulary words organise every organic family: the alkyl group (R–, the carbon skeleton minus one H), the functional group (the reactive patch that decides the chemistry), and the homologous series (the family ladder whose rungs differ by one CH₂). NEB 2022 asked exactly this — definition, three characteristics, and the alkanol general formula.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Alkyl groups

    • An alkyl group is a monovalent group obtained by removing one hydrogen from an alkane: general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₁, written R–.
    • Methyl CH₃– (from methane), ethyl C₂H₅– (from ethane), propyl CH₃CH₂CH₂–, isopropyl (CH₃)₂CH–.
    • Alkyl groups are not independent molecules — they exist attached to something (in compounds, or as intermediates).

    2.2 Functional groups

    • The functional group is the atom or group responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound: –OH (alcohol), –CHO (aldehyde), >C=O (ketone), –COOH (carboxylic acid), –COOR (ester), –X (halide), –NH₂ (amine), C=C / C≡C (unsaturation).
    • The skeleton is mostly passive; the functional group is where reactions happen — one group, one family, one set of tests.

    2.3 Homologous series

    • A homologous series is a family of compounds with the same functional group, adjacent members differing by –CH₂– (14 u), sharing a general formula, gradation of physical properties, and similar chemical behaviour and preparation.
    • Alkanes: CH₄, C₂H₆, C₃H₈… (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂). Alkanols: CH₃OH, C₂H₅OH, C₃H₇OH… (CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 The CH₂ increment and its consequences

    • Each CH₂ adds 14 u to molecular mass — so melting/boiling points rise steadily along the series (graduation of physical properties), while the functional group keeps chemical properties uniform. Two predictions from one rule: heavier members are less volatile; all members react the same way.

    4.2 Why functional groups dominate

    • C–C and C–H bonds are strong and non-polar — the skeleton is inert. The functional group (polar bond, π bond, or lone pair) is the reactive site: ethanol and propanol differ only in skeleton length, so both behave as alcohols.

    4.3 Characteristics of a homologous series (the NEB 2022 list)

    1. Same general formula — all members fit one formula (CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH for alkanols).
    2. Successive members differ by CH₂ (14 u) — CH₃OH → C₂H₅OH → C₃H₇OH.
    3. Gradation of physical properties — m.p./b.p./density rise smoothly along the series.
    4. Same functional group → similar chemical properties — all alkanols give the same reactions.
    5. Similar methods of preparation — the same route (e.g. hydration of alkenes) makes successive members.
    6. Members can often be prepared from one another by fixed unit steps.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    R– is not a free molecule: methyl "CH₃" alone is a reactive fragment; in compounds it is a group with one open valency.

  • Same series ≠ same molecular formula: homologues differ by CH₂; isomers share a formula but may sit in different series (ethanol and dimethyl ether are both C₂H₆O but not homologues).
  • Gradation is in physical properties only: chemical properties are similar along the series (same group), not graded — the gradation word belongs to m.p./b.p./density/solubility.

State any three characteristics of a homologous series, and write the general formula of the alkanol series with two examples. — A functional group is the atom or group of atoms in a molecule that is responsible for its characteristic chemical reactions (e.g. –OH of alcohols, –COOH of acids). A homologous series is a family of organic compounds containing the same functional group, whose successive members differ by a –CH₂– unit (14 u) and which can be represented by a single general formula. Universal Facts

  • Adjacent homologues differ by 14 u — the mass of one CH2 — which is why homologous series were first noticed as arithmetic progressions in molecular mass.

Examples

  • The alkanol ladder — methanol, ethanol, propan-1-ol differ only by CH2 steps: boiling points climb 65 -> 78 -> 97 C while every member burns, oxidises and esterifies like an alcohol — gradation in physics, uniformity in chemistry.

Summary

Three words organise the families. An alkyl group (R–, CₙH₂ₙ₊₁) is an alkane minus one hydrogen — methyl, ethyl, propyl — the monovalent skeleton piece. A functional group (–OH, –CHO, >C=O, –COOH, –COOR, –X, –NH₂) is the reactive patch that decides the family's chemistry, since the C–C/C–H skeleton is inert. A homologous series is the family ladder: same functional group, one general formula (alkanols CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH), successive members differing by CH₂ (14 u) — giving a gradual rise in physical properties (methanol 65 °C → ethanol 78 °C → propan-1-ol 97 °C) with uniform chemical behaviour and similar preparations.
