  • Postulates of Bohr's atomic model and its application

    1. Topic Introduction

    Bohr's atomic model (1913) was the first quantum model of the atom. It resolved Rutherford's stability crisis by proposing that electrons occupy discrete, quantized orbits where they do not radiate energy. The model successfully explained the hydrogen spectrum and laid the foundation for quantum mechanics.

    Why it matters. Bohr's model introduced the revolutionary concept of quantization — that energy, angular momentum, and orbits are not continuous but come in discrete packets. Every subsequent development in atomic physics (quantum numbers, orbitals, Schrödinger equation) builds on Bohr's insight that classical physics fails at the atomic scale.

    What you will learn: (i) Bohr's four postulates and their physical meaning; (ii) derivation of radius, velocity, energy formulas for hydrogen-like ions; (iii) application to spectral lines and series; (iv) successes and limitations of the model.

    Learning outcomes. You can: state all four postulates with their physical significance; derive rₙ, vₙ, Eₙ for hydrogen-like ions; calculate wavelengths for spectral transitions; explain why the model works for H/He⁺/Li²⁺ but fails for multi-electron atoms.

  • 2. Fundamental Conceptual Understanding

    What Bohr's model is

    A semi-classical model where electrons move in circular orbits around the nucleus, but only certain orbits are allowed — those where the angular momentum is an integer multiple of h/2π. In these stationary states, electrons do not radiate energy.

    Why it exists

    Rutherford's model predicted atoms should collapse in ~10⁻¹¹ s (orbiting electrons radiate per Maxwell). Bohr introduced the radical postulate that electrons in certain orbits are stable and do not radiate. Radiation occurs only during jumps between orbits.

    How it works

    Electrons orbit in discrete stationary states (n = 1, 2, 3...). Energy is quantized: Eₙ = -13.6 Z²/n² eV. When an electron jumps from nᵢ to n_f, it emits/absorbs a photon of energy ΔE = hν = Eᵢ - E_f.

    Where it applies

    Only hydrogen-like systems (single electron): H, He⁺, Li²⁺, Be³⁺, etc. Fails for multi-electron atoms due to electron-electron repulsion and screening.

    What its components are

    Postulates (stationary orbits, quantized angular momentum, photon emission/absorption, frequency condition), quantization formulas (rₙ, vₙ, Eₙ), spectral series formulas.

    How components interact

    Quantized angular momentum → discrete orbits → discrete energy levels → discrete spectral lines. The frequency condition (ν = ΔE/h) connects energy jumps to photon wavelength.

    What makes it biologically important

    Atomic spectroscopy (astronomy, plasma diagnostics), laser physics (population inversion between quantized levels), quantum biology (photosynthesis energy levels), MRI (nuclear spin transitions — analogous quantization).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Bohr's Four Postulates (stated in order)

    1. Postulate 1 — Stationary states: Electrons move in certain discrete circular orbits around the nucleus. In these stationary states, electrons do not radiate energy despite being accelerated. Each orbit corresponds to a definite total energy.
    1. Postulate 2 — Quantization of angular momentum: The angular momentum of the electron in a stationary state is an integral multiple of h/2π:

    L = mvr = n·h/2π (n = 1, 2, 3, ...) where n = principal quantum number.

    1. Postulate 3 — Frequency condition: Radiation is emitted or absorbed only when an electron jumps between stationary states. The photon frequency ν satisfies:

    hν = Eᵢ - E_f where Eᵢ and E_f are the initial and final state energies.

    1. Postulate 4 — Correspondence principle: For large quantum numbers (n → ∞), the quantum mechanical predictions approach the classical results. The frequency of revolution equals the radiation frequency.

    4.4 Worked examples

    Example 1: Radius of 2nd orbit of He⁺ (Z=2, n=2) r₂ = 4·a₀/2 = 2a₀ = 1.058 Å

    Example 2: Energy of n=3 state in Li²⁺ (Z=3, n=3) E₃ = -13.6 × 9 / 9 = -13.6 eV

    Example 3: Wavelength of 4→2 transition in hydrogen (Balmer β) 1/λ = R(1/2² - 1/4²) = R(1/4 - 1/16) = 3R/16 λ = 16/(3R) = 486.1 nm

    Example 4: Ionization energy of He⁺ IE = 13.6 × 2² = 54.4 eV

    4.7 Worked numericals

    1. Radius of 2nd Bohr orbit of Li²⁺ (Z=3, n=2): r₂ = n²a₀/Z = 4×0.529/3 = 0.705 Å
    2. Velocity of electron in 3rd orbit of Be³⁺ (Z=4, n=3): v₃ = Z·v₁/n = 4×2.18×10⁶/3 = 2.91×10⁶ m/s
    3. Energy of n=4 state in He⁺: E₄ = -13.6×4/16 = -3.4 eV

    5. Ionization energy of Li²⁺: IE = 13.6×9 = 122.4 eV

  • 6. Chemical Processes and Mechanisms

    Process: Electron transition and photon emission/absorption

    1. Definition: Electron jumps between quantized energy levels, emitting or absorbing a photon.
    2. Location: Atoms in gas discharge tubes, stellar atmospheres, flames, laboratory spectrometers.
    3. Requirements: Energy input (electric discharge, heat, photon absorption) to excite electrons.
    4. Components: Atom with quantized levels, energy source, spectrometer to detect photons.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Ground state electron absorbs energy; (ii) Electron jumps to higher level (nᵢ > n_f); (iii) Electron is unstable in excited state; (iv) Electron drops to lower level (n_f < nᵢ); (v) Photon emitted with hν = Eᵢ - E_f.
    6. Result: Emission line at specific wavelength; absorption line if white light passes through cool gas.
    7. Significance: Every spectral line is a fingerprint of the atom; basis of all spectroscopy.

    Process: Bohr model derivation of energy levels

    1. Definition: Deriving quantized energy levels from classical mechanics + quantization postulate.
    2. Location: Theoretical physics / chemistry textbook derivation.
    3. Requirements: Coulomb's law, Newton's 2nd law, Bohr's quantization postulate.
    4. Components: Coulomb force, centripetal force, angular momentum quantization, energy conservation.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Coulomb force = centripetal force: kZe²/r² = mv²/r; (ii) Angular momentum quantization: mvr = nħ; (iii) Solve for rₙ = n²a₀/Z; (iv) Solve for vₙ = Ze²/2ε₀nh; (v) Total energy E = KE + PE = ½mv² - kZe²/r = -kZe²/2r = -13.6 Z²/n² eV.
    6. Result: Quantized energy levels Eₙ = -13.6 Z²/n² eV.
    7. Significance: First successful prediction of atomic energy levels; foundation for all atomic physics.

  • 10. Topic Mastery Check

    Important definitions

    1. Stationary state — allowed electron orbit where electron doesn't radiate; definite energy.
    2. Quantized angular momentum — mvr = n·h/2π; only discrete orbits allowed.
    3. Frequency condition — hν = Eᵢ - E_f; photon energy = energy difference between states.
    4. Correspondence principle — quantum predictions → classical for large n.
    5. Bohr radius — a₀ = 0.529 Å; n=1 radius for hydrogen.
    6. Rydberg constant — R = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹; 1/λ = RZ²(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²).
    7. Ground state — n=1, lowest energy (E₁ = -13.6Z² eV).
    8. Ionization energy — energy to reach n=∞; 13.6Z² eV for hydrogen-like.

    Key concepts to remember

    • Bohr's 4 postulates: stationary states, quantized L, frequency condition, correspondence.
    • Formulas: rₙ = n²a₀/Z, vₙ = Zv₁/n, Eₙ = -13.6Z²/n² eV.
    • Spectral series: Lyman (n_f=1, UV), Balmer (n_f=2, visible), Paschen (n_f=3, IR).
    • Works only for hydrogen-like (single electron) systems.
    • Fails for multi-electron atoms, fine structure, Zeeman/Stark effects.

    Important classifications

    • Spectral series: Lyman (UV), Balmer (visible), Paschen (IR), Brackett (IR), Pfund (far IR).
    • Hydrogen-like systems: H, He⁺, Li²⁺, Be³⁺... (all single-electron).
    • Transitions: Emission (electron drops), Absorption (electron rises).

    Common conceptual confusions

    • Bohr's model works for ALL atoms — NO, only hydrogen-like (single electron).
    • Electron moves in a definite path like a planet — NO, it's a probability cloud (orbitals).
    • Angular momentum is nħ for all quantum states — NO, QM gives √[l(l+1)]ħ.
    • Bohr's orbits are the same as orbitals — NO, orbitals are probability clouds.
    • All spectral lines are equally intense — NO, intensities depend on transition probabilities.
    • Rydberg formula works for all atoms — NO, only hydrogen-like.

    Answers

    1. Postulates: (1) Stationary states; (2) mvr = nħ; (3) hν = Eᵢ - E_f; (4) correspondence n→∞.
    2. Coulomb = centripetal: kZe²/r² = mv²/r; with mvr = nħ → rₙ = n²a₀/Z.
    3. Negative = bound state; zero = free electron at rest; positive = unbound (not possible for ground state).
    4. r₂(He⁺) = 4a₀/2 = 2a₀ = 1.058 Å.
    5. 3→2 transition: 1/λ = R(1/4 - 1/9) = 5R/36 → λ = 656.3 nm.
    6. Multi-electron repulsion, screening; Bohr assumes single electron.
    7. For n→∞, quantum frequency = classical orbital frequency.
    8. 5×4/2 = 10 lines.
    9. Eₙ = -kZe²/2r; KE = ½mv² = kZe²/2r = -E; PE = -kZe²/r = 2E.
    10. ΔE = 13.6(1/4 - 1/16) = 2.55 eV → λ = 1240/2.55 = 486.1 nm.
    11. He⁺: 1/λ = 4R(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²); H: 1/λ = R(1/2² - 1/nᵢ²); equate: n_f(He⁺) = 2, n_f(H) = 2 etc.
    12. r₂ = 2a₀ = 1.058 Å; v₂ = 2×v₁/2 = v₁ = 2.18×10⁶ m/s.
    13. v₁(H) = 2.18×10⁶ m/s; v₁(He⁺) = 2×2.18×10⁶ = 4.36×10⁶ m/s → ratio 1:2.
    14. v₃(Be³⁺) = 4×2.18×10⁶/3 = 2.91×10⁶ m/s.
    15. n=6 → 6×5/2 = 15 lines.
    16. IE(Li²⁺) = 13.6×3² = 122.4 eV.

    PYQ Solutions

    Q: State the postulates of Bohr's model and derive the expression for radius of nth orbit. (3 marks) Answer: Postulates: (1) Stationary states — electrons orbit without radiating; (2) mvr = n·h/2π; (3) hν = Eᵢ - E_f; (4) Correspondence n→∞. Derivation: kZe²/r² = mv²/r (Coulomb = centripetal) + mvr = nħ → rₙ = n²·4πε₀ħ²/me²Z = n²a₀/Z. Keywords: stationary states, mvr = nħ, hν = ΔE, n²a₀/Z.

    Q: Derive En = -13.6 Z²/n² eV and calculate ionisation energy of He⁺. (3 marks) Answer: From Coulomb: mv²/r = kZe²/r² → v² = kZe²/mr. From mvr = nħ → r = n²ħ²/kme²Z. Total E = KE + PE = ½mv² - kZe²/r = -kZe²/2r. Substitute r: Eₙ = -mk²Z²e⁴/2ħ²n² = -13.6 Z²/n² eV. IE(He⁺) = 13.6 × 2² = 54.4 eV. Keywords: kZe²/r² = mv²/r, mvr = nħ, E = -kZe²/2r, IE = 54.4 eV.

    Q: Show that KE = -E and PE = 2E for an electron in a Bohr orbit. (2 marks) Answer: E = KE + PE; virial theorem for 1/r potential: KE = -½PE. So KE = -E, PE = 2E. Or directly: KE = kZe²/2r, PE = -kZe²/r, E = -kZe²/2r → KE = -E, PE = 2E. Keywords: virial theorem, KE = -E, PE = 2E.

    Q: Calculate radius and velocity of electron in second orbit of hydrogen. (3 marks) Answer: n=2: r₂ = 4a₀ = 4×0.529 = 2.116 Å. v₂ = v₁/2 = 2.18×10⁶/2 = 1.09×10⁶ m/s. Keywords: r₂ = 4a₀ = 2.116 Å, v₂ = v₁/2 = 1.09×10⁶ m/s.

    Q: Explain, using Bohr's postulates, why hydrogen atom is stable and why its spectrum is a line spectrum. (5 marks) Answer: Stability: Postulate 1 — electron in stationary state doesn't radiate; energy is constant. Line spectrum: Postulate 3 — electron jumps between discrete states; photon energy hν = Eᵢ - E_f. Since energy levels are discrete (Eₙ = -13.6/n²), ΔE is discrete → line spectrum. Keywords: stationary states, no radiation, discrete levels, hν = Eᵢ - E_f, discrete photons.

    Q: An electron falls from n=4 to n=2 in hydrogen; find wavelength of emitted photon. (3 marks) Answer: ΔE = 13.6(1/2² - 1/4²) = 13.6(1/4 - 1/16) = 13.6×3/16 = 2.55 eV. λ = hc/ΔE = 1240/2.55 = 486.1 nm (Balmer β). Keywords: ΔE = 2.55 eV, λ = 486.1 nm, Balmer β.

    Q: A line of He⁺ has same wavelength as Balmer line of H; relate quantum numbers using Z² factor. (3 marks) Answer: For H: 1/λ = R(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²). For He⁺ (Z=2): 1/λ = 4R(1/n_f'² - 1/nᵢ'²). Equate: 1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ² = 4(1/n_f'² - 1/nᵢ'²). For Balmer n_f=2: 1/4 - 1/nᵢ² = 4(1/n_f'² - 1/nᵢ'²). e.g. n_f'=1, nᵢ'=2 gives same λ as H n_f=2, nᵢ=4. Keywords: 1/λ = RZ²(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²), Z=2 for He⁺, equate wavelengths.

Confusion

  • ❌ Bohr's model works for all atoms. ✅ Only for hydrogen-like (single electron) systems: H, He⁺, Li²⁺, Be³⁺...
  • ❌ Bohr's orbits are the same as orbitals. ✅ Orbits are definite paths; orbitals are probability clouds (ψ²).
  • ❌ Angular momentum is nħ for all quantum states. ✅ Quantum mechanics gives √[l(l+1)]ħ; only Bohr's circular orbits give nħ.
  • ❌ Bohr's model explains fine structure and Zeeman splitting. ✅ It cannot; needs spin and magnetic quantum numbers from QM.
  • ❌ The electron in a Bohr orbit has a definite position and velocity. ✅ Heisenberg's principle forbids this; QM uses orbitals (ψ²).
  • ❌ Rydberg formula works for all elements. ✅ Only for hydrogen-like systems; multi-electron atoms have complex spectra.

Bounds

  • Covers only: Bohr's postulates, derived formulas, hydrogen-like applications, spectral series (within Atomic Structure).
  • Out of scope: detailed quantum mechanical model, spin-orbit coupling, multi-electron atoms — later topics.
  • State the limit/condition where a rule stops being valid before applying it.

Universal Facts

  • Bohr's postulates: stationary states; mvr = nħ; hν = Eᵢ - E_f; correspondence n→∞.
  • Radius: rₙ = n²a₀/Z; Velocity: vₙ = Zv₁/n; Energy: Eₙ = -13.6Z²/n² eV.
  • Rydberg formula: 1/λ = RZ²(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²); R = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹.
  • Lyman (n_f=1, UV), Balmer (n_f=2, visible), Paschen (n_f=3, IR).
  • Works only for hydrogen-like ions (H, He⁺, Li²⁺...); fails for multi-electron atoms.

Examples

  • Hydrogen n=2: r=2.116 Å, v=1.09×10⁶ m/s, E=-3.4 eV.
  • He⁺ n=1: r=0.265 Å, v=4.36×10⁶ m/s, E=-54.4 eV.
  • Balmer series (visible): 3→2 = 656.3 nm, 4→2 = 486.1 nm, 5→2 = 434.0 nm.
  • Ionization energies: H 13.6 eV, He⁺ 54.4 eV, Li²⁺ 122.4 eV.

Formulas

  • mvr = n·h/2π (quantized angular momentum)
  • rₙ = n²a₀/Z where a₀ = 4πε₀ħ²/me² = 0.529 Å
  • vₙ = Z·e²/2ε₀nh = Z·v₁/n where v₁ = 2.18×10⁶ m/s
  • Eₙ = -13.6·Z²/n² eV (total energy)
  • 1/λ = R·Z²(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²) where R = 1.097×10⁷ m⁻¹
  • IE = 13.6·Z² eV (ionization energy)
  • KE = -E; PE = 2E (virial theorem for 1/r potential)
  • Number of spectral lines from de-excitation n → 1: n(n-1)/2

Numericals

  • Hydrogen n=2: r=2.116 Å, v=1.09×10⁶ m/s, E=-3.4 eV.
  • He⁺ n=1: r=0.265 Å, v=4.36×10⁶ m/s, E=-54.4 eV; IE=54.4 eV.
  • Li²⁺ n=1: IE=122.4 eV.
  • Balmer series: 3→2 = 656.3 nm, 4→2 = 486.1 nm, 5→2 = 434.0 nm, limit 364.6 nm.
  • Lyman limit = 91.2 nm; Paschen limit = 820.4 nm; Brackett limit = 1458 nm.
  • n=6 de-excitation: 6×5/2 = 15 spectral lines.
  • He⁺ 2→1: ΔE = 40.8 eV; λ = 30.4 nm (Lyman-α for Z=2).

Key Points

  • Bohr's 4 postulates: stationary states; mvr = nħ; hν = Eᵢ - E_f; correspondence n→∞.
  • Formulas: rₙ = n²a₀/Z, vₙ = Zv₁/n, Eₙ = -13.6Z²/n² eV.
  • Rydberg: 1/λ = RZ²(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²); Lyman (UV), Balmer (vis), Paschen (IR).
  • Works for H, He⁺, Li²⁺...; fails for multi-e⁻ atoms, fine structure, Zeeman/Stark.
  • KE = -E, PE = 2E; IE = 13.6Z² eV.

Summary

Bohr's model (1913) introduced quantized orbits (mvr = nħ) where electrons don't radiate. Derived formulas: rₙ = n²a₀/Z, vₙ = Zv₁/n, Eₙ = -13.6Z²/n² eV. Explains hydrogen spectrum via 1/λ = RZ²(1/n_f² - 1/nᵢ²). Works for H-like ions only; fails for multi-electron atoms, fine structure, Zeeman/Stark. Correspondence principle: n→∞ recovers classical physics. Figures: Bohr orbits, energy level diagram, spectral series regions.
