Notes

  • Alpha-Particle Scattering Experiment: Rutherford (1911) bombarded thin gold foil (~0.00004 cm thick) with fast alpha particles from a radioactive source. A fluorescent zinc sulphide screen detected scattered particles. This experiment fundamentally changed our understanding of atomic structure.
  • Key Observations: (1) Most alpha particles passed straight through undeflected — atom is mostly empty space. (2) Some were deflected at small angles — positive charge is concentrated. (3) About 1 in 8000 bounced back (>90°) — dense, massive nucleus exists. Rutherford said it was 'as if you fired a 15-inch shell at tissue paper and it came back'.
  • Rutherford's Nuclear Model: Atom consists of a tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus (~10⁻¹⁵ m radius) containing nearly all the mass. Electrons orbit the nucleus at large distances (~10⁻¹⁰ m). The atom is mostly empty space. Nuclear charge = +Ze where Z is atomic number.
  • Nuclear Size: Nuclear radius R ≈ R₀A^(1/3) where R₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ m (1.2 fm) and A is mass number. Nucleus is ~10⁵ times smaller than the atom. If atom were a football stadium, nucleus would be a marble at center.
  • Distance of Closest Approach: For head-on collision, d = (1/4πε₀)(2Ze²/K) where K is alpha particle kinetic energy. This gives upper limit of nuclear size. For gold (Z=79) with 5.5 MeV alpha: d ≈ 4.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m.
  • Limitation 1 — Atomic Stability: According to Maxwell's electromagnetic theory, an accelerating charged particle radiates energy. An orbiting electron should continuously lose energy and spiral into the nucleus in ~10⁻¹¹ s. Rutherford's model cannot explain why atoms are stable.
  • Limitation 2 — Line Spectra: Rutherford's model predicts continuous emission spectrum (electron spiraling inward emits all frequencies). But hydrogen shows discrete line spectrum (Balmer, Lyman series). The model cannot explain quantized energy levels or spectral lines.
  • Limitation 3 — Chemical Behavior: The model provides no explanation for chemical bonding, valency, or periodic properties. It says nothing about electron arrangement or energy distribution that determines reactivity.
  • Impact Parameter: b = perpendicular distance of initial velocity vector from nucleus center. Smaller b → larger scattering angle. Relationship: b ∝ cot(θ/2). Head-on collision has b = 0, θ = 180°.
  • Transition to Bohr Model: Rutherford's limitations directly motivated Bohr (1913) to propose quantized orbits where electrons do not radiate in stationary states, explaining both stability and line spectra.

Specific Conditions

  • Thin Foil Required: Gold foil must be extremely thin (~400 atoms thick) so alpha particles interact with at most one nucleus. Thick foils cause multiple scattering, invalidating single-collision analysis.
  • Point Charge Approximation: Alpha particle and nucleus treated as point charges. Valid when distance >> nuclear/alpha sizes. Breaks down at very close approach where nuclear forces matter.
  • Classical Mechanics Applied: Rutherford used Newtonian mechanics and Coulomb's law. Quantum effects ignored — valid for heavy alpha particles but not for electron behavior.
  • Vacuum Conditions: Experiment performed in vacuum to prevent alpha particles from being absorbed or scattered by air molecules before reaching the foil.

Key Points

  • Most alpha particles passed through → atom is mostly empty space.
  • Few bounced back → tiny, dense, positively charged nucleus.
  • Nuclear radius ~10⁻¹⁵ m; atomic radius ~10⁻¹⁰ m (factor of 10⁵).
  • Model explains scattering but fails on stability and spectra.
  • Electron orbital motion should cause energy loss → collapse (doesn't happen).
  • Discrete spectra require quantized energy levels (Bohr's fix).

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Rutherford's alpha scattering experiment established the nuclear model of the atom with a dense central nucleus.
  • Statement 2: The atom is mostly empty space — evidenced by most alpha particles passing through undeflected.
  • Statement 3: Backscattering of alpha particles proved existence of a concentrated positive charge in the nucleus.
  • Statement 4: Rutherford's model fails to explain atomic stability because orbiting electrons should radiate energy per classical electrodynamics.
  • Statement 5: The model cannot account for discrete atomic spectra, necessitating quantum mechanical treatment by Bohr.

Important Notes

  • Geiger and Marsden performed the actual experiment under Rutherford's direction (1909). Rutherford interpreted results theoretically in 1911.
  • The term 'nucleus' was coined by Rutherford in 1912. Before this, Thomson's plum pudding model dominated.
  • Rutherford scattering formula N(θ) ∝ 1/sin⁴(θ/2) was verified experimentally, confirming Coulomb interaction.
  • NEB frequently asks to describe the experiment with diagram, state observations, and list limitations.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Most passed → empty space. Few deflected → concentrated charge. Very few back → massive nucleus.
  • Stability problem: orbiting e⁻ should radiate → spiral in → atom collapses (doesn't happen).
  • Spectra problem: predicts continuous, observes discrete lines.
  • d ∝ 1/K: higher energy alpha gets closer to nucleus.
  • R = R₀A^(1/3): nuclear size grows slowly with mass number.

Examples

  • Alpha particle (K = 5.5 MeV) head-on with gold nucleus (Z = 79): d = (9×10⁹)(2×79×(1.6×10⁻¹⁹)²)/(5.5×10⁶×1.6×10⁻¹⁹) ≈ 4.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ m.
  • Gold nucleus radius (A = 197): R = 1.2 × 197^(1/3) ≈ 1.2 × 5.82 ≈ 7.0 fm = 7.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ m.
  • If atom scaled to 100 m diameter sphere, nucleus would be ~1 mm — demonstrating extreme emptiness.
  • Scattering at 90° requires impact parameter b such that cot(45°) = 1; at 180°, b = 0 (direct hit).

Universal Facts

  • Rutherford's experiment is considered one of the most important experiments in physics history, ranked alongside Michelson-Morley and Young's double slit.
  • The backscattering fraction (~1/8000) was so unexpected that Rutherford called it 'the most incredible event of my life'.
  • Chadwick discovered the neutron in 1932, completing the nuclear composition picture that Rutherford initiated.
  • Modern particle accelerators use the same scattering principle to probe sub-nuclear structure at femtometer scales.

Confusion

  • Thinking nucleus contains electrons: Nucleus contains only protons and neutrons. Electrons orbit outside.
  • Believing all alpha particles bounce back: Only ~1/8000 scatter >90°. Most pass through completely.
  • Confusing nuclear size with atomic size: Nucleus ~10⁻¹⁵ m, atom ~10⁻¹⁰ m — factor of 100,000 difference.
  • Assuming Rutherford explained electron orbits: He proposed orbits exist but couldn't explain their stability or energy quantization.
  • Mixing up Thomson and Rutherford models: Thomson = positive sphere with embedded electrons; Rutherford = tiny nucleus with orbiting electrons.
