Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Filling Rules That Build the Periodic Table

    Quantum numbers give every electron an address; three rules decide the order of occupation: the Aufbau principle (fill lowest energy first), Pauli's exclusion principle (two electrons maximum per orbital, opposite spins) and Hund's rule (degenerate orbitals fill singly with parallel spins first). Apply all three and the electron configurations of every element up to Z = 30 — and the structure of the periodic table — follow mechanically. NEB 2023 asked Aufbau directly (3m); the configurations themselves are permanent exam currency.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Aufbau principle (building-up)

    Electrons occupy the lowest-energy orbital available before entering higher ones. Energy order (the diagonal rule):

    1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p …

    • The (n + l) rule predicts the order: lower (n + l) fills first; on a tie, lower n fills first.
    • 4s: n + l = 4 + 0 = 4 — fills before 3d: n + l = 3 + 2 = 5.
    • The order is set by penetration: s electrons penetrate closer to the nucleus than p, d, f of the same shell, so 4s is stabilised below 3d.

    2.2 Pauli's exclusion principle

    No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of all four quantum numbers.

    • Consequence: an orbital holds at most 2 electrons, with opposite spins (↑↓).
    • Capacities: orbital 2, subshell 2(2l + 1), shell 2n².

    2.3 Hund's rule (of maximum multiplicity)

    For degenerate orbitals (same energy — the three p, five d, seven f):

    1. Electrons fill singly first, with parallel spins (↑ ↑ ↑).
    2. Pairing starts only after every orbital in the subshell is singly occupied.

    Reason: parallel spins in separate orbitals minimise repulsion and maximise exchange energy — half-filled and fully-filled subshells are extra stable (explains Cr: 3d⁵4s¹ and Cu: 3d¹⁰4s¹).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 NEB 2023 (3m, verified PYQ) — Explain Aufbau's principle with an example

    Statement: electrons enter the orbital of lowest energy first; higher orbitals are occupied only after all lower ones are filled. The energy order follows the (n + l) rule — lower (n + l) first, and on a tie the lower n first.

    Example: potassium (Z = 19) — after the argon core [Ar], the next electron enters 4s (n + l = 4) rather than 3d (n + l = 5): K = [Ar] 4s¹, not [Ar] 3d¹. Similarly Ca = [Ar] 4s², and only from scandium do the 3d orbitals begin to fill.

    4.2 Why 4s fills before 3d

    Penetration and shielding. A 4s electron penetrates through the inner shells close to the nucleus, feeling nearly the full nuclear charge; a 3d electron barely penetrates and is heavily shielded. The net energy of 4s is therefore lower than 3d despite the larger n — the (n + l) rule is the bookkeeping, penetration is the physics.

    4.3 The two famous exceptions

    • Cr (Z = 24): expected [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴, actual [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵ — a half-filled d subshell gains extra exchange energy (five parallel-spin electrons) and the s electron supplies it.
    • Cu (Z = 29): expected [Ar] 4s² 3d⁹, actual [Ar] 4s¹ 3d¹⁰ — a completely filled d subshell is even more stable.
    • Symmetry + exchange energy make half-filled and fully-filled subshells special — the same reason N (2p³) is more stable than O (2p⁴).

    4.4 Hund's rule in action — the p³ → p⁴ step

    Nitrogen's three 2p electrons occupy the three separate p orbitals with parallel spins (↑ ↑ ↑). Oxygen's fourth electron must pair (↑↓ ↑ ↑), paying pairing repulsion — which is why first-ionisation-energy data show the famous N > O dip in period 2.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Ionisation order vs filling order: electrons are removed from the outermost shell — Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶, not [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴. 4s fills first but also empties first.

  • Hund's rule scope: it applies only within a degenerate set (p³, d⁵…); it never overrides Aufbau's energy ordering across different subshells.
  • Exceptions are ground-state facts: Cr and Cu configurations apply to the neutral ground-state atoms; their ions follow the normal removal order (Cu⁺ = 3d¹⁰).

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m): Write the configuration of Fe (Z = 26) and of Fe³⁺; why is Fe³⁺ more stable than Fe²⁺? — Fe: [Ar] 4s² 3d⁶; Fe³⁺: [Ar] 3d⁵ (4s² removed first, then one 3d). Fe³⁺ has an exactly half-filled 3d⁵ — maximum exchange energy and spherical symmetry — so it is extra stable.

  • NEB-style (2m): State Hund's rule and illustrate with nitrogen. — Degenerate orbitals are occupied singly with parallel spins before any pairing. N (Z = 7): 1s² 2s² 2p³ → the three 2p electrons sit in three separate p orbitals as ↑ ↑ ↑.
  • NEB-style (2m): Predict the ground-state configuration of Cr (Z = 24) and explain the anomaly. — [Ar] 4s¹ 3d⁵ instead of [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴: the half-filled 3d⁵ gains maximum exchange energy, and promoting one 4s electron to 3d costs little because 4s and 3d are nearly degenerate.

Confusion

  • 4s before 3d — but also 4s out first: filling order and removal order are different questions. Ions strip the highest-n shell first.
  • Pairing needs a partner orbital, not the same orbital: Hund's rule forbids pairing while a degenerate orbital is still empty — O (2p⁴) pairs only because all three p orbitals already hold one electron.

Summary

Three rules build every ground-state configuration: Aufbau fills the lowest-energy orbital first (order 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p…, predicted by the (n + l) rule — hence K = [Ar] 4s¹, NEB 2023's example); Pauli caps each orbital at two opposite-spin electrons by forbidding any shared set of four quantum numbers; Hund fills degenerate orbitals singly with parallel spins before pairing (N: 2p ↑ ↑ ↑). Exchange-energy stability bends the rules at Cr ([Ar] 4s¹3d⁵) and Cu ([Ar] 4s¹3d¹⁰), and makes Fe³⁺ ([Ar] 3d⁵) more stable than Fe²⁺. For ions, removal follows "last in, first out": 4s empties before 3d. Master the three rules and the configurations up to Z = 30 — and the shape of the periodic table itself — follow mechanically.

  • Diagonal chant: "1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p 5s 4d 5p." (n + l) check for any disputed pair. Ions: "last in, first out" — strip the highest shell.

Exam Notes

  • Write configurations in full for Z ≤ 18; core notation [Ar] is fine beyond. For ions, show the neutral configuration first, then strike out — the marking scheme follows the steps.

Pauli's exclusion principle limits an orbital to:

  • 2 electrons of opposite spin

  • 4 electrons

B

All four quantum numbers cannot repeat.

Singly, parallel spins, then pair.
