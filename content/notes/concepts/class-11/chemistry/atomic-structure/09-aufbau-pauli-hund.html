Notes

  • 1. Meaning

  • These three rules distribute electrons among the orbitals of an atom in its ground state: Aufbau fixes the order of filling, Pauli fixes the capacity of an orbital, and Hund fixes how degenerate orbitals are filled.

  • 2. Aufbau principle and the (n + l) rule

  • Electrons fill the available orbital of lowest energy first. Order is given by the Madelung (n + l) rule: smaller (n + l) first; for equal (n + l), smaller n first. Thus 4s (n + l = 4) fills before 3d (n + l = 5), giving 1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p < 5s < 4d < 5p < 6s < 4f < 5d < 6p < 7s < 5f < 6d < 7p.

  • 3. Pauli's exclusion principle

  • No two electrons in an atom can have all four quantum numbers identical; equivalently an orbital holds at most two electrons with opposite spins (↑↓). Consequences: subshell capacities 2, 6, 10, 14; shell capacity 2n².

  • 4. Hund's rule of maximum multiplicity

  • Electrons enter degenerate orbitals (same n and l, e.g. the three 2p) singly with parallel spins before pairing; pairing starts only after every degenerate orbital has one electron. Reason: pairing costs Coulomb repulsion energy while parallel spins gain exchange energy. Example: N 2p³ = ↑ ↑ ↑ (three unpaired); O 2p⁴ = ↑↓ ↑ ↑ (two unpaired).

  • 5. Extra stability of half-filled and fully-filled subshells

  • d⁵, d¹⁰, p³ and p⁶ arrangements gain exchange energy and symmetry, which explains the anomalies at Z = 24 and 29: Cr = [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ (not 3d⁴4s²) and Cu = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ (not 3d⁹4s²). Similar anomalies occur at Nb, Mo, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag, Pt and Au.

  • 6. Electronic configurations, Z = 1–30 (noble-gas shorthand)

  • H 1s¹ · He 1s² · Li [He]2s¹ · Be [He]2s² · B [He]2s²2p¹ · C 2p² · N 2p³ · O 2p⁴ · F 2p⁵ · Ne 2p⁶ · Na [Ne]3s¹ · Mg 3s² · Al 3s²3p¹ · Si 3p² · P 3p³ · S 3p⁴ · Cl 3p⁵ · Ar 3p⁶ · K [Ar]4s¹ · Ca 4s² · Sc 3d¹4s² · Ti 3d²4s² · V 3d³4s² · Cr 3d⁵4s¹ · Mn 3d⁵4s² · Fe 3d⁶4s² · Co 3d⁷4s² · Ni 3d⁸4s² · Cu 3d¹⁰4s¹ · Zn 3d¹⁰4s².

  • 7. Configurations of ions

  • Electrons are removed from the highest-n (outermost) shell first even though 4s filled before 3d: Fe ([Ar]3d⁶4s²) → Fe²⁺ ([Ar]3d⁶) → Fe³⁺ ([Ar]3d⁵, half-filled and extra stable); Cu ([Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹) → Cu⁺ ([Ar]3d¹⁰); Mn → Mn²⁺ ([Ar]3d⁵). This explains why Fe³⁺ and Mn²⁺ are the commonest oxidation states of those metals.

  • 8. Applications

  • • Predicts valency, oxidation states and magnetic behaviour (Fe³⁺: 5 unpaired → paramagnetic; Zn²⁺: 3d¹⁰ → diamagnetic; Cu²⁺: 1 unpaired) and the colours of transition-metal ions.
  • • Explains the structure of the periodic table — s, p, d, f blocks and period lengths 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32 — and is the basis of hybridisation and complex chemistry.

  • 9. Limitations

  • The (n + l) rule is only approximate — the many anomalous configurations of Cr, Cu, Nb, Mo, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag, Pt, Au and of the lanthanides/actinides show that electron repulsion, exchange energy and relativistic effects decide the real order; the orbital energy order changes with Z and inside ions or ligand fields; Hund's rule has exceptions in excited states; Aufbau fails for heavy elements; and none of the rules predicts reactivity or reaction rates.

Confusion

  • ❌ 4s is always lower in energy than 3d. ✅ It fills first in neutral atoms, but in ions the 4s electrons are removed first (Fe²⁺ = 3d⁶).
  • ❌ Pauli's principle forbids two electrons in one orbital. ✅ They may share an orbital if their spins are opposite (different mₛ).
  • ❌ Hund's rule says electrons pair as soon as possible. ✅ Pairing begins only after every degenerate orbital holds one electron.
  • ❌ Cr is [Ar]3d⁴4s². ✅ It is [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ because the half-filled d subshell wins on exchange energy.

  • Fe³⁺ = [Ar]3d⁵ (5 unpaired); Zn²⁺ = [Ar]3d¹⁰ (0 unpaired).

Examples

  • Problem: Configuration and magnetic behaviour of Cu²⁺. Solution: Cu = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹ → Cu²⁺ = [Ar]3d⁹ → one unpaired electron → paramagnetic, μ ≈ 1.73 BM.
  • Problem: How many Zn electrons have l = 2? Solution: Zn = [Ar]3d¹⁰4s² — the 3d subshell (l = 2) holds 10 electrons.

Important Notes

  • For ions always remove electrons from the highest-n shell first (4s before 3d) — a standard exam trap.
  • Explain the Cr/Cu anomalies with exchange energy and half-filled stability, not as arbitrary exceptions.
