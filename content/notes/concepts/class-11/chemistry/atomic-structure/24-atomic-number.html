Notes

  • Focus of this tab

  • The atomic number Z: its definition, the X-ray evidence that made it the true identity of an element, and its uses. It belongs logically with Rutherford's model because Z is the nuclear charge his experiment revealed.

  • Definition and meaning

  • The atomic number Z is the number of protons in the nucleus — equivalently the nuclear charge in units of +e, or the number of electrons in the neutral atom. It is written as a left subscript (₁₁Na). In 1920 Rutherford named the nuclear charge 'atomic number'; it is now the defining property of an element.

  • Moseley's X-ray evidence (1913)

  • Henry Moseley bombarded metal targets with fast cathode rays and measured the characteristic X-ray frequencies: ν​=a(Z−b). Plotting √ν against Z gives a straight line, but √ν against atomic mass does not — proving that Z, not atomic mass, is the fundamental quantity. This (i) corrected the anomalous pairs (Ar/K, Co/Ni, Te/I), (ii) gave isotopes one place in the table, (iii) fixed hydrogen's position, and (iv) predicted missing elements by gaps in the line.

  • Uses of Z

  • • Identifies the element and fixes its position in the modern periodic table (periods by shell filling, groups by valence configuration).
  • • Gives the electron count of a neutral atom (e⁻ = Z) and the neutron count (n = A − Z).
  • • Enters Bohr-type formulas: rₙ ∝ n²/Z, Eₙ ∝ −Z²/n², ν of spectral lines ∝ Z² (used for He⁺, Li²⁺).
  • • In medicine/engineering Z governs X-ray absorption (bone vs tissue imaging) and shielding choices (lead, Z = 82).

  • Relation to the other identifiers

  • Mass number A = Z + N (whole number); atomic mass is the weighted average over isotopes (e.g. Cl = 35.5 u from 75% ³⁵Cl + 25% ³⁷Cl). Only Z is unique to a chemical element — that is why the modern periodic law is stated in terms of Z.

Confusion

  • ❌ Atomic number is the number of neutrons. ✅ It is the number of protons (nuclear charge); neutrons = A − Z.
  • ❌ Elements are defined by atomic mass. ✅ Moseley showed the identity is Z; mass varies with isotopic composition.
  • ❌ An ion has a different atomic number from its atom. ✅ Z never changes in chemistry — only the electron count does (Na and Na⁺ both Z = 11).

Practice

  • Define atomic number and describe Moseley's experiment that established it.
  • Write Moseley's law and explain how it corrected Mendeleev's anomalous pairs.
  • An atom has Z = 20 and A = 40; find p, n, e and write its mass number.
  • Why does the plot of √ν versus atomic mass fail to be a straight line?

Universal Facts

  • Moseley died at Gallipoli in 1915 at 27, shortly after establishing atomic number.
  • The 'missing' Z = 43 (technetium) and 61 (promethium) were later found — their X-ray lines had been predicted by gaps.
  • Beryllium's low Z = 4 makes it the window material of X-ray tubes (low absorption).

Examples

  • Problem: An element has A = 23 and 12 neutrons. Identify it. Solution: Z = 23 − 12 = 11 → sodium (Na); in the neutral atom it has 11 electrons.

Important Notes

  • Always connect Z to the nuclear charge Rutherford measured — the two ideas belong together.
  • Quote Moseley's law with the square root of frequency, not frequency itself.
