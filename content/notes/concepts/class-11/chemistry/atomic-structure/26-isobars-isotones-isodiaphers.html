Notes

  • Focus of this tab

  • The family of 'same/different' atom pairs that the nuclear model makes possible — definitions, examples and exam traps. These follow directly from the concepts of Z and A.

  • Definitions with examples

  • • Isotopes (same Z, different A): ¹H, ²H, ³H; ³⁵Cl/³⁷Cl; ¹²C/¹³C/¹⁴C; ²³⁵U/²³⁸U. Same chemical properties (same electronic configuration), different physical properties and different mass.
  • • Isobars (same A, different Z): ⁴⁰Ar (18 p) and ⁴⁰Ca (20 p); ¹⁴C and ¹⁴N. Different elements, same mass number — different chemical properties.
  • • Isotones (same neutron number N = A − Z): ³⁶S, ³⁷Cl, ³⁸Ar, ³⁹K, ⁴⁰Ca (all N = 20); ³H and ⁴Li (N = 2).
  • • Isodiaphers (same difference N − Z): ²³⁹U and ²³⁵Th (N − Z = 51); ²³⁵U and ²³¹Th.
  • • (Bonus) Isoelectronic species (same electron count): N³⁻, O²⁻, F⁻, Ne, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ — all 10 electrons.

  • Why they exist

  • Rutherford's nucleus separates identity (Z) from mass (A = Z + N). Once the neutron was found (1932), atoms of one element could differ in mass (isotopes) and atoms of different elements could share a mass number (isobars) — resolving the contradictions that Dalton's 'identical atoms' postulate created.

  • Chemical versus nuclear behaviour

  • Isotopes are chemically almost identical (the electrons decide chemistry) but differ in nuclear stability (¹⁴C decays, ¹²C does not) — the basis of carbon dating, radioisotope tracers (¹³¹I in thyroid studies, ⁶⁰Co in radiotherapy) and isotope separation by mass (gaseous diffusion of UF₆). Isobars, being different elements, are chemically completely different.

  • Average atomic mass (numerical pattern)

  • Average mass = Σ(fractional abundance × isotopic mass). Chlorine: 0.75 × 35 + 0.25 × 37 = 35.5 u; neon: 0.905 × 20 + 0.0027 × 21 + 0.0923 × 22 ≈ 20.18 u. The fractional pattern explains why most atomic masses are not whole numbers — Dalton's 'identical atoms in whole-number ratios' fails here.

Confusion

  • ❌ Isotopes have different chemical properties. ✅ Nearly identical chemically; they differ in mass and nuclear stability.
  • ❌ Isobars are atoms of the same element. ✅ Same A but different Z — different elements entirely.
  • ❌ Isotones are defined by equal protons. ✅ Equal neutron number (N = A − Z).
  • ❌ ¹²C and ¹⁴C belong to different elements. ✅ Both are carbon (Z = 6); ¹⁴C is simply radioactive.

Universal Facts

  • Carbon dating uses the ¹⁴C/¹²C ratio (half-life 5730 years).
  • ³⁵Cl/³⁷Cl in a 3 : 1 ratio gives the familiar 35.5 u average mass.
  • Radio-iodine ¹³¹I concentrates in the thyroid because it is chemically indistinguishable from ordinary iodine.

Examples

  • Problem: Boron is 20% ¹⁰B and 80% ¹¹B. Find its atomic mass. Solution: 0.20 × 10 + 0.80 × 11 = 10.8 u — matching the tabulated 10.81 u.
  • Problem: Show that ¹⁴C and ¹⁴N are isobars. Solution: ¹⁴C: Z = 6, N = 8, A = 14; ¹⁴N: Z = 7, N = 7, A = 14 — same A, different Z.

Important Notes

  • State the defining quantity first (Z, A, N, N − Z) — the four names are easy to mix up.
  • Connect isotopes back to Dalton's limitation: identical atoms of an element is the false postulate.
