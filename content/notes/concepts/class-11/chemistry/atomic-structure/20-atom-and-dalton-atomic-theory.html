Notes

  • Focus of this tab

  • The discovery chain that produced the nuclear atom — Dalton → electron → proton → neutron → Thomson → Rutherford — so that 'Rutherford's model and its limitations' can be answered with its historical context.

  • The particles, in order of discovery

  • • Electron (J. J. Thomson, 1897): cathode rays are deflected by E and B fields; e/m = 1.76 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹, identical for all cathode materials → universal negative particle.
  • • Proton (E. Goldstein's anode/canal rays, 1886, identified as a particle by Thomson 1907; named by Rutherford 1919–20): positive rays bend oppositely in fields; the lightest positive particle (from hydrogen) has charge +e and mass 1836 × electron mass. Rutherford produced it deliberately: ¹⁴N + ⁴He → ¹⁷O + ¹p (first nuclear transmutation).
  • • Neutron (James Chadwick, 1932): beryllium bombarded by α-particles emits a neutral, highly penetrating radiation: ⁹Be + ⁴He → ¹²C + ¹n; mass ≈ 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, slightly heavier than the proton, charge 0.

  • How each discovery reshaped the atom model

  • • Dalton's indivisible atom (1803) fell once electrons and protons emerged — atoms have internal structure.
  • • Thomson (1904) embedded electrons in diffuse positive charge — failed on α-scattering.
  • • Rutherford (1911) put the positive charge and mass in a tiny nucleus with electrons around it — failed on stability and line spectra.
  • • The neutron completed the nuclear picture: nuclei contain Z protons and A − Z neutrons, explaining isotopes (same Z, different N) that Dalton's 'identical atoms' postulate could not.

  • Particle data table (memorise)

  • Electron: charge −1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, mass 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg (0.000549 u, 1/1837 of H atom). Proton: +1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg (1.00728 u). Neutron: 0, 1.675 × 10⁻²⁷ kg (1.00867 u). Charge/mass ratio of the electron is the largest, which is why electron beams are easiest to deflect.

  • Why the neutron took so long

  • A neutral particle leaves no track in electric or magnetic fields and is not detected by ionisation chambers — it was first mistaken for γ-radiation. Chadwick's insight was to use the recoiling protons ejected from paraffin wax to measure its mass by momentum conservation.

Confusion

  • ❌ Goldstein discovered the proton in 1886. ✅ Goldstein discovered canal (anode) rays; the proton as the lightest positive particle was characterised later and named by Rutherford.
  • ❌ The neutron is lighter than the proton. ✅ It is slightly heavier (1.675 vs 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg), which is why free neutrons β-decay.
  • ❌ Electrons contribute most of the atomic mass. ✅ They contribute ≈0.05%; nearly all mass is nuclear.

Universal Facts

  • Chadwick's 1932 neutron discovery completed the particle trio and earned the 1935 Nobel Prize.
  • The proton is 1836 times heavier than the electron; the neutron is 1839 times heavier.
  • Cathode-ray e/m is independent of the gas and electrode material — electrons are in all matter.

Examples

  • Problem: An atom has Z = 17 and A = 35. Find p, n, e. Solution: protons = 17, electrons = 17 (neutral atom), neutrons = A − Z = 18 — the isotope ³⁵Cl.

Important Notes

  • Keep the discovery order strict: electron (1897) → proton (named 1919–20) → neutron (1932).
  • Mass number A = protons + neutrons; atomic number Z = protons = electrons in a neutral atom.
