  • Elementary idea of quantum mechanical model: de Broglie's wave equation

    1. Topic Introduction

    Louis de Broglie's 1924 hypothesis that matter has wave properties was the conceptual bridge between Bohr's quantized orbits and Schrödinger's wave mechanics. His equation λ = h/p — linking a particle's wavelength to its momentum — explained why Bohr's angular momentum quantization (mvr = nħ) works: the electron wave must form a standing wave around the orbit. The 1927 Davisson-Germer experiment confirmed matter waves by diffracting electrons off a nickel crystal.

    Why it matters. Wave-particle duality is the cornerstone of quantum mechanics. Without de Broglie's insight, Bohr's ad hoc quantization condition would remain unexplained; Schrödinger's wave equation would lack its physical motivation; electron microscopy (which uses matter waves) would not exist. The wave nature of matter explains why electrons in atoms occupy orbitals (probability clouds) rather than orbits.

    What you will learn: (i) de Broglie's wave equation and its derivation from photon equations; (ii) why wave nature is negligible for macroscopic objects; (iii) standing wave condition and Bohr's quantization; (iv) Davisson-Germer experiment as proof of matter waves; (v) applications: electron microscopy, LEED, neutron diffraction.

    Learning outcomes. You can: state and derive λ = h/p; calculate de Broglie wavelength for electrons, protons, macroscopic objects; explain why wave nature is unobservable for everyday objects; describe Davisson-Germer experiment and how it confirms λ = h/p; apply the standing wave condition to derive Bohr's quantization; apply matter wave concepts to electron microscopy.

  • 2. Fundamental Conceptual Understanding

    What de Broglie's hypothesis is

    In 1924, Louis de Broglie proposed that all matter has wave properties. Just as light (photons) exhibits wave-particle duality, material particles (electrons, protons, atoms, molecules) also have an associated wavelength:

    λ = h / p

    where λ = de Broglie wavelength, h = Planck's constant (6.626×10⁻³⁴ J·s), p = momentum = mv (for non-relativistic speeds).

    Why it exists

    Einstein's photon equations (E = hν, E = mc²) and Planck's quantum hypothesis (E = hν) suggested a deep symmetry: if light (energy) has particle properties, matter (particles) should have wave properties. De Broglie extended this symmetry: just as E = hν and p = E/c = h/λ for photons, matter should have λ = h/p.

    How it works

    A moving particle has momentum p = mv. Its associated matter wave has wavelength λ = h/mv. The wave represents the probability amplitude of finding the particle; its intensity (ψ²) gives the probability density. For bound particles (electrons in atoms), the wave must satisfy boundary conditions → standing waves → quantized energy levels.

    Where it applies

    • Electrons in atoms (standing waves → quantized orbits)
    • Electron diffraction (Davisson-Germer, Thomson)
    • Electron microscopy (λ ~ 0.01-0.1 nm for 100-1000 keV electrons)
    • Neutron diffraction (crystal structure determination)
    • Atom interferometry (Bose-Einstein condensates)
    • Quantum computing (matter wave qubits)

    What its components are

    Particle (mass m, velocity v), momentum (p = mv), wavelength (λ = h/p), wave function (ψ), probability density (|ψ|²), standing wave condition (nλ = 2πr for circular orbits).

    How components interact

    Momentum determines wavelength; wavelength must satisfy boundary conditions; standing wave condition quantizes the orbit; quantization explains atomic stability and spectra. The wave function ψ evolves by Schrödinger equation; |ψ|² gives probability density.

    What makes it biologically/physically important

    • Electron microscopy: λ ~ 0.002 nm (200 keV) → atomic resolution imaging of viruses, proteins, materials.
    • LEED (Low Energy Electron Diffraction): surface crystallography, catalysis research.
    • Neutron diffraction: hydrogen positions in proteins, magnetic structures.
    • Quantum biology: photosynthetic exciton transport may involve matter wave coherence.
    • STM/AFM: quantum tunneling (wave nature) enables atomic-scale imaging.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Derivation of de Broglie's equation

    From photon equations:

    • Photon energy: E = hν = hc/λ
    • Photon momentum: p = E/c = h/λ
    • Therefore: λ = h/p for photons

    De Broglie's extension (1924): If photons (particles of light) have λ = h/p, then material particles (with momentum p) should also have an associated wavelength: λ = h / p

    For non-relativistic particles (v << c): p = mv → λ = h / mv

    For relativistic particles: p = γmv → λ = h / γmv

    Derivation from Einstein/Planck relations: E = hν = hc/λ (photon) E = mc² (rest energy) + kinetic For photons: p = E/c = h/λ → λ = h/p De Broglie: this relation is universal — applies to all particles.

    4.2 Why wave nature is unobservable for macroscopic objects

    Example: Cricket ball (m = 0.15 kg, v = 30 m/s) p = 0.15 × 30 = 4.5 kg·m/s λ = h/p = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / 4.5 = 1.47×10⁻³⁴ m

    This is 45 orders of magnitude smaller than atomic sizes (~10⁻¹⁰ m). The wave nature is completely negligible — diffraction requires apertures comparable to λ.

    General rule: λ ∝ 1/m. Heavy objects have immeasurably small λ.

    4.3 de Broglie wavelength for microscopic particles

    Electron (m = 9.11×10⁻³¹ kg):

    • At 100 V: K = 100 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁷ J; v = √(2K/m) = 5.93×10⁶ m/s; λ = h/mv = 1.23×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.123 nm
    • At 1000 V: λ = 0.0388 nm
    • At 100 keV: λ = 0.0037 nm (relativistic correction needed)

    Proton (m = 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg):

    • At same kinetic energy as electron, λ is √(m_e/m_p) ≈ 1/43 times smaller.

    Thermal neutron (E = 0.025 eV, λ = 0.18 nm): Ideal for crystal diffraction.

    4.3 Standing wave condition and Bohr's quantization

    de Broglie's insight (1924): If the electron wave forms a standing wave around the nucleus, the circumference must contain an integer number of wavelengths:

    nλ = 2πr (n = 1, 2, 3...)

    Substitute λ = h/mv: n(h/mv) = 2πr → mvr = n·h/2π = nħ

    This is Bohr's quantization condition — derived from wave mechanics, not postulated!

    Verification for n=1 hydrogen: r₁ = a₀ = 5.29×10⁻¹¹ m Circumference = 2πa₀ = 3.32×10⁻¹⁰ m v₁ = 2.18×10⁶ m/s λ = h/mv₁ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 2.18×10⁶) = 3.32×10⁻¹⁰ m Matches exactly: one wavelength fits the n=1 orbit.

    4.4 Davisson-Germer experiment (1927) — proof of matter waves

    Setup:

    • Electron gun: electrons accelerated through voltage V (54 V)
    • Target: Single crystal of Nickel (Ni), cut along (111) plane
    • Detector: Movable Faraday cup measuring scattered electron intensity
    • Vacuum chamber to prevent air scattering

    Procedure:

    1. Electrons accelerated by 54 V → K = 54 eV
    2. Compute expected λ = h/√(2meV) = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / √(2×9.11×10⁻³¹×1.6×10⁻¹⁹×54) = 1.67×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.167 nm
    3. Ni crystal: cubic, lattice parameter a = 0.352 nm; (111) plane spacing d = a/√3 = 0.203 nm
    4. Electrons scatter from crystal planes; measure intensity vs scattering angle φ

    Result:

    • Peak intensity at φ = 50° (angle between incident and scattered beam)
    • Bragg's law: nλ = 2d sin(φ/2)
    • For n=1: λ = 2×0.203×sin(25°) = 0.171 nm
    • Matches de Broglie prediction (0.167 nm) within experimental error!

    Significance: First direct experimental confirmation of de Broglie's hypothesis; Nobel Prize 1937 (shared with G.P. Thomson).

    4.5 G.P. Thomson experiment (1927) — independent confirmation

    Setup: Electrons accelerated through thin metal foil (celluloid, gold, aluminum). Observation: Concentric diffraction rings on photographic plate. Analysis: Ring radii satisfy Bragg's law with λ = h/√(2meV). Significance: Independent confirmation using transmission (not reflection) geometry; electrons behave as waves passing through matter.

    4.5 Worked numericals

    1. de Broglie wavelength of electron at 100 V:

    K = 100 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁷ J; v = √(2K/m) = 5.93×10⁶ m/s λ = h/mv = 6.626×10⁻³⁴ / (9.11×10⁻³¹ × 5.93×10⁶) = 1.23×10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.123 nm

    1. de Broglie wavelength of electron at 54 V (Davisson-Germer):

    λ = h/√(2meV) = 1.226/√V nm = 1.226/√54 = 0.167 nm

    1. Verify n=1 hydrogen standing wave:

    r₁ = 0.529 Å = 5.29×10⁻¹¹ m; circumference = 2πr₁ = 3.32×10⁻¹⁰ m v₁ = 2.18×10⁶ m/s; λ = h/mv₁ = 3.32×10⁻¹⁰ m One wavelength fits exactly!

    1. de Broglie wavelength of 1 keV electron:

    λ = 1.226/√1000 = 0.0388 nm

    1. de Broglie wavelength of 200 keV electron (TEM):

    Relativistic: λ = h/√(2meV(1+eV/2mc²)) ≈ 0.0025 nm

    1. Cricket ball (0.15 kg, 30 m/s): λ = 1.47×10⁻³⁴ m (immeasurable)

  • 6. Chemical Processes and Mechanisms

    Process: Electron diffraction (Davisson-Germer style)

    1. Definition: Diffraction of electrons by a crystal lattice to measure de Broglie wavelength.
    2. Location: Electron diffraction apparatus, LEED system, TEM.
    3. Requirements: Electron gun (known voltage), single crystal target, vacuum, detector.
    4. Components: Electron gun, high voltage supply, crystal target (Ni, Cu, Si), vacuum chamber, movable detector/phosphor screen.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Evacuate chamber; (ii) Heat filament to emit electrons; (iii) Accelerate electrons through known voltage V; (iv) Direct beam at crystal; (v) Measure scattered intensity vs angle φ; (vi) Identify Bragg peaks; (vii) Compute λ from Bragg's law: nλ = 2d sin(φ/2); (viii) Compare with de Broglie prediction λ = h/√(2meV).
    6. Result: λ_measured = λ_deBroglie within experimental error → confirms matter waves.
    7. Significance: Direct experimental proof of wave-particle duality for matter.

    Process: Electron microscopy (TEM/SEM)

    1. Definition: Imaging using electron matter waves instead of light.
    2. Location: Electron microscope facility (TEM, SEM, STEM).
    3. Requirements: Electron gun (high voltage 80-300 kV), electromagnetic lenses, vacuum, detector.
    4. Components: Electron gun, condenser lenses, objective lens, projector lenses, CCD/direct electron detector, vacuum system.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Generate electron beam (thermionic or field emission); (ii) Accelerate to 80-300 keV; (iii) Focus with condenser lenses; (iv) Transmit through thin sample (TEM) or scan surface (SEM); (v) Magnify with objective/projector lenses; (vi) Detect on CCD/direct electron detector; (vii) Reconstruct image/phase.
    6. Result: Atomic resolution images (TEM), 3D surface topography (SEM), diffraction patterns, elemental analysis (EDS/EELS).
    7. Significance: Only technique achieving sub-Ångström resolution; essential for structural biology, materials science, nanotechnology.

    Process: Neutron diffraction for protein crystallography

    1. Definition: Using thermal neutron diffraction to locate hydrogen atoms in proteins.
    2. Location: Neutron source (reactor or spallation), protein crystallography beamline.
    3. Requirements: Large protein crystals (>0.1 mm³), neutron flux >10⁶ n/cm²/s, large-area detector.
    4. Components: Neutron guide, monochromator (λ=0.1-0.2 nm), sample stage, 2D detector, shielding.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Grow large protein crystal; (ii) Mount in neutron beam; (iii) Collect diffraction data at multiple orientations; (iv) Process data (index, integrate, scale); (v) Solve structure (molecular replacement); (vi) Refine with neutron + X-ray data; (vii) Locate H/D atoms precisely.
    6. Result: Protein structure with visible H atoms (protonation states, water networks, H-bonding).
    7. Significance: X-rays scatter from electrons (H nearly invisible); neutrons scatter from nuclei (H visible, strong contrast H vs D).

    Common conceptual confusions

    • de Broglie wave is a physical wave in space like sound — NO, it's a probability amplitude wave (ψ).
    • Wave nature is significant for all particles — NO, λ ∝ 1/m; macroscopic objects have λ ~ 10⁻³⁴ m (unmeasurable).
    • Phase velocity is the particle speed — NO, group velocity v_g = dω/dk = v; phase velocity v_p = ω/k = E/p > v.
    • de Broglie wave travels with the particle — NO, wave packet (superposition) travels with particle; phase velocity ≠ particle speed.
    • Davisson-Germer proved electrons ARE waves — NO, proved electrons exhibit wave BEHAVIOR (diffraction); particle properties remain.
    • Standing wave picture is exact — NO, uncertainty principle gives Δp/p ≈ 50% for n=1; not a precise orbit.

    2. The vapour density of an oxide of nitrogen is 46; find its molecular formula if it contains 30.4% nitrogen. (Not related — use de Broglie: electron at 100 V → λ = 0.123 nm)
    3. An electron is accelerated through 100 V. What is its de Broglie wavelength? If the voltage is increased to 400 V, how does λ change?
    4. Davisson-Germer: 54 V electrons on Ni (d=0.203 nm for (111) planes). At what scattering angle does the first Bragg peak occur?
    5. A cricket ball (0.15 kg) moves at 30 m/s. Calculate its de Broglie wavelength. Why is it unobservable?
    6. Electron at 200 keV in TEM. What is λ? What is the theoretical resolution limit?
    7. Thermal neutron (0.025 eV) diffraction. What is λ? Why are neutrons good for H atoms?

    Answers

    1. de Broglie hypothesis: all matter has wave properties; λ = h/p = h/mv (non-relativistic).
    2. Photon: E = hν = hc/λ, p = E/c = h/λ → λ = h/p. Extended to matter: λ = h/p.
    3. 100 V electron: λ = 1.226/√100 = 0.123 nm (non-relativistic formula λ = 1.226/√V nm).
    4. Cricket ball: m=0.15 kg, v=30 m/s → p=4.5 kg·m/s → λ = 6.626×10⁻³⁴/4.5 = 1.47×10⁻³⁴ m. 45 orders of magnitude smaller than atom — diffraction impossible.
    5. Standing wave: nλ = 2πr. λ = h/mv → n(h/mv) = 2πr → mvr = n·h/2π = nħ. Bohr's quantisation derived!
    6. Davisson-Germer: 54 V electrons on Ni (111). λ = 1.226/√54 = 0.167 nm. Bragg peak at φ=50°: λ = 2d sin(φ/2) = 2×0.203×sin(25°) = 0.171 nm. Matches! Applications: electron microscopy, LEED.
    7. ψ: probability amplitude (complex); ψ²: probability density (Born rule).
    8. Cricket ball: m=0.15 kg, v=30 m/s → p=4.5 → λ=1.47×10⁻³⁴ m. 45 orders smaller than atom → no diffraction.
    9. 100 V: λ=0.123 nm; 400 V: λ=0.061 nm (λ ∝ 1/√V, 4× voltage → λ halves).
    10. Ni d=0.203 nm (111); φ=50°: λ = 2d sin(25°) = 0.171 nm; de Broglie λ=0.167 nm → match.
    11. Cricket ball: 1.47×10⁻³⁴ m; 45 orders smaller than atom — no diffraction aperture exists.
    12. 200 keV: relativistic λ ≈ 0.0025 nm; Rayleigh δ ≈ 0.61λ/NA ≈ 0.15 nm (atomic resolution).
    13. Thermal neutron: 0.025 eV → λ = 0.18 nm. Neutrons scatter from nuclei (H visible), X-rays from electrons (H invisible).
    14. Wave-particle duality symmetric: Photon (E=hν, p=h/λ) ↔ Matter (λ=h/p, E=hν).

    Q: State and explain de Broglie's wave equation; derive λ = h/mv for a material particle. (2 marks) Answer: de Broglie hypothesis: all matter has wave properties; wavelength λ = h/p. For photon: E = hν = hc/λ, p = E/c = h/λ → λ = h/p. Extended to matter: λ = h/p = h/mv (non-relativistic). Keywords: matter waves, λ = h/p, photon analogy, λ = h/mv.

    Q: Explain why the wave nature of matter is not observed for ordinary objects. (2 marks) Answer: λ = h/mv. For cricket ball (0.15 kg, 30 m/s): p = 4.5 kg·m/s, λ = 1.47×10⁻³⁴ m — 45 orders of magnitude smaller than atomic size. No aperture can diffract such a small wavelength. Keywords: λ = h/mv, cricket ball example, 10⁻³⁴ m, 45 orders smaller.

    Q: Show that the standing-wave condition nλ = 2πr leads to Bohr's quantisation of angular momentum. (3 marks) Answer: de Broglie: λ = h/mv. Standing wave around orbit: nλ = 2πr. Substitute: n(h/mv) = 2πr → mvr = n·h/2π = nħ. This is Bohr's quantisation condition mvr = nħ, derived from wave mechanics. Keywords: λ = h/mv, nλ = 2πr, mvr = nħ, standing wave.

    Q: Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through 100 V. (3 marks) Answer: Non-relativistic: λ = h/√(2meV) = 1.226/√V nm. For V = 100 V: λ = 1.226/10 = 0.123 nm. Keywords: λ = 1.226/√V nm, 100 V → 0.123 nm.

    Q: Describe the Davisson-Germer experiment as evidence for matter waves and give two applications of electron waves. (3 marks) Answer: Electrons accelerated through 54 V strike Ni(111) crystal. Bragg peak at φ = 50° gives λ = 2d sin(25°) = 0.171 nm. de Broglie predicts λ = 1.226/√54 = 0.167 nm. Match confirms matter waves. Applications: (1) Electron microscopy (TEM/SEM/cryo-EM) for atomic resolution imaging; (2) LEED for surface crystallography and catalysis research. Keywords: 54 V, Ni(111), Bragg peak 50°, 0.167 nm match, TEM/SEM, LEED.

    Q: Derive de Broglie's relation from the photon equations E = hν and E = mc². (3 marks) Answer: Photon: E = hν = hc/λ; E = pc (since m=0) → p = E/c = h/λ → λ = h/p. De Broglie extended this to material particles: λ = h/p = h/mv (non-relativistic). Keywords: E = hν, E = pc, p = h/λ, λ = h/p, matter wave extension.

    Q: Calculate λ for an electron accelerated through 100 V and through 50 V. (3 marks) Answer: λ = 1.226/√V nm. For 100 V: 1.226/10 = 0.123 nm. For 50 V: 1.226/√50 = 1.226/7.07 = 0.173 nm. Keywords: 1.226/√V, 100 V → 0.123 nm, 50 V → 0.173 nm.

    Q: Describe the Davisson-Germer experiment and show how it verifies λ = h/mv. (3 marks) Answer: 54 V electrons on Ni(111) crystal. d = 0.203 nm. Bragg peak at φ = 50°: λ = 2d sin(φ/2) = 2×0.203×sin(25°) = 0.171 nm. de Broglie: λ = 1.226/√54 = 0.167 nm. Match within 2% confirms λ = h/mv. Keywords: 54 V, Ni(111), Bragg peak 50°, 0.167 nm vs 0.171 nm match.

    Q: Why is the wave nature of a cricket ball unobservable? (2 marks) Answer: m = 0.15 kg, v = 30 m/s → p = 4.5 kg·m/s. λ = h/p = 6.626×10⁻³⁴/4.5 = 1.47×10⁻³⁴ m. 45 orders of magnitude smaller than atomic size; no diffraction aperture exists. Keywords: cricket ball, 1.47×10⁻³⁴ m, 45 orders smaller, no aperture.

    Q: Write the time-independent Schrödinger equation for a particle in one dimension and explain each term. (3 marks) Answer: -ħ²/2m d²ψ/dx² + V(x)ψ = Eψ. Terms: -ħ²/2m d²ψ/dx² = kinetic energy operator; V(x)ψ = potential energy; Eψ = total energy eigenvalue; ψ(x) = wave function. Keywords: -ħ²/2m d²/dx², V(x), E, ψ(x), kinetic + potential = total.

    Q: What is the physical significance of ψ and ψ²? (2 marks) Answer: ψ(x) is the probability amplitude (complex-valued). |ψ(x)|² dx is the probability of finding the particle between x and x+dx. ψ itself is not directly observable; |ψ|² is the probability density. Keywords: ψ = probability amplitude, |ψ|² = probability density, Born rule.

    Q: How do the quantum numbers arise from the solution for the hydrogen atom? (3 marks) Answer: Solving Schrödinger equation in spherical coordinates gives three separation constants: radial equation → n (principal); polar angle → l (azimuthal); azimuthal angle → m (magnetic). Spin (s) from Dirac equation/sterngerlach. n = 1,2,3...; l = 0...n-1; m = -l...+l; s = ±½. Keywords: separation constants, radial/polar/azimuthal, n/l/m/s, quantum numbers.

    Q: Give two results of the quantum-mechanical model that Bohr's model cannot provide. (2 marks) Answer: (1) Fine structure (spin-orbit splitting, e.g., Na D-lines doublet). (2) Orbitals (probability clouds ψ²) instead of definite orbits; Zeeman/Stark effects explained via mₗ, s. (Also: chemical bonding via orbital overlap; multi-electron atoms via Pauli exclusion.) Keywords: fine structure, orbitals ψ², Zeeman/Stark, chemical bonding, multi-electron atoms.

Confusion

  • ❌ de Broglie wave is a physical wave like sound. ✅ It's a probability amplitude wave (ψ); |ψ|² gives probability density.
  • ❌ Wave nature is significant for all particles. ✅ λ ∝ 1/m; macroscopic objects have λ ~ 10⁻³⁴ m (unmeasurable).
  • ❌ Phase velocity equals particle speed. ✅ Group velocity v_g = dω/dk = v; phase velocity v_p = ω/k = E/p > v.
  • ❌ de Broglie wave travels with the particle. ✅ Wave packet (superposition) travels with particle; phase velocity ≠ particle speed.
  • ❌ Davisson-Germer proves electrons ARE waves. ✅ Proves electrons exhibit wave BEHAVIOR (diffraction); particle properties remain.
  • ❌ Standing wave picture is exact. ✅ Uncertainty principle gives Δp/p ≈ 50% for n=1; standing wave is not a precise orbit.
  • ❌ Davisson-Germer proves electrons ARE waves. ✅ Proves electrons exhibit wave BEHAVIOR (diffraction); particle properties remain.
  • ❌ Standing wave picture is exact. ✅ Uncertainty principle gives Δp/p ≈ 50% for n=1; standing wave is not a precise orbit.
