Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Matter Learns to Wave

    Bohr postulated quantisation; de Broglie (1924) derived it. If light — a wave — can behave as particles (photoelectric effect), then particles — electrons — should behave as waves. An electron wave can circle the nucleus only if a whole number of wavelengths fits the orbit; that condition is Bohr's quantisation, no longer arbitrary. This single idea, developed by Schrödinger into the wave equation, replaced orbits with orbitals and opened the quantum mechanical model.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The de Broglie relation

    Every moving particle has a wavelength:

    λ = h / mv = h / p (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s)

    • For macroscopic objects m is large → λ is absurdly small → wave nature is undetectable.
    • For an electron (m = 9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg) at ordinary speeds λ ≈ 10⁻¹⁰ m — atomic dimensions — so wave behaviour shows.

    2.2 Deriving Bohr's quantisation (the 3m chain)

    1. An electron wave must be a standing wave on its orbit — otherwise it destructively interferes with itself and dies.
    2. Standing-wave condition: circumference = whole number of wavelengths → 2πr = nλ.
    3. Substitute λ = h/mv: 2πr = nh/mv → mvr = nh/2π — exactly Bohr's quantisation of angular momentum.

    2.3 Experimental proof — Davisson and Germer (1927)

    Electrons fired at a nickel crystal were diffracted, producing a diffraction pattern like X-rays — direct proof that electrons diffract as waves, with the wavelength matching de Broglie's prediction. (Thomson's electron diffraction through thin films confirmed it independently.)

    2.4 The quantum mechanical model (elementary idea)

    • Schrödinger's wave equation treats the electron as a standing wave in three dimensions.
    • Its solutions, wave functions ψ, describe orbitals — regions of high probability (ψ²) of finding the electron.
    • Energy is still quantised; the definite orbit is replaced by a probability cloud obeying the uncertainty principle.
    • Four quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ) label each solution.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Worked numerical — wavelength of an electron (NEB-style template)

    Q: Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron moving with a velocity of 10⁶ m s⁻¹. λ = h/mv = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / (9.1 × 10⁻³¹ × 10⁶) = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / 9.1 × 10⁻²⁵ = 7.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m ≈ 0.73 nm — comparable to atomic spacing, which is why crystals diffract electrons.

    4.2 Worked numerical — the cricket ball contrast

    Q: Find the wavelength of a 150 g cricket ball at 40 m s⁻¹. λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / (0.150 × 40) = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ / 6 = 1.1 × 10⁻³⁴ m — smaller than any measurable length by 20 orders of magnitude: macroscopic wave nature is unobservable. This contrast is the favourite NEB application question.

    4.3 Why only certain orbits survive

    An orbit where the circumference is 2.5λ (say) would overlap crests on troughs — the wave cancels itself. Only orbits with 2πr = nλ reinforce; hence discrete radii, discrete energies, discrete spectrum. Quantisation is geometry, not decree.

    4.4 From ψ to orbitals

    The Schrödinger equation ψ solutions depend on three quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ); each allowed combination is one orbital with a characteristic shape (s spherical, p dumbbell). ψ² gives the probability picture. Bohr's radius r = 0.529 n² Å survives only as the most probable distance of the 1s electron — not a track.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    ψ vs ψ²: ψ is the wave function (no direct physical meaning); ψ² is the probability density — the measurable one. Quoting ψ as "probability" loses the mark.

  • Diffraction vs scattering: diffraction is wave-bending around/through regular arrays (needs wavelength ~ spacing); scattering is any direction change. Davisson–Germer is diffraction — the wave proof.
  • de Broglie applies to everything: the relation is universal; for cricket balls λ is simply too small to matter. Saying "large objects have no wavelength" is wrong — they have unobservable ones.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m): State the de Broglie relation and its significance. — λ = h/mv: every moving particle has a wavelength inversely proportional to its momentum. It explains why microscopic particles show wave behaviour and derives Bohr's quantisation (2πr = nλ → mvr = nh/2π).

  • NEB-style (3m): Show that Bohr's quantisation condition follows from de Broglie's hypothesis. — The electron wave on a stationary orbit must be a standing wave: 2πr = nλ. With λ = h/mv: 2πr = nh/mv → mvr = nh/2π — Bohr's postulate of quantised angular momentum, now derived rather than decreed.
  • NEB-style (2m): An electron and a cricket ball move at the same speed. Which has the longer wavelength and why? — The electron: λ = h/mv, so the smaller mass (9.1 × 10⁻³¹ kg vs 0.15 kg) gives the larger λ — electron λ ~ nm scale (observable), ball λ ~ 10⁻³⁴ m (unobservable).

Confusion

  • ψ vs ψ²: ψ has no physical interpretation; ψ² is the probability density. Never interchange them.
  • Orbit vs orbital: orbit = Bohr's fixed circular track; orbital = quantum probability region. The words differ by one letter and one revolution of meaning.

Summary

de Broglie (1924) turned Bohr's postulate into physics: every moving particle has wavelength λ = h/mv, so an orbiting electron is a wave that survives only when a whole number of wavelengths fits its circumference (2πr = nλ) — which rearranges exactly to Bohr's mvr = nh/2π.
