  • Orbitals and shape of s and p orbitals only

    1. Topic Introduction

    An orbital is the region in space where the probability of finding an electron is maximum — a three-dimensional standing wave of the electron's matter wave. Unlike Bohr's definite circular orbits, orbitals are probability clouds described by the wave function ψ. The shape of an orbital is the surface enclosing 90-95% of the electron probability density. For NEB Grade 11, we focus on s (spherical) and p (dumbbell) orbitals — the building blocks of all chemistry up to Z=30.

    Why it matters. Orbital shapes determine everything: chemical bonding (overlap of orbitals), molecular geometry (VSEPR), magnetic properties (unpaired electrons in p orbitals), periodic trends (shielding, penetration), and spectroscopy (selection rules). The s orbital's spherical symmetry gives non-directional bonds; p orbitals' directional lobes give π bonds and molecular shape.

    What you will learn: (i) definition of orbital vs orbit; (ii) shapes of s and p orbitals with nodal planes; (iii) radial and angular nodes; (iv) radial probability distribution; (v) penetration and shielding explaining energy order; (vi) box diagrams for electronic configuration; (vii) magnetic behaviour from unpaired electrons.

    Learning outcomes. You can: draw and describe s and p orbital shapes; identify nodal planes and count radial/angular nodes; explain why s orbitals are lower in energy than p in the same shell (penetration); draw box diagrams for elements up to Z=30; predict paramagnetism/diamagnetism from unpaired electrons.

  • 2. Fundamental Conceptual Understanding

    What an orbital is

    An orbital is a three-dimensional region in space where the probability of finding an electron (|ψ|²) is high (>90%). It is the solution of the Schrödinger equation for the hydrogen atom, characterized by three quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ). Unlike Bohr's orbit — a definite path — an orbital is a probability cloud with no definite trajectory.

    Why orbitals exist

    The wave nature of electrons (de Broglie) and the uncertainty principle (Heisenberg) forbid definite orbits. The Schrödinger equation gives allowed energy states (orbitals) where the electron wave forms a standing wave. Each orbital holds a maximum of 2 electrons (Pauli exclusion principle).

    How orbitals work

    The wave function ψ(n, l, mₗ) describes the orbital. |ψ|² gives the probability density. The shape is the boundary surface of constant |ψ|² enclosing 90-95% probability. Nodes are surfaces where ψ = 0 (zero probability). Radial nodes = n - l - 1; angular nodes = l.

    Where orbitals apply

    Every atom and molecule: electronic configuration, chemical bonding, molecular geometry, spectroscopy, magnetic properties, periodic trends, reactivity. The s and p orbitals cover all main-group chemistry up to Z=30.

    What their components are

    Quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ), wave function ψ, probability density |ψ|², radial part R(r), angular part Y(θ,φ), nodes (radial and angular), boundary surface.

    How components interact

    Quantum numbers define the orbital; ψ gives amplitude; |ψ|² gives probability; nodes divide regions of opposite phase; penetration determines energy ordering; overlap of orbitals forms bonds.

    What makes them biologically/physically important

    • Chemical bonding: s-s (σ), s-p (σ), p-p (σ, π) overlap determines bond type and strength.
    • Molecular geometry: p orbital orientation → VSEPR shapes (tetrahedral, trigonal planar, etc.).
    • Magnetic properties: unpaired electrons in p orbitals → paramagnetism (O₂, NO).
    • Spectroscopy: selection rules (Δl = ±1) from orbital symmetry.
    • Periodic trends: s orbital penetration → lower energy → ionization energy, atomic radius trends.

    4.1 Orbit vs Orbital

    AspectOrbit (Bohr)Orbital (Quantum)

    VelocityExact vUncertain (Δx·Δp ≥ ħ/2)
    Quantum numbersn onlyn, l, mₗ, mₛ
    Max electrons2n² (by assumption)2 per orbital (Pauli)
    ShapeCircles: sphere; p: dumbbell; d: cloverleaf
    Uncertainty principleViolated (Δx·Δp = 0)Satisfied (Δx·Δp ≥ ħ/2)

    4.2 s orbitals (l = 0)

    • Shape: Spherical (spherical symmetry)
    • Number per shell: 1 (mₗ = 0 only)
    • Electrons: 2 (opposite spins)
    • Angular nodes: 0 (l = 0)
    • Radial nodes: n - 1
    • Radial probability: Peaks at r = n²a₀ (most probable radius)
    • Energy: Lowest in a given shell due to penetration

    Radial nodes for s orbitals:

    • 1s: n=1, l=0 → radial nodes = 0
    • 2s: n=2, l=0 → radial nodes = 1 (one spherical node)
    • 3s: n=3, l=0 → radial nodes = 2
    • 4s: n=4, l=0 → radial nodes = 3

    4.3 p orbitals (l = 1)

    • Shape: Dumbbell (two lobes with opposite phase)
    • Number per shell: 3 (mₗ = -1, 0, +1)
    • Electrons: 6 max (2 per orbital)
    • Angular nodes: 1 (one nodal plane each)
    • Radial nodes: n - 2
    • Orientations: px (x-axis), py (y-axis), pz (z-axis)
    • Nodal plane: xy-plane for pz, yz-plane for px, xz-plane for py

    Radial nodes for p orbitals:

    • 2p: n=2, l=1 → radial nodes = 0
    • 3p: n=3, l=1 → radial nodes = 1
    • 4p: n=4, l=1 → radial nodes = 2

    4.5 Radial probability distribution

    Definition: Probability of finding electron in spherical shell between r and r+dr: P(r)dr = 4πr²|R(r)|² dr

    Key points:

    • Not the same as |ψ|² maximum (which is at nucleus for s orbitals)
    • For 1s: peak at r = a₀ = 0.529 Å (Bohr radius)
    • For 2s: two peaks (inner near nucleus, outer at ~5.2a₀); node between
    • For 2p: peak at r = 4a₀ = 2.12 Å
    • Most probable radius r_mp = n²a₀/Z for hydrogen-like ions

    4.6 Penetration and shielding (energy ordering)

    Penetration: Ability of electron to approach nucleus. Order for same n: s > p > d > f

    Why s penetrates more: s orbitals have non-zero probability at nucleus (ψ(0) ≠ 0); p, d, f have nodes at nucleus (ψ(0) = 0).

    Shielding: Inner electrons screen outer electrons from nuclear charge. Effective nuclear charge: Z_eff = Z - σ where σ = shielding constant.

    Energy ordering in multi-electron atoms: E(ns) < E(np) < E(nd) < E(nf) For different n: (n+l) rule (Madelung rule) determines filling order.

    4.8 Worked examples

    1. Nodes in 3p orbital: n=3, l=1 → radial nodes = 3-1-1 = 1; angular nodes = 1. Total nodes = 2.
    2. Nodes in 4s orbital: n=4, l=0 → radial nodes = 3; angular nodes = 0. Total nodes = 3.
    3. Radial probability peak for 2p: r_mp = n²a₀ = 4a₀ = 2.12 Å.
    4. Why 3s < 3p < 3d energy? Penetration: 3s penetrates core, feels higher Z_eff; 3d has poor penetration, heavily shielded.
    5. Box diagram for N (Z=7): 1s² 2s² 2p³ → 2p: ↑ ↑ ↑ (3 unpaired, paramagnetic).

  • 6. Chemical Processes and Mechanisms

    Process: Orbital overlap and bond formation

    1. Definition: Formation of chemical bonds by overlap of atomic orbitals.
    2. Location: Molecular formation, chemical reactions, solid state.
    3. Requirements: Orbitals of appropriate symmetry, energy match, electron pairing.
    4. Components: Atomic orbitals (s, p), overlap integral S, bond order.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Atoms approach; (ii) Orbitals overlap (S = ∫ψ₁ψ₂ dτ); (iii) Constructive interference → bonding orbital (lower energy); (iv) Electron pairing in bonding orbital; (v) Bond formation; (v) Destructive interference → antibonding orbital (empty or partially filled).
    6. Result: Chemical bond (covalent) with bond order = (bonding - antibonding)/2.
    7. Significance: Orbital overlap explains bond strength, directionality, and molecular geometry.

    Process: Box diagram construction (electronic configuration)

    1. Definition: Visual representation of electron filling following Aufbau, Pauli, Hund.
    2. Location: Electronic configuration problems, magnetic property prediction.
    3. Requirements: Atomic number Z, filling order (n+l rule), Hund's rule, Pauli exclusion.
    4. Components: Boxes (orbitals), arrows (electrons, ↑ or ↓).
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Determine Z; (ii) Fill in order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d... (n+l rule); (iii) Each box max 2 arrows opposite (Pauli); (iv) Degenerate boxes fill singly first, parallel spins (Hund); (v) Count unpaired for magnetic behaviour.
    6. Result: Box diagram showing electron distribution, unpaired count, magnetic behaviour.
    7. Significance: Predicts paramagnetism/diamagnetism, reactivity, oxidation states.

    Process: Magnetic behaviour prediction

    1. Definition: Determining paramagnetism (unpaired electrons) or diamagnetism (all paired).
    2. Location: Inorganic chemistry, coordination compounds, molecular oxygen.
    3. Requirements: Electronic configuration, box diagram, Hund's rule.
    4. Components: Unpaired electron count u, spin-only magnetic moment μ = √[u(u+2)] BM.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Write configuration; (ii) Draw box diagram; (iii) Count unpaired electrons u; (iv) μ = √[u(u+2)] BM; (v) Paramagnetic if u > 0; diamagnetic if u = 0.
    6. Result: Magnetic moment in Bohr magnetons; classification as para/dia.
    7. Significance: Diagnoses electronic structure; used in coordination chemistry, MRI contrast agents.

  • 10. Topic Mastery Check

    Important definitions

    1. Orbital — region where |ψ|² > 90%; 3D standing wave; max 2 electrons.
    2. s orbital — spherical, l=0, 1 orbital, 2 electrons, radial nodes = n-1.
    3. p orbital — dumbbell, l=1, 3 orbitals (px, py, pz), 6 electrons, 1 angular node each.
    4. Radial node — spherical surface where ψ = 0; number = n-l-1.
    5. Angular node — plane/conical surface where ψ = 0; number = l.
    6. Nodal plane — angular node that is a plane; p orbitals have one each (px→yz, py→xz, pz→xy).
    7. Penetration — ability to approach nucleus; order s > p > d > f.
    8. Radial probability — 4πr²|R(r)|² dr; peak at most probable radius.
    9. Most probable radius — r_mp = n²a₀/Z for hydrogen-like ions.

    Key concepts to remember

    • Orbital = probability cloud (|ψ|²); orbit = definite path (Bohr, wrong).
    • s: spherical, 1 per shell, 2 e⁻, radial nodes = n-1, non-directional.
    • p: dumbbell, 3 per shell (px, py, pz), 6 e⁻, 1 nodal plane each.
    • Nodes: radial = n-l-1, angular = l, total = n-1.
    • Penetration: s > p > d > f → energy s < p < d < f (same n).
    • Radial probability P(r) = 4πr²|R|²; peak at r_mp = n²a₀/Z.
    • 1s peak at a₀; 2p peak at 4a₀; 3d peak at 9a₀.
    • Box diagram: fill by n+l rule; Hund's = singly first, parallel; Pauli = max 2 opposite.

    Important classifications

    • Orbital shapes: s (sphere), p (dumbbell), d (cloverleaf), f (complex).
    • By l value: l=0 (s), l=1 (p), l=2 (d), l=3 (f).
    • By nodes: radial (spherical, n-l-1), angular (planar/conical, l).
    • By directionality: non-directional (s) vs directional (p, d, f).
    • By penetration: s > p > d > f → energy ordering.

    Common conceptual confusions

    • s orbital has no radial nodes — NO (1s: 0; 2s: 1; 3s: 2; radial nodes = n-1).
    • p orbital has no radial nodes — NO (2p: 0; 3p: 1; 4p: 2; radial nodes = n-2).
    • |ψ|² maximum = radial probability peak — NO (1s |ψ|² max at nucleus; P(r) peak at a₀).
    • Radial probability peak is at nucleus for s orbitals — NO (1s peak at a₀).
    • p orbitals have no radial nodes — NO (only 2p has 0; 3p, 4p have radial nodes).
    • All three p orbitals have the same nodal plane — NO (px→yz, py→xz, pz→xy — different).
    • s and p have same energy in same shell — NO (s < p due to penetration).
    • Box diagram: pairing before filling all degenerate orbitals — NO (Hund's rule: singly first).

    PYQ Solutions

    Q: Draw and describe the shapes of s and p orbitals; mark the nodal plane of a p orbital. (3 marks) Answer: s: spherical, 1 per shell, 2 e⁻. p: dumbbell, 3 per shell (px, py, pz), 6 e⁻. Nodal planes: px→yz, py→xz, pz→xy. Diagram: sphere for s; three perpendicular dumbbells for p with nodal planes shown. Keywords: spherical, dumbbell, 3 orbitals, nodal planes yz/xz/xy.

    Q: Why are s orbitals non-directional while p orbitals are directional? Give a chemical consequence. (3 marks) Answer: s orbital is spherical (ψ depends only on r, not θ,φ) — same probability in all directions → non-directional bonds (σ only). p orbital has angular dependence (Y₁ᵐ) with lobes along axes → directional → forms π bonds (side-on overlap) in addition to σ. Consequence: p orbitals enable double/triple bonds, molecular geometry, conjugation. Keywords: spherical symmetry, angular dependence, π bonds, molecular geometry.

    Q: How many radial and angular nodes are there in a 3p orbital? (2 marks) Answer: n=3, l=1. Radial nodes = n-l-1 = 3-1-1 = 1. Angular nodes = l = 1. Total nodes = n-1 = 2. Keywords: n=3, l=1, radial=1, angular=1, total=2.

    Q: Explain, using penetration, why 3s lies lower in energy than 3p in a multi-electron atom. (3 marks) Answer: 3s orbital has non-zero probability at nucleus (ψ(0) ≠ 0) → penetrates inner core → experiences higher effective nuclear charge Z_eff. 3p orbital has a node at nucleus (ψ(0) = 0) → cannot penetrate → more shielded → lower Z_eff → higher energy. Keywords: ψ(0) ≠ 0, penetrates core, higher Z_eff, 3p node at nucleus, shielded, higher energy.

    Q: Draw box diagrams for N, O and Ne and count unpaired electrons. (3 marks) Answer: N (Z=7): 1s² 2s² 2p³ → 2p: ↑ ↑ ↑ (3 unpaired). O (Z=8): 1s² 2s² 2p⁴ → 2p: ↑↓ ↑ ↑ (2 unpaired). Ne (Z=10): 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ → 2p: ↑↓ ↑↓ ↑↓ (0 unpaired). Keywords: N: 3 unpaired, O: 2 unpaired, Ne: 0 unpaired, box diagrams with Hund's rule.

    Q: State the three rules (Aufbau, Pauli, Hund) that govern box diagrams. (2 marks) Answer: Aufbau: orbitals fill in increasing energy order (n+l rule). Pauli: no two electrons share all four quantum numbers (max 2 per orbital, opposite spins). Hund: degenerate orbitals fill singly first with parallel spins before pairing. Keywords: n+l rule, max 2 opposite spins, singly first parallel spins.

    Q: Map the orbitals of the M shell with their capacities. (2 marks) Answer: M shell (n=3): 3s (1 orbital, 2 e⁻), 3p (3 orbitals, 6 e⁻), 3d (5 orbitals, 10 e⁻). Total: 9 orbitals, 18 e⁻. Capacity = 2n² = 18. Keywords: 3s 2, 3p 6, 3d 10, total 18, 2n²=18.

    Q: Predict whether Fe³⁺ is paramagnetic using box notation. (3 marks) Answer: Fe (Z=26): [Ar]4s²3d⁶. Fe³⁺: [Ar]3d⁵. 3d box: ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ ↑ (5 unpaired). μ = √[5(5+2)] = √35 ≈ 5.92 BM. Paramagnetic. Keywords: [Ar]3d⁵, 5 unpaired, μ = √35 ≈ 5.92 BM, paramagnetic.

    Q: Distinguish orbit from orbital in a six-point table. (3 marks) Answer:

Confusion

  • ❌ s orbital has no radial nodes. ✅ 1s: 0; 2s: 1; 3s: 2; radial nodes = n-1.
  • ❌ p orbital has no radial nodes. ✅ 2p: 0; 3p: 1; 4p: 2; radial nodes = n-2.
  • ❌ |ψ|² maximum = radial probability peak. ✅ 1s: |ψ|² max at nucleus; P(r) peak at a₀.
  • ❌ p orbitals have no radial nodes. ✅ Only 2p has 0; 3p, 4p have radial nodes.
  • ❌ All three p orbitals share the same nodal plane. ✅ px→yz, py→xz, pz→xy — different planes.
  • ❌ s and p orbitals have the same energy in the same shell. ✅ s < p due to penetration.
  • ❌ Box diagram: pair electrons before filling all degenerate orbitals. ✅ Hund's rule: singly first, parallel spins.
  • ❌ Radial probability peak for s orbital is at the nucleus. ✅ 1s radial probability peaks at a₀; |ψ|² max at nucleus.

Universal Facts

  • s orbital: spherical, 1 per shell, 2 e⁻, radial nodes = n-1, angular = 0.
  • p orbital: dumbbell, 3 per shell (px, py, pz), 6 e⁻, 1 angular node (nodal plane).
  • Nodes: radial = n-l-1, angular = l, total = n-1.
  • Penetration: s > p > d > f → energy s < p < d < f (same n).
  • Radial probability: P(r) = 4πr²|R|²; peak at r_mp = n²a₀/Z.
  • Box diagram: n+l rule; Hund's (singly first, parallel); Pauli (max 2 opposite).

Examples

  • 3p: 1 radial node, 1 angular node (nodal plane), total 2 nodes.
  • 4s: 3 radial nodes, 0 angular nodes, total 3 nodes.
  • N (Z=7): 2p³ → ↑ ↑ ↑ (3 unpaired, paramagnetic).
  • O (Z=8): 2p⁴ → ↑↓ ↑ ↑ (2 unpaired, paramagnetic).
  • Fe³⁺ (3d⁵): 5 unpaired, μ = √35 ≈ 5.92 BM (paramagnetic).
  • O₂: π* has 2 unpaired → paramagnetic.

Formulas

  • Radial nodes = n - l - 1
  • Angular nodes = l
  • Total nodes = n - 1
  • P(r) = 4πr²|R(r)|² dr (radial probability)
  • r_mp = n²a₀/Z (most probable radius for hydrogen-like)
  • Penetration order: s > p > d > f
  • Energy order (same n): E(s) < E(p) < E(d) < E(f)
  • Box diagram: max 2 e⁻ per box (Pauli); singly first, parallel (Hund); n+l rule (Aufbau)
  • Paramagnetic: unpaired e⁻ (u > 0); μ = √[u(u+2)] BM
  • Diamagnetic: all paired (u = 0)

Numericals

  • 3p: radial nodes = 1, angular nodes = 1, total = 2.
  • 4s: radial nodes = 3, angular nodes = 0, total = 3.
  • 2p radial probability peak: 4a₀ = 2.12 Å.
  • 3d most probable radius: 9a₀ = 4.76 Å.
  • N (Z=7): 2p³ → 3 unpaired; μ = √15 ≈ 3.87 BM.
  • O (Z=8): 2p⁴ → 2 unpaired; μ = √8 ≈ 2.83 BM.
  • Fe³⁺ (3d⁵): 5 unpaired; μ = √35 ≈ 5.92 BM.
  • 1s radial probability peak: a₀ = 0.529 Å.

Key Points

  • s: spherical, 1 per shell, 2 e⁻, radial nodes n-1, non-directional, high penetration.
  • p: dumbbell, 3 per shell (px, py, pz), 6 e⁻, 1 nodal plane each.
  • Nodes: radial = n-l-1, angular = l, total = n-1.
  • Penetration s > p > d > f → energy s < p < d < f (same n).
  • Radial probability P(r) = 4πr²|R|²; peak at r_mp = n²a₀/Z.
  • Box diagram: n+l rule; Hund's (singly first, parallel); Pauli (max 2 opposite).
  • Paramagnetic = unpaired e⁻; Diamagnetic = all paired.
