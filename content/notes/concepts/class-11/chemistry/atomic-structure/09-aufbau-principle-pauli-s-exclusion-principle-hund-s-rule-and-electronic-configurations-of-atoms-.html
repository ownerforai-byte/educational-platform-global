    1. Topic Introduction

    The periodic table is not a random list — it is a map of electron configurations. Three principles govern how electrons fill orbitals: Aufbau (build up in order of energy), Pauli (no two electrons identical), and Hund (maximize unpaired spins). Together they generate the electronic configuration of every element, which in turn determines its chemical properties, position in the periodic table, magnetic behaviour, and reactivity.

    Why it matters. Electronic configuration is the Rosetta Stone of chemistry. It explains: why Na is in Group 1 (3s¹), why Cr is [Ar]3d⁵4s¹ not [Ar]3d⁴4s² (half-filled d stability), why Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ have different colours (d⁶ vs d⁵), why O₂ is paramagnetic (unpaired π* electrons), and why the periodic table has its block structure (s, p, d, f). Every subsequent chemistry unit — bonding, periodicity, redox, coordination — starts from the electron configuration.

    What you will learn: (i) Aufbau principle and the (n+l) rule for filling order; (ii) Pauli exclusion principle and its consequences; (iii) Hund's rule and exchange energy; (iv) Electronic configurations of all elements Z=1 to 30; (v) Anomalous configurations of Cr and Cu; (vi) Ion configurations and oxidation states; (vii) Magnetic behaviour from unpaired electrons.

    Learning outcomes. You can: write ground-state configurations for any element Z=1–30; explain the (n+l) rule and anomalies (Cr, Cu); derive ion configurations; predict magnetic moment; identify block (s, p, d) from configuration.

  • 2. Fundamental Conceptual Understanding

    What the three principles are

    1. Aufbau principle (build-up): Electrons fill orbitals in order of increasing energy. The filling order is determined by the (n+l) rule: lower (n+l) fills first; for equal (n+l), lower n fills first.
    2. Pauli exclusion principle: No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ). An orbital can hold at most 2 electrons with opposite spins (mₛ = +½ and -½).
    3. Hund's rule (maximum multiplicity): When filling degenerate orbitals (same n, l), electrons occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before pairing up. This maximizes total spin and exchange energy.

    Why they exist

    • Aufbau: Minimizes total energy by filling lowest available orbitals first. The (n+l) rule arises from penetration and shielding: lower l penetrates more → lower energy.
    • Pauli: Fundamental consequence of fermion nature of electrons (spin-½ particles). Fermions obey Fermi-Dirac statistics; no two can occupy the same quantum state.
    • Hund: Electron-electron repulsion is minimized when electrons are in different orbitals (less spatial overlap) with parallel spins (exchange energy stabilizes).

    How they work

    The filling sequence for Z=1–30: 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p

    The (n+l) values:

    • 1s: 1+0=1
    • 2s: 2+0=2; 2p: 2+1=3
    • 3s: 3+0=3; 3p: 3+1=4; 3d: 3+2=5
    • 4s: 4+0=4; 4p: 4+1=5

    Order by (n+l): 1s (1) < 2s (2) < 2p (3) = 3s (3) → 2p first (lower n) < 3s < 3p (4) < 4s (4) = 3d (5) → 4s first (lower n) < 3d < 4p (5) = 4p (5)

    Where they apply

    Every element's ground state configuration, ion formation, periodic table blocks (s, p, d, f), magnetic properties, oxidation states, chemical reactivity, spectroscopy, periodic trends.

    What their components are

    Orbitals (1s, 2s, 2p...), quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ), filling order, electron spin, exchange energy, magnetic moment, ion formation rules.

    How components interact

    (n+l) rule → filling order → configuration → block position → chemical properties. Pauli limits to 2 per orbital. Hund fills degenerate orbitals singly first. Ion formation: remove from outermost (highest n) first, then from highest l.

    What makes them biologically/physically important

    • Haemoglobin Fe²⁺ (d⁶) vs Fe³⁺ (d⁵) oxygen binding.
    • Vitamin B₁₂ Co³⁺ (d⁶ low-spin) methyl transfer.
    • Photosystem II Mn₄Ca cluster (Mn oxidation states).
    • Cytochrome c Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ electron transfer.
    • MRI contrast agents Gd³⁺ (f⁷, 7 unpaired).
    • Superoxide dismutase Cu/Zn (d¹⁰/d¹⁰) antioxidant.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Aufbau principle and the (n+l) rule

    Aufbau principle: Electrons occupy the lowest energy orbitals available.

    The (n+l) rule (Madelung rule): Orbitals are filled in order of increasing (n+l). For equal (n+l), the orbital with lower n fills first.

    Filling order for Z=1–30:

    Orbitalnln+lOrder
    1s1011
    2s2022
    2p2133
    3s3034 (n=3 > n=2 for 2p)
    3p3145
    4s4046 (n=4 > n=3 for 3p)
    3d3257 (n=3 < n=4 for 4p)
    4p4158

    Resulting filling sequence: 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p

    Memory aid: Diagonal rule — write subshells in rows by n, columns by l; read diagonally.

    4.2 Pauli exclusion principle

    Statement: No two electrons in an atom can have the same set of four quantum numbers (n, l, mₗ, mₛ).

    Consequences:

    • Maximum 2 electrons per orbital (n, l, mₗ fixed → mₛ = +½ or -½).
    • s subshell (1 orbital): max 2 e⁻.
    • p subshell (3 orbitals): max 6 e⁻.
    • d subshell (5 orbitals): max 10 e⁻.
    • f subshell (7 orbitals): max 14 e⁻.
    • Period lengths: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32... (2×2², 2×2²+6, 2×3²...).

    Fermion nature: Electrons are spin-½ fermions; Fermi-Dirac statistics → Pauli exclusion.

    4.3 Hund's rule (maximum multiplicity)

    Statement: When filling degenerate orbitals (same n, l), electrons occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins before any orbital gets a second electron.

    Reason: Exchange energy. Electrons with parallel spins have antisymmetric spatial wave function → lower electron-electron repulsion → stabilization.

    Energy gain: For each pair of parallel spins in degenerate orbitals, exchange energy K > 0 lowers the energy. Maximum parallel spins = maximum exchange stabilization.

    Example — Carbon (Z=6): 1s² 2s² 2p²

    • Wrong: 2p: ↑↓ _ _ (paired, one orbital)
    • Correct: 2p: ↑ ↑ _ (Hund's rule: singly, parallel)

    Example — Nitrogen (Z=7): 1s² 2s² 2p³

    • 2p: ↑ ↑ ↑ (three unpaired, maximum S = 3/2)

    4.5 Anomalous configurations: Cr and Cu

    Chromium (Z=24): Expected [Ar]4s²3d⁴ → Actual [Ar]4s¹3d⁵

    • Reason: Half-filled d subshell (d⁵) gains extra exchange energy and symmetry.
    • Exchange energy: 5 parallel spins in d orbitals → 10 exchange pairs → large stabilization.
    • Symmetry: d⁵ is spherically symmetric (half-filled).

    Copper (Z=29): Expected [Ar]4s²3d⁹ → Actual [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰

    • Reason: Fully-filled d subshell (d¹⁰) gains exchange energy and symmetry.
    • Exchange energy: d¹⁰ has maximum pairing but full d shell stability.
    • Symmetry: d¹⁰ is spherically symmetric (fully-filled).

    Other anomalies (beyond Z=30): Nb, Mo, Tc, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag, La, Ac, etc. — not in NEB scope.

    4.6 Ion configurations and oxidation states

    Rules for ion formation:

    1. Remove electrons from highest n first (outermost shell).
    2. For same n, remove from highest l first (p before s, d before p, etc.).
    3. For transition metals: remove ns electrons before (n-1)d.

    Examples:

    • Na (Z=11): [Ne]3s¹ → Na⁺: [Ne] (lost 3s¹)
    • Ca (Z=20): [Ar]4s² → Ca²⁺: [Ar] (lost 4s²)
    • Fe (Z=26): [Ar]4s²3d⁶ → Fe²⁺: [Ar]3d⁶ (4s² lost); Fe³⁺: [Ar]3d⁵ (4s² + 1 3d lost)
    • Cu (Z=29): [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ → Cu⁺: [Ar]3d¹⁰ (4s¹ lost); Cu²⁺: [Ar]3d⁹ (4s¹ + 1 3d lost)
    • Zn (Z=30): [Ar]4s²3d¹⁰ → Zn²⁺: [Ar]3d¹⁰ (4s² lost)
    • Cr (Z=24): [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ → Cr³⁺: [Ar]3d³ (4s¹ + 2 3d lost)

    4.8 Magnetic behaviour

    Spin-only magnetic moment: μ = √[n(n+2)] BM (Bohr magnetons), where n = number of unpaired electrons.

  • 6. Chemical Processes and Mechanisms

    Process: Writing electronic configuration (step-by-step)

    1. Definition: Determining the ground-state electron distribution for an atom/ion.
    2. Location: Every chemistry problem involving periodicity, bonding, magnetism.
    3. Requirements: Atomic number Z, (n+l) rule, Pauli exclusion, Hund's rule.
    4. Components: Subshells in filling order, electron count, Pauli limit (2 per orbital), Hund's rule.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Write Z; (ii) Fill subshells in (n+l) order: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p...; (iii) Apply Pauli: max 2 per orbital; (iv) Apply Hund: degenerate subshells fill singly first, parallel spins; (v) For ions: remove from highest n first, then highest l; (vi) Check for anomalies (Cr, Cu); (vii) Write in order of increasing n (standard notation).
    6. Result: Ground-state configuration (e.g., 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁶).
    7. Significance: Foundation for all chemical properties — periodicity, bonding, magnetism, reactivity.

    Process: Determining magnetic moment

    1. Definition: Calculating spin-only magnetic moment from unpaired electrons.
    2. Location: Inorganic chemistry, coordination compounds, magnetic susceptibility measurements.
    3. Requirements: Electronic configuration, box diagram, unpaired electron count.
    4. Components: Configuration, box diagram, unpaired count n, μ = √[n(n+2)] BM.
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Write configuration; (ii) Draw box diagram for valence subshell; (iii) Count unpaired electrons n; (iv) Compute μ = √[n(n+2)] BM; (v) Classify: n>0 → paramagnetic; n=0 → diamagnetic.
    6. Result: Magnetic moment in Bohr magnetons; paramagnetic/diamagnetic classification.
    7. Significance: Identifies oxidation state, spin state, geometry of complexes.

    Process: Ion configuration derivation

    1. Definition: Deriving electron configuration of an ion from neutral atom.
    2. Location: Redox reactions, transition metal chemistry, coordination compounds.
    3. Requirements: Neutral atom configuration, oxidation state, removal rules.
    4. Components: Neutral config, oxidation state, removal rules (highest n first, then highest l).
    5. Sequential steps: (i) Write neutral atom config; (ii) Identify electrons to remove (oxidation state); (iii) Remove from highest n first; (iv) For same n, remove from highest l; (v) For transition metals: ns before (n-1)d; (vi) Write resulting ion config.
    6. Result: Ion configuration (e.g., Fe³⁺: [Ar]3d⁵).
    7. Significance: Predicts oxidation states, magnetic properties, complex formation, redox behaviour.

    Within this topic

    • (n+l) rule → filling order → configuration → block → properties. Complete chain from quantum numbers to chemical behaviour.
    • Pauli limits occupancy → period lengths.
    • Hund fills degenerate orbitals → unpaired electrons → magnetism.
    • Anomalies (Cr, Cu) from exchange energy → half/filled d stability.
    • Ion formation: remove highest n first → ns before (n-1)d.
    • Configuration → block → group → properties.

    To other units

    • Orbitals (previous topic): s, p, d orbitals are the boxes in box diagrams.
    • Periodic Table (next unit): Block (s, p, d) from configuration → position in table.

    • Redox: Oxidation states from ion configurations; transition metals have multiple states.
    • Coordination Chemistry: d-electron count determines geometry, magnetism, colour.
    • Spectroscopy: d-d transitions, charge transfer depend on dⁿ configuration.

    To real life

    • Periodic table layout: s-block (Groups 1-2), p-block (13-18), d-block (3-12) from configuration.
    • Catalysts: dⁿ configuration determines activity (Fe³⁺ d⁵, Cu²⁺ d⁹).
    • MRI contrast: Gd³⁺ f⁷ (7 unpaired) from configuration.
    • Batteries: Li⁺ (1s²), Co³⁺/Co⁴⁺ (d⁶/d⁵) in cathodes.
    • Pigments: dⁿ configuration determines colour (transition metal complexes).
    • Corrosion: Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ redox from d⁶/d⁵ configurations.

  • 10. Topic Mastery Check

    Important definitions

    1. Aufbau principle — electrons fill orbitals in increasing energy order (n+l rule).
    2. (n+l) rule — lower (n+l) fills first; equal (n+l) → lower n first.
    3. Pauli exclusion principle — no two electrons share all four quantum numbers; max 2 per orbital, opposite spins.
    4. Hund's rule — degenerate orbitals fill singly first with parallel spins; maximizes exchange energy.
    5. Exchange energy — stabilization from parallel spins in degenerate orbitals (K > 0).
    6. Electronic configuration — distribution of electrons in orbitals (e.g., 1s² 2s² 2p⁶...).
    7. Anomalous configuration — Cr: [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ (half-filled d⁵); Cu: [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ (filled d¹⁰).
    8. Valence electrons — electrons in outermost shell (highest n).
    9. Ion formation — remove highest n first, then highest l; for transition metals, ns before (n-1)d.
    10. Block classification — s: ns¹⁻²; p: np¹⁻⁶; d: (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ns⁰⁻².
    11. Spin-only magnetic moment — μ = √[n(n+2)] BM where n = unpaired electrons.

    Key concepts to remember

    • Filling order: 1s → 2s → 2p → 3s → 3p → 4s → 3d → 4p (up to Z=30).
    • (n+l) rule: 1s(1) < 2s(2) < 2p(3)=3s(3) → 2p first < 3s < 3p(4) < 4s(4)=3d(5) → 4s first < 3d < 4p(5).
    • Pauli: max 2 per orbital, opposite spins (mₛ = +½, -½).
    • Hund: degenerate orbitals fill singly first, parallel spins (maximizes exchange energy).
    • Cr: [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ (half-filled d⁵ stability); Cu: [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ (filled d¹⁰ stability).
    • Ion formation: remove highest n first; transition metals lose ns before (n-1)d.
    • Block: s (ns¹⁻²), p (np¹⁻⁶), d ((n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ns⁰⁻²).
    • Magnetic moment: μ = √[n(n+2)] BM; paramagnetic if n>0, diamagnetic if n=0.

    Important classifications

    • By block: s-block (Groups 1-2), p-block (13-18), d-block (3-12).
    • By configuration: s², s¹, p¹⁻⁶, d¹⁻¹⁰.
    • By magnetism: Paramagnetic (unpaired), Diamagnetic (paired).
    • By anomaly: Normal (follows (n+l)) vs Anomalous (Cr, Cu).

    Common conceptual confusions

    • 4s fills after 3d — NO, 4s (n+l=4) fills before 3d (n+l=5).
    • 3d fills before 4s — NO, 4s fills first (lower n for equal n+l).
    • Ion formation removes 3d before 4s — NO, removes 4s first (higher n).
    • Cr is [Ar]4s²3d⁴ — NO, it's [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ (half-filled d⁵).
    • Cu is [Ar]4s²3d⁹ — NO, it's [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ (filled d¹⁰).
    • Pauli means electrons can't be in same orbital — NO, they can if spins opposite.
    • Hund means electrons pair as soon as possible — NO, singly first, parallel spins.
    • Block from group number — NO, block from last filled subshell (configuration).
    • Magnetic moment formula is μ = n BM — NO, μ = √[n(n+2)] BM.

    PYQ Solutions

    Q: State Aufbau's principle, Pauli's exclusion principle and Hund's rule with one example each. (2 marks) Answer: Aufbau: electrons fill orbitals in increasing energy order (e.g., 1s before 2s). Pauli: no two electrons can have the same four quantum numbers; max 2 per orbital with opposite spins (e.g., 1s²). Hund: degenerate orbitals fill singly first with parallel spins (e.g., 2p³: ↑ ↑ ↑). Keywords: increasing energy, four quantum numbers, singly first parallel spins.

    Q: Apply the (n + l) rule to arrange 3d, 4s, 4p, 3p and 5s in order of filling. (2 marks) Answer: 3p (n+l=4) < 4s (4) < 3d (5) < 4p (5) < 5s (5). For equal (n+l), lower n first: 4s before 3d; 3d before 4p; 4p before 5s. Keywords: 3p=4, 4s=4, 3d=5, 4p=5, 5s=5; lower n first for ties.

    Q: Give the configurations of Z = 24, 26 and 29 and explain the anomalies. (3 marks) Answer: Z=24 (Cr): [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ (half-filled d⁵, exchange energy). Z=26 (Fe): [Ar]4s²3d⁶ (normal). Z=29 (Cu): [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ (filled d¹⁰, exchange energy). Keywords: Cr half-filled d⁵, Fe normal, Cu filled d¹⁰, exchange energy.

    Q: Write Fe, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ configurations and explain why Fe³⁺ is extra stable. (3 marks) Answer: Fe: [Ar]4s²3d⁶; Fe²⁺: [Ar]3d⁶ (lost 4s²); Fe³⁺: [Ar]3d⁵ (lost 4s² + 1 3d). Fe³⁺ is extra stable due to half-filled d⁵ configuration (maximum exchange energy, spherical symmetry). Keywords: Fe [Ar]4s²3d⁶, Fe²⁺ [Ar]3d⁶, Fe³⁺ [Ar]3d⁵, half-filled d⁵ stability.

    Q: How many unpaired electrons occur in Mn²⁺ and Cu²⁺? State the magnetic behaviour of each. (3 marks) Answer: Mn²⁺ (Z=25): [Ar]3d⁵ → 5 unpaired, paramagnetic, μ = √35 ≈ 5.92 BM. Cu²⁺ (Z=29): [Ar]3d⁹ → 1 unpaired, paramagnetic, μ = √3 ≈ 1.73 BM. Keywords: Mn²⁺ d⁵ 5 unpaired, Cu²⁺ d⁹ 1 unpaired, paramagnetic, magnetic moments.

Confusion

  • ❌ 4s fills after 3d. ✅ 4s (n+l=4) fills before 3d (n+l=5).
  • ❌ 3d fills before 4s. ✅ 4s fills first; lower n for equal n+l (4s vs 3p both n+l=4, but 3p fills first).
  • ❌ Ion formation removes 3d before 4s. ✅ Removes highest n first: 4s (n=4) before 3d (n=3).
  • ❌ Cr = [Ar]4s²3d⁴. ✅ Cr = [Ar]4s¹3d⁵ (half-filled d⁵ exchange energy).
  • ❌ Cu = [Ar]4s²3d⁹. ✅ Cu = [Ar]4s¹3d¹⁰ (filled d¹⁰ exchange energy).
  • ❌ Pauli exclusion means electrons can't share an orbital. ✅ They CAN share if spins are opposite (mₛ = +½ and -½).
  • ❌ Hund's rule means electrons pair as soon as possible. ✅ Singly first, parallel spins — pairing only after all degenerate orbitals have one electron.
  • ❌ Block determined by group number. ✅ Block determined by last filled subshell in configuration (s, p, d, f).
  • ❌ Magnetic moment formula is μ = n BM. ✅ μ = √[n(n+2)] BM (spin-only formula).
  • ❌ Anomalous configurations only for Cr and Cu. ✅ Others exist beyond Z=30 (Nb, Mo, Tc, Ru, Rh, Pd, Ag...).
