Notes

  • Focus of this tab

  • The discovery of the electron through cathode-ray experiments — the first subatomic particle, and the starting point of every atomic model. (It sits in this unit as the experimental foundation of electronic structure.)

  • The cathode-ray tube experiments

  • • A gas at low pressure (≈10⁻² atm) in a discharge tube with ≈10 000 V across the electrodes emits rays from the cathode that travel in straight lines (shadow of the Maltese cross), carry momentum (paddle-wheel rotation) and heat the target.
  • • Electric field: the rays bend toward the positive plate → negatively charged.
  • • Magnetic field: they bend in the direction expected for negative charges.
  • • The deflection is independent of the gas and cathode material → the particle is a universal constituent of all matter.

  • Thomson's measurements (1897)

  • Crossed E and B fields (velocity selector): undeflected when Eq = Bqv → v = E/B. Then switching off B gives the deflection, yielding e/m = 1.76 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹ — about 1837 times larger than the hydrogen ion's e/m. Since the charge is later shown to be the same unit charge, the particle must be ≈1837 times lighter than hydrogen: a new particle, the electron.

  • Millikan's oil-drop experiment (1909)

  • Balancing the electric force against the weight of a charged oil drop gave the charge of a single electron: e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C (quantised — all drops carry integer multiples of e). Combined with e/m, the electron mass is m = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg (0.000549 u).

  • Why this matters for the atomic models

  • Once atoms contain light negative electrons, something positive must balance them — the direct motivation for Thomson's plum-pudding model and then Rutherford's nucleus. The electron also completes the electronic-structure story: its arrangement in orbitals is what this unit's quantum numbers describe.

Confusion

  • ❌ Cathode rays are electromagnetic waves. ✅ They are streams of charged particles — waves are not deflected by E/B fields.
  • ❌ The cathode-ray properties depend on the gas used. ✅ They are independent of gas and electrode material, which is why the particle is universal.
  • ❌ Millikan measured e/m. ✅ He measured e alone; Thomson had already measured e/m.

Universal Facts

  • The electron is ≈1837 times lighter than a hydrogen atom — the lightest known charged particle.
  • Thomson received the 1906 Nobel Prize; Millikan the 1923 Nobel Prize.
  • Cathode rays travel in straight lines but are deflected by fields — the basis of the old CRT television.

Examples

  • Problem: An electron beam passes undeflected through E = 3.4 × 10⁴ V/m and B = 2 × 10⁻³ T. Find its speed. Solution: v = E/B = 1.7 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹.
