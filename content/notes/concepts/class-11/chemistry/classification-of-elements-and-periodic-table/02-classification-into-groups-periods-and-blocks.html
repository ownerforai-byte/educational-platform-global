Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Reading the Table Like a Map

    The long-form table is not a memory chart but a map of electronic configurations: every period is a shell being filled, every group is a family of outer configurations, and the four blocks are the four subshell types. Learn to translate position ⇄ configuration and the table predicts chemistry — valency, metallic character, ion formation — without memorising individual elements.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Periods — the horizontal story

    PeriodnElementsFilled subshellsType
    112 (H, He)1sShortest
    228 (Li → Ne)2s 2pShort
    338 (Na → Ar)3s 3pShort
    4418 (K → Kr)4s 3d 4pLong
    5518 (Rb → Xe)5s 4d 5pLong
    6632 (Cs → Rn)6s 4f 5d 6pLongest
    7732 (Fr → Og)7s 5f 6d 7pLongest (completed)

    Period number = highest principal quantum number n occupied.

    2.2 Groups — the vertical story

    • 18 groups. Same group ⇒ same number of valence (outer-shell) electrons ⇒ similar chemistry.
    • Group number rules:
    • s-block: group = number of outer s electrons (group 1: ns¹; group 2: ns²).
    • p-block: group = 10 + outer (s + p) electrons (group 13: ns²np¹ … group 18: ns²np⁶).
    • d-block: group = outer s electrons + 10 (Sc 4s²3d¹ → 3; Zn 4s²3d¹⁰ → 12).
    • f-block: placed as group 3 members of periods 6–7 (lanthanoids, actinoids).

    2.3 The four blocks

    BlockLast electron entersGroupsCharacterExamples
    sns1–2 (+He, H)Reactive metalsNa, Mg
    pnp13–18Metals + metalloids + non-metals + noble gasesAl, Si, Cl, Ne
    d(n−1)d3–12Transition metalsFe, Cu, Zn
    f(n−2)f3 (rows 6–7)Inner-transitionCe, U
    • Normal elements: s- and p-block (outer shell being filled).
    • Transition elements: d-block (penultimate d subshell being filled; all are metals, variable valency, coloured ions).
    • Inner-transition elements: f-block (antepenultimate f subshell; lanthanoids 4f, actinoids 5f).

    2.4 Diagonal relationships and special positions

    • H and He sit in period 1; H is placed in group 1 by convention (1s¹) though its chemistry is unique.
    • Diagonal pairs (Li–Mg, Be–Al, B–Si) resemble each other because their charge/size ratios match — a first-period effect.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Worked drill — position from configuration (the exam skill)

    Q: An element has Z = 26. Find its period, group, block and name. Fe: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁶.

    • Highest n = 4 → period 4.
    • Last electron enters 3d → d-block.
    • Group = outer s electrons + 10 = 2 + 10 = group 8 (older: VIII).
    • Name: iron — a transition metal.

    4.2 Why 4s, 3d, 4p order creates the long period

    In period 4 the filling sequence is 4s (2 e⁻) → 3d (10 e⁻) → 4p (6 e⁻): 18 electrons instead of period 3's 8. The ten d electrons belong to the penultimate shell (n = 3), so they add ten transition metals in the middle of the row — that is the structural difference between short and long periods.

    4.3 Group 17 in one line

    F, Cl, Br, I: all ns²np⁵ — seven valence electrons, one short of an octet → strongest electron gainers (halogens), all form −1 ions, all diatomic. The configuration is the chemistry.

    4.4 Locating Z = 19, 20, 31, 36, 55 (NEB 2024 — see topic 22 for the table)

    K: period 4 group 1 (s); Ca: period 4 group 2 (s); Ga: period 4 group 13 (p); Kr: period 4 group 18 (p); Cs: period 6 group 1 (s). Same drill every time.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Group of d-block elements: add 10 to the outer s electrons — Fe (4s²) is group 8, not 2. The "outer electrons only" rule belongs to s/p blocks.

  • Zn, Cd, Hg — transition or not? Strictly, a transition element needs a partly filled d subshell in the atom or a common ion; Zn²⁺/Cd²⁺/Hg²⁺ are d¹⁰, so many texts exclude them (CBSE/NEP convention lists them as exceptions).
  • He's block: He ends period 1 but its configuration is 1s² — placed with group 18 (noble gases) by property, though it is formally an s-orbital filling.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m): Name the blocks and state which subshell each fills. — s-block: ns (groups 1–2); p-block: np (13–18); d-block: (n−1)d (3–12, transition metals); f-block: (n−2)f (lanthanoids/actinoids, inner-transition).

  • NEB-style (2m): Why are d-block elements called transition elements? — They stand between the electropositive s-block and electronegative p-block, and their properties transition between the two: their penultimate d subshell is being filled, giving variable valency, coloured ions and catalytic behaviour.
  • NEB-style (3m): An element has configuration [Kr] 5s² 4d¹⁰ 5p². Determine its period, group, block and name. — Highest n = 5 → period 5; last electron in 5p → p-block; outer electrons 5s²5p² = 4 → group 14; the element is tin (Sn, Z = 50).

Confusion

  • Period ≠ group: period counts shells (highest n); group counts outer electrons. K and Cs share group 1 but sit in periods 4 and 6.
  • d-block group arithmetic: Sc = 4s²3d¹ → 2 + 10 = group 3; Mn = 4s²3d⁵ → group 7; Zn = 4s²3d¹⁰ → group 12. Adding s + d electrons gives wrong answers.

Practice

  • Why does period 6 contain 32 elements while period 3 contains only 8?
  • Classify Zn as transition or non-transition and justify both readings.
  • Why do Li and Mg resemble each other more than Li and Na do?

Universal Facts

  • The table's 18-group, 7-period shape is mandated by orbital capacities: 2 + 8 + 8 + 18 + 18 + 32 + 32 = 118 elements complete seven shells — Og (Z = 118) closed period 7 in 2016.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Name the four blocks of the periodic table, the subshell each fills, and one characteristic of each block. — s-block: fills ns (groups 1–2); reactive metals forming +1/+2 ions. p-block: fills np (13–18); metals, metalloids, non-metals and noble gases. d-block: fills (n−1)d (3–12); transition metals with variable valency and coloured ions. f-block: fills (n−2)f (lanthanoids and actinoids); inner-transition metals placed below the main table.
  • NEB-style (2m): How many elements are in each of the seven periods and why do periods 4 and 5 hold 18? — 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32. Periods 4–5 hold 18 because after the ns² pair, ten (n−1)d electrons fill before the six np electrons — adding a transition series of ten elements to the eight of a short period.
  • NEB-style (3m): An element has Z = 26. Write its configuration and determine its period, group, block and name. — Fe: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d⁶. Highest n = 4 → period 4; last electron enters 3d → d-block; group = s electrons + 10 = 2 + 10 = group 8; the element is iron, a transition metal.
  • NEB-style (2m): Distinguish normal and transition elements. — Normal (representative) elements: s- and p-block; their outer shell is being filled; fixed valency (= group number or 8 − group). Transition elements: d-block; the penultimate d subshell is being filled; variable valency, coloured ions, catalysis are characteristic.

Summary

The long-form table is a configuration map: 7 periods (2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32, 32 elements — the period number is the highest shell n being filled) and 18 groups (same outer configuration = same family: s-block groups 1–2, p-block 13–18 counted as 10 + outer electrons, d-block 3–12 counted as s electrons + 10). The four blocks are named for the subshell receiving the last electron: s (reactive metals), p (the full metal→non-metal sweep ending in noble gases), d (transition metals — variable valency, coloured ions) and f (inner-transition lanthanoids and actinoids). Long periods exist because ten (n−1)d electrons fill between ns and np. Every "locate the element" question — Fe (Z = 26) → period 4, group 8, d-block — is the same three-step read-off from the configuration.
