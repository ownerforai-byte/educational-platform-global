  • 1. Structural Architecture of the Modern Periodic Table (Bohr's Long Form)

  • • Based on Moseley's Modern Periodic Law: The physical and chemical properties of elements are periodic functions of their atomic numbers (nuclear charge, Z).
  • • Consists of 7 horizontal rows called Periods and 18 vertical columns called Groups (IUPAC 1 to 18).
  • • Period 1: 2 elements (H, He) — shortest period (fills 1s subshell).
  • • Periods 2 & 3: 8 elements each (Li–Ne, Na–Ar) — short periods (fill 2s, 2p and 3s, 3p subshells).
  • • Periods 4 & 5: 18 elements each (K–Kr, Rb–Xe) — long periods (fill 4s, 3d, 4p and 5s, 4d, 5p subshells).
  • • Period 6: 32 elements (Cs–Rn, including 14 Lanthanides from ₅₈Ce to ₇₁Lu) — very long period (fills 6s, 4f, 5d, 6p).
  • • Period 7: 32 elements (Fr–Og, including 14 Actinides from ₉₀Th to ₁₀₃Lr) — complete radioactive period (fills 7s, 5f, 6d, 7p).

  • 2. s-Block Elements (Groups 1 & 2)

  • • Definition: Elements in which the differentiating (last) electron enters the outermost s-orbital.
  • • General Valence Electronic Configuration: ns¹⁻² (Group 1 Alkali metals: ns¹; Group 2 Alkaline earth metals: ns²; where n = 2 to 7).
  • • Group 1 (Alkali Metals): ₃Li, ₁₁Na, ₁₉K, ₃₇Rb, ₅₅Cs, ₈₇Fr. Soft, silvery-white metals, highly electropositive, lowest ionization enthalpies in their periods.
  • • Group 2 (Alkaline Earth Metals): ₄Be, ₁₂Mg, ₂₀Ca, ₃₈Sr, ₅₆Ba, ₈₈Ra. Harder than alkali metals, +2 fixed oxidation state, basic oxides (except amphoteric BeO).
  • • Key Physical Characteristics: Low melting and boiling points due to weak metallic bonding (only 1 or 2 valence electrons in bonding sea). Densities are very low (Li, Na, K float on or displace water).
  • • Flame Test: Readily excited s-electrons emit signature colors: Li (Crimson), Na (Golden Yellow), K (Lilac/Violet), Rb (Red-Violet), Cs (Sky Blue), Ca (Brick Red), Sr (Crimson), Ba (Apple Green). Note: Be and Mg do NOT give flame coloration because their electrons are held tightly by high effective nuclear charge.
  • • Chemical Properties: Extremely strong reducing agents (standard reduction potentials: Li is most negative at -3.04 V due to exceptionally high hydration enthalpy: ΔH_hyd of Li⁺). React vigorously with water to liberate H₂: 2M + 2H₂O → 2M⁺ + 2OH⁻ + H₂↑.
  • • Ammoniated Electrons: Dissolve in anhydrous liquid ammonia to yield deep blue, electrically conductive, paramagnetic solutions: M + (x+y)NH₃ → [M(NH₃)_x]⁺ + [e(NH₃)_y]⁻.
  • • Diagonal Relationship: Li resembles Mg, and Be resembles Al due to similar ionic charge/radius ratio (polarizing power) and nearly identical electronegativities.

  • 3. p-Block Elements (Groups 13 to 18)

  • • Definition: Elements in which the differentiating electron enters any of the three degenerate outermost p-orbitals.
  • • General Valence Electronic Configuration: ns² np¹⁻⁶ (He: 1s² is placed in Group 18 due to its filled shell noble gas behavior).
  • • Families: Group 13 (Boron family: ns² np¹), Group 14 (Carbon family: ns² np²), Group 15 (Pnictogens: ns² np³), Group 16 (Chalcogens: ns² np⁴), Group 17 (Halogens: ns² np⁵), Group 18 (Noble/Inert Gases: ns² np⁶).
  • • Uniqueness: Only block containing all three categories of matter: Metals (e.g., Al, Ga, In, Tl, Sn, Pb, Bi), Metalloids (B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po), and Non-metals (C, N, O, F, Ne, P, S, Cl, Ar, Se, Br, Kr, I, Xe, Rn).
  • • Inert Pair Effect: Reluctance of the valence s-electrons to participate in chemical bonding in heavier elements of post-transition p-block elements due to ineffective shielding of intervening d and f electrons. Consequence: Lower oxidation states become progressively more stable down the group: Tl⁺ > Tl³⁺, Pb²⁺ > Pb⁴⁺, Bi³⁺ > Bi⁵⁺. Hence, PbO₂ and PbCl₄ act as strong oxidizing agents.
  • • Catenation & Multiple Bonding: Carbon shows the highest catenation power due to strong C–C single bond strength (348 kJ/mol). Second-row p-elements readily form stable pπ–pπ multiple bonds (C=C, C≡C, N≡N, C=O), whereas heavier analogues form weaker dπ–pπ or dπ–dπ bonds.
  • • Anomalous Behavior of 2nd Period Elements (B, C, N, O, F): Small atomic radii, high electronegativity, absence of vacant d-orbitals in valence shell (maximum covalency strictly limited to 4, cannot form hypervalent species like SF₆ or PCl₅).

  • 4. d-Block Elements (Transition Metals, Groups 3 to 12)

  • • Definition: Elements in which the differentiating electron enters the penultimate (n-1)d subshell.
  • • General Valence Electronic Configuration: (n-1)d¹⁻¹⁰ ns¹⁻² (where n = 4, 5, 6, 7).
  • • Four Transition Series: 3d-series (₂₁Sc to ₃₀Zn), 4d-series (₃₉Y to ₄₈Cd), 5d-series (₅₇La, ₇₂Hf to ₈₀Hg), 6d-series (incomplete/actinoid-linked).
  • • True vs Non-Typical Transition Elements: A transition element is defined as an element having a partially filled d-orbital in its elemental ground state or in any of its common oxidation states. Zinc (₃₀Zn), Cadmium (₄₈Cd), and Mercury (₈₀Hg) have completely filled d-orbitals in elemental ground state (d¹⁰ s²) and in their +2 oxidation state (d¹⁰). Hence, they are non-typical or pseudo-transition elements.
  • • Variable Oxidation States: Due to minimal energy difference between (n-1)d and ns electrons, both sets participate in bonding. Manganese (Mn) exhibits maximum oxidation states in 3d series from +2 to +7 (e.g., KMnO₄). Osmium (Os) and Ruthenium (Ru) reach +8 (OsO₄).
  • • Color of Transition Metal Ions: Due to d-d electronic transitions. Degenerate d-orbitals split in presence of ligand fields into lower t₂g and upper eg levels. Absorption of visible light promotes electrons: Cu²⁺ (3d⁹, blue), Fe³⁺ (3d⁵, yellow/brown), Cr³⁺ (3d³, green), Ni²⁺ (3d⁸, green). Ions with d⁰ (Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺) or d¹⁰ (Zn²⁺, Cu⁺, Ag⁺) are colorless.
  • • Magnetic Properties: Due to unpaired electrons. Magnetic moment calculated via spin-only formula: μ = √(n(n + 2)) Bohr Magnetons (BM), where n = number of unpaired electrons. Max in 3d is Fe³⁺ / Mn²⁺ (n = 5, μ = 5.92 BM).
  • • Catalytic Properties: High surface area and ability to adopt multiple oxidation states to form unstable reaction intermediates (e.g., Finely divided Fe in Haber process, V₂O₅ in Contact process for H₂SO₄, Ni in alkene hydrogenation, Pt in Ostwald process).
  • • Interstitial Compounds & Alloys: Small non-metal atoms (H, C, N, B) lodge into interstitial voids of metallic lattices (e.g., Steel, TiC, Fe₃H), imparting extreme hardness, high melting points, and chemical inertness.

  • 5. f-Block Elements (Inner Transition Elements)

  • • Definition: Elements in which the differentiating electron enters the anti-penultimate (n-2)f subshell.
  • • General Valence Electronic Configuration: (n-2)f¹⁻¹⁴ (n-1)d⁰⁻¹ ns² (where n = 6 for 4f Lanthanides, n = 7 for 5f Actinides).
  • • Lanthanide Series (4f): 14 elements from Cerium (₅₈Ce) to Lutetium (₇₁Lu). Preceded by Lanthanum (₅₇La).
  • • Actinide Series (5f): 14 elements from Thorium (₉₀Th) to Lawrencium (₁₀₃Lr). Preceded by Actinium (₈₉Ac).
  • • Oxidation States: +3 is the universal, predominant, and most thermodynamically stable oxidation state for all Lanthanides. Some show +2 or +4 when achieving f⁰, f⁷, or f¹⁴ stable subshells (e.g., Ce⁴⁺ is 4f⁰, Eu²⁺ is 4f⁷, Yb²⁺ is 4f¹⁴). Actinides show wider range (+3, +4, +5, +6, +7) because 5f, 6d, and 7s have comparable energy levels.
  • • Lanthanide Contraction (Crucial NEB Exam Concept): Steady and progressive decrease in atomic and ionic radii (Ln³⁺) from La to Lu. Cause: Highly diffuse shape and poor screening/shielding power of 4f electrons, allowing nuclear charge (Z) to pull the outer 6s shell inward.
  • • Consequences of Lanthanide Contraction:
    1. Identical radii of 4d and 5d pairs: Zr (160 pm) ≈ Hf (159 pm), Nb (146 pm) ≈ Ta (146 pm), Mo ≈ W. They occur together in nature and are extremely difficult to separate chemically.
    1. Decrease in basic strength of hydroxides: As size shrinks from La³⁺ to Lu³⁺, covalent character of Ln–OH bond increases (Fajans' Rule). Thus, La(OH)₃ is the most basic, and Lu(OH)₃ is the least basic (most amphoteric).
    1. Gradual increase in ionization energy, electronegativity, and electrode potential across the series.
  • • Actinides Character: All are radioactive. Beyond Uranium (Z > 92) are synthetic man-made transuranic elements.

  • 6. Special High-Yield Classes of Elements in NEB Syllabus

  • • Coinage Metals (Group 11 / IB): Copper (₂₉Cu), Silver (₄₇Ag), Gold (₇₉Au).
    • Possess anomalous ground state configuration: (n-1)d¹⁰ ns¹ (extra stability of fully filled d-subshell).
    • Chemically inert, high positive reduction potentials (Cu: +0.34 V, Ag: +0.80 V, Au: +1.50 V). Resist oxidation by atmospheric O₂ and non-oxidizing acids (HCl, dilute H₂SO₄).
    • Dissolve in oxidizing acids: Cu + 4HNO₃ (conc) → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂↑ + 2H₂O.
    • Gold dissolves only in Aqua Regia (3 parts conc. HCl : 1 part conc. HNO₃): Au + HNO₃ + 4HCl → H[AuCl₄] + NO↑ + 2H₂O.
    • Unmatched electrical and thermal conductivity: Ag > Cu > Au.
    • Form stable complexes: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ (Tollens' reagent for aldehyde detection), [Cu(NH₃)₄]²⁺ (Schweizer's reagent for dissolving cellulose).
  • • Volatile Metals (Group 12 / IIB): Zinc (₃₀Zn), Cadmium (₄₈Cd), Mercury (₈₀Hg).
    • Electronic configuration: (n-1)d¹⁰ ns². Completely filled d-orbitals in both atomic and ionic (+2) states.
    • Melting & Boiling points: Unusually low because paired electrons cannot participate in metallic lattice bonding. Hg is the only liquid metal at standard room temperature (MP: -38.8°C; BP: 356.7°C). Relativistic contraction pulls 6s electrons close to the nucleus, rendering them inert.
    • Distinct from typical transition metals: Show only +2 oxidation state (never variable valency, except Hg which forms mercurous dimer [Hg–Hg]²⁺). Their compounds (ZnSO₄, CdCl₂, HgCl₂) are diamagnetic and white/colorless (no d-d transitions).
    • Amalgams: Mercury forms liquid or solid alloys called amalgams with nearly all metals (e.g., Sodium amalgam Na-Hg, Zinc amalgam Zn-Hg used in Clemmensen reduction). Exceptions: Iron (Fe), Platinum (Pt), and Tungsten (W) do not form amalgams and are used as storage containers.

  • 7. Comprehensive Classification: Metals vs Non-Metals vs Metalloids

  • • Metals (~78% of elements): Electropositive elements with low ionization enthalpies (1–3 valence electrons). Form giant metallic crystal lattices with delocalized electron gas. Form basic oxides (e.g., Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH). Good conductors, malleable, ductile, high sonority.
  • • Non-Metals (18 elements total): Electronegative elements with high ionization enthalpies and electron affinities (4–7 valence electrons, plus noble gases). High electronegativity. Form covalent or molecular lattices. Form acidic oxides (SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄) or neutral oxides (CO, NO, N₂O). Electrical insulators (except graphite).
  • • Metalloids (Semimetals — B, Si, Ge, As, Sb, Te, Po): Borderline elements situated along the diagonal dividing line between metals and non-metals in the p-block.
    • Possess intermediate electrical conductivity that increases with rising temperature (semiconductors, negative temperature coefficient of resistance).
    • Form amphoteric or weakly acidic oxides (e.g., As₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AsCl₃ + 3H₂O; As₂O₃ + 6NaOH → 2Na₃AsO₃ + 3H₂O).
    • Doping: Silicon and Germanium are doped with Group 13 elements (B, Ga) to create p-type semiconductors, or Group 15 elements (P, As) to create n-type semiconductors, forming p-n junctions for solar cells and microprocessors.

Confusion

  • ❌ All transition elements are d-block, and all d-block elements are transition elements. ✅ All transition elements belong to d-block, but Zn, Cd, and Hg are d-block elements that are NOT transition elements because their d-subshell is completely filled (d¹⁰) in ground state and common +2 oxidation state.
  • ❌ Hydrogen is an alkali metal because it sits in Group 1. ✅ Hydrogen has an 1s¹ configuration but is a non-metal with high ionization enthalpy (1312 kJ/mol), forming covalent bonds and H⁻ hydride ions.
  • ❌ Helium belongs to p-block because it sits in Group 18. ✅ Helium has the valence configuration 1s² (s-block), but is placed in Group 18 above p-block noble gases due to its completely filled valence shell and inert noble chemical properties.
  • ❌ Fluorine has the highest electron gain enthalpy (electron affinity) in the periodic table. ✅ Chlorine (Cl) has higher negative electron gain enthalpy than Fluorine (-349 kJ/mol vs -328 kJ/mol) because Fluorine's 2p subshell is very small and compact, causing strong inter-electronic repulsions that hinder incoming electrons.
  • ❌ Ionization enthalpy always increases monotonically across a period from left to right. ✅ Exceptions exist: Beryllium (1s² 2s²) has higher IE₁ than Boron (1s² 2s² 2p¹) because 2s electrons penetrate closer to the nucleus and are fully paired; Nitrogen (2s² 2p³) has higher IE₁ than Oxygen (2s² 2p⁴) due to the extra stability of half-filled p-orbitals.
  • ❌ Lanthanum (₅₇La) and Actinium (₈₉Ac) are f-block elements. ✅ La and Ac are d-block elements (outer configuration 5d¹ 6s² and 6d¹ 7s²). The f-block begins with Cerium (₅₈Ce) and Thorium (₉₀Th).
  • ❌ Lanthanide contraction means the atoms expand down the group. ✅ Contraction means atomic/ionic radii decrease steadily across the 4f series from Ce to Lu due to poor shielding by diffuse 4f orbitals.
  • ❌ Copper, Silver, and Gold are non-transition metals because their ground state is d¹⁰. ✅ They are transition metals because their common oxidation states have partially filled d-orbitals: Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹, Au³⁺ is 5d⁸.
  • ❌ Graphite is an insulator like other non-metals. ✅ Graphite conducts electricity along its hexagonal sheets due to free delocalized π-electrons resulting from sp² hybridization.
  • ❌ All oxides of metals are basic. ✅ Most are basic, but metal oxides with high oxidation states are acidic (e.g., CrO₃ with Cr⁶⁺, Mn₂O₇ with Mn⁷⁺), and some are amphoteric (Al₂O₃, ZnO, PbO, SnO).
  • ❌ Coinage metals include Zinc, Cadmium, and Mercury. ✅ Coinage metals are strictly Group 11 (Cu, Ag, Au). Group 12 (Zn, Cd, Hg) are Volatile Metals.
  • ❌ Mercury dissolves all metals to form amalgams. ✅ Mercury does NOT amalgamate with Iron (Fe), Platinum (Pt), or Tungsten (W); therefore, mercury is safely shipped and stored in iron flasks.

Universal Facts

  • Atomic number (Z), representing nuclear charge, uniquely dictates chemical identity and electron configurations.
  • Half-filled (p³, d⁵, f⁷) and fully filled (p⁶, d¹⁰, f¹⁴) subshells possess maximum symmetry and exchange energy stability.
  • The periodic table represents the direct geometric visualization of quantum mechanical energy level solutions (Aufbau principle and Hund's rule).
  • Poor shielding ability follows the order: s > p > d > f; this fundamental rule dictates both the inert pair effect and lanthanide contraction.
