Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — One Contest, Six Trends

    Atomic radius, ionic radius, ionisation energy, electron affinity, electronegativity and metallic character are not six facts to memorise — they are six readings of one contest: effective nuclear charge (Z_eff) pulling electrons in versus shells and shielding pushing them out. Learn the contest and each trend's period/group direction — and the famous irregularities NEB 2022 asked — follow as derivations.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Atomic radius

    • Definition problem: an electron cloud has no edge, so radius is defined operationally:
    • Covalent radius: half the internuclear distance between two bonded identical atoms (Cl–Cl → 99 pm).
    • van der Waals radius: half the closest distance between non-bonded atoms (larger than covalent).
    • Metallic radius: half the nearest-neighbour distance in a metal lattice.
    • Across a period: Z_eff rises, same shell pulled in → radius decreases (Li 152 pm → F 71 pm; Na 186 → Cl 99). Noble gases (van der Waals radii) break the run.
    • Down a group: a new shell opens, n grows → radius increases (Li 152 → Na 186 → K 231 pm).

    2.2 Ionic radius

    • Cation < parent atom: losing the outer shell and raising Z_eff per remaining electron (Na 186 pm → Na⁺ 95 pm).
    • Anion > parent atom: added electrons increase repulsion, Z_eff per electron drops (Cl 99 pm → Cl⁻ 181 pm).
    • Isoelectronic series (same electron count): radius falls as Z rises — N³⁻ > O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺ (all 10 e⁻).

    2.3 Ionisation energy (IE / ionisation enthalpy)

    • IE₁: energy to remove the most loosely bound electron from an isolated gaseous atom: M(g) → M⁺(g) + e⁻.
    • Across a period: Z_eff ↑ → IE increases (Na 496 → Ar 1520 kJ mol⁻¹), with dips at Be→B and N→O.
    • Down a group: n ↑, shielding ↑ → IE decreases (Li 520 → Cs 376).
    • Successive IEs always rise: IE₁ < IE₂ < IE₃ — and a huge jump when the shell is emptied (Na: IE₂ ≫ IE₁, proving Na⁺ = [Ne]).

    2.4 Electron affinity (EA) and electronegativity (EN)

    • EA: energy released when an isolated gaseous atom gains an electron: Cl(g) + e⁻ → Cl⁻(g), −349 kJ mol⁻¹. Across a period EA generally increases (more negative); down a group it decreases. Nobel-gas EAs are positive (no tendency to gain).
    • EN: the tendency of a bonded atom to attract the shared electron pair (Pauling scale, F = 4.0 highest). Same directions as EA but a property of bonded atoms, unitless.
    • EA vs EN: EA is energy (measured, isolated atom); EN is relative tendency (scaled, bonded atom).

    2.5 Metallic character

    • Ease of losing electrons. Across a period: decreases (Na strongly metallic → Cl non-metallic); down a group: increases (Li → Cs, Cs is the most reactive metal). Metalloids line the B–Si–Ge–As–Sb–Te staircase.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 NEB 2022 (3m, verified PYQ) — the two irregularities

    (a) IE₁(Mg) > IE₁(Al) although Mg lies left of Al. Mg: [Ne] 3s²; Al: [Ne] 3s² 3p¹. Mg's first electron comes from a completely filled, stable 3s² subshell; Al's comes from the higher-energy, shielded 3p electron, which is easier to remove. Values: Mg 738 vs Al 578 kJ mol⁻¹. The Be→B dip has the same cause (2s² vs 2p¹).

    (b) Atomic radius of Na is greater than that of Cl. Na: [Ne] 3s¹, Z_eff ≈ +1; Cl: [Ne] 3s² 3p⁵, Z_eff ≈ +7. Same shell (n = 3), but Cl's far stronger net pull drags it inward: Na 186 pm vs Cl 99 pm. Radius is set by Z_eff, not by the electron count.

    4.2 The other two standard dips (complete the picture)

    • Be → B: B's 2p¹ electron is higher in energy and better shielded than Be's 2s² pair → IE falls.
    • N → O: N is 2p³ (half-filled, exchange-stabilised); O's fourth p electron must pair and suffers repulsion → IE falls.
    • Group 18 anomaly in EA: noble gases refuse electrons (filled shells), so their EA is positive (energy must be supplied) — the extreme end of the trend.

    4.3 Successive ionisation energies read the configuration

    Na (1s²2s²2p⁶3s¹): IE₁ = 496, IE₂ = 4562, IE₃ = 6912 kJ mol⁻¹ — the 9× jump at IE₂ marks the crossing from the 3rd shell into the neon core. Magnesium's jump comes at IE₃. This is how IE data prove shell structure — a favourite application question.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Radius dip exceptions: noble-gas radii are van der Waals radii — larger than the neighbouring halogen's covalent radius; do not read them as a trend break in bonding radius.

  • IE dips are about subshell stability: Be→B is s² vs p¹ (energy level); N→O is half-filled vs paired (repulsion). Two different causes — quote the right one for the right pair.
  • EA vs EN: EA is measurable energy for an isolated atom; EN is a relative tendency of a bonded atom. Cl has the most negative EA but F has the highest EN.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m): Why is IE₂ of sodium enormously higher than IE₁? — After IE₁, Na⁺ is [Ne] (1s²2s²2p⁶); IE₂ must remove an electron from the filled inner shell against a much higher Z_eff — 4562 vs 496 kJ mol⁻¹. The jump proves the 3s electron was the only valence electron.

  • NEB-style (2m): Arrange O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ in decreasing size and justify. — All are 10-electron (isoelectronic); radius falls as nuclear charge rises: O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺ (Z = 8, 9, 11, 12, 13 pulling the same cloud).
  • NEB-style (2m): Why is Cs the most reactive metal and F the most reactive non-metal? — Cs: largest alkali metal, lowest IE (376 kJ mol⁻¹) — loses its electron most easily. F: smallest halogen, highest EN (4.0) and very negative EA — gains an electron most avidly. The two extremes meet in CsF, the most ionic of fluorides.

Six trends, one contest. Across a period Z_eff climbs (same-shell shielding is poor): radii shrink (Na 186 → Cl 99 pm), ionisation energy climbs with two dips — Be→B (2s² vs 2p¹) and N→O (half-filled vs pairing) — electron affinity and electronegativity rise (Cl most negative EA, F highest EN), and metallic character falls. Down a group each new shell wins the contest: radii grow, IE falls, metals get more reactive (Cs the practical extreme). Ionic sizes obey Z_eff per electron: cations shrink below their atoms (Na⁺ 95 pm), anions swell above theirs (Cl⁻ 181 pm), and isoelectronic ions shrink as Z rises (O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺). NEB 2022's irregularities — Mg > Al in IE and Na > Cl in radius — are not exceptions to the trends but their sharpest demonstrations: subshell stability and Z_eff decide everything.

Ionic radius

  • Higher charge on the same ion means smaller radius (Fe3+ < Fe2+)

Ionisation energy

Points
  • Energy to remove the most loosely bound electron from a gaseous atom

Electron affinity and electronegativity

Points
  • Electron affinity is the energy change on adding an electron; more negative across a period
  • Electronegativity is the tendency to attract a shared pair; Pauling scale
  • Both increase across a period and decrease down a group

Metallic character

Points
  • Decreases across a period, increases down a group
  • Metals lose electrons readily; non-metals gain them
  • Metalloids show intermediate behaviour
