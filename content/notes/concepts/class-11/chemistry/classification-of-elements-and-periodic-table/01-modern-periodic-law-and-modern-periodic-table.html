Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — From Atomic Mass to Atomic Number

    Mendeleev arranged elements by atomic mass and left brilliant gaps — but mass-based ordering put tellurium (127.6) before iodine (126.9) and stacked argon against potassium wrongly. Moseley's X-ray work (1913) gave each element a true serial number — the atomic number Z — and the modern periodic law re-founded the table on it. NEB 2021 asked the Mendeleev-vs-modern contrast directly (3m); it remains the standard opener for this unit.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The Modern Periodic Law

    "The physical and chemical properties of the elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers."

    • Atomic number Z = number of protons = the element's true identity.
    • Properties repeat because electronic configurations repeat: each new shell begins a new period, and elements with the same outer-shell configuration land in the same group.
    • The periodicity is in valence-shell electron count, not in mass.

    2.2 The long form of the periodic table

    • 18 groups (vertical columns), 7 periods (horizontal rows).
    • Groups 1–2: s-block; 3–12: d-block; 13–18: p-block; lanthanoids/actinoids: f-block (placed separately).
    • A period number = the shell (n) being filled; a group number reflects the outer-shell configuration.
    • Elements are arranged in order of increasing Z, left to right.

    2.3 Why atomic number, not atomic mass

    • Z fixes the electron count and hence the configuration — the real cause of chemical behaviour.
    • Mass anomalies vanish: Te (Z = 52) correctly precedes I (Z = 53) despite Te's larger mass; Ar (Z = 18) correctly precedes K (Z = 19).
    • Isotopes of an element (different masses) occupy one place — mass ordering could not handle them.

    4.2 NEB 2024 (3m, verified PYQ) — locate Z = 19, 20, 31, 36, 55

    Work from the configuration, not memory:

    ZElementConfigurationPeriodGroupBlock
    19K[Ar] 4s¹41s
    20Ca[Ar] 4s²42s
    31Ga[Ar] 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p¹413p
    36Kr[Ar] 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p⁶418p
    55Cs[Xe] 6s¹61s

    Method: write the configuration → period = highest n → group = number of outer electrons (s/p block) or 10 + outer electrons (d block).

    4.3 How periodicity emerges

    Across a period the outer-shell count climbs 1 → 8 while Z grows: nuclear pull strengthens, radii shrink, ionisation energy climbs, metallic character falls. Down a group a new shell opens: radii jump, ionisation energy falls, metallic character rises. Every property is this one tug-of-war — Z↑ pulling vs n↑ shielding — read at different positions. That is the whole logic of the unit.

    4.4 Advantages of the long form

    1. Position strictly by Z — no anomalies.
    2. Separates metals (left/centre), non-metals (right), metalloids (staircase).
    3. d-block and f-block get their own homes; the table maps directly onto electronic configuration.
    4. Horizontal similarity (period trends) becomes visible alongside vertical (group) similarity.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Periodic function of Z — not of mass: quoting the modern law with "atomic weight" is an automatic zero; the one word that matters is atomic number.

  • Period vs group: period = row = shell being filled (highest n); group = column = outer configuration. Z = 19 and Z = 55 share group 1 but sit in different periods.
  • d-block group counting: for d-block elements group = outer s electrons + 10 (Sc: 4s²3d¹ → group 3; Zn: 4s²3d¹⁰ → group 12).

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m): Why do properties of elements repeat periodically? — Because electronic configurations repeat: each period starts filling a new shell, and elements with the same outer-shell configuration (same valence count) show the same chemistry — the cause Mendeleev's law could not supply.

  • NEB-style (2m): Where do ¹H, ²H and ³H sit in the modern table and why? — All three (protium, deuterium, tritium) occupy the single position of hydrogen (Z = 1): the modern law orders by Z, and isotopes differ in mass number, not Z.
  • NEB-style (2m): An element has configuration [Ar] 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p². Give its period, group and block. — Highest n = 4 → period 4; six outer electrons (4s² 4p²) → group 14; last electron enters p → p-block. (The element is Ge.)

Confusion

  • Z vs A: atomic number (protons) orders the table; atomic mass number (protons + neutrons) does not — that was Mendeleev's error.
  • Group of d-block elements: count s electrons + 10, not s + d electrons (Sc: 4s²3d¹ is group 3, not 3).

  • Why did mass-based ordering fail for Ar–K while Z-based ordering succeeds?
  • An element ends 5s² 4d¹⁰ 5p⁵ — name its period, group and block without looking it up.
  • Why is periodicity better explained by outer-shell configuration than by atomic mass?

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2021 (3m): How does the Modern Periodic Law differ from Mendeleev's Periodic Law? State any three points of difference. — (1) Basis: Mendeleev orders by atomic mass; the modern law orders by atomic number. (2) Anomalies: mass ordering forced Te (127.6) before I (126.9) wrongly and mispaired Ar–K; Z-ordering places all elements correctly. (3) Isotopes: mass law has no place for them; the modern law gives all isotopes of an element one position (same Z). (Also: the modern law explains the cause of periodicity — repeating outer electronic configurations — which Mendeleev's could not.)
  • NEB 2024 (3m): Identify the elements with atomic numbers 19, 20, 31, 36 and 55, and state the period and group of each in the long form of the periodic table. — 19: K, [Ar]4s¹ → period 4, group 1. 20: Ca, [Ar]4s² → period 4, group 2. 31: Ga, [Ar]4s²3d¹⁰4p¹ → period 4, group 13. 36: Kr, [Ar]4s²3d¹⁰4p⁶ → period 4, group 18. 55: Cs, [Xe]6s¹ → period 6, group 1.
  • NEB-style (2m): State the Modern Periodic Law and name the scientist whose work established it. — "Physical and chemical properties of the elements are a periodic function of their atomic numbers" — established by H. G. J. Moseley (1913) through X-ray spectral studies.
  • NEB-style (2m): An element has the configuration [Ar] 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p³. Give its period, group, block and name. — Highest n = 4 → period 4; last electron in p → p-block; outer electrons 4s²4p³ = 5 → group 15; the element is arsenic (As, Z = 33).

Summary

The modern periodic law re-founds Mendeleev's table on atomic number: properties are a periodic function of Z (Moseley, 1913), because electronic configurations — the true cause of chemical behaviour — repeat at regular intervals of Z. Every mass-based anomaly dissolves: Te (Z = 52) precedes I (Z = 53), Ar (18) precedes K (19), and all isotopes share one position. The long form arranges elements by increasing Z into 7 periods and 18 groups in s/p/d/f blocks; period = the highest shell n being filled, group = outer electrons (plus 10 for the d-block). NEB 2021's law contrast (mass→Z, anomalies→none, isotopes→one place) and NEB 2024's location drill (Z = 19, 20, 31, 36, 55 → K, Ca, Ga, Kr, Cs) are the two template questions; the method is always the same — write the configuration, read off the position.
