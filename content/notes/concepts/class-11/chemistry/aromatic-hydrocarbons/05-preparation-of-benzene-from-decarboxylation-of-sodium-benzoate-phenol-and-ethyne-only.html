Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Making Benzene: Three Named Routes

    The syllabus names three laboratory preparations of benzene: decarboxylation of sodium benzoate with soda lime, distillation of phenol with zinc dust, and trimerisation of ethyne through a red-hot iron tube. Each is a one-line equation with one non-negotiable condition — the conditions are the marks.

  • 2. Theorem Mirror

    • Decarboxylation: C₆H₅COONa + NaOH(CaO, heat) → C₆H₆ + Na₂CO₃ — soda lime (NaOH + CaO) decarboxylates the salt; the ring carbon keeps its hydrogen.
    • From phenol: C₆H₅OH + Zn (heat) → C₆H₆ + ZnO — zinc dust reduces the C–O bond; distillation drives the benzene over.
    • From ethyne: 3C₂H₂ →(red-hot Fe tube, ~873 K) C₆H₆ — three alkynes cyclise; the reaction is Berthelot's synthesis.
    • All three preserve the six-carbon ring or build it: decarboxylation and Zn-reduction modify a pre-existing ring; trimerisation assembles it from three C₂ units.

  • 3. Deep Conceptual — What Each Route Teaches About the Ring

    • Decarboxylation is a proton-for-carboxylate swap: soda lime's NaOH abstracts the carboxyl proton machinery and the CO₂ leaves as carbonate; the aryl carbon receives H. The reaction is the standard exit route from any aromatic acid — the ring itself is untouched, which is why the method generalises (toluene from sodium o/m/p-methylbenzoate, etc.).
    • The Zn route is bond-specific reduction: Zn removes oxygen as ZnO and replaces the C–O with C–H. Phenol → benzene is the reverse of benzene's industrial hydroxylation — the same C–O bond viewed from both directions; the high temperature (red heat) reflects the C–O bond's strength in an aryl ether/phenol context.
    • Trimerisation builds the aromatic ring from acetylene's strain: ethyne is high in energy (triple bond); cyclising three of them into the aromatic sextet releases that stored energy into the new ring's stabilisation — the thermodynamic push is partly the aromaticity itself. The red-hot tube supplies the activation; no catalyst is named in the syllabus beyond the hot iron surface.

  • 4. Deep Conceptual — Conditions Are Part of the Equation

    • Each route's condition is diagnostic, not decorative: soda lime (not NaOH alone — CaO raises the fusion temperature and absorbs water); zinc dust (not Zn granules — surface area); red-hot iron tube ~873 K (not a flame — the temperature is the reagent). In the exam, an equation without its condition is half an answer.
    • The three routes span three logic families: functional-group removal (−CO₂⁻ → −H), functional-group reduction (−OH → −H), and ring construction (3 × C₂ → C₆). Recognising which family a question invokes tells you which condition to quote.

  • 5. Worked Example Types

    • Type A — write the preparation: equation + condition, for each of the three routes.
    • Type B — condition justification: why soda lime, why zinc dust, why red heat.
    • Type C — route selection: given a starting material, choose and justify the route.
    • Type D — generalisation: apply decarboxylation to substituted benzoates.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "NaOH alone decarboxylates": the soda-lime mixture (NaOH + CaO) is required — CaO's role is fusion-temperature and water absorption. "Ethyne trimerisation needs a catalyst like Ni": the syllabus condition is the red-hot iron tube (~873 K); quoting organometallic catalysts imports outside chemistry.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (6m): Prepare benzene in the laboratory from (a) sodium benzoate and (b) phenol, writing balanced equations with conditions. — (a) Soda-lime decarboxylation: heat sodium benzoate strongly with soda lime (NaOH + CaO): C₆H₅COONa + NaOH --(CaO, Δ)--> C₆H₆ + Na₂CO₃. The carboxylate group leaves as carbonate and the ring carbon takes a hydrogen. (b) Zinc-dust reduction: distil phenol with zinc dust: C₆H₅OH + Zn --(Δ)--> C₆H₆ + ZnO. Zinc removes the phenolic oxygen as ZnO; the C–O bond is replaced by C–H. Conditions quoted: strong heat with the soda-lime mixture for (a); red heat with excess zinc dust for (b). Deep point: the two routes are functionally opposite operations on the ring's substituent — one deletes a carboxylate, one deletes an oxygen — and both leave the aromatic sextet untouched; the ring's stability is why such drastic conditions (fusion, red heat) still give clean products.
  • NEB-style (5m): Describe the preparation of benzene from ethyne, giving the equation and conditions, and explain the thermodynamic driving force. — Reaction: pass ethyne through a red-hot iron tube at about 873 K: 3C₂H₂ --(red-hot Fe, 873 K)--> C₆H₆ (Berthelot's synthesis). Driving force: three C≡C triple bonds (high-energy, each ~835 kJ/mol of bond energy with two π components) are converted into the aromatic ring's delocalised sextet — the product sits far lower in energy because the cyclic conjugation gains the ~150 kJ/mol resonance stabilisation on top of normal bond energies. Role of the tube: the red-hot iron supplies the activation energy for cyclisation; the hot surface also favours the six-membered cyclic transition over linear oligomers. Deep point: the trimerisation is the one route that builds the ring rather than editing it — and the fact that heat alone accomplishes it is evidence of how much energy the aromatic sextet stores back: the reaction is exothermic precisely because the product is aromatic.
  • NEB-style (5m): Why is soda lime (a mixture of NaOH and CaO) used for decarboxylation instead of sodium hydroxide alone? Give the equation. — NaOH's job: provides the OH⁻ that converts the acid–carboxylate chemistry and accepts the sodium of the carboxylate into the carbonate by-product. CaO's jobs: (1) raises the fusion temperature — pure NaOH melts at 318 °C and the reaction needs a strongly heated, dry, pasty fusion; CaO is refractory and keeps the mass solid at the working temperature; (2) absorbs water — dry conditions suppress side hydrolysis and keep the fusion from frothing. Equation: C₆H₅COONa + NaOH --(CaO, Δ)--> C₆H₆ + Na₂CO₃. Deep point: "soda lime" is a conditions-and-reagents package — the exam's condition slot (CaO, Δ) encodes two physical reasons; being able to say why CaO is there, rather than only that it is, converts a recall mark into an understanding mark.
  • NEB-style (6m): Sodium p-methylbenzoate is subjected to soda-lime decarboxylation. Predict the product, write the equation, and explain the generalisation. — Product: the carboxylate carbon is replaced by hydrogen; the para-methyl substituent rides through untouched ⟹ toluene (methylbenzene). Equation: CH₃–C₆H₄–COONa + NaOH --(CaO, Δ)--> CH₃–C₆H₅ + Na₂CO₃. Generalisation: decarboxylation edits only the −COONa group: any ring substituent (methyl, chloro, methoxy) is carried into the product at its original position — the reaction is a universal "carboxyl-out, hydrogen-in" operator on aromatic rings. Check: the product's formula C₇H₈ matches toluene; the para relationship is moot after deletion (the ring is monosubstituted). Deep point: the generalisation is why decarboxylation appears in synthesis plans, not just preparations — it is how a carboxyl group acts as a temporary blocking or directing group that is later removed; the ring's substituent map survives the operation, which is the whole point.

Key Points

  • Three routes: (1) C6H5COONa + NaOH(CaO, heat) -> C6H6 + Na2CO3 - soda-lime decarboxylation; (2) C6H5OH + Zn(heat) -> C6H6 + ZnO - zinc-dust reduction; (3) 3C2H2 -> (red-hot Fe tube, ~873 K) C6H6 - trimerisation. Conditions are diagnostic: soda lime not NaOH alone; zinc dust for surface area; red-hot tube as the reagent. Three logic families: group removal, group reduction, ring construction.

Summary

Benzene's three laboratory routes span three logics: soda-lime decarboxylation deletes a carboxylate (C₆H₅COONa + NaOH/CaO → C₆H₆ + Na₂CO₃), zinc-dust reduction deletes an oxygen (C₆H₅OH + Zn → C₆H₆ + ZnO), and Berthelot's red-hot trimerisation builds the ring from three ethynes (3C₂H₂, Fe tube, 873 K). The conditions — soda lime's CaO, the dust-fine zinc, the red heat — are diagnostic parts of each equation, and the generalised decarboxylation turns the reaction into a synthesis tool: carboxyl out, hydrogen in, substituent map intact.
