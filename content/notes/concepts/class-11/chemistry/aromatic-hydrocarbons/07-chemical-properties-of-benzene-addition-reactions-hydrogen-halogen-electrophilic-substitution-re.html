Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Benzene's Chemistry: Two Doors, One Preference

    Benzene reacts by addition (H₂/Ni, X₂/UV, O₃) only under forcing conditions, and by electrophilic substitution (nitration, sulphonation, halogenation, Friedel–Crafts) readily — the preference is the resonance energy at work. Orientation in disubstitution follows the director classification: o/p versus meta.

  • 2. Theorem Mirror

    • Addition routes: hydrogenation C₆H₆ + 3H₂ --(Ni, 475 K)--> C₆H₆H₁₂ (cyclohexane); halogen addition C₆H₆ + 3Cl₂ --(UV/sunlight)--> C₆H₆Cl₆ (benzene hexachloride, BHC); ozonolysis → tri-ozonide → hydrolysis gives 3 glyoxal (proving the three-bond skeleton).
    • Nitration: C₆H₆ + HNO₃ --(conc H₂SO₄, 50–60 °C)--> C₆H₅NO₂ + H₂O; electrophile NO₂⁺ generated: HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ ⇌ NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻.
    • Sulphonation: C₆H₆ + conc H₂SO₄ (or fuming/oleum) → C₆H₅SO₃H + H₂O; electrophile SO₃.
    • Halogenation: C₆H₆ + Cl₂ --(FeCl₃, dark)--> C₆H₅Cl + HCl; the Lewis acid polarises X₂.
    • Friedel–Crafts alkylation: C₆H₆ + R–Cl --(anhyd AlCl₃)--> C₆H₅–R + HCl; limitations — polyalkylation (product more reactive), carbocation rearrangement, fails on strongly deactivated rings.
    • Friedel–Crafts acylation: C₆H₆ + RCOCl --(AlCl₃)--> C₆H₅COR + HCl; acylium ion R–C≡O⁺ does not rearrange; the acyl group deactivates ⟹ mono-acylation.
    • Orientation: activating o/p directors (−OH, −NH₂, −OR, −R; halogens deactivating but o/p) vs meta directors (−NO₂, −SO₃H, −CHO, −COOH, −CN); toluene side-chain oxidation (KMnO₄) → benzoic acid.

  • 3. Deep Conceptual — The Substitution Mechanism as One Story

    • Every electrophilic substitution is the same three acts: (1) the electrophile E⁺ attacks the π cloud forming the σ-complex (arenium/Wheland intermediate) — the ring loses aromaticity at one carbon; (2) the σ-complex is stabilised by delocalising the positive charge over o/p positions (resonance structures); (3) a base removes the proton from the attacked carbon, restoring the aromatic sextet. Step 1 is slow (rate-determining); step 3 is fast and driven by the regained resonance energy.
    • Why the σ-complex explains orientation: a director's effect is read in the σ-complex — an electron-donating group stabilises the positive charge when the electrophile attacks ortho/para to it (the charge sits next door, donation helps); an electron-withdrawing group destabilises those same forms ⟹ meta attack, whose σ-complex keeps the charge away from the deactivator. Orientation is σ-complex stability bookkeeping.

  • 4. Deep Conceptual — Directors: Two Questions, Two Answers

    • Reactivity (activating vs deactivating) answers: does the substituent push electrons into the ring (−OH, −NH₂, −CH₃: activate) or pull (−NO₂, −COOH: deactivate)? Halogens pull inductively but donate by resonance — net deactivating yet o/p-directing: the one famous split verdict.
    • Regiochemistry (o/p vs meta) answers: where does the σ-complex's positive charge sit relative to the substituent? Donors stabilise charge near them ⟹ o/p; withdrawers destabilise it ⟹ meta. The two questions are independent — which is why halogens can be deactivating and o/p-directing without contradiction.

  • 5. Worked Example Types

    • Type A — write and condition: each named reaction with reagents/conditions.
    • Type B — mechanism: nitration's NO₂⁺ generation and the three-act substitution.
    • Type C — orientation prediction: given substituents, predict the major product.
    • Type D — Friedel–Crafts diagnosis: limitations and the acylation-vs-alkylation contrast.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    "FeCl₃ is consumed": it is a catalyst — regenerated when the HCl leaves. "o/p directors always activate": halogens direct o/p while deactivating — reactivity and regiochemistry are separate axes. "Friedel–Crafts works on nitrobenzene": strongly deactivated rings refuse it — the limitation is the deactivation itself.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2022 (3m): Give the reaction of benzene with (a) a mixture of concentrated nitric and sulphuric acids, (b) fuming sulphuric acid. — (a) Nitration: C₆H₆ + HNO₃ --(conc H₂SO₄, 50–60 °C)--> C₆H₅NO₂ (nitrobenzene) + H₂O. The sulphuric acid generates the electrophile: HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ ⇌ NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻. (b) Sulphonation: C₆H₆ + fuming H₂SO₄ (H₂SO₄ + SO₃) ⇌ C₆H₅SO₃H (benzene sulphonic acid) + H₂O; the electrophile is SO₃ (or protonated SO₃), attacking through its sulphur. Both are electrophilic substitutions — the ring's hydrogen is replaced, the aromatic sextet restored. Deep point: the two reactions share one mechanism but different electrophile-generation chemistry — nitration's acid pair manufactures NO₂⁺ in a measurable equilibrium (the ion is directly observed in spectra), while sulphonation's electrophile comes premade in the fuming acid; quoting the electrophile is the mark-earning step in both.
  • NEB 2024 (4m): Describe the mechanism of nitration of benzene. — Step 1 — electrophile generation: HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ ⇌ NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻ — the sulphuric acid protonates nitric acid (H₂NO₃⁺), which loses water to give the nitronium ion NO₂⁺ (linear, O=N=O). Step 2 — electrophilic attack (slow, rate-determining): the π electrons of benzene attack N of NO₂⁺, forming the σ-complex (arenium ion) — a cyclohexadienyl cation in which the attacked carbon bears both the NO₂ group and the H, sp³-hybridised; the positive charge is delocalised over the ortho and para positions (three resonance forms). Step 3 — deprotonation (fast): HSO₄⁻ removes the proton from the sp³ carbon; the C–H bond electrons restore the aromatic π sextet ⟹ nitrobenzene + H₂SO₄ (catalyst regenerated). Energy profile: step 2's barrier is the highest — the ring pays temporary aromaticity; step 3 is downhill because aromaticity returns. Deep point: the mechanism is the aromaticity story in miniature — lose the sextet in step 2 (slow, costly), regain it in step 3 (fast, repaid); the resonance energy is the thermodynamic motor of the whole substitution class.
  • NEB 2020 (3m): Explain why benzene undergoes electrophilic substitution rather than electrophilic addition. Compare with the behaviour of a simple alkene. — Benzene: addition (e.g. of Br₂, HBr) would convert the aromatic ring into a cyclohexadiene — destroying the delocalised π system and forfeiting its ~150 kJ/mol resonance energy; substitution keeps the sextet intact (only one C–H bond trades for C–E) ⟹ substitution is thermodynamically favoured and observed under mild conditions; addition needs forcing conditions (Ni/475 K for H₂; UV for Cl₂). Alkene: cyclohexene has no aromatic stabilisation to lose — its π bond is localised and electron-rich, so electrophilic addition (decolourising bromine water instantly, no catalyst) is fast and characteristic. Contrast in one line: the alkene spends its π bond; benzene invests its π system and only lends it. Deep point: the comparison isolates the variable — same six-carbon ring, one with a delocalised sextet and one without; the opposite chemistries (addition vs substitution) are the resonance energy made visible in reactivity.
  • NEB 2024 (3m): What is Friedel-Crafts alkylation? Write one of its limitations. — Definition: electrophilic substitution of benzene by an alkyl group using an alkyl halide and a Lewis acid catalyst: C₆H₆ + R–Cl --(anhyd AlCl₃)--> C₆H₅–R + HCl. Electrophile generation: AlCl₃ coordinates the chlorine (R–Cl···AlCl₃), polarising the C–Cl bond to the point of ionisation ⟹ R⁺ (or a tight R⁺···AlCl₄⁻ pair) attacks the ring; the mechanism follows the standard three acts, with HCl leaving and AlCl₃ regenerated. Limitation (any one): (1) polyalkylation — the alkyl product (e.g. toluene) is more reactive than benzene (the methyl group activates), so it alkylates again ⟹ mixtures; (2) carbocation rearrangement — primary halides give rearranged products (n-propyl chloride delivers isopropylbenzene via the more stable secondary cation); (3) deactivated rings refuse the reaction — nitrobenzene, benzoic acid and other strongly deactivated aromatics do not undergo Friedel–Crafts. Deep point: limitation (1) is the mirror of the director logic — the product's activating substituent invites over-reaction; acylation avoids it because the acyl group deactivates, which is why acylation is the cleaner synthetic door.
  • NEB 2024 (3m): What are ortho-para directors and meta directors? Give two examples of each. — Ortho–para directors: substituents that direct an incoming electrophile to the 2, 4 and 6 positions (ortho/para to themselves) — they donate electron density into the ring, stabilising the σ-complex forms in which the positive charge lies adjacent to them. Examples: −OH (phenol), −NH₂ (aniline); also −OCH₃, −CH₃, and the special case halogens (−Cl, −Br). Meta directors: substituents that direct to the 3 and 5 positions — they withdraw electron density, destabilising σ-complex charge adjacent to them, so attack occurs meta (charge kept away). Examples: −NO₂ (nitro), −SO₃H (sulphonic acid); also −CHO, −COOH, −CN. The split case: halogens withdraw inductively (deactivating) but donate by resonance (o/p-directing) — the two effects act on different questions. Deep point: the classification is really a statement about σ-complex resonance structures — draw the arenium ion for ortho attack and see whether the substituent can share or must endure the positive charge; the verdict follows from one drawing.
  • NEB 2024 (2m): How is benzoic acid prepared from toluene? Write the reaction. — Reaction: side-chain oxidation with hot alkaline KMnO₄ followed by acidification: C₆H₅CH₃ --(KMnO₄, OH⁻, Δ)--> C₆H₅COO⁻K⁺ --(H⁺)--> C₆H₅COOH (benzoic acid). Key point: the oxidation attacks the side chain's benzylic carbon — any alkylbenzene with at least one benzylic hydrogen gives benzoic acid regardless of chain length (toluene, ethylbenzene, propylbenzene all converge to the same acid). Deep point: the reaction reads the ring's protecting power backwards — the aromatic ring survives vigorous permanganate that destroys the entire side chain; the benzylic C–H is the vulnerable point, and its presence (not the chain's length) is the condition.

Summary

Benzene's chemistry is one mechanism worn by many electrophiles: NO₂⁺ (nitration), SO₃ (sulphonation), X⁺/FeX₃ (halogenation), R⁺/AlCl₃ and RCO⁺/AlCl₃ (Friedel–Crafts) — attack forms the σ-complex, deprotonation restores the sextet, and the regained resonance energy drives step three. Addition survives only under forcing conditions (Ni, UV) because it must repay 150 kJ/mol. Orientation is σ-complex bookkeeping: donors direct o/p, withdrawers direct meta, halogens split the axes — and the Friedel–Crafts limitations (polyalkylation, rearrangement, deactivated rings) are the director logic read in reverse.
