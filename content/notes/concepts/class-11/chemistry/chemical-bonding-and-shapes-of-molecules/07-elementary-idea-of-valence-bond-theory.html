Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Bonds as Overlapping Clouds

    Lewis structures say that atoms share; valence bond theory (VBT — Heitler–London, 1927; Pauling's extension) says how: a covalent bond forms when two half-filled atomic orbitals overlap, and the shared pair settles in the overlap region between the two nuclei. One idea — overlap — explains bond strength, bond direction, sigma versus pi, and the reason ethene's four hydrogen atoms all sit in one plane (NEB 2023's verified question).

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The mechanism of bond formation

    • Approach: as two atoms carrying half-filled orbitals approach, their orbitals begin to overlap.
    • At the overlap: electron density concentrates between the two nuclei; both nuclei are attracted to this shared cloud — potential energy falls.
    • Equilibrium: at a characteristic internuclear distance (bond length), attraction and repulsion balance at minimum energy — the bond is formed.
    • Excess spin pairing: the two electrons must have opposite spins (paired) in the overlap region.

    2.2 Sigma (σ) bond

    • Formed by head-on (axial) overlap: s–s (H₂), s–p (HCl), p–p (Cl₂, F₂).
    • Electron density is symmetric about the internuclear axis — cylindrically symmetrical.
    • Strongest overlap type: maximum electron density directly between nuclei; free rotation of bonded atoms about the axis.

    2.3 Pi (π) bond

    • Formed by sideways (lateral) overlap of two p orbitals above and below the internuclear axis.
    • Electron density lies above and below the axis, not on it — two lobes separated by a nodal plane containing the axis.
    • Weaker than σ (less effective overlap); no free rotation — rotation would break the sideways overlap.
    • A π bond never forms alone: it always accompanies a σ bond between the same two atoms (double bond = 1σ + 1π; triple = 1σ + 2π).

    2.4 Strength and direction consequences

    • Bond energy: σ (H–H 436 kJ mol⁻¹) > π component; C=C (614) < 2 × C–C (2 × 348) — the π bond contributes less than a full σ.
    • Bond direction: overlap is strongest along the orbital's lobe direction — bonds point where the orbitals point (foreshadowing hybridisation, topic 34).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Verified PYQ (NEB 2023): sigma and pi bonds in ethene, C₂H₄

    • Each carbon is sp² hybridised (topic 34): three sp² orbitals in a plane at 120°, one unhybridised p orbital perpendicular to the plane.
    • Five σ bonds: two C–H σ per carbon (sp²–s overlap) + one C–C σ (sp²–sp² head-on overlap) — these lie in the molecular plane.
    • One π bond: the two unhybridised p orbitals (one on each C, parallel and perpendicular to the plane) overlap sideways above and below the C–C axis — the π cloud.
    • H–C–H bond angle ≈ 120° (trigonal planar geometry at each carbon); all six atoms of ethene lie in one plane, and rotation about C=C is blocked by the π bond.

    4.2 Overlap types and their strength ladder

    • s–s (H₂): spherical orbitals overlap head-on; moderate strength.
    • s–p (HCl): H's 1s overlaps the lobe of Cl's 3p along the axis; directional.
    • p–p head-on (F₂, Cl₂): lobes meet along the internuclear axis → σ.
    • p–p sideways (ethene's π): parallel p orbitals overlap above/below the axis → π, weaker.
    • Greater overlap → shorter, stronger bond: this is why σ > π and why bond energies rank triple > double > single overall (more overlap in total).

    4.3 The rotation consequence — cis/trans isomerism

    • A single C–C σ bond rotates freely (ethane's conformers).
    • A C=C cannot rotate without destroying the π overlap — so substituent positions lock: cis/trans (geometric) isomerism appears. VBT's π bond is the reason alkenes have isomers that alkanes lack.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    A double bond is not two identical bonds: it is one strong σ + one weaker π — breaking a double bond first breaks the π (addition reactions attack the π first).

  • π bonds cannot exist alone: between two atoms, the first bond is always σ; π is always the second or third. "A molecule with only π bonds" is impossible.
  • Overlap requires half-filled orbitals: fully filled orbitals repel (no net bond); the spin-pairing condition is part of the definition.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified PYQ (NEB 2023): Using valence bond theory, explain the formation of sigma and pi bonds in ethene. State the hybridisation of each carbon and the H–C–H bond angle. — Each carbon is sp² hybridised: three sp² orbitals (trigonal planar, 120°) and one unhybridised p orbital perpendicular to the plane. The C–C σ bond forms by head-on sp²–sp² overlap; four C–H σ bonds form by sp²–s overlap — all five σ bonds lie in one plane. The two parallel unhybridised p orbitals overlap sideways to form the π bond, its cloud above and below the molecular plane. H–C–H ≈ 120°; the π bond locks the molecule planar and blocks rotation about C=C.

  • NEB-style (2m): Why is a π bond weaker than a σ bond? — Sideways overlap of parallel p orbitals is less effective than head-on overlap: the electron density sits above and below the internuclear axis rather than concentrated between the nuclei, so the nuclei are less strongly held — lower bond energy, easier to break.
  • NEB-style (2m): Account for the bond energies: C–C 348, C=C 614, C≡C 839 kJ mol⁻¹. — C=C = σ + π = 348 + 266; C≡C = σ + 2π = 348 + 2 × 245.5. Each added π bond adds energy but less than a σ would — the increments shrink (266 then 245), showing π overlap is progressively less effective.

Confusion

  • σ first, π second — always: the first bond between any pair of atoms is σ; describing C=C as "two π bonds" is a standard error.
  • Rotation and conformation: ethane rotates (σ only); ethene cannot (π locks it) — the π bond is the gatekeeper of geometric isomerism.

Practice

  • Why does the π bond block rotation about C=C but the σ bond does not?
  • Explain why F₂ (s…p–p σ) has a lower bond energy than Cl₂ despite F's higher electronegativity.
  • Identify the σ and π bonds in HCN (H–C≡N) and give the total counts.

Universal Facts

  • Retinal's π-system isomerisation (cis → trans) is the photochemical trigger of vision — VBT's rotation lock doing biology's work.

  • NEB-style (2m): Distinguish sigma and pi bonds on the basis of overlap, electron density and rotation. — Sigma: head-on (axial) overlap of s–s, s–p or p–p orbitals; electron density is symmetric about and concentrated on the internuclear axis; stronger; free rotation of the bonded atoms is possible; can exist alone. Pi: sideways overlap of two parallel p orbitals; electron density lies above and below the axis with a nodal plane through it; weaker; rotation is blocked (it would destroy the overlap); always accompanies a sigma bond.
  • NEB-style (2m): Explain the bond-energy data: C–C 348, C=C 614, C≡C 839 kJ mol⁻¹. — A single bond is one σ (348). The double bond adds one π: 614 − 348 = 266 kJ mol⁻¹ for the π component. The triple bond adds a second π: 839 − 614 = 225 kJ mol⁻¹ for the second π. Each π contributes less than a σ would, and successive π bonds contribute less than the first — sideways overlap is intrinsically weaker and becomes less effective as electron density accumulates. Total strength still rises with multiplicity, but in shrinking increments.

Formulas

    • Double bond = 1σ + 1π; triple = 1σ + 2π
    • C–C 348, C=C 614, C≡C 839 kJ mol⁻¹ (π increments < σ)
    • H–H: 74 pm, 436 kJ mol⁻¹ (σ s–s benchmark)

Key Points

  • VBT: covalent bond = overlap of half-filled orbitals, opposite spins, energy minimum at bond length. σ: head-on (s–s, s–p, p–p axial), density on axis, stronger, free rotation, can exist alone. π: sideways p–p, density above/below axis, weaker, no rotation, never alone. Ethene (NEB 2023): sp² carbons, 5 σ (sp²–sp², sp²–s) + 1 π (p–p sideways), H–C–H ≈120°, planar.

Summary

Valence bond theory turns the dot picture into physics: a covalent bond is the overlap of half-filled orbitals with opposite-spin pairing, energy minimum at the bond length where nuclear attraction to the shared cloud beats repulsion. Head-on overlap builds the sigma bond — density on the axis, the strongest link, free rotation, able to exist alone (H₂ s–s, HCl s–p, Cl₂ p–p). Sideways overlap of parallel p orbitals builds the pi bond — density above and below the axis, weaker, rotation-locked, and never found without its sigma partner (double = 1σ + 1π, triple = 1σ + 2π). Ethene is the showcase (NEB 2023): two sp² carbons, five in-plane sigma bonds, one sideways pi cloud, H–C–H ≈120°, and a planarity enforced by the π overlap — the same lock that creates cis/trans isomerism wherever a C=C carries substituents.
