Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Mixing Orbitals to Point Bonds

    Carbon's ground-state configuration (1s² 2s² 2p²) promises two bonds; methane delivers four identical ones at 109.5°. The rescue is hybridisation: before bonding, the central atom's s and p orbitals of the same shell blend into new equivalent hybrid orbitals, whose shapes and directions are tailor-made for bonding. sp³ → tetrahedral, sp² → trigonal planar, sp → linear — three recipes that rationalise the shapes VSEPR counts (and the NEB 2024 methane question directly).

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 What hybridisation is (and is not)

    • Definition: the intermixing of orbitals of slightly different energies (same shell: one s + p orbitals) to form an equal number of identical hybrid orbitals, oriented for maximum overlap.
    • Key features: number of hybrids = number of atomic orbitals mixed; hybrids have one large lobe (better overlap → stronger bonds); hybridisation happens at the moment of bonding, not in the free atom; it needs promoted electrons (energy invested, repaid by stronger bonds).
    • It is a mathematical convenience turned physical picture — the orbitals that point where the bonds point.

    2.2 The three s–p recipes

    TypeMixHybridsGeometryAngleLeftover p
    sp³1 s + 3 p4tetrahedral109.5°none
    sp²1 s + 2 p3trigonal planar120°1 p ⟂
    sp1 s + 1 p2linear180°2 p ⟂

    2.3 Worked molecules

    • CH₄ (sp³): C is excited to 2s¹2p³; four sp³ hybrids point to a tetrahedron's corners; four σ (sp³–s) bonds; H–C–H = 109.5°. Also NH₃, H₂O — same sp³ set, but lone pairs occupy hybrids and compress angles (107°, 104.5°).
    • C₂H₄ / BF₃ (sp²): three sp² hybrids in a plane at 120°; the unused p orbital stands perpendicular — in ethene it forms the π bond (topic 33); in BF₃ it stays empty (Lewis acid).
    • C₂H₂ / BeCl₂ (sp): two sp hybrids at 180°; two leftover p orbitals — in ethyne, two π bonds build the triple bond; in BeCl₂, two σ (sp–p) bonds give linear BeCl₂.
    • Extension (NEB 2024 asked): SF₆ and IF₇ — SF₆ is sp³d² (octahedral, 90°); IF₇ is sp³d³ (pentagonal bipyramidal) — d-orbital hybridisations beyond the s–p syllabus, but quotable as VSEPR-consistent extensions.

    2.4 Hybridisation and bond strength

    • Hybrid orbitals overlap more effectively (large directed lobe) than pure p orbitals — bonds are stronger and shorter.
    • More s-character → stronger, shorter bond and larger angle: sp (50% s) > sp² (33%) > sp³ (25%). Ethyne's C–H (sp) is the strongest C–H bond in organic chemistry.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Verified PYQ (NEB 2024/04): what is hybridisation? Show sp³ explains methane

    • Definition (section 2.1), then the methane argument:
    • Ground-state C: 1s² 2s² 2p² — only two unpaired electrons → predicts CH₂.
    • Promotion: 2s electron promoted to 2p → 2s¹ 2p³ (four unpaired; energy cost paid back fourfold by stronger bonds).
    • Hybridisation: 2s + three 2p blend → four equivalent sp³ hybrids, each with one electron, directed to a tetrahedron's corners (109.5°).
    • Bonding: each hybrid overlaps head-on with H's 1s → four identical σ bonds, all H–C–H = 109.5° — matching experiment exactly (VSEPR's domain count and VBT's orbital picture agree).

    4.2 Ethene and ethyne — hybrids plus leftover p orbitals

    • C₂H₄: each C sp²; three sp² per carbon (two C–H σ + one C–C σ), the perpendicular p orbitals overlap sideways → π. Planar, 120°.
    • C₂H₂: each C sp; two sp per carbon (one C–H σ + one C–C σ), two leftover p orbitals per carbon → two π bonds (perpendicular pair of π clouds). Linear, 180°; C≡C 120 pm.

    4.3 NH₃ and H₂O — sp³ with occupants

    • Both N and O use four sp³ hybrids; in NH₃ one hybrid holds a lone pair (3 BP + 1 LP → pyramidal, 107°); in H₂O two hold lone pairs (2 BP + 2 LP → bent, 104.5°).
    • Hybridisation explains the electron geometry; lone-pair repulsion (VSEPR) explains the observed shape — the two theories are complementary, not rival.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Hybridisation is per-bonding-event, not a free-atom property: an isolated carbon does not "contain" sp³ orbitals; they exist in the molecule, formed as bonds form.

  • Lone pairs occupy hybrids too: NH₃ and H₂O are sp³ — the lone pairs sit in sp³ hybrids; that is why their angles are near-tetrahedral rather than 90°.
  • More p-character ≠ stronger bond: s-character strengthens (closer to nucleus) — sp bonds beat sp³ bonds in strength and shortness.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified PYQ (NEB 2024/04): What is hybridisation? Show how sp³ hybridisation explains the shape of methane. — Hybridisation is the intermixing of atomic orbitals of comparable energy on the same atom to form an equal number of equivalent, identically oriented hybrid orbitals. Carbon: ground state 1s²2s²2p² (two unpaired); promotion → 2s¹2p³; hybridisation → four sp³ hybrids at 109.5°; four head-on overlaps with H 1s → four equivalent σ bonds; tetrahedral CH₄, H–C–H = 109.5°.

  • Verified PYQ (NEB 2024/04): Predict the shape and hybridisation of SF₆ and IF₇. — SF₆: six bond pairs → octahedral, sp³d² (d²sp³), all angles 90°. IF₇: seven bond pairs → pentagonal bipyramidal, sp³d³ — five equatorial I–F at 72°, two axial at 90°. Both exceed the s–p syllabus (d orbitals involved) but follow the same logic: hybrids = domains.
  • NEB-style (3m): Explain the formation of the ethyne molecule using hybridisation. — Each carbon is sp hybridised: promotion, then 2s + 2pₓ → two sp hybrids at 180°, leaving two perpendicular p orbitals. One sp on each C overlaps head-on → C–C σ; the other sp overlaps H's 1s → C–H σ (linear H–C≡C–H). The two pairs of parallel p orbitals overlap sideways → two π bonds (mutually perpendicular clouds). Result: linear molecule, C≡C = 120 pm, strongest C–H bonds (50% s-character).

Confusion

  • sp³ ≠ four bonds always: NH₃ (3 bonds) and H₂O (2 bonds) are sp³ — the hybrid count is four; bonds are what remain after lone pairs occupy hybrids.
  • Promotion is an investment, not a loss: the promotion energy is repaid by the extra bond energies of four strong sp³ bonds versus two weak pure-p bonds.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2024 (3m): What is hybridisation? Show how sp³ hybridisation explains the shape of methane. — Definition: hybridisation is the intermixing of atomic orbitals of nearly equal energy on the same atom to form an equal number of equivalent hybrid orbitals oriented for maximum overlap. Methane: ground-state carbon (1s²2s²2p²) has only two unpaired p electrons — insufficient for four bonds. One 2s electron is promoted to 2p (2s¹2p³, four unpaired electrons; the promotion energy is repaid by forming two extra strong bonds). The 2s and three 2p orbitals then hybridise into four equivalent sp³ hybrids directed toward the corners of a tetrahedron. Each hybrid overlaps head-on with a hydrogen 1s orbital → four identical σ bonds with H–C–H = 109.5° — the observed tetrahedral shape, with all four C–H bonds equal in length and strength.
  • NEB 2024 (2m): Predict the shape and hybridisation of SF₆ and IF₇. — SF₆: sulphur forms six bond pairs with no lone pair → six electron domains → octahedral shape, sp³d² hybridisation, all F–S–F angles 90°. IF₇: iodine forms seven bond pairs → pentagonal bipyramidal shape, sp³d³ hybridisation — five equatorial I–F bonds at 72° to each other and two axial bonds at 90°. Both require d-orbital participation (period ≥ 3), extending the sp/sp²/sp³ series by the same "hybrids = domains" rule.
  • NEB-style (3m): Using hybridisation, describe the bonding in ethyne (C₂H₂) and compare its C–H bonds with ethane's. — Each carbon is sp hybridised: after promotion, 2s + one 2p mix into two sp hybrids at 180°, leaving two mutually perpendicular unhybridised p orbitals. One sp hybrid of each carbon overlaps head-on → the C–C σ bond; the other sp overlaps a hydrogen 1s → C–H σ. The two pairs of parallel p orbitals overlap sideways → two π bonds with perpendicular clouds, completing the triple bond (C≡C, 120 pm). The molecule is linear (180°). Comparison: ethyne's sp hybrids carry 50% s-character versus ethane's sp³ 25% — s electrons sit closer to the nucleus, so ethyne's C–H bonds are stronger and shorter (106 pm vs 109 pm), and their carbon acidity is correspondingly higher.

Formulas

    • sp³: 4 hybrids, 109.5°; sp²: 3 hybrids + 1p, 120°; sp: 2 hybrids + 2p, 180°
    • s-character: sp 50% > sp² 33% > sp³ 25%
    • Bond strength/shortness ∝ s-character

Key Points

  • Hybridisation: same-shell s+p orbitals mix → equivalent directed hybrids; number conserved; large lobes overlap better. sp³ (4, tetrahedral 109.5°): CH₄, NH₃, H₂O. sp² (3, trigonal planar 120° + 1 p ⟂): C₂H₄, BF₃, SO₂. sp (2, linear 180° + 2 p ⟂): C₂H₂, BeCl₂, CO₂. s-character ladder sp > sp² > sp³ in bond strength. Extensions: SF₆ sp³d² octahedral; IF₇ sp³d³ pentagonal bipyramidal (NEB 2024).

Summary

Hybridisation is the orbital answer to VSEPR's shape count: same-shell s and p orbitals blend into equivalent, strongly directed hybrids — four sp³ pointing to a tetrahedron (CH₄ at 109.5°), three sp² in a plane (C₂H₄, BF₃ at 120°, one p orbital left over for π), two sp in a line (C₂H₂, BeCl₂ at 180°, two p orbitals for two π bonds). The mechanism needs promotion (carbon's 2s²2p² → 2s¹2p³) — an investment repaid by stronger, shorter bonds, since hybrid lobes overlap better and extra s-character (sp 50% > sp² 33% > sp³ 25%) pulls electrons closer. Lone pairs occupy hybrids too — NH₃ and H₂O are sp³ with angles compressed to 107° and 104.5° by VSEPR repulsion. Beyond the s–p syllabus the same rule extends: SF₆ sp³d² octahedral, IF₇ sp³d³ pentagonal bipyramidal (NEB 2024). Domain count gives the hybrid type; the leftover p orbitals give the π bonds; the shape follows.

Universal Facts

  • Ethyne's sp C–H bonds (50% s-character) are acidic enough (pKₐ ≈ 25) to form acetylides with strong bases — hybridisation strength you can deprotonate.

Sigma and pi frames
