Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Chemistry in Dots and Lines

    A Lewis dot structure is a molecule's electron budget made visible: every valence electron accounted for, either as a bonding pair (a line) or a lone pair (dots). Draw it correctly and the shape, polarity and reactivity of the molecule follow almost mechanically. NEB has asked the CO₂ dot structure directly (2022, 2024) and the H₂O/NH₃ structures with their shapes (2024) — this topic is the drawing manual.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The drawing procedure

    1. Count total valence electrons (adjust for charge: add for anions, subtract for cations).
    2. Choose the skeleton — least electronegative atom central (H is always terminal).
    3. Place one bond pair between every connected pair; complete the octets (duets for H) of outer atoms with lone pairs.
    4. Park leftovers on the central atom; if its octet is short, convert outer lone pairs into double/triple bonds.
    5. Verify: electrons used = electrons counted; every atom at its octet (or a known exception: Be, B, expanded P/S).

    2.2 The standard gallery (s and p block)

    • H₂O: 8 e⁻ → two O–H bonds + two lone pairs on O (bent, 104.5°).
    • NH₃: 8 e⁻ → three N–H bonds + one lone pair (pyramidal, 107°).
    • CH₄: 8 e⁻ → four C–H bonds, no lone pairs (tetrahedral, 109.5°).
    • CO₂: 16 e⁻ → two C=O double bonds, two lone pairs per O (linear, 180°).
    • BF₃: 24 e⁻ → three B–F bonds, no lone pair on B — incomplete octet (6 e⁻) (trigonal planar, 120°).
    • PCl₅: 40 e⁻ → five P–Cl bonds — expanded octet (10 e⁻) (trigonal bipyramidal).
    • SF₆: 48 e⁻ → six S–F bonds — expanded octet (12 e⁻) (octahedral).
    • H₂S: 8 e⁻ → two S–H bonds + two lone pairs (bent, 92°).

    2.3 Formal charge — choosing between rival structures

    • FC = valence e⁻ − (non-bonding e⁻ + ½ bonding e⁻).
    • The best structure minimises formal charges and puts negative FC on the more electronegative atom.
    • Example O₃: the two resonance forms each carry FC +1 on central O and −1 on one terminal O — formal charge bookkeeping justifies resonance (topic 31).

    2.4 Polyatomic ions

    • NH₄⁺: 5 + 4 − 1 = 8 e⁻ → four N–H bonds; formal charge +1 on N.
    • OH⁻: 6 + 1 + 1 = 8 e⁻ → one O–H bond, three lone pairs, FC −1 on O.
    • CO₃²⁻: 4 + 18 + 2 = 24 e⁻ → one C=O, two C–O⁻, one lone pair on C — and resonance spreads the double bond (topic 31).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Verified PYQ (NEB 2022 & 2024): Lewis dot structure of CO₂ and its shape

    • Count: 4 + 2(6) = 16 electrons.
    • Skeleton O–C–O; place one pair in each C–O bond (4 e⁻), complete O octets with lone pairs (12 e⁻) — carbon has only 4 e⁻.
    • Convert one lone pair from each O into a bonding pair → O=C=O, two double bonds.
    • Each atom reaches an octet; all 16 electrons placed. Shape: linear, 180° (two electron domains around C).

    4.2 Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): Lewis structures of H₂O and NH₃ with VSEPR shapes

    • H₂O: 6 + 2(1) = 8 e⁻ → two O–H bonds + two lone pairs on O. Four electron domains → tetrahedral electron geometry, but molecular shape bent (V-shaped), ≈104.5° — lone pairs compress the angle.
    • NH₃: 5 + 3(1) = 8 e⁻ → three N–H bonds + one lone pair. Four domains → pyramidal shape, ≈107° — one lone pair compresses 109.5° to 107°.

    4.3 Reading the exceptions straight off the dots

    • BF₃'s drawing forces 6 electrons onto B — the structure itself announces the incomplete octet and the Lewis-acid character.
    • PCl₅/SF₆ demand 10/12 electrons on the central atom — only legal for period ≥ 3.
    • The dot count is also the raw material for VSEPR: bonding pairs + lone pairs = electron domains, and domains set the shape (topic 32).

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Lone pairs are not optional decoration: omitting them changes the VSEPR count and hence the predicted shape — H₂O without its lone pairs would be drawn "linear" and lose all the marks.

  • Total electrons ≠ electrons shown as dots around one atom: in CO₂ the 16 electrons distribute over three atoms; the audit line "used = counted" is the safety check.
  • Formal charge ≠ real charge: it is a bookkeeping index for choosing among structures; the molecule's real charge distribution comes from electronegativity (partial charges), not FC.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified PYQ (NEB 2022/2024): Write the Lewis dot structure of CO₂. What is its molecular shape? — 16 valence electrons; O=C=O with two lone pairs on each oxygen; carbon's octet completed by two double bonds. Two electron domains, no lone pairs on C → linear, 180°, and the two C=O bond dipoles cancel, making the molecule non-polar.

  • Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): Draw the Lewis structures of H₂O and NH₃ and predict their shapes using VSEPR theory. — H₂O: two bonds + two lone pairs → bent, ≈104.5°. NH₃: three bonds + one lone pair → pyramidal, ≈107°. Both angles sit below 109.5° because lone pair–bond pair repulsion exceeds bond pair–bond pair repulsion.
  • NEB-style (2m): Draw the Lewis structure of BF₃ and comment on boron's octet. — 24 valence electrons; three B–F single bonds, three lone pairs on each F; boron holds only 6 electrons — an incomplete octet, making BF₃ a strong Lewis acid that completes its octet by accepting a pair (e.g. from NH₃).

Confusion

  • Draw lone pairs first, shapes later: every VSEPR answer begins with the completed Lewis structure — lone pairs included — or the domain count collapses.
  • Formal charge vs oxidation state: FC assigns bonding electrons equally; oxidation state assigns them to the more electronegative atom — the two bookkeeping systems give different numbers on purpose.

Practice

  • Why must hydrogen always occupy a terminal position in a Lewis structure?

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2022 & 2024 (2m): Write the Lewis dot structure of CO₂. What is its molecular shape? — Carbon dioxide has 4 + 2(6) = 16 valence electrons. Skeleton O–C–O: one pair in each C–O link, six electrons (three lone pairs) on each oxygen complete their octets but leave carbon with only four. Moving one lone pair from each oxygen into bonding gives O=C=O — two double bonds, two lone pairs per oxygen, every atom at an octet. Carbon carries two electron domains and no lone pairs, so VSEPR gives a linear molecule, 180°; the equal, opposite C=O dipoles cancel and CO₂ is non-polar.
  • NEB 2024 (3m): Draw the Lewis structures of H₂O and NH₃ and predict their shapes using VSEPR theory. — H₂O: 8 valence electrons → two O–H bonding pairs plus two lone pairs on oxygen. Four electron domains give tetrahedral electron geometry; the two lone pairs push the bonds together, so the molecular shape is bent (V-shaped) with H–O–H ≈ 104.5°. NH₃: 8 valence electrons → three N–H bonds plus one lone pair on nitrogen. Four domains again, but one lone pair compresses the angle less: pyramidal shape, H–N–H ≈ 107°. Both angles fall below the ideal 109.5° because lone pair–bond pair repulsion exceeds bond pair–bond pair repulsion.
  • NEB-style (2m): Compute the formal charges in the ozone molecule and state what they imply. — For O₃ (central O double-bonded to one O, single-bonded to the other): central O = 6 − (2 + 3) = +1; double-bond terminal O = 6 − (4 + 2) = 0; single-bond terminal O = 6 − (6 + 1) = −1. The mirror-image structure carries the −1 on the other terminal oxygen. Neither structure alone is real — the charges and the two different O–O lengths (128 and 136 pm) show the true molecule is a resonance hybrid of the two forms.

Formulas

    • Total valence e⁻ = Σ(valence e⁻) ± charge
    • FC = V − (N + ½B)
    • Domains = bonding pairs + lone pairs (central atom)

Key Points

  • Procedure: count valence e⁻ (± charge) → skeleton (least EN central) → bonds → outer octets → leftovers on central atom → double/triple bonds if short → verify. Formal charge = valence − (non-bonding + ½ bonding); minimise FC, negative on the more EN atom.

Summary

A Lewis structure is an electron audit made visible. Count the valence electrons (adjusting for charge), pick the least electronegative central atom, spend electrons on bonds first and outer octets second, park the remainder on the centre, and promote lone pairs to double or triple bonds when the central octet falls short. The gallery every exam draws from: H₂O (2 bonds + 2 lone pairs, bent 104.5°), NH₃ (3 + 1, pyramidal 107°), CH₄ (4 + 0, tetrahedral 109.5°), CO₂ (two C=O, linear 180° — NEB 2022 and 2024), BF₃ (incomplete octet, 6 e⁻), PCl₅ (10 e⁻) and SF₆ (12 e⁻) as expanded-octet legal cases, H₂S (bent ≈92°). Formal charge (V − N − ½B) referees rival structures and sets up resonance. Lone pairs are never optional: they are the hinge between the dot picture and the VSEPR shape.
