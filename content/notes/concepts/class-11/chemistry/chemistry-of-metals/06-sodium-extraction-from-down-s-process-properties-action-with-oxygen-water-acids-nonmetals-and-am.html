Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Sodium, the Industry Metal

    Sodium is the cheapest strong reducer and the cheapest strong base source, and it cannot be won from its compounds by chemistry — only by electricity. The Downs cell (verified PYQs NEB 2023 and NEB 2024) is the classic answer, and its product feeds the reactions with oxygen, water, acids, non-metals and ammonia that fill this topic.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The Downs cell

    • Electrolyte: molten NaCl + CaCl₂ (≈ 42 : 58) — the CaCl₂ lowers the melting point from 801 °C to about 600 °C and raises conductivity.
    • Cathode (steel): Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na; molten sodium floats (ρ 0.97 < melt) and is collected in a bell and siphoned.
    • Anode (graphite): 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻; a steel gauze diaphragm between the electrodes keeps the products apart — without it Na + Cl₂ would recombine, and Na would form the unsafe NaCl·Ca mixture.
    • Ca²⁺ is not reduced at the cathode despite being present: at 600 °C only Na⁺ discharges practically (overpotential/electrode kinetics); any calcium formed dissolves back into the melt.
    • Overall: 2NaCl(l) → 2Na + Cl₂.

    • Na₂O₂ bleaching, NaOH manufacture (with Hg or via amalgam), sodium vapour lamps (589 nm), coolant in fast nuclear reactors (liquid metal, huge heat capacity, low mp), reducing agent (Ti from TiCl₄, zirconium), NaPb alloy for anti-knock lead additive precursor, laboratory desiccant (in ether).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why the Downs cell adds CaCl₂

    • Pure NaCl melts at 801 °C — sodium boils at 883 °C, so a pure-salt cell runs within 80 degrees of the metal's boiling point: the sodium vapourises, contaminates the chlorine, and the cell corrodes. The 42:58 NaCl:CaCl₂ eutectic melts at ~600 °C, comfortably below both, and the mixture conducts better. Ca²⁺ stays in the melt because its discharge needs a higher voltage than Na⁺ at the working conditions.

    4.2 Why sodium reacts so violently with water

    • Thermodynamics is enormous (2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂, strongly exothermic) and kinetics help: the reaction heat melts sodium (mp 98 °C), the melt spreads, the H₂ film ignites in air, and the NaOH formed locally accelerates attack. The classic school demonstration — a pea of sodium skating on water — is the same equation.

    4.3 Sodium and ammonia — the subtle one

    • In liquid ammonia sodium dissolves to a deep blue conducting solution of solvated electrons (Na⁺ + e⁻(NH₃)⁻); with an iron catalyst (or on warming) the electron reduces NH₃ to amide: Na + NH₃ → NaNH₂ + ½H₂. In gaseous ammonia at room temperature sodium does not react — the reaction needs heat or the solution route.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    The diaphragm is essential, not decorative: without it the cell would short-circuit chemically — sodium would reduce chlorine back to NaCl, and heat + mixing could cause a violent local reaction.

  • CaCl₂ is not reduced: its job is melting point and conductivity; Ca²⁺ remains in the melt — quoting "Ca is also deposited" is a classic wrong answer.
  • Na₂O₂ is the excess-oxygen product: limited air gives Na₂O; excess gives Na₂O₂ — the exam expects both equations.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified PYQ NEB 2024 / NEB 2023 (3m): Explain Down's process for the extraction of sodium. — Sodium is extracted by the electrolysis of fused sodium chloride in the Downs cell. Pure NaCl melts at 801 °C — too hot (sodium boils at 883 °C) — so it is mixed with calcium chloride (≈42 : 58), which lowers the melting point to about 600 °C and improves conductivity. The cell has a graphite anode and a steel cathode separated by a steel-gauze diaphragm. On electrolysis: cathode — Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na; the molten sodium floats up inside a bell-shaped collector and is drawn off. Anode — 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻; chlorine leaves through a hood. The diaphragm prevents the sodium from meeting the chlorine (they would recombine to NaCl) and keeps the calcium, which is not discharged at this temperature, in the melt. Overall: 2NaCl(l) → 2Na + Cl₂.

  • Verified PYQ NEB 2023 (3m): Compare the extraction of sodium by the Downs cell with the extraction of magnesium by the electrolysis of fused magnesium chloride. Give the electrode reactions. — Both are molten-salt electrolyses with graphite anodes liberating chlorine and steel cathodes, and both need CaCl₂/NaCl mixtures to lower the melting point. Downs cell: electrolyte NaCl + CaCl₂ at ~600 °C; cathode reaction Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na (sodium floats and is collected in a bell); anode 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻; a steel gauze diaphragm separates the products. Magnesium cell: electrolyte fused MgCl₂ (dried, mixed with NaCl and CaCl₂) at ~700 °C; cathode Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Mg (molten magnesium floats to the surface and is skimmed); anode 2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻; chlorine is prevented from recombining with magnesium by a diaphragm and by withdrawing the metal. The differences: sodium's cell runs cooler (600 vs ~700 °C), uses a central ring cathode arrangement, and calcium stays unreduced, while magnesium is won from its own chloride with iron-pot cathodes.
  • NEB-style (2m): What happens when sodium is heated (i) in limited air, (ii) in excess air/oxygen? — (i) Limited air: sodium forms the normal oxide, 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O (white solid). (ii) Excess air/oxygen: sodium burns with a golden-yellow flame to the peroxide, 2Na + O₂ → Na₂O₂, which contains the peroxide ion O₂²⁻ and is used for bleaching and for oxygen generation (Na₂O₂ + CO₂ → Na₂CO₃ + ½O₂).

Summary

Sodium cannot be reduced chemically, so the Downs cell electrolyses molten NaCl + CaCl₂ (42:58) — the eutectic melts at ~600 °C, 200 degrees below sodium's boiling point — between a graphite anode (2Cl⁻ → Cl₂ + 2e⁻) and steel cathodes (Na⁺ + e⁻ → Na), with a steel-gauze diaphragm keeping the products apart; the floating sodium is siphoned from a bell and Ca²⁺ stays in the melt. The metal then lives up to its group: limited air gives Na₂O, excess air the peroxide Na₂O₂ (bleach, O₂ source with CO₂); water gives 2NaOH + H₂ violently; acids, chlorine and hydrogen give NaCl and the ionic hydride NaH; liquid ammonia dissolves it into a blue solvated-electron solution that yields sodamide NaNH₂ with a catalyst. Its uses follow the same properties: sodium vapour lamps at 589 nm, fast-reactor coolant, and a reducer that strips titanium and zirconium from their chlorides.
