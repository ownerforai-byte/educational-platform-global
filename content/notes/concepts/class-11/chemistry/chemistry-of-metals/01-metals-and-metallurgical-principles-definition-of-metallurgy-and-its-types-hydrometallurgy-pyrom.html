Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Where Metals Come From

    Only a handful of metals — gold, platinum, some copper and silver — occur native. Everything else sits in the crust as ores: ionic compounds (oxides, sulphides, carbonates, chlorides) from which the metal must be won. Metallurgy is the whole science of that winning: concentrate the ore, convert it to a reducible form, reduce it, refine it. The three broad routes — hydrometallurgy (aqueous chemistry), pyrometallurgy (heat), electrometallurgy (electricity) — are the map for this entire unit.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Metallurgy and its types

    • Metallurgy = the science and technology of extracting metals from their ores and preparing them for use.
    • Hydrometallurgy — extraction through aqueous solution chemistry: leaching the ore with a solvent (acid, cyanide), then precipitating or displacing the metal. Example: gold and silver by cyanide leaching (MacArthur–Forrest), copper by leaching with dilute H₂SO₄ followed by displacement with scrap iron.
    • Pyrometallurgy — extraction at high temperature: roasting, smelting, reduction in a blast furnace, converting. Example: iron from haematite in the blast furnace; copper from copper pyrites.
    • Electrometallurgy — extraction or refining by electrolysis: the most active metals (Na, K, Mg, Ca, Al) cannot be reduced by carbon or hydrogen and are won by electrolysis of their molten salts. Example: Downs cell for sodium, Hall–Héroult for aluminium.

    2.2 The steps common to all routes

    1. Mining and crushing of the ore.
    2. Concentration (dressing) — separate the wanted mineral from gangue: gravity washing, magnetic separation (magnetite), froth flotation (sulphides).
    3. Conversion to a reducible compound — calcination or roasting.
    4. Reduction to the metal — carbon (coke), thermite, or electrolysis.
    5. Refining — poling, liquation, electrolytic refinement.

    2.3 Why the route depends on reactivity

    • Metals high in the activity series (Na, K, Ca, Mg, Al): strong affinity for oxygen, carbon cannot reduce their oxides → electrometallurgy.
    • Middle of the series (Zn, Fe, Pb, Cu): oxides reducible by carbon at furnace temperature → pyrometallurgy.
    • Low in the series (Ag, Au, Pt): occur native or are won by solution chemistry → hydrometallurgy.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why active metals demand electricity

    • ΔG for M + ½O₂ → MO is strongly negative for Na, Mg, Al — carbon reduction would need temperatures at which carbides form and the oxide still wins. Electrolysis forces the reaction backwards by electrical work: at the cathode the metal ion is reduced directly from the molten salt (water would hydrogen-evolve first).
    • Aluminium is the boundary case: its oxide is reducible in principle (thermite proves it — Al takes oxygen from Fe₂O₃), but carbon reduction of Al₂O₃ needs ~2000 °C and forms Al₄C₃, so industry uses electrolysis of Al₂O₃ dissolved in molten cryolite at ~950 °C.

    4.2 Why sulphide ores get roasted and carbonate ores get calcined

    • A sulphide is not directly reducible by carbon; roasting turns it into the oxide (which is): 2ZnS + 3O₂ → 2ZnO + 2SO₂.
    • A carbonate releases CO₂ on heating, leaving the porous oxide: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂. Both products — the oxide — then reduce with coke.

    4.3 The economic meaning of "ore"

    • A mineral becomes an ore only when extraction is profitable: bauxite is an ore of aluminium, clay (also Al₂O₃·2SiO₂·2H₂O chemistry) is not — the separation cost is too high. Grade, location and the price of the metal all enter.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Ore vs mineral: every ore is a mineral, but not every mineral is an ore — bauxite is an ore of Al, clay is a mineral of Al but not an ore, because extraction is not profitable.

  • Electrometallurgy is not electrorefining: extraction electrolysis pulls the metal out of a molten salt (Downs, Hall–Héroult); electrorefining purifies an already-extracted metal from an aqueous solution (copper). Both are electrolysis, different steps of metallurgy.
  • Aluminium is not "too reactive for electrolysis": it is too reactive for carbon reduction at practical temperatures; the thermite reaction shows Al reduces Fe₂O₃ violently. The choice of electrolysis is economic and chemical (carbide formation), not a absolutes statement.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): What is metallurgy? Name its three types with one example of each. — Metallurgy is the science of the processes by which a metal is extracted from its ore and refined for use. Hydrometallurgy extracts the metal through aqueous solution chemistry — gold is leached with dilute NaCN with air present, 2Au + 4CN⁻ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 2[Au(CN)₂]⁻ + 4OH⁻, and the complex is displaced with zinc. Pyrometallurgy extracts the metal at high temperature — iron is won by reducing roasted haematite with CO in the blast furnace, Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Electrometallurgy extracts (or refines) the metal by electrolysis — sodium is obtained by electrolysis of molten NaCl in the Downs cell, 2NaCl(l) → 2Na(cathode) + Cl₂(anode).

  • NEB-style (2m): Why are alkali metals extracted by electrometallurgy and not by carbon reduction? — Alkali metals sit at the very top of the activity series: their oxides/chlorides are extremely stable (very negative ΔG of formation), so carbon cannot reduce them at any practical temperature — the metal would rather stay bonded. Their ions also cannot be reduced from aqueous solution, because water is reduced to H₂ first. Hence the metal is won by electrolysis of the molten salt, where the cation is discharged directly at the cathode (Downs cell for Na: 2NaCl(l) → 2Na + Cl₂).

Confusion

  • ❌ All atoms are identical in an element. ✅ Isotopes have same protons but different neutrons.
  • ❌ Atoms are indivisible. ✅ Atoms consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • ❌ Electrons orbit in fixed paths like planets. ✅ Electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals).
  • ❌ Heisenberg uncertainty means we cannot measure position and momentum precisely simultaneously — not that we lack good instruments.
  • ❌ de Broglie wavelength applies only to light. ✅ All matter has wave nature; detectable mainly for small masses like electrons.
  • ❌ Quantum number l can be any integer. ✅ l ranges from 0 to n−1.
  • ❌ Magnetic quantum number ml determines energy. ✅ ml determines orbital orientation, not energy (in absence of magnetic field).
  • ❌ Spin quantum number ms can be ±½ or any value. ✅ ms is always +½ or −½.
  • ❌ Aufbau principle means filling from bottom to top of the diagram. ✅ Fill lowest-energy orbitals first: 1s→2s→2p→3s→3p→4s→3d→4p→...
  • ❌ Pauli exclusion allows two electrons per orbital with any spin. ✅ Two electrons in same orbital must have opposite spins.
  • ❌ Hund's rule means pair electrons before spreading. ✅ Electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly first with parallel spins.
  • ❌ The mole is just a number like dozen. ✅ 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro constant) — a bridge between atomic and macroscopic scales.
  • ❌ Molar mass and molecular mass are the same thing. ✅ Molecular mass is in amu; molar mass is in g/mol — numerically equal but different units.
  • ❌ Limiting reagent is the reactant with smallest mass. ✅ Limiting reagent is the one that produces the least product (determined by moles, not mass).
  • ❌ % yield can exceed 100%. ✅ % yield >100% indicates impure product or incomplete drying.
  • ❌ Molarity and molality are the same. ✅ Molarity = mol/L solution (changes with T); Molality = mol/kg solvent (temperature-independent).
  • ❌ A strong acid completely ionizes in water. ✅ Strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) are ~100% ionized; weak acids partially ionize.
  • ❌ pH can be negative. ✅ pH < 0 is possible for concentrations >1M (e.g., 2M HCl has pH ≈ −0.3).
  • ❌ Kc changes when concentration changes. ✅ Kc is constant at constant temperature; only temperature changes K.
  • ❌ Adding catalyst shifts equilibrium position. ✅ Catalyst speeds both forward and backward reactions equally; no shift in equilibrium.
  • ❌ High pressure always favors products. ✅ High pressure favors side with fewer gas moles (Le Chatelier).
  • ❌ Exothermic reactions are spontaneous. ✅ Spontaneity depends on ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, not just ΔH.
  • ❌ Endothermic reactions cannot be spontaneous. ✅ Endothermic reactions can be spontaneous if TΔS > ΔH (e.g., ice melting above 0°C).
  • ❌ ΔH and ΔU are always the same. ✅ ΔH = ΔU + PΔV; they differ when gas moles change.
  • ❌ Heat (q) and work (w) are state functions. ✅ q and w are path functions; only ΔU, ΔH, ΔS, ΔG are state functions.
  • ❌ Oxidation and reduction happen separately. ✅ Oxidation and reduction always occur together (redox).
  • ❌ Oxidizing agent gets oxidized. ✅ Oxidizing agent accepts electrons and gets reduced.
  • ❌ Reducing agent gets reduced. ✅ Reducing agent donates electrons and gets oxidized.
  • ❌ Faraday's laws apply only to metals. ✅ Faraday's laws apply to any substance deposited/dissolved at electrodes.
  • ❌ Real gases obey PV=nRT at all conditions. ✅ Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • ❌ van der Waals constants a and b are same for all gases. ✅ a (attraction) and b (volume) are gas-specific.
  • ❌ Surface tension increases with temperature. ✅ Surface tension decreases as temperature increases.
  • ❌ Viscosity decreases with temperature for liquids. ✅ Viscosity of liquids decreases with T; gases increase with T.
  • ❌ Colligative properties depend on nature of solute. ✅ Colligative properties depend only on number of particles, not identity.
  • ❌ Raoult's law applies to all solutions. ✅ Raoult's law is exact for ideal solutions; approximate for dilute real solutions.
  • ❌ Osmotic pressure is a colligative property. ✅ Yes — π = CRT depends on solute particle concentration.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): What is metallurgy? Name its three types with one example of each. — Metallurgy is the science of extracting metals from their ores and preparing the pure metal for use; it covers concentration of the ore, conversion to a reducible compound, reduction and refining. Hydrometallurgy — extraction through aqueous solution chemistry: gold ore is leached with dilute NaCN in the presence of air, 2Au + 4CN⁻ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 2[Au(CN)₂]⁻ + 4OH⁻, and gold is then displaced from the complex by zinc, 2[Au(CN)₂]⁻ + Zn → 2Au + [Zn(CN)₄]²⁻. Pyrometallurgy — extraction at high temperature with coke and flux: roasted haematite is reduced in the blast furnace, Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂.   • NEB-style (2m): Why is aluminium extracted by electrolysis although aluminium is a strong reducing agent in the thermite reaction? — In the thermite reaction aluminium takes oxygen from iron(III) oxide, 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe, showing Al has greater affinity for oxygen than iron. But to reduce its own oxide Al₂O₃, carbon would have to compete with one of the most negative free energies of formation known, needing ~2000 °C, at which the product aluminium forms carbide Al₄C₃ and the process is uneconomic. Therefore the oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite (Na₃AlF₆, which lowers the melting point to ~950 °C) and aluminium is deposited at the cathode by electrolysis — the electrical work replaces the impossible chemical reduction.

Key Points

  • Metallurgy = extracting + refining metals from ores. Three types: hydrometallurgy (aqueous; Au cyanide leaching 2Au + 4CN- + O2 + 2H2O -> 2[Au(CN)2]- + 4OH-, displacement with Zn), pyrometallurgy (heat/coke; blast furnace Fe2O3 + 3CO -> 2Fe + 3CO2), electrometallurgy (electrolysis of molten salts; Downs Na, Hall-Heroult Al). Route follows activity series: very active Na/K/Mg/Ca/Al -> electrolysis; middle Zn/Fe/Pb/Cu -> carbon reduction; noble Ag/Au/Pt -> native or hydrometallurgy. Steps: crushing -> concentration -> calcination/roasting -> reduction -> refining. Ore = profitable mineral (bauxite yes, clay no). Al boundary case: thermite shows Al reduces Fe2O3, but carbon reduction of Al2O3 impractical (2000 C, Al4C3) -> electrolysis in cryolite.

Summary

Metallurgy is the science of winning a metal from its ore and refining it; its three branches map onto the activity series. Hydrometallurgy uses aqueous chemistry — gold is dissolved by cyanide leaching (2Au + 4CN⁻ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 2[Au(CN)₂]⁻ + 4OH⁻) and knocked out of solution by zinc. Pyrometallurgy uses heat and coke — the blast furnace reduces roasted haematite with carbon monoxide (Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂). Electrometallurgy uses electrolysis of molten salts for the metals carbon cannot touch — sodium in the Downs cell, aluminium in Hall–Héroult cryolite melt. The five common steps are crushing, concentration, calcination/roasting, reduction and refining; and an ore is defined economically — a mineral becomes an ore only when its extraction pays (bauxite yes, clay no).

Hydrometallurgy — extraction through aqueous solution chemistry: gold ore is leached with dilute NaCN in the presence of air, 2Au + 4CN⁻ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 2[Au(CN)₂]⁻ + 4OH⁻, and gold is then displaced from the complex by zinc, 2[Au(CN)₂]⁻ + Zn → 2Au + [Zn(CN)₄]²⁻. Pyrometallurgy — extraction at high temperature with coke and flux: roasted haematite is reduced in the blast furnace, Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Electrometallurgy — extraction or refining by electrolysis: sodium is liberated from molten NaCl in the Downs cell, 2NaCl(l) → 2Na(cathode) + Cl₂(anode), because carbon cannot reduce so stable a chloride.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why is aluminium extracted by electrolysis although aluminium is a strong reducing agent in the thermite reaction? — In the thermite reaction aluminium takes oxygen from iron(III) oxide, 2Al + Fe₂O₃ → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe, showing Al has greater affinity for oxygen than iron. But to reduce its own oxide Al₂O₃, carbon would have to compete with one of the most negative free energies of formation known, needing ~2000 °C, at which the product aluminium forms carbide Al₄C₃ and the process is uneconomic. Therefore the oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite (Na₃AlF₆, which lowers the melting point to ~950 °C) and aluminium is deposited at the cathode by electrolysis — the electrical work replaces the impossible chemical reduction.
