Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Other Alkaline Earths

    Group IIA — Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra — carries two valence electrons and a different personality from Group IA: harder, denser, higher-melting, less reactive, and famous for flame colours and sulphate solubility. This topic is the trend table NEB asks as a 2–3 marker.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Electronic structure

    • General configuration [NG]ns²; oxidation state +2 only (Be²⁺ exceptional covalency). Removing the second s-electron costs more (second IE ~1800 kJ/mol) but the +2 lattice energies repay it — hence no +1 chemistry in solution.

    2.2 Trends down the group

    PropertyTrendReason/numbers
    Radiusincreasesnew shell (Be 111 pm covalent → Ba 198)
    IE₁decreasesBe 899 → Ba 503 kJ/mol
    Densitygenerally increasesBe 1.85 → Ba 3.62 g/mL (Mg 1.74 dip)
    Melting pointno regular trendCa 842, Sr 777, Ba 727 °C — irregular
    Reactivity with waterincreasesBe none; Mg slow (hot); Ca brisk; Sr/Ba vigorous
    Flame colourCa brick-red, Sr crimson, Ba apple-greenBe/Mg none (small atoms, high IE)
    ElectropositivityincreasesBe least, Ba most

    2.3 Signature chemistry

    • With water: M + 2H₂O → M(OH)₂ + H₂ (Ca briskly cold, Mg slowly hot).
    • With oxygen: burn to MO; Ba also forms BaO₂ (peroxide).
    • With nitrogen: 3M + N₂ → M₃N₂ (all form nitrides — unlike alkali metals except Li).
    • With hydrogen: M + H₂ → MH₂ (ionic hydrides; CaH₂ = "hydrolith" for H₂ generation).
    • Hydroxides: solubility increases down (Be(OH)₂ insoluble → Ba(OH)₂ quite soluble); basicity increases (Be(OH)₂ amphoteric).
    • Carbonates/sulphates: carbonate solubility decreases down; sulphate solubility decreases sharply down (MgSO₄ soluble → BaSO₄ insoluble) — the basis of the SO₄²⁻ test with BaCl₂.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why Be and Mg show no flame colour

    • Flame colour requires easy excitation of valence electrons. Be and Mg have high ionisation energies (899, 738 kJ/mol) and small atoms whose excited states lie too high for Bunsen-flame energy — the electrons are not excited, so no emission. Ca, Sr, Ba excite readily and glow brick-red, crimson, apple-green.

    4.2 The sulphate solubility ladder

    • MgSO₄, CaSO₄ (sparingly), SrSO₄ (very low), BaSO₄ (insoluble, 2.4 mg/L). Lattice energy falls slowly down the group while hydration energy falls fast — the balance tips against dissolution. Result: BaCl₂ is the standard reagent for sulphate, and BaSO₄'s insolubility + non-absorption makes it the barium meal for X-rays (Ba²⁺ is toxic when absorbed — BaSO₄ is not).

    4.3 Why Ca reacts briskly with cold water but Mg barely

    • Reactivity tracks ionisation energy and oxide/hydroxide solubility: Ca²⁺'s hydroxide is moderately soluble, so the surface stays clean and reaction continues; Mg(OH)₂ is insoluble and coats the metal, quenching the reaction — until hot water or steam strips it (Mg + H₂O(steam) → MgO + H₂).

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Melting point has no monotonic trend here: unlike IA (falling), IIA mp values zig-zag (Be 1287, Mg 650, Ca 842, Sr 777, Ba 727 °C) — quoting "decreases down" is wrong.

  • Be is the odd one: amphoteric BeO/Be(OH)₂, covalent compounds, no flame colour — its tiny polarising ion makes it more like aluminium.
  • BaSO₄ in the stomach is safe; BaCl₂ is not: insolubility is the safety — Ba²⁺ absorbed is toxic (blocks K⁺ channels); the insoluble sulphate passes through unabsorbed.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Describe the general characteristics of alkaline earth metals: configuration, trends in ionisation energy and reactivity, and their reaction with water. — Alkaline earth metals (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra) have the outer configuration ns² and show only the +2 oxidation state. The ionisation energy decreases down the group (Be 899 → Ba 503 kJ/mol) as shells are added, so reactivity increases: with water, beryllium does not react even with steam, magnesium reacts only slowly with cold water ( briskly with steam, Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂), calcium reacts briskly with cold water (Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂↑), and strontium and barium react vigorously. The hydroxides MOH formed grow more soluble and more basic down the group.

  • NEB-style (2m): Give the flame colours of calcium, strontium and barium, and explain why beryllium and magnesium give none. — Calcium: brick-red; strontium: crimson; barium: apple-green. In the flame the outer electrons of the vapourised atoms are excited and emit their characteristic wavelengths on falling back. Beryllium and magnesium give no flame colour because their valence electrons are held too tightly (high ionisation energies, 899 and 738 kJ/mol) for Bunsen-flame energy to excite them — no excitation, no emission.
  • NEB-style (2m): Arrange the sulphates of Group IIA in order of solubility and explain the trend. — MgSO₄ (soluble) > CaSO₄ (sparingly) > SrSO₄ (very sparingly) > BaSO₄ (practically insoluble). Down the group the sulphate lattice energy decreases only slightly (the large SO₄²⁻ dominates), while the hydration energy of the shrinking… rather, growing M²⁺ ion decreases rapidly; the free-energy balance shifts against dissolution, so solubility falls. The near-insolubility of BaSO₄ makes BaCl₂ the test reagent for sulphate ions and BaSO₄ safe as the X-ray "barium meal".

  • NEB-style (3m): Describe the general characteristics of the alkaline earth metals: electronic configuration, trends in ionisation energy and reactivity, and their reaction with water. — The alkaline earth metals (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra) have the outer configuration ns² and exhibit only the +2 oxidation state, because the energy cost of removing the second s-electron is repaid by the large lattice energies of M²⁺ compounds. The first ionisation energy decreases down the group (Be 899 → Mg 738 → Ca 590 → Sr 550 → Ba 503 kJ/mol) as each element adds a shell, so metallic character and reactivity increase. With water: beryllium does not react even with steam (its oxide film and high IE); magnesium reacts only slowly with cold water but vigorously with steam, Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂; calcium reacts briskly with cold water, Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂↑; strontium and barium react vigorously. The hydroxides become more soluble and more strongly basic down the group.
  • NEB-style (2m): Give the flame colours of calcium, strontium and barium salts, and account for the absence of flame colour with beryllium and magnesium. — Calcium salts colour the flame brick-red, strontium salts crimson, and barium salts apple-green — the electrons of the vapourised metal atoms are excited by the flame and emit their characteristic wavelengths as they return. Beryllium and magnesium show no colour because their outer electrons are bound too strongly — ionisation energies of 899 and 738 kJ/mol — for the Bunsen flame's energy to excite them to any emissive level; without excitation there is no emission.
  • NEB-style (2m): State the trend in the solubility of the sulphates of Group IIA metals and explain it. — Solubility decreases sharply down the group: MgSO₄ is soluble, CaSO₄ sparingly soluble, SrSO₄ very sparingly soluble, and BaSO₄ practically insoluble (about 2.4 mg/L). The explanation balances two energies: descending the group, the lattice energy of MSO₄ falls only slightly (the big SO₄²⁻ ion dominates the lattice), while the hydration energy of the M²⁺ ion falls rapidly (the ion gets bigger and holds water less tightly). The dissolution free energy therefore turns increasingly unfavourable — and the near-zero solubility of BaSO₄ is exploited both as the BaCl₂ test for sulphate ions and as the safe X-ray contrast "barium meal".

Summary

Group IIA — Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra — carries ns² and lives at +2 only: the second ionisation energy (~1800 kJ/mol) is repaid by +2 lattice energies, making these metals harder, denser and higher-melting than the alkalis (though their melting points follow no tidy trend). Down the group radius grows, IE falls (899 → 503 kJ/mol), and reactivity climbs: Be ignores even steam, Mg needs heat (Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂), Ca fizzes briskly in cold water (Ca(OH)₂ + H₂), Sr and Ba surge. Flame colours — brick-red Ca, crimson Sr, apple-green Ba — skip Be and Mg, whose electrons flame energy cannot excite. All form nitrides M₃N₂ and hydrides MH₂ (CaH₂, "hydrolith"); hydroxides grow more soluble and basic downward (Be(OH)₂ amphoteric), carbonates grow less soluble, and the sulphate ladder — MgSO₄ soluble down to BaSO₄ insoluble — delivers both the BaCl₂ sulphate test and the safe barium meal.
