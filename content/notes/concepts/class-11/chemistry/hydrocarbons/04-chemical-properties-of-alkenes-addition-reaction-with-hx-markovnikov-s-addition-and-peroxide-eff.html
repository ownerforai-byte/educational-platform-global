Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The π Bond Under Attack

    A π bond is an exposed electron cloud — every electrophile wants it. Alkene chemistry is electrophilic addition: HX, H₂O, H₂SO₄ and O₃ all add across the double bond, with Markovnikov's rule and the peroxide effect deciding which carbon gets which fragment.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Addition of HX and Markovnikov's rule

    • CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CHBr–CH₃ (2-bromopropane, major).
    • Markovnikov's rule: the negative part of the addendum attaches to the carbon with fewer hydrogens; equivalently H⁺ goes to the carbon already holding more H.
    • Reason: the more stable carbocation (3° > 2° > 1°) forms on the more substituted carbon.

    2.2 The peroxide effect (anti-Markovnikov)

    • With peroxides present, HBr alone adds the reverse way: CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CH₂–CH₂Br (1-bromopropane).
    • Mechanism is free-radical: Br· adds first to give the more stable radical; only HBr shows this (HF/HCl/HI bonds are energetically wrong).

    2.3 Addition of H₂O and H₂SO₄

    • Hydration: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O (conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst) → CH₃CH₂OH — industrial ethanol.
    • CH₃–CH=CH₂ + H₂O → isopropanol (Markovnikov).
    • CH₂=CH₂ + H₂SO₄ → CH₃CH₂OSO₃H (ethyl hydrogen sulphate) → hydrolysis gives ethanol.

    2.4 Ozonolysis

    • O₃ at ~195 K adds to C=C forming an ozonide; Zn/H₂O cleaves it to carbonyl compounds.
    • CH₂=CH₂ → 2HCHO (formaldehyde); CH₃–CH=CH–CH₃ → 2CH₃CHO; terminal carbons give HCHO.
    • Locates the double bond — a structure-detection tool.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 The carbocation is the hinge

    • H⁺ adds first (electrophilic), creating a carbocation; the more substituted cation forms preferentially, and the anion then attacks it. Every Markovnikov question is a carbocation-stability question.

    4.2 Why only HBr misbehaves

    • The radical chain needs both steps exothermic: Br· adding to the alkene is, but Cl· is too reactive (unselective) and I· too weak (reversible). Hence the peroxide effect is HBr's private quirk.

    4.3 Ozonolysis as structure detection

    • Count the carbonyl fragments back together and the original double-bond position reappears — ozonolysis is how unknown alkene structures are read.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    The rule speaks of the negative part: it is Br⁻ that goes to the H-poor carbon — students misplace the H instead.

  • Peroxide effect is HBr-only: writing it for HCl or HI is a classic error — bond energetics forbid the chain.
  • Ozonolysis reductive workup: Zn/H₂O stops at aldehydes/ketones; oxidative workup would push to acids — quote Zn.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): State Markovnikov's rule and apply it to the addition of HBr to propene. (3m) — Markovnikov's rule: when an unsymmetrical reagent (HX) adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part of the reagent (X⁻) attaches to the carbon of the double bond bearing fewer hydrogen atoms, while H⁺ goes to the carbon bearing more hydrogens — because addition proceeds through the more stable carbocation. Application to propene: CH₃–CH=CH₂ has H on C2 (one H) and C1 (two H). H⁺ adds to C1, generating the secondary carbocation at C2 (rather than a primary one at C1); Br⁻ then attacks C2: CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CHBr–CH₃ (2-bromopropane, major product).

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): What happens when ethene undergoes ozonolysis? Write the products. (3m) — Ethene adds ozone at low temperature (~195 K) to form an ozonide, which on reductive cleavage with Zn/H₂O splits the C=C completely: CH₂=CH₂ + O₃ → ozonide → 2 HCHO. Both carbons of the double bond carry hydrogens, so both fragments are formaldehyde (methanal). Ozonolysis of an alkene always gives carbonyl compounds; terminal =CH₂ carbons give HCHO, which is how the position of a double bond is located.
  • NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): What happens when but-2-ene is treated with ozone followed by zinc and water? Give the equations involved and identify the products. (3m) — Step 1 — ozonide formation (at ~195 K): CH₃–CH=CH–CH₃ + O₃ → but-2-ene ozonide (a cyclic peroxide across the double bond). Step 2 — reductive cleavage (Zn/H₂O): the ozonide decomposes to carbonyl fragments: CH₃–CH=CH–CH₃ → 2 CH₃CHO — two molecules of ethanal (acetaldehyde). Because but-2-ene is symmetrical, both fragments are identical; an unsymmetrical alkene would give two different carbonyls, letting the original C=C position be reconstructed.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): State Markovnikov's rule and apply it to the addition of HBr to propene. (3m) — Markovnikov's rule: when an unsymmetrical reagent such as HX adds to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part of the reagent attaches to the doubly-bonded carbon that carries fewer hydrogen atoms (and H⁺ to the carbon with more hydrogens). The rule works because the first step is electrophilic: H⁺ adds to give a carbocation, and the pathway through the more stable carbocation (3° > 2° > 1°) dominates. Application: in propene (CH₃–CH=CH₂), C1 carries two H and C2 one H. H⁺ therefore adds to C1, placing the positive charge on C2 as a secondary carbocation (a primary cation at C1 would be far less stable); Br⁻ then attacks C2: CH₃–CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃–CHBr–CH₃, i.e. 2-bromopropane is the major product — not 1-bromopropane.
  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): What happens when ethene undergoes ozonolysis? Write the products. (3m) — Ethene first adds ozone across the double bond at low temperature (~195 K) to form a cyclic ozonide. On reductive work-up with zinc and water, the ozonide cleaves at the C=C bond to give carbonyl compounds: CH₂=CH₂ + O₃ → ozonide —(Zn/H₂O)→ 2 HCHO. Since both alkene carbons in ethene bear two hydrogens, both fragments are formaldehyde (methanal) — two moles per mole of ethene. Ozonolysis always cuts the double bond entirely and delivers a carbonyl at each former alkene carbon; terminal =CH₂ groups always yield HCHO, which is how chemists locate an unknown double bond.
  • NEB 2022 (verified PYQ): What happens when but-2-ene is treated with ozone followed by zinc and water? Give the equations involved and identify the products. (3m) — Step 1 — ozonide formation: but-2-ene adds ozone at low temperature (~195 K) to give but-2-ene ozonide, a five-membered cyclic peroxide in which the two carbons of the old double bond are bridged by the three oxygens: CH₃–CH=CH–CH₃ + O₃ → ozonide. Step 2 — reductive cleavage: treatment with zinc dust and water decomposes the ozonide, each former alkene carbon becoming a carbonyl: ozonide —(Zn/H₂O)→ 2 CH₃CHO. The products are two molecules of ethanal (acetaldehyde) — but-2-ene is symmetrical, so both halves are identical. Had the alkene been unsymmetrical, the two different carbonyl fragments would have revealed exactly where the original double bond sat.

Key Points

Summary

The alkene π bond invites electrophiles. HX adds by Markovnikov's rule — the negative part lands on the carbon with fewer hydrogens, because H⁺ first creates the more stable carbocation (propene + HBr → 2-bromopropane). In peroxides, HBr alone reverses (Kharasch): a free-radical chain puts Br on the H-rich carbon → 1-bromopropane — a quirk HCl, HBr's neighbours, cannot copy for bond-energetic reasons. Hydration with H₂SO₄/H₂O gives alcohols (ethene → ethanol, via ethyl hydrogen sulphate); direct H₂SO₄ addition forms the sulphate ester first. Ozonolysis — O₃ at ~195 K to the ozonide, then Zn/H₂O — cuts the C=C into carbonyls: ethene → 2 HCHO, but-2-ene → 2 CH₃CHO; read the fragments backwards and the double bond's position reappears.
