Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Two Drops That Tell the Truth

    Unsaturation announces itself in colour: bromine water decolourises (addition across π), and Baeyer's reagent — cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO₄ — loses its purple and deposits a brown solid (Baeyer's test). Ethene and ethyne pass both; ethane fails both.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Bromine water test

    • CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ (aq) → BrCH₂CH₂Br (1,2-dibromoethane) — the red-brown/orange colour of Br₂ disappears instantly at room temperature, without catalyst.
    • Ethyne: HC≡CH + 2Br₂ → CHBr₂CHBr₂ (1,1,2,2-tetrabromoethane) — colour goes, twice over.
    • Alkanes: no decolourisation in the dark/cold (substitution needs UV) — the negative control.

    2.2 Baeyer's test

    • Cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO₄ (Baeyer's reagent) adds two OH groups across C=C: 3CH₂=CH₂ + 2KMnO₄ + 4H₂O → 3CH₂(OH)CH₂(OH) + 2MnO₂↓ + 2KOH.
    • Purple → brown MnO₂ precipitate = unsaturation; the product of ethene is ethane-1,2-diol (glycol).
    • Ethyne oxidises further (to oxalic acid ultimately) but still kills the purple.

    2.3 The decision logic

    • Decolourised Br₂ water (no UV) OR discharged KMnO₄ (cold) → π bond present. Both negative → saturated.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why the tests work

    • π electrons are exposed and nucleophilic; Br₂ polarises and adds; KMnO₄ (Mn⁷⁺) is reduced to Mn⁴⁺ (brown MnO₂) while the alkene is oxidised. Saturated σ-frameworks can neither add nor reduce under these mild conditions.

    4.2 Conditions are the message

    • Cold and dilute keeps KMnO₄ from over-oxidising and cleaving the bond (hot/concentrated would cut the C=C to acids/CO₂). No UV for the bromine test keeps alkanes honest (they'd substitute in sunlight).

    4.3 Reading the results

    • Both tests are decolourisation tests: the eye is the instrument. Ethene and ethyne: instant discharge. Ethane: colour persists. Terminal vs internal alkynes: both positive — these tests detect unsaturation, not acidity.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Dark condition matters for Br₂: alkanes substitute in UV light — the test is only valid without light.

  • Brown ≠ purple: MnO₂ (brown solid) is the positive sign; faded purple without precipitate is ambiguous — look for the MnO₂.
  • Both tests say "unsaturated": neither distinguishes C=C from C≡C alone — pair them with the ammoniacal tests for the full picture.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): How will you distinguish ethane, ethene and ethyne using bromine water? (2m) — Add each gas to bromine water (red-brown). Ethene: the colour is instantly discharged — addition gives 1,2-dibromoethane: CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → BrCH₂–CH₂Br. Ethyne: the colour is also discharged (addition of two Br₂): HC≡CH + 2Br₂ → CHBr₂–CHBr₂ (1,1,2,2-tetrabromoethane). Ethane: no decolourisation in the dark — saturated alkanes do not add to bromine water (they would substitute only in UV light). Ethene and ethyne both decolourise; to separate those two, use the ammoniacal AgNO₃ test (ethyne gives white Ag₂C₂, ethene nothing).

  • NEB-style (2m): Describe Baeyer's test for ethene with equation. — Shake ethene with cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO₄ (Baeyer's reagent): the purple colour disappears and a brown precipitate of MnO₂ forms, while the alkene is converted to a diol: 3CH₂=CH₂ + 2KMnO₄ + 4H₂O → 3CH₂(OH)–CH₂(OH) + 2MnO₂↓ + 2KOH — ethene becomes ethane-1,2-diol (glycol). A saturated hydrocarbon leaves the reagent unchanged.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why must the bromine water test be done away with sunlight? — In UV light, alkanes undergo free-radical substitution with bromine (slowly decolourising it), which would give a false positive for ethane. The test's discriminating power rests on addition (instant, dark, cold) versus substitution (UV only) — so the bromine water test is performed in the dark and in the cold, where only π bonds can consume the bromine.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): How will you distinguish ethane, ethene and ethyne using bromine water? (2m) — Bubble each gas through bromine water (red-brown) in the dark and cold. Ethene: the colour is instantly discharged by addition across the double bond: CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → BrCH₂–CH₂Br (1,2-dibromoethane). Ethyne: the colour is also discharged, consuming two equivalents of bromine across the triple bond: HC≡CH + 2Br₂ → CHBr₂–CHBr₂ (1,1,2,2-tetrabromoethane). Ethane: no decolourisation — a saturated alkane cannot add to bromine and will substitute only under UV light (hence the dark condition). So ethene and ethyne both decolourise bromine water while ethane does not; to separate ethene from ethyne, follow with the ammoniacal silver nitrate test — ethyne alone gives the white Ag₂C₂ precipitate.
  • NEB-style (2m): Describe Baeyer's test for ethene with equation. — Shake ethene with cold, dilute, alkaline potassium permanganate — Baeyer's reagent. The purple permanganate is decolourised and a brown precipitate of MnO₂ appears, while the double bond is converted into two hydroxyl groups (syn dihydroxylation): 3CH₂=CH₂ + 2KMnO₄ + 4H₂O → 3CH₂(OH)–CH₂(OH) + 2MnO₂↓ + 2KOH. The organic product is ethane-1,2-diol (glycol). Any unsaturated hydrocarbon gives this purple-to-brown change; saturated hydrocarbons leave the reagent untouched. The conditions matter — hot or concentrated KMnO₄ would cleave the double bond to acids instead of stopping at the diol.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why must the bromine water test be performed away from sunlight? — Because ultraviolet light initiates free-radical substitution of bromine onto alkanes: ethane in sunlight would slowly consume Br₂ and fade the colour, producing a false positive for unsaturation. The test distinguishes π bonds from σ bonds only through the contrast between instant addition (alkenes/alkynes, dark, cold) and UV-dependent substitution (alkanes). Performed in the dark, bromine water responds exclusively to unsaturation, and the negative control — ethane keeping its colour — becomes trustworthy.

Key Points

  • Bromine water test: CH2=CH2 + Br2(aq) -> BrCH2CH2Br (instant decolourisation, dark, cold); HCCH + 2Br2 -> CHBr2CHBr2 (tetrabromoethane); ethane no change in dark (UV would substitute - false positive risk). Baeyer's test: cold dilute alkaline KMnO4; 3CH2=CH2 + 2KMnO4 + 4H2O -> 3CH2OH-CH2OH (ethane-1,2-diol, glycol) + 2MnO2 brown ppt + 2KOH; purple -> brown = unsaturated; hot/conc KMnO4 would cleave to acids - keep it cold and dilute. Both tests detect unsaturation generally; ammoniacal tests separate terminal alkyne from alkene.

Summary

Two colour tests expose unsaturation. Bromine water: ethene discharges the red-brown instantly — CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → BrCH₂CH₂Br; ethyne consumes two Br₂ — CHBr₂CHBr₂; ethane does nothing in the dark (UV would substitute, so darkness keeps the test honest). Baeyer's test: cold, dilute, alkaline KMnO₄ is decolourised with brown MnO₂ depositing while the π bond gains two OH groups — ethene → ethane-1,2-diol; keep it cold and dilute or the bond cleaves instead. Both tests certify "unsaturated" but cannot by themselves split ethene from ethyne — that final call belongs to the ammoniacal AgNO₃ test, where only ethyne's acidic ≡C–H gives the white precipitate.
