Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Two π Bonds, One Carbon Rod

    Alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂) carry a C≡C triple bond. The syllabus routes to them start from the elements themselves (carbon + hydrogen → ethyne via calcium carbide) and from vicinal dihalides (1,2-dibromoethane) and haloform-derived substrates (chloroform/iodoform routes).

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 From carbon and hydrogen — industrial ethyne

    • CaO + 3C → CaC₂ + CO (electric furnace, ~2000 °C) — calcium carbide.
    • CaC₂ + 2H₂O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)₂ — ethyne (acetylene) on contact with water.
    • Impure carbide gives PH₃/H₂S contamination — purified through acid or CuCl/NH₃ washes.

    2.2 From 1,2-dibromoethane (vicinal dihalide)

    • BrCH₂CH₂Br + 2 alc. KOH → HC≡CH + 2KBr + 2H₂O — double dehydrohalogenation.
    • First equivalent gives the vinyl halide (CH₂=CHBr); the second (stronger base, NaNH₂ often needed) removes the second HX.

    2.3 From chloroform/iodoform routes

    • CHCl₃ + 2Ag (or Ag powder, Δ) → HC≡CH + 2AgCl — dehalogenation of a dihalomethane.
    • CHI₃ + 2Ag → HC≡CH + 2AgI — the silver-salt route (2×2 dehalogenation).
    • General: dihalomethanes + heated silver → alkyne + 2AgX.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why two eliminations are needed

    • A triple bond needs two π bonds; the first elimination makes the alkene (vinyl halide), the second must remove HX from an sp² carbon — harder, hence the stronger base (NaNH₂) for the second step in modern practice.

    4.2 The carbide chemistry

    • CaC₂ is an acetylide salt: C₂²⁻ + 2H₂O → C₂H₂ + 2OH⁻. The water attacks the carbide carbon; the reaction is violently exothermic — the old miners' lamp chemistry.

    4.3 Silver's role

    • Hot silver scavenges halogens as stable AgX, pulling two halides off the small carbon framework and leaving the C≡C behind.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Calcium carbide is not carbon: the furnace step (CaO + 3C → CaC₂ + CO) precedes the hydrolysis — write both equations.

  • Vinyl halide intermediate: the first elimination stops at CH₂=CHBr; the second needs stronger conditions.
  • Ag route stoichiometry: two Ag per halomethane — 2AgCl or 2AgI by-products belong in the equation.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): How is ethyne prepared from calcium carbide? Give both equations. — Step 1: limestone-derived quicklime (CaO) is heated with coke in an electric furnace at ~2000 °C: CaO + 3C → CaC₂ + CO↑ — calcium carbide forms. Step 2: carbide is treated with water: CaC₂ + 2H₂O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)₂ — ethyne (acetylene) evolves as a gas (the carbide's C₂²⁻ anion is protonated by water). Technical carbide carries phosphide/sulphide impurities, so the gas is washed to remove PH₃ and H₂S before use.

  • NEB-style (2m): Prepare ethyne from 1,2-dibromoethane. — 1,2-Dibromoethane (a vicinal dihalide) undergoes double dehydrohalogenation with two equivalents of alcoholic KOH (strong base, heat): BrCH₂CH₂Br + 2KOH → HC≡CH + 2KBr + 2H₂O. The first elimination gives the vinyl halide CH₂=CHBr; the second removes HBr from that sp² carbon to complete the triple bond.
  • NEB-style (2m): How does chloroform give ethyne? — Chloroform is dehalogenated by heated silver powder: CHCl₃ + 2Ag → HC≡CH + 2AgCl. Silver abstracts the three chlorines' partners stepwise — formally removing two equivalents of HCl equivalents — leaving the C≡C triple bond; the same chemistry works for iodoform (CHI₃ + 2Ag → HC≡CH + 2AgI).

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): How is ethyne prepared from calcium carbide? Give both equations. — Step 1 — carbide manufacture: quicklime (CaO) and coke (C) are heated in an electric furnace at about 2000 °C: CaO + 3C → CaC₂ + CO↑. Calcium carbide is effectively the salt of the acetylide anion (C₂²⁻). Step 2 — hydrolysis: carbide is treated with water: CaC₂ + 2H₂O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)₂. Water protonates the carbide dianion, releasing ethyne (acetylene) gas and slaked lime. The reaction is strongly exothermic. Technical carbide contains calcium phosphide and sulphide impurities, so the crude gas carries PH₃ and H₂S and is washed (through acid or ammoniacal CuCl) before use — pure ethyne has the characteristic garlic-like odour of the impurities removed.
  • NEB-style (2m): Prepare ethyne from 1,2-dibromoethane. — 1,2-Dibromoethane is a vicinal dihalide (halogens on adjacent carbons). Heating it with two equivalents of alcoholic KOH effects double dehydrohalogenation: BrCH₂CH₂Br + 2KOH(alc.) → HC≡CH + 2KBr + 2H₂O. The first elimination removes HBr to give the vinyl halide, bromoethene (CH₂=CHBr); the second must remove HBr from that sp² carbon, which is harder and needs the stronger basic medium (in modern practice NaNH₂ in liquid NH₃). Completion of the second elimination creates the C≡C triple bond of ethyne.
  • NEB-style (2m): How does chloroform give ethyne? — Chloroform is heated with silver powder, which scavenges halogen as insoluble silver chloride: CHCl₃ + 2Ag → HC≡CH + 2AgCl. Formally two equivalents of HCl are stripped from the single carbon framework, and the carbons couple into the triple bond. The identical chemistry applies to iodoform: CHI₃ + 2Ag → HC≡CH + 2AgI. In both cases the driving force is the stability of the AgX lattice — silver's love of halide pays for the dehalogenation.

Key Points

  • Alkynes CnH2n-2, sp, C≡C. Preparation: (1) from elements: CaO + 3C -> CaC2 + CO (electric furnace ~2000C); CaC2 + 2H2O -> HCCH + Ca(OH)2 (industrial ethyne; impure gas washed of PH3/H2S). (2) From 1,2-dibromoethane (vicinal dihalide): BrCH2CH2Br + 2 alc. KOH -> HCCH + 2KBr + 2H2O; first elimination gives vinyl halide CH2=CHBr, second needs stronger base (NaNH2). (3) From chloroform/iodoform: CHCl3 + 2Ag (hot) -> HCCH + 2AgCl; CHI3 + 2Ag -> HCCH + 2AgI (dehalogenation by silver).

Summary

Alkynes (CₙH₂ₙ₋₂) come from three doors. From the elements: electric-furnace fusion of lime and coke — CaO + 3C → CaC₂ + CO — followed by hydrolysis — CaC₂ + 2H₂O → HC≡CH + Ca(OH)₂ — the industrial acetylene route (gas washed free of PH₃/H₂S). From vicinal dihalides: 1,2-dibromoethane + 2 alc. KOH → HC≡CH + 2KBr + 2H₂O, a double dehydrohalogenation that pauses at the vinyl halide before the tougher second elimination (NaNH₂ territory). From haloforms: hot silver strips the halogens — CHCl₃ + 2Ag → HC≡CH + 2AgCl (and CHI₃ → 2AgI) — driven by the stability of the silver halide lattice. Two eliminations or a carbide's dianion: either way, the triple bond is assembled.
