Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Quiet Family Speaks

    Alkanes have only strong, non-polar C–C and C–H σ bonds, so they ignore ionic reagents and burn or substitute instead. Their chemistry is free-radical substitution (halogenation), plus harsh-condition nitration, sulphonation, and combustion/oxidation — with ethane's controlled oxidation as the set example.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Halogenation (free-radical substitution)

    • CH₄ + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + HCl (diffused sunlight/UV); continues: CH₂Cl₂ → CHCl₃ → CCl₄.
    • Mechanism: initiation (Cl₂ → 2Cl·), propagation (Cl· + CH₄ → CH₃· + HCl; CH₃· + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl·), termination (radical combination).
    • Reactivity of halogens: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂ (fluorine explodes; iodine is reversible).
    • Higher alkanes give mixtures — H abstraction at any position.

    2.2 Nitration (vapour phase)

    • CH₄ + HNO₃ → CH₃NO₂ + H₂O at ~400 °C — the radical route (nitroethane from ethane similarly); ionic nitration fails on the inert C–H bonds.

    2.3 Sulphonation

    • Only longer alkanes react: C₆H₁₄ + H₂SO₄ (fuming) → C₆H₁₃SO₃H + H₂O — a sulphonic acid; methane/ethane are too unreactive.

    2.4 Oxidation

    • Complete: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O (ΔH ≈ −890 kJ/mol) — the fuel reaction.
    • Controlled (ethane): with O₂ over a heated catalyst or limited conditions, ethane gives CH₃CHO (acetaldehyde) and can proceed to CH₃COOH; methane gives CH₃OH/HCHO under controlled oxidation.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why substitution, not addition

    • With no π bond to attack, ionic reagents find no foothold; radicals (which need only one electron) can abstract H from strong C–H bonds when heat or light supplies the activation energy.

    4.2 The product mixture problem

    • Once CH₃Cl forms, further chlorination continues — CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃, CCl₄ — so methane chlorination is a mixture; excess methane suppresses over-chlorination. For higher alkanes, different H positions (1°/2°/3°) give positional mixtures.

    4.3 Oxidation as energy chemistry

    • Complete combustion is why alkanes are fuels; controlled oxidation is why they are also feedstock — the same C–H bonds, two destinies, set by oxygen supply and catalyst.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Substitution, not addition: alkanes have nothing to add to; every halogenation replaces an H.

  • Nitration needs vapour phase: aqueous HNO₃ does nothing to methane — the ~400 °C vapour reaction is the exam answer.
  • Iodination runs backwards: HI reduces the alkyl iodide back, so I₂ halogenation needs HNO₃ or HIO₃ to remove HI.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2020 (verified PYQ): Explain the mechanism of the free-radical chlorination of methane in the presence of ultraviolet light, and account for the mixture of products formed. (3m) — Initiation: UV light splits chlorine homolytically: Cl₂ → 2Cl·. Propagation: Cl· abstracts a hydrogen: Cl· + CH₄ → CH₃· + HCl; the methyl radical then attacks another chlorine molecule: CH₃· + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl· — regenerating Cl·, so one photon sustains many conversions. Termination: radicals combine — Cl· + Cl· → Cl₂, CH₃· + Cl· → CH₃Cl, CH₃· + CH₃· → C₂H₆. The mixture: the reaction does not stop at CH₃Cl — the product is itself an alkane derivative whose C–H bonds can be substituted further, so CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃ and CCl₄ form successively; excess methane and controlled Cl₂ limit over-chlorination.

  • NEB-style (2m): How does ethane undergo controlled oxidation? — Ethane heated with a limited supply of O₂ over a heated catalyst (e.g. Cu at ~200 °C, or controlled air oxidation) gives acetaldehyde: CH₃CH₃ + O₂ → CH₃CHO + H₂O; further mild oxidation gives acetic acid (CH₃COOH). Unlike combustion, only part of the molecule is oxidised — the product is an oxygenate, not CO₂.
  • NEB-style (2m): Arrange F₂, Cl₂, Br₂, I₂ in order of alkane halogenation reactivity with reason. — F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. Reactivity tracks how easily the halogen radical abstracts hydrogen and how strong the new H–X bond formed is: fluorine's F–H bond is enormously strong (violently exothermic, explosive), chlorine is fast, bromine slower but more selective, and iodination is reversible because HI reduces the product back — it needs an oxidant to remove HI.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2020 (verified PYQ): Explain the mechanism of the free-radical chlorination of methane in the presence of ultraviolet light, and account for the mixture of products formed. (3m) — Initiation: ultraviolet light supplies the energy to split the Cl–Cl bond homolytically: Cl₂ → 2Cl·. Propagation: the chlorine radical abstracts a hydrogen atom from methane: Cl· + CH₄ → CH₃· + HCl; the methyl radical then attacks a chlorine molecule: CH₃· + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl· — the Cl· consumed in step one is regenerated in step two, so the two propagation steps cycle and one absorbed photon produces many molecules of product (a chain reaction). Termination: the chain ends when two radicals combine: Cl· + Cl· → Cl₂, CH₃· + Cl· → CH₃Cl, or CH₃· + CH₃· → C₂H₆ (traces of ethane confirm the radical path). Why a mixture: chlorination does not stop at monochlorination — CH₃Cl still has C–H bonds, so Cl· continues substituting: CH₃Cl → CH₂Cl₂ → CHCl₃ → CCl₄. Unless methane is kept in large excess, all four chlorinated products form together; over-chlorination is suppressed by using excess methane and only limited Cl₂.
  • NEB-style (2m): How does ethane undergo controlled oxidation? — Under controlled oxidation (limited oxygen, heated catalyst such as Cu at about 200 °C, or controlled air oxidation at high pressure), ethane is only partially oxidised to an oxygenated product: CH₃CH₃ + O₂ → CH₃CHO + H₂O (acetaldehyde); under further mild conditions it proceeds to acetic acid, CH₃COOH. This contrasts with complete combustion, where the whole molecule burns to CO₂ and H₂O — the oxygen supply and catalyst decide whether ethane is a fuel or a feedstock.
  • NEB-style (2m): Arrange F₂, Cl₂, Br₂ and I₂ in order of reactivity toward alkane halogenation, with reason. — The order is F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂. Reactivity follows the ease of H-abstraction and the strength of the H–X bond formed: fluorine reacts explosively (the C–F and H–F bonds are extremely strong, the reaction uncontrolled); chlorine reacts fast under UV but gives product mixtures; bromine is slower but more selective (prefers the weakest C–H bond); iodination is reversible — the HI formed reduces the alkyl iodide back to the alkane — so it proceeds only when an oxidising agent (HNO₃ or HIO₃) destroys the HI.

Summary

Alkanes answer only to radicals and brute conditions. Halogenation is free-radical substitution under UV: initiation (Cl₂ → 2Cl·), propagation (Cl· + CH₄ → CH₃· + HCl; CH₃· + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl·), termination (radical combination) — the NEB 2020 mechanism, whose product mixture arises because CH₃Cl keeps reacting on to CH₂Cl₂, CHCl₃ and CCl₄. Halogen reactivity runs F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂ (iodination reversible). Nitration needs HNO₃ vapour at ~400 °C (CH₄ → CH₃NO₂); sulphonation needs fuming H₂SO₄ and a long chain (C₆H₁₃SO₃H). Oxidation splits into complete combustion (CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O, ≈890 kJ — the fuel reaction) and controlled oxidation, which stops at oxygenates: ethane → CH₃CHO → CH₃COOH. Same bonds, two destinies — set by oxygen supply and catalyst.
