Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Add Like an Alkene, Acid Like No Other

    The C≡C bond adds H₂, HX and H₂O just as C=C does (twice over), but the sp carbons hold their electrons so tightly that a terminal alkyne hydrogen becomes acidic — the only hydrocarbon H that metals can replace. That acidity powers the ammoniacal AgNO₃ and Cu₂Cl₂ tests.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Addition of H₂

    • HC≡CH + H₂ (Lindlar) → CH₂=CH₂; + H₂ (Ni, excess) → CH₃–CH₃.
    • Two π bonds → up to two equivalents of H₂; catalyst choice sets the stop point.

    2.2 Addition of HX

    • HC≡CH + HCl (HgCl₂ catalyst) → CH₂=CHCl (vinyl chloride — PVC feedstock).
    • HC≡CH + HBr → CH₂=CHBr → CH₃CHBr₂ (Markovnikov, twice for internal alkynes).

    2.3 Addition of H₂O (hydration)

    • HC≡CH + H₂O (H₂SO₄, HgSO₄) → CH₃CHO — Markovnikov water addition gives the enol, which tautomerises to acetaldehyde.
    • Internal alkynes give ketones; only ethyne gives an aldehyde.

    2.4 Acidic nature and metal tests

    • Terminal ≡C–H (sp, 50% s-character) is weakly acidic (pKa ≈ 25): HC≡CH + Na → HC≡CNa + ½H₂; + NaNH₂ → sodium acetylide.
    • Ammoniacal AgNO₃: HC≡CH → white precipitate Ag₂C₂ (silver acetylide).
    • Ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂: → red precipitate Cu₂C₂ (copper acetylide).
    • Only terminal alkynes respond — internal ones (no ≡C–H) do not.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why sp hydrogens are acidic

    • An sp orbital is 50% s-character: the carbanion's lone pair sits closer to the nucleus, stabilising it. pKa ladder: alkane ≈ 50, alkene ≈ 44, alkyne ≈ 25 — weak acids, but acids.

    4.2 Hydration's tautomer detour

    • Water adds Markovnikov-wise to give vinyl alcohol (enol) — unstable; the H migrates from O to C and the product is the carbonyl. Ethyne's enol is the special one that lands on an aldehyde; all other alkynes land on ketones.

    4.3 The tests as structure probes

    • Ag₂C₂ (white) and Cu₂C₂ (red) form only when a ≡C–H exists — so these precipitates distinguish terminal from internal alkynes. Dry acetylides of heavy metals are explosive — handle wet, dispose fresh.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Ethyne hydration gives an aldehyde, not an alcohol or ketone: the enol tautomerises all the way to CH₃CHO — the one aldehyde in the family.

  • Only terminal alkynes give precipitates: internal alkynes have no ≡C–H — a negative AgNO₃ test is informative, not a failure.
  • Acidity is relative: pKa 25 is "acidic" only next to 44 and 50 — water and alcohols are far stronger acids.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Acetylene is acidic in nature. Explain with a chemical test. (3m) — In ethyne (HC≡CH) the C–H bond uses an sp hybrid orbital (50% s-character), which holds the bonding electrons closer to the carbon nucleus; when the proton is removed, the resulting acetylide carbanion is correspondingly stabilised (pKa ≈ 25 — far more acidic than alkenes, pKa ≈ 44, or alkanes, pKa ≈ 50). Hence terminal alkynes react with strong bases and active metals: HC≡CH + 2Na → 2HC≡CNa + H₂↑ (sodium acetylide + hydrogen). Chemical test: with ammoniacal silver nitrate, ethyne gives a white precipitate of silver acetylide, Ag₂C₂: HC≡CH + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ → Ag₂C₂↓ + 2NH₄⁺ + 2NH₃; with ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂ it gives a red precipitate of Cu₂C₂. These precipitations prove the acidic ≡C–H — and only terminal alkynes give them.

  • NEB-style (2m): What is the product of hydration of ethyne and how is it formed? — Ethyne + H₂O (dilute H₂SO₄, HgSO₄ catalyst) → acetaldehyde (CH₃CHO). Water adds Markovnikov-style across the triple bond to give vinyl alcohol (CH₂=CHOH), an enol, which is unstable and tautomerises — the O–H hydrogen migrates to carbon and the π bond shifts — giving the carbonyl compound acetaldehyde. Ethyne is the only alkyne whose hydration yields an aldehyde; all other alkynes give ketones.
  • NEB-style (2m): Distinguish between but-1-yne and but-2-yne. — But-1-yne is terminal (has ≡C–H): with ammoniacal AgNO₃ it gives a white Ag₂C₂ precipitate, and with ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂ a red Cu₂C₂ precipitate. But-2-yne is internal (no ≡C–H): no precipitate with either reagent. The acidic terminal hydrogen is the entire difference — sp-carbon acidity is absent in the internal isomer.

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): Acetylene is acidic in nature. Explain with a chemical test. (3m) — Why acidic: in ethyne the C–H bond is formed from an sp hybrid orbital with 50% s-character; s electrons lie closer to the nucleus, so the carbon holds its bonding electrons tightly. When a proton is removed, the remaining acetylide carbanion is unusually stable for a hydrocarbon — ethyne has pKa ≈ 25, far more acidic than ethene (≈44) or ethane (≈50). Both precipitations replace the acidic ≡C–H protons with metal — direct evidence of acidity.   • NEB-style (2m): What is the product of hydration of ethyne and how is it formed? — Ethyne adds water in the presence of dilute H₂SO₄ and HgSO₄ catalyst: the water adds Markovnikov-fashion across the triple bond to give vinyl alcohol, CH₂=CHOH — an enol. Enols are unstable: the hydrogen on oxygen migrates to the adjacent carbon while the π bond shifts, a process called tautomerisation, yielding the carbonyl compound acetaldehyde, CH₃CHO: HC≡CH + H₂O —(H₂SO₄/HgSO₄)→ CH₃CHO. But-2-yne (CH₃–C≡C–CH₃) is internal: it has no ≡C–H hydrogen, so no precipitate forms with either reagent. Key Points

Summary

Alkynes add like alkenes — twice. H₂: Lindlar stops at the alkene, excess Ni gives the alkane. HX: HCl over HgCl₂ gives vinyl chloride; HBr adds Markovnikov, twice for the full dihalide. H₂O (H₂SO₄/HgSO₄): Markovnikov hydration gives the enol, which tautomerises — ethyne lands on acetaldehyde (the family's only aldehyde), all others on ketones. The signature chemistry is acidity: the sp C–H (50% s-character, pKa ≈ 25) surrenders its proton to Na or NaNH₂ forming acetylides, and to ammoniacal AgNO₃ (white Ag₂C₂) and ammoniacal Cu₂Cl₂ (red Cu₂C₂) — precipitates that certify a terminal alkyne, since internal ones carry no ≡C–H and stay silent.
