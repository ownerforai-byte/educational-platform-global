Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Making the π Bond

    Alkenes carry one C=C double bond (CₙH₂ₙ, sp², planar). Their preparations all create that π bond by elimination: water out of an alcohol (dehydration), HX out of a haloalkane (dehydrohalogenation), or H₂ off an alkyne (catalytic hydrogenation of the triple bond).

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Dehydration of alcohols

    • C₂H₅OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O with conc. H₂SO₄ at ~170 °C (or Al₂O₃, ~350 °C).
    • Ethanol → ethene; propan-2-ol → propene. Acid-catalysed: protonate –OH, lose water, lose H⁺.
    • Competing substitution (ethoxyethane) at lower temperature (~140 °C) — temperature decides.

    2.2 Dehydrohalogenation (β-elimination)

    • CH₃CH₂Br + alc. KOH → CH₂=CH₂ + KBr + H₂O — alcoholic KOH, heat.
    • The β-hydrogen (next carbon over) is removed along with the halogen; Saytzeff: the more substituted (more stable) alkene dominates when two β-sites exist.

    2.3 Catalytic hydrogenation of alkynes (partial)

    • HC≡CH + H₂ (Lindlar catalyst: Pd on BaSO₄ poisoned with quinoline) → CH₂=CH₂.
    • Partial hydrogenation stops at the alkene; with excess H₂/Ni it would run on to ethane.
    • Na/NH₃ (liquid) also reduces alkynes to alkenes (trans-alkene for internal alkynes).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Temperature controls the exit

    • Ethanol + H₂SO₄ at 170 °C → elimination (ethene); at 140 °C → substitution (ethoxyethane). Same reagents, different destiny — a classic exam discriminator.

    4.2 Why alcoholic KOH, not aqueous

    • Aqueous KOH substitutes (gives the alcohol); alcoholic KOH provides the base that removes the β-H — elimination needs the anhydrous, basic medium.

    4.3 Stopping at the alkene

    • Alkynes hydrogenate twice as fast as alkenes once formed, so ordinary catalysts overshoot. Poisoning the catalyst (Lindlar) deactivates it just enough to halt at the alkene — chemoselectivity by sabotage.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    170 vs 140 °C: ethene at 170 °C, ether at 140 °C — quote both numbers to earn the mark.

  • Alc. KOH ≠ aq. KOH: the solvent word decides elimination vs substitution.
  • Lindlar stops at the alkene: full hydrogenation (Ni, excess H₂) gives the alkane — the poisoned catalyst is the point.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): How is ethane prepared from ethene? Write the reaction mechanism. (3m) — Ethene is converted to ethane by catalytic hydrogenation: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃ over Ni (~200 °C) or Pt/Pd at room temperature. Mechanism (surface catalysis): (1) adsorption — H₂ dissociates into H atoms adsorbed on the metal surface, and ethene's π bond coordinates to the surface; (2) syn addition — two surface H atoms add to the same face of the double bond stepwise, first forming an adsorbed ethyl intermediate, then ethane; (3) desorption — the saturated ethane leaves the surface, freeing the catalyst. The addition is stereospecific (syn) because both hydrogens come from the metal face.

  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): How is ethane prepared from ethene? Write the reaction mechanism. (3m) — Same chemistry: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃ (Ni/Pt catalyst). Mechanism: H₂ is adsorbed and dissociated on the catalyst surface into H atoms; ethene adsorbs through its π bond; the surface H atoms add one after the other to the same face (syn addition) giving the adsorbed ethyl radical → ethane; the product desorbs, regenerating the catalyst. Conditions: Ni at ~200 °C, or Pt/Pd at room temperature.
  • NEB-style (2m): How is ethene obtained from ethanol? — By dehydration with conc. H₂SO₄ at ~170 °C: C₂H₅OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O (or over Al₂O₃ at ~350 °C). The acid protonates the –OH, water departs as a good leaving group, and a β-hydrogen is lost to form the π bond. (At ~140 °C the same reagents give ethoxyethane instead — temperature is decisive.)

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2023 (verified PYQ): How is ethane prepared from ethene? Write the reaction mechanism. (3m) — Ethane is prepared from ethene by catalytic hydrogenation: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃, over nickel at ~200 °C (or Pt/Pd at room temperature). Mechanism (heterogeneous surface catalysis): (1) Adsorption — the H₂ molecule adsorbs onto the metal surface and dissociates into hydrogen atoms; ethene adsorbs alongside it through its π bond. (2) Syn addition — the surface hydrogen atoms add to the double bond one at a time and from the same face: the first H converts ethene into an adsorbed ethyl intermediate, the second completes ethane. Because both hydrogens arrive from the metal surface, the addition is stereospecific (syn). (3) Desorption — the saturated ethane, no longer able to bind the surface, leaves and frees the catalyst for the next cycle.
  • NEB 2024 (verified PYQ): How is ethane prepared from ethene? Write the reaction mechanism. (3m) — By catalytic hydrogenation: CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃ (Ni/Pt catalyst, Ni ~200 °C or Pt/Pd at room temperature). Mechanism: the reaction occurs on the catalyst surface — (1) H₂ is adsorbed and split into H atoms on the metal; ethene adsorbs through its π electrons. (2) The two surface hydrogens add stepwise to the same face of the double bond (syn addition), passing through an adsorbed ethyl intermediate to give ethane. (3) Ethane desorbs from the surface, regenerating the catalyst. The overall change is reduction of the π bond to a σ bond — one mole of H₂ per mole of alkene.
  • NEB-style (2m): How is ethene obtained from ethanol? — By dehydration of ethanol with concentrated H₂SO₄ at about 170 °C (or by passing vapour over Al₂O₃ at ~350 °C): C₂H₅OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O. Mechanistically the acid protonates the –OH group, making water a good leaving group; water departs and a β-hydrogen is eliminated to create the π bond. Note the temperature discrimination: at ~140 °C the same reagents give ethoxyethane (substitution) instead of ethene (elimination).

Key Points

  • Alkenes CnH2n, sp2 planar, one C=C. Preparation: (1) dehydration of alcohols — C2H5OH -> CH2=CH2 + H2O, conc H2SO4 ~170C or Al2O3 ~350C; 140C gives ether instead. (2) Dehydrohalogenation — CH3CH2Br + alc. KOH -> CH2=CH2 + KBr + H2O; beta-elimination; Saytzeff rule (more substituted alkene major). (3) Partial catalytic hydrogenation of alkynes — HCCH + H2 (Lindlar, Pd/BaSO4 poisoned with quinoline) -> CH2=CH2; or Na/liquid NH3 (trans); excess H2/Ni would give alkane. Full hydrogenation of alkene: CH2=CH2 + H2 -> CH3CH3 (Ni 200C / Pt rt), surface mechanism: adsorption of H2 (dissociation) + alkene, syn addition, desorption.

Summary

Alkenes (CₙH₂ₙ) are built by elimination. Dehydration: ethanol loses water over conc. H₂SO₄ at 170 °C (Al₂O₃ at 350 °C) → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O — but at 140 °C the same mix gives the ether, so the temperature is the discriminator. Dehydrohalogenation: alc. KOH pulls the β-hydrogen while the halide leaves — CH₃CH₂Br → CH₂=CH₂ + KBr + H₂O — with Saytzeff's rule favouring the more substituted alkene. Partial hydrogenation: an alkyne plus H₂ over the Lindlar catalyst (Pd/BaSO₄ poisoned with quinoline) stops exactly at the alkene, since ordinary Ni with excess H₂ would run through to the alkane. Conversely, ethene → ethane is catalytic hydrogenation — CH₂=CH₂ + H₂ → CH₃–CH₃ — via the surface mechanism the NEB 2023 and 2024 papers ask for: adsorption (H₂ dissociates on the metal), syn addition (both H from the same face, via an adsorbed ethyl), desorption.
