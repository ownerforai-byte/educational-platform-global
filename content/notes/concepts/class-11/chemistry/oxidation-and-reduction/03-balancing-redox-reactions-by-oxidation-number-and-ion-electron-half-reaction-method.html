Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Making Electrons Balance on Paper

    A redox equation must balance twice: atoms and electrons. Two formal methods deliver this — the oxidation-number method (compare numbers, equalise total increase and decrease) and the ion-electron (half-reaction) method (split into oxidation and reduction halves, balance each, add). Both converge on the same balanced equation; exams ask for either by name (NEB 2023 and NEB 2024 both tested them). Alongside sits the equivalent-mass calculation that turns a balanced redox into titration arithmetic.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Oxidation-number method — the steps

    1. Write the skeleton equation and assign oxidation numbers to every atom.
    2. Identify the elements whose numbers change; note the increase and decrease per atom.
    3. Equalise total increase = total decrease by multiplying species with suitable coefficients.
    4. Balance the remaining atoms by inspection (metals, then non-metals, then O with H₂O, then H with H⁺ if ionic/acidic).
    5. For ionic equations, add electrons implicitly via the numbers and check the charge balance.

    2.2 Ion-electron (half-reaction) method — the steps

    1. Write the two half-reactions — oxidation and reduction — in ionic form.
    2. Balance each: atoms other than O and H first; then O by adding H₂O; then H by adding H⁺ (acidic medium; for basic medium, finish with OH⁻); finally charge by adding electrons.
    3. Multiply the halves so electrons lost = electrons gained.
    4. Add the halves; cancel common species (electrons, H⁺, H₂O appearing on both sides).

    2.3 Equivalent mass of an oxidant/reductant

    • Equivalent mass = formula mass ÷ n-factor, where n-factor = electrons gained or lost per formula unit in that reaction.
    • K₂Cr₂O₇ in acid: each Cr falls +6 → +3 (3 e⁻ × 2 Cr) → n = 6 → equivalent mass = 294/6 = 49.
    • KMnO₄ in acid: Mn +7 → +2 → n = 5 → 158/5 = 31.6; in neutral/basic (Mn +7 → +4, MnO₂): n = 3 → 158/3 ≈ 52.7.
    • Equivalent mass depends on the reaction — the same compound carries different equivalents in different media.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): ion-electron method — MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺ (acidic)

    • Reduction half: MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺. Balance O: MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O. Balance H: 8H⁺ on the left. Balance charge: 8H⁺ + MnO₄⁻ (net +7) → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O (net +2) → add 5e⁻ left: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O.
    • Oxidation half: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻.
    • Equalise electrons: ×5 the iron half; add: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O.
    • Check: charge left = −1 + 10 + 8 = +17; right = +2 + 15 = +17 ✓.

    4.2 Verified PYQ (NEB 2023): oxidation-number method — MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻ + H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + CO₂ + H₂O

    • Oxidation numbers: Mn +7 → +2 (decrease 5); C +3 → +4 (increase 1 per C, 2 per C₂O₄²⁻).
    • Equalise: increase per oxalate = 2, decrease per Mn = 5 → LCM 10 → 2 MnO₄⁻ and 5 C₂O₄²⁻.
    • Skeleton with coefficients: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂. Balance O/H with H⁺/H₂O: left has 8 + 10 = 18 O; right has 20 O + x from water → add 8H₂O right and 16H⁺ left: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O.
    • Check charge: left −2 −10 +16 = +4; right +4 ✓.

    4.3 Verified PYQ (NEB 2021): equivalent mass of K₂Cr₂O₇ as oxidant in acid

    • In acid, Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O: 6 electrons per formula unit (each Cr +6 → +3).
    • Formula mass K₂Cr₂O₇ = 2(39) + 2(52) + 7(16) = 294.
    • Equivalent mass = 294/6 = 49.

    4.4 Choosing the method

    • Both give identical results; the ion-electron method is cleaner for ionic/acidic equations and required when half-reactions feed into electrochemistry (topic 41–42, cells); the oxidation-number method is fastest for molecular equations (C + HNO₃, Cu + H₂SO₄).

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Electrons never appear in the final answer: they cancel when the halves are added — an equation with electrons left in it is unfinished work.

  • Medium changes n-factor: KMnO₄ takes 5 electrons in acid but only 3 in neutral solution (MnO₂ product) — quote the medium with every equivalent mass.
  • Charge balance is the proof: after balancing, check total charge on both sides; if it differs, an electron count is wrong.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): Balance by the ion-electron method: MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺ (acidic medium). — Reduction half: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O (O by water, H by H⁺, charge by electrons). Oxidation half: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻. Multiplying the iron half by 5 and adding: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O. Charges balance at +17 both sides.

  • Verified PYQ (NEB 2023): Balance by the oxidation number method: MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻ + H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + CO₂ + H₂O. — Mn falls +7 → +2 (5 per Mn); carbon rises +3 → +4 (2 per oxalate). Equalising at LCM 10: 2 MnO₄⁻ and 5 C₂O₄²⁻. Balancing O and H with water and H⁺: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O. Charge check: +4 on both sides.
  • Verified PYQ (NEB 2021): Calculate the equivalent mass of K₂Cr₂O₇ acting as an oxidising agent in acid medium. — In acid, dichromate accepts 6 electrons: Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O (each Cr drops +6 → +3). Formula mass = 2(39) + 2(52) + 7(16) = 294. Equivalent mass = 294/6 = 49.
  • NEB-style (3m): Balance Cu + dilute HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + NO + H₂O by the oxidation-number method. — Cu: 0 → +2 (increase 2); N in HNO₃: +5 → +2 in NO (decrease 3). LCM 6: 3 Cu and 2 HNO₃ (reduced). Nitrate conservation needs 8 HNO₃ total (2 reduced + 6 providing Cu(NO₃)₂'s nitrate): 3Cu + 8HNO₃ → 3Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO + 4H₂O. Hydrogen: 8 left, 8 in 4H₂O ✓.

Confusion

  • Balance O with H₂O and H with H⁺ — in that order: reversing the sequence scrambles ionic equations; charge (electrons) always comes last.
  • Equivalent mass is reaction-specific: K₂Cr₂O₇ is 49 as an oxidant but different in other roles — always derive n from the actual half-reaction quoted.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2024 (3m): Balance the redox reaction by the ion-electron method: MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺ (acidic medium). — Reduction half: MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺. Balance oxygen with water: MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O. Balance hydrogen with H⁺: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O. Balance charge with electrons: left +7, right +2, so add 5e⁻ to the left: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O. Oxidation half: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻. Equalise electrons: multiply the iron half by 5. Add and cancel 5e⁻: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O. Charge check: left = −1 + 10 + 8 = +17; right = +2 + 15 = +17 ✓.
  • NEB 2023 (3m): Balance by the oxidation number (half-reaction) method: MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻ + H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + CO₂ + H₂O. — Assign numbers: Mn falls from +7 to +2 (decrease of 5 per Mn); carbon rises from +3 to +4 (increase of 1 per C, i.e. 2 per C₂O₄²⁻). Total increase must equal total decrease: LCM of 5 and 2 is 10, so coefficient 2 for MnO₄⁻ and 5 for C₂O₄²⁻. Skeleton: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂. Oxygen count: left 8 + 10 = 18; right 20 — add 8H₂O to the right and 16H⁺ to the left to balance hydrogen. Balanced: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O. Charge check: left = −2 − 10 + 16 = +4; right = +4 ✓.
  • NEB 2021 (2m): Calculate the equivalent mass (equivalent weight) of potassium dichromate, K₂Cr₂O₇, acting as an oxidising agent in acidic medium. — In acid, the dichromate half-reaction is Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O: each chromium falls from +6 to +3 (3 electrons), and there are two chromium atoms, so the n-factor is 6. Formula mass of K₂Cr₂O₇ = 2(39) + 2(52) + 7(16) = 294. Equivalent mass = 294 ÷ 6 = 49. This fixed value (unlike permanganate, which changes with medium) makes dichromate a preferred primary standard in redox titrations.

Key Points

  • Oxidation-number method: assign ONs, equalise increase = decrease, balance rest by inspection. Ion-electron: split halves, balance O→H₂O, H→H⁺, charge→e⁻, equalise electrons, add, cancel. Worked: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O (NEB 2024); 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O (NEB 2023). Equivalent mass = M/n: K₂Cr₂O₇ 294/6 = 49 (NEB 2021); KMnO₄ acid 158/5 = 31.6, neutral 52.7. Charge balance is the final proof.

Summary

Balancing redox is double-entry bookkeeping: atoms on one ledger, electrons on the other. The oxidation-number method equalises totals — find every number that moves, multiply until total increase equals total decrease, then mop up O with water and H with H⁺ (NEB 2023's MnO₄⁻/C₂O₄²⁻ lands on 2 : 5 : 16 → 2 : 10 : 8). The ion-electron method splits the reaction into two half-reactions, balances each in the fixed order O → H₂O, H → H⁺, charge → e⁻, equalises electrons, and adds (NEB 2024's MnO₄⁻/Fe²⁺ lands on 1 : 5 : 8 → 1 : 5 : 4); the electrons cancel and a charge check (+17 = +17) proves the work. The same half-reactions define n-factors — 5 for acid KMnO₄, 6 for K₂Cr₂O₇, 3 for neutral KMnO₄ — and dividing formula mass by n gives equivalent masses (31.6, 49, 52.7): the numbers that turn balanced equations into titration arithmetic. Medium matters, electrons never survive to the final equation, and the charge balance is always the last word.

Key Points
  • Oxidation-number method: assign ONs, equalise increase = decrease, balance rest by inspection. Ion-electron: split halves, balance O→H₂O, H→H⁺, charge→e⁻, equalise electrons, add, cancel. Worked: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O (NEB 2024); 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O (NEB 2023). Equivalent mass = M/n: K₂Cr₂O₇ 294/6 = 49 (NEB 2021); KMnO₄ acid 158/5 = 31.6, neutral 52.7. Charge balance is the final proof.
Important Concepts
  • Both methods are the same conservation law in two costumes: electrons (or oxidation-number units) lost must equal gained. The ion-electron half-reactions reappear verbatim as electrode reactions in electrolysis and cells — learn them once, use them thrice.
Examples
  • The permanganate titration — every KMnO₄-vs-oxalate practical in the lab runs on the NEB 2023 equation: 2:5:16 stoichiometry, purple to colourless at the endpoint.
Universal Facts
  • Hydrogen sulphide scrubbers, bleaching plants and iron-and-manganese water filters all run balanced redox equations of this topic's type — the arithmetic keeps industrial chemistry lawful.
Important Statements
  • "In a balanced redox equation, the total increase in oxidation numbers equals the total decrease — equivalently, electrons lost equal electrons gained."
Important Notes
  • Verified PYQs: NEB 2024 — ion-electron balancing of MnO₄⁻/Fe²⁺; NEB 2023 — oxidation-number balancing of MnO₄⁻/C₂O₄²⁻; NEB 2021 — equivalent mass of K₂Cr₂O₇ = 49 (all solved above). NEB 2022's H₂O₂ question is answered in topic 38. Other items are NEB-style practice.
Exam Short Tricks
  • Acid half-reaction recipe: "O → H₂O, H → H⁺, charge → e⁻". Common products: MnO₄⁻/H⁺ → Mn²⁺ (5e), Cr₂O₇²⁻/H⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ (6e), MnO₄⁻/H₂O → MnO₂ (3e). Eq masses: 31.6, 49, 52.7.
Exam Notes
  • Write every intermediate step of the half-reactions — examiners award marks for O, H, charge balancing separately. Finish with the charge check; stating "+17 both sides" is a visible proof line.

Practice Questions
  • NEB 2024 (3m): Balance the redox reaction by the ion-electron method: MnO₄⁻ + Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + Fe³⁺ (acidic medium). — Reduction half: MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺. Balance oxygen with water: MnO₄⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O. Balance hydrogen with H⁺: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O. Balance charge with electrons: left +7, right +2, so add 5e⁻ to the left: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O. Oxidation half: Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻. Equalise electrons: multiply the iron half by 5. Add and cancel 5e⁻: MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O. Charge check: left = −1 + 10 + 8 = +17; right = +2 + 15 = +17 ✓.
  • NEB 2023 (3m): Balance by the oxidation number (half-reaction) method: MnO₄⁻ + C₂O₄²⁻ + H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + CO₂ + H₂O. — Assign numbers: Mn falls from +7 to +2 (decrease of 5 per Mn); carbon rises from +3 to +4 (increase of 1 per C, i.e. 2 per C₂O₄²⁻). Total increase must equal total decrease: LCM of 5 and 2 is 10, so coefficient 2 for MnO₄⁻ and 5 for C₂O₄²⁻. Skeleton: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂. Oxygen count: left 8 + 10 = 18; right 20 — add 8H₂O to the right and 16H⁺ to the left to balance hydrogen. Balanced: 2MnO₄⁻ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ + 16H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 10CO₂ + 8H₂O. Charge check: left = −2 − 10 + 16 = +4; right = +4 ✓.
  • NEB 2021 (2m): Calculate the equivalent mass (equivalent weight) of potassium dichromate, K₂Cr₂O₇, acting as an oxidising agent in acidic medium. — In acid, the dichromate half-reaction is Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O: each chromium falls from +6 to +3 (3 electrons), and there are two chromium atoms, so the n-factor is 6. Formula mass of K₂Cr₂O₇ = 2(39) + 2(52) + 7(16) = 294. Equivalent mass = 294 ÷ 6 = 49. This fixed value (unlike permanganate, which changes with medium) makes dichromate a preferred primary standard in redox titrations.
