Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Weighing Electricity

    Faraday's two laws (1833) turn coulombs into grams: the mass deposited at an electrode is proportional to the charge passed (first law) and, for the same charge through different electrolytes, proportional to the equivalent mass (second law). One constant — 96500 C per mole of electrons — wires the whole quantitative side of electrolysis together, and every numerical in this chapter is one division away from it.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Faraday's first law

    • Statement: the mass of a substance deposited (or liberated) at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of charge passed through the electrolyte.
    • m ∝ Q; Q = I × t (current in amperes × time in seconds), so m = Z × I × t.
    • Z (electrochemical equivalent, ECE): mass deposited by 1 coulomb — Z = E/96500, where E is the equivalent mass.

    2.2 Faraday's second law

    • Statement: when the same quantity of charge passes through different electrolytes connected in series, the masses deposited are proportional to their equivalent masses.
    • m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂ — silver deposits 108 g for every 31.75 g of copper from the same charge.
    • Consequence: one faraday (96500 C) deposits one equivalent mass of any substance.

    2.3 The faraday and the electron count

    • 1 faraday (F) = 96500 C = the charge on 1 mole of electrons (e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C × 6.022 × 10²³ ≈ 96485 ≈ 96500 C).
    • Charge needed to deposit 1 mole of an ion = n × F, where n is the ion's charge: Na⁺ needs 1F, Cu²⁺ 2F, Al³⁺ 3F.
    • ECE values: Ag: 108/96500 = 1.118 mg/C; Cu: 31.75/96500 = 0.329 mg/C; Al: 9/96500 = 0.0932 mg/C.

    2.4 Worked numericals (the exam templates)

    • Silver, direct: current 2 A for 30 minutes through AgNO₃. Q = 2 × 1800 = 3600 C. m = (108 × 3600)/96500 = 4.03 g.
    • Copper, direct: current 1.5 A for 1 hour through CuSO₄. Q = 1.5 × 3600 = 5400 C. m = (31.75 × 5400)/96500 = 1.77 g.
    • Second law, series cells: the same charge deposits 5.4 g Ag from AgNO₃; what Cu mass deposits from CuSO₄? m(Cu) = m(Ag) × E(Cu)/E(Ag) = 5.4 × 31.75/108 = 1.59 g.
    • Gas volume: 1 F liberates 1 equivalent: ½ mol H₂ (11.2 L at STP) or ¼ mol O₂ (5.6 L). Current 2 A for 9650 s → Q = 19300 C = 0.2 F → H₂ = 2.24 L, O₂ = 1.12 L.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why one faraday deposits one equivalent

    • Every mole of electrons carries 96500 C; an ion of charge n needs n electrons per ion, so 1 mol electrons (1 F) reduces exactly 1/n mol — that is, one equivalent — of the ion. The second law is this sentence restated: equal charge → equal equivalents → masses in the ratio of equivalent masses.

    4.2 Setting up any numerical

    1. Compute Q = I × t (seconds!).
    2. Convert to faradays: n(F) = Q/96500.
    3. Mass = (equivalent mass) × n(F); or volume of gas = (22.4/n) × n(F) litres at STP.
    4. For series cells, skip recomputation — apply m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂ directly.

    4.3 The copper voltameter as charge meter

    • Weigh the cathode before and after; the gain ÷ (E(Cu) × 96500) gives the charge — a coulometer. Historically this defined the ampere practically; exam numericals often hide a "find the current" reversal of the same algebra.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Time must be seconds: I × t with minutes or hours silently multiplies errors by 60 or 3600 — convert first, always.

  • n is the ion's charge, not the element's valence in the compound: Cu²⁺ → 2F per mole even though "copper sulphate" mentions no charge; Al³⁺ → 3F.
  • First law needs one cell; second law needs several: mixing them up in numericals produces ratio errors — check what the question actually varies.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): State Faraday's first law and calculate the mass of silver deposited when a current of 2 A passes through AgNO₃ solution for 30 minutes. — First law: the mass deposited at an electrode is directly proportional to the quantity of charge passed, m = ZIt. Charge: Q = 2 × (30 × 60) = 3600 C. Faradays: 3600/96500 = 0.0373 F. Since Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag needs 1 F per mole (E = 108): m = (108 × 3600)/96500 ≈ 4.03 g.

  • NEB-style (3m): The same current passes through two cells in series containing AgNO₃ and CuSO₄. If 5.4 g of silver deposits, what mass of copper deposits? — By Faraday's second law, m(Ag)/m(Cu) = E(Ag)/E(Cu). E(Ag) = 108 (n = 1); E(Cu) = 63.5/2 = 31.75 (n = 2). m(Cu) = 5.4 × 31.75/108 = 1.59 g.
  • NEB-style (2m): Calculate the volume of oxygen liberated at STP when a current of 2 A passes through acidulated water for 9650 s. — Q = 2 × 9650 = 19300 C = 0.2 F. Oxygen: 4e⁻ per O₂, so 1 F gives 5.6 L; 0.2 F gives 1.12 L of O₂ at STP (and 2.24 L of H₂ at the cathode).

Confusion

  • Z and E are different numbers: Z is per coulomb (mg/C), E is per faraday (g/F = equivalent mass); m = ZIt and m = (E × Q)/96500 are the same formula written twice.
  • Deposited vs liberated: metals deposit as atoms (mass directly); gases liberate as molecules — use 22.4/n litres per faraday, not 22.4.

  • NEB-style (3m): The same quantity of electricity is passed through two cells in series containing AgNO₃ and CuSO₄ solutions. If 5.4 g of silver is deposited in the first cell, calculate the mass of copper deposited in the second (Ag = 108, Cu = 63.5). — By Faraday's second law, masses deposited by the same charge in different electrolytes are proportional to their equivalent masses: m(Ag)/m(Cu) = E(Ag)/E(Cu). Equivalent mass of Ag: 108/1 = 108 (Ag⁺ takes 1 electron). Equivalent mass of Cu: 63.5/2 = 31.75 (Cu²⁺ takes 2 electrons). Therefore m(Cu) = m(Ag) × E(Cu)/E(Ag) = 5.4 × 31.75/108 = 5.4 × 0.294 ≈ 1.59 g of copper. The same charge deposits one equivalent of each substance — 0.05 mol of Ag and 0.025 mol of Cu.
  • NEB-style (2m): A current of 2 A is passed through acidulated water for 9650 s. Calculate the volume of oxygen liberated at the anode at STP (F = 96500 C). — Charge passed: Q = I × t = 2 × 9650 = 19300 C. Faradays: 19300/96500 = 0.2 F. At the anode, 4OH⁻ → O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻: four faradays liberate one mole of O₂ (22.4 L at STP), so one faraday liberates 5.6 L. Volume of O₂ = 0.2 × 5.6 = 1.12 L at STP (while 0.2 F at the cathode liberates 0.2 × 11.2 = 2.24 L of hydrogen — the 2 : 1 volume ratio of water's electrolysis).

Summary

Faraday's laws are the exchange rate between electricity and matter. The first law — mass proportional to charge (m = ZIt, with the electrochemical equivalent Z = E/96500) — turns amperes and seconds into grams: 2 A for 30 minutes through AgNO₃ is 3600 C, 0.0373 faradays, 4.03 g of silver. The second law — for the same charge through series electrolytes, masses are proportional to equivalent masses (m₁/m₂ = E₁/E₂) — follows because one faraday (96500 C, one mole of electrons) always deposits exactly one equivalent: 108 g of Ag⁺ (n = 1), 31.75 g of Cu²⁺ (n = 2), 9 g of Al³⁺ (n = 3), 11.2 L of H₂ or 5.6 L of O₂ at STP. Every numerical is the same pipeline — Q = It, divide by 96500, multiply by E (or by 22.4/n for gases) — with seconds as the unit trap and the ion's charge as the n-factor. The copper coulometer closes the loop: weighing an electrode is measuring charge.
