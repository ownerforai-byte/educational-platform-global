Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Why Gases Are the Simplest State

    Solids have shape, liquids have surface, gases have nothing but motion. The kinetic molecular theory explains every gas law from five postulates about point particles in random flight — and it succeeds because gas molecules are so far apart that they barely interact. This topic supplies the model; the next topics supply its equations.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The five postulates (NEB 2024 verified list)

    1. Tiny particles: gases consist of a very large number of molecules, tiny compared with the distances between them — the molecular volume is negligible against the container volume.
    2. Random motion: molecules move in all directions in straight lines with high speeds, colliding with each other and the walls; their paths change direction at every collision.
    3. No forces: intermolecular attractions and repulsions are negligible — molecules are independent except during instant collisions.
    4. Perfectly elastic collisions: collisions (molecule–molecule and molecule–wall) conserve kinetic energy; no energy is lost.
    5. Kinetic energy and temperature: the average kinetic energy of molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature (KE = 3/2 kT per molecule); at a given T, all gases have the same average KE regardless of mass.

    2.2 How the postulates generate pressure

    • Pressure is the rate and force of wall collisions: each wall impact transfers momentum 2mv; billions per second per cm² give a steady macroscopic pressure.
    • Heavier molecules move slower at the same T (same average KE), so pressure depends only on number density and T — not on the gas's identity.

    2.3 Molecular speeds

    • Root mean square speed: u(rms) = √(3RT/M) — rises with T, falls with molar mass.
    • Average speed: u(av) = √(8RT/πM); most probable speed: u(mp) = √(2RT/M); ratio u(mp) : u(av) : u(rms) = 1 : 1.128 : 1.225.
    • At 25 °C, O₂ molecules average about 480 m/s — faster than sound.

    2.4 Kinetic gas equation

    • PV = ⅓ nMu²(rms) — connects the macroscopic (PV) with the microscopic (molecular speeds); combined with PV = nRT it yields the speed formulas above.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why the model works only for gases

    • Gas molecules occupy ~0.01 % of the container volume and attract each other negligibly — the two idealisations (negligible volume, negligible forces) are excellent for gases, poor for liquids and meaningless for solids.
    • This is also the seed of real-gas deviation (topic 46): both idealisations fail at high pressure and low temperature.

    4.2 Temperature is a speed dial

    • Doubling absolute temperature multiplies u(rms) by √2, and multiplies collision force and frequency so that P ∝ T at constant V — Charles's law emerges from postulate 5.
    • At absolute zero, translational motion ceases — the model's natural floor.

    4.3 Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): the postulates as an exam answer

    • Quote the five postulates of section 2.1 verbatim, then add the pay-off line: "these assumptions explain the gas laws and define an ideal gas." Marks sit in the completeness — omitting elastic collisions or the KE–T proportionality loses the tail marks.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Elastic does not mean "bouncy" in the everyday sense: elastic collisions conserve kinetic energy — the gas does not cool as its molecules collide, which is why a sealed bottle stays at room temperature.

  • Same KE, different speeds: at 25 °C helium and oxygen molecules have the same average kinetic energy; helium simply moves ~2.8× faster (√(32/4)) — students conflate energy with speed.
  • RMS, not average, enters the equations: the kinetic gas equation uses u(rms) because pressure depends on the mean of v², not the mean of v.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): Write the postulates of the kinetic molecular theory of gases. — (1) A gas consists of a very large number of tiny molecules whose volume is negligible compared with the container. (2) The molecules move randomly in straight lines at high speed, colliding with one another and with the walls. (3) Intermolecular forces of attraction and repulsion are negligible. (4) All collisions are perfectly elastic — kinetic energy is conserved. (5) The average kinetic energy of the molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature and is the same for all gases at that temperature. Consequence: pressure is the collective effect of wall collisions, and the model reproduces Boyle's, Charles's and Dalton's laws.

  • NEB-style (2m): Calculate the ratio of u(rms) for H₂ and O₂ at the same temperature. — u(rms) ∝ 1/√M: ratio = √(32/2) = √16 = 4 : 1 — hydrogen molecules move four times faster than oxygen molecules at the same temperature, the same arithmetic Graham's law uses.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why does a gas fill its container completely while a liquid does not? — Gas molecules have negligible intermolecular attraction (postulate 3) and high random speeds, so nothing restrains expansion until walls stop them; liquid molecules attract strongly enough to maintain a fixed volume with a surface.

Practice Questions

  • NEB 2024 (4m): Write the postulates of the kinetic molecular theory of gases. — The kinetic molecular theory describes a gas by five postulates: (1) A gas consists of a very large number of tiny particles (molecules) whose actual volume is negligible compared with the volume of the container — most of a gas is empty space. (2) The molecules are in continuous random motion, travelling in straight lines with high speeds and changing direction at every collision with one another or with the walls. (3) The forces of intermolecular attraction and repulsion are negligible — molecules move independently except at the instant of collision. (4) All collisions — molecule–molecule and molecule–wall — are perfectly elastic, so the total kinetic energy of the gas remains constant at a fixed temperature. (5) The average kinetic energy of the molecules is directly proportional to the absolute temperature (KE = 3/2 kT) and is the same for all gases at the same temperature, regardless of molar mass. Consequence: pressure is the cumulative momentum transfer of wall collisions, and from these assumptions Boyle's law, Charles's law and Dalton's law of partial pressures all follow; a gas obeying them exactly is an ideal gas.
  • NEB-style (2m): Calculate the ratio of the root mean square speeds of hydrogen and oxygen molecules at the same temperature. — From the kinetic gas equation, u(rms) = √(3RT/M), so at the same temperature u(rms) ∝ 1/√M. For H₂ (M = 2) and O₂ (M = 32): ratio = √(32/2) = √16 = 4 : 1. Hydrogen molecules move four times faster than oxygen molecules at the same temperature — which is also why hydrogen effuses four times faster (Graham's law).
  • NEB-style (2m): Explain why a gas exerts pressure on the walls of its container according to the kinetic theory. — Gas molecules move randomly at high speeds and collide with the walls (postulate 2). Each collision transfers momentum 2mv to the wall (the molecule reverses its momentum component). Billions of such collisions per second per square centimetre produce a steady average force per unit area — the pressure. At constant temperature the molecular speeds are constant, so pressure rises only when collisions become more frequent (smaller volume) or harder (higher temperature) — the microscopic origin of Boyle's and Charles's laws.

Summary

The kinetic molecular theory rebuilds a whole gas from five sentences: molecules are tiny points in vast empty space, flying randomly in straight lines, feeling no forces between collisions, bouncing perfectly elastically, and carrying average kinetic energy proportional to absolute temperature — the same for every gas. From these, pressure emerges as the drumbeat of wall collisions (PV = ⅓nMu²(rms)), temperature becomes a molecular speed dial, and Boyle's, Charles's and Dalton's laws stop being empirical rules and become theorems. The three speeds of the Maxwell distribution — most probable √(2RT/M), average √(8RT/πM), root-mean-square √(3RT/M) in the ratio 1 : 1.128 : 1.225 — set up Graham's law and every speed comparison: hydrogen's √(32/2) = 4× speed advantage over oxygen at the same temperature. The model's two idealisations (negligible volume, negligible forces) are precisely the assumptions real gases will break at high pressure and low temperature — the story of topic 46.
