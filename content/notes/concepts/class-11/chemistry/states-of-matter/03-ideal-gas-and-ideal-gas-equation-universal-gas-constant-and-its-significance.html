Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — One Equation to Rule the Gas Laws

    PV = nRT compresses Boyle, Charles and Avogadro into four symbols, and its constant R is the most travelled number in physical chemistry — the same 8.314 J/mol·K (or 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K) that later powers entropy, equilibrium and electrochemistry. Deriving it from the partial laws is a standard NEB question (2023 and 2024 both asked it), and every gas numerical reduces to choosing the right units for R.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The derivation (Boyle + Charles + Avogadro)

    • Let n moles of gas occupy volume V at pressure P and temperature T.
    • Boyle: V ∝ 1/P (T, n fixed). Charles: V ∝ T (P, n fixed). Avogadro: V ∝ n (T, P fixed).
    • Combining all three: V ∝ nT/P → V = R·nT/P → PV = nRT.
    • R is the proportionality constant — the universal gas constant — experimentally the same for every gas.

    2.2 The universal gas constant R and its units

    • R = PV/nT; its units follow from the units chosen for P and V.
    • R = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K — for volumes in litres and pressures in atmospheres.
    • R = 8.314 J/mol·K — SI (P in Pa, V in m³); also 8.314 kPa·dm³/mol·K.
    • R = 1.987 cal/mol·K ≈ 2 cal/mol·K — thermochemistry contexts.
    • Numerical check: 1 mol at STP → R = (1 atm × 22.4 L)/(1 mol × 273 K) = 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K.

    2.3 Significance of R

    • R is universal — same value for H₂, O₂, CO₂, any ideal gas; it converts the proportionalities of the partial laws into an exact equation.
    • In energy form, R per molecule is the Boltzmann constant k = R/N_A = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K — the bridge from moles to molecules.

    2.4 Using PV = nRT

    • n = PV/RT — mass from m = n × M.
    • Density: d = PM/RT; molar mass from vapour density: M = dRT/P.
    • Numerical: 0.5 mol gas at 300 K in 5 L → P = nRT/V = 0.5 × 0.0821 × 300/5 = 2.46 atm.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Verified PYQ (NEB 2023 + 2024): state Boyle's and Charles's laws; derive the ideal gas equation

    • Boyle's law: at constant temperature, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is inversely proportional to its pressure (P₁V₁ = P₂V₂).
    • Charles's law: at constant pressure, the volume of a fixed mass of gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature (V₁/T₁ = V₂/T₂).
    • Derivation: with n fixed, V ∝ 1/P (Boyle) and V ∝ T (Charles); combining, V ∝ T/P; introducing Avogadro's proportionality to n gives V ∝ nT/P. Writing the proportionality as equality with constant R: V = RnT/P → PV = nRT. Each of Boyle, Charles and Avogadro is recovered by holding the other variables constant — the equation is their common parent.

    4.2 Unit discipline

    • Match R to the data: P in atm with V in L → R = 0.0821; P in Pa with V in m³ → R = 8.314. Mixing them is the classic numerical error; a quick dimensional check (L·atm vs J) catches it.

    4.3 What "ideal" buys and costs

    • The equation assumes negligible molecular volume and no intermolecular forces — exact at low P and high T, increasingly wrong otherwise (topic 46 quantifies the failure).

  • 6. Common Confusions

    R is not "8.314 always": the value must match the pressure–volume units; 0.0821 with atm–litres, 8.314 with Pa–m³.

  • Temperature in Kelvin again: PV = nRT inherits Charles's absolute scale; a Celsius slip scales the answer by 273/300 or worse.
  • Ideal gases do not exist: the equation is a limiting model; real gases approach it at low pressure and high temperature but never coincide.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified PYQ (NEB 2023/2024): State Boyle's law and Charles's law. Derive the ideal gas equation. — Boyle: V ∝ 1/P at constant T and n. Charles: V ∝ T at constant P and n. Combining: V ∝ T/P for fixed n; adding Avogadro's V ∝ n: V ∝ nT/P. Introducing the constant R: V = RnT/P, hence PV = nRT. Setting the other variables constant recovers each partial law, confirming the combination.

  • NEB-style (2m): Calculate the pressure exerted by 0.5 mol of an ideal gas occupying 5 L at 300 K (R = 0.0821). — P = nRT/V = (0.5 × 0.0821 × 300)/5 = 12.315/5 = 2.46 atm.
  • NEB-style (2m): Find the molar mass of a gas whose density is 1.96 g/L at 1 atm and 273 K. — M = dRT/P = (1.96 × 0.0821 × 273)/1 ≈ 44 g/mol — carbon dioxide.

Summary

Three empirical laws fold into one equation. Boyle contributes V ∝ 1/P, Charles contributes V ∝ T, Avogadro contributes V ∝ n; multiplied together they give V ∝ nT/P, and the constant that makes it an equality is R — the universal gas constant, 0.0821 L·atm/mol·K in laboratory units, 8.314 J/mol·K in SI, 1.987 cal/mol·K in thermochemistry, and identical for every gas — the experimental soul of Avogadro's law. Per molecule, R shrinks to Boltzmann's k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K. The equation's working forms — n = PV/RT for moles, d = PM/RT for density, M = dRT/P for molar mass — turn state data into identity answers (1.96 g/L at STP reads 44: carbon dioxide). "Ideal" is the price: negligible molecular volume and no intermolecular forces hold only at low pressure and high temperature, and the next topic prices the failure.
