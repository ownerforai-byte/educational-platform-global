Notes

  • Focus of this tab

  • What defines a gas and the four measurable variables (P, V, T, n) whose relationships the gas laws and kinetic theory explain — the orientation before the postulates.

  • Defining characteristics of the gaseous state

  • • No fixed shape or volume — a gas fills its container completely.
  • • Very low density compared with liquids/solids (air ≈ 1.3 kg m⁻³ vs water 1000 kg m⁻³); highly compressible; diffuse rapidly.
  • • Molecules are far apart (intermolecular distances ≈ 10 molecular diameters at STP) with negligible attraction — hence expansion and compressibility.
  • • Exerts pressure uniformly in all directions (momentum transfer on wall collisions).
  • • Mixes completely and homogeneously (diffusion, effusion through pinholes).

  • The four state variables

  • • Pressure (P): force per unit area; units Pa (N m⁻²), atm (1 atm = 101325 Pa = 760 mm Hg), bar (1 bar = 10⁵ Pa); measured with barometers/manometers.
  • • Volume (V): space occupied; 1 m³ = 1000 L = 10⁶ cm³ (mL).
  • • Temperature (T): kelvin only in gas laws; T(K) = t(°C) + 273.15.
  • • Amount (n): moles; links to mass via molar mass and to particles via N_A.

  • What the gas laws connect

  • Boyle (P–V at fixed T), Charles (V–T at fixed P), Gay-Lussac (P–T at fixed V), Avogadro (V–n at fixed P, T), combined equation and PV = nRT. Each law fixes two variables and studies the other two — the 'constant-holding' logic of all gas-law experiments.

  • Why gases need a molecular model

  • The measured regularities (PV = nRT for all gases at low P) are too universal to be accidental: they point to a common microscopic structure. The kinetic theory supplies it — postulates → PV = ⅓mnc̄² → PV = nRT — and its idealisations (point molecules, no attraction) mark exactly where real gases deviate.
  • The postulate → consequence mapping: each idealisation of the kinetic theory, what it explains, and which real-gas failure it causes. A different cut through the material from the main postulates note.

  • Postulate → consequence → failure table

  • • 'Molecules are point masses (negligible volume)' → explains compressibility and large empty space → fails at high pressure (free volume < container volume, real PV > nRT).
  • • 'No intermolecular attraction except during collision' → explains indefinite expansion and Dalton's partial pressures → fails near liquefaction (attraction lowers observed pressure, real Z < 1) and is the reason gases condense at all.
  • • 'Collisions perfectly elastic' → kinetic energy redistributed, not lost; gas temperature stays constant in a rigid container → fails slightly (real collisions excite vibrations; polyatomic gases need internal degrees of freedom for their specific heats).
  • • 'Random straight-line motion, huge numbers' → statistics (Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution) explains steady macroscopic pressure from microscopic chaos → fails for very low densities where molecule counts fluctuate.
  • • 'KE ∝ T' → defines absolute temperature microscopically: KE = (3/2)kT per molecule, (3/2)RT per mole → explains why T must be kelvin and why all gases share the same R.

  • The central derivation chain

  • PV=31​mncˉ2 (pressure from momentum transfer) + KE ∝ T (postulate) → PV=nRT → Boyle's, Charles' and Avogadro's laws fall out immediately. One set of postulates explains all the gas laws at once — the model's greatest virtue.

  • What the model explains beyond the laws

  • Graham's diffusion/effusion (r ∝ 1/√M), Dalton's partial pressures (independent molecules), Graham's law of effusion, viscosity (momentum transfer between layers) and Brownian-motion style randomness of molecular speeds.

  • Where the idealisations bite (recap for exams)

  • High P: molecular volume matters. Low T / near Tc: attraction matters. Polyatomic gases: rotation/vibration. Very light gases at low T: quantum effects. Liquid state: attraction and volume together — outside the model entirely.

Confusion

  • ❌ Gases have negligible mass. ✅ They have small but definite mass — 22.4 L of air weighs about 29 g at STP.
  • ❌ Temperature in gas laws may be taken in °C. ✅ Absolute (kelvin) temperature only; 0 °C is 273.15 K.
  • ❌ A gas exerts pressure only downward. ✅ Pressure is isotropic — equal in every direction.
  • ❌ 'Negligible molecular volume' is a minor technicality. ✅ It is one of the two postulates whose failure defines all real-gas deviation (the other is no attraction).
  • ❌ Kinetic theory explains why gases liquefy. ✅ It cannot — liquefaction comes from the attractive forces it assumes away; that requires the van der Waals model.
  • ❌ Elastic collisions mean molecules never exchange energy. ✅ They exchange freely but the total KE is conserved.

Universal Facts

  • Air is a mixture (78% N₂, 21% O₂, ~1% Ar/CO₂) that behaves nearly ideally at 1 atm — the everyday ideal gas.
  • One mole of any gas at STP occupies 22.4 L and, for air, weighs ≈28.97 g (average molar mass).
  • The mean free path at 1 atm is ≈10⁻⁷ m — molecules travel ~1000 diameters between collisions.
  • Every postulate is an idealisation — the model is defined by what it ignores.
  • The pressure equation was derived by Clausius (1857); Maxwell gave the speed distribution in 1860.
  • The two failed idealisations reappear as the van der Waals constants b (volume) and a (attraction).

Examples

  • Problem: Express 2.5 atm in mm Hg and Pa. Solution: 2.5 × 760 = 1900 mm Hg; 2.5 × 101325 = 253 312 Pa ≈ 2.53 × 10⁵ Pa.
  • Problem: Which postulate fails when a gas is compressed to 200 atm? Solution: 'molecular volume is negligible' — the molecules' own volume is no longer negligible against the container volume, so the free space shrinks and observed pressure exceeds the ideal prediction (Z > 1).

Important Notes

  • Fix the four variables (P, V, T, n) in mind before the laws — every law is a two-at-a-time relation among them.
  • Use kelvin always; the °C slip is the classic numerical error.
  • Learn the postulates as a cause–effect chain; examiners award marks for linking postulate → law → limitation.
  • Quote the two 'failure postulates' explicitly in any limitations answer.
