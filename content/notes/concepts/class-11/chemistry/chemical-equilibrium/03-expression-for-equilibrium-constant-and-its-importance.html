Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Reading the Constant, Computing the Concentration

    Kc is more than a fraction: its size grades how far a reaction proceeds, its units encode the stoichiometry, and its algebra solves the classic NEB numerical — "given some equilibrium concentrations, find the missing one." This topic turns the law of mass action into a working calculator.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 What the size of Kc means

    • Kc very large (≫1): products dominate at equilibrium — reaction nearly complete (e.g. Kc ~ 10²⁵ for H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O at 25 °C).
    • Kc very small (≪1): reactants dominate — hardly any conversion (e.g. N₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2NO, Kc ~ 10⁻³¹ at 25 °C).
    • Kc ≈ 1: appreciable amounts of both — a genuine balance.

    2.2 Importance of the equilibrium constant

    1. Predicts extent: how much product to expect at equilibrium.
    2. Predicts direction: compare the reaction quotient Qc with Kc (Qc < Kc → forward; Qc > Kc → reverse; Qc = Kc → at equilibrium).
    3. Links to thermodynamics: Kc fixes ΔG° (ΔG° = −RT ln K), so the constant is chemistry's yield-and-energy bridge.
    4. Industrial design: Haber and contact processes tune conditions against a known Kc.

    2.3 The ICE-style calculation

    • For many problems: write the balanced equation, tabulate known equilibrium concentrations, substitute into the Kc expression, solve for the unknown — usually a square root or a simple cube.

    2.4 Reaction quotient Qc

    • Same expression as Kc but with non-equilibrium concentrations. Qc < Kc: forward reaction runs; Qc = Kc: equilibrium; Qc > Kc: reverse reaction runs.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 The verified NEB numerical, step by step

    • Problem (NEB 2024): For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), Kc = 0.5 at a certain temperature. If [N₂] = 1.0 M and [H₂] = 0.5 M at equilibrium, calculate the equilibrium concentration of ammonia.
    • Kc expression: Kc = [NH₃]²/([N₂][H₂]³).
    • Substitute: 0.5 = [NH₃]² / (1.0 × (0.5)³) = [NH₃]² / (1.0 × 0.125) = [NH₃]² / 0.125.
    • Solve: [NH₃]² = 0.5 × 0.125 = 0.0625 → [NH₃] = √0.0625 = 0.25 M.

    4.2 Reading direction with Qc — a worked mini-case

    • N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ with Kc = 4.0 at some T; a flask holds [N₂O₄] = 0.5 M, [NO₂] = 1.0 M.
    • Qc = (1.0)²/0.5 = 2.0 < 4.0 → the system is product-poor relative to equilibrium → forward reaction proceeds (more NO₂ forms) until Qc climbs to 4.0.

    4.3 Why Kc ignores starting amounts

    • Whatever the initial mixture, the reaction runs until the fraction equals Kc — the constant is the destination, the initial concentrations merely the road.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Powers, not multipliers: the NH₃ numerical dies if [H₂]³ is computed as 3 × 0.5. The coefficient is an exponent: (0.5)³ = 0.125.

  • Kc ≠ yield percentage: a large Kc says the equilibrium mixture is product-rich, not that any arbitrary starting mixture converts fully — extent is judged at equilibrium only.
  • Qc is Kc's living twin: same formula, different moment; mixing the two names costs the direction argument.

  • 7. Exam Application

    Verified NEB 2024 (numerical): For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), Kc = 0.5 at a certain temperature. If [N₂] = 1.0 M and [H₂] = 0.5 M at equilibrium, calculate the equilibrium concentration of ammonia. — Kc = [NH₃]²/([N₂][H₂]³) = 0.5. Substituting: 0.5 = [NH₃]²/(1.0 × 0.125) = [NH₃]²/0.125, so [NH₃]² = 0.0625 and [NH₃] = 0.25 M.

  • NEB-style (2m): State two pieces of information that the magnitude of Kc provides about a reaction. — (1) Extent: a very large Kc means products dominate at equilibrium (reaction nearly complete); a very small Kc means reactants dominate (negligible conversion); Kc ≈ 1 means appreciable amounts of both. (2) Direction of spontaneous change: comparing the reaction quotient Qc with Kc tells which way the system must move — Qc < Kc drives the forward reaction, Qc > Kc the reverse.
  • NEB-style (2m): For N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, Kc = 4.0 at temperature T. A vessel contains [N₂O₄] = 0.5 M and [NO₂] = 1.0 M. In which direction does the reaction proceed? — Qc = [NO₂]²/[N₂O₄] = (1.0)²/0.5 = 2.0. Since Qc (2.0) < Kc (4.0), the mixture contains too little product for equilibrium: the forward reaction proceeds, converting N₂O₄ to NO₂ until Qc rises to 4.0.

Confusion

  • ❌ All atoms are identical in an element. ✅ Isotopes have same protons but different neutrons.
  • ❌ Atoms are indivisible. ✅ Atoms consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • ❌ Electrons orbit in fixed paths like planets. ✅ Electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals).
  • ❌ Heisenberg uncertainty means we cannot measure position and momentum precisely simultaneously — not that we lack good instruments.
  • ❌ de Broglie wavelength applies only to light. ✅ All matter has wave nature; detectable mainly for small masses like electrons.
  • ❌ Quantum number l can be any integer. ✅ l ranges from 0 to n−1.
  • ❌ Magnetic quantum number ml determines energy. ✅ ml determines orbital orientation, not energy (in absence of magnetic field).
  • ❌ Spin quantum number ms can be ±½ or any value. ✅ ms is always +½ or −½.
  • ❌ Aufbau principle means filling from bottom to top of the diagram. ✅ Fill lowest-energy orbitals first: 1s→2s→2p→3s→3p→4s→3d→4p→...
  • ❌ Pauli exclusion allows two electrons per orbital with any spin. ✅ Two electrons in same orbital must have opposite spins.
  • ❌ Hund's rule means pair electrons before spreading. ✅ Electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly first with parallel spins.
  • ❌ The mole is just a number like dozen. ✅ 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro constant) — a bridge between atomic and macroscopic scales.
  • ❌ Molar mass and molecular mass are the same thing. ✅ Molecular mass is in amu; molar mass is in g/mol — numerically equal but different units.
  • ❌ Limiting reagent is the reactant with smallest mass. ✅ Limiting reagent is the one that produces the least product (determined by moles, not mass).
  • ❌ % yield can exceed 100%. ✅ % yield >100% indicates impure product or incomplete drying.
  • ❌ Molarity and molality are the same. ✅ Molarity = mol/L solution (changes with T); Molality = mol/kg solvent (temperature-independent).
  • ❌ A strong acid completely ionizes in water. ✅ Strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) are ~100% ionized; weak acids partially ionize.
  • ❌ pH can be negative. ✅ pH < 0 is possible for concentrations >1M (e.g., 2M HCl has pH ≈ −0.3).
  • ❌ Kc changes when concentration changes. ✅ Kc is constant at constant temperature; only temperature changes K.
  • ❌ Adding catalyst shifts equilibrium position. ✅ Catalyst speeds both forward and backward reactions equally; no shift in equilibrium.
  • ❌ High pressure always favors products. ✅ High pressure favors side with fewer gas moles (Le Chatelier).
  • ❌ Exothermic reactions are spontaneous. ✅ Spontaneity depends on ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, not just ΔH.
  • ❌ Endothermic reactions cannot be spontaneous. ✅ Endothermic reactions can be spontaneous if TΔS > ΔH (e.g., ice melting above 0°C).
  • ❌ ΔH and ΔU are always the same. ✅ ΔH = ΔU + PΔV; they differ when gas moles change.
  • ❌ Heat (q) and work (w) are state functions. ✅ q and w are path functions; only ΔU, ΔH, ΔS, ΔG are state functions.
  • ❌ Oxidation and reduction happen separately. ✅ Oxidation and reduction always occur together (redox).
  • ❌ Oxidizing agent gets oxidized. ✅ Oxidizing agent accepts electrons and gets reduced.
  • ❌ Reducing agent gets reduced. ✅ Reducing agent donates electrons and gets oxidized.
  • ❌ Faraday's laws apply only to metals. ✅ Faraday's laws apply to any substance deposited/dissolved at electrodes.
  • ❌ Real gases obey PV=nRT at all conditions. ✅ Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • ❌ van der Waals constants a and b are same for all gases. ✅ a (attraction) and b (volume) are gas-specific.
  • ❌ Surface tension increases with temperature. ✅ Surface tension decreases as temperature increases.
  • ❌ Viscosity decreases with temperature for liquids. ✅ Viscosity of liquids decreases with T; gases increase with T.
  • ❌ Colligative properties depend on nature of solute. ✅ Colligative properties depend only on number of particles, not identity.
  • ❌ Raoult's law applies to all solutions. ✅ Raoult's law is exact for ideal solutions; approximate for dilute real solutions.
  • ❌ Osmotic pressure is a colligative property. ✅ Yes — π = CRT depends on solute particle concentration.
  • ❌ Equilibrium means reaction stopped. ✅ Equilibrium means forward rate = backward rate (dynamic, not static).
  • ❌ K = 1 means no reaction. ✅ K = 1 means reactants and products are equally favored at equilibrium.

Practice

  • For PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂, Kc = 0.08 at 250 °C. At equilibrium [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = 0.2 M. Find [PCl₅].
  • Kc = 50 for A ⇌ B. A mixture has [A] = 2 M, [B] = 0.1 M. Which way does it move?
  • Classify as nearly complete / negligible / balanced: Kc = 10¹², Kc = 10⁻⁸, Kc = 0.8.

Universal Facts

  • Atmospheric nitrogen and oxygen coexist because Kc for NO formation is ~10⁻³¹ at 25 °C — the same reaction runs furiously inside engines at 2500 K, where Kc leaps and smog is born.

Practice Questions

  • Verified NEB 2024 (2m): For the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), Kc = 0.5 at a certain temperature. If [N₂] = 1.0 M and [H₂] = 0.5 M at equilibrium, calculate the equilibrium concentration of ammonia. — The equilibrium constant expression is Kc = [NH₃]²/([N₂][H₂]³). Substituting the given values: 0.5 = [NH₃]² / (1.0 × (0.5)³) = [NH₃]² / (1.0 × 0.125) = [NH₃]² / 0.125. Hence [NH₃]² = 0.5 × 0.125 = 0.0625, and taking the square root gives [NH₃] = 0.25 mol/L. (Note the coefficient 3 on H₂ enters as a cube, and the coefficient 2 on NH₃ as a square — the stoichiometry is carried entirely by the exponents.)
  • NEB-style (2m): State two uses of the equilibrium constant. — (1) Predicting the extent of reaction: the magnitude of Kc tells how far the reaction proceeds before equilibrium — a very large Kc means the products overwhelmingly dominate the equilibrium mixture (the reaction is virtually complete), a very small Kc means the reactants remain dominant (negligible conversion), and Kc ≈ 1 means appreciable quantities of both. (2) Predicting the direction of a reaction: by computing the reaction quotient Qc from the concentrations present and comparing it with Kc — if Qc < Kc the forward reaction proceeds, if Qc > Kc the reverse reaction proceeds, and if Qc = Kc the system is already at equilibrium. (Additionally, Kc connects to energetics through ΔG° = −RT ln K and guides industrial condition-setting, e.g. the Haber process.)
  • NEB-style (2m): For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), Kc = 4.0 at temperature T. A vessel contains [N₂O₄] = 0.5 M and [NO₂] = 1.0 M. Predict the direction of the net reaction. — Compute the reaction quotient with the same expression as Kc: Qc = [NO₂]²/[N₂O₄] = (1.0)²/(0.5) = 2.0. Since Qc (2.0) is less than Kc (4.0), the ratio of products to reactants is below its equilibrium value — the vessel holds too little NO₂. The forward reaction proceeds (N₂O₄ → NO₂), consuming N₂O₄ and raising [NO₂] until Qc climbs to 4.0 and equilibrium is restored.

Formulas

    • Kc = [C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b
    • Qc = same expression, non-equilibrium concentrations
    • ΔG° = −RT ln K
    • N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃: Kc = [NH₃]²/([N₂][H₂]³); NEB 2024: [NH₃] = 0.25 M

Key Points

  • Kc ≫ 1: products dominate; Kc ≪ 1: reactants dominate; Kc ≈ 1: both appreciable. Qc (same expression, any moment): Qc<Kc forward, Qc>Kc reverse, Qc=Kc equilibrium. Kc depends only on T. Importance: extent, direction, ΔG° = −RT ln K, industrial design. Worked NEB 2024: Kc = 0.5 = [NH3]2/(1.0 × 0.125) → [NH3] = 0.25 M.

Summary

Kc is a number that speaks. Big Kc (10⁵ and up) declares the reaction practically complete; small Kc (10⁻⁵ and down) declares it stillborn; Kc near 1 declares a true contest. The same fraction evaluated off-equilibrium is the reaction quotient Qc — Qc < Kc drives forward, Qc > Kc drives back, Qc = Kc is the resting state — making the constant a compass as well as a scoreboard. Its practical importance reaches into thermodynamics (ΔG° = −RT ln K) and industry (Haber and contact processes tune pressure and temperature against a known Kc). Numerically, the skill is mechanical: write the mass-action fraction, substitute the known equilibrium concentrations with coefficients as exponents, and solve — as NEB 2024 required for ammonia synthesis, where Kc = 0.5, [N₂] = 1.0 M and [H₂] = 0.5 M gave [NH₃]² = 0.0625 and [NH₃] = 0.25 M. Powers as exponents, solids as absent, temperature as the only parent: three habits that make every equilibrium numerical routine.

Substituting the given values: 0.5 = [NH₃]² / (1.0 × (0.5)³) = [NH₃]² / (1.0 × 0.125) = [NH₃]² / 0.125. Hence [NH₃]² = 0.5 × 0.125 = 0.0625, and taking the square root gives [NH₃] = 0.25 mol/L. (Note the coefficient 3 on H₂ enters as a cube, and the coefficient 2 on NH₃ as a square — the stoichiometry is carried entirely by the exponents.)
  • NEB-style (2m): State two uses of the equilibrium constant. — (1) Predicting the extent of reaction: the magnitude of Kc tells how far the reaction proceeds before equilibrium — a very large Kc means the products overwhelmingly dominate the equilibrium mixture (the reaction is virtually complete), a very small Kc means the reactants remain dominant (negligible conversion), and Kc ≈ 1 means appreciable quantities of both. (2) Predicting the direction of a reaction: by computing the reaction quotient Qc from the concentrations present and comparing it with Kc — if Qc < Kc the forward reaction proceeds, if Qc > Kc the reverse reaction proceeds, and if Qc = Kc the system is already at equilibrium. (Additionally, Kc connects to energetics through ΔG° = −RT ln K and guides industrial condition-setting, e.g. the Haber process.)
  • NEB-style (2m): For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), Kc = 4.0 at temperature T. A vessel contains [N₂O₄] = 0.5 M and [NO₂] = 1.0 M. Predict the direction of the net reaction. — Compute the reaction quotient with the same expression as Kc: Qc = [NO₂]²/[N₂O₄] = (1.0)²/(0.5) = 2.0. Since Qc (2.0) is less than Kc (4.0), the ratio of products to reactants is below its equilibrium value — the vessel holds too little NO₂. The forward reaction proceeds (N₂O₄ → NO₂), consuming N₂O₄ and raising [NO₂] until Qc climbs to 4.0 and equilibrium is restored.
