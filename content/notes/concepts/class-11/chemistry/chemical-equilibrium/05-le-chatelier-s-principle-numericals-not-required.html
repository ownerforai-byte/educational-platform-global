  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Rule That Runs the Chemical Industry

    Le Chatelier's principle (1888) predicts which way an equilibrium moves when conditions change: the system shifts to oppose the change. One sentence decides the Haber process's pressure, the contact process's temperature, and the marks on every equilibrium question — including two verified NEB sets (2024 and 2022) solved below.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The principle

    • Statement: when a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change in concentration, pressure (volume), or temperature, the equilibrium shifts in the direction that tends to reduce (oppose) the effect of that change.
    • It is qualitative — it predicts direction, never the new Kc (which only temperature alters).

    2.2 Effect of concentration

    • Adding a reactant (or removing a product): shifts the equilibrium forward (toward products) to consume the addition.
    • Removing a reactant (or adding a product): shifts backward.
    • Root: adding reactant raises Qc's denominator; the fraction drops below Kc and only forward reaction restores it.
    • Industrial use: continuous removal of NH₃ in Haber, or SO₃ in the contact process, drags the equilibrium forward — yield without changing Kc.

    2.3 Effect of pressure (volume)

    • Increasing pressure (decreasing volume) shifts toward the side with fewer gaseous moles; decreasing pressure shifts toward more gas moles.
    • Δn = 0 reactions (e.g. H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI): pressure has no effect on the position.
    • Inert gas at constant volume: no shift (partial pressures unchanged); inert gas at constant pressure: shifts toward more gas moles (dilution effect).

    2.4 Effect of temperature

    • Heating favours the endothermic direction; cooling favours the exothermic direction — heat is treated as a "product" of exothermic reactions and a "reactant" of endothermic ones.
    • Temperature is the only factor that changes Kc itself: for an exothermic reaction Kc falls as T rises; for an endothermic one Kc rises.

    2.5 Catalyst

    • No effect on position — speeds both directions equally; equilibrium arrives sooner at the same composition.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Verified NEB 2024 — pressure on ammonia synthesis

    • Problem: State Le Chatelier's principle. Predict the effect of increasing pressure on N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g).
    • Principle: when a system at equilibrium is subjected to a change (concentration, pressure, temperature), it shifts in the direction that opposes the change.
    • Analysis: left side has 1 + 3 = 4 gas moles; right side has 2. Increasing pressure squeezes the system, which opposes by shifting toward fewer gas moles — the forward direction. More ammonia forms; the yield of NH₃ increases.

    4.2 Verified NEB 2022 — the N₂O₄/NO₂ balance

    • Problem: For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), ΔH = +57 kJ mol⁻¹, state and explain the effect of (i) increasing temperature and (ii) increasing pressure.
    • (i) Temperature: the forward reaction is endothermic (ΔH positive). Heating adds heat, which behaves like a reactant; the system consumes it by shifting forward — more NO₂ forms, and the brown colour deepens (NO₂ is brown, N₂O₄ colourless). Kc increases with temperature.
    • (ii) Pressure: left side 1 gas mole, right side 2. Increasing pressure shifts toward fewer moles — backward toward N₂O₄; the colour pales.

    4.3 The industrial synthesis of the principle

    • Haber (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, exothermic): high pressure (forward, fewer moles) but moderate temperature (~450 °C) — high T would kill the exothermic yield, so a catalyst supplies the rate that low-ish temperature costs.
    • Contact process (2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, exothermic): excess air (concentration push) and pressure near atmospheric (Δn = 3 → 2 favours forward, but the gain is small so high pressure is not worth it); optimal ~450 °C with V₂O₅ catalyst.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Pressure acts on gas moles, not molecules' weight: count moles of gas only — solids and liquids (CaCO₃ ⇌ CaO + CO₂: Δn = +1 from gases alone) are invisible to pressure.

  • Heating helps endothermic, not "everything": students shift every reaction forward on heating; the direction is decided by which side absorbs the heat.
  • Temperature is special: concentration and pressure changes move the position but leave Kc untouched; only temperature writes a new Kc — mixing this up loses both reasoning marks.

Practice Questions

  • Verified NEB 2024 (3m): State Le Chatelier's principle. Predict the effect of increasing pressure on the equilibrium N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). — Principle: when a change is imposed on a system at equilibrium (change in concentration, pressure or temperature), the equilibrium shifts in the direction that tends to oppose or nullify the effect of that change. Prediction: the balanced equation shows 1 + 3 = 4 moles of gas on the reactant side and 2 moles of gas on the product side. Increasing the pressure (by decreasing the volume) stresses the system by compression; the equilibrium opposes this by shifting toward the side occupying fewer moles of gas — the forward (product) direction. Therefore more NH₃ is formed at equilibrium: the yield of ammonia increases. (Note: the value of Kc itself is unchanged — pressure shifts the position, not the constant.)
  • Verified NEB 2022 (2m): For the equilibrium N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), ΔH = +57 kJ mol⁻¹. State and explain the effect of increasing temperature on this equilibrium. — Since ΔH is positive, the forward reaction (N₂O₄ → 2NO₂) is endothermic and the reverse is exothermic. When the temperature is raised, heat is supplied to the system; by Le Chatelier's principle the equilibrium shifts in the direction that absorbs the added heat — the endothermic forward direction. More N₂O₄ dissociates: the concentration of NO₂ increases and the reddish-brown colour of the mixture deepens. The equilibrium constant Kc itself increases with temperature for this endothermic reaction.
  • NEB-style (2m): In the Haber process, ammonia is continuously removed by liquefaction. Explain, using Le Chatelier's principle, why this increases the yield. — Removing the product NH₃ decreases its concentration, so the reaction quotient Qc = [NH₃]²/([N₂][H₂]³) falls below Kc. The system is now product-poor, and by Le Chatelier's principle the equilibrium shifts forward to replace the removed ammonia, consuming more N₂ and H₂. Because the product never accumulates, the reverse reaction is perpetually starved and the overall conversion approaches completion — a higher practical yield than a static closed vessel allows, even though Kc itself is unchanged.

Key Points

  • Principle: equilibrium shifts to OPPOSE the imposed change (concentration/pressure/temperature). Concentration: add reactant/remove product -> forward; reverse -> backward. Pressure: toward fewer gas moles (dn = 0: no effect; inert gas at constant V: no effect). Temperature: heat favours endothermic side, cooling favours exothermic side; ONLY temperature changes Kc. Catalyst: no shift. NEB 2024: pressure up on N2+3H2=2NH3 -> forward (4->2 moles). NEB 2022: N2O4=2NO2, dH = +57: heat -> forward (browner), pressure -> backward (paler). Haber: high P, moderate T, NH3 removal. Contact: excess O2, ~450 C, V2O5.

Summary

Le Chatelier's principle is chemistry's thermostat: disturb an equilibrium and it moves to oppose you. Concentration changes shift toward consuming what you added (or restoring what you removed) — the arithmetic behind Qc falling below or rising above Kc. Pressure changes squeeze the side with more gas moles, so the balance retreats to fewer moles — 4 → 2 for ammonia synthesis (NEB 2024: shift forward, higher yield), 1 → 2 for N₂O₄ dissociation (shift backward); Δn = 0 systems like HI feel nothing. Temperature is the profound lever: heat joins the endothermic side, so warming N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ (ΔH = +57 kJ/mol, NEB 2022) drives it forward and deepens the brown, while warming the exothermic Haber reaction would strangle its own yield — hence the compromise ~450 °C with a catalyst. Only temperature rewrites Kc; catalysts change nothing but the clock. Industry reads the principle like a manual: Haber runs high pressure, moderate heat, and continuous ammonia removal; the contact process floods in cheap oxygen and condenses its SO₃. Count the gas moles, place the heat, oppose the change — the whole topic in one habit.
