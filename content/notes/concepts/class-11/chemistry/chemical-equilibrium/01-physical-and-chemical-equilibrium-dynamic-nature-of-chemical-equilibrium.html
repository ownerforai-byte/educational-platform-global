Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Reactions That Refuse to Finish

    Most reactions in a closed flask never "complete": forward and reverse processes run side by side until their rates tie. That tie is equilibrium — dynamic, not dead. This topic separates physical from chemical equilibrium, establishes the reversibility and constancy criteria, and sets up the quantitative machinery (mass action, Kc, Kp) of the next three topics.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Reversible reactions and the approach to equilibrium

    • A reversible reaction proceeds in both directions simultaneously, written with the double arrow ⇌: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃.
    • Starting from reactants only, the forward rate is initially maximal and falls as reactants deplete; the reverse rate starts at zero and climbs as products accumulate.
    • When the two rates become equal, concentrations stop changing — the equilibrium state.

    2.2 Physical vs chemical equilibrium

    • Physical equilibrium: the same substance changes state or phase, composition unchanged — H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂O(vap) in a closed flask; dissolution of sugar in saturated solution; sublimation of iodine.
    • Chemical equilibrium: different substances interconvert with recomposition — CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g); N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂; esterification CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.
    • Both share the same signature: opposing processes at equal rates, macroscopic constancy.

    2.3 Dynamic nature — the defining idea

    • Equilibrium is not a stop: both reactions continue at equal rates, so the net composition is constant while molecules endlessly interconvert.
    • Evidence: radioactive tracer experiments — labelled reactants added at equilibrium appear in products, proving the forward reaction still runs.
    • The equilibrium is reached from either side — starting with pure reactants or pure products gives the same final state (at the same T).

    2.4 Characteristics of chemical equilibrium

    1. Constancy of composition: concentrations of all species stay constant (not equal).
    2. Dynamic activity: forward and reverse reactions continue at equal rates.
    3. Attainable from either direction.
    4. Influence of catalysts: a catalyst speeds both directions equally — equilibrium is reached faster but its position is unchanged.
    5. Dependence on conditions: temperature, pressure, concentration shifts can move the equilibrium point (Le Chatelier, topic 56).

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 The rate picture of approach to equilibrium

    • Plot rate vs time: forward rate falls from its maximum, reverse rate rises from zero; they cross at the equilibrium time. After the crossing, both continue — equal, opposite, unending.

    4.2 Why equilibrium needs a closed system

    • In an open vessel products escape and the reverse reaction never builds — the reaction runs to completion. The sealed flask is what lets products accumulate and push back.

    4.3 Tracer evidence for dynamism

    • Inject a trace of radioactive reactant at equilibrium: radioactivity appears in products at unchanged concentrations — the only explanation is ongoing forward conversion. Equilibrium is a balance of activity, not a halt.

    4.4 Equilibrium is independent of the starting point

    • From pure N₂O₄ or pure NO₂ at the same temperature, the same equilibrium mixture results (same Kc) — position depends on conditions, not history.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Equal rates ≠ equal concentrations: at equilibrium the forward and reverse rates are equal, but the amounts of reactants and products are generally unequal — Kc decides the ratio.

  • A catalyst does not shift equilibrium: it only shortens the road — same position, faster arrival. Answers that claim catalysts change yield lose the mark.
  • Dynamic means active: "nothing is happening" is the classic misread — everything is happening, in balance.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Distinguish physical and chemical equilibrium with one example each, and explain the dynamic nature of chemical equilibrium. — Physical equilibrium involves a change of state with no new substances: in a closed flask at 25 °C, water evaporates at the same rate as vapour condenses (H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂O(g)), so the vapour pressure is constant. Chemical equilibrium involves interconversion of different substances: in CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g), calcium carbonate decomposes at the same rate as calcium oxide and carbon dioxide recombine. Dynamic nature: at equilibrium neither reaction stops — both continue at equal rates, so concentrations stay constant while molecules endlessly interconvert. Tracer experiments prove it: radioactive reactant added at equilibrium appears in the products without any change in concentrations.

  • NEB-style (2m): List four characteristics of chemical equilibrium. — (1) Concentrations of all species remain constant (though not necessarily equal). (2) The forward and reverse reactions proceed at equal rates — the equilibrium is dynamic. (3) The same equilibrium is reached whether one starts from reactants or from products. (4) A catalyst accelerates both directions equally, so it changes only the time to reach equilibrium, not its position; temperature, pressure and concentration changes can shift the position.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why can a reversible reaction not go to completion in a closed vessel? — As the forward reaction proceeds, products accumulate; they begin to react back. The reverse rate grows as the forward rate falls, until the two are equal — beyond which no net change is possible. The accumulated products are the brake that stops completion; only if products continuously escape (open system) can the reaction go to completion.

Confusion

  • ❌ All atoms are identical in an element. ✅ Isotopes have same protons but different neutrons.
  • ❌ Atoms are indivisible. ✅ Atoms consist of protons, neutrons, and electrons.
  • ❌ Electrons orbit in fixed paths like planets. ✅ Electrons exist in probability clouds (orbitals).
  • ❌ Heisenberg uncertainty means we cannot measure position and momentum precisely simultaneously — not that we lack good instruments.
  • ❌ de Broglie wavelength applies only to light. ✅ All matter has wave nature; detectable mainly for small masses like electrons.
  • ❌ Quantum number l can be any integer. ✅ l ranges from 0 to n−1.
  • ❌ Magnetic quantum number ml determines energy. ✅ ml determines orbital orientation, not energy (in absence of magnetic field).
  • ❌ Spin quantum number ms can be ±½ or any value. ✅ ms is always +½ or −½.
  • ❌ Aufbau principle means filling from bottom to top of the diagram. ✅ Fill lowest-energy orbitals first: 1s→2s→2p→3s→3p→4s→3d→4p→...
  • ❌ Pauli exclusion allows two electrons per orbital with any spin. ✅ Two electrons in same orbital must have opposite spins.
  • ❌ Hund's rule means pair electrons before spreading. ✅ Electrons fill degenerate orbitals singly first with parallel spins.
  • ❌ The mole is just a number like dozen. ✅ 1 mole = 6.022×10²³ particles (Avogadro constant) — a bridge between atomic and macroscopic scales.
  • ❌ Molar mass and molecular mass are the same thing. ✅ Molecular mass is in amu; molar mass is in g/mol — numerically equal but different units.
  • ❌ Limiting reagent is the reactant with smallest mass. ✅ Limiting reagent is the one that produces the least product (determined by moles, not mass).
  • ❌ % yield can exceed 100%. ✅ % yield >100% indicates impure product or incomplete drying.
  • ❌ Molarity and molality are the same. ✅ Molarity = mol/L solution (changes with T); Molality = mol/kg solvent (temperature-independent).
  • ❌ A strong acid completely ionizes in water. ✅ Strong acids (HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄) are ~100% ionized; weak acids partially ionize.
  • ❌ pH can be negative. ✅ pH < 0 is possible for concentrations >1M (e.g., 2M HCl has pH ≈ −0.3).
  • ❌ Kc changes when concentration changes. ✅ Kc is constant at constant temperature; only temperature changes K.
  • ❌ Adding catalyst shifts equilibrium position. ✅ Catalyst speeds both forward and backward reactions equally; no shift in equilibrium.
  • ❌ High pressure always favors products. ✅ High pressure favors side with fewer gas moles (Le Chatelier).
  • ❌ Exothermic reactions are spontaneous. ✅ Spontaneity depends on ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, not just ΔH.
  • ❌ Endothermic reactions cannot be spontaneous. ✅ Endothermic reactions can be spontaneous if TΔS > ΔH (e.g., ice melting above 0°C).
  • ❌ ΔH and ΔU are always the same. ✅ ΔH = ΔU + PΔV; they differ when gas moles change.
  • ❌ Heat (q) and work (w) are state functions. ✅ q and w are path functions; only ΔU, ΔH, ΔS, ΔG are state functions.
  • ❌ Oxidation and reduction happen separately. ✅ Oxidation and reduction always occur together (redox).
  • ❌ Oxidizing agent gets oxidized. ✅ Oxidizing agent accepts electrons and gets reduced.
  • ❌ Reducing agent gets reduced. ✅ Reducing agent donates electrons and gets oxidized.
  • ❌ Faraday's laws apply only to metals. ✅ Faraday's laws apply to any substance deposited/dissolved at electrodes.
  • ❌ Real gases obey PV=nRT at all conditions. ✅ Real gases deviate at high pressure and low temperature.
  • ❌ van der Waals constants a and b are same for all gases. ✅ a (attraction) and b (volume) are gas-specific.
  • ❌ Surface tension increases with temperature. ✅ Surface tension decreases as temperature increases.
  • ❌ Viscosity decreases with temperature for liquids. ✅ Viscosity of liquids decreases with T; gases increase with T.
  • ❌ Colligative properties depend on nature of solute. ✅ Colligative properties depend only on number of particles, not identity.
  • ❌ Raoult's law applies to all solutions. ✅ Raoult's law is exact for ideal solutions; approximate for dilute real solutions.
  • ❌ Osmotic pressure is a colligative property. ✅ Yes — π = CRT depends on solute particle concentration.
  • ❌ Equilibrium means reaction stopped. ✅ Equilibrium means forward rate = backward rate (dynamic, not static).
  • ❌ K = 1 means no reaction. ✅ K = 1 means reactants and products are equally favored at equilibrium.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Distinguish physical and chemical equilibrium with one example each, and explain why chemical equilibrium is called dynamic. — Physical equilibrium is established between two states of the same substance, with no new substances formed: in a closed flask at constant temperature, water evaporates at the same rate as vapour condenses (H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂O(g)), giving a constant vapour pressure. Chemical equilibrium is established between different substances that interconvert: in CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g), decomposition and recombination run at equal rates, so the masses of all three stay constant. Dynamic nature: at equilibrium neither opposing reaction stops — both continue at exactly equal rates, so the composition is constant while individual molecules endlessly interconvert between reactant and product. The experimental proof is the radioactive tracer experiment: a trace of labelled reactant injected at equilibrium appears in the products even though the measured concentrations do not change — proof that the forward reaction continues at equilibrium.
  • NEB-style (2m): State four characteristics of chemical equilibrium. — (1) Constancy of composition: the concentrations of all reactants and products become and remain constant (though not necessarily equal to each other). (2) Dynamic activity: the forward and reverse reactions continue indefinitely at equal rates. (3) Approach from either side: starting from pure reactants or from pure products at the same temperature leads to the same equilibrium state. (4) Catalyst and conditions: a catalyst speeds both directions equally and changes only the time needed to reach equilibrium, never its position; temperature, pressure and concentration changes can shift the equilibrium position (Le Chatelier's principle).
  • NEB-style (2m): Why can a reversible reaction never go to completion in a closed vessel? — In a closed vessel the products cannot escape, so as the forward reaction runs, products accumulate and begin to react backwards. The reverse rate rises while the forward rate falls (reactants depleting), until the two rates become equal — the equilibrium state. From that moment there is no net change in either direction, so the reaction stops short of completion by built-in design. Completion is possible only in an open system where a product is continuously removed (escape of a gas, precipitation), because then the reverse reaction never gets material to work with.

Key Points

  • Reversible reaction: both directions at once (⇌); closed system required. Physical equilibrium: phase change, no new substances (H₂O(l) ⇌ H₂O(g)); chemical: recomposition (CaCO₃ ⇌ CaO + CO₂). Summary

Equilibrium is chemistry's steady state: opposing processes running at equal rates so that nothing observable changes while everything molecular continues. A reversible reaction in a closed vessel marches toward that tie automatically — forward rate falling, reverse rate climbing, crossing at equilibrium. Physical equilibrium trades only states (water and its vapour; sugar and its saturated solution); chemical equilibrium trades identity (CaCO₃ with CaO and CO₂; N₂O₄ with NO₂). The state is dynamic — proven by radioactive tracers that flow from reactant to product at unchanged concentrations — and it is reached from either side, sits at constant but unequal concentrations, is sped but not shifted by catalysts, and moves only when conditions (temperature, pressure, concentration) change. Equal rates, not equal amounts: that sentence is the hinge on which the next three topics — mass action, Kc, Kp, and Le Chatelier — all turn.
