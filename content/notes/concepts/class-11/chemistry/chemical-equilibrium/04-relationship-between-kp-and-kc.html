Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Two Constants, One Reaction, One Bridge

    Gas-phase equilibria can be written in concentrations (Kc) or in partial pressures (Kp). The two are not rivals but conversions of the same fact, and Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn is the bridge — where Δn is the change in gas moles and T is absolute temperature. Master the sign of Δn and every conversion becomes a one-liner.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Kp — the pressure-side constant

    • For gas reactions, the active mass of each gas is proportional to its partial pressure (from the ideal gas law: pV = nRT → p = (n/V)RT = CRT).
    • For aA(g) + bB(g) ⇌ cC(g) + dD(g): Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d / (pA)^a(pB)^b — partial pressures replacing concentrations.

    2.2 Deriving the bridge

    • Each partial pressure p_i = C_iRT = [i]RT.
    • Substitute into Kp: Kp = ([C]RT)^c([D]RT)^d / ([A]RT)^a([B]RT)^b = Kc × (RT)^(c+d−a−b).
    • Define Δn = moles of gaseous products − moles of gaseous reactants:

    Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn

    2.3 The three cases of Δn

    • Δn = 0 (no gas-mole change): Kp = Kc — e.g. H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI.
    • Δn > 0 (more gas on the product side): Kp > Kc (for RT > 1) — e.g. N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂ (Δn = +1).
    • Δn < 0 (fewer gas moles in products): Kp < Kc — e.g. N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ (Δn = −2).

    2.4 Units and the temperature rule

    • Kp carries the units of pressure^Δn (atm^Δn or bar^Δn); Kc carries (mol/L)^Δn. When Δn = 0 both are dimensionless.
    • R must match the pressure unit: 0.0821 L·atm/(mol·K) for atm; 8.314 J/(mol·K) for bar/pascal-based work.
    • T is always kelvin; and Kp, like Kc, depends only on temperature.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why partial pressures are active masses in disguise

    • The ideal gas law rearranges to p = CRT: concentration times RT. So pressure is just concentration wearing a different coat — which is why the two constants differ only by the (RT)^Δn correction for the change in gas moles.

    4.2 Worked conversions

    • H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI: Δn = 2 − 2 = 0 → Kp = Kc.
    • N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂: Δn = 2 − 1 = +1 → Kp = Kc(RT) — at 300 K with Kc = 0.16: Kp = 0.16 × 0.0821 × 300 ≈ 3.94.
    • N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃: Δn = 2 − 4 = −2 → Kp = Kc(RT)⁻² = Kc/(RT)² — at 500 K with Kc = 0.061: Kp = 0.061/(0.0821 × 500)² ≈ 3.6 × 10⁻⁵.
    • PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂: Δn = +1 → Kp = Kc(RT).

    4.3 The reverse and scaled equations

    • The same flip/scale rules as Kc apply to Kp: reverse → 1/Kp; double coefficients → (Kp)²; Δn changes sign or doubles accordingly.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Δn counts gases only: for CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g), Δn = +1 (the solids contribute nothing); counting all moles (3 − 2 = +1 here coincidentally, but wrong in general) misleads — only gas moles enter.

  • Kelvin, always: using 25 °C as 25 in the bridge is the standard error — T must be 298 K.
  • R must match the pressure unit: pairing atm pressures with R = 8.314 (SI) mixes systems — 0.0821 goes with atm.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (3m): Derive the relationship Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn. — For a gas-phase reaction aA(g) + bB(g) ⇌ cC(g) + dD(g), the ideal gas law gives the partial pressure of each gas as p_i = n_iRT/V = [i]RT, i.e. partial pressure equals molar concentration times RT. The pressure-based constant is Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d/(pA)^a(pB)^b. Substituting p_i = [i]RT: Kp = ([C]RT)^c([D]RT)^d/([A]RT)^a([B]RT)^b = ([C]^c[D]^d/[A]^a[B]^b) × (RT)^(c+d−a−b). The bracketed ratio is exactly Kc, and the exponent c + d − a − b is the change in gaseous moles Δn. Hence Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn, with T in kelvin.

  • NEB-style (2m): For which of the following is Kp = Kc: (a) N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, (b) H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, (c) N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂? Give Δn for each. — (a) Δn = 2 − 4 = −2, so Kp = Kc(RT)⁻² < Kc. (b) Δn = 2 − 2 = 0, so Kp = Kc. (c) Δn = 2 − 1 = +1, so Kp = Kc(RT) > Kc. Only (b) has equal constants, because the gas-mole count does not change.
  • NEB-style (2m numerical): For N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, Kc = 0.16 at 300 K. Calculate Kp (R = 0.0821 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹). — Δn = 2 − 1 = +1, so Kp = Kc(RT) = 0.16 × 0.0821 × 300 = 3.94 (units atm¹).

Confusion

  • ❌ All cells look the same under a microscope. ✅ Prokaryotic cells lack membrane-bound organelles; eukaryotic cells have them.
  • ❌ DNA and RNA are identical in structure. ✅ DNA is double-stranded with deoxyribose and thymine; RNA is single-stranded with ribose and uracil.
  • ❌ Mitosis produces genetically different cells. ✅ Mitosis produces genetically identical diploid daughter cells.
  • ❌ Meiosis produces identical cells. ✅ Meiosis produces 4 genetically diverse haploid gametes.
  • ❌ Crossing over occurs in mitosis. ✅ Crossing over occurs in Prophase I of meiosis only.
  • ❌ All enzymes are proteins. ✅ Most enzymes are proteins; some RNA molecules (ribozymes) also have catalytic activity.
  • ❌ ATP is the only energy currency. ✅ GTP, UTP, and CTP also serve as energy carriers in specific pathways.
  • ❌ Photosynthesis occurs in all plant cells. ✅ Photosynthesis occurs only in cells containing chloroplasts (mainly mesophyll).
  • ❌ Respiration and photosynthesis are opposite processes with same equation. ✅ They are complementary but occur in different organelles and under different conditions.
  • ❌ Oxygen is produced during respiration. ✅ Oxygen is consumed in aerobic respiration; produced during photosynthesis.
  • ❌ Food chain shows all feeding relationships. ✅ Food chain is linear; food web shows interconnected feeding relationships.
  • ❌ 90% of energy is transferred to next trophic level. ✅ Only ~10% energy transfers; ~90% lost as heat.
  • ❌ Biomagnification decreases toxin concentration up the food chain. ✅ Biomagnification increases toxin concentration at higher trophic levels.
  • ❌ Carbon cycle only involves plants and animals. ✅ Carbon cycle involves atmosphere, oceans, fossils, rocks, and decomposers.
  • ❌ Nitrogen fixation only occurs in bacteria. ✅ Some lightning and industrial processes (Haber) also fix nitrogen.
  • ❌ All fungi are harmful. ✅ Many fungi are beneficial (decomposers, mycorrhizae, food, antibiotics like penicillin).
  • ❌ Viruses are living organisms. ✅ Viruses are acellular — they cannot reproduce without a host and lack metabolism.
  • ❌ Bacteria are always harmful. ✅ Most bacteria are harmless or beneficial (gut flora, nitrogen fixation, decomposition).
  • ❌ Gram-positive and Gram-negative differ only in color. ✅ Gram-positive has thick peptidoglycan layer; Gram-negative has thin layer + outer membrane.
  • ❌ Natural selection creates perfect organisms. ✅ Natural selection favors traits suited to current environment; environments change.
  • ❌ Evolution is goal-oriented. ✅ Evolution has no goal; it acts on random variation filtered by selection.
  • ❌ Homologous and analogous structures mean the same thing. ✅ Homologous = common ancestry (divergent); Analogous = similar function, different origin (convergent).
  • ❌ Fossils are preserved bones only. ✅ Fossils include impressions, trace fossils, amber-preserved organisms, and molecular fossils.
  • ❌ Lamarckism is completely wrong. ✅ Lamarck's inheritance of acquired characteristics is largely rejected, but epigenetics shows some environmental effects can be inherited.
  • ❌ Five-kingdom classification is outdated. ✅ Six-kingdom (adding Archaea) and phylogenetic systems are more current, but five-kingdom remains useful for education.
  • ❌ Binomial nomenclature names change frequently. ✅ Names are stable once formally published; changes require formal revision by taxonomic authorities.
  • ❌ All algae are aquatic. ✅ Some algae live in soil, on tree bark, or in symbiotic relationships (lichens).
  • ❌ Bryophytes have vascular tissue. ✅ Bryophytes (mosses, liverworts, hornworts) lack true vascular tissue (xylem/phloem).
  • ❌ Gymnosperms produce flowers. ✅ Gymnosperms have naked seeds in cones; angiosperms produce flowers and enclosed seeds.
  • ❌ Double fertilization occurs in gymnosperms. ✅ Double fertilization (zygote + endosperm) is unique to angiosperms.
  • ❌ Earthworms have an open circulatory system. ✅ Earthworms have a closed circulatory system with dorsal and ventral vessels.
  • ❌ Frogs breathe only through lungs. ✅ Frogs use cutaneous respiration (skin), buccal cavity, and lungs.
  • ❌ Conservation only means protecting animals. ✅ Conservation includes genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity; in-situ and ex-situ strategies.
  • ❌ In-situ and ex-situ conservation are the same. ✅ In-situ = conservation in natural habitat (national parks); Ex-situ = conservation outside habitat (zoos, seed banks).

Examples

  • The ammonia check — Kc = 0.061 at 500 K for N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃: dividing by (0.0821 × 500)² ≈ 1681 gives Kp ≈ 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ — four orders smaller, because four gas moles collapse into two.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Derive the relationship between Kp and Kc for a gaseous reaction. — Consider aA(g) + bB(g) ⇌ cC(g) + dD(g). By the ideal gas law, the partial pressure of any gaseous component is p_i = n_iRT/V = [i]RT, where [i] is its molar concentration. The pressure-based equilibrium constant is Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d / (pA)^a(pB)^b. Substituting p_i = [i]RT throughout: Kp = ([C]RT)^c([D]RT)^d / ([A]RT)^a([B]RT)^b = ([C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b) × (RT)^(c+d−a−b). The ratio in brackets is the concentration-based constant Kc, and the exponent c + d − a − b is the change in the number of gaseous moles, Δn. Therefore Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn, with T in kelvin and R consistent with the pressure units. Special cases: Δn = 0 gives Kp = Kc; Δn > 0 gives Kp > Kc; Δn < 0 gives Kp < Kc.
  • NEB-style (2m): For which reaction is Kp = Kc: (a) N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), (b) H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), (c) N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g)? Compute Δn for each. — (a) Δn = (2) − (1 + 3) = −2; Kp = Kc(RT)⁻² = Kc/(RT)², so Kp < Kc. (b) Δn = (2) − (1 + 1) = 0; Kp = Kc(RT)⁰ = Kc — the constants are equal because the total gaseous moles are unchanged. (c) Δn = (2) − (1) = +1; Kp = Kc(RT), so Kp > Kc. Only reaction (b) has Kp = Kc.
  • NEB-style (2m numerical): For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), Kc = 0.16 at 300 K. Calculate Kp (R = 0.0821 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹). — Δn = 2 − 1 = +1, so Kp = Kc(RT)^1 = 0.16 × 0.0821 × 300. Multiplying: 0.0821 × 300 = 24.63; 0.16 × 24.63 ≈ 3.94. Since Δn = +1, Kp carries the units of pressure (atm¹). (Cross-check: at 300 K, RT ≈ 24.6 > 1, so Kp must exceed Kc — 3.94 > 0.16, consistent.)

Formulas

    • p_i = [i]RT
    • Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d / (pA)^a(pB)^b
    • Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn; Kc = Kp/(RT)^Δn
    • Δn = Σ gas moles (products) − Σ gas moles (reactants)
    • Worked: Kc = 0.16, T = 300 K, Δn = +1 → Kp = 3.94

Key Points

  • Partial pressure = CRT (ideal gas) - pressures are concentrations in disguise. Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d/(pA)^a(pB)^b. Bridge: Kp = Kc(RT)^dn, dn = gas moles products - reactants; T in kelvin; R matches pressure unit. dn = 0: Kp = Kc (HI). dn = +1: Kp = Kc(RT) (N2O4, PCl5). dn = -2: Kp = Kc/(RT)2 (NH3). Units atm^dn. Worked: Kc = 0.16 at 300 K for N2O4 gives Kp = 3.94.

Summary

Gas equilibria speak two dialects — concentrations and partial pressures — and Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn is the dictionary. The derivation is one ideal-gas line: p = [i]RT turns every pressure into a concentration, and collecting the leftover RT factors leaves (RT) raised to Δn, the change in gaseous moles between products and reactants. The sign of Δn then does all the talking: Δn = 0 (H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI) makes the constants identical; Δn = +1 (N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂) multiplies Kc by RT, so Kp exceeds Kc; Δn = −2 (N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃) divides by (RT)², so Kp collapses below Kc — 0.061 at 500 K becomes 3.6 × 10⁻⁵. Units follow Δn on each side ((mol/L)^Δn for Kc, atm^Δn for Kp), temperature must be kelvin, and R must match the pressure unit. Worked once — Kc = 0.16 at 300 K giving Kp = 3.94 for the N₂O₄/NO₂ pair — the conversion becomes a two-line exam answer.

  • Partial pressure = CRT (ideal gas) - pressures are concentrations in disguise. Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d/(pA)^a(pB)^b. Bridge: Kp = Kc(RT)^dn, dn = gas moles products - reactants; T in kelvin; R matches pressure unit. dn = 0: Kp = Kc (HI). dn = +1: Kp = Kc(RT) (N2O4, PCl5). dn = -2: Kp = Kc/(RT)2 (NH3). Units atm^dn. Worked: Kc = 0.16 at 300 K for N2O4 gives Kp = 3.94.
Important Concepts
  • One sign decides everything: Δn. Zero means the two constants are twins; positive makes Kp larger; negative makes Kc larger — read the balanced equation before touching the calculator.
Examples
  • The ammonia check — Kc = 0.061 at 500 K for N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃: dividing by (0.0821 × 500)² ≈ 1681 gives Kp ≈ 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ — four orders smaller, because four gas moles collapse into two.
Universal Facts
  • High-altitude physiology and pressure cookers both live on p = CRT: the same relation that turns concentrations into partial pressures governs how thin air and sealed steam behave.
Important Statements
  • "Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn, where Δn is the difference between the number of moles of gaseous products and gaseous reactants."
Important Notes
  • No verified PYQ on the Kp–Kc relationship exists in the current bank (the 2024 set tests Le Chatelier and a Kc calculation — solved in topics 56 and 54); all items above are NEB-style practice.
Exam Short Tricks
  • Count gas moles first: Δn = 0 → answer "Kp = Kc" instantly. Bridge ladder: +1 → ×RT; −1 → ÷RT; −2 → ÷(RT)². Kelvin conversion: °C + 273.
Exam Notes
  • In derivations, state p = [i]RT explicitly — that single ideal-gas line is where the (RT)^Δn factor comes from, and examiners look for it.
Formulas
    • p_i = [i]RT
    • Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d / (pA)^a(pB)^b
    • Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn; Kc = Kp/(RT)^Δn
    • Δn = Σ gas moles (products) − Σ gas moles (reactants)
    • Worked: Kc = 0.16, T = 300 K, Δn = +1 → Kp = 3.94

Practice Questions
  • NEB-style (3m): Derive the relationship between Kp and Kc for a gaseous reaction. — Consider aA(g) + bB(g) ⇌ cC(g) + dD(g). By the ideal gas law, the partial pressure of any gaseous component is p_i = n_iRT/V = [i]RT, where [i] is its molar concentration. The pressure-based equilibrium constant is Kp = (pC)^c(pD)^d / (pA)^a(pB)^b. Substituting p_i = [i]RT throughout: Kp = ([C]RT)^c([D]RT)^d / ([A]RT)^a([B]RT)^b = ([C]^c[D]^d / [A]^a[B]^b) × (RT)^(c+d−a−b). The ratio in brackets is the concentration-based constant Kc, and the exponent c + d − a − b is the change in the number of gaseous moles, Δn. Therefore Kp = Kc(RT)^Δn, with T in kelvin and R consistent with the pressure units. Special cases: Δn = 0 gives Kp = Kc; Δn > 0 gives Kp > Kc; Δn < 0 gives Kp < Kc.
  • NEB-style (2m): For which reaction is Kp = Kc: (a) N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), (b) H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), (c) N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g)? Compute Δn for each. — (a) Δn = (2) − (1 + 3) = −2; Kp = Kc(RT)⁻² = Kc/(RT)², so Kp < Kc. (b) Δn = (2) − (1 + 1) = 0; Kp = Kc(RT)⁰ = Kc — the constants are equal because the total gaseous moles are unchanged. (c) Δn = (2) − (1) = +1; Kp = Kc(RT), so Kp > Kc. Only reaction (b) has Kp = Kc.
  • NEB-style (2m numerical): For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), Kc = 0.16 at 300 K. Calculate Kp (R = 0.0821 L atm mol⁻¹ K⁻¹). — Δn = 2 − 1 = +1, so Kp = Kc(RT)^1 = 0.16 × 0.0821 × 300. Multiplying: 0.0821 × 300 = 24.63; 0.16 × 24.63 ≈ 3.94. Since Δn = +1, Kp carries the units of pressure (atm¹). (Cross-check: at 300 K, RT ≈ 24.6 > 1, so Kp must exceed Kc — 3.94 > 0.16, consistent.)
