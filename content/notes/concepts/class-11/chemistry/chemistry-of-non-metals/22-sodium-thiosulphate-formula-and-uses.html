  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Photographer's Fixer and the Iodine Clock

    Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O (hypo): formula, preparation, and its uses — photography, iodometry, chlorine removal, gold extraction.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Formula and structure

    • Sodium thiosulphate pentahydrate: Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O — "hypo".
    • Structure: [S–SO₃]²⁻ — one sulphate-like SO₃ unit plus one sulphane S (the "extra" sulphur at oxidation state −1; the two S average +2).
    • White crystalline solid, efflorescent, mp ~48 °C, very soluble in water; decomposes on strong heating: 4Na₂S₂O₃ → 3Na₂SO₄ + Na₂S₄... (simplified: gives Na₂SO₄ + Na₂S + SO₂).

    2.2 Preparation

    • Na₂SO₃ + S —(boil)→ Na₂S₂O₃ (sulphite boiled with sulphur flowers).
    • By-product routes: Na₂S + Na₂CO₃ + SO₂ → ... (industrial).
    • Crystallises as the pentahydrate from aqueous solution.

    2.3 Key reactions (the working chemistry)

    • With iodine (iodometry): 2Na₂S₂O₃ + I₂ → Na₂S₄O₆ (sodium tetrathionate) + 2NaI — the standard titration: starch endpoint, blue → colourless.
    • With dilute acids (decomposition): Na₂S₂O₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + S↓ + SO₂ + H₂O — yellow sulphur + pungent gas (why hypo is never mixed with acid); the basis of the "iodine clock"/thiosulphate turbidity experiments.
    • With chlorine (antichlor): Na₂S₂O₃ + 4Cl₂ + 5H₂O → 2NaHSO₄ + 8HCl — removes excess Cl₂ from bleached fabric.
    • Silver halide solvent (photography): AgBr + 2Na₂S₂O₃ → Na₃[Ag(S₂O₃)₂] + NaBr — dissolves unexposed AgBr in film "fixing".
    • Gold leaching: 4Au + 8Na₂S₂O₃ + O₂ + 2H₂O → 2Na₄[Au(S₂O₃)₂] + 4NaOH (alternative to cyanidation).

    2.4 Uses

    • Photography: fixer/fixing bath — dissolves unexposed AgBr, making the image permanent.
    • Iodometric titration: standard volumetric reagent for iodine (and indirectly oxidisers via KI liberation).
    • Antichlor: removes residual chlorine after bleaching textiles/paper.
    • Medicine: cyanide poisoning antidote (converts CN⁻ to thiocyanate, SCN⁻, via rhodanese).
    • Gold/silver extraction (thiosulphate leaching); water dechlorination in aquariums; iodine-clock demonstrations.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why hypo fixes photographs

    • Film emulsion = AgBr grains; exposure reduces some to black Ag (the image). The remaining light-sensitive AgBr must be removed or the film darkens on further exposure. Thiosulphate complexes Ag⁺ strongly: AgBr + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → [Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻ + Br⁻, dissolving AgBr while leaving the metallic silver image intact — the image becomes permanent.

    4.2 Why iodometry works

    • I₂ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ → 2I⁻ + S₄O₆²⁻ is fast, quantitative and has a clean endpoint with starch (blue just before the end, vanishing at completion). Any oxidiser can be measured indirectly: oxidise excess KI, titrate the liberated I₂.

    4.3 Why acid destroys hypo

    • Protonation destabilises [S₂O₃]²⁻: it falls apart to SO₂ + S (the −1 sulphane sulphur precipitates yellow). The reaction is the visual test for thiosulphate and the reason fixing baths are kept alkaline.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    • "Hypo is sodium sulphate." No — sulphate is SO₄²⁻; thiosulphate is SO₃S²⁻, with the extra sulphur doing the complexing/reducing work.
    • "Thiosulphate titrates all oxidants directly." Indirectly: the oxidant liberates I₂ from KI, and the I₂ is titrated — the iodine is the middleman.
    • "Fixing develops the image." Development (reducing exposed Ag⁺ to Ag) comes first; fixing then removes the unexposed AgBr. Two different baths.

  • NEB-style (2m): Give the molecular formula of sodium thiosulphate and describe its preparation. — Formula: Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O (sodium thiosulphate pentahydrate, "hypo"). The anion is [S–SO₃]²⁻ — a sulphate with one oxygen replaced by sulphur; the two sulphurs average an oxidation state of +2 (one is +5, the other −1).   • NEB-style (2m): Write the equation for the reaction of sodium thiosulphate with iodine and state its analytical use. — 2Na₂S₂O₃ + I₂ → Na₂S₄O₆ + 2NaI — iodine oxidises thiosulphate to tetrathionate (S₄O₆²⁻) while being reduced to iodide. Use (iodometry): any oxidising agent is treated with excess KI, liberating an equivalent amount of I₂; the liberated iodine is then titrated against standard hypo using starch indicator — the deep blue colour persists until just before the endpoint and vanishes sharply when the last trace of I₂ is consumed. This is the standard method for estimating oxidisers (Cu²⁺, bleaching powder chlorine, etc.).

This is the standard method for estimating oxidisers (Cu²⁺, bleaching powder chlorine, etc.).

Summary

Sodium thiosulphate — Na₂S₂O₃·5H₂O, "hypo" — is sulphate with one oxygen swapped for a sulphane sulphur, and that one atom does all the work. Prepared by boiling sulphite with sulphur (Na₂SO₃ + S → Na₂S₂O₃), it is a mild reducer and a strong Ag⁺ ligand. Its four careers: photography — the fixer bath dissolves unexposed AgBr as [Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻, making the developed image permanent; iodometry — 2Na₂S₂O₃ + I₂ → Na₂S₄O₆ + 2NaI, the starch-endpoint titration behind every oxidant estimate; antichlor — removing residual Cl₂ from bleached fabric (Na₂S₂O₃ + 4Cl₂ + 5H₂O → 2NaHSO₄ + 8HCl); and medicine — the cyanide-poisoning antidote that converts CN⁻ to thiocyanate. Add acid and it betrays itself: yellow sulphur and SO₂ (Na₂S₂O₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + S + SO₂ + H₂O) — the reason fixing baths stay alkaline and the visual test for the ion.
