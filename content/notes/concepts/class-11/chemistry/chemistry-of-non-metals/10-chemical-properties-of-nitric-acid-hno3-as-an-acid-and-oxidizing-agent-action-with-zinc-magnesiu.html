Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Nitric Acid's Full Job Description

    Row 65 named the oxyacids and sketched the Ostwald process; this topic puts nitric acid through its full working day — as a strong acid, as an oxidising agent of graded strength, its action on Zn, Mg, Fe, Cu, S, C, SO₂ and H₂S, and the brown-ring test for the nitrate ion.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 As an acid

    • Strong monobasic acid: HNO₃ → H⁺ + NO₃⁻ (fully ionised); turns blue litmus red, neutralises bases and basic oxides, reacts with carbonates and metals below hydrogen in reactivity without any redox:
    • NaOH + HNO₃ → NaNO₃ + H₂O; Na₂CO₃ + 2HNO₃ → 2NaNO₃ + H₂O + CO₂.
    • With metals it cannot simply displace H₂ — its oxidising power dominates: dilute HNO₃ + Mg/Mn gives a little H₂ at the start, but the acid quickly oxidises it to water.

    2.2 As an oxidising agent — the concentration code

    ConditionN reduced toExample
    conc. HNO₃+4 (NO₂)Cu + 4HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O
    dil. HNO₃+2 (NO)3Cu + 8HNO₃ → 3Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO + 4H₂O
    very dil. + Zn/Mg/Mn−3 (NH₄⁺)4Zn + 10HNO₃ → 4Zn(NO₃)₂ + NH₄NO₃ + 3H₂O
    very dil. + Mg/Mn (alt.)+1 (N₂O)4Mg + 10HNO₃ → 4Mg(NO₃)₂ + N₂O + 5H₂O
    • Non-metals are oxidised to their highest oxyacids:
    • S + 6HNO₃(conc) → H₂SO₄ + 6NO₂ + 2H₂O
    • C + 4HNO₃(conc) → CO₂ + 4NO₂ + 2H₂O
    • P₄ + 20HNO₃(conc) → 4H₃PO₄ + 20NO₂ + 4H₂O
    • Compounds are oxidised too:
    • SO₂ + 2HNO₃ → H₂SO₄ + 2NO₂ (S +4 → +6)
    • 3H₂S + 2HNO₃(dil) → 2NO + 3S + 4H₂O (S −2 → 0)
    • FeSO₄ + 2HNO₃ → FeSO₄·NO (brown ring, next section) — nitrosyl ferrous sulphate
    • Passivity: Fe, Al, Cr become passive in conc. HNO₃ (oxide film) — Al tankers ship the acid.

    2.3 The brown-ring test for NO₃⁻

    • Freshly prepared FeSO₄ solution + nitrate solution; conc. H₂SO₄ poured down the side.
    • A brown ring at the junction: the complex [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺ (nitrosyl ferrous sulphate, nitrosonium-bound).
    • Equation: FeSO₄ + NO → FeSO₄·NO (brown). Chemistry: 6Fe²⁺ + 2NO₃⁻ + 8H⁺ → 6Fe³⁺ + 2NO + 4H₂O, then Fe²⁺ + NO → [FeNO]²⁺.
    • The ring must not be shaken — it is fragile; H₂SO₄ must be added carefully along the tube wall.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why the reduction product deepens with dilution

    • The oxidising species is effectively NO₃⁻ + H⁺. Concentrated acid delivers a high oxidant flux, so the reduction stops early at NO₂ (a one-electron step). Dilute acid, with more water and less oxidant per unit volume, allows the reduction to proceed further to NO; very dilute acid with an active metal pushes all the way to ammonium.
    • Practical reading: brown fumes = concentrated; colourless gas browning at the tube mouth = dilute.

    4.2 Why non-metals give oxyacids, not salts

    • Non-metals have no cations to offer; the nitrate simply accepts electrons while the non-metal is pushed to its highest stable oxidation state (S→+6 in H₂SO₄, C→+4 in CO₂, P→+5 in H₃PO₄). Iodine is an exception in some texts (gives HIO₃ in some conditions) — stick to S, C, P for NEB.

    4.3 Why the brown ring is brown

    • NO is a π-acceptor ligand; binding it to Fe²⁺ creates a charge-transfer complex absorbing in the visible — the colour is the evidence of the nitrosyl complex, and its fragility (it decomposes on shaking or warming) is part of the test's discipline.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    • "HNO₃ + active metal always gives H₂." No — nitric acid is an oxidising acid; only very dilute acid with Mg/Mn gives traces of H₂ before oxidation takes over. Never write Zn + HNO₃ → Zn(NO₃)₂ + H₂ in an NEB answer.
    • "Passive metals never react with any nitric acid." Passivity is specific to concentrated acid; dilute HNO₃ dissolves Fe and Al normally.
    • "The brown ring is Fe₂O₃." It is the nitrosyl complex [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺ — a coordination compound, not an oxide.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Describe the brown-ring test for the nitrate ion, giving the chemistry of ring formation. — Take the nitrate solution in a test tube and add an equal volume of freshly prepared FeSO₄ solution; mix. Hold the tube inclined and run concentrated H₂SO₄ carefully down the wall so it forms a separate lower layer. At the junction of the two liquids a brown ring appears. Chemistry: the acid protonates and decomposes some nitrate to nitric oxide, which is caught by Fe²⁺: 6Fe²⁺ + 2NO₃⁻ + 8H⁺ → 6Fe³⁺ + 2NO + 4H₂O, followed by complex formation Fe²⁺ + NO + 5H₂O → [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺ (nitrosyl ferrous sulphate), a brown charge-transfer complex. The ring is delicate: shaking or warming destroys it, so the acid must be added gently.
  • NEB-style (2m): Write balanced equations for the action of (a) concentrated and (b) very dilute nitric acid on zinc, naming the nitrogen product in each. — (a) Concentrated: Zn + 4HNO₃(conc) → Zn(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂ + 2H₂O — nitrogen is reduced only to NO₂, seen as brown fumes. (b) Very dilute: 4Zn + 10HNO₃(very dil) → 4Zn(NO₃)₂ + NH₄NO₃ + 3H₂O — the reduction reaches NH₄⁺ (N at −3), the deepest cut nitric acid makes; the ammonium nitrate stays dissolved. The comparison shows concentration controlling reduction depth.
  • NEB-style (2m): What happens when concentrated HNO3 is heated with sulphur? Give the equation and identify the oxidation states. — Sulphur is oxidised to sulphuric acid while the nitrate is reduced to brown NO₂: S + 6HNO₃(conc) → H₂SO₄ + 6NO₂ + 2H₂O. Sulphur climbs from 0 to +6; nitrogen falls from +5 to +4. The reaction shows nitric acid oxidising a non-metal all the way to its highest oxyacid rather than forming a salt — there is no cation for the nitrate to pair with.

Summary

Nitric acid wears two hats. As a strong monobasic acid it neutralises bases and carbonates like any acid; as a graded oxidising agent it is unmatched. The concentration code rules everything: concentrated acid stops at brown NO₂ (Cu + 4HNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2NO₂), dilute acid reaches colourless NO (3Cu + 8HNO₃), and very dilute acid with Zn, Mg or Mn drives nitrogen to N₂O or NH₄NO₃ — hydrogen is almost never liberated, because the nitrate ion itself is the species being reduced. Non-metals are pushed to their highest oxyacids (S → H₂SO₄, C → CO₂, P → H₃PO₄); compounds like SO₂ and H₂S are oxidised in turn. Iron, aluminium and chromium turn passive behind oxide films in the concentrated acid — the reason aluminium tankers carry it. The brown-ring test (nitrate + fresh FeSO₄, conc. H₂SO₄ layered) identifies NO₃⁻ through the fragile nitrosyl complex [Fe(H₂O)₅NO]²⁺ at the junction of the two liquids.
