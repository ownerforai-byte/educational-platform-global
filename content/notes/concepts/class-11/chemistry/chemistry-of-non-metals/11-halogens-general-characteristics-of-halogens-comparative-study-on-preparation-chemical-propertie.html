  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Salt-Formers on the Left Edge of the Table

    Group VIIA: F, Cl, Br, I (and At). Seven outer electrons, one short of a shell — hence the name halogen (salt-former). This topic covers their general characteristics, preparation, reactions with water, alkali and ammonia, oxidising character, bleaching action, and the uses of Cl₂, Br₂ and I₂.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 General characteristics

    PropertyTrend down the group
    Atomic radiusincreases
    Ionisation energydecreases
    Electronegativitydecreases (F 4.0 → I 2.5)
    Oxidising powerdecreases: F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂
    Physical stategas → gas → liquid → solid (F₂, Cl₂ gases; Br₂ liquid; I₂ solid)
    Colourpale yellow → greenish-yellow → red-brown → violet-black
    Melting/boiling pointincrease (van der Waals forces grow)
    Bond energyCl–Cl 243 > Br–Br 193 > I–I 151 kJ/mol (F₂ anomalously low, 159)
    • All exist as diatomic X₂ molecules; all form X⁻ (halide) ions readily — the most electronegative family.
    • Displacement series: F₂ displaces Cl⁻, Br⁻, I⁻; Cl₂ displaces Br⁻, I⁻; Br₂ displaces I⁻ only.

    2.2 Preparation

    • Cl₂ (lab): MnO₂ + 4HCl(conc) → MnCl₂ + Cl₂ + 2H₂O; or KMnO₄ + conc. HCl. Industrial: electrolysis of brine (Nelson cell), Deacon process (CuCl₂, 450 °C: 4HCl + O₂ → 2Cl₂ + 2H₂O).
    • Br₂ (lab): KBr + MnO₂ + conc. H₂SO₄; industrial: from seawater brines — Cl₂ passed into MgBr₂ solution displaces Br₂.
    • I₂ (lab): KI + MnO₂ + conc. H₂SO₄; industrial: from seaweed ashes (kelp) or brine wells — Cl₂ displaces I₂; also CuSO₄ + KI route (2CuSO₄ + 4KI → 2CuI + I₂ + 2K₂SO₄).

    2.3 Reactions with water, alkali, ammonia

    • Water: Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HOCl (chlorine water; HOCl = bleaching agent, bleaches by oxidation, permanent on fabrics). I₂ barely reacts (hydrolysis negligible); F₂ decomposes water violently (2F₂ + 2H₂O → 4HF + O₂).
    • Cold dilute alkali: Cl₂ + 2NaOH(cold, dil) → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O (household bleach).
    • Hot concentrated alkali: 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH(hot, conc) → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O (disproportionation deepens).
    • Ammonia: excess NH₃: 8NH₃ + 3Cl₂ → 6NH₄Cl + N₂ (white fumes of NH₄Cl; a test). Limited NH₃: NH₃ + 3Cl₂ → NCl₃ (explosive nitrogen trichloride).

    2.4 Oxidising character and bleaching

    • Order F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂: each halogen oxidises the halide ions of every heavier halogen. Evidence: Cl₂ water turns KBr solution orange, KI solution blue-black.
    • Bleaching by oxidation: Cl₂ + H₂O → HOCl → HCl + [O]; the nascent oxygen destroys colouring matter (dyes) permanently — unlike SO₂, whose bleaching is temporary reduction.
    • Dry Cl₂ does NOT bleach — water is essential (the demo with dry litmus vs moist litmus).

    2.5 Uses

    • Cl₂: water sterilisation, bleaching powder Ca(OCl)Cl manufacture, PVC plastics, CCl₄/solvents, HCl production, bleaching paper/textiles.
    • Br₂: AgBr photographic films, ethylene dibromide (petrol additive, historical), fire retardants, dyes.
    • I₂: tincture iodine antiseptic, iodised salt (KI), thyroid medicine, AgI for cloud seeding, starch indicator in iodometry.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why oxidising power falls down the group

    • Oxidising power = electron affinity + hydration energy of X⁻ − bond energy. Down the group the atom grows, the incoming electron is held less tightly, and X⁻ is stabilised less in water — so F₂ grabs electrons hardest, I₂ barely. Fluorine's F–F bond is anomalously weak (lone-pair repulsion), making it even more reactive than the trend suggests.

    4.2 Disproportionation in alkali: temperature tunes the product

    • Cold dilute NaOH: chlorine stops at +1 (NaOCl). Hot concentrated NaOH: the hypochlorite itself disproportionates to −1 and +5 (NaCl + NaClO₃). Same starting materials, temperature picks the destination — a favourite exam contrast.

    4.3 Verified PYQ (NEB 2024): oxoacids of chlorine strength order

    • HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄ in acid strength. More oxygen atoms stabilise and delocalise the negative charge of the conjugate base (ClO⁻ < ClO₂⁻ < ClO₃⁻ < ClO₄⁻), so the acid loses its proton more readily. Oxidation state of Cl rises +1 → +3 → +5 → +7 across the series.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    • "Chlorine bleaches dry litmus." False — dry Cl₂ has no bleaching power; HOCl forms only in the presence of water. The dry-vs-moist litmus experiment is the standard proof.
    • "Bleaching by SO₂ and Cl₂ is the same." Opposite mechanisms: SO₂ reduces (temporary, colour returns on oxidation by air), Cl₂ oxidises (permanent).
    • "Fluorine fits the trend everywhere." Its F–F bond is anomalously weak, its hydration energy unusually high — it is more reactive than any simple trend predicts, and it decomposes water instead of dissolving.

Practice Questions

  • Verified NEB 2024 (2m): Arrange HClO, HClO2, HClO3 and HClO4 in order of increasing acid strength and explain. — Order: HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄. Explanation: acid strength depends on how easily the O–H proton is lost, i.e. on the stability of the conjugate base ClOₙ⁻. As the number of oxygen atoms increases from one to four, the single negative charge is delocalised over more and more equivalent oxygen atoms (ClO⁻ has one oxygen to share it; ClO₄⁻ has four in full tetrahedral symmetry). The more the charge is spread, the more stable the anion and the stronger the parent acid. Hence perchloric acid HClO₄ (Cl = +7) is one of the strongest acids known, while hypochlorous acid HClO (Cl = +1) is weak.
  • NEB-style (2m): What happens when chlorine is passed through (a) cold dilute NaOH and (b) hot concentrated NaOH? — (a) Cold, dilute: chlorine disproportionates mildly: Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaOCl + H₂O — chlorine at 0 splits to −1 (NaCl) and +1 (NaOCl, sodium hypochlorite — household bleach). (b) Hot, concentrated: the hypochlorite formed first itself disproportionates further, so the net reaction is 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O — chlorine ends at −1 and +5 (sodium chlorate). Temperature and concentration deepen the disproportionation: +1 at low T, +5 at high T.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why does dry chlorine fail to bleach dry litmus, while moist chlorine bleaches it? — Bleaching is done by hypochlorous acid, not by chlorine itself. In water, chlorine hydrolyses: Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HOCl, and HOCl decomposes to HCl + [O]; the nascent oxygen oxidises the dye to a colourless product (permanent bleaching). Without water no HOCl forms, so dry chlorine leaves dry litmus untouched. The experiment — dry gas over dry litmus (no change) versus the same gas over moist litmus (bleached) — proves water's essential role.

Summary

The halogens are the salt-formers: diatomic, seven-electron, electronegative to a fault. Down the group the atoms swell, electronegativity and oxidising power sink (F₂ > Cl₂ > Br₂ > I₂), and the elements thicken from pale-yellow gas to violet-black solid. Each halogen displaces the halide of every heavier sibling — Cl₂ water turns KBr orange and KI blue-black. Chlorine water is really HOCl in solution: the acid that bleaches by oxidation (permanent), but only when water is present — dry Cl₂ bleaches nothing. In alkali, chlorine disproportionates: cold dilute NaOH catches it at +1 (NaCl + NaOCl), hot concentrated NaOH drives it to +5 (5NaCl + NaClO₃). Excess ammonia converts it to white NH₄Cl fumes plus N₂ (a test), while limited ammonia yields explosive NCl₃. The verified NEB 2024 question asks why HClO < HClO₂ < HClO₃ < HClO₄ in strength: each extra oxygen delocalises the conjugate base's charge further, stabilising the anion and strengthening the acid. Uses: Cl₂ sterilises water and builds PVC; Br₂ lives in photographic AgBr; I₂ disinfects wounds and iodises salt.

  • Oxidising power falls down Group VIIA because the growing atom holds the incoming electron less tightly and stabilises X- less in water — so every lighter halogen displaces every heavier halide, and fluorine sits at the top with an anomalously weak F-F bond making it the strongest oxidiser of all.

Important Statements

  • "Halogens are the most reactive non-metal family; their oxidising power decreases from fluorine to iodine."

Which halogen is the strongest oxidising agent?

Options

  • F2
Answer

D

Explanation

Weak F-F bond plus high hydration energy of F-.

  • NaClO4

B

Disproportionation stops at +1 when cold.

B
