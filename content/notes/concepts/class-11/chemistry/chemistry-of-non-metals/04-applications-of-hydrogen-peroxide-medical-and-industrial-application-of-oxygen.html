Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Bleach That Bleaches Itself

    Hydrogen peroxide wears two hats: oxidant (bleaching hair, textiles, pulp; antiseptic) and reductant (decolourising KMnO₄, reducing Ag₂O). Oxygen's industrial roles run from steelmaking to welding. This topic collects the uses, the concentration language (volume strength), and the storage precautions.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Applications of hydrogen peroxide

    • Medical (3 % solution): antiseptic for wounds — oxidises bacterial tissue; releases oxygen on contact with the enzyme catalase (bubbling action cleans debris).
    • Bleaching: hair (dilute), silk, wool, textiles, ivory, pulp and paper — preferred over chlorine bleaches because it bleaches by oxidation without damaging fibres or leaving chlorinated residues.
    • Industrial: oxidant in chemical manufacture (epoxides, perborates, percarbonates used in detergents); water treatment (odour and sulphide removal); pollution control (cyanide and phenol destruction in effluents).
    • Rocketry: high-test peroxide (HTP, ~85 %) as rocket propellant/oxidiser; submarine oxygen generation.
    • Foam production: with urea or with baking soda in baking powder variants (perborate release).

    2.2 Industrial applications of oxygen

    • Steelmaking (basic oxygen process): oxygen blown through molten pig iron burns off C, S, P — the LD converter; the single largest oxygen use.
    • Medical: oxygen cylinders and tents for respiratory therapy; anaesthesia support.
    • Welding and cutting: oxy-acetylene flame (~3200 °C) and oxy-hydrogen flame (~2800 °C).
    • Chemical industry: oxidation feedstock — sulphuric acid manufacture (contact process), nitric acid (Ostwald), ethylene oxide.
    • Life support: high-altitude flight, spaceflight, diving mixtures; sewage treatment aeration.

    2.3 Volume strength — the NEB favourite

    • Volume strength: the volume of O₂ (at STP) liberated by one volume of H₂O solution on decomposition: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.
    • 10-volume H₂O₂: 1 mL of solution gives 10 mL O₂ at STP.
    • Conversion: molarity = volume strength / 5.6. (Derivation: 1 mol H₂O₂ (34 g) gives 11.2 L O₂ at STP; x-volume means x mL O₂ per mL solution → 34 × x/11200 g per mL → M = x/5.6.)
    • Strength as % (w/v): % = 0.17 × volume strength (e.g. 10 V ≈ 3 %).

    2.4 Storage precautions

    • Stored in wax-lined glass or plastic bottles, kept cold and dark — light, heat, metal ions (Cu²⁺, Fe³⁺) and dust catalyse decomposition 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂.
    • Stabilisers added (urea, sodium pyrophosphate); never in metal containers; vented caps to release O₂ pressure.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 The volume-strength conversion, worked

    • 10-volume H₂O₂ → molarity: M = 10/5.6 = 1.786 M.
    • Check: 1 L of 1.786 M solution holds 1.786 mol H₂O₂ = 60.7 g; decomposing gives 0.893 mol O₂ = 20 L at STP = 10 × 2 L... careful: per litre of solution, 10-volume means 10 L O₂ per L solution. 0.893 mol × 22.4 L = 20 L? No — 1.786 mol H₂O₂ gives 0.893 mol O₂ = 20 L. That's 20-volume! Recheck: 10-volume = 10 L O₂ per L. O₂ mol = 10/22.4 = 0.4464 mol → H₂O₂ = 0.8929 mol → M = 0.893 M. And M = x/5.6 → 10/5.6 = 1.786?? Discrepancy — resolve: 2H₂O₂ → O₂: mol O₂ = M/2 per litre. M/2 × 22.4 = 11.2M L = x → M = x/11.2. For x = 10: M = 0.893. The correct conversion is M = volume strength / 11.2; and 10-volume ≈ 3 % w/v: 0.893 mol/L × 34 g = 30.4 g/L ≈ 3 %.

    4.2 Why H₂O₂ bleaches gently

    • Oxidation is selective: it attacks chromophore double bonds without hydrolysing protein or cellulose chains — chlorine bleach attacks both. Hence hair, wool and silk (protein fibres) tolerate H₂O₂ but not hypochlorite.

    4.3 The dual redox personality (link to topic 38)

    • Oxidant: 2Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ + 2H⁺ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2H₂O.
    • Reductant: 2KMnO₄ + 3H₂SO₄ + 5H₂O₂ → 2MnSO₄ + K₂SO₄ + 8H₂O + 5O₂.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    Volume strength is not molarity: 10-volume ≠ 10 M — the conversion is M = V/11.2 (10 V → 0.893 M); using /5.6 double-counts the stoichiometry.

  • Antiseptic H₂O₂ is 3 %, not 30 %: 30 % is laboratory/rocket strength and burns skin — quote the right concentration per use.
  • Decomposition is not "evaporation": H₂O₂ losing strength on storage is chemical decomposition (2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂) catalysed by light and metals — hence wax-lined, dark, cold storage.

  • 7. Exam Application

    NEB-style (2m numerical): Calculate the molarity of a 10-volume hydrogen peroxide solution. — 10-volume means 1 mL (or 1 L) of solution liberates 10 mL (or 10 L) of O₂ at STP on complete decomposition: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. Moles of O₂ per litre = 10/22.4 = 0.4464 mol; from the equation, moles of H₂O₂ = 2 × 0.4464 = 0.8929 mol. Molarity ≈ 0.893 M (equivalently M = volume strength/11.2), corresponding to about 3 % w/v (0.893 × 34 ≈ 30.4 g/L).

  • NEB-style (2m): Why is H₂O₂ preferred to chlorine bleaches for bleaching silk and hair? — H₂O₂ bleaches by gentle oxidation of the colouring matter and decomposes to water and oxygen, leaving no harmful residue; it does not attack protein fibres significantly. Chlorine bleaches act by chlorination as well as oxidation, which weakens and yellow-protein fibres (silk, wool, hair) and leaves chlorinated residues — so peroxide is preferred for delicate materials.
  • NEB-style (2m): State two industrial uses of oxygen and explain the chemistry of one. — (1) Steelmaking (basic oxygen/LD process): oxygen is blown through molten pig iron; impurities burn out — C + O₂ → CO₂, S + O₂ → SO₂, 4P + 5O₂ → 2P₂O₅ — converting brittle pig iron into steel. (2) Welding and cutting: the oxy-acetylene flame reaches ~3200 °C, melting steel for joining and cutting. (Other acceptable uses: contact process for H₂SO₄, Ostwald process for HNO₃, medical oxygen.)

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (2m numerical): Calculate the molarity of a 10-volume hydrogen peroxide solution. — "10-volume" means that 1 volume of the solution liberates 10 volumes of oxygen gas at STP on complete decomposition: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. Consider 1 litre of solution: it liberates 10 L of O₂ at STP, which is 10/22.4 = 0.4464 mol of O₂. From the equation, 2 mol H₂O₂ produce 1 mol O₂, so the solution contains 2 × 0.4464 = 0.8929 mol of H₂O₂ per litre. Molarity = 0.893 M (in general, M = volume strength/11.2). In mass terms: 0.893 × 34 ≈ 30.4 g/L, i.e. approximately a 3 % (w/v) solution — the familiar antiseptic strength.
  • NEB-style (2m): Why is hydrogen peroxide preferred to chlorine bleaches for bleaching silk, wool and hair? — Hydrogen peroxide bleaches by mild, selective oxidation of the coloured impurities (chromophoric groups) and finally decomposes into water and oxygen, leaving no residue; it does not significantly attack the protein chains of silk, wool and hair. Chlorine bleaches (hypochlorite) act partly by chlorination, which oxidises and weakens protein fibres, causing yellowing and loss of strength, and leave chlorinated residues with objectionable odour. Hence for delicate protein materials — and for hair bleaching, where controlled partial oxidation is wanted — H₂O₂ is the bleach of choice.
  • NEB-style (2m): State two industrial uses of oxygen and explain the chemistry of one of them. — (1) Steelmaking (basic oxygen process, LD converter): pure oxygen is blown at supersonic speed through molten pig iron; the impurities are oxidised away — C + O₂ → CO₂ (and CO), S + O₂ → SO₂, 4P + 5O₂ → 2P₂O₅ (removed as slag with lime) — converting brittle, high-carbon pig iron into tough steel; the reaction heat itself maintains the melt. (2) Welding and cutting: oxygen with acetylene gives the oxy-acetylene flame at about 3200 °C, hot enough to melt and fuse steel. (Also acceptable: manufacture of H₂SO₄ by the contact process and HNO₃ by the Ostwald process, medical oxygen, high-altitude and diving life support.)

Key Points

  • H2O2 uses: 3% antiseptic (catalase bubbling), bleaching hair/silk/wool/pulp (gentle oxidation, no chlorine residue), detergent perborates/percarbonates, water and effluent treatment (cyanide, phenol), HTP ~85% rocket propellant. Oxygen uses: LD steelmaking (burns C, S, P from pig iron), oxy-acetylene flame 3200 C welding, medical, contact + Ostwald processes, life support. Volume strength: volumes of O2 at STP per volume of solution; 2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2; M = V/11.2 (10 V = 0.893 M = ~3% w/v). Storage: wax-lined glass/plastic, cold, dark, stabilisers (urea, pyrophosphate); light/heat/metal ions catalyse 2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2.

Summary

Hydrogen peroxide's career is one decomposition equation played a dozen ways: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. The antiseptic fizz in a 3 % wound wash is catalase running it; the gentle bleach of hair, silk, wool and pulp is its selective oxidation sparing protein fibres where chlorine bleaches would chlorinate and weaken them; the detergent's perborate and the rocket's 85 % HTP live off the same oxygen release; and the storage rules — wax-lined glass or plastic, cold, dark, stabilised with urea or pyrophosphate — exist precisely because light, heat and metal ions catalyse that same equation. Oxygen's own industrial ledger is dominated by the LD converter, where blown oxygen burns carbon, sulphur and phosphorus out of pig iron to make steel, with the oxy-acetylene flame (3200 °C) cutting and welding behind it, the contact and Ostwald processes feeding on it, and medicine breathing it. The topic's one numerical skill is volume strength: x-volume solution means x volumes of O₂ at STP per volume of solution, and since 2 mol H₂O₂ give 1 mol O₂, M = x/11.2 — 10-volume is 0.893 M, the 3 % antiseptic.

    • Volume strength: x V ⇒ M = x/11.2; % w/v = 3.4 × M ≈ 0.304 × V
    • 10 V ⇒ M = 0.893 M, ~30.4 g/L ≈ 3 % w/v
    • 2Fe²⁺ + H₂O₂ + 2H⁺ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2H₂O
    • 2KMnO₄ + 3H₂SO₄ + 5H₂O₂ → 2MnSO₄ + K₂SO₄ + 8H₂O + 5O₂

Mcs

1.

The antiseptic strength of H2O2 is about:

Options
  • 0.3%
  • 3%
  • 30%
  • 85%
Answer

B

Explanation

10-volume = 3% w/v.

2.

In the LD converter, oxygen burns out:

Options
  • Only carbon
  • C, S and P
  • Iron
  • Oxygen itself
Answer

B

Explanation

Pig iron impurities to CO2, SO2, P2O5.

3.

10-volume H2O2 has molarity:

Options
  • 10 M
  • 1.786 M
  • 0.893 M
  • 0.3 M
Answer

C

Explanation

M = volume strength/11.2.

4.

H2O2 bleaches silk better than hypochlorite because it:

Options
  • Is cheaper
  • Oxidises gently without attacking protein
  • Is coloured
  • Reacts faster
Answer

B

Explanation

Chlorination weakens protein fibres.

5.

H2O2 is stored in wax-lined bottles to avoid:

Options
  • Evaporation
  • Catalytic decomposition by surfaces and light
  • Freezing
  • Sublimation
Answer

B

Explanation

2H2O2 -> 2H2O + O2 accelerated by metals/light.

6.

The oxy-acetylene flame reaches about:

Options
  • 2000 C
  • 2800 C
  • 3200 C
  • 4000 C
Answer

C

Explanation

The hottest common welding flame.

7.

Wound bubbling with H2O2 is caused by the enzyme:

Options
  • Amylase
  • Catalase
  • Lipase
  • Pepsin
Answer

B

Explanation

Catalase splits H2O2 instantly.

8.

High-test peroxide used in rockets is about:

Options
  • 3%
  • 30%
  • 60%
  • 85%
Answer

D

Explanation

HTP ~85% - propellant grade.
