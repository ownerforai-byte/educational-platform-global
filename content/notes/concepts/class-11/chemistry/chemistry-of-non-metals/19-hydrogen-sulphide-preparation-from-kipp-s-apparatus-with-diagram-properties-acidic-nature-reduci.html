Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Stinking Gas That Analyses Metals

    H₂S: prepared from Kipp's apparatus, acidic in water, a classical analytical reagent (metal sulphide precipitation in groups), and a reducing agent. Preparation, properties, analytical role, uses.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Preparation — Kipp's apparatus

    • FeS + dil. H₂SO₄ → FeSO₄ + H₂S↑ (in the middle globe of Kipp's apparatus; iron sulphide lumps in the middle bulb, acid in the top reservoir).
    • Kipp's apparatus: three-bulb glass device — acid in the top bulb flows down to the middle bulb containing FeS lumps; gas evolves; when the stopcock is closed, gas pressure pushes acid back up into the top bulb, separating it from FeS and stopping the reaction. On-demand gas generator, no heating needed.
    • Pure dry H₂S: antimony sulphide + conc. HCl: Sb₂S₃ + 6HCl → 2SbCl₃ + 3H₂S (no FeSO₄ spray impurity); dried over P₂O₅ (not conc. H₂SO₄ — H₂S is oxidised by it).
    • Alternative: dilute HCl on FeS works too; heating paraffin with sulphur also gives H₂S.

    2.2 Properties

    • Colourless gas, rotten-egg smell, highly poisonous (more toxic than HCN in some ranges; paralyses olfactory nerve — danger of not smelling it), slightly heavier than air, soluble in water (≈ 0.1 M saturated).
    • Dibasic weak acid in water: H₂S ⇌ H⁺ + HS⁻ ⇌ 2H⁺ + S²⁻; gives two series of salts (hydrosulphides NaHS, sulphides Na₂S).
    • Acidic nature: turns blue litmus red; with bases: NaOH + H₂S → NaHS; 2NaOH + H₂S → Na₂S + H₂O; with metals above H in activity (Fe, Zn, Mg): Fe + H₂S → FeS + H₂.
    • Reducing nature (the analytical workhorse):
    • 2FeCl₃ + H₂S → 2FeCl₂ + 2HCl + S↓ (yellow S precipitate)
    • H₂S + Cl₂ → 2HCl + S; H₂S + I₂ → 2HI + S (decolourises iodine)
    • 2HNO₃(dil) + 3H₂S → 2NO + 3S + 4H₂O
    • SO₂ + 2H₂S → 3S + 2H₂O (sulphur fog — the classic "oxidation chamber" demo)
    • 5H₂S + 2KMnO₄ + 3H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2MnSO₄ + 5S + 8H₂O
    • Combustion: 2H₂S + 3O₂ → 2H₂O + 2SO₂ (excess air); 2H₂S + O₂ → 2S + 2H₂O (limited air — sulphur deposits).

    2.3 Analytical reagent — qualitative analysis

    • In qualitative inorganic analysis, H₂S in acidic medium precipitates the Group II sulphides (CuS black, CdS yellow, Bi₂S₃ brown, As₂S₃ yellow, Sb₂S₃ orange, SnS brown, HgS black) — very low Ksp, precipitate even at low S²⁻.
    • In alkaline (NH₄OH) medium (more S²⁻ available) it precipitates Group IV sulphides: ZnS (white), MnS (flesh), NiS/CoS (black), FeS (black).
    • Selectivity comes from controlling S²⁻ via [H⁺]: acid suppresses ionisation, alkaline liberates it.

  • 4. Detailed Explanation

    4.1 Why Kipp's apparatus is clever

    • Gas pressure does the valving: open stopcock → acid falls to FeS → gas flows; close stopcock → pressure lifts acid out of contact → reaction stops. No taps of reagents, no heating — a 19th-century design still in every qualitative-analysis lab.

    4.2 Why H₂S separates metals into groups

    • Sulphide Ksp values span enormous ranges: CuS ~10⁻³⁶ vs ZnS ~10⁻²³. In acidic solution S²⁻ is so scarce only the ultra-insoluble Group II sulphides precipitate; in alkaline solution S²⁻ multiplies and the more soluble Group IV sulphides join. One reagent, two selectivity regimes — controlled by a pH knob.

    4.3 Why H₂S is a good reducer

    • S²⁻ → S⁰ is an easy two-electron climb; H₂S hands electrons to Fe³⁺, I₂, KMnO₄, HNO₃ and SO₂ alike, precipitating yellow sulphur. The yellow deposit is the visible signature of every one of these redox answers.

  • 6. Common Confusions

    • "Dry H₂S is dried over conc. H₂SO₄." No — conc. H₂SO₄ oxidises H₂S to sulphur; use P₂O₅.
    • "H₂S is a strong acid because S is big." It is a weak dibasic acid — the S–H bond is strong and H₂S is poorly ionised; acidity is not size alone.
    • "Group IV sulphides precipitate in acid too." Only in alkaline medium — the S²⁻ concentration control is the whole point of the group separation.

Practice Questions

  • NEB-style (3m): Describe the preparation of H2S using Kipp's apparatus with a labelled description. — Kipp's apparatus is a three-bulb glass generator. The top bulb is a reservoir of dilute sulphuric acid; the middle bulb holds lumps of iron sulphide (FeS) and connects to the top through a tube and to the bottom bulb through a funnel; the bottom bulb collects the evolved gas and carries the delivery stopcock. Working: opening the stopcock lets acid run down into the middle bulb, contacting FeS: FeS + H₂SO₄(dil) → FeSO₄ + H₂S↑; the gas collects in the bottom bulb and flows out. Closing the stopcock lets the accumulating gas pressure push the acid back up into the top reservoir, separating it from the FeS — the reaction stops itself. The device thus delivers gas on demand without heating or attention. The gas is dried over P₂O₅ (concentrated H₂SO₄ would oxidise it).
  • NEB-style (2m): Show that H2S is a reducing agent with two balanced equations. — (1) With ferric chloride: 2FeCl₃ + H₂S → 2FeCl₂ + 2HCl + S↓ — Fe³⁺ is reduced to Fe²⁺ while sulphide is oxidised to elemental sulphur, seen as a yellow precipitate. (2) With iodine: H₂S + I₂ → 2HI + S↓ — iodine is decolourised to iodide as sulphur deposits. (Also acceptable: SO₂ + 2H₂S → 3S + 2H₂O, the sulphur fog; or the KMnO₄ decolourisation: 5H₂S + 2KMnO₄ + 3H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2MnSO₄ + 5S + 8H₂O.) In every case H₂S climbs from the −2 to the 0 oxidation state — the definition of a reducer.
  • NEB-style (2m): How does H2S act as an analytical reagent in qualitative analysis? — H₂S supplies S²⁻ ions whose concentration is controlled by acidity. In acidic medium the ionisation of H₂S is suppressed, so only the extremely insoluble Group II sulphides precipitate: CuS (black), CdS (yellow), HgS (black), As₂S₃ (yellow), Sb₂S₃ (orange). In alkaline medium (NH₄OH/NH₄Cl buffer) the S²⁻ concentration rises, and the more soluble Group IV sulphides precipitate: ZnS (white), MnS (flesh-coloured), NiS/CoS (black), FeS (black). The acid-base control of S²⁻ is what separates the metal groups — the reagent's selectivity is a pH effect.

Summary

Hydrogen sulphide is the qualitative analyst's scalpel. Made on demand in Kipp's apparatus (FeS + dil. H₂SO₄ → FeSO₄ + H₂S; three bulbs, pressure-valve self-stopping) and dried over P₂O₅, it is a colourless rotten-egg gas — dangerously poisonous and able to numb its own smell. In water it is a weak dibasic acid (NaHS, Na₂S). Its reducing power precipitates yellow sulphur everywhere: 2FeCl₃ + H₂S → 2FeCl₂ + 2HCl + S, H₂S + I₂ → 2HI + S, SO₂ + 2H₂S → 3S + 2H₂O, and the permanganate decolourisation. Burned in limited air it deposits sulphur; in excess air it makes SO₂. Analytically, it splits the metals: acid medium drops the ultra-insoluble Group II sulphides (CuS, CdS, HgS, As₂S₃), alkaline medium adds the Group IV sulphides (ZnS, MnS, NiS, FeS) — selectivity by pH, one reagent, two regimes.
