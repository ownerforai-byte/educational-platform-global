Notes

  • Refraction: Bending of light when it passes from one transparent medium to another due to change in speed of light in different media.
  • Laws of Refraction (Snell's Laws): (1) Incident ray, refracted ray, and normal at point of incidence all lie in the same plane. (2) The ratio of sine of angle of incidence to sine of angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media: sin i / sin r = constant = ₂₁μ (refractive index of second medium w.r.t. first).
  • Refractive Index: ₂₁μ = v₁/v₂ = sin i / sin r. Absolute refractive index μ = c/v (ratio of speed in vacuum to speed in medium). μ ≥ 1, μ_vacuum = 1, μ_air ≈ 1.0003, μ_water ≈ 1.33, μ_glass ≈ 1.5, μ_diamond ≈ 2.42.
  • Optical Density: Medium with higher refractive index is optically denser. Light bends towards normal when entering denser medium, away from normal when entering rarer medium.
  • Lateral Shift: When light passes through a parallel-sided glass slab, emergent ray is parallel to incident ray but laterally displaced. Shift S = t·sin(i-r)/cos r where t = thickness.
  • Real and Apparent Depth: Object in denser medium viewed from rarer appears nearer: apparent depth = real depth / μ. Conversely, object in rarer viewed from denser appears farther: apparent depth = μ × real depth.
  • Refraction Through Multiple Media: sin i / sin r = constant at each interface. For parallel interfaces, emergent ray direction depends only on first and last medium: sin i₁ / sin r_final = μ_final/μ_initial.

Specific Conditions

  • Snell's Law Validity: Applies to isotropic media, light ray (not wavefront), and transparent interface. Not valid for anisotropic crystals or absorbing media.
  • Small Angle Approximation: For small angles, sin θ ≈ tan θ ≈ θ (radians), so apparent depth formula simplifies.
  • Normal Incidence: i = 0 → r = 0 — no bending at normal incidence, only speed changes.

Formulas

  • Snell's law: sin i / sin r = ₂₁μ = v₁/v₂
  • Absolute refractive index: μ = c/v
  • Lateral shift: S = t·sin(i-r)/cos r
  • Apparent depth: d_app = d_real/μ (viewing from rarer)
  • Apparent depth: d_app = μ·d_real (viewing from denser)
  • Speed in medium: v = c/μ

Key Points

  • Refraction: bending due to speed change at interface.
  • Snell's law: sin i/sin r = constant (refractive index).
  • Absolute index μ = c/v ≥ 1.
  • Towards normal entering denser; away from normal entering rarer.
  • Lateral shift through parallel slab: emergent parallel to incident.
  • Apparent depth: d_app = d_real/μ (denser to rarer viewing).

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Snell's law — sin i/sin r = constant for given pair of media.
  • Statement 2: Refractive index μ = c/v — ratio of speed in vacuum to speed in medium.
  • Statement 3: Light bends towards normal entering optically denser medium.
  • Statement 4: Through parallel-sided slab, emergent ray is parallel to incident ray with lateral shift.
  • Statement 5: Apparent depth = real depth/μ when viewed from rarer medium.

Important Notes

  • Frequency remains constant during refraction; wavelength and speed change.
  • Refractive index depends on wavelength (dispersion) — blue bends more than red.
  • μ is dimensionless and characteristic of the medium-wavelength pair.
  • Normal incidence: no deviation, but speed and wavelength change.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Denser medium: bends towards normal (r < i). Rarer medium: bends away (r > i).
  • Apparent depth = real depth / μ (object in water viewed from air).
  • Lateral shift through glass slab: emergent ∥ incident, shifted sideways.
  • μ = c/v — higher μ means slower light in medium.

Examples

  • Light enters water (μ = 1.33) from air at i = 45°. sin r = sin 45°/1.33 = 0.707/1.33 = 0.532, r = 32.1°. Bends towards normal.
  • Object at bottom of pond 1 m deep viewed from air: apparent depth = 1/1.33 = 0.75 m.
  • Glass slab t = 6 cm, μ = 1.5, i = 60°: sin r = sin 60°/1.5 = 0.577, r = 35.3°. S = 6 × sin(24.7°)/cos(35.3°) = 6 × 0.418/0.816 = 3.07 cm.
  • Speed of light in glass (μ = 1.5): v = c/1.5 = 3×10⁸/1.5 = 2×10⁸ m/s.

Universal Facts

  • Diamond's high refractive index (2.42) and small critical angle (24.4°) make it brilliant — multiple internal reflections trap light.
  • Mirages are caused by refraction in air layers of different temperatures/densities.
  • Atmospheric refraction makes the sun visible before actual sunrise and after sunset.
  • The refractive index of air varies with temperature and density — causes star twinkling (astronomical scintillation).

Confusion

  • Confusing bending direction: Towards normal when entering denser (slower) medium; away when entering rarer.
  • Thinking apparent depth formula works both ways: d_app = d_real/μ only when viewing from rarer medium.
  • Believing frequency changes in refraction: Frequency stays constant; speed and wavelength change.
  • Confusing refractive index μ with relative index ₂₁μ.
  • Forgetting incident ray, refracted ray, normal are coplanar (first law).

  • Away from normal

  • Parallel to surface

A

Glass is optically denser — light bends towards normal.

2.

Refractive index μ = c/v means:

Options
  • Light faster in medium
  • Light slower in medium
  • Speed unchanged
  • Light stops
Answer

B

Explanation

μ > 1 means v = c/μ < c — light slower in medium.
