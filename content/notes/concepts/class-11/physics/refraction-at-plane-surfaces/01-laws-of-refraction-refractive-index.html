Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Light Changes Direction (and Speed)

    Light bends entering water because it slows down. Refraction's two laws — and the refractive index that measures the slowing — govern lenses, prisms, mirages, fibre optics and why a swimming pool looks shallower than it is.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2023, 2024)

    2.1 Laws of refraction

    1. First law: the incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal at the point of incidence lie in the same plane.
    2. Second law (Snell's law): the ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is a constant for a given pair of media and colour of light:

    sinrsini​=n21​=constantorn1​sini=n2​sinr

    2.2 Refractive index

    • Absolute refractive index of a medium: n=vc​ — the ratio of the speed of light in vacuum to its speed in the medium (n ≥ 1).
    • Relative refractive index: n21​=n1​n2​​=v2​v1​​.
    • Typical values: vacuum 1, air 1.0003, water 4/3, glass 1.5, diamond 2.42.
    • Denser medium = larger n = slower light.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (3m, 2023 & 2024): State Snell's law of refraction. If the refractive index of glass is 1.5, find the speed of light in glass.

    • Snell's law: n1​sini=n2​sinr — the ratio sin i/sin r is constant for a given pair of media.
    • v=nc​=1.53×108​=2×108 m/s.

    Q (3m, 2024): State Snell's law. A ray of light travelling in water (n = 4/3) is incident on glass (n = 3/2) at an angle of incidence 60°. Find the angle of refraction.

    • nw​sini=ng​sinr: 34​sin60°=23​sinr
    • sinr=34​×0.866×32​=0.770 → r≈50.3°

    Q (NEB-style): Light enters from air into a medium at 45° and refracts at 30°. Find the refractive index and speed of light in it. n=sin30°sin45°​=0.50.707​=1.41; v=1.413×108​≈2.1×108 m/s.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 71: the relative index n₂₁ = v₁/v₂ chains across media.
    • To Topic 73: Snell's law with sin r = 1 defines the critical angle.
    • To Fermat/least time: light bends because it "chooses" the fastest path — refraction is a speed effect.

    4.2 Common examination mistakes

    1. Confusing the angle with the normal — all angles in refraction are measured from the normal.
    2. Using n = v/c instead of n = c/v.
    3. Forgetting that Snell's law constant depends on the pair of media and the colour of light.

Specific Conditions

  • Snell's Law Validity: Applies to isotropic media, light ray (not wavefront), and transparent interface. Not valid for anisotropic crystals or absorbing media.
  • Small Angle Approximation: For small angles, sin θ ≈ tan θ ≈ θ (radians), so apparent depth formula simplifies.
  • Normal Incidence: i = 0 → r = 0 — no bending at normal incidence, only speed changes.

Formulas

    • n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r
    • n = c/v; n₂₁ = n₂/n₁ = v₁/v₂
    • n_water = 4/3; n_glass = 1.5; n_diamond = 2.42

Key Points

  • Snell's law: n₁ sin i = n₂ sin r; n = c/v.
  • Light bends towards the normal entering a denser (higher-n) medium.

Important Statements

  • "The ratio of the sine of the angle of incidence to the sine of the angle of refraction is constant for a given pair of media and colour of light."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: Snell + speed in glass 2023 3m and 2024 3m; Snell + water-glass numerical 2024 3m.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "v = c/n is the one-line speed answer; keep sin 60° = 0.866 handy."

Exam Notes

  • State the law in words AND symbols — both parts carry marks.

Examples

  • NEB 2024 — water (4/3) → glass (3/2) at i = 60°: r ≈ 50.3°.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (2m): The speed of light in a liquid is 2.25×10⁸ m/s. Find its refractive index. — n = c/v = 3×10⁸/2.25×10⁸ = 1.33 ≈ 4/3 (the liquid is water-like).

Practice

  • Why does a straight pole look bent at the water surface?

Universal Facts

  • Diamond's n = 2.42 gives a critical angle of only 24.4° — light bounces inside almost completely, creating the sparkle.

Confusion

  • Angles from the normal, never from the surface.
  • Denser ≠ heavier: 'optically denser' means larger n and slower light — not mass density.

  • Away from normal
  • No bending
  • Parallel to surface

Explanation

Glass is optically denser — light bends towards normal.

2.

Refractive index μ = c/v means:

Options
  • Light faster in medium
  • Light slower in medium
  • Speed unchanged
  • Light stops
Answer

B

Explanation

μ > 1 means v = c/μ < c — light slower in medium.

Apparent depth of object in water (μ=1.33) viewed from air:

  • Real depth × 1.33
  • Real depth / 1.33
  • Real depth
  • Real depth × 2
Answer

B

Explanation

d_app = d_real/μ when viewing from rarer medium.

Lateral shift in glass slab: emergent ray is:

Options
  • Parallel to incident ray
  • Perpendicular to incident ray
  • At angle to incident ray
  • Absorbed
Answer

A

Explanation

For parallel faces, emergent ray ∥ incident ray with lateral displacement.

5.

Snell's law: sin i/sin r =

Options
  • μ₁/μ₂
  • μ₂/μ₁
  • v₂/v₁
  • Both B and C
Answer

D

Explanation

₂₁μ = sin i/sin r = v₁/v₂ = μ₂/μ₁.

Summary

Refraction obeys two laws: the incident ray, refracted ray and normal are coplanar, and Snell's law holds — sinrsini​=n21​, a constant for the given pair of media and colour. The refractive index n=c/v compares light's vacuum speed with its speed in the medium: glass (n = 1.5) slows light to 2×108 m/s (NEB 2023 and 2024, 3m). For a ray crossing water (4/3) into glass (3/2) at 60°, Snell's law gives r ≈ 50.3° (NEB 2024, 3m). Denser optics mean slower light, bending towards the normal; the index chains across media and sets the critical angle for total internal reflection.
