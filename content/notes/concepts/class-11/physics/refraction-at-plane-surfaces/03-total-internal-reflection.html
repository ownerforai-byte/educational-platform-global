Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Light Trapped Inside

    Past a certain angle, light hitting the inside of a glass–air boundary doesn't escape at all — it reflects perfectly, better than any mirror. Total internal reflection powers optical fibres, the glitter of diamonds and the prisms in binoculars.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021, 2022, 2024)

    2.1 From refraction to total reflection

    As the angle of incidence i (in the denser medium) grows, the angle of refraction r grows faster. At the critical angle C, the refracted ray grazes along the surface (r = 90°). Beyond C there is no refracted ray — the light is completely returned: total internal reflection.

    sinC=n1​n2​​=n12​1​(n1​>n2​)

    For glass → air (n = 1.5): sinC=1/1.5 → C ≈ 41.8°. For diamond (n = 2.42): C ≈ 24.4°.

    2.2 Two conditions (NEB 2022, 2024)

    1. Light must travel from a denser to a rarer medium (n1​>n2​).
    2. The angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle (i>C).

    Total internal reflection is a perfect reflection — no absorption losses, unlike metallic mirrors.

    2.3 Applications

    • Totally reflecting prisms (NEB 2024, 3m): right-angled isosceles glass prisms turn light by 90° or 180° through TIR instead of silvered mirrors — no absorption losses. Uses: periscopes (two prisms), binoculars (folding a long path into a short body), SLR camera viewfinders.
    • Optical fibres: light zig-zags along a thin glass core by repeated TIR — the backbone of telecom and endoscopy.
    • Brilliance of diamonds: tiny critical angle (24.4°) traps light until it exits through the top.
    • Mirages: hot air layers near the road refract/reflect light upward, creating "pools" of sky.

    2.4 Worked numericals

    Q (2m, 2024): Find the critical angle for a glass–air boundary if the refractive index of glass is 1.5. sinC=n1​=1.51​=0.6667⇒C=41.8° Any ray inside glass striking the boundary above 41.8° is totally reflected.

    Q (2m, 2021): The critical angle of glass is 42°. Find the refractive index of glass. n=sinC1​=sin42°1​=0.6691​≈1.49

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 70/71: the critical angle is Snell's law with r = 90°.
    • To Topic 72: a slab beyond C becomes a mirror — the basis of light guides.
    • To modern physics: fibre-optic TIR carries the internet across oceans.

Formulas

    • sin C = 1/n = n₂/n₁

Summary

When light in a denser medium meets a rarer boundary, the refracted ray bends away; at the critical angle C it grazes the surface (sinC=1/n), and beyond C the light is totally internally reflected — returned completely into the denser medium. Two conditions: travel denser → rarer and i > C (NEB 2022, 2024, 2m). For glass (n = 1.5) C ≈ 41.8° (NEB 2024, 2m); a measured C = 42° gives n ≈ 1.49 (NEB 2021, 2m). Because TIR is lossless it powers totally reflecting prisms (periscopes, binoculars — NEB 2024, 3m), optical fibres, diamond brilliance and mirages.
