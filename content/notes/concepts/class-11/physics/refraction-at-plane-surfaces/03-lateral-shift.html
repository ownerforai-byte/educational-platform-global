  • A glass slab of thickness 5 cm (n=1.5) is struck by a ray at i=45°. Find the lateral shift. sinr=sin45°/1.5=0.7071/1.5=0.4714, so r=28.1°. d=5⋅cos28.1°sin(45°−28.1°)​=5⋅0.883sin16.9°​=5⋅0.8830.291​=1.65 cm.
  • Show that for small angles of incidence, the lateral shift is approximately d≈t⋅i⋅(1−1/n). For small i and r: sin(i−r)≈i−r and cosr≈1. From Snell's law: r≈i/n. Therefore d≈t(i−i/n)=t⋅i⋅(1−1/n). This linear approximation is useful for paraxial optics.
  • A ray passes through a glass slab and emerges with a lateral shift of 2 cm. If the slab thickness is doubled to 10 cm (same material, same angle of incidence), what is the new lateral shift? Since d∝t, doubling the thickness doubles the shift: dnew​=2×2=4 cm.
  • A transparent slab of thickness 3 cm produces a lateral shift of 1 cm for a ray incident at 60°. Find the refractive index of the slab. d=tsin(i−r)/cosr. 1=3sin(60°−r)/cosr. sin(60°−r)=cosr/3. Using the identity sin(60°−r)=sin60°cosr−cos60°sinr: 0.866cosr−0.5sinr=cosr/3. Solving: r≈30°. Then n=sin60°/sin30°=0.866/0.5=1.73. This is a high-index glass or crystal.
  • Why does a fish in water appear displaced when viewed from above at an angle? Light from the fish refracts at the water-air interface. The emergent ray appears to come from a point closer to the surface (apparent depth effect) AND laterally shifted. The combined effect makes the fish appear at a different position than its actual location.
  • A ray enters a parallel-sided slab of refractive index n at angle i. Show that the lateral shift is maximum when i→90° (grazing incidence). As i→90°, r→arcsin(1/n). The shift d=tsin(i−r)/cosr→tsin(90°−r)/cosr=tcosr/cosr=t. The maximum lateral shift equals the slab thickness.
