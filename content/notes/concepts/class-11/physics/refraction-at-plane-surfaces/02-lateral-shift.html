Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Slab That Shifts but Doesn't Bend

    Light through a thick glass window comes out parallel to where it started — yet shifted sideways. That lateral shift explains why a thick slab never quite lines up with itself and why refracted images through panes look slightly displaced.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Refraction through a parallel-sided slab

    A ray striking a glass slab of thickness t:

    1. Bends towards the normal entering glass (air → glass: r < i).
    2. Travels inside and meets the second face.
    3. Bends away from the normal leaving glass (glass → air: e = i).

    Since the faces are parallel, the internal angle r at the second face equals the internal angle at the first — Snell's law then forces the emergent angle to equal the incident angle: the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray.

    2.2 Lateral shift

    The only change is a sideways displacement measured perpendicular to the original path: x=cosrtsin(i−r)​

    • Grows with thickness t of the slab.
    • Grows with angle of incidence i (zero at normal incidence).
    • Grows with refractive index n of the glass (larger n → smaller r → bigger numerator).
    • For a thin slab (t → 0), x → 0 — the shift is ignored in thin-lens optics.

    2.3 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): A ray strikes a 6 cm thick glass slab (n = 1.5) at 60°. Find the lateral shift.

    • sinr=1.5sin60°​=1.50.866​=0.577 → r = 35.3°
    • x=cos35.3°6×sin(60°−35.3°)​=cos35.3°6×sin24.7°​=0.8166×0.418​≈3.07 cm

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 70: the slab works because Snell's law is reversible — air→glass and glass→air with the same r cancel.
    • To Topic 73: if the internal angle exceeded the critical angle, the second face would totally reflect — the slab becomes a light guide.
    • To prisms (Unit 16): non-parallel faces stop the cancellation, producing net deviation and dispersion.

Confusion

  • Parallel, not coincident: the emergent ray keeps the incident direction but sits displaced sideways by x.
  • i = e for a slab — the two refractions cancel in angle, not in position.

Practice Questions

  • Short (2m): Why is the lateral shift zero at normal incidence? — At i = 0 the ray enters without bending (r = 0); the incident and emergent paths are collinear, so sin(i − r) = 0 and x = 0.

Formulas

    • x = t sin(i − r)/cos r
    • i = e (slab); r same at both faces

Summary

A ray passing through a parallel-sided slab refracts towards the normal at the first face and away at the second; because the faces are parallel the internal angles match, so the emergent angle equals the incident angle (i = e) and the emergent ray is parallel to the incident one. The path is, however, displaced sideways by the lateral shift x=cosrtsin(i−r)​, which grows with the thickness of the slab, the angle of incidence and the refractive index of the glass (NEB 2024, 2m), and vanishes at normal incidence. Prisms break this cancellation by using non-parallel faces — the seed of dispersion.
