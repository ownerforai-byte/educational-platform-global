Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Chaining Media Together

    Light travels from water into glass — how do their refractive indices combine? The relation between refractive indices lets you jump between any pair of media using air (or vacuum) as the common currency, and it explains why pools look shallow and spearfishing takes aim below the target.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Relative and absolute indices

    • Relative refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1:

    n21​=n1​n2​​=v2​v1​​=sinrsini​

    • The index of medium 1 w.r.t. 2 is simply the reciprocal: n12​=n21​1​.
    • Absolute indices (w.r.t. vacuum) chain: if light goes 1 → 2 → 3, the effective relative index n31​=n1​n3​​, and intermediate media don't change the final bending.

    2.2 Real and apparent depth

    When looking vertically into a denser medium of index n: apparent depth=nreal depth​ Objects under water appear raised — the pool looks shallower, the fish looks higher. Conversely, viewed from below, objects above appear farther.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (3m, 2024): A fish is 1.2 m below the water surface (μ = 4/3). At what apparent depth does it appear to an observer above the surface? apparent depth=μreal depth​=4/31.2​=1.2×43​=0.9 m The fish appears raised — spear-fishing must aim below the seen position.

    Q (NEB-style): Light passes from water (n = 4/3) into glass (n = 3/2). Find n_glass,water. ng,w​=nw​ng​​=4/31.5​=44.5​=1.125.

    Q (NEB-style): A tank appears 1.8 m deep. If n_water = 4/3, find its real depth. Real depth = apparent × n = 1.8×34​=2.4 m.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 70: the relative index IS the Snell's-law constant.
    • To Topic 73: the same geometry, at grazing incidence, defines the critical angle.
    • To apparent bending of a stick: refraction at the surface shifts the apparent position of every submerged point.

    4.2 Likely additional forms (NEB-style)

    1. Define relative refractive index and write n₂₁ in terms of n₁ and n₂ (2m).
    2. Real depth from apparent depth (2–3m).
    3. Show n₂₁ = v₁/v₂ (3m).

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Inverting the fraction: apparent depth = real/n, not real × n.
    2. Mixing absolute and relative indices in one equation.
    3. Forgetting that n₁₂ = 1/n₂₁.

Confusion

  • Apparent depth = real/n — the fish looks shallower (closer to the surface), not deeper.
  • Relative vs absolute: n₂₁ = n₂/n₁; absolute indices are measured against vacuum.

Practice

  • Why does a bucket of water look shallower than it is?

Universal Facts

  • A pool 2 m deep looks only ~1.5 m deep through the water — the classic apparent-depth illusion.

Summary

The relative refractive index of medium 2 with respect to medium 1 is n21​=n2​/n1​=v1​/v2​=sini/sinr; reversing the media simply inverts it (n12​=1/n21​), and absolute indices (measured against vacuum) chain through any number of media. A direct consequence is apparent depth: looking vertically into a medium of index n, depths shrink by the factor n — apparent depth = real depth/n. NEB 2024's fish (1.2 m deep in water, μ = 4/3) appears at 0.9 m (3m), which is exactly why spear-fishers aim below the visible fish.
