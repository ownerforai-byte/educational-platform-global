Notes

  • Physical Origin of Stored Energy: Charging a capacitor involves transferring incremental charges dq from one plate to the other against the opposing potential difference established by already accumulated charge. The mechanical work done by an external charging source (battery) is stored as electrostatic potential energy in the electric field between the plates.
  • Mathematical Derivation: Suppose at an intermediate stage of charging, the plate holds charge q and instantaneous potential difference v=Cq​. The infinitesimal work required to transfer an additional charge dq is dW=vdq=Cq​dq. Integrating from initial uncharged state (q=0) to full charge (q=Q): W=∫0Q​Cq​dq=C1​[2q2​]0Q​=2CQ2​.
  • Equivalent Expressions for Energy: Using Q=CV, the stored electrostatic potential energy U can be represented in three equivalent forms: U=2CQ2​=21​CV2=21​QV.
  • Energy Dissipated During Charging by Battery: A battery of EMF V supplies a total charge Q to fully charge the capacitor. The total work done by the battery is Wbattery​=QV=CV2. However, the energy stored in the capacitor is only U=21​CV2. Exactly half the energy supplied by the battery (50%, or 21​CV2) is inevitably dissipated as Joule heat in connecting leads and internal resistance, regardless of circuit resistance.
  • Electrostatic Energy Density: Energy is localized throughout the dielectric medium of volume V=Ad between the plates. Substituting C=dε0​A​ and V=Ed into U=21​CV2: U=21​(dε0​A​)(Ed)2=21​ε0​E2(Ad). Defining energy density u as energy per unit volume: u=AdU​=21​ε0​E2. In a medium of relative permittivity εr​: u=21​εE2=21​εr​ε0​E2.
  • Pulling the plates of an isolated charged capacitor farther apart lowers the capacitance while the charge stays fixed, so the stored energy Q squared over 2C rises. That extra energy is the work you do against the attraction of the plates.

Confusion

  • Thinking all battery energy enters the capacitor: The battery delivers W=QV=CV2, but the capacitor only stores U=21​QV=21​CV2. The remaining 50% is always converted into heat in circuit wires during transient charging.
  • Factor of 1/2 misconception: Why is there a factor of 21​? Because the potential across the plates starts at 0 and linearly rises to V, making the effective average charging potential Vˉ=20+V​=2V​.
  • Believing energy resides in the metal plates: Electrostatic potential energy is stored in the electric field throughout the space (dielectric volume) between plates, not inside the conducting metal itself.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Practice

  • A 12μF capacitor is connected across a 50 V battery. Calculate (a) charge stored, (b) electrostatic energy stored, and (c) energy supplied by the battery. Solution: (a) Q=CV=(12×10−6)(50)=600μC=0.6 mC. (b) U=21​CV2=0.5×(12×10−6)×(2500)=0.015 J=15 mJ. (c) W=QV=(600×10−6)(50)=0.030 J=30 mJ. Exactly half (15 mJ) is lost as heat.
  • Calculate the energy density in an electric field of strength 3×106 V/m (dielectric breakdown field of air). Solution: u=21​ε0​E2=0.5×(8.854×10−12)×(3×106)2=0.5×8.854×10−12×9×1012=39.84 J/m3.
  • Write U = (1/2) C V^2 and say what each symbol means.
  • Mark the known values, then substitute one step at a time.
  • Finish with the number, the unit, and the direction when the quantity has one.

Universal Facts

  • The energy density equation u=21​ε0​E2 is universally valid for any electrostatic field configuration, not just parallel plates, and applies to electromagnetic radiation (photons) traversing deep space.
  • Defibrillators deliver a life-saving pulse of roughly 200 to 360 Joules stored in an internal high-voltage capacitor directly through a patient's heart in less than 10 milliseconds.

Examples

  • Defibrillator: Charges an internal 32 μF capacitor to 5000 V to store U = ½(32×10⁻⁶)(25×10⁶) = 400 J, delivering a synchronized electric shock to reset lethal cardiac fibrillations.
  • Photoflash capacitor: A photoflash circuit charges a small capacitor to 330 V to deliver instantaneous high power: P = U / Δt = 15 J / 1 ms = 15 kW.
  • Pulsed laser fusion: High-energy glass lasers use room-sized capacitor banks storing hundreds of megajoules to trigger fusion target implosions.

Practice Questions

  • Q1. Derive the expression for the electrostatic potential energy stored in a charged capacitor, U = ½ CV².
  • Q2. Deduce an expression for the electrostatic energy density of a parallel plate capacitor and show that it is proportional to the square of the electric field.
  • Q3. A capacitor is charged by a battery of potential V. Prove that the energy stored in the capacitor is only half the work done by the battery.
  • Q4. If the plate separation of an isolated charged capacitor is doubled, what happens to its stored energy, and where does this change in energy come from?

Formulas

  • Stored electrostatic energy: U=21​CV2=2CQ2​=21​QV
  • Work done by charging battery: Wbattery​=QV=CV2
  • Charging efficiency: η=Wbattery​U​=50%
  • Electrostatic energy density: u=21​ε0​E2 (in vacuum) or u=21​εE2 (in medium)

Key Points

  • Electrostatic energy stored equals work performed in transferring charge against accumulating electric potential: U = ½ CV².
  • A battery does work W = QV in delivering charge Q at constant voltage V; exactly 50% is stored, 50% is lost as heat.
  • Energy is physically localized in the electric field between plates with volume density u = ½ ε₀ E².
  • For an isolated charged capacitor, increasing plate separation d increases stored energy U because positive external work is done against electrostatic attraction.

Summary

The electrostatic energy stored in a charged capacitor is the total mechanical work done against mutual charge repulsion during charging, expressed as U = ½ CV² = Q² / (2C) = ½ QV. A battery of EMF V does total work W = QV, meaning 50% is stored while 50% is dissipated as heat. The energy is distributed throughout the space occupied by the electric field with uniform electrostatic energy density u = ½ ε₀ E².

Special Notes

  • When plates of an isolated charged capacitor are separated further from d to 2d, capacitance halves to C/2. Stored energy U = Q² / (2C) doubles. The increase in energy ΔU = U_initial comes directly from the external mechanical work done pulling apart the oppositely charged attractive plates.
  • When plate separation is doubled while connected to a battery, voltage V remains constant. Capacitance halves, so stored energy U = ½ CV² is halved.

Important Statements

  • Pulling the plates of an isolated charged capacitor farther apart lowers the capacitance while the charge stays fixed, so the stored energy Q squared over 2C rises. That extra energy is the work you do against the attraction of the plates.
  • The working relations are U = (1/2) C V^2 and Battery work = C V^2. Name each symbol, convert the data into SI units, and only then substitute.
  • An isolated 2.0 μF capacitor holds 20 μC. The plates are moved so that C becomes 1.0 μF. Find the change in stored energy. Q stays 20 μC because the capacitor is isolated. U1 = Q^2/(2C) = (4.0 × 10^−10) / (4.0 × 10^−6) = 0.10 J.

Important Notes

  • Always identify if Q or V is constant when evaluating work and energy changes: use U = Q²/(2C) when isolated (Q constant); use U = ½ CV² when connected (V constant).
  • Do not skip the unit conversion.
  • Match the formula to the situation drawn, not to a neighbouring topic.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Average voltage trick: W=Q×Vavg​=Q×2V​=21​QV.
  • Battery work is always double stored capacitor energy: Wbattery​=2Ucap​.
  • Energy density is quadratic with field: doubling E quadruples energy density u.

Exam Notes

  • Derivation of U=21​CV2 by integration and subsequent derivation of energy density u=21​ε0​E2 is an essential 3 to 4-mark question in NEB exams.
  • Definition, then formula, then substitution.
  • A vector answer is incomplete without its direction.

Exercises

1.

An isolated 2.0 μF capacitor holds 20 μC. The plates are moved so that C becomes 1.0 μF. Find the change in stored energy.

Steps
  • Q stays 20 μC because the capacitor is isolated.
  • U1 = Q^2/(2C) = (4.0 × 10^−10) / (4.0 × 10^−6) = 0.10 J.
  • After the move, C = 1.0 × 10^−6 F.
  • U2 = 4.0 × 10^−10 / 2.0 × 10^−6 = 0.20 J.
  • The stored energy rises by 0.10 J, supplied by the work of separating the plates.
Answer

The energy rises by 0.10 J.
