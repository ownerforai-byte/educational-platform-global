Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Charge Bucket

    A capacitor stores charge and energy in the electric field between two conductors. Its capacitance — charge per volt — is the "bucket size" of electrostatics, measured in farads, and it powers everything from camera flashes to computer memory.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024)

    2.1 Capacitor and capacitance

    A capacitor is a pair of conductors separated by an insulator (air or dielectric). Charging it places +Q on one plate and −Q on the other, creating a potential difference V.

    Capacitance: C=VQ​SI unit: farad (F) — the charge stored per unit potential difference. Practical units: μF (10⁻⁶ F), pF (10⁻¹² F). Capacitance depends only on geometry and the dielectric — not on Q or V themselves.

    2.2 Charging physics

    • Work done charging: energy stored in the field between the plates (Topic 96).
    • The field between plates is uniform: E = V/d.
    • Charge is equal and opposite on the two plates — the pair acts as one component.

    2.3 Uses of capacitors (NEB 2024, 2m)

    1. Smoothing/filtering ripples in power supplies.
    2. Tuning radio circuits (with an inductor).
    3. Storing firing energy in camera flashes and defibrillators.
    4. Blocking DC while passing AC between amplifier stages; power-factor correction.

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): A 12 μF capacitor holds 0.06 J. Find the voltage. V=2U/C​=2×0.06/12×10−6​=100 V.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 94: the parallel-plate geometry gives C = ε₀A/d explicitly.
    • To Topic 95: capacitors combine in series and parallel (opposite to resistors).
    • To Topic 96: U = ½CV² — the stored energy viewpoint.

Confusion

  • C is a geometry constant: doubling Q doubles V, leaving C = Q/V unchanged.
  • Farad is huge: practical capacitors are μF or pF.

Practice

Examples

  • Defibrillator — a capacitor dumps stored energy through the chest in milliseconds.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (2m): A 4 μF capacitor at 250 V. Find the charge. — Q = CV = 4×10⁻⁶ × 250 = 10⁻³ C = 1 mC.

Formulas

    • C = Q/V; unit farad; 1 μF = 10⁻⁶ F; 1 pF = 10⁻¹² F
    • Q = CV; V = Q/C

Key Points

  • C = Q/V; unit farad (F) = C/V.

Summary

A capacitor is two conductors separated by an insulator; charging places ±Q on its plates with potential difference V. Capacitance C=Q/V (farad) measures charge stored per volt — fixed by geometry and dielectric, not by charge or voltage. Practical values run in μF and pF. Capacitors smooth power supplies, tune radios, store flash/defibrillator energy, and block DC while passing AC (NEB 2024, 2m). The definition and the parallel-plate derivation of C = ε₀A/d anchor NEB's favourite 5m question (2023, 2024).

Special Notes

  • Standard dielectric breakdown of dry air occurs at an electric field of E_max ≈ 3 × 10^6 V/m; beyond this, air ionizes and spark discharge neutralizes the plates.
  • In electrostatic equilibrium, the surface of any conducting capacitor plate is an equipotential surface.

Important Statements

  • "Capacitance is the ratio of the charge on either conductor to the potential difference between them."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQ: four uses of capacitors (2024, 2m). The 2023/2024 5m capacitance-derivation questions are cited under Topic 94.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Q = CV is the capacitor's Ohm's-law analogue."

Mcs

1.

If the charge on a capacitor is doubled, its capacitance:

Options
  • is doubled
  • is halved
  • remains unchanged
  • quadruples
Answer

C

Explanation

Capacitance is determined solely by geometry and dielectric medium. When charge Q is doubled, potential difference V doubles proportionally, leaving C = Q/V invariant.

2.

The dimensional formula of capacitance is:

Options
  • [M⁻¹ L⁻² T⁴ I²]
  • [M¹ L² T⁻⁴ I⁻²]
  • [M⁻¹ L⁻² T² I²]
  • [M¹ L² T⁻² I¹]
Answer

A

Explanation

From C = Q/V = Q² / W = (I·T)² / (M·L²·T⁻²) = M⁻¹ L⁻² T⁴ I².

3.

The capacitance of the Earth modeled as an isolated conducting sphere of radius 6400 km is approximately:

Options
  • 1 F
  • 711 μF
  • 9000 μF
  • 6400 μF
Answer

B

Explanation

C = 4πε₀R = (6.4 × 10⁶) / (9 × 10⁹) ≈ 7.11 × 10⁻⁴ F = 711 μF.

Important Concepts

  • Capacitance = charge stored per volt — the bucket size of an electric field.

Important Tasks

  • Derive C = 4πε₀R for an isolated sphere.
  • State the units, dimensions, and physical factors determining capacitance.
  • Calculate the capacitance of Earth and explain why 1 Farad is practically unachievable for isolated objects.
