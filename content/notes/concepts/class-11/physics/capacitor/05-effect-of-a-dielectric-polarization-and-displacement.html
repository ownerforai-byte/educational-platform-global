  • 1. Topic Introduction — Slipping an Insulator into the Field

    Slide a slab of glass between capacitor plates and the capacitance jumps — with the battery disconnected, the voltage drops on its own. The dielectric's molecules polarise, partially cancelling the internal field, and C rises by the dielectric constant εᵣ.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 What is a dielectric?

    A dielectric is an insulating material (mica, paper, glass, ceramic, pure water) whose molecules polarise in an electric field — no free electrons, but charges shift slightly within each molecule.

    • Polar dielectrics (water, HCl): molecules are permanent dipoles that align with the field.
    • Non-polar dielectrics (N₂, benzene): the field induces stretched dipoles.

    2.2 Polarization and the increased capacitance (NEB 2024, 3m)

    Inserting a dielectric slab between charged plates:

    1. The field polarises the slab — induced (+ and −) surface charges appear on its faces.
    2. These induced charges create an opposing internal field, weakening the net field inside: E = E₀/εᵣ.
    3. With Q fixed (battery disconnected), V = Ed falls by the factor εᵣ.
    4. Since C = Q/V, capacitance rises:

    C=εr​C0​=dεr​ε0​A​ εᵣ (relative permittivity/dielectric constant) ranges from ~1 (air) to ~6 (mica) to ~80 (water).

    Polarization is the aligned-dipole state; displacement refers to the slight shift of bound charges (or, in modern terms, the displacement field D = ε₀E + P).

    2.3 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): A 177 pF air capacitor is filled with mica (εᵣ = 6). Find the new capacitance. C=εr​C0​=6×177=1062 pF ≈ 1.06 nF.

    Q (NEB-style): With the battery disconnected, a 500 V air capacitor's gap is filled with εᵣ = 5 material. Find the new voltage and field ratio. V' = V/εᵣ = 100 V; E' = E₀/5.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 94: C = εᵣε₀A/d is the dielectric-corrected parallel-plate result.
    • To Topic 96: with V fixed by a battery, stored energy rises εᵣ-fold; with Q fixed, it falls.
    • To conductors: a conductor reduces E to zero inside; a dielectric only reduces it — partial cancellation.

1.

Which of the following is an example of a polar dielectric?

Options
  • Oxygen (O₂)
  • Water (H₂O)
  • Hydrogen (H₂)
  • Carbon dioxide (CO₂)
Answer

B

Explanation

Water (H₂O) possesses an asymmetric bent molecular geometry with a permanent electric dipole moment, making it a polar dielectric.

2.

The relation between dielectric constant κ and electric susceptibility χ_e is:

Options
  • κ = χ_e - 1
  • κ = 1 + χ_e
  • κ = 1 / χ_e
  • κ = χ_e / ε₀
Answer

B

Explanation

From D = ε₀E + P and substituting D = κε₀E and P = χ_e ε₀E, dividing by ε₀E gives κ = 1 + χ_e.

3.

The SI unit of the electric polarization vector P is:

Options
  • C·m
  • C/m²
  • N/C
  • V/m
Answer

B

Explanation

Polarization P is dipole moment per volume: (C·m) / m³ = C/m², identical to surface charge density.

Summary

A dielectric is an insulator whose molecules polarise in a field — permanent dipoles align (polar) or induced dipoles stretch (non-polar). Between capacitor plates the polarised slab develops induced surface charges that oppose the plate field, weakening the net field to E₀/εᵣ; with charge fixed the voltage falls and C=Q/V rises to εr​C0​ (NEB 2024, 3m) — giving the corrected parallel-plate formula C = εᵣε₀A/d. εᵣ spans ~1 for air to ~80 for water. Whether the energy rises (battery connected, V fixed) or falls (Q fixed) depends on which quantity the source holds fixed — the subtle half of the story.
