Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Atom's ID Card

    Two integers — Z and A — identify any nucleus completely. Z fixes the element and its chemistry; A fixes the isotope and its nuclear behaviour. And the pair explains why all nuclear matter packs to the same astonishing density.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Atomic number and mass number

    • Atomic number Z: the number of protons — defines the element and its position in the periodic table; equals the nuclear charge +Ze and, for a neutral atom, the electron count.
    • Mass number A: the total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons); A = Z + N.
    • Notation: ZA​X — e.g. 92238​U has Z = 92, A = 238, N = 146.

    2.2 Nuclear radius and density

    Nuclear radius grows with the cube root of mass number: R=R0​A1/3,R0​≈1.2 fm=1.2×10−15 m Nuclear volume V=34​πR3∝A, and mass ∝ A, so: ρ=34​πR03​AAmn​​=34​πR03​mn​​≈2×1017 kg/m3(constant!) — nuclear matter is incompressible: every nucleus packs nucleons at the same density.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): Find the radius of the 92238​U nucleus. R=1.2×10−15×2381/3=1.2×10−15×6.2≈7.4×10−15 m=7.4 fm

    Q (NEB-style): Compare the densities of 11​H and 92238​U nuclei. Both ≈ 2×10¹⁷ kg/m³ — density is independent of A; only the radius grows.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 105: Rutherford's discovery motivates R ∝ A^(1/3) from scattering data.
    • To Topic 107: same Z, different A = isotopes; same A, different Z = isobars.
    • To Topic 109: A and Z enter the mass-defect formula directly.

  • 4. Exam Orientation and Previous Year Questions (NEB)

    4.1 Verified PYQs from the solved-paper bank

    No verified PYQ for this exact topic in the bank. Practise the R = R₀A^(1/3) numericals and the constant-density argument as NEB-style practice (2–3m).

    4.2 Likely question forms

    1. Define Z and A with an example (2m).
    2. Show nuclear density is independent of A (3m).
    3. Radius numerical for a named nucleus (2–3m).

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Using R ∝ A instead of R ∝ A^(1/3).
    2. Confusing Z (protons) with A (nucleons).
    3. Forgetting fm = 10⁻¹⁵ m in radius calculations.

Specific Conditions

  • Constant Nuclear Density: The result ρ ≈ constant applies to all nuclei (light and heavy) — strong evidence that nuclear force saturates and nucleons are tightly packed.
  • Approximate Radius Formula: R = R₀A^(1/3) is empirical, valid for A > ~10. Very light nuclei show deviations.
  • Atomic Mass Unit: 1 u = 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 931.5 MeV/c². Carbon-12 defined as exactly 12 u.

Formulas

    • A = Z + N
    • R = 1.2×10⁻¹⁵ A^(1/3) m
    • ρ = 3m_n/(4πR₀³) ≈ 2×10¹⁷ kg/m³

Key Points

  • A = Z + N; R = 1.2A^(1/3) fm; ρ ≈ 2×10¹⁷ kg/m³ for all nuclei.

Important Statements

  • "The mass number A equals the number of protons plus neutrons; the atomic number Z equals the number of protons."

Important Notes

  • No verified PYQ; NEB-style practice (definitions 2m, density 3m, radius 2–3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "R/R₀ = A^(1/3) — cube-root the mass number."

Exam Notes

  • Show the density derivation in two lines: mass ∝ A, volume ∝ A.

Examples

  • Uranium-238 — Z = 92, N = 146, R ≈ 7.4 fm.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (2m): Find the radius of 612​C. — R = 1.2 × 12^(1/3) = 1.2 × 2.29 ≈ 2.7 fm.

Practice

  • Why don't nuclei collapse further?

Universal Facts

  • A sugar-cube of nuclear matter would mass about a billion tonnes.

Confusion

  • Z defines the element; A defines the isotope — chemistry follows Z, nuclear behaviour follows A.
  • Radius grows as A^(1/3), not A — that cube root is why density stays constant.

Summary

Every nucleus is labelled by atomic number Z (protons — fixes the element and chemistry) and mass number A (protons + neutrons; A = Z + N), written ZA​X (uranium-238: Z = 92, N = 146). Scattering data show the radius grows as R=1.2A1/3 fm, so volume ∝ A while mass ∝ A — hence nuclear density ≈ 2×10¹⁷ kg/m³ is the same for every nucleus: nuclear matter is effectively incompressible. A uranium nucleus (R ≈ 7.4 fm) and a carbon nucleus (R ≈ 2.7 fm) differ in size, not in density.
