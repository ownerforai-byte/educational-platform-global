Notes

  • Mass Number (A): Total number of nucleons (protons + neutrons) in nucleus. A = Z + N. Dimensionless integer. Nuclei with same A but different Z are isobars.
  • Atomic Number (Z): Number of protons in nucleus = number of electrons in neutral atom. Determines element identity and chemical properties. Nuclei with same Z but different N are isotopes.
  • Neutron Number (N): Number of neutrons = A - Z. Nuclei with same N but different Z are isotones.
  • Nuclear Density: Density ρ = mass/volume. Nuclear mass ≈ A × m_p (m_p ≈ 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg). Volume = (4/3)πR³ = (4/3)π(R₀A^(1/3))³ = (4/3)πR₀³A. Density ρ = A·m_p / ((4/3)πR₀³A) = 3m_p/(4πR₀³) — INDEPENDENT of A!
  • Nuclear Density Value: ρ ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³ — about 10¹⁴ times denser than ordinary matter. Comparable to neutron star density.
  • Nuclear Size: Radius R = R₀A^(1/3), R₀ ≈ 1.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ m. Volume ∝ A — nucleus grows linearly with nucleon count, explaining constant density.
  • Isobars, Isotopes, Isotones: Isobars: same A, different Z. Isotopes: same Z, different A (same element). Isotones: same N, different Z.
  • Nuclear Mass vs Atomic Mass: Nuclear mass = atomic mass - mass of Z electrons + binding energy of electrons/c² (electron binding negligible, ~eV scale vs MeV nuclear).

Specific Conditions

  • Constant Nuclear Density: The result ρ ≈ constant applies to all nuclei (light and heavy) — strong evidence that nuclear force saturates and nucleons are tightly packed.
  • Approximate Radius Formula: R = R₀A^(1/3) is empirical, valid for A > ~10. Very light nuclei show deviations.
  • Atomic Mass Unit: 1 u = 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 931.5 MeV/c². Carbon-12 defined as exactly 12 u.

Formulas

  • Mass number: A = Z + N
  • Neutron number: N = A - Z
  • Nuclear radius: R = R₀A^(1/3), R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm
  • Nuclear volume: V = (4/3)πR₀³A
  • Nuclear density: ρ = 3m_p/(4πR₀³) ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³
  • 1 u = 1.6605 × 10⁻²⁷ kg = 931.5 MeV/c²
  • Atomic mass ≈ nuclear mass + Z·m_e

Key Points

  • A = Z + N: mass number = protons + neutrons.
  • Z determines element identity; N determines neutron count.
  • Nuclear density ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³ — same for all nuclei.
  • Nuclear density is INDEPENDENT of mass number A.
  • Isotopes: same Z; Isobars: same A; Isotones: same N.
  • R = R₀A^(1/3): nuclear volume grows linearly with A.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Mass number A = Z + N where Z is atomic number and N is neutron number.
  • Statement 2: Atomic number Z equals proton number and defines the element.
  • Statement 3: Nuclear density ρ ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³ is approximately the same for all nuclei.
  • Statement 4: Nuclear density is independent of mass number A.
  • Statement 5: Nuclear radius R = R₀A^(1/3) with R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm.

Important Notes

  • Nuclear density being constant means nuclear force is short-range and saturates — each nucleon interacts only with nearest neighbors.
  • 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c² is the key conversion for nuclear energy calculations.
  • Isotopes have same chemical properties (same Z/electron configuration) but different nuclear properties.
  • Nuclear density (~10¹⁷ kg/m³) is vastly greater than atomic density (~10³ kg/m³) because atom is mostly empty space.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Nuclear density is the SAME for all nuclei — memorize ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³.
  • R = R₀A^(1/3) — volume ∝ A, radius ∝ A^(1/3).
  • Isotopes: same Z; Isobars: same A; Isotones: same N.
  • 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c² for mass-energy conversions.
  • N = A - Z for neutron count.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks for nuclear density calculation and why it is constant.
  • Distinguish isotopes, isobars, isotones — common conceptual question.
  • Calculate nuclear radius from mass number.
  • Understand atomic mass unit and MeV/c² conversion.

Examples

  • Nuclear density: ρ = 3(1.67×10⁻²⁷)/(4π(1.2×10⁻¹⁵)³) ≈ 2.3 × 10¹⁷ kg/m³.
  • Carbon-12: Z = 6, A = 12, N = 6. Radius R = 1.2 × 12^(1/3) ≈ 2.7 fm.
  • Uranium-238: Z = 92, A = 238, N = 146. Radius R = 1.2 × 238^(1/3) ≈ 7.4 fm.
  • Isotopes of hydrogen: ¹H (Z=1,N=0), ²H/D (Z=1,N=1), ³H/T (Z=1,N=2).

Universal Facts

  • All nuclei have essentially the same density — unlike atoms whose densities vary widely.
  • Neutron stars have densities comparable to nuclear density — they are essentially giant nuclei held by gravity.
  • The constancy of nuclear density implies nuclear force saturates — each nucleon interacts with only a few neighbors.
  • 1 u is defined such that carbon-12 atom has mass exactly 12 u.

Confusion

  • Confusing mass number A with atomic mass: A is integer count; atomic mass is in u (includes mass defect).
  • Thinking isotopes have different chemical properties: Same Z → same electrons → same chemistry.
  • Believing nuclear density varies with element: It is constant (~10¹⁷ kg/m³) for all nuclei.
  • Confusing isobars (same A) with isotopes (same Z).
  • Thinking neutrons contribute to chemical properties: Neutrons affect mass/nuclear stability, not chemistry.
