Notes

  • Mass-Energy Equivalence: Einstein's special relativity (1905) established that mass and energy are equivalent: E = mc². A mass m at rest possesses rest energy E₀ = mc². This is the most famous equation in physics.
  • Rest Energy: Every particle has rest energy E₀ = m₀c² even when not moving. For electron: E₀ = 0.511 MeV; for proton: E₀ = 938 MeV; for 1 kg: E₀ = 9 × 10¹⁶ J (enough to power a city for years).
  • Mass Defect and Binding Energy: In nuclear reactions, the mass of products differs from reactants. The mass difference Δm appears as energy: ΔE = Δmc². This is the basis of nuclear fission and fusion energy.
  • Atomic Mass Unit Energy: 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c². A mass defect of 1 u releases 931.5 MeV — a convenient unit for nuclear calculations.
  • Pair Production and Annihilation: Energy can create matter (pair production: γ → e⁻ + e⁺, requires E ≥ 2m_ec² = 1.022 MeV) and matter can become energy (annihilation: e⁻ + e⁺ → 2γ).
  • Nuclear Binding Energy per Nucleon: Typical values ~8 MeV per nucleon. Iron-56 has maximum binding energy (~8.8 MeV/nucleon) — most stable nucleus. Fusion of light nuclei and fission of heavy nuclei both release energy by moving toward Fe-56.
  • Correspondence Principle: E = mc² reduces to Newtonian mechanics at v ≪ c. The kinetic energy correction: KE = (γ - 1)m₀c² ≈ ½m₀v² for small v.

Specific Conditions

  • Rest Frame: E = mc² gives energy in the object's rest frame. For moving objects: E² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)² (energy-momentum relation).
  • Nuclear vs Chemical: Chemical reactions involve eV-scale energies (negligible mass change). Nuclear reactions involve MeV-scale energies (measurable mass defect).
  • Conservation: Mass-energy is conserved, not mass and energy separately. In E = mc², mass converts to energy and vice versa.

Formulas

  • Mass-energy equivalence: E = mc²
  • Rest energy: E₀ = m₀c²
  • Energy released: ΔE = Δmc²
  • 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c²
  • Relativistic energy: E² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)²
  • Relativistic kinetic energy: KE = (γ - 1)m₀c²
  • Lorentz factor: γ = 1/√(1 - v²/c²)

Key Points

  • E = mc² — mass and energy are equivalent and interchangeable.
  • Rest energy E₀ = m₀c² is enormous even for small mass.
  • Nuclear energy comes from mass defect: ΔE = Δmc².
  • 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c² for nuclear calculations.
  • Pair production and annihilation demonstrate mass-energy conversion.
  • Iron-56 has maximum binding energy per nucleon (~8.8 MeV).

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Mass and energy are equivalent — E = mc² (Einstein's mass-energy relation).
  • Statement 2: Rest energy of mass m is E₀ = mc².
  • Statement 3: Energy released in nuclear reactions equals mass defect times c²: ΔE = Δmc².
  • Statement 4: 1 u of mass corresponds to 931.5 MeV of energy.
  • Statement 5: Mass-energy is conserved, not mass and energy separately.

Important Notes

  • c² = 9 × 10¹⁶ m²/s² — means tiny mass converts to enormous energy.
  • Chemical reactions involve eV energies — mass changes are too small to measure (~10⁻¹⁰ of mass).
  • Nuclear reactions involve MeV energies — mass defect is measurable (~0.1% of mass).
  • The sun converts ~4 million tonnes of mass to energy every second via fusion.

Exam Short Tricks

  • E = mc² — use c = 3 × 10⁸ m/s, c² = 9 × 10¹⁶ J/kg.
  • 1 u = 931.5 MeV — quick conversion for nuclear mass defect.
  • Energy from mass defect: ΔE(MeV) = Δm(u) × 931.5.
  • Rest energy of electron = 0.511 MeV, proton = 938 MeV.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks for E = mc² statement, rest energy calculation, and mass defect energy conversion.
  • Calculate energy released in fission/fusion using ΔE = Δmc².
  • Distinguish rest energy from relativistic total energy.
  • Pair production threshold energy = 2m_ec² = 1.022 MeV.

Examples

  • Energy from 1 kg mass: E = 1 × (3×10⁸)² = 9 × 10¹⁶ J.
  • Mass defect of 0.1 u: ΔE = 0.1 × 931.5 = 93.15 MeV.
  • Sun's mass-energy conversion: ~4 × 10⁹ kg/s converted to energy via fusion.
  • Electron rest energy: E₀ = (9.11×10⁻³¹)(3×10⁸)² = 8.2 × 10⁻¹⁴ J = 0.511 MeV.

Universal Facts

  • The sun converts about 4 million tonnes of matter into energy every second through hydrogen fusion.
  • 1 gram of matter completely converted yields ~9 × 10¹³ J — equivalent to ~21 kilotons of TNT (Hiroshima-scale bomb).
  • E = mc² was published in Einstein's 1905 'annus mirabilis' paper — one of four revolutionary papers that year.
  • Nuclear binding energy per nucleon (~8 MeV) is a million times larger than chemical binding energies (~eV).

Confusion

  • Thinking mass is destroyed in nuclear reactions: Mass is converted to energy, total mass-energy is conserved.
  • Believing E = mc² applies only to nuclear reactions: It applies to ALL energy transformations (chemical, too, but mass change is negligible).
  • Confusing rest mass with relativistic mass: Modern physics uses invariant/rest mass m₀; E = m₀c² is rest energy.
  • Thinking c² is just a conversion factor: It is enormous (9 × 10¹⁶) — small mass = huge energy.
  • Believing mass can be negative: Mass is always positive; energy can be negative (binding energy) but mass-energy total is positive.
