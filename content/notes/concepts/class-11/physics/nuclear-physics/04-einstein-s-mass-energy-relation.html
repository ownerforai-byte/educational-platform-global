Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Mass Is Frozen Energy

    Einstein's 1905 insight collapsed the wall between mass and energy: E = mc². A single kilogram holds 9×10¹⁶ joules — the output of a large power plant for years. Nuclear physics is where that equivalence stops being a curiosity and becomes arithmetic.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The relation

    E=mc2

    • Energy and mass are equivalent and interconvertible; c = 3×10⁸ m/s is the conversion factor.
    • Rest mass energy of a particle: E₀ = m₀c². For 1 u: E = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ × (3×10⁸)² ≈ 931.5 MeV — the key nuclear conversion.
    • Electron (9.1×10⁻³¹ kg): E₀ = 9.1×10⁻³¹ × 9×10¹⁶ ≈ 8.2×10⁻¹⁴ J = 0.51 MeV.

    2.2 Consequences

    • Mass increases with speed: m = m₀/√(1 − v²/c²) — nothing with mass reaches c.
    • Energy has inertia: a hot object weighs slightly more than a cold one; a stretched spring too.
    • Pair creation/annihilation (Topic 110) is E = mc² in action both ways.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): Convert 1 kg of matter entirely to energy. E=mc2=1×(3×108)2=9×1016 J — comparable to the yearly output of several large power stations.

    Q (NEB-style): How much mass does a 100 W lamp convert in one year? m=c2Pt​=9×1016100×3.15×107​≈3.5×10−8 kg — 35 micrograms: everyday energy conversion involves immeasurably tiny mass.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 109: mass defect × c² = binding energy — the relation's nuclear workhorse role.
    • To Topic 110: pair creation and annihilation are mass ⇄ energy at the particle level.
    • To Topic 111: fission and fusion release energy as lost mass.

Specific Conditions

  • Rest Frame: E = mc² gives energy in the object's rest frame. For moving objects: E² = (pc)² + (m₀c²)² (energy-momentum relation).
  • Nuclear vs Chemical: Chemical reactions involve eV-scale energies (negligible mass change). Nuclear reactions involve MeV-scale energies (measurable mass defect).
  • Conservation: Mass-energy is conserved, not mass and energy separately. In E = mc², mass converts to energy and vice versa.

    • 1 u = 931.5 MeV/c² → E = 931.5 MeV

Summary

Einstein's mass-energy relation E = mc² makes mass and energy two faces of one quantity, with c² = 9×10¹⁶ as the exchange rate. One atomic mass unit corresponds to 931.5 MeV; an electron's rest energy is 0.51 MeV. Consequences: mass grows with speed (m = m₀/√(1 − v²/c²)), energy has inertia, and particle physics routinely converts mass ⇄ energy (pair creation, annihilation). Converting 1 kg entirely to energy yields 9×10¹⁶ J; a 100 W lamp converts only ~35 μg per year — everyday physics hides the equivalence, nuclear physics cashes it.

Binding energy

Points
  • BE = Delta m x c^2
  • Binding energy per nucleon measures nuclear stability
  • Peaks at about 8.8 MeV per nucleon near iron-56

Name

Why fission and fusion both release energy

Points
  • Fission splits heavy nuclei toward the iron peak, increasing BE per nucleon
  • Fusion combines light nuclei toward the peak, also increasing BE per nucleon
  • Both move the system toward maximum stability
2.
Name

Packing fraction

Points
  • Packing fraction = (M - A)/A, a measure of departure from A units
  • Negative packing fraction means the nucleus is bound and stable
  • A curve of packing fraction against mass number reveals the same iron peak
