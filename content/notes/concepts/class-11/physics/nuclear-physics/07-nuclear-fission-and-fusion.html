Notes

  • Nuclear Fission: Splitting of a heavy nucleus (A > 230) into two lighter nuclei (fission fragments) plus neutrons and energy. Example: ²³⁵U + n → ²³⁵U* → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n + ~200 MeV. Triggered by thermal (slow) neutron capture.
  • Chain Reaction: Fission releases neutrons (2-3 per event) that trigger more fissions. Critical mass: minimum mass for self-sustaining chain reaction. Subcritical: reaction dies; critical: steady; supercritical: exponential growth (explosion).
  • Nuclear Reactor: Controlled chain reaction. Components: fuel (²³⁵U/²³⁹Pu), moderator (heavy water, graphite) slows neutrons, control rods (boron, cadmium) absorb neutrons, coolant removes heat, concrete shield.
  • Nuclear Fusion: Joining of light nuclei (A < 20) into heavier nucleus, releasing energy. Example: ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV. Requires extreme temperature (~10⁸ K) and pressure to overcome Coulomb repulsion.
  • Thermonuclear Fusion: Fusion occurring at high temperatures (stars, hydrogen bombs). In sun: proton-proton chain converts 4H → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2ν + γ (~26.7 MeV).
  • Energy Comparison: Fission: ~200 MeV per nucleus (~0.1% mass converted). Fusion: ~17.6 MeV per D-T reaction (~0.4% mass converted). Fusion releases more energy per unit mass.
  • Fission Fragments: Typically A ≈ 90-140, radioactive, undergo beta decay. Neutrons emitted: prompt (immediate) and delayed (from fission fragments, important for reactor control).
  • Fusion Fuel: Deuterium (²H from seawater) and tritium (³H from lithium). Deuterium is abundant — fusion fuel is virtually unlimited.

Specific Conditions

  • Criticality: Chain reaction self-sustains when each fission causes exactly one more fission (k = 1). k < 1 subcritical, k > 1 supercritical.
  • Fusion Temperature: ~10⁸ K needed — much hotter than sun's core (~1.5×10⁷ K) because terrestrial fusion uses D-T (easier than p-p).
  • Fissile vs Fertile: ²³⁵U, ²³³U, ²³⁹Pu are fissile (fission with thermal neutrons). ²³⁸U, ²³²Th are fertile (convert to fissile via neutron capture).

Formulas

  • Energy per fission: ~200 MeV per ²³⁵U nucleus
  • Energy per D-T fusion: 17.6 MeV
  • Mass-energy: E = Δmc²
  • Fission: ²³⁵U + n → fragments + 2-3n + ~200 MeV
  • Fusion: ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV
  • Sun: 4¹H → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2ν + ~26.7 MeV

Key Points

  • Fission: heavy nucleus splits, triggered by neutron, ~200 MeV released.
  • Fusion: light nuclei join, needs high T and P, ~17.6 MeV per D-T reaction.
  • Chain reaction: neutrons from one fission trigger more fissions.
  • Critical mass: minimum mass for self-sustaining chain reaction.
  • Reactor: controlled chain reaction with moderator, control rods, coolant.
  • Fusion powers stars; terrestrial fusion needs ~10⁸ K.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Nuclear fission is splitting of heavy nucleus into lighter fragments with energy release.
  • Statement 2: Nuclear fusion is joining of light nuclei into heavier nucleus with energy release.
  • Statement 3: Chain reaction occurs when fission neutrons trigger additional fissions.
  • Statement 4: Critical mass is the minimum mass for self-sustaining chain reaction.
  • Statement 5: Fusion requires temperatures ~10⁸ K to overcome Coulomb repulsion.

Important Notes

  • Fission fragments are radioactive — nuclear waste problem.
  • Control rods absorb neutrons to regulate reactor power.
  • Moderator slows fast neutrons to thermal energies for efficient ²³⁵U fission.
  • Fusion has no long-lived radioactive waste and fuel is abundant, but confinement is technically difficult.
  • Hydrogen bomb uses fission primary to trigger fusion secondary.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Fission ~200 MeV/nucleus; Fusion ~17.6 MeV/D-T reaction.
  • Fusion releases MORE energy per kg than fission.
  • Chain reaction: k = 1 critical, k < 1 dies, k > 1 explodes.
  • Moderator slows neutrons; control rods absorb them.

Examples

  • ²³⁵U + n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n + 200 MeV.
  • ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV.
  • 1 kg ²³⁵U fission: ~8 × 10¹³ J — equivalent to ~20,000 tons TNT.
  • Sun converts 4 × 10⁹ kg/s mass to energy via fusion.

Universal Facts

  • First controlled fission chain reaction: Chicago Pile-1, 1942 (Enrico Fermi).
  • First atomic bomb (fission): Trinity test, 1945. First hydrogen bomb (fusion): 1952.
  • Commercial fusion power remains elusive — ITER project aims to demonstrate net energy gain.
  • Fusion fuel from seawater: deuterium is 1 in 6500 hydrogen atoms in ocean water.

Confusion

  • Confusing fission (split) with fusion (join).
  • Thinking fusion releases more per reaction: Per reaction fission releases more (~200 vs 17.6 MeV), but per kg fusion releases more.
  • Believing all uranium can fuel reactors: Only ²³⁵U (0.7% of natural uranium) is fissile with thermal neutrons.
  • Thinking fusion is easy: Needs extreme confinement (magnetic or inertial) — no commercial plant yet.
  • Confusing critical mass with criticality: Critical mass is amount; criticality (k=1) is the condition.

Summary

Reactors control fission with moderator and control rods. Fusion powers stars and offers clean energy but is technically challenging.
