Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Splitting and Joining for Energy

    Both routes to nuclear energy walk up the binding-energy curve toward iron: fission splits a heavy nucleus, fusion joins light ones. Each gram of fuel converts milligrams of mass into energy millions of times beyond any chemical reaction.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022)

    2.1 Nuclear fission

    A heavy nucleus (A > 200) absorbs a neutron and splits into two medium nuclei plus neutrons and energy: 92235​U+n→56141​Ba+3692​Kr+3n+energy

    • The fragments have higher BE/A than uranium — the mass difference appears as ~200 MeV per fission.
    • Released neutrons can trigger further fissions: a chain reaction (controlled in reactors via moderators and control rods; uncontrolled in weapons).
    • Used in nuclear reactors.

    2.2 Nuclear fusion

    Two light nuclei merge into a heavier one, releasing energy: 12​H+13​H→24​He+n+17.6 MeV

    • Requires enormous temperature (~10⁸ K) to overcome Coulomb repulsion — hence "thermonuclear".
    • Powers the Sun and stars; the goal of fusion reactors (plasma confinement). Per kilogram, fusion releases more energy than fission.

    2.3 Why both release energy (NEB 2022, 3m)

    The BE/A curve peaks at iron-56. Heavy nuclei fission and light nuclei fuse — each move toward the iron peak raises BE/A, and the increase times the nucleon count is the energy released, supplied by the corresponding mass loss (Δm c²).

    • To Topic 109: the BE/A curve is the master explanation of both processes.
    • To Topic 108: the released energy equals the mass loss × c².
    • To Topic 107: fission fragments are radioactive isotopes with their own half-lives.

Specific Conditions

  • Criticality: Chain reaction self-sustains when each fission causes exactly one more fission (k = 1). k < 1 subcritical, k > 1 supercritical.
  • Fusion Temperature: ~10⁸ K needed — much hotter than sun's core (~1.5×10⁷ K) because terrestrial fusion uses D-T (easier than p-p).
  • Fissile vs Fertile: ²³⁵U, ²³³U, ²³⁹Pu are fissile (fission with thermal neutrons). ²³⁸U, ²³²Th are fertile (convert to fissile via neutron capture).

Formulas

    • ²³⁵U + n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n + ~200 MeV
    • ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV
    • E = Δmc²

Key Points

  • ²³⁵U + n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n + ~200 MeV; ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV.

Important Statements

  • "Fission splits heavy nuclei; fusion combines light nuclei; both release energy because the binding energy per nucleon increases toward iron."

Examples

Practice Questions

Practice

  • Why does a fusion reactor need confinement while a fission reactor doesn't?

Universal Facts

  • Fission leaves radioactive waste; fusion's main product is helium.

Confusion

  • Fission = split (heavy), fusion = join (light) — both climb toward the iron peak.
  • Fusion beats fission per kilogram — but needs ~10⁸ K, which is why reactors use fission.

Summary

Fission splits a heavy nucleus: ²³⁵U + n → ¹⁴¹Ba + ⁹²Kr + 3n + ~200 MeV, with the freed neutrons sustaining a chain reaction (controlled in reactors by moderators and control rods). Fusion joins light nuclei: ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n + 17.6 MeV, but demands ~10⁸ K to overcome Coulomb repulsion — the engine of the Sun. Both release energy because the binding energy per nucleon rises toward iron-56 (NEB 2022, 3m); per kilogram fusion releases more energy and leaves only helium, while fission produces radioactive waste.
