Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Finding the Needle in the Atom

    Thomson's "plum pudding" atom filled space with positive charge. Rutherford's gold-foil experiment blew it apart: nearly all the mass and all the positive charge sit in a tiny, dense nucleus — an object 10⁻¹⁵ m across holding 99.9% of the atom's mass.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Rutherford's scattering experiment (1911)

    α-particles (helium nuclei) were fired at a thin gold foil (~10⁻⁷ m, a few hundred atoms thick):

    • Most passed straight through — the atom is mostly empty space.
    • A few deflected by large angles (>90°) — some encountered a small, massive, positively charged core.

    Rutherford concluded: a tiny nucleus (radius ~10⁻¹⁵ m vs atomic radius ~10⁻¹⁰ m) contains all positive charge and nearly all mass; electrons orbit far outside.

    2.2 Nuclear composition

    • Protons: charge +e, mass 1.673×10⁻²⁷ kg; number = atomic number Z.
    • Neutrons: charge 0, mass 1.675×10⁻²⁷ kg; number N = A − Z, where A is the mass number.
    • Nucleons = protons + neutrons (A of them). Nuclear charge = +Ze.

    2.3 Nuclear properties

    • Nuclear density is enormous (~2×10¹⁷ kg/m³) and roughly constant for all nuclei — mass ∝ volume.
    • Nuclear forces bind nucleons against electrical repulsion — short-range, strongly attractive.

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): Estimate the density of nuclear matter, taking a nucleon's mass as 1.67×10⁻²⁷ kg and the nuclear radius as R = 1.2×10⁻¹⁵ A^(1/3) m for A = 1. ρ=34​πR3Amn​​=34​π(1.2×10−15)31.67×10−27​≈2.3×1017 kg/m3 — a teaspoon of nuclear matter would weigh ~a billion tonnes.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 106: density's constancy follows from R ∝ A^(1/3).
    • To Topic 107: A and Z label isotopes — same Z, different A.
    • To Topic 109: nuclear forces explain the binding energy.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying α-particles were "reflected by electrons" — electrons are far too light.

    3. Omitting the observation-to-inference chain in the experiment description.

Specific Conditions

  • Alpha Particle Source: Rutherford used alpha particles (helium nuclei, +2e charge, ~5 MeV energy) from radioactive decay (radium/polonium).
  • Thin Foil: Gold foil used because gold is highly malleable — can be made extremely thin (~400 atoms thick) so alpha particles interact with at most one atom.
  • Point Charge Approximation: Alpha particle treated as point charge; nucleus treated as point charge — valid since distances are much larger than nuclear/alpha sizes.

Formulas

    • R = R₀A^(1/3), R₀ ≈ 1.2 fm
    • ρ_nucleus ≈ 2×10¹⁷ kg/m³
    • N = A − Z

Key Points

  • Rutherford: mostly empty atom; tiny dense positive nucleus; Z protons, N = A − Z neutrons.

Important Statements

  • "Nearly all the mass and all the positive charge of an atom are concentrated in a tiny central nucleus."

Important Notes

  • No verified PYQ; NEB-style practice (Rutherford description 3–4m, density 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "R ∝ A^(1/3); density constant — quote both."

Exam Notes

  • Present the experiment as observation → inference, point by point.

Examples

  • Gold foil — ~1 in 8000 α-particles deflected >90°.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): Show nuclear density is independent of A. — Mass ∝ A, volume ∝ R³ ∝ A (since R ∝ A^(1/3)), so ρ = mass/volume is constant ≈ 2×10¹⁷ kg/m³.

Practice

  • Why did Rutherford use gold foil?

Universal Facts

  • Nuclear density ~2×10¹⁷ kg/m³ is nearly the same for every nucleus from helium to uranium.

Confusion

  • Nucleus vs atom size: 10⁻¹⁵ m vs 10⁻¹⁰ m — the nucleus is 100,000× smaller but holds ~all the mass.
  • Electrons cannot deflect α-particles — they are ~7300× lighter.

Summary

Rutherford fired α-particles at thin gold foil: most passed undeflected (the atom is mostly empty space), but ~1 in 8000 suffered large-angle deflections — impossible unless a tiny, massive, positive nucleus concentrated the charge and mass. The nucleus (R ~ 10⁻¹⁵ m, vs atom 10⁻¹⁰ m) holds Z protons and N = A − Z neutrons, bound by the short-range strong nuclear force. Its radius scales as R = 1.2A^(1/3) fm, so mass ∝ volume and nuclear density ≈ 2×10¹⁷ kg/m³ — constant for all nuclei, denser than any everyday matter by a factor of 10¹⁴.

Size and density

Points

  • Nuclear density is roughly constant across all nuclei, about 2.3 x 10^17 kg/m^3
  • The nucleus is about 10^5 times smaller than the atom but holds almost all its mass

Level 3 — Upgraded

Subtopics
1.
Name

Nuclear forces

Points
  • Strong nuclear force binds nucleons, is short-ranged and charge-independent
  • It is strongly attractive at about 1-2 fm and repulsive at very short range
  • Competes with electrostatic repulsion between protons, which limits nucleus size
2.
Name

Why heavy nuclei are unstable

Points
  • Coulomb repulsion grows as Z^2 while the strong force grows only as A
  • Beyond bismuth (Z = 83) no stable nucleus exists
  • Explains alpha decay and spontaneous fission in heavy elements
