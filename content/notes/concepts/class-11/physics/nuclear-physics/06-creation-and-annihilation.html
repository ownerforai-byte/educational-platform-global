Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Matter Made and Unmade from Light

    A γ-ray photon with 1.02 MeV can turn into an electron and a positron; an electron meeting a positron vanishes into two photons. Pair creation and annihilation are E = mc² running in both directions — mass manufactured from energy and energy reclaimed from mass.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Pair creation

    A photon with energy ≥ 2m_ec² = 1.02 MeV, passing close to a nucleus (needed for momentum conservation), can materialise into an electron–positron pair: γ→e−+e+

    • Threshold: 1.02 MeV — anything above goes into kinetic energy.
    • Charge is conserved: 0 → (−e) + (+e).
    • Occurs only near a nucleus — a single photon in empty space cannot conserve momentum.

    2.2 Annihilation

    An electron and positron at rest meet and annihilate, their entire rest mass becoming electromagnetic energy — almost always two photons emitted in opposite directions (momentum conservation): e−+e+→2γ,each photon 0.51 MeV Two photons (not one) are required to conserve both energy and momentum.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): Find the minimum photon energy for pair production. Emin​=2me​c2=2×0.511 MeV=1.02 MeV

    Q (NEB-style): A 2.04 MeV photon creates a pair. Find the total kinetic energy of the pair. KEtotal​=2.04−1.02=1.02 MeV — shared between the electron and positron (plus a tiny recoil of the nucleus).

    2.4 Why it matters

    PET scanners image annihilation photons; pair production dominates γ-ray absorption at high energies; positrons probe materials. Both processes confirm mass–energy equivalence at the particle scale.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 108: both processes are E = mc² — creation converts energy to mass, annihilation the reverse.
    • To Topic 109: rest energies (0.51 MeV per particle) set the thresholds.
    • To photons (Unit 6): the photon's energy and momentum bookkeeping governs both.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Writing 0.51 MeV as the pair-production threshold (it is 2 × 0.51 = 1.02 MeV).

    3. Ignoring charge conservation in the equations.

Specific Conditions

  • Threshold Energy: Pair production requires photon energy ≥ 1.022 MeV (2 × electron rest energy). Below threshold, no pair creation.
  • Third Body Required: Pair production cannot occur in vacuum — needs nucleus/electron to conserve momentum.
  • Photon Disappears: In pair production, the photon is completely absorbed — it does not survive.

Formulas

    • γ → e⁻ + e⁺: E_min = 2m_ec² = 1.02 MeV
    • e⁻ + e⁺ → 2γ: each 0.51 MeV
    • Excess photon energy → kinetic energy

Key Points

  • γ → e⁻ + e⁺ (needs ≥ 1.02 MeV, near a nucleus); e⁻ + e⁺ → 2γ (0.51 MeV each).

Important Statements

  • "A photon of energy at least 1.02 MeV can create an electron–positron pair in the field of a nucleus."

Examples

Universal Facts

  • Pair creation dominates γ-ray absorption above a few MeV; the photoelectric effect rules at low energies.

Confusion

  • Pair threshold = 1.02 MeV (two rest masses); each annihilation photon = 0.51 MeV.
  • Two photons, not one, from annihilation — momentum conservation demands it.

Summary

Pair creation: a photon of at least 1.02 MeV (2 × 0.51 MeV, the two rest masses) converts to an electron–positron pair — but only near a nucleus, whose recoil conserves momentum. Annihilation: an electron and positron at rest destroy each other into two 0.51 MeV photons flying apart — two photons are mandatory for momentum conservation. Both are E = mc² live: energy becomes mass and mass becomes energy, with charge conserved throughout (0 → −e + e; −e + e → 0). PET imaging, high-energy γ absorption and positron probes all exploit these processes.
