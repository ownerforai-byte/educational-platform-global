Notes

  • Fission splits a heavy nucleus into medium-mass fragments, often after neutron absorption. Fusion combines light nuclei. Either can release energy when products have greater total binding energy than reactants.
  • Reaction energy follows mass-energy conservation: Q=(mi−mf)c². Positive Q means the final rest mass is lower and the difference appears as kinetic energy, radiation or excitation.
  • Uranium-235 fission can release several neutrons. A sustained chain reaction requires, on average, enough neutrons to induce further fissions; a reactor controls this multiplication with neutron absorbers and moderators.
  • Deuterium–tritium fusion can form helium-4 and a neutron, releasing about 17.6 MeV. The positively charged nuclei must approach closely despite Coulomb repulsion, requiring high temperature and adequate confinement.
  • Fission is used in nuclear power stations; fusion powers stars. Practical fusion requires maintaining a hot dense plasma long enough for reaction energy to exceed losses.
  • Fission and fusion release energy for the same structural reason: the products sit higher on the curve of binding energy per nucleon. Fission starts from a very heavy nucleus. Fusion starts from very light ones. A typical fission releases about 200 MeV, carried by the fragments, neutrons and radiation.

Confusion

  • Mass is converted to other forms of energy; total energy is conserved.
  • High temperature alone does not guarantee useful fusion; confinement matters.

Specific Conditions

  • Fission vs Fusion Energy Release: Both release energy by moving toward higher BE/A (iron peak). Fission splits heavy nuclei (A>56) into medium fragments. Fusion combines light nuclei (A<56) into heavier ones. Both increase the average BE/A of the system.
  • Chain Reaction Requirement: Fission of 235U by thermal neutrons produces ~2.4 fast neutrons/fission. A sustained chain reaction requires at least 1 neutron to cause another fission (k≥1). Critical mass is the minimum mass where neutron losses are balanced by productions. Moderators (graphite, water) slow neutrons to thermal energies to increase capture probability.
  • Fusion Conditions: Requires extreme temperature (T∼107−108 K) for nuclei to overcome Coulomb barrier (kT∼1 keV vs barrier ∼1 MeV). Quantum tunneling allows fusion at lower energies. Plasma confinement (magnetic/inertial) is needed to maintain density and temperature long enough for significant fusion (Lawson criterion: nτ>1020 m⁻³·s).

Exercises

1.

One fission releases 200 MeV. Express that energy in joules. Use 1 eV = 1.6 × 10^−19 J.

Steps
  • 200 MeV = 2.00 × 10^8 eV.
  • Energy = 2.00 × 10^8 × 1.6 × 10^−19.
  • That product is 3.2 × 10^−11 J.
  • This is one nucleus, not one gram.
  • Fusion of light nuclei releases energy per nucleon for a different mass number, but the joule conversion is the same.
Answer

3.2 × 10^−11 J.
