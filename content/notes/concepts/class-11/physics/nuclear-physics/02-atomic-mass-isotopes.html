Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Same Element, Different Weight

    Chlorine's atomic "mass" is 35.5 — yet no chlorine atom weighs that. Nature makes the same element in several masses (isotopes), and the periodic table lists their weighted average. Half-life governs how fast radioactive isotopes vanish.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024, 2020)

    2.1 Atomic mass unit and atomic mass

    • Atomic mass unit: 1 u = 1/12 the mass of a neutral 612​C atom = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg.
    • Proton ≈ 1.0073 u, neutron ≈ 1.0087 u, electron ≈ 0.00055 u.
    • An element's atomic mass on the periodic table is the weighted average of its naturally occurring isotopes.

    2.2 Isotopes, isobars, isotones

    • Isotopes: same Z, different A — 11​H, 12​H, 13​H (protium, deuterium, tritium); 1735​Cl and 1737​Cl.
    • Isobars: same A, different Z — 1840​Ar and 2040​Ca.
    • Isotones: same N — same neutron number, different Z and A.

    2.3 Weighted average — why Cl is 35.5

    Chlorine is 75% 1735​Cl and 25% 1737​Cl: mˉ=0.75(35)+0.25(37)=35.5 u

    2.4 Radioactive isotopes and half-life (NEB 2023, 2024, 2020)

    Many isotopes are unstable and decay radioactively. The decay constant λ is the probability per unit time that a nucleus decays (NEB 2024, 2m): N=N0​e−λt,T1/2​=λln2​=λ0.693​,τ=λ1​ After n half-lives the surviving fraction is (1/2)n (NEB 2020, 1m MCQ).

    Q (3m, 2023): A radioactive sample has a half-life of 8 days. What fraction remains after 24 days, and what is the activity if the initial activity is 800 Bq?

    • n = 24/8 = 3 half-lives → fraction = (1/2)3 = 1/8
    • Activity also falls by the same factor: A = 800 × 1/8 = 100 Bq

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 106: Z and A classify the isotope family.
    • To Topic 108: mass and energy interchange — mass "deficits" power stars.
    • To Topic 111: fission products are neutron-rich isotopes.

Specific Conditions

  • Mass Defect Definition: Δm=Zmp​+Nmn​−Mnucleus​ where mp​≈1.007276 u, mn​≈1.008665 u. The defect is always positive — the bound nucleus is lighter than its free constituents.
  • Binding Energy Conversion: BE=Δm⋅c2=Δm×931.494 MeV. The binding energy is the energy needed to separate the nucleus into free nucleons. It is positive for bound nuclei.
  • Isotope Chemistry vs Nuclear Properties: Isotopes (same Z, different A) have identical chemical properties (electron configuration) but different nuclear masses, stabilities, and half-lives. Chemical separation is impossible; nuclear methods (centrifugation, diffusion, laser) are required.

Key Points

  • 1 u = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg; T₁/₂ = 0.693/λ; fraction left after n half-lives = (1/2)ⁿ.

Summary

The atomic mass unit is 1/12 of carbon-12: 1 u = 1.66×10⁻²⁷ kg. Isotopes share Z but differ in A (¹H, ²H, ³H; ³⁵Cl/³⁷Cl), isobars share A (⁴⁰Ar/⁴⁰Ca), isotones share N; chlorine's 35.5 u is the weighted average (75% × 35 + 25% × 37). Radioactive isotopes decay with decay constant λ — the per-second decay probability (NEB 2024, 2m): N = N₀e^(−λt), half-life T₁/₂ = 0.693/λ, mean life τ = 1/λ. After n half-lives the surviving fraction is (1/2)ⁿ (NEB 2020, 1m); NEB 2023's 8-day sample after 24 days keeps 1/8 of its atoms and 1/8 of its 800 Bq activity — 100 Bq.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "n = t/T₁/₂; fraction = (1/2)ⁿ; multiply the initial activity by the same fraction."
