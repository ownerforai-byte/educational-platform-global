Notes

  • Mass Defect: The mass defect Δm is the difference between the sum of individual nucleon masses and the actual nuclear mass: Δm = Z·m_p + N·m_n - M_nucleus. This "missing mass" is converted to binding energy when the nucleus forms.
  • Binding Energy: The energy required to completely separate a nucleus into its constituent nucleons: BE = Δm·c². It is also the energy released when the nucleus forms from separate nucleons. MEV scale: BE ≈ Δm(u) × 931.5 MeV.
  • Binding Energy per Nucleon: BE/A — average energy holding each nucleon in the nucleus. Values range from ~1 MeV (light nuclei) to ~8.8 MeV (Fe-56) to ~7.6 MeV (uranium). Higher BE/A = more stable nucleus.
  • Packing Fraction: f = (M - A)/A × 10⁴ where M is atomic mass in u and A is mass number. Can be positive or negative. Related to stability — lower (more negative) packing fraction generally means more stable.
  • BE/A Curve: Rises steeply for light nuclei, peaks at Fe-56 (~8.8 MeV), then slowly decreases for heavy nuclei. Explains why fusion (light → medium) and fission (heavy → medium) both release energy.
  • Nuclear Stability: Nuclei with BE/A near maximum (Fe-56 region) are most stable. Very light and very heavy nuclei are less stable and can undergo fusion/fission respectively.
  • Semi-Empirical Mass Formula: BE = a_v·A - a_s·A^(2/3) - a_c·Z²/A^(1/3) - a_a·(A-2Z)²/A + pairing term. Volume, surface, Coulomb, asymmetry, pairing contributions.

Specific Conditions

  • Atomic vs Nuclear Mass: Atomic mass includes electrons; for BE calculation use nuclear mass. Electron masses cancel if using atomic masses consistently (Z electrons on both sides).
  • BE/A for Stability: Compare BE/A, not total BE — total BE always increases with A but stability depends on per-nucleon value.
  • Packing Fraction Sign: Negative packing fraction means mass < A (mass defect) — bound nucleus. Positive means unstable relative to nucleons.

Formulas

  • Mass defect: Δm = Z·m_p + N·m_n - M
  • Binding energy: BE = Δm·c² = Δm(u) × 931.5 MeV
  • BE per nucleon: BE/A
  • Packing fraction: f = (M - A)/A × 10⁴
  • Nuclear mass: M = Z·m_p + N·m_n - Δm
  • Semi-empirical: BE = a_v·A - a_s·A^(2/3) - a_c·Z²/A^(1/3) - a_a·(A-2Z)²/A + δ

Key Points

  • Mass defect Δm = sum of nucleon masses - actual nuclear mass.
  • Binding energy BE = Δm·c² — energy to separate nucleus into nucleons.
  • BE/A peaks at Fe-56 (~8.8 MeV) — most stable nucleus.
  • Packing fraction f = (M - A)/A × 10⁴.
  • Fusion (light → Fe) and fission (heavy → Fe) both release energy.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Mass defect Δm = Z·m_p + N·m_n - M_nucleus.
  • Statement 2: Binding energy BE = Δm·c² holds nucleus together.
  • Statement 3: Binding energy per nucleon BE/A indicates nuclear stability.
  • Statement 4: BE/A peaks at iron-56 (~8.8 MeV/nucleon).
  • Statement 5: Packing fraction f = (M - A)/A × 10⁴.

Important Notes

  • BE/A, not total BE, determines stability — compare nuclei of different sizes using BE/A.
  • Using atomic masses: electron masses cancel in Δm calculation if consistent.
  • Fe-56 is the most stable nucleus — end point of both fusion and fission energy release.
  • Heavy nuclei (A > 200) can release energy by fission; light nuclei (A < 20) by fusion.

Exam Short Tricks

  • BE(MeV) = Δm(u) × 931.5 — quick conversion.
  • BE/A peak at Fe-56 = ~8.8 MeV — memorize.
  • Fusion releases energy for light nuclei; fission for heavy nuclei.
  • Packing fraction negative → bound/stable nucleus.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks for mass defect and binding energy calculations.
  • Draw and interpret BE/A curve — standard exam question.

Examples

  • Helium-4: 2p + 2n mass = 2(1.007825) + 2(1.008665) = 4.03298 u; actual M = 4.00260 u; Δm = 0.03038 u; BE = 0.03038 × 931.5 = 28.3 MeV; BE/A = 7.07 MeV.
  • Uranium-238: Δm ≈ 1.9 u; BE ≈ 1.9 × 931.5 ≈ 1770 MeV; BE/A ≈ 7.4 MeV.
  • Iron-56: BE/A ≈ 8.8 MeV — maximum on curve.
  • Packing fraction for ¹⁶O: M = 15.9949 u, A = 16: f = (15.9949 - 16)/16 × 10⁴ = -32.

Universal Facts

  • Binding energy per nucleon (~8 MeV) is a million times larger than chemical bond energy (~eV) — nuclear energy density is enormous.
  • Iron-56 (BE/A = 8.8 MeV) is the most stable nucleus — stellar nucleosynthesis ends at iron.
  • The sun's energy comes from hydrogen fusion (H → He) because BE/A increases from H to He.
  • Nuclear fission of uranium releases ~200 MeV per nucleus — mostly as kinetic energy of fragments.

Confusion

  • Confusing total BE with BE/A: Total BE increases with A but stability depends on BE/A.
  • Thinking mass defect is 'lost' mass: It's converted to binding energy — mass-energy conserved.
  • Using nuclear masses vs atomic masses inconsistently: Electron masses must cancel.
  • Believing all heavy nuclei fission: Only certain isotopes (U-235, Pu-239) are fissile with thermal neutrons.
  • Confusing packing fraction sign convention.
