Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Missing Mass That Holds Nuclei Together

    Weigh a helium nucleus: its parts weigh more than the whole. The missing 0.76% — the mass defect — left as binding energy, is the glue of every nucleus and the ledger behind fission and fusion.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023)

    2.1 Mass defect

    Mass defect is the difference between the sum of the masses of the individual nucleons and the actual mass of the nucleus (NEB 2023, 3m): Δm=[Zmp​+(A−Z)mn​]−Mnucleus​ — always positive for bound nuclei: the assembled nucleus weighs less than its parts.

    2.2 Binding energy

    The binding energy is the energy equivalent of the mass defect — the work needed to pull the nucleus completely apart: BE=Δm×c2(in u: BE MeV=Δm×931.5) Higher binding energy per nucleon → more stable nucleus.

    2.3 Packing fraction and BE per nucleon

    • Packing fraction: f=AΔm​=AMnucleus​−A​ (in u per nucleon) — negative f means the nucleus is bound (mass less than A nucleons); the more negative, the tighter the packing.
    • Binding energy per nucleon: BE/A=AΔmc2​ — the stability measure. It peaks at ~8.8 MeV near iron-56 (A ≈ 56), then declines slowly toward uranium.

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): For 24​He: m_p = 1.0073 u, m_n = 1.0087 u, M = 4.0015 u. Find Δm and BE.

    • Δm = [2(1.0073) + 2(1.0087)] − 4.0015 = 4.0320 − 4.0015 = 0.0305 u
    • BE = 0.0305 × 931.5 ≈ 28.4 MeV → BE/A ≈ 7.1 MeV/nucleon

    2.5 The binding-energy curve

    Rising steeply from light nuclei, peaking at iron-56, declining for heavy nuclei. Energy is released by any move up the curve: fusion of light nuclei, fission of heavy ones — both walk toward the iron peak.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 108: BE = Δmc² is Einstein's relation applied to nuclei.
    • To Topic 110: rest energies of particles set the energy scales of creation/annihilation.
    • To Topic 111: the curve's shape explains why fission and fusion both release energy.

Specific Conditions

  • Atomic vs Nuclear Mass: Atomic mass includes electrons; for BE calculation use nuclear mass. Electron masses cancel if using atomic masses consistently (Z electrons on both sides).
  • BE/A for Stability: Compare BE/A, not total BE — total BE always increases with A but stability depends on per-nucleon value.
  • Packing Fraction Sign: Negative packing fraction means mass < A (mass defect) — bound nucleus. Positive means unstable relative to nucleons.

Formulas

    • Δm = Zm_p + (A−Z)m_n − M
    • BE = Δmc² = Δm × 931.5 MeV (u)
    • Packing fraction f = (M − A)/A
    • BE/A peaks ≈ 8.8 MeV at ⁵⁶Fe

Summary

The mass defect Δm = [Zm_p + (A−Z)m_n] − M is the shortfall between the free nucleons' total mass and the actual nuclear mass (NEB 2023, 3m) — bound nuclei weigh less than their parts. Its energy equivalent, BE = Δmc² (Δm × 931.5 MeV per u), is the energy to dismantle the nucleus; the packing fraction Δm/A and BE per nucleon measure stability. Helium-4: Δm = 0.0305 u → BE ≈ 28.4 MeV (7.1 MeV/nucleon). The BE/A curve rises to ~8.8 MeV at iron-56 then declines — so fusion of light nuclei and fission of heavy nuclei both release energy by moving toward the iron peak.
