Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — One Equation for Every Mirror Image

    Real or virtual, magnified or diminished — every image a spherical mirror forms obeys a single compact relation: v1​+u1​=f1​. NEB loves it: the 2024 paper carried both the 5-mark derivation and the 4-mark f=R/2 proof.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021, 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Sign convention (NEB 2021, 3m)

    New Cartesian convention:

    1. All distances are measured from the pole P of the mirror.
    2. Distances measured in the direction of incident light are positive; against it (in front of the mirror) are negative.
    3. Heights above the principal axis are positive, below negative.
    4. Focal length: concave → negative (focus in front), convex → positive.

    2.2 Relation f = R/2 (NEB 2024, 4m)

    A ray parallel to the principal axis strikes the mirror at height h. The normal at the point of incidence is the radius through that point, making angle θ with the axis. After reflection the ray turns through 2θ and crosses the axis at F. For paraxial rays (tanθ≈θ): tanθ=Rh​≈fh​-geometry⇒f=2R​ Valid only for paraxial rays — wide beams suffer spherical aberration.

    2.3 Mirror formula derivation (NEB 2024, 5m)

    For a concave mirror with object AB and image A′B′, using similar triangles in the ray diagram and the Cartesian convention (distances in front negative): A′B′AB​=FB′FB​⇒v1​+u1​=f1​=R2​ The same algebra covers convex mirrors once signs are inserted.

    2.4 Magnification

    Linear magnification m=hh′​=−uv​ (Cartesian). ∣m∣>1 → enlarged; negative m → inverted (real); positive m → erect (virtual) (NEB 2024, 2m).

    2.5 Worked numericals

    Q (4m, 2022): An object is placed 30 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 10 cm. Find the position and magnification of the image and state its nature.

    • u=−30 cm, f=−10 cm: v1​=f1​−u1​=−101​+301​=−151​ → v=−15 cm
    • m=−uv​=−0.5 → image 15 cm in front, real, inverted, half-size.

    Q (3m, 2024): An object is placed 10 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the image position and magnification; state the nature.

    • f=−15 cm, u=−10 cm: v1​=−151​+101​=301​ → v=+30 cm (behind the mirror → virtual)
    • m=−uv​=+3 → erect, 3× magnified.

    Q (3m, 2022): A concave mirror forms a real image three times the size of an object placed 30 cm in front of it. Find the focal length.

    • m=−3=−uv​ with u=−30 → v=−90 cm
    • f1​=v1​+u1​=−901​−301​=−904​ → f=−22.5 cm.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 68: the formula reproduces every qualitative case of image formation.
    • To f = R/2: the mirror formula's "1/f" is 2/R — aperture geometry in disguise.
    • To refraction formula (Unit 15): v1​−u1​=f1​ for lenses is the refracting twin of the mirror formula.

    4.2 Common examination mistakes

    1. Sign errors: forgetting u is negative for real objects in the Cartesian convention.
    2. Reporting a negative v as "behind the mirror" — negative v means in front (real) for a concave mirror.
    3. Using tan θ without the paraxial approximation in the f = R/2 derivation.

Confusion

  • Sign errors kill mirror-formula marks: in the Cartesian convention u is negative for a real object and f is negative for concave mirrors.
  • Negative magnification = inverted (real) image, not a "negative-size" image.

Examples

  • NEB 2024 — u = −10 cm, f = −15 cm → v = +30 cm, m = +3 (virtual, erect, magnified).

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A concave mirror of f = 20 cm forms a real image twice the object size. Find u. — m = −2 = −v/u → v = 2u. With u negative and v negative: 1/(2u) + 1/u = 1/f → 3/(2u) = 1/(−20) → u = −30 cm; object 30 cm in front, image 60 cm in front.

Formulas

    • 1/v + 1/u = 1/f = 2/R
    • m = h'/h = −v/u
    • f = R/2 (paraxial rays)

Key Points

  • 1/v + 1/u = 1/f; m = h'/h = −v/u; f = R/2.

Summary

The new Cartesian sign convention measures all distances from the pole, against incident light as negative, and gives f < 0 for concave, f > 0 for convex mirrors (NEB 2021, 3m). For paraxial rays a mirror of radius R focuses at f=R/2 (NEB 2024, 4m), and similar-triangle geometry yields the mirror formula v1​+u1​=f1​ (NEB 2024, 5m). Magnification is m=h′/h=−v/u. NEB 2022's 30 cm/10 cm case gives v = −15 cm, m = −0.5 (real, inverted, half-size, 4m); NEB 2024's 10 cm/15 cm case gives v = +30 cm, m = +3 (virtual, erect, magnified, 3m); the m = −3, u = 30 cm problem yields f = −22.5 cm (2022, 3m).

Special Notes

  • Do not substitute the thin-lens equation for the mirror equation.
  • f=R/2 assumes a spherical reflecting surface and small angles.

Important Statements

  • "For paraxial rays the quantities 1/v, 1/u and 1/f satisfy 1/v + 1/u = 1/f."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: sign convention 2021 3m; magnification + focal length 2022 3m; numerical 2022 4m; mirror formula derivation 2024 5m; f = R/2 2024 4m; numerical 2024 3m; magnification definition 2024 2m.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Solve every numerical with 1/v = 1/f − 1/u, then m = −v/u."

Exam Notes

  • State the sign convention explicitly before any derivation — the 2024 5m asks for it.

Important Concepts

  • Sign convention first, algebra second — the formula is universal, the signs carry the physics.

Exercises

1.

A concave mirror has f = −10 cm. An object is at u = −6.0 cm. Find v and say what kind of image it is.

Steps
  • 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = −1/10 − 1/(−6).
  • 1/v = −0.10 + 0.167 = +0.067.
  • v = +15 cm.
  • Positive v means a virtual image behind the mirror.
  • m = −v/u = −(15)/(−6) = +2.5, so the image is upright and enlarged.
Answer

v = +15 cm, a virtual upright image, 2.5 times as tall.
