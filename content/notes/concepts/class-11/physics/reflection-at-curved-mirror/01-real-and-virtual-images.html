Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Where Images Come From

    Bring an object up to a spoon and its face appears; move it out and the image flips. Spherical mirrors — concave and convex — build images by redirecting light rays, and where those rays actually (or apparently) meet decides whether the image is real or virtual.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2020, 2023, 2024)

    2.1 Real vs virtual images

    • Real image: reflected rays actually intersect; can be caught on a screen; always inverted (for a mirror acting alone). Formed in front of a concave mirror.
    • Virtual image: reflected rays only appear to diverge from a point behind the mirror; cannot be caught on a screen; always erect. Formed behind a concave mirror (object inside F) or behind a convex mirror (always).

    2.2 Concave vs convex mirror (NEB 2020)

    PointConcave mirrorConvex mirror
    Reflecting surfaceInner (cave) sideOuter side
    NatureConvergingDiverging
    Focal length (Cartesian)NegativePositive
    ImagesReal or virtual; inverted or erect; any sizeAlways virtual, erect, diminished
    UsesHeadlights, torches, projectors, dentist mirrors, telescopesRear-view and blind-corner mirrors

    2.3 Image formation by a concave mirror (NEB 2023)

    Object position → image nature:

    • Beyond C: between F and C — real, inverted, diminished.
    • At C: at C — real, inverted, same size (m=−1).
    • Between C and F: beyond C — real, inverted, magnified (projector principle).
    • At F (u=f): rays reflect parallel to the axis — image at infinity (NEB 2023, 3m). Used in searchlights and headlights.
    • Between P and F: behind the mirror — virtual, erect, magnified (shaving mirror).

    2.4 Applications (NEB 2024)

    • Concave mirror in a car headlight (2m): the bulb sits at the focus; rays from the focus reflect parallel to the principal axis, producing a strong directed beam instead of scattering.
    • Convex rear-view mirror (2m): always erect, diminished image with a wide field of view.
    • Concave shaving/make-up mirror: object inside F → erect, magnified virtual image.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To ray optics of plane mirrors (Class X base): a plane mirror is the limiting case m = +1, always virtual.
    • To Topic 69: the mirror formula turns these qualitative cases into numbers.
    • To reversibility of light: the "focus → parallel beam" property is just the reverse of "parallel rays → focus".

    4.2 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying virtual images are always formed by convex mirrors only — a concave mirror makes them too (object inside F).
    2. Missing "real images can be caught on a screen" in definitions.
    3. Forgetting that the headlight answer needs the focus → parallel beam idea.

Confusion

  • Virtual ≠ fake: a virtual image has a definite position and can be photographed — the rays just don't really meet there.
  • Concave mirrors can give both kinds of image; convex mirrors give only virtual, erect, diminished ones.

Summary

Spherical mirrors form images by reflection. A real image is where reflected rays truly intersect — it can be thrown on a screen and is inverted; a virtual image is where rays only appear to meet — erect, behind the mirror, never on a screen. A concave (converging, f negative) mirror gives the whole range: diminished inside C, equal at C, magnified between C and F, image at infinity when the object is at F (rays reflect parallel — searchlights), and a virtual erect magnified image inside F (shaving mirror). A convex (diverging, f positive) mirror always gives a virtual, erect, diminished image with a wide field — ideal for rear-view mirrors (NEB 2020, 2023, 2024).

Special Notes

  • Virtual describes ray geometry, not image brightness.
  • State the equation’s sign convention when giving image distances.

Important Statements

  • "A real image is formed by the actual intersection of reflected rays; a virtual image by their apparent intersection."
