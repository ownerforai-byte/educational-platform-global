Notes

  • Dimensional formula expresses a physical quantity in terms of the seven base quantities using symbols M (mass), L (length), T (time), I (current), Θ (temperature), J (luminous intensity) and N (amount of substance). Examples: velocity [M0L1T−1], force [M1L1T−2], energy [M1L2T−2], pressure [M1L−1T−2].
  • Principle of homogeneity: a physically correct equation must have identical dimensions in every term. You can add 3 apples to 4 apples but not 3 apples to 4 metres — likewise v=u+at works because every term is a velocity [LT−1].
  • Use 1 — checking equations: replace every quantity by its dimensional formula and verify the two sides match. If they don't, the equation is certainly wrong. If they do match, the equation is probably right (never proof — a factor of ½ is dimensionless and invisible).
  • Use 2 — deriving relations (converting between systems): assume a product of powers form and solve for the exponents. Example: period of a simple pendulum T may depend on mass m, length l and gravity g. Write T=kmalbgc; matching dimensions [T]=[M]a[L]b[LT−2]c gives a=0, b+c=0, −2c=1 → c=−1/2, b=1/2 → T=kl/g​ (experiment gives k=2π).
  • Use 3 — converting units: to convert a quantity from one system to another, use n1​u1​=n2​u2​ with n_2 = n_1 \left(\ rac{M_1}{M_2}\ ight)^a \left(\ rac{L_1}{L_2}\ ight)^b \left(\ rac{T_1}{T_2}\ ight)^c for a quantity of dimensions [MaLbTc]. Example: 1 N in CGS: [MLT−2], n2​=1 imes(1000/1)(100/1)(1/1)=105 dyn.
  • Limitations of dimensional analysis: (1) it cannot find dimensionless constants (2π, ½, sin θ); (2) it fails for quantities related by addition/subtraction or trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions; (3) it cannot reveal whether a quantity is scalar or vector; (4) it cannot derive an equation involving more unknown powers than available base dimensions.
  • Dimensionless quantities: angles (radian), strain, refractive index, relative density, coefficient of friction, all pure ratios. They have [M0L0T0] and are unchanged on unit conversion — which is exactly why trigonometric arguments must be dimensionless.
  • Dimensional variables commonly asked in NEB: Planck's constant [ML2T−1] (same as angular momentum), gravitational constant [M−1L3T−2], coefficient of viscosity [ML−1T−1], surface tension [MT−2], power [ML2T−3], pressure/energy density [ML−1T−2].
  • NEB exam focus: write dimensional formulas of 8–10 standard quantities; check homogeneity of a given equation; derive a relation (pendulum, wave speed on string v∝T/μ​, or time of flight) using powers method; convert a force/energy value between SI and CGS.

  • Dimensions and uses of dimensional analysis

    1. Introduction

    Physical equations have grammar: the dimensions on the left must match the dimensions on the right, or the sentence is meaningless. Dimensions ([M] mass, [L] length, [T] time, [I] current…) are the parts of speech of physics, and dimensional analysis is the proofreading — checking homogeneity, converting units, and even discovering new relations such as the pendulum formula before a single experiment is timed.

    A Nepali pump-set seller converts 5 HP to watts (5 × 746 = 3730 W) by pure dimensional arithmetic; a pipe fitting ordered as "2-inch" arrives as 5.08 cm by the same move. NEB's favourite derivation of Class 11 — T = 2π√(l/g) from dimensions alone — shows the method inventing, not just checking.

    What you will learn: (i) dimensions and dimensional formulae of common quantities; (ii) the principle of homogeneity; (iii) four uses — checking, deriving, converting, finding dimensions; (iv) the three limitations; (v) the SI-2019 constant-based definitions behind the units.

    Learning outcomes. You can: write any dimensional formula; test any equation's homogeneity; derive the pendulum period and centripetal force dimensionally; convert N↔dyne and J↔erg; and state exactly where the method goes blind.

  • 2. Concepts and laws

    Dimensions and dimensional formulae

    1. Definition. The dimension of a physical quantity is the power to which each base quantity (M, L, T, I, K, mol, cd) is raised to express it: velocity = [M⁰LT⁻¹], force = [MLT⁻²], energy = [ML²T⁻²].
    2. Reading table. Area [M⁰L²T⁰], volume [L³], density [ML⁻³], acceleration [LT⁻²], momentum [MLT⁻¹], pressure [ML⁻¹T⁻²], power [ML²T⁻³], charge [IT], Planck constant [ML²T⁻¹], gravitational constant [M⁻¹L³T⁻²] (CODATA 6.674×10⁻¹¹ m³kg⁻¹s⁻² for numericals).
    3. Dimensionless quantities. Pure numbers, angles (radian), strain, refractive index, relative density, all trigonometric/exponential ratios — dimensions [M⁰L⁰T⁰]. Dimensional analysis cannot see them (limitation 1).
    4. SI-2019 validity note. Since 2019 the base units ride on exact constants: second on ΔνCs (9 192 631 770 Hz), metre on c (299 792 458 m/s), kilogram on h (6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J·s), ampere on e, mole on N_A (6.02214076×10²³ mol⁻¹), kelvin on k_B. Constants are exact by definition — quote them without error bars.

    Principle of homogeneity (with assumptions)

    1. Statement. In a correct physical equation, the dimensions of every term on both sides are identical: [LHS] = [RHS] term by term. Words: you cannot add apples (length) to oranges (time).
    2. Assumptions box. Applies to equations linking physical quantities with consistent unit systems; transcendental functions (sin, log, exp) take dimensionless arguments only; the check passes necessary-but-not-sufficient correctness (a wrong dimensionless constant slips through).
    3. Instant checks. v = u + at: [LT⁻¹] = [LT⁻¹] + [LT⁻²][T] ✓. s = ut + ½at²: [L] = [L] + [L] ✓. E = mc³? [ML²T⁻²] vs [ML³T⁻³] ✗ — rejected before any lab work.

  • 3. Derivation block — the flagship method

    Derivation 1: period of a simple pendulum (assume → equate → solve)

    1. Assume T ∝ m^a · l^b · g^c (mass, length, gravity may matter) with dimensionless constant k: T = k·m^a·l^b·g^c.
    2. Write dimensions. [T] = [M]^a·[L]^b·[LT⁻²]^c = [M^a L^(b+c) T^(−2c)].
    3. Equate powers (homogeneity): M: a = 0; T: −2c = 1 → c = −1/2; L: b + c = 0 → b = 1/2.
    4. Result. T = k·l^(1/2)·g^(−1/2) = k√(l/g). Experiment fixes k = 2π: T = 2π√(l/g). Mass drops out (a = 0) — the famous prediction that the bob's mass doesn't matter.
    5. Validity condition. Small amplitudes only (sinθ ≈ θ fails beyond ~10°); massless inextensible string; point bob. NEB's most-asked derivation — memorise the four steps, not just the line.

    Derivation 2: centripetal force (same machine, new result)

    1. Assume F ∝ m^a·v^b·r^c: [MLT⁻²] = [M]^a·[LT⁻¹]^b·[L]^c = [M^a L^(b+c) T^(−b)].
    2. Equate. M: a = 1; T: −b = −2 → b = 2; L: b + c = 1 → c = −1.
    3. Result. F = k·mv²/r; dynamics fixes k = 1: F = mv²/r. Two derivations, one machine — that is the method's power.

    Uses and limitations

    1. Check equations (above) — catches wrong formulae in seconds.
    2. Derive relations — pendulum, centripetal force, wave speed v = √(P/ρ) pattern.
    3. Convert units — 1 N = 1 kg·m/s² = 1000 g × 100 cm/s² = 10⁵ dyne; 1 J = 10⁷ erg. General: n₂ = n₁·(M₁/M₂)^a·(L₁/L₂)^b·(T₁/T₂)^c.
    4. Find dimensions — of G from F = Gm₁m₂/r² → [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]; of h from E = hν → [ML²T⁻¹]; of k (spring) from F = kx → [MT⁻²].
    5. Limitations (all three, with reason). (i) Dimensionless constants invisible (k = 2π never emerges). (ii) Fails for sums of 3+ unknownsBerry-style underdetermined systems and for transcendental/exponential relations (s = ut + ½at² works — three terms but known form; truly unknown 4-variable laws underdetermine). Stated sharply: cannot fix pure numbers, cannot handle functions like sin(ωt) beyond their arguments, cannot separate quantities sharing dimensions (work vs torque both [ML²T⁻²]).

  • 6. Worked numericals (Given → Formula → Substitution → Answer with unit)

    1. Check v² = u² + 2as. [L²T⁻²] vs [L²T⁻²] + [LT⁻²][L] = [L²T⁻²] + [L²T⁻²] ✓ homogeneous — passes the grammar test.
    2. Dimensions of G. From F = Gm₁m₂/r²: [G] = [F][r²]/[m²] = [MLT⁻²][L²]/[M²] = [M⁻¹L³T⁻²]. Value for numericals: 6.674×10⁻¹¹ N·m²/kg² (CODATA).
    3. Dimensions of Planck's h. From E = hν: [h] = [E]/[ν] = [ML²T⁻²]/[T⁻¹] = [ML²T⁻¹]; exact 6.62607015×10⁻³⁴ J·s (SI 2019 definition).
    4. Convert 1 N to dyne. 1 N = 1 kg·m/s² = (1000 g)(100 cm)/s² = 10⁵ g·cm/s² = 10⁵ dyne. General rule above handles J→erg (10⁷) the same way.
    5. Wave speed on a string. Assume v ∝ T^a·μ^b (tension, mass/length): [LT⁻¹] = [MLT⁻²]^a·[ML⁻¹]^b = [M^(a+b) L^(a−b) T^(−2a)]. M: a+b = 0; T: −2a = −1 → a = 1/2, b = −1/2. v = k√(T/μ) — the derivation machine's third product.
    6. Spring constant. From F = kx: [k] = [F]/[x] = [MLT⁻²]/[L] = [MT⁻²] — same dimensions as surface tension's cousin? No: surface tension is force/length = [MT⁻²] too — the same-dimension clash that limitation (iii) warns about.

    7. Key terms, signs and traps

    • Dimension = power of M, L, T, I… in a quantity; dimensional formula its [M^aL^bT^c…] packet.
    • Homogeneity = term-by-term dimensional equality; necessary, not sufficient.
    • Dimensionless = [M⁰L⁰T⁰]: angles, strain, ratios, constants like π and 2π.
    • The machine = assume (with k) → expand → equate → solve; experiment supplies k.
    • SI 2019 = units from exact constants (c, h, e, N_A, k_B, ΔνCs, K_cd); prototype kilogram retired.
    • Traps: equating work with torque (same dimensions, different physics) · forgetting k is invisible · applying the method to sin/cos/log arguments (must be dimensionless) · dropping the radian (dimensionless but informative).

    9. Summary and self-check

    Dimensions [M L T …] are physics grammar; homogeneity (LHS = RHS termwise) proofreads every equation. The four uses: check (v² = u²+2as ✓), derive (pendulum T = 2π√(l/g) with mass dropping out; F = mv²/r), convert (1 N = 10⁵ dyne, 1 J = 10⁷ erg), and find dimensions (G [M⁻¹L³T⁻²], h [ML²T⁻¹]). Blind spots: dimensionless k, transcendental arguments, same-dimension clashes (work/torque). SI 2019 grounds units in exact constants. Figures: balance schematic, pendulum flow, SI dependency and base-unit plates.

    Self-check. (1) Derive T = 2π√(l/g) stating each validity condition. (2) Test s = ut + ½at² and E = mc³. (3) Find [η] (viscosity) from F = 6πηrv. (4) Convert 72 km/h to m/s and 1 kWh to joule dimensionally. (5) Why can't dimensions distinguish work from torque?

    Common mistakes. Writing k from dimensions (impossible) · equating coefficients across different dimensions · feeding degrees into sin without converting (arguments dimensionless, but consistency still rules) · citing the prototype kilogram (retired 2019).

Confusion

  • ❌ 'If an equation is dimensionally correct, it is physically correct.' ✅ Homogeneity is a necessary but not sufficient test — T=l/g​ and T=3l/g​ are both dimensionally correct; only one is true (the constant 2π is invisible to dimensions).
  • ❌ 'Dimensional formula and dimensional equation are the same.' ✅ The formula is [F]=[MLT−2]; the equation is F=[MLT−2] — the equation shows the quantity equated to its dimensions.
  • ❌ 'Angle has units but no dimensions, so it is not a physical quantity.' ✅ Angle is a dimensionless [M0L0T0] SUPPLEMENTARY quantity — it has a unit (radian) but zero exponents in M, L, T.
  • ❌ 'Trigonometric, exponential and log functions can be applied to dimensional quantities.' ✅ Their arguments MUST be dimensionless — sin(ωt) is valid only because ωt is an angle (dimensionless).
  • ❌ '1 newton = 1 dyn.' ✅ 1 extN=105 extdyn — dimensional conversion ([MLT−2]: 1000 g × 100 cm) gives the factor 105.
  • ❌ 'Dimensional analysis can determine the scalar/vector nature of a quantity.' ✅ It cannot — work and torque have identical dimensions [ML2T−2] yet work is scalar, torque is vector.
  • Homogeneity passing does not prove correctness — wrong dimensionless constants slip through.
  • The 2019 SI retired the prototype kilogram: kg now comes from Planck's h, exact.
  • Work and torque share [ML²T⁻²] but differ physically — dimensions don't see vectors or points of application.

  • Convert 1 joule into ergs. — Energy [ML2T−2]: 1 imes(1000)1(100)2=107 erg.

Universal Facts

  • Dimensional analysis was pioneered by Fourier (1822) and developed into a systematic tool by Rayleigh; the Buckingham π theorem is its formal generalisation used throughout engineering.
  • The 2019 SI redefinition fixed h (Planck constant), c, e, kB​, NA​ etc. exactly — every unit is now dimensionally anchored to fundamental constants.
  • Physicists testing new theories always run a 'dimensional audit' first; many wrong published equations have been caught because their terms failed to balance dimensionally.
  • Stefan's law (E=σT4) was derived dimensionally by Stefan in 1879 from Tyndall's experimental data, five years before Boltzmann derived it theoretically.

Key Points

  • Dimensional homogeneity is a necessary test of correctness — it can reject an equation but can never fully prove one.
  • Dimensionless constants (½, 2π, e) are invisible to dimensional analysis.
  • Quantities with identical dimensions can still be physically different (work vs torque; energy vs moment).
  • The powers method can only solve for as many unknown exponents as there are independent base dimensions (usually 3: M, L, T).
  • Angle (radian) and steradian are supplementary, dimensionless [M0L0T0] units.
  • Any term inside sin, cos, exp, log must be dimensionless.
  • Dimensions: velocity [LT⁻¹], force [MLT⁻²], energy [ML²T⁻²]; dimensionless: angles, strain, π.
  • Machine: assume T=k·m^a l^b g^c → expand → equate → T=k√(l/g), mass drops out.
  • Uses: check, derive, convert (10⁵, 10⁷), find dimensions; limits: k invisible, transcendental, same-dimension clash.

Summary

Dimensions [M L T I …] are the grammar of equations; homogeneity (LHS = RHS termwise) proofreads them. Uses: check (v²=u²+2as ✓, E=mc³ ✗), derive (pendulum T=2π√(l/g) with mass dropping out; centripetal F=mv²/r; string v=k√(T/μ)), convert (1 N=10⁵ dyne, 1 J=10⁷ erg), find dimensions (G [M⁻¹L³T⁻²], h [ML²T⁻¹]). Blind spots: dimensionless k, transcendental arguments, same-dimension clashes. SI 2019 fixes units to exact constants (c, h, e, N_A, k_B). Figures: homogeneity balance, pendulum derivation flow, SI dependency chart (Pisanty), base-unit plate (DePiep).

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Memorise the standard table — NEB asks dimensional formulas of force, momentum, power, pressure, G, h, viscosity and surface tension nearly every year.
  • Note 2: In derivation questions, ALWAYS begin by writing the assumed product-of-powers form and end by interpreting the exponents.
  • Note 3: When converting units, keep the dimensional exponents of the QUANTITY (not of the units) — the commonest source of sign errors.
  • Note 4: Dimensionless groups (Reynolds number, Mach number) are the basis of scale-model testing in engineering — same dimensions in, same physics out.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: The dimensions of every term in a physically valid equation must be identical (principle of dimensional homogeneity).
  • Statement 2: Dimensional methods can determine the FORM of a relation but never its dimensionless constants.
  • Statement 3: The numerical value of a physical quantity changes with the unit system, but the quantity itself (n imesu) is invariant.

Important Notes

  • Note 1: Work and torque share dimensions [ML2T−2] but differ physically (scalar vs vector) — dimensions do not define a quantity.
  • Note 2: The pendulum derivation gives T=kl/g​; the constant k=2π must come from experiment or full theory.
  • Note 3: Only three base dimensions (M, L, T) are usually available, so at most three unknown exponents can be determined.
  • Note 4: Dimensionless arguments are mandatory in trigonometric, exponential and logarithmic functions.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: Build dimensional formulas from known definitions rather than memorising: force = ma, then chain (power = F·v, pressure = F/A, etc.).
  • Trick 2: 'Same dimensions' pairs frequently asked: work–torque–energy; Planck's constant–angular momentum; impulse–momentum; pressure–energy density; gravitational potential–velocity squared.
  • Trick 3: For 'find dimensions of a/b in equation' questions, apply homogeneity term-by-term first, then take the ratio.
  • Trick 4: For unit conversion, write the dimension of the quantity and multiply/divide by the length–mass–time scale factors in that order.
  • Trick 5: If an MCQ shows an equation mixing velocity and acceleration as separate added terms, reject it instantly — homogeneity fails.

Exam Notes

  • Exam Tip 1: A 2-mark derivation by the powers method (pendulum, string wave, Stokes' law) appears in NEB almost every year — practise the full written format.
  • Exam Tip 2: Always state limitations when asked to 'derive and comment' — omitting them costs the last mark.
  • Exam Tip 3: For conversion numericals, show n1​u1​=n2​u2​ explicitly; the final factor without working earns half marks only.
  • Exam Tip 4: Check answers dimensionally at the end of every long derivation — it takes 30 seconds and catches most slips.

Important Concepts

  • Concept 1: Base quantities (M, L, T, I, Θ, J, N) and derived quantities expressed as dimensional formulas.
  • Concept 2: Principle of homogeneity — the fundamental test of dimensional validity of any equation.
  • Concept 3: Method of powers for deriving relations: assume product form, equate exponents per base dimension.
  • Concept 4: Unit conversion via dimensional formula: n₂ = n₁ × (scale factors)^(exponents).
  • Concept 5: Dimensionless quantities (strain, angle, refractive index) and their special role in functional arguments.

Specific Conditions

  • Homogeneity Constraint: Only quantities with the same dimensional formula can be added, subtracted, or equated in an equation. Term-by-term analysis is mandatory.
  • Function Argument Constraint: The arguments of trigonometric (e.g., sin θ, cos θ), exponential (e.g., ex), and logarithmic functions must be dimensionless. This is a critical check for determining the dimensions of unknown constants in an equation.
  • Dimensionless Constant Constraint: Dimensional analysis inherently cannot identify or verify dimensionless constants (e.g., 2π, 0.5). These factors must be determined via independent theoretical derivation or experimental calibration.
