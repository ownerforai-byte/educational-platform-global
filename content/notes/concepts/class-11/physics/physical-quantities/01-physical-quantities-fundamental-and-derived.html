Physical Quantities — Fundamental & Derived

Class 11PhysicsPhysical Quantities

Notes

  • A physical quantity is a property that can be measured. The report of a measurement has a numerical value and a unit. A number without a unit is not a finished physics answer, except for a pure ratio.
  • The SI base quantities used here are length, mass, time, electric current, temperature, amount of substance and luminous intensity. Their base units are the metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela.
  • A derived quantity is built from base quantities by multiplication or division. Speed is length over time, so its unit is m/s. Force is mass times acceleration, so the newton is kg m/s^2.
  • A plane angle in radians is a ratio of two lengths, so it is dimensionless. That does not make the angle unimportant. It means no extra base unit is required for it in the SI list used in class.
  • Prefixes scale a unit by a power of ten. The kilometre is 10^3 m, the millimetre is 10^-3 m, and the micrometre is 10^-6 m. Convert to base units before you multiply.
  • Two quantities may be added only when they have the same dimension and are expressed in the same unit. Adding 2 m to 30 cm requires converting 30 cm to 0.30 m first.

Confusion

  • Mass and weight are different quantities. The kilogram is the unit of mass.
  • A dimensionless quantity can still be physical, as a radian angle is.
  • The newton is derived, not a base unit.
  • Temperature in a formula is the kelvin scale when the formula was built that way.

Exercises

1.

Express a force of 5.0 N in base SI units and state the base quantities involved.

Steps
  • One newton is the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s^2.
  • So 1 N = 1 kg m/s^2.
  • 5.0 N = 5.0 kg m/s^2.
  • The base quantities in that unit are mass, length and time.
  • No extra prefix remains.
Answer

5.0 kg m/s^2, from mass, length and time.
