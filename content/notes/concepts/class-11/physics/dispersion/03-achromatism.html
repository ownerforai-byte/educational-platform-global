Notes

  • An optical pair is achromatic for two chosen colours when their wavelength-dependent deviations cancel and both colours share a final focus. The lenses remain dispersive individually; their effects compensate.
  • For two thin lenses in contact with signed focal lengths f1,f2 and dispersive powers ω1,ω2, primary achromatism requires ω1/f1+ω2/f2=0. A crown converging lens is commonly paired with a more dispersive flint diverging lens.
  • Worked design: f1=+20 cm, ω1=0.02 and ω2=0.04 require 0.02/20+0.04/f2=0, giving f2=−40 cm. The negative sign identifies a diverging flint element.
  • The net power is 1/F=1/f1+1/f2, so the doublet can remain converging while cancelling colour spread. Camera, telescope and microscope objectives use achromatic groups to reduce coloured fringes.
  • Primary achromatism corrects two selected wavelengths; other colours may still form a secondary spectrum. More elaborate apochromatic systems correct additional wavelengths.
  • The contact condition uses powers, so the dispersive powers are divided by the focal lengths. If the two lenses are separated by a distance, the condition changes and a Huygens eyepiece uses that separated arrangement. Quoting the contact rule for a separated pair gives the wrong flint lens.

Confusion

  • Do not omit the negative focal length of the diverging lens.
  • The simple condition assumes thin lenses in contact.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Summary

An achromatic doublet balances wavelength dispersion with signed lens powers: ω1/f1+ω2/f2=0. It reduces but does not eliminate every colour error.

Specific Conditions

  • Dispersion Definition: Dispersion is the separation of white light into its constituent colors (spectrum) due to the dependence of the refractive index n on the wavelength λ of light (n(λ)).
  • Cauchy's Equation: For many transparent materials, the refractive index follows Cauchy's equation n(λ)=A+B/λ2+C/λ4. This explains why blue light (λ smaller) is refracted more than red light (λ larger).
  • Prism Validity: The formula for dispersive power ω=(nv​−nr​)/(ny​−1) assumes small angle of deviation and that the prism is in air. For non-thin prisms or different mediums, angular dispersion depends on the geometry.

Exercises

1.

Two lenses in contact have ω1 = 0.030, f1 = +15 cm and ω2 = 0.045. Find the flint focal length that cancels the colour.

Steps
  • Use ω1/f1 + ω2/f2 = 0 because the lenses touch.
  • 0.030/15 + 0.045/f2 = 0.
  • 0.0020 + 0.045/f2 = 0.
  • f2 = −0.045/0.0020 = −22.5 cm.
  • Net power is 1/15 + 1/(−22.5) = 0.0667 − 0.0444 = 0.022 per cm, still converging.
Answer

f2 = −22.5 cm.
