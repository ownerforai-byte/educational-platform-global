Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Unweaving the Rainbow

    A prism fans white light into a rainbow of colours because glass slows each wavelength differently. Measuring that spread — the dispersive power — is how optical glass is graded, and it explains why prisms make spectra and lenses make chromatic mistakes.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2020, 2021, 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Dispersion and its cause (NEB 2022, 2024)

    Dispersion: the splitting of white light into its constituent colours (VIBGYOR) as it passes through a dispersive medium such as a prism.

    Cause (NEB 2024, 3m): the refractive index of glass depends on wavelength (Cauchy's relation n=a+b/λ2). Violet (shortest λ) has the largest n and is deviated most; red (longest λ) has the smallest n and is deviated least — the beam fans into a spectrum (NEB 2024, 2m).

    Deviation vs dispersion (NEB 2020, 3m): deviation is the bending of a single ray (δ=i+e−A) — it occurs even for monochromatic light; dispersion is the splitting of colours — it needs a mixture of wavelengths.

    2.2 Pure spectrum

    A pure spectrum has the colours sharply separated without overlap — produced with a narrow slit, a collimating lens (parallel rays), a prism and a telescope (the spectroscope). An impure spectrum has overlapping, blurred colours.

    2.3 Angular dispersion and dispersive power (NEB 2021, 2024)

    • Angular dispersion: δv​−δr​ — the angle between the extreme violet and red rays.
    • Dispersive power ω = spread ÷ mean deviation:

    ω=δy​δv​−δr​​=ny​−1nv​−nr​​ — a pure number characterising the glass (NEB 2021, 4m derivation via δ = (n−1)A for a thin prism; NEB 2024, 3m definition).

    2.4 Worked numericals

    Q (3m, 2024): For a glass, n_v = 1.53, n_r = 1.51 and n_y = 1.52. Calculate its dispersive power. ω=ny​−1nv​−nr​​=1.52−11.53−1.51​=0.520.02​≈0.0385

    Q (NEB-style): A thin prism of A = 5° has n_v = 1.523, n_r = 1.513. Find the angular dispersion. δv​−δr​=(nv​−nr​)A=0.010×5°=0.05°.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 76: the thin-prism formula δ = (n−1)A is the engine of the dispersive-power derivation.
    • To Topic 81: chromatic aberration in lenses is dispersion's defect; the rainbow is dispersion's spectacle.
    • To spectroscopy: the pure spectrum is the principle of the spectroscope and atomic physics.

    1. Confusing dispersion (splitting of colours) with deviation (bending of a ray).
    2. Writing ω = (n_v − n_r)/n_y instead of dividing by (n_y − 1).
    3. Saying dispersion happens in a parallel slab — parallel faces recombine the colours.

  • ω divides by (n_y − 1), not by n_y — the mean deviation needs the (−1).

Key Points

Summary

Dispersion splits white light into VIBGYOR because a medium's refractive index depends on wavelength (n=a+b/λ2): violet (largest n) deviates most, red least (NEB 2022, 2024). Deviation — bending of one ray — happens even for monochromatic light; dispersion needs mixed wavelengths (NEB 2020). A pure spectrum (sharp, non-overlapping colours) comes from slit + collimator + prism + telescope. Quantitatively, angular dispersion is δ_v − δ_r and the dispersive power is ω=δy​δv​−δr​​=ny​−1nv​−nr​​ (NEB 2021, 2024) — for n_v = 1.53, n_r = 1.51, n_y = 1.52, ω ≈ 0.0385 (NEB 2024, 3m). The primary rainbow is the same physics inside a raindrop: refraction, one TIR, refraction — a 42° cone with violet inside (NEB 2024, 3m).

Special Notes

  • Do not equate a broad rainbow with a pure spectrum; slit width affects purity.
  • The thin-prism ratio assumes small deviations.

Important Statements

Specific Conditions

  • Dispersion Definition: Dispersion is the separation of white light into its constituent colors (spectrum) due to the dependence of the refractive index n on the wavelength λ of light (n(λ)).
  • Cauchy's Equation: For many transparent materials, the refractive index follows Cauchy's equation n(λ)=A+B/λ2+C/λ4. This explains why blue light (λ smaller) is refracted more than red light (λ larger).
  • Prism Validity: The formula for dispersive power ω=(nv​−nr​)/(ny​−1) assumes small angle of deviation and that the prism is in air. For non-thin prisms or different mediums, angular dispersion depends on the geometry.

Exercises

1.

Crown glass has μ_v = 1.53, μ_r = 1.51 and mean μ = 1.52. Find ω and say what a pure spectrum requires.

Steps
  • ω = (1.53 − 1.51) / (1.52 − 1) = 0.020 / 0.52.
  • ω = 0.038.
  • A pure spectrum needs a narrow slit and parallel light through the prism.
  • The focusing lens then puts each colour in its own place.
  • Overlap of those images would make an impure spectrum.
Answer

0.038. A pure spectrum needs a slit, a collimator and a focusing lens so colours do not overlap.
