Notes

  • A prism forms a spectrum because refractive index varies with wavelength. In normal dispersion, violet has a larger index and deviation than red. A pure spectrum has narrow, well-separated colour images with little overlap.
  • For a thin prism, angular dispersion is Δδ=(nv−nr)A and yellow mean deviation is δy=(ny−1)A. Their ratio is ω=Δδ/δy=(nv−nr)/(ny−1).
  • Worked example: if δy=3° and ω=0.06, the red–violet spread is Δδ=0.18°. A material with twice the dispersive power gives twice the spread at the same mean deviation.
  • To obtain a purer spectrum, use a narrow slit, collimate the incident beam and focus the dispersed rays with a lens. A wide slit broadens each colour image and causes neighbouring bands to overlap.
  • A spectrometer uses a collimator slit and telescope to form and inspect a sharp spectrum. Resolution improves when the source image is narrow and the optical alignment is correct.
  • A pure spectrum is formed when each colour is brought to its own sharp image and those images do not overlap. A slit at the focus of a collimating lens sends parallel light into the prism, and a telescope then focuses each colour separately.

Specific Conditions

  • Dispersion Definition: Dispersion is the separation of white light into its constituent colors (spectrum) due to the dependence of the refractive index n on the wavelength λ of light (n(λ)).
  • Cauchy's Equation: For many transparent materials, the refractive index follows Cauchy's equation n(λ)=A+B/λ2+C/λ4. This explains why blue light (λ smaller) is refracted more than red light (λ larger).
  • Prism Validity: The formula for dispersive power ω=(nv​−nr​)/(ny​−1) assumes small angle of deviation and that the prism is in air. For non-thin prisms or different mediums, angular dispersion depends on the geometry.

Exercises

1.

Crown glass has μ_v = 1.53, μ_r = 1.51 and mean μ = 1.52. Find ω and say what a pure spectrum requires.

Steps
  • ω = (1.53 − 1.51) / (1.52 − 1) = 0.020 / 0.52.
  • ω = 0.038.
  • A pure spectrum needs a narrow slit and parallel light through the prism.
  • The focusing lens then puts each colour in its own place.
  • Overlap of those images would make an impure spectrum.
Answer

0.038. A pure spectrum needs a slit, a collimator and a focusing lens so colours do not overlap.
