Chromatic and Spherical Aberration

Notes

  • Chromatic aberration is colour fringing caused by a lens having different focal lengths for different wavelengths. In ordinary glass, blue/violet light is refracted more strongly than red, so their images do not share one focus.
  • Longitudinal chromatic aberration is the axial separation Δf=|fv−fr| of two colour foci. Transverse chromatic aberration is a wavelength-dependent lateral image displacement, often visible at the edge of a wide field.
  • Spherical aberration has a different cause: marginal and paraxial rays of the same colour focus at different points because the surface is spherical. It can be reduced with an aperture stop or aspheric surfaces.
  • Worked comparison: if red focuses at 50.0 cm and blue at 49.0 cm, longitudinal chromatic aberration is 1.0 cm. This is axial focus separation, not a 1 cm sideways image shift.
  • An achromatic doublet pairs converging crown glass with diverging flint glass so two selected colours focus together. Stopping down reduces marginal-ray error but does not remove material dispersion.
  • A single lens focuses violet closer to the lens than red, because violet has the higher index and a shorter focal length. The gap between those foci along the axis is the longitudinal chromatic aberration, about ω times the mean focal length.

Confusion

  • Stopping down does not make glass non-dispersive.
  • Primary achromatism reduces selected colour separation but may leave secondary spectrum.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Summary

Chromatic aberration comes from wavelength-dependent focal length; spherical aberration comes from paraxial–marginal focus separation. Their corrections differ.

Specific Conditions

  • Dispersion Definition: Dispersion is the separation of white light into its constituent colors (spectrum) due to the dependence of the refractive index n on the wavelength λ of light (n(λ)).
  • Cauchy's Equation: For many transparent materials, the refractive index follows Cauchy's equation n(λ)=A+B/λ2+C/λ4. This explains why blue light (λ smaller) is refracted more than red light (λ larger).
  • Prism Validity: The formula for dispersive power ω=(nv​−nr​)/(ny​−1) assumes small angle of deviation and that the prism is in air. For non-thin prisms or different mediums, angular dispersion depends on the geometry.

Exercises

1.

A lens has mean f = 20 cm and ω = 0.040. Estimate the longitudinal chromatic aberration.

Steps
  • Longitudinal chromatic aberration ≈ ω f.
  • ω f = 0.040 × 20 cm.
  • The gap is 0.80 cm.
  • Violet focuses nearer the lens than red.
  • A screen cannot be sharp for both colours at once.
Answer

0.80 cm, with violet nearer the lens.
