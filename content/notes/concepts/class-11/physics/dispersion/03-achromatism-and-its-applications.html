Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Cancelling Colour

    Pair a crown-glass prism with an oppositely oriented flint-glass prism and the colours recombine while the light still bends — an achromatic combination. The same trick in lens form built the achromatic doublet that saved photography and astronomy from colour fringes.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Achromatic combination of prisms (NEB 2024, 3m)

    Two prisms of different glasses (different dispersive powers) cemented with their refracting edges oppositely oriented:

    • Their dispersions are equal and opposite → colours recombine (white stays white).
    • Their mean deviations add (small net bending).

    Condition for achromatism: (δv​−δr​)1​=(δv​−δr​)2​⇒ω1​δy1​=ω2​δy2​ Since ω differs between glasses, the prism angles are chosen unequal (A₂ = A₁ω₁/ω₂) to satisfy it.

    2.2 Achromatic doublet (lenses)

    Two thin lenses in contact — a convex crown-glass lens and a concave flint-glass lens — chosen so their chromatic aberrations cancel: f1​ω1​​+f2​ω2​​=0 The combination still converges (net positive power) but focuses all colours together. Applications: telescope and camera objectives, microscope objectives, eyepieces.

    2.3 Applications of achromatism

    • Achromatic telescope objectives: sharp star images without colour fringes.
    • Camera lenses: multiple elements, each correcting the others' colour errors.
    • Spectacle lenses and microscopes: doublets keep images colour-clean.
    • Note: achromatism corrects two chosen colours (e.g. red and violet/blue); residual "secondary spectrum" remains.

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): A crown prism (ω₁ = 0.02, δ_y = 4°) is paired with a flint prism (ω₂ = 0.04). Find the flint prism's mean deviation for achromatism. ω1​δy1​=ω2​δy2​ → δy2​=0.040.02×4°​=2°.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 80: the condition ω₁δ_y1 = ω₂δ_y2 comes straight from the dispersive-power definition.
    • To Topic 81: achromatism is the systematic cure for chromatic aberration.
    • To lens combinations (Topic 79): powers add in contact — the doublet exploits the same algebra with opposite signs.

Key Points

Summary

An achromatic combination pairs two prisms of different glasses (crown + flint) with their edges oppositely oriented so that their angular dispersions are equal and opposite — colours recombine while a net deviation remains. The condition is ω1​δy1​=ω2​δy2​, fixed by choosing unequal angles (A₂ = A₁ω₁/ω₂) (NEB 2024, 3m). In lenses the same logic builds the achromatic doublet — a convex crown lens with a concave flint lens in contact satisfying ω1​/f1​+ω2​/f2​=0 — the colour-corrected heart of telescope, camera and microscope objectives. Achromatism kills the chromatic defect while leaving the useful bending intact.

Special Notes

  • Do not omit the negative focal length of the diverging lens.
  • The simple condition assumes thin lenses in contact.

Important Statements

  • "An achromatic combination produces dispersion-free deviation: the angular dispersions of its parts are equal and opposite."

Specific Conditions

  • Dispersion Definition: Dispersion is the separation of white light into its constituent colors (spectrum) due to the dependence of the refractive index n on the wavelength λ of light (n(λ)).
  • Cauchy's Equation: For many transparent materials, the refractive index follows Cauchy's equation n(λ)=A+B/λ2+C/λ4. This explains why blue light (λ smaller) is refracted more than red light (λ larger).
  • Prism Validity: The formula for dispersive power ω=(nv​−nr​)/(ny​−1) assumes small angle of deviation and that the prism is in air. For non-thin prisms or different mediums, angular dispersion depends on the geometry.

Exercises

1.

A crown lens has ω1 = 0.020 and f1 = +10 cm. Flint has ω2 = 0.040. Find f2 for an achromatic pair in contact.

Steps
  • ω1/f1 + ω2/f2 = 0.
  • 0.020/10 + 0.040/f2 = 0.
  • 0.0020 + 0.040/f2 = 0.
  • f2 = −0.040/0.0020 = −20 cm.
  • The flint lens is diverging, with half the power of the crown lens, and the pair still converges.
Answer

f2 = −20 cm.
