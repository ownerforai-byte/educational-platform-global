Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Motion Depends on Who Is Watching

    A passenger walking forward at 1 m/s in a train moving at 20 m/s moves at 21 m/s relative to the ground but 1 m/s relative to the train. Velocity has meaning only relative to a frame — this topic defines relative velocity and works the standard cases: same direction, opposite direction, perpendicular crossing (rain-umbrella, boat-river).

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2017)

    Relative velocity: the velocity of body A as observed from body B: vAB​=vA​−vB​ (velocity of A minus velocity of B, vector subtraction).

    CaseRelative velocity
    Same directionvAB​=vA​−vB​ (difference of magnitudes)
    Opposite directionsvAB​=vA​+vB​ (sum of magnitudes)
    At angle θmagnitude vA2​+vB2​−2vA​vB​cosθ​ (vector subtraction)

    Worked numerical (NEB 2017, 2m): Two cars move in the same direction at 20 m/s and 25 m/s. Find their relative velocity. vrel​=25−20= 5 m/s (the faster car pulls ahead at 5 m/s; from the slower car, the faster one approaches at 5 m/s).

    2.1 Classic applications

    • Overtaking: time to catch = initial separation / relative speed.
    • Rain and umbrella: rain falling vertically at vr​; person walking at vp​ sees rain at vrain,person​=vr​−vp​ — slanting backward at angle tanθ=vp​/vr​ from vertical; tilt the umbrella forward by θ.
    • Boat–river: to cross straight across, the boat must aim upstream at angle θ with sinθ=vriver​/vboat​; crossing time = width/vboat​cosθ... (for straight crossing, effective upstream drift cancelled); if aimed straight across, drift = vriver​× crossing time.
    • Head-on collision closing speed: sum of speeds.

  • 3. Worked Numericals

    Q1: A boat crosses a 200 m river, current 3 m/s, boat speed 4 m/s in still water, aimed straight across. Find crossing time and drift. Crossing time: t = width/vboat​ = 200/4 = 50 s. Drift: = vriver​×t = 3 × 50 = 150 m downstream. Resultant velocity: 42+32​=5 m/s at tan−1(3/4)=36.9° from straight-across.

    Q2: Rain falls vertically at 8 m/s; a cyclist moves at 6 m/s. At what angle should the umbrella be held? tanθ=vp​/vr​=6/8=0.75→θ=36.9° from vertical, tilted forward.

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 6: relative velocity is velocity defined against a moving frame — same derivative idea, different reference.
    • To Vectors: relative velocity is vector subtraction — the magnitude formula mirrors the cosine rule.
    • To Dynamics: all of Newton's laws hold in inertial frames; relative velocity tells you when your frame differs from another's.
    • To real life: overtaking safety, aircraft headwinds/tailwinds, and river ferries are relative-velocity problems.

    5.2 Model answer

    Q (2m, 2017): Relative velocity of A with respect to B is the velocity of A as seen from B — the vector difference vAB​=vA​−vB​. For the two cars moving in the same direction: vrel​=25−20=5 m/s — the faster car moves at 5 m/s relative to the slower one.

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Subtracting in the wrong order — vAB​=vA​−vB​, not the reverse.
    2. Adding speeds for same-direction motion (they subtract) or subtracting for opposite motion (they add).
    3. Ignoring direction in angled relative-velocity problems — use vector subtraction, not magnitude arithmetic.
    4. In boat–river problems, forgetting that aiming straight across always causes downstream drift.
    5. Giving the rain-umbrella angle without saying which way to tilt the umbrella.

Confusion

  • v_AB vs v_BA: v_AB = v_A − v_B (A seen from B); v_BA is its negative — same magnitude, opposite direction.
  • Relative speed in same vs opposite direction: same direction subtracts; opposite directions add.

Practice

  • Why does rain appear slanted to a moving observer?

Universal Facts

  • Aeroplane timetables account for jet streams — the same aircraft flies Kathmandu–Doha faster westward-to-eastward because the wind is a relative-velocity term.

Specific Conditions

  • Vector Subtraction Rule: vB/A​=vB​−vA​ is the velocity of B as measured in A's rest frame. The order matters: vA/B​=−vB/A​. This is a vector equation — subtract components, not speeds.
  • Galilean Relativity Limit: Velocity addition v=v′+u (where u is frame velocity) is valid only for speeds ≪c. At relativistic speeds, Einstein's velocity addition v=(v′+u)/(1+v′u/c2) must be used. For everyday kinematics, Galilean addition is exact.
  • Apparent vs Real Motion: In the rain/river problems, the actual velocity of the rain/boat is unchanged; only the observed (relative) velocity changes when the observer moves. The rain still falls vertically; the umbrella is tilted to match the apparent velocity.

Key Points

  • v_AB = v_A − v_B (vector subtraction).
  • Same direction: difference of speeds; opposite: sum; angle θ: √(v_A² + v_B² − 2v_Av_B cos θ).
  • Rain-umbrella: tan θ = v_person/v_rain, tilt forward. Boat-river: aim straight → drift = v_river × t.

Important Statements

  • "There is no absolute rest — only frames."

Important Notes

  • "Same direction minus, opposite plus, angled — cosine rule."

Important Concepts

  • All velocity is relative: stating a velocity without naming the frame is incomplete — the ground frame is just a convention.

Examples

  • 20 m/s and 25 m/s same direction — relative velocity 5 m/s (NEB 2017).

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A river 200 m wide flows at 3 m/s; a boat aimed straight across moves at 4 m/s. Find crossing time and downstream drift. — t = 200/4 = 50 s; drift = 3 × 50 = 150 m; resultant speed 5 m/s at 36.9° from straight-across.

Formulas

    • v_AB = v_A − v_B
    • Same direction: v_rel = |v_A − v_B|; opposite: v_rel = v_A + v_B
    • Rain-umbrella: tan θ = v_p/v_r

Summary

Velocity is frame-dependent: the relative velocity of A with respect to B is the vector difference vAB​=vA​−vB​. Same-direction motion subtracts speeds (20 and 25 m/s give 5 m/s — NEB 2017); opposite-direction motion adds them; angled motion needs the cosine rule. Classic applications follow: the rain-umbrella problem tilts the umbrella forward by tanθ=vp​/vr​; the boat-river problem shows that aiming straight across always drifts downstream by vriver​× crossing time, while cancelling the drift requires aiming upstream. Every stated velocity is an answer to "relative to what?" — the ground frame is only a convention.

MCQS

1.

The relative velocity of A with respect to B is:

Options
  • v_A + v_B
  • v_A − v_B
  • v_B − v_A
  • v_A·v_B
Answer

B

Explanation

v_AB = v_A − v_B — vector subtraction.

2.

Two cars at 20 m/s and 25 m/s in the same direction have relative velocity:

Options
  • 45 m/s
  • 5 m/s
  • 22.5 m/s
  • 100 m/s
Answer

B

Explanation

Same direction: 25 − 20 = 5 m/s (NEB 2017).

3.

Two trains approach each other head-on at 15 m/s and 20 m/s. Closing speed is:

Options
  • 5 m/s
  • 17.5 m/s
  • 35 m/s
  • 300 m/s
Answer

C

Explanation

Opposite directions add: 15 + 20 = 35 m/s.

4.

Rain falls vertically; a person walks forward. To the person, rain appears:

Options
  • Vertical
  • Slanted backward
  • Slanted forward
  • Horizontal always
Answer

B

Explanation

v_rain,person = v_r − v_p points backward — tilt the umbrella forward.

5.

A boat aimed straight across a river will:

Options
  • Cross straight
  • Drift downstream
  • Drift upstream
  • Sink
Answer

B

Explanation

The current adds a downstream component — drift = v_river × crossing time.

6.

To cross a river straight across, the boat must aim:

Options
  • Straight across
  • Upstream at an angle
  • Downstream
  • Anywhere
Answer

B

Explanation

Aim upstream so the upstream component cancels the current.

7.

The magnitude of v_AB for velocities at angle θ is:

Options
  • v_A + v_B
  • √(v_A² + v_B² − 2v_Av_B cos θ)
  • √(v_A² + v_B² + 2v_Av_B cos θ)
  • v_A − v_B always
Answer

B

Explanation

Vector subtraction uses the cosine rule with a minus sign.

8.

Overtaking time equals:

Options
  • Separation × relative speed
  • Separation / relative speed
  • Separation × sum of speeds
  • Independent of separation
Answer

B

Explanation

Time = gap divided by the closing (relative) speed.
