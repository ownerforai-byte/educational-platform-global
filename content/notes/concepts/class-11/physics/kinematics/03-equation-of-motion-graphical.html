Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Three Equations from Two Graphs

    For motion with constant acceleration, three equations connect the five quantities u, v, a, t, s. The graphical derivation — reading slopes and areas off the velocity–time graph — shows where they come from without calculus, and is itself the examinable derivation.

  • 2. The Three Equations of Motion

    EquationLinksUse when asked for
    v=u+atv, u, a, tvelocity after time t (no s)
    s=ut+21​at2s, u, a, tdisplacement after time t (no v)
    v2=u2+2asv, u, a, sdisplacement without time (no t)

    2.1 Graphical derivation (the examinable one)

    Draw the velocity–time graph for uniform acceleration: a straight line from u (at t = 0) to v (at time t).

    1. First equation (slope): acceleration = slope of the v–t line:

    a=tv−u​⇒v=u+at.

    1. Second equation (area): displacement = area under the v–t line = area of the rectangle (ut) + area of the triangle (21​⋅t⋅(v−u)):

    s=ut+21​t(v−u)=ut+21​at2 (substituting v−u=at).

    1. Third equation (total area): area = 2u+v​⋅t (average velocity × time); substituting t=(v−u)/a:

    s=2u+v​⋅av−u​=2av2−u2​⇒v2=u2+2as.

    3. Worked numericals (NEB 2023)

    Q (3m, 2023): A car moving at 10 m/s accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 4 s. Find the final velocity and the distance travelled. Given: u = 10 m/s, a = 2 m/s², t = 4 s. Substitution: v=u+at=10+8=18 m/s; s=ut+21​at2=40+16=56 m. Answer: v = 18 m/s, s = 56 m.

    Q (3m, 2014): From rest to 20 m/s in 10 s: a = 2 m/s², s = 100 m (shown in Topic 6).

    3.1 Choosing the right equation

    List what is given and what is asked; pick the equation that contains exactly those plus one more. If t is neither given nor asked → use v2=u2+2as. If s is not involved → v=u+at. If v is not involved → s=ut+21​at2.

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 6: the equations are the integrals of a=dv/dt for constant a; the graphical derivation is the pre-calculus version.
    • To Topic 9: free fall is these equations with a = g, u = 0 (dropped) or u = 0 downward, and sign conventions.
    • To Topic 10: projectile motion applies the equations separately to horizontal (a = 0) and vertical (a = g) components.
    • To Topic 2 (dimensions): each equation is dimensionally homogeneous — the NEB favourite check.

    5.2 Model answer

    Q (3m, 2023): Given u = 10 m/s, a = 2 m/s², t = 4 s. Final velocity: v=u+at=10+2×4=18 m/s. Distance: s=ut+21​at2=10×4+21​×2×16=40+16=56 m. v = 18 m/s, s = 56 m. (A v–t sketch: line from 10 to 18 m/s; the area 56 m.)

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Using s=ut+21​at2 with a in the wrong sign (deceleration must enter as negative).
    2. Mixing up which equation omits which quantity.
    3. In the graphical derivation, forgetting to label u, v, t and the rectangle/triangle areas.
    4. Assuming uniform acceleration when the question says "uniform velocity".
    5. Dropping units in the final answer.

Confusion

  • Uniform velocity vs uniform acceleration: uniform velocity means a = 0 (v–t graph horizontal); uniform acceleration means the v–t graph is a straight sloped line.
  • Distance vs displacement in these equations: the equations give displacement; total path length needs separate handling when direction reverses.

Practice

  • Why does the third equation have no time term?

Universal Facts

  • The third equation lets you find stopping distance without knowing the braking time — the basis of road-safety calculations.

Examples

  • 10 m/s, 2 m/s², 4 s — v = 18 m/s, s = 56 m (NEB 2023).

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A train decelerates uniformly from 30 m/s to rest in 150 m. Find the deceleration and the time taken. — v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 900 − 2a(150) → a = 3 m/s² (deceleration); t = (v−u)/a = 30/3 = 10 s.

Formulas

    • v = u + at
    • s = ut + ½at²
    • v² = u² + 2as
    • s = (u + v)t/2

Key Points

  • v = u + at (slope of v–t line); s = ut + ½at² (rectangle + triangle area); v² = u² + 2as (average-velocity area).
  • Choose the equation by which quantity is absent: no t → v² = u² + 2as; no s → v = u + at; no v → s = ut + ½at².

Summary

For constant acceleration, three equations connect u, v, a, t and s — and the velocity–time graph derives all of them. The slope of the v–t line gives a=(v−u)/t, i.e. v=u+at; the area splits into a rectangle ut and a triangle 21​at2, giving s=ut+21​at2; the total area 2u+v​t rearranges to v2=u2+2as. Choosing the right equation means picking the one that omits the quantity you neither have nor need. NEB numericals: 10 m/s with 2 m/s² for 4 s gives v = 18 m/s and s = 56 m (2023); from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s gives a = 2 m/s² and s = 100 m (2014).

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Always draw and label the v–t graph in derivation questions — the diagram itself carries marks.
  • Note 2: For two-stage journeys (accelerate then brake), apply the equations to EACH stage and add the areas.
  • Note 3: Convert km/h to m/s before substituting (divide by 3.6) — the most common numerical slip.
  • Note 4: If the body reverses direction, compute each leg separately and add magnitudes for distance, not displacement.

Important Statements

  • "Slope and area: the v–t graph holds all three equations."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: car numerical (2023, 3m; 2014, 3m). The graphical derivation itself is a standard theory question.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Missing t? Use v² = u² + 2as."

Exam Notes

  • Most-tested: two-equation numericals (2014, 2023). Be ready to draw and label the v–t graph for the derivation.

  • 10 m

Which equation is most direct for finding stopping distance when brakes give constant deceleration?

C

The equations of motion are NOT valid for:

C

  • Graphs are derivations: slope gives a, area gives s — the three equations are geometry on the v–t plane.

  • Task 2: Solve ten numericals classifying them by which equation fits (u/v/a/t/s given-asked table).
  • Task 3: Verify sn​=u+2a​(2n−1) by computing s(n)−s(n−1) explicitly.
  • Task 4: Solve a two-stage train problem (accelerate, cruise, brake) via the v–t area method.
  • Task 5: Explain why doubling speed quadruples braking distance, using v2=u2+2as with v=0.

MCQS

1.

The equation that does not contain displacement is:

Options
  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as
  • s = (u+v)t/2
Answer

A

Explanation

v = u + at links velocity, acceleration and time only.

2.

The slope of a velocity–time graph gives:

Options
  • Displacement
  • Acceleration
  • Distance
  • Jerk
Answer

B

Explanation

a = dv/dt — the slope for uniform acceleration.

3.

A car at 10 m/s with a = 2 m/s² for 4 s reaches:

Options
  • 14 m/s
  • 18 m/s
  • 20 m/s
  • 28 m/s
Answer

B

Explanation

v = 10 + 2×4 = 18 m/s (NEB 2023).

4.

The distance travelled in the same time is:

Options
  • 48 m
  • 56 m
  • 64 m
  • 72 m
Answer

B

Explanation

s = 10×4 + ½×2×16 = 56 m (NEB 2023).

5.

In the graphical derivation, s = ut + ½at² comes from:

Options
  • The slope of the line
  • Rectangle plus triangle area under the v–t line
  • The intercept
  • The tangent at t = 0
Answer

B

Explanation

Area = ut (rectangle) + ½t(v−u) (triangle) = ut + ½at².

6.

To find stopping distance without the stopping time, use:

Options
  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as
  • s = (u+v)t/2
Answer

C

Explanation

The third equation omits time — ideal when t is unknown.

7.

A body thrown up has acceleration:

Options
  • Zero at the top
  • −g throughout (upward positive convention)
  • g upward
  • Increasing with time
Answer

B

Explanation

Gravity acts at every instant — a = −g with upward positive.

8.

The equations of motion apply only when:

Options
  • Velocity is constant
  • Acceleration is constant
  • Motion is circular
  • Mass is constant
Answer

B

Explanation

They are derived assuming uniform acceleration.
