Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — From Average to Instantaneous

    A car's speedometer does not show its average speed over the whole trip; it shows the velocity right now. Defining velocity and acceleration at an instant — as limits of shrinking time intervals — is the conceptual heart of kinematics, and the gateway to calculus-based physics.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    QuantityDefinitionUnitNature
    Position (r)Location relative to an origin, with directionmVector
    Displacement (Δr)Change of position: final − initialmVector
    Average velocityvav​=Δr/Δt over a finite intervalm/sVector
    Instantaneous velocityv=limΔt→0​Δr/Δt=dr/dt — velocity at an instantm/sVector
    Average accelerationaav​=Δv/Δtm/s²Vector
    Instantaneous accelerationa=limΔt→0​Δv/Δt=dv/dtm/s²Vector

    Key distinctions:

    • Speed (scalar) = path length/time; velocity (vector) = displacement/time. A body in circular motion at constant speed has continuously changing velocity.
    • A body can have zero velocity and non-zero acceleration at an instant (a ball at the top of its vertical flight: v = 0, a = g downward).
    • Negative acceleration means acceleration opposite to the chosen positive direction — not necessarily "slowing".

  • 3. Graphical Meaning

    • On a position–time graph, instantaneous velocity is the slope of the tangent at that instant (average velocity is the slope of the chord).
    • On a velocity–time graph, instantaneous acceleration is the slope of the tangent; the area under the v–t curve gives displacement.

    Worked numerical (NEB 2014, 3m): A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s. Find its acceleration and the distance covered. Given: u = 0, v = 20 m/s, t = 10 s. Formula: a=(v−u)/t; s=ut+21​at2. Substitution: a=20/10=2 m/s²; s=0+21​(2)(102)=100 m. Answer: a = 2 m/s², s = 100 m.

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 7: relative velocity is velocity measured from a moving frame.
    • To Topic 8: the equations of motion are the integrals of constant acceleration — the graphical treatment makes this visible.
    • To Vectors: velocity and acceleration are vectors; components are handled by resolution.
    • To Calculus (Maths): v=dr/dt, a=dv/dt — kinematics is applied differentiation.

    5.2 Model answers

    Q1 (2m, 2024): Position is the location of a body relative to a chosen origin, expressed with direction (a vector, in metres). Velocity is the rate of change of position — v=limΔt→0​Δr/Δt=dr/dt (m/s). Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity — a=dv/dt (m/s²). All three are vector quantities.

    Q2 (3m, 2014): see worked numerical in §3 — a = 2 m/s², s = 100 m.

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Confusing speed with velocity — speed has no direction.
    2. Saying a body at the top of vertical flight has zero acceleration — v = 0 but a = g.
    3. Using average velocity when the question asks for instantaneous velocity (tangent slope).
    4. Forgetting that acceleration is a vector — direction must be stated or implied by sign.
    5. Treating "negative acceleration" as always deceleration.

Confusion

  • Speed vs velocity: speed is the scalar path-length rate; velocity is the vector displacement rate — equal only in straight-line motion without reversal.
  • Zero velocity vs zero acceleration: at the top of a vertical throw v = 0 but a = g downward; in uniform velocity motion a = 0.

Practice

  • Can a body have constant speed and non-zero acceleration?

Universal Facts

  • A body can move with increasing speed and decreasing acceleration — acceleration measures how fast velocity changes, not how large velocity is.

Examples

  • Car from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s — a = 2 m/s², s = 100 m (NEB 2014).

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A car moving at 10 m/s accelerates uniformly at 2 m/s² for 4 s. Find the final velocity and distance travelled. — v = u + at = 10 + 2×4 = 18 m/s; s = ut + ½at² = 10×4 + ½×2×16 = 40 + 16 = 56 m (NEB 2023).

Formulas

    • v = dr/dt; a = dv/dt
    • Average: v_av = Δr/Δt; a_av = Δv/Δt
    • Uniform a: v = u + at; s = ut + ½at²

Key Points

  • Instantaneous velocity v = dr/dt (slope of x–t tangent); instantaneous acceleration a = dv/dt (slope of v–t tangent).
  • Area under v–t graph = displacement.
  • Uniform acceleration numericals: a = (v−u)/t; s = ut + ½at².

Summary

Kinematics begins with three vectors: position (location from an origin), velocity (rate of change of position, v=dr/dt) and acceleration (rate of change of velocity, a=dv/dt). Average values span a finite interval; instantaneous values are their limits as the interval shrinks — the tangent slope on a position–time graph gives instantaneous velocity, the tangent slope on a velocity–time graph gives acceleration, and the area under v–t gives displacement. Speed is velocity without direction; a body can hold zero velocity with full acceleration (top of a vertical throw). Uniform-acceleration numericals flow from a=(v−u)/t and s=ut+21​at2 — the NEB 2014 car reaching 20 m/s in 10 s gains a = 2 m/s² and s = 100 m.

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Carry units through calculus: if x is in metres and t in seconds, v is m/s and a is m/s² automatically.
  • Note 2: NEB graph questions award marks for BOTH slope reasoning and area reasoning — label which you used.
  • Note 3: When integrating acceleration, always determine integration constants from given initial conditions.
  • Note 4: In 'is the particle speeding up?' questions, compare signs of v and a at that instant — not the sign of a alone.

Important Statements

  • "The speedometer reads instantaneous speed; the odometer averages."

Important Tasks

  • Task 1: Differentiate five polynomial position functions, evaluating v and a at specified instants.
  • Task 2: Sketch x–t, v–t and a–t triplet graphs for: uniform motion, uniformly accelerated motion, and simple back-and-forth motion.
  • Task 3: Given a(t)=4t with v(0)=5 m/s and x(0)=2 m, find v(t) and x(t).
  • Task 4: Solve the NEB past question: particle at rest — find times, positions and accelerations at those times.
  • Task 5: Explain to a classmate why a speedometer shows instantaneous speed and why trip-average speed can differ, using the limit definition.

Specific Conditions

  • Limit Definition Universality: Instantaneous velocity v=dx/dt and acceleration a=dv/dt are defined as limits (Δto0). They apply to any motion — uniform or non-uniform, constant or variable acceleration. The SUVAT equations (v=u+at, etc.) are special cases valid only for constant a.
  • Graph Duality — Slopes vs Areas: On x-t graph: slope of tangent = v. On v-t graph: slope of tangent = a; area under curve = displacement. On a-t graph: area under curve = change in velocity. Mixing up slope/area is the most common graph error.
  • Sign Logic for Speeding/Slowing: Speeding up ⇔ v and a have same sign. Slowing down ⇔ v and a have opposite signs. This is true regardless of coordinate choice. At a turning point (v=0), aeq0@@KATEX11162@@v=0@@KATEX11163@@a=g).

Details

Topic

Acceleration

Details

  • Instantaneous acceleration a = dv/dt = d²x/dt²
  • Slope of the v–t tangent gives a
  • Negative a means velocity more negative — not necessarily slowing down
3.
Topic

Graph reading

Details
  • x–t slope → velocity; curvature shows acceleration
  • v–t slope → acceleration; area under v–t → displacement
  • a–t area → change in velocity
  • Straight x–t line = uniform velocity; straight v–t line = uniform acceleration
4.
Topic

Standard traps

Details
  • Zero velocity does not mean zero acceleration (a ball at its peak)
  • Zero acceleration with non-zero velocity is uniform motion
  • Displacement can be zero while distance is not
  • Direction of a need not match direction of v

MCQS

1.

Instantaneous velocity is:

Options
  • Total distance/total time
  • The limit of Δr/Δt as Δt → 0
  • Always equal to speed
  • The area under the x–t graph
Answer

B

Explanation

It is the derivative dr/dt — the tangent slope of the position–time graph.

2.

At the highest point of a ball thrown vertically upward:

Options
  • v = 0, a = 0
  • v = 0, a = g downward
  • v = g, a = 0
  • v maximum, a = g
Answer

B

Explanation

Velocity vanishes at the top but gravity keeps acting — a = 9.8 m/s² downward.

3.

The area under a velocity–time graph gives:

Options
  • Acceleration
  • Displacement
  • Jerk
  • Force
Answer

B

Explanation

∫v dt = displacement.

4.

A car goes from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s. Its acceleration is:

Options
  • 1 m/s²
  • 2 m/s²
  • 5 m/s²
  • 20 m/s²
Answer

B

Explanation

a = (20 − 0)/10 = 2 m/s² (NEB 2014).

5.

Which pair are both vectors?

Options
  • Speed and distance
  • Velocity and acceleration
  • Distance and displacement
  • Speed and acceleration
Answer

B

Explanation

Velocity and acceleration both carry direction; speed and distance are scalars.

6.

A body with negative acceleration:

Options
  • Always moves backward
  • Has acceleration opposite to the chosen positive direction
  • Must be slowing in every frame
  • Has zero velocity
Answer

B

Explanation

The sign is relative to the axis choice — 'deceleration' depends on the direction of motion.

7.

Average velocity over an interval equals the slope of:

Options
  • The tangent to the x–t graph
  • The chord of the x–t graph
  • The tangent to the v–t graph
  • The area under v–t
Answer

B

Explanation

Average velocity is Δr/Δt — the chord slope; instantaneous is the tangent.

8.

A body in uniform circular motion has:

Options
  • Constant velocity
  • Constant speed and changing velocity
  • Zero acceleration
  • Constant acceleration magnitude and direction
Answer

B

Explanation

Speed is constant but direction changes continuously — velocity changes, so acceleration exists.
