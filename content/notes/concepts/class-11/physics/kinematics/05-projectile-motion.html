Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Two Motions Wearing One Body

    A projectile is free fall with a head start: uniform horizontal velocity (a = 0) superposed on vertical free fall (a = g). Because the components are independent, the whole parabolic story unfolds from two one-dimensional problems sharing one clock.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Projectile launched at angle θ with speed u (level ground)

    QuantityFormula
    Horizontal componentux​=ucosθ (constant throughout)
    Vertical componentuy​=usinθ (changes by g)
    Time to maximum heighttup​=usinθ/g
    Time of flightT=2usinθ/g
    Maximum heightH=u2sin2θ/2g
    Horizontal rangeR=u2sin2θ/g
    Trajectoryparabola: y=xtanθ−gx2/2u2cos2θ

    Key results:

    • R is maximum at θ = 45° (Rmax​=u2/g).
    • Complementary angles (θ and 90° − θ) give the same range — R(30°)=R(60°); ratio 1:1.
    • Range ratio for two angles: R1​/R2​=sin2θ1​/sin2θ2​.

    2.2 Horizontal projection from a height

    A body projected horizontally with speed u from height h:

    • Time of flight: t=2h/g​ (depends only on h).
    • Horizontal range: R=u2h/g​.
    • Velocity on striking: v=u2+2gh​ at angle tanα=2gh​/u below horizontal.

    2.3 Vertical projection

    T=2u/g, H=u2/2g (Topic 9's results).

  • 3. Worked Numericals

    Q1 (3m, 2024): A body is projected with velocity 20 m/s at 30° to the horizontal. Find its maximum height and horizontal range. (g = 10 m/s²) Given: u = 20 m/s, θ = 30°. Substitution: H=u2sin2θ/2g=400×0.25/20=5 m. R=u2sin2θ/g=400×sin60°/10=40×0.866=34.6 m. Answer: H = 5 m, R ≈ 34.6 m.

    Q2 (5m, 2022): A projectile is fired horizontally from a tower 80 m high with velocity 20 m/s. Find (i) time of flight, (ii) horizontal range, (iii) velocity on striking the ground. (g = 10 m/s²) Substitution: (i) t=2h/g​=16​=4 s. (ii) R=ut=20×4=80 m. (iii) vy​=gt=40 m/s; v=u2+vy2​​=400+1600​=2000​=44.7 m/s at tanα=40/20=2→α=63.4° below horizontal. Answer: t = 4 s, R = 80 m, v ≈ 44.7 m/s at 63.4° below horizontal.

    Q3 (4m, 2022): A stone is projected vertically upwards with 49 m/s (g = 9.8). Find time to maximum height, maximum height, total time. tup​=u/g=5 s; H=u2/2g=122.5 m; T=2u/g=10 s.

  • 4. Derivation asked at NEB (2019, 2022)

    Time of flight is double the time to maximum height (3m, 2019): At maximum height the vertical velocity vanishes: 0=usinθ−gtup​→tup​=usinθ/g. The projectile lands when the vertical displacement returns to zero: 0=usinθT−21​gT2→T=2usinθ/g. Hence T = 2 t_up — the flight is symmetric about the highest point.

    Range is the same for complementary angles (3m, 2022): R=gu2sin2θ​. For θ and (90° − θ): sin2(90°−θ)=sin(180°−2θ)=sin2θ — identical. So R(30°)=R(60°); the ratio of ranges for 30° and 60° is 1:1.

  • 5. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 4 (resolution): the whole topic is resolution in action — ucosθ and usinθ start two independent problems.
    • To Topic 9: the vertical half is free fall; the horizontal half is uniform velocity.
    • To Energy: speed at any height follows from energy conservation — v2=u2−2gy.
    • To real life: ballistics, water fountains, javelin throws and fire hoses all obey R=u2sin2θ/g.

Confusion

  • Angle projection vs horizontal projection: angled launch uses T = 2u sinθ/g; horizontal launch from a height uses t = √(2h/g) — different formulas for different setups.

Formulas

    • uₓ = u cos θ; u_y = u sin θ
    • T = 2u sin θ/g; H = u² sin²θ/2g; R = u² sin 2θ/g

Key Points

  • Components: u cos θ constant horizontal; u sin θ vertical under g.
  • T = 2u sinθ/g; H = u²sin²θ/2g; R = u²sin2θ/g; R max at 45°.
  • Complementary angles (θ, 90°−θ) give equal ranges — 30° and 60° ratio 1:1.
  • Horizontal projection: t = √(2h/g), R = ut, v = √(u² + 2gh).

Summary

A projectile runs two independent motions on one clock: uniform horizontal velocity ucosθ and vertical free fall from usinθ. For an angled launch on level ground, the time of flight is T=2usinθ/g (exactly twice the time to the top — NEB 2019), the maximum height is H=u2sin2θ/2g, and the range is R=u2sin2θ/g — maximal at 45° and identical for complementary angles, so 30° and 60° ranges stand in ratio 1:1 (NEB 2022). Horizontal projection from a tower is the mirror case: t=2h/g​ sets the clock (4 s for 80 m), range R=ut, and impact speed u2+2gh​ at 63.4° below horizontal (NEB 2022, 5m). NEB 2024's 20 m/s at 30° lands at H = 5 m, R = 34.6 m.

Special Notes

  • Note 1: In horizontal-launch problems, get the time from the VERTICAL fall first, then multiply by vx​.
  • Note 2: Memorise sin37°=0.6, cos37°=0.8 and sin2θ values for 30°/45°/60° — NEB numericals use them constantly.
  • Note 3: 'Same range' pairs: compare heights as sin2θ:sin2(90°−θ) — the steeper shot rises 3× higher for the 30°/60° pair.
  • Note 4: State the assumptions (no air resistance, constant g, flat ground) in theory answers — they define the model.

Specific Conditions

  • Independence of Components: Horizontal motion (constant vx​=ucosheta) and vertical motion (free fall vy​=usinheta−gt) are completely independent. They share only the time variable t. This is the foundational principle — no horizontal force means vx​ is constant.
  • Trajectory is a Parabola: Eliminating t gives y = x an heta - rac{g x^2}{2u^2\cos^2 heta}, a downward-opening parabola. This assumes no air resistance, constant g, and flat ground. With drag, the trajectory is not a parabola and range decreases.
  • Symmetry and Range Maxima: Time of flight T = rac{2u\sin heta}{g}, max height H = rac{u^2\sin^2 heta}{2g}, range R = rac{u^2\sin 2 heta}{g}. Maximum range at heta=45∘. Complementary angles (heta and 90∘−heta) give identical ranges because sin2heta=sin(180∘−2heta). Speed at launch = speed at landing (symmetry).
