Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Cleanest Laboratory in Physics

    Drop anything near Earth's surface and, ignoring air resistance, it falls with the same constant acceleration g ≈ 9.8 m/s² — regardless of mass. Free fall is the equations of motion with a fixed a, and its sign bookkeeping (up positive or down positive) is the examinable skill.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    • Free fall: motion under gravity alone (no air resistance, no thrust).
    • g ≈ 9.8 m/s² (often taken as 10 m/s² in NEB numericals) directed downward everywhere near the surface; independent of mass, size, material (Galileo's result).
    • Sign convention: choose one positive direction and keep it. With up positive: a = −g; with down positive: a = +g.

    Worked numerical (NEB 2023, 3m): A stone is dropped from a height of 200 m. Calculate (i) the time to hit the ground, (ii) the velocity on impact. (g = 10 m/s²) Given: u = 0, h = 200 m (down positive), a = g = 10 m/s². Substitution: (i) h=21​gt2→200=5t2→t2=40→t=6.32 s. (ii) v=gt=10×6.32=63.2 m/s (or v=2gh​=4000​=63.2 m/s). Answer: t ≈ 6.32 s, v ≈ 63.2 m/s downward.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 8: free fall is s=ut+21​at2 with a = g — the same three equations.
    • To Topic 10: projectile motion is free fall superposed on uniform horizontal motion.
    • To Energy (Unit 5): conservation of energy verified for a freely falling body — mgh converts to ½mv² (NEB 2020 asks exactly this).
    • To Gravitation (Unit 8): g's value and its variation with altitude/depth are gravitation topics; here g is a constant.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Mixing sign conventions mid-problem — pick up-positive or down-positive and hold it.
    2. Using v = u + at with u = 0 forgotten for dropped bodies.
    3. Giving the impact velocity without its direction.
    4. Forgetting that mass never enters free-fall kinematics (no air resistance).
    5. Confusing time of flight (2u/g) with time to maximum height (u/g).

Confusion

  • Dropped vs thrown down: dropped means u = 0; thrown down means u ≠ 0 downward — different v and t.
  • Time to top vs time of flight: t_up = u/g; total flight T = 2u/g — double, by symmetry.

Practice

    • Thrown up: t_up = u/g; h_max = u²/2g; T = 2u/g

Key Points

  • Free fall: a = g ≈ 9.8 (or 10) m/s² downward, independent of mass.
  • Dropped: h = ½gt², v = √(2gh). Thrown up: t_up = u/g, h_max = u²/2g, T = 2u/g.
  • Fix one sign convention and keep it through the whole problem.

Summary

Free fall strips motion to its essence: constant acceleration g ≈ 9.8 m/s² downward, independent of mass. With one sign convention held throughout, the equations of motion specialise: a dropped body falls h=21​gt2 and hits at v=2gh​ — a stone from 200 m takes 6.32 s and lands at 63.2 m/s (NEB 2023, g = 10). A body thrown up with speed u rises for u/g seconds to hmax​=u2/2g and returns after T=2u/g with equal and opposite velocity. Mass never enters; air resistance is the only spoiler. The same motion verifies conservation of energy — mgh becoming ½mv² — and is the vertical half of every projectile.

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Take g=9.8 m/s² unless the question says 10 — and say which you used.
  • Note 2: For a body thrown up, do not put t=T/2 anywhere except the top; the position equation is valid for the whole flight.
  • Note 3: Impact-speed questions: v=2gh​ works even when t is unknown — fastest route.
  • Note 4: Two-body fall problems: write y1​(t) and y2​(t) with one origin; separation is their difference — accelerations add no relative change if both are g.

Important Concepts

  • Mass never appears: in vacuum a feather and a hammer fall identically — Apollo 15 proved it on the Moon.

Important Tasks

  • Task 1: Solve five thrown-up problems: hmax​, tup​, T, and return speed, all from u and g.
  • Task 2: Prove ascent time = descent time twice — once by equations, once by the v–t graph symmetry.
  • Task 3: Solve a mixed case: thrown DOWN from a height with initial speed (find t and v at ground).
  • Task 4: Solve a lift problem (coin dropped in an ascending/descending lift) in both frames.
  • Task 5: Derive h=21​gt2 from the v–t area for a dropped body and verify the 1:3:5 pattern for n=1,2,3.

Specific Conditions

  • Universality of Free Fall: All bodies fall with the same acceleration g≈9.8extm/s2 in vacuum (no air resistance). This is a consequence of the equivalence principle — inertial mass equals gravitational mass. The Apollo 15 hammer-feather drop on the Moon confirmed this.
  • Sign Convention Discipline: Choose one positive direction (e.g., up = +) and use it consistently. For up-positive: a=−g, v=u−gt, h = ut - rac{1}{2}gt^2. The sign of g goes into the equation ONCE — never mix conventions.
  • Up-Down Symmetry (No Air Resistance): Time up = time down = u/g. Velocity at equal heights going up and down are equal in magnitude, opposite in direction. At the top, v=0 but a=g downward — zero velocity does NOT mean zero acceleration.

A stone dropped from 200 m (g = 10 m/s²) hits the ground after about:

  • 6.3 s

  • 20 s
Answer

B

t = √(2h/g) = √40 = 6.32 s (NEB 2023).

2.

Its impact speed is:

Options
  • 40 m/s
  • 63.2 m/s
  • 100 m/s
  • 200 m/s
Answer

B

Explanation

v = √(2gh) = √4000 = 63.2 m/s.

3.

The time to reach maximum height for u thrown up is:

Options
  • u/g
  • 2u/g
  • g/u
  • u²/2g
Answer

A

Explanation

v = u − gt = 0 → t_up = u/g.

4.

The total time of flight is:

Options
  • u/g
  • 2u/g
  • u²/g
  • √(2u/g)
Answer

B

Explanation

By symmetry the fall takes the same time: T = 2u/g.

5.

Two bodies of different masses dropped together in vacuum:

Options
  • Heavier lands first
  • Lighter lands first
  • Land together
  • Never land
Answer

C

Explanation

Free-fall acceleration is mass-independent — Galileo's principle.

6.

With up chosen positive, a freely falling body has acceleration:

Options
  • +g
  • −g
  • 0
  • g/2
Answer

B

Explanation

Acceleration points downward — negative in the up-positive convention.

7.

The maximum height of a body thrown up at u is:

Options
  • u²/g
  • u²/2g
  • 2u²/g
  • u/2g
Answer

B

Explanation

v² = u² − 2gh at the top → h_max = u²/2g.

8.

A ball returns to the thrower with velocity:

Options
  • u, same direction
  • −u (equal magnitude, opposite direction)
  • Zero
  • 2u
Answer

B

Explanation

Symmetric flight returns it with equal speed downward.
