Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Surprisingly Slow Electron March

    Flip a switch and the lamp lights instantly — yet the electrons themselves drift at less than a millimetre per second. Current is not speed but charge per second: I = neAv_d links the micro-world of drifting electrons to the amperes of a circuit.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Electric current

    Current is the rate of flow of electric charge: I=tQ​SI unit: ampere (A) = C/s

    • Conventional current flows in the direction positive charges would move (opposite to electron motion).
    • Current is a scalar with direction convention — it adds algebraically at junctions.

    2.2 Drift velocity

    Free electrons in a conductor move randomly at ~10⁶ m/s, but under an applied field they acquire a slow average velocity along the wire — the drift velocity v_d (typically ~10⁻⁴ m/s, a fraction of a millimetre per second). Collisions with the lattice constantly reset the motion; the field only tilts the average.

    2.3 The relation I = neAv_d (NEB 2024, 3m)

    Consider a wire of cross-section A, carrier density n (electrons per m³), drift speed v_d. In time t, all electrons within length v_d·t of a cross-section pass through it:

    • Number: N=nAvd​t
    • Charge: Q=Ne=neAvd​t

    I=tQ​=neAvd​​

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): A copper wire (n = 8.5×10²⁸ /m³) of cross-section 1 mm² carries 2 A. Find v_d. vd​=neAI​=8.5×1028×1.6×10−19×10−62​≈1.5×10−4 m/s — about 0.5 km/h: the signal travels near light speed, the electrons barely crawl.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 99: the drift motion meets lattice resistance — Ohm's law's microscopic origin.
    • To current density: J = I/A = nev_d, a vector version.
    • To fields: the driving field E inside the wire relates to v_d via mobility: v_d = μE.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Confusing drift speed with random thermal speed (~10⁶ m/s).
    2. Forgetting to convert mm² to m² (1 mm² = 10⁻⁶ m²).
    3. Saying current "is" electron speed — current is charge flow rate.

Confusion

  • Drift speed ≠ signal speed: electrons crawl at ~0.1 mm/s, but the field (and the lamp) responds at near light speed.
  • Current is a scalar with a direction convention — not a true vector like force.

Practice

  • Why does a lamp light immediately if electrons drift so slowly?

Universal Facts

  • The random thermal speed of electrons (~10⁶ m/s) is a trillion times the drift speed — the drift is a tiny bias on chaos.

Formulas

Key Points

Summary

Electric current is the rate of charge flow, I = Q/t, measured in amperes (C/s), flowing (by convention) as positive charge would — opposite to electron motion. The electrons themselves drift: random thermal motion at ~10⁶ m/s acquires a tiny average drift velocity v_d (~10⁻⁴ m/s) along the wire. Counting carriers crossing a section in time t gives the key relation I=neAvd​ (NEB 2024, 3m): carrier density n × charge e × area A × drift speed. A 1 mm² copper wire carrying 2 A has v_d ≈ 0.15 mm/s — the lamp lights instantly because the field propagates near light speed, not the electrons.
