Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Why Batteries Sag Under Load

    A battery's label promises 1.5 V or 12 V, but the voltage you measure drops when it works hard. The hidden culprit is internal resistance — and Kirchhoff's laws are the tools that track every volt and every ampere around a real circuit.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2024)

    2.1 EMF and terminal voltage

    • EMF (E): the work done per unit charge by the source in driving charge around the whole circuit — the open-circuit terminal voltage.
    • Terminal voltage (V): the actual potential difference across the terminals when current I flows:

    V=E−Ir — the internal drop Ir is "lost" inside the source. (When charging, V = E + Ir.)

    2.2 Kirchhoff's laws (NEB 2022, 2024, 5m)

    1. Current law (KCL): the algebraic sum of currents at a junction is zero — conservation of charge.
    2. Voltage law (KVL): the algebraic sum of emfs round any closed loop equals the sum of IR drops — conservation of energy.

    Application (NEB 2024): a battery of emf 5 V with internal resistance 2 Ω drives a 5 Ω external resistor. Single loop: E = I(R + r) → I = 5/(5 + 2) = 0.71 A.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (3m, 2024): A cell of emf 2 V and internal resistance 0.5 Ω is connected to a 3.5 Ω resistor. Calculate the terminal voltage.

    • I=R+rE​=3.5+0.52​=0.5 A
    • V=E−Ir=2−(0.5)(0.5)= 1.75 V (equivalently V = IR = 0.5 × 3.5 = 1.75 V ✓)

    Q (NEB-style): A 12 V battery with r = 0.5 Ω delivers 10 A to a starter motor. Find the terminal voltage. V = 12 − 10 × 0.5 = 7 V — the dimming headlights when starting a car.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 101: Kirchhoff's laws generalise the series/parallel rules to any network.
    • To Topic 102: the potentiometer reads E directly by avoiding the Ir drop.
    • To Topic 104: the internal drop wastes power as heat inside the battery.

Specific Conditions

  • EMF vs Terminal Voltage: EMF E is the open-circuit terminal voltage (I=0). Terminal voltage V=E−Ir drops as current increases due to internal resistance.
  • Internal Resistance Effects: Internal resistance causes voltage drop (Ir), power loss (I2r), and heating inside the source. It limits maximum deliverable current and power.
  • Maximum Power Transfer: Maximum power is delivered to load when load resistance equals internal resistance (R=r). This is important for matching circuits but not for efficiency (efficiency is only 50% at this point).

Formulas

    • V = E − Ir (discharge); V = E + Ir (charging)
    • I = E/(R + r)
    • KCL: ΣI = 0 at a junction; KVL: ΣE = ΣIR in a loop

Key Points

  • V = E − Ir; I = E/(R + r); KCL junctions, KVL loops.

Important Statements

  • "Kirchhoff's voltage law: the algebraic sum of the emfs in a closed loop equals the algebraic sum of the potential drops."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: Kirchhoff + circuit (2022, 2024 — 5m each); terminal voltage (2024, 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "I = E/(R + r); V = IR = E − Ir — compute both ways as a check."

Exam Notes

  • State the conservation principle behind each Kirchhoff law — examiners look for it.

Examples

  • NEB 2024 — E = 2 V, r = 0.5 Ω, R = 3.5 Ω: V = 1.75 V.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): E = 9 V, r = 1 Ω, R = 8 Ω. Find I, V and the internal drop. — I = 1 A; V = 8 V; internal drop = 1 V (dissipated inside the battery).

Practice

  • Why does a battery feel warm after heavy use?

Universal Facts

  • Car headlights dim at starting because the starter's huge current makes Ir eat the battery's emf.

Confusion

  • EMF ≠ terminal voltage: V = E − Ir once current flows; they coincide only when I = 0.
  • KCL conserves charge; KVL conserves energy — quote both physics reasons.

1.

The terminal voltage of a cell supplying current is:

Options
  • Equal to EMF
  • Less than EMF
  • Greater than EMF
  • Zero
Answer

B

Explanation

V=E−Ir, so terminal voltage is less than EMF when current flows.

2.

Internal resistance of a cell causes:

Options
  • Increase in terminal voltage
  • Decrease in terminal voltage
  • No effect on terminal voltage
  • Increase in EMF
Answer

B

Explanation

Internal resistance causes voltage drop Ir, reducing terminal voltage below EMF.

3.

Cells in parallel have equivalent:

Options
  • EMF added, r added
  • EMF same, r divided by n
  • EMF divided by n, r same
  • EMF same, r multiplied by n
Answer

B

Explanation

Parallel identical cells: Eeq​=E, req​=r/n.

4.

Maximum power is delivered to load when:

Options
  • R = 0
  • R = r
  • R >> r
  • R << r
Answer

B

Explanation

Maximum power transfer theorem: R=r for max power to load.

5.

Open circuit terminal voltage equals:

Options
  • Zero
  • EMF
  • Less than EMF
  • Greater than EMF
Answer

B

Explanation

When I=0, V=E−0=E.

Summary

A real source has internal resistance r. Its emf E is the work per unit charge driving charge around the whole circuit (the open-circuit voltage); the terminal voltage under current I is V=E−Ir — the internal drop is lost inside. Kirchhoff's laws organise any circuit: KCL (ΣI = 0 at a junction — charge conservation) and KVL (ΣE = ΣIR round a loop — energy conservation). NEB 2022 and 2024 both asked the 5m statement-plus-application: 5 V emf, 2 Ω internal, 5 Ω load → I = 5/7 ≈ 0.71 A. The 2024 3m numerical: E = 2 V, r = 0.5 Ω, R = 3.5 Ω gives I = 0.5 A and V = 1.75 V.
