Notes

  • EMF Definition: Electromotive force (EMF, E) is the energy supplied by a source per unit charge to move charge through the complete circuit. It is NOT a force but a potential difference measured in volts. E=W/q where W is work done by the source and q is charge moved.
  • Terminal Potential Difference: When current I flows, the terminal voltage V across the source terminals is V=E−Ir, where r is internal resistance. The voltage drop Ir inside the source is called lost volts.
  • Internal Resistance: Every real source (cell, battery, generator) has internal resistance r due to electrolyte resistance, electrode resistance, and chemical factors. It causes the terminal voltage to drop below EMF when current is drawn.
  • Open Circuit: When I=0 (open circuit), V=E — the terminal voltage equals the EMF since there is no internal voltage drop.
  • Short Circuit: When terminals are shorted (R=0), I=E/r — maximum current flows, limited only by internal resistance. This can damage the source.
  • Cells in Series: For n identical cells each of EMF E and internal resistance r in series: total EMF =nE, total internal resistance =nr, equivalent V=nE−Inr.
  • Cells in Parallel: For n identical cells each of EMF E and internal resistance r in parallel: equivalent EMF =E, equivalent internal resistance =r/n (current capacity increases).
  • Mixed Grouping: Cells in series-parallel combinations balance voltage and current requirements for specific applications.
  • Determination of EMF and Internal Resistance: Measure terminal voltage V for different currents I. Plot V vs I: y-intercept =E, slope =−r. Alternatively use a potentiometer for accurate EMF measurement.
  • Power Delivered: Power delivered to external load R is P=I2R=(E/(R+r))2R. Maximum power transfer occurs when R=r, with Pmax​=E2/4r.

Specific Conditions

  • EMF vs Terminal Voltage: EMF E is the open-circuit terminal voltage (I=0). Terminal voltage V=E−Ir drops as current increases due to internal resistance.
  • Internal Resistance Effects: Internal resistance causes voltage drop (Ir), power loss (I2r), and heating inside the source. It limits maximum deliverable current and power.
  • Maximum Power Transfer: Maximum power is delivered to load when load resistance equals internal resistance (R=r). This is important for matching circuits but not for efficiency (efficiency is only 50% at this point).

Formulas

  • EMF: E=W/q
  • Terminal voltage: V=E−Ir
  • Lost volts: Vlost​=Ir
  • Circuit current: I=E/(R+r)
  • Cells in series: Eeq​=nE, req​=nr
  • Cells in parallel: Eeq​=E, req​=r/n
  • Power to load: P=I2R=(E/(R+r))2R
  • Maximum power condition: R=r
  • Maximum power: Pmax​=E2/4r

Key Points

  • EMF is energy per unit charge supplied by the source; measured in volts.
  • Terminal voltage V=E−Ir; internal drop Ir is called lost volts.
  • Open circuit: V=E; short circuit: I=E/r.
  • Cells in series add EMF and internal resistance; parallel cells reduce effective internal resistance.
  • Maximum power transfer when external resistance equals internal resistance (R=r).

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: EMF is the energy supplied by a source per unit charge circulated through the complete circuit.
  • Statement 2: Terminal potential difference is V=E−Ir, where r is internal resistance.
  • Statement 3: Internal resistance causes voltage drop and power loss inside real sources.
  • Statement 4: Cells in series: total EMF adds, total internal resistance adds. Cells in parallel: EMF unchanged, internal resistance divides.
  • Statement 5: Maximum power is delivered to load when R=r (load resistance equals internal resistance).

Important Notes

  • EMF is measured in open circuit (I=0); terminal voltage is measured under load (I=0).
  • Internal resistance increases as a cell ages or discharges — old batteries have higher r and lower terminal voltage under load.
  • Short-circuit current I=E/r can be very large and damage the source — never short a battery.
  • EMF is not a force despite the name — it is energy per unit charge (volts).

Exam Short Tricks

  • Open circuit: terminal voltage = EMF. Closed circuit: terminal voltage = EMF - Ir.
  • Series cells: EMFs add, internal resistances add. Parallel cells: EMF same, internal resistance divides.
  • Max power transfer: load R = internal r. Efficiency at max power = 50%.
  • Plot V vs I: y-intercept = EMF, slope magnitude = internal resistance r.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks to derive V=E−Ir and explain internal resistance effects.
  • Numericals on cells in series/parallel are common — calculate equivalent EMF and internal resistance.
  • Maximum power transfer condition (R=r) is a favourite conceptual question.
  • Potentiometer method for measuring EMF accurately (no current drawn from cell).

Examples

  • A cell has EMF 1.5 V and internal resistance 0.5Ω. Connected to a 4Ω resistor: I=1.5/(4+0.5)=0.333 A, terminal V=1.5−0.333×0.5=1.33 V. Lost volts = 0.17 V.
  • Three cells of EMF 2 V each and r=0.2Ω each in series: Etotal​=6 V, rtotal​=0.6Ω.
  • Three identical cells (E=1.5 V, r=0.3Ω) in parallel: Eeq​=1.5 V, req​=0.1Ω.
  • A battery of EMF 12 V and r=0.5Ω delivers max power when R=0.5Ω. Pmax​=122/(4×0.5)=72 W.

  • Determine equivalent EMF and internal resistance for series/parallel cell combinations.
  • Apply maximum power transfer condition (R=r) to circuit problems.

Universal Facts

  • EMF is not a force — despite the name, it is energy supplied per unit charge (volts).
  • A real cell's internal resistance increases as it discharges — old batteries sag under load.
  • Maximum power transfer occurs at R=r but efficiency is only 50% — for efficiency, use R≫r.
  • The potential difference across a cell being charged is V=E+Ir (charging current adds to EMF).
  • Potentiometer measures true EMF because it draws no current from the cell at balance point.

Confusion

  • Confusing EMF with terminal voltage: EMF is open-circuit voltage; terminal voltage drops under load due to Ir.
  • Thinking EMF is a force: EMF is energy per unit charge (volts), not a mechanical force.
  • Believing parallel cells add EMF: Parallel identical cells have same EMF as one cell; only current capacity increases.
  • Assuming max power = max efficiency: At R=r, power is max but efficiency is only 50%.
  • Ignoring internal resistance: Real sources always have internal resistance affecting terminal voltage.
