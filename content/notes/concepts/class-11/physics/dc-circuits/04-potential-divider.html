Notes

  • Potential Divider: This topic covers the fundamental concepts of potential divider in the physics curriculum.
  • Understanding potential divider is essential for solving problems and building higher-level mathematical skills.
  • Key definitions and theorems related to potential divider should be memorized and practiced regularly.
  • Definition: two or more resistors in series across a voltage source, used to obtain a fraction of the source voltage.
  • Output voltage: Vout​=Vin​\ racR2​R1​+R2​, where R2​ is the resistor across which the output is taken.
  • Why useful: allows you to set any voltage between 0 and Vin​ by adjusting the ratio of resistances.
  • Loading effect: if a load resistor RL​ is connected across R2​, the output voltage changes because R2​ is now in parallel with RL​, reducing the effective resistance.
  • Potentiometer: a continuous potential divider using a uniform resistance wire. The output voltage is proportional to the length of wire between the wiper and one end.
  • Voltage divider rule: for n resistors in series, the voltage across resistor Ri​ is Vi​=Vtotal​cdotRi​/Rtotal​.
  • Unloaded divider: when no current is drawn from the output, the output voltage is exactly Vin​cdotR2​/(R1​+R2​).
  • Loaded divider analysis: with load RL​ across R2​, the effective resistance of the lower branch becomes R2′​=R2​RL​/(R2​+RL​). The new output is Vout​=Vin​cdotR2′​/(R1​+R2′​).
  • Design rule: to minimize loading effects, choose R1​,R2​llRL​ so the divider draws much more current than the load.
  • Potentiometer as voltage source: a potentiometer can provide any fraction of the input voltage from 0 to Vin​. The three terminals are the two ends of the resistive track and the sliding wiper.
  • Current through the divider: even with no load, the divider draws current I=Vin​/(R1​+R2​), which causes power dissipation P=Vin2​/(R1​+R2​).

Confusion

  • Carefully distinguish between similar-looking concepts in potential divider.
  • Pay attention to the conditions under which formulas and theorems apply.
  • Practice with multiple examples to solidify your understanding.
  • The output voltage of a potential divider changes when a load is connected. The simple formula Vout​=Vin​cdotR2​/(R1​+R2​) is only valid for the unloaded case.
  • A potential divider should not be used as a voltage source for high-current loads because the output voltage drops significantly. Use a voltage regulator or op-amp buffer instead.
  • The potential divider formula does not depend on the total voltage - only the ratio of resistances matters. Doubling all resistor values keeps the same output voltage but halves the current draw.
  • When using a potentiometer as a voltage divider, connecting the output to a load reduces the voltage because the load is in parallel with the lower portion of the potentiometer.
  • The power rating of the resistors in a potential divider matters. If R1​=R2​=1, extkOmega across 12 V, each dissipates P=V2/R=144/1000=0.144 W. Standard 0.25 W resistors are adequate.

Universal Facts

  • This topic is a cornerstone of the physics curriculum and appears consistently in examinations.
  • Understanding potential divider builds the foundation for more advanced topics in mathematics.
  • Real-world applications of potential divider span engineering, physics, economics, and computer science.
  • The potential divider is one of the most useful circuits in electronics. It appears in volume controls, sensor interfaces, bias networks for transistors, and reference voltage generation.
  • A potentiometer used as a voltage divider has three terminals: two fixed ends and one sliding contact. The sliding contact divides the total resistance in any ratio.
  • The loading effect of a potential divider is minimized when the divider resistance is much smaller than the load resistance, but this increases power consumption. There is always a trade-off.
  • Potentiometers can also measure unknown voltages or internal resistances with high accuracy because at the balancing point, no current flows through the galvanometer.
  • A voltage divider using equal resistors (R1​=R2​) produces exactly half the input voltage, regardless of the values chosen. This is the simplest potential divider.

Specific Conditions

  • No-Load Assumption: The simple voltage divider formula Vout​=Vin​R1​+R2​R2​​ assumes the output current is zero (infinite load resistance). Any load drawing current changes the effective resistance of the lower branch.
  • Loading Effect Formalism: When a load RL​ is connected across R2​, the effective resistance becomes R2′​=R2​∥RL​=R2​+RL​R2​RL​​. The new output voltage is Vout​=Vin​R1​+R2′​R2′​​.
  • Design Trade-off: To minimize loading effects, the divider resistance (R1​+R2​) should be much smaller than RL​. However, this increases power dissipation (P=Vin2​/(R1​+R2​)). The designer must balance voltage stability against power efficiency.
