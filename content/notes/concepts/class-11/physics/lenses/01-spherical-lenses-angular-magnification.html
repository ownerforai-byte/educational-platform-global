Notes

  • Spherical Lens: A lens is a transparent medium bounded by two spherical surfaces (or one plane and one spherical). Convex lens (converging) is thicker at center; concave lens (diverging) is thinner at center.
  • Lens Maker's Formula: f1​=(μ−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​) where μ is refractive index of lens material relative to medium, R1​ and R2​ are radii of curvature (Cartesian sign convention).
  • Lens Formula: v1​−u1​=f1​ using sign convention (u negative for object on left). Magnification m=v/u=hi​/ho​.
  • Power of Lens: P=1/f (f in metres). Unit: dioptre (D). Convex lens has positive power; concave has negative power. Lenses in contact: P=P1​+P2​.
  • Combination of Lenses: Two lenses separated by distance d: equivalent focal length F1​=f1​1​+f2​1​−f1​f2​d​. Power: P=P1​+P2​−dP1​P2​.
  • Angular Magnification: For a simple magnifier, angular magnification M=θi​/θo​. When image at near point: M=1+D/f; when image at infinity: M=D/f, where D=25 cm (least distance of distinct vision).
  • Compound Microscope: Total magnification M=mo​×me​=(vo​/uo​)(1+D/fe​) (final image at near point) or (vo​/uo​)(D/fe​) (at infinity).
  • Astronomical Telescope: Angular magnification M=fo​/fe​ (normal adjustment, final at infinity) or M=(fo​/fe​)(1+fe​/D) (final at near point). Length L=fo​+fe​.

Specific Conditions

  • Sign Convention: Light travels left to right. Distances measured from optical centre. Object distance u is negative (object on left), focal length f positive for convex, negative for concave.
  • Thin Lens Approximation: Lens formulas assume thin lens (thickness negligible compared to radii and object/image distances).
  • Paraxial Rays: Formulas valid for rays close to principal axis making small angles.

Formulas

  • Lens maker: f1​=(μ−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​)
  • Lens formula: v1​−u1​=f1​
  • Magnification: m=v/u=hi​/ho​
  • Power: P=1/f (dioptre, f in m)
  • Lenses in contact: P=P1​+P2​
  • Angular magnification (image at D): M=1+D/f
  • Angular magnification (image at infinity): M=D/f
  • Compound microscope: M=(vo​/uo​)(1+D/fe​)
  • Telescope: M=fo​/fe​ (normal adjustment)

Key Points

  • Convex lens converges; concave lens diverges.
  • Lens maker's formula relates f to μ, R₁, R₂.
  • Power P = 1/f in dioptres; positive for convex, negative for concave.
  • Angular magnification: M = 1 + D/f (near point), M = D/f (infinity).
  • Telescope magnification: M = fₒ/fₑ.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Lens maker's formula: f1​=(μ−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​).
  • Statement 2: Lens formula: v1​−u1​=f1​ with sign convention.
  • Statement 3: Power of lens P=1/f measured in dioptres (D).
  • Statement 4: Angular magnification of simple magnifier: M=1+D/f (near point), M=D/f (infinity).
  • Statement 5: Astronomical telescope magnification: M=fo​/fe​ in normal adjustment.

Important Notes

  • Always apply sign convention: u negative for real object, f positive for convex lens.
  • D = 25 cm is the standard least distance of distinct vision for normal eye.
  • For lenses in contact, powers add directly: P = P₁ + P₂.
  • Real images are inverted (m negative); virtual images are erect (m positive).

Exam Short Tricks

  • Convex lens: f positive, P positive. Concave lens: f negative, P negative.
  • For magnifier: image at D → M = 1 + D/f; image at ∞ → M = D/f.
  • Telescope: M = fₒ/fₑ — long objective, short eyepiece for high magnification.
  • Lenses in contact: add powers, not focal lengths.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks for lens maker's formula derivation and lens formula numericals.
  • Simple magnifier and telescope magnification are standard exam topics.
  • Power of lens and combination of lenses are common short-answer questions.
  • Sign convention errors lose marks — always state it clearly.

Examples

  • A convex lens has f = 20 cm. Power P = 1/0.20 = 5 D.
  • Lens with μ = 1.5, R₁ = +30 cm, R₂ = -30 cm (biconvex): 1/f = (1.5-1)(1/30 - 1/(-30)) = 0.5 × 2/30 = 1/30, so f = 30 cm.
  • Simple magnifier f = 5 cm. Image at D = 25 cm: M = 1 + 25/5 = 6. Image at infinity: M = 25/5 = 5.
  • Telescope fₒ = 100 cm, fₑ = 5 cm: M = 100/5 = 20×, length L = 105 cm.

Universal Facts

  • The human eye has a variable-focus lens (accommodation) and forms images on the retina.
  • Cameras use convex lens systems to focus light onto sensors/film.
  • Galileo's telescope used a convex objective and concave eyepiece; Kepler's design uses two convex lenses.
  • The magnifying power depends on the angle subtended at the eye, not the size of the image on the retina.

Confusion

  • Confusing lens formula sign: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f vs mirror formula 1/v + 1/u = 1/f.
  • Thinking magnification m = v/u applies to angular magnification: Linear m = v/u, angular M = θ'/θ — different concepts.
  • Believing power adds for separated lenses: Powers add only for lenses in contact (d = 0).
  • Using D = 25 cm for all eyes: D varies with age and individual (near point changes).

1.

Power of a lens of focal length 50 cm is:

Options
  • 2 D
  • 0.5 D
  • 5 D
  • 0.2 D
Answer

A

Explanation

P = 1/f = 1/0.5 m = 2 D.

2.

Lens maker's formula depends on:

Options
  • Refractive index only
  • Radii of curvature only
  • Refractive index and radii of curvature
  • Focal length only
Answer

C

Explanation

1/f = (μ-1)(1/R₁ - 1/R₂) — depends on μ, R₁, R₂.

3.

Angular magnification of simple magnifier when image at near point:

Options
  • D/f
  • 1 + D/f
  • f/D
  • 1 - D/f
Answer

B

Explanation

M = 1 + D/f when final image at least distance of distinct vision D.

4.

Astronomical telescope magnification in normal adjustment:

Options
  • fₑ/fₒ
  • fₒ/fₑ
  • fₒ + fₑ
  • fₒ × fₑ
Answer

B

Explanation

M = fₒ/fₑ for telescope in normal adjustment (final image at infinity).

5.

For two lenses in contact, equivalent power:

Options
  • P₁ × P₂
  • P₁ + P₂
  • P₁ - P₂
  • P₁/P₂
Answer

B

Explanation

Powers add for lenses in contact: P = P₁ + P₂.

Summary
