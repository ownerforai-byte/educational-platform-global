Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Lens Formula

    A lens bends light twice — once entering, once leaving — and the combined effect focuses rays to an image. The lens formula v1​−u1​=f1​, its magnification m=v/u, and the angular magnification of a magnifier make up the working toolkit of all simple optics.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024)

    2.1 Spherical lenses

    A spherical lens has two spherical refracting surfaces. Convex (converging) lenses are thicker in the middle; concave (diverging) lenses thinner. Key points: optical centre O, principal foci F and F′, focal length f (positive for convex, negative for concave).

    2.2 Lens formula and magnification

    v1​−u1​=f1​m=uv​=hh′​ (Cartesian convention: distances measured from the lens; real object u negative, real image v positive for a convex lens; f positive for convex.)

    2.3 Angular magnification

    Angular magnification = angle subtended at the eye with the instrument ÷ angle subtended by the unaided eye. A simple microscope (single short-focus convex lens, object just inside F) with the image at the near point D gives: m=1+fD​(D≈25 cm) and for relaxed viewing at infinity, m ≈ D/f (NEB 2024, 2m).

    2.4 Worked numericals

    Q (3m, 2024): An object is placed 30 cm from a convex lens of focal length 20 cm. Find the image distance and magnification. v1​=f1​+u1​-form: v1​=201​−−301​=201​+301​=605​⇒v=60 cm m=uv​=−3060​=−2 — real image 60 cm behind the lens, inverted and twice as large.

    Q (4m, 2023): A convex lens of focal length 15 cm forms a real image twice the size of the object. Find the positions of the object and the image.

    • Real image → inverted → m = −2 → v = 2u (with signs: u = −x, v = +2x)
    • 2x1​+x1​=151​ → 2x3​=151​ → x = 22.5 cm
    • Object 22.5 cm in front; image 45 cm behind the lens.

    Q (2m, 2024): A simple microscope has f = 5 cm; find its magnifying power with the image at the near point. m=1+fD​=1+525​=6.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 78: the lens maker's formula predicts f from the surfaces' radii.
    • To Topic 79: P = 1/f in dioptres; lens combinations add powers.
    • To the mirror formula (Topic 69): same algebra, different signs — minus in the lens formula, plus in the mirror formula.

    4.2 Common examination mistakes

    1. Sign chaos: the lens formula has 1/v − 1/u, not 1/v + 1/u.
    2. Forgetting that a real image in a lens is on the opposite side from the object.
    3. Using m = v/u without the sign — inverted images need m < 0.

Specific Conditions

  • Sign Convention: Light travels left to right. Distances measured from optical centre. Object distance u is negative (object on left), focal length f positive for convex, negative for concave.
  • Thin Lens Approximation: Lens formulas assume thin lens (thickness negligible compared to radii and object/image distances).
  • Paraxial Rays: Formulas valid for rays close to principal axis making small angles.

Formulas

    • 1/v − 1/u = 1/f
    • m = v/u = h'/h
    • Simple microscope: m = 1 + D/f (near point); m ≈ D/f (relaxed)

Key Points

  • 1/v − 1/u = 1/f; m = v/u; angular magnification m = 1 + D/f (near point).

Important Statements

  • "The angular magnification of a simple microscope is 1 + D/f with the image at the near point."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: lens numerical 2023 4m and 2024 3m; simple microscope 2024 2m.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "1/v = 1/f + 1/u with signs; m = v/u carries the sign of the image."

Exam Notes

  • State image nature (real/virtual, erect/inverted) after every lens numerical.

Examples

  • NEB 2024 — u = 30 cm, f = 20 cm → v = 60 cm, m = −2.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (4m): A convex lens of f = 12 cm forms a real image 3× the object's size. Find u and v. — m = −3 → v = 3u (signs: u = −x, v = 3x). 1/(3x) + 1/x = 1/12 → 4/(3x) = 1/12 → x = 16 cm. Object 16 cm in front, image 48 cm behind.

Practice

  • Why does a magnifying glass work better with the object just inside the focus?

Universal Facts

  • The eye's own lens changes focal length (accommodation) to keep images on the retina — a variable-focus lens.

Confusion

  • Lens vs mirror formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f for lenses; 1/v + 1/u = 1/f for mirrors — a minus-sign apart.
  • Real image through a lens forms on the far side — light actually passes through the lens.

1.

Power of a lens of focal length 50 cm is:

Options
  • 2 D
  • 0.5 D
  • 5 D
  • 0.2 D
Answer

A

Explanation

P = 1/f = 1/0.5 m = 2 D.

2.

Lens maker's formula depends on:

Options
  • Refractive index only
  • Radii of curvature only
  • Refractive index and radii of curvature
  • Focal length only
Answer

C

Explanation

1/f = (μ-1)(1/R₁ - 1/R₂) — depends on μ, R₁, R₂.

3.

Angular magnification of simple magnifier when image at near point:

Options
  • D/f
  • 1 + D/f
  • f/D
  • 1 - D/f
Answer

B

Explanation

M = 1 + D/f when final image at least distance of distinct vision D.

4.

Astronomical telescope magnification in normal adjustment:

Options
  • fₑ/fₒ
  • fₒ/fₑ
  • fₒ + fₑ
  • fₒ × fₑ
Answer

B

Explanation

M = fₒ/fₑ for telescope in normal adjustment (final image at infinity).

5.

For two lenses in contact, equivalent power:

Options
  • P₁ × P₂
  • P₁ + P₂
  • P₁ - P₂
  • P₁/P₂
Answer

B

Explanation

Powers add for lenses in contact: P = P₁ + P₂.

Summary

Spherical lenses refract light twice: convex lenses converge (f > 0), concave lenses diverge (f < 0). The lens formula v1​−u1​=f1​ with m=v/u locates and sizes images; real images form on the far side and are inverted. NEB 2024's case (u = 30 cm, f = 20 cm) gives v = 60 cm, m = −2 (3m); NEB 2023's real image twice the size with f = 15 cm puts the object at 22.5 cm and the image at 45 cm (4m). Angular magnification — the ratio of angles subtended with and without the instrument — for a simple microscope at the near point is m=1+D/f with D ≈ 25 cm (NEB 2024, 2m): a 5 cm lens magnifies ×6.
