Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Measuring a Lens in Dioptres

    Opticians never quote focal lengths; they quote power — the reciprocal of focal length in dioptres. A +3 D lens bends light three times as strongly as a +1 D lens, and contact prescriptions, camera lenses and spectacles all speak this language.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Power of a lens

    P=f1​(f in metres),unit: dioptre (D)=m−1

    • Convex (converging) lens → positive power; concave (diverging) → negative.
    • A shorter focal length means a stronger lens: f = 25 cm → P = +4 D; f = 50 cm → P = +2 D.
    • Physically, power measures the angle through which the lens bends a ray at height h from the axis.

    2.2 Combination of thin lenses in contact

    For two or more thin lenses touching, powers simply add: P=P1​+P2​+⋯F1​=f1​1​+f2​1​ — the reason optometrists can stack lens powers in a trial frame.

    2.3 Vision correction (NEB 2024, 3m)

    • Myopia (near-sight): eyeball too long — distant objects focus in front of the retina. Corrected with a concave (diverging) lens of negative power, pushing the image back onto the retina.
    • Hypermetropia (far-sight): eyeball too short — near objects focus behind the retina. Corrected with a convex (converging) lens of positive power, pulling the focus forward.

    2.4 Worked numericals

    Q (2m, 2024): Two thin lenses of powers +5 D and −2 D are placed in contact. Find the power and focal length of the combination. P=+5+(−2)=+3 D (net converging)f=P(in D)100​=3100​≈33.3 cm

    Q (NEB-style): A person needs glasses of power −2.5 D. What defect do they have and what is the focal length? Negative power → concave lens → myopia; f = −100/2.5 = −40 cm.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 77: P = 1/f converts between the two lens "currencies".
    • To Topic 78: the lens maker's formula gives f, hence P, at design time.
    • To combinations: adding powers is the optical analogue of adding conductances in parallel.

Summary

The power of a lens is P=1/f with f in metres, measured in dioptres (D): convex lenses positive, concave negative; a shorter focal length is a stronger lens. Thin lenses in contact simply add powers: P = P₁ + P₂ — NEB 2024's +5 D and −2 D pair gives +3 D, f = 100/3 ≈ 33.3 cm (2m). Power language powers vision correction: myopia (eyeball too long, image in front of the retina) needs a concave lens; hypermetropia (eyeball too short, image behind the retina) needs a convex lens (NEB 2024, 3m).

Special Notes

  • Using centimetres directly produces a power 100 times too large.
  • Separated or thick lens systems require an additional relation.

Important Statements

  • "The power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length expressed in metres."

Specific Conditions

  • Lens Power Definition: Power P=1/f (in meters) measured in diopters (D). A converging lens has P>0, a diverging lens has P<0. Power is additive for thin lenses in contact: Ptotal​=∑Pi​.
  • Combination of Thin Lenses: For thin lenses in contact, the total focal length is given by 1/ftotal​=∑1/fi​, or equivalently Ptotal​=∑Pi​. This assumes negligible separation between lenses. For separated lenses, use the equivalent focal length formula including the separation d.
  • Power vs Magnification: Lens power P=1/f relates to focal length, not directly to magnification. Magnification m=v/u=f/(f−u) depends on object distance. A high-power lens (short f) gives larger magnification for a given object distance, but m is not simply proportional to P.

Exercises

1.

A glass lens has P = 5.0 D in air, from (μ − 1) = 0.50. In water the relative factor becomes 0.17 instead of 0.50. Estimate the power in water.

Steps
  • Power is proportional to (μ_relative − 1) for the same shape.
  • The factor falls from 0.50 to 0.17.
  • The ratio is 0.17/0.50 = 0.34.
  • P_water ≈ 5.0 × 0.34 = 1.7 D.
  • The focal length becomes longer.
Answer

About 1.7 D in water.
