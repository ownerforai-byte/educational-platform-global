Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Designing a Lens on Paper

    Given two spherical surfaces and a glass of known refractive index, the lens maker's formula tells you the focal length before the lens is even ground. NEB 2024 asked for it twice — a 5-mark derivation and a 3-mark numerical.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Statement

    For a thin lens in air: f1​=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​)

    • n = refractive index of the lens material (relative to surroundings).
    • R₁, R₂ = radii of curvature of the first and second surfaces (Cartesian signs: convex towards the incident light → positive).
    • f depends on the material (n) and the geometry (R₁, R₂) — not on where the object is.

    2.2 Derivation sketch (NEB 2022, 2024, 5m)

    Refraction at a single spherical surface: vn2​​−un1​​=Rn2​−n1​​.

    1. First surface: light from air (n₁ = 1) into glass (n₂ = n) forms an intermediate image at distance v′:

    v′n​−u1​=R1​n−1​

    1. Second surface: the intermediate image acts as the object; light leaves glass into air:

    v1​−v′n​=R2​1−n​=−R2​(n−1)​

    1. Adding the two equations eliminates v′:

    v1​−u1​=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​)=f1​ using v1​−u1​=f1​ for a thin lens.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (3m, 2024): A convex lens has surfaces of radii 20 cm and −20 cm and n = 1.5. Calculate its focal length. f1​=(1.5−1)(201​−−201​)=0.5×(201​+201​)=0.5×0.1=0.05 f=20 cm

    Q (NEB-style): A biconvex lens of equal radii 25 cm has f = 20 cm in air. Find n. f1​=(n−1)×252​ → 0.05=(n−1)×0.08 → n=1.625.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 77: the formula's right side is the lens formula's 1/f.
    • To Topic 79: with f known, P = 1/f follows immediately.
    • To immersion: surrounded by liquid (n_medium > 1), the effective (n−1) shrinks and f grows — a lens loses power in water.

    2. Dropping the (n − 1) factor.
    3. Forgetting that R is negative for a surface concave towards the incident light.

Confusion

  • The lens works because n ≠ surroundings: in a liquid with n equal to the glass, the lens stops focusing.
  • Signs of R: convex towards incident light → positive; concave → negative.

Key Points

Summary

The lens maker's formula for a thin lens in air is f1​=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​): focal length is fixed by the material's refractive index and the two surface radii. The derivation (NEB 2022, 2024, 5m) applies single-surface refraction vn2​​−un1​​=Rn2​−n1​​ at each surface and adds the equations, cancelling the intermediate image distance. NEB 2024's numerical (R₁ = 20 cm, R₂ = −20 cm, n = 1.5) gives 1/f = 0.5 × 0.1 = 0.05 → f = 20 cm (3m). The formula explains why lenses weaken in water and why the eye needs goggles underwater.

Special Notes

  • The formula assumes a thin lens and paraxial rays.
  • Do not replace signed radii by their magnitudes.

Important Statements

  • "The focal length of a thin lens is determined by the refractive index of its material and the radii of curvature of its surfaces."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: derivation 2022 5m and 2024 5m; numerical 2024 3m.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Biconvex equal radii: 1/f = (n−1) × 2/R."

Exam Notes

  • The 5m derivation appeared in 2022 and 2024 — a repeat favourite.

Important Concepts

  • Two refractions in series — the intermediate image at the first surface is the object for the second.

Important Tasks

  • Find f for n=1.60, R1=+30 cm, R2=−30 cm.
  • Why do radii carry signs?
  • How does immersion in a higher-index liquid change lens power?

Specific Conditions

  • Thin Lens Approximation: The lens maker's formula f1​=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​) assumes a thin lens (thickness ≪ radii of curvature). For thick lenses, the full Gullstrand formula with principal planes must be used.
  • Sign Convention for Radii: Using the Cartesian sign convention (light travels left to right): R is positive if the center of curvature is to the right of the surface (convex toward incident light), negative if to the left (concave). This is opposite to some older conventions.
  • Material and Medium Dependence: The formula uses relative refractive index n=nlens​/nmedium​. For a lens in air, nmedium​≈1. If immersed in water (n≈1.33), the focal length increases by factor nlens​/nwater​ relative to air.

Exercises

1.

A plano-convex lens has μ = 1.60 and curved radius 20 cm, with the flat face of infinite radius. Find f and the power.

Steps
  • 1/R_flat = 0.
  • 1/f = (0.60)(1/20) = 0.030 cm^−1.
  • f = 33 cm = 0.33 m.
  • P = 1/0.33 = 3.0 D.
  • The curved face alone sets the power.
Answer

f = 33 cm, power 3.0 D.
