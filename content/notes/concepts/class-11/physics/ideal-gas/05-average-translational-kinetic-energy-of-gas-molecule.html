Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Temperature Is Energy

    Match the kinetic-theory pressure to the ideal gas equation and temperature stops being a thermometer reading: it becomes the average translational kinetic energy of molecules, 23​kB​T per molecule. At 300 K every molecule — any gas, any mass — carries exactly 6.2×10⁻²¹ J of translational energy.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Derivation

    From the pressure derivation: P=31​VNm​c2ˉ, and the ideal gas equation PV=NkB​T: 31​VNm​c2ˉ⋅V=NkB​T⇒31​mc2ˉ=kB​T KEˉtrans​=21​mc2ˉ=23​kB​T​ — depends only on temperature, not on the gas's identity or mass.

    2.2 Per-mole and total forms

    • Per mole: KEˉmol​=23​RT.
    • Total for N molecules: U=23​NkB​T=23​nRT (internal energy of a monatomic ideal gas).

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (2m, 2024): Calculate the average kinetic energy per molecule of a gas at 300 K. (k = 1.38×10⁻²³ J/K) KEˉ=23​kB​T=23​×1.38×10−23×300=6.21×10−21 J

    Q (NEB-style): At what temperature is the average KE per molecule 1.38×10⁻²⁰ J? T=3kB​2KEˉ​=3×1.38×10−232×1.38×10−20​=667 K.

    Q (NEB-style): Find the total translational KE of 2 mol of a monatomic gas at 300 K. U=23​nRT=1.5×2×8.31×300=7.5×103 J.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 64: one-line consequence of matching the two pressure expressions.
    • To equipartition (Topic 67): the ½k_BT per degree of freedom generalises this.
    • To absolute zero: at T = 0 translational KE vanishes — the physical meaning of absolute zero.

Confusion

  • Per molecule use k_B; per mole use R — the factor-1000 trap.
  • KE depends only on T — hydrogen and oxygen molecules at the same T carry the same average translational KE.

Practice

Universal Facts

  • At absolute zero, translational molecular motion ceases — that is what 0 K means.

Formulas

    • KE = (3/2)k_BT (per molecule)
    • KE = (3/2)RT (per mole)
    • U = (3/2)nRT (monatomic ideal gas)

Key Points

  • KE = (3/2)k_BT per molecule = (3/2)RT per mole.

Summary

Equating the kinetic-theory pressure P=31​VNm​c2ˉ with the gas equation PV=NkB​T gives 21​mc2ˉ=23​kB​T: the average translational kinetic energy of a gas molecule depends only on absolute temperature — 23​kB​T per molecule, 23​RT per mole, 23​nRT total for a monatomic ideal gas. At 300 K that is 6.21×10−21 J per molecule (NEB 2024, 2m), identical for hydrogen and oxygen alike; only the speeds differ (vrms​∝1/m​). Absolute zero is where this energy vanishes.

Special Notes

  • Do not confuse kB per molecule with R per mole.
  • Polyatomic-gas internal energy may include rotational and vibrational modes.

Important Statements

  • "The average translational kinetic energy of a gas molecule is directly proportional to the absolute temperature."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQ: KE per molecule at 300 K (2024, 2m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "1.5 × 1.38×10⁻²³ × T — memorise 6.2×10⁻²¹ J at 300 K."

Exam Notes

  • A 2m plug-in; the derivation (3m) may also be asked.

Important Concepts

  • Temperature IS molecular kinetic energy — the deepest statement of kinetic theory.

Important Tasks

  • Find mean translational energy per molecule at 400 K using kB=1.38×10⁻²³ J K⁻¹.
  • Why do lighter molecules move faster at the same temperature?
  • Does (3/2)kBT include rotational energy?

Specific Conditions

  • Equipartition Result: The average translational kinetic energy per molecule is \langle KE_{trans} angle = rac{3}{2} k_B T = rac{3}{2} rac{R}{N_A} T. This comes from 3 translational degrees of freedom, each contributing rac12kB​T via equipartition.
  • Temperature Proportionality: ⟨KEtrans​angle∝T — absolute temperature is a direct measure of average translational kinetic energy. This is why Kelvin scale is fundamental: at T=0, translational KE is zero (classically).
  • Internal Energy of Monatomic Gas: For a monatomic ideal gas, U = N \langle KE_{trans} angle = rac{3}{2} nRT. The internal energy is purely translational KE. For diatomic/polyatomic gases, U includes rotational and vibrational contributions as well.
