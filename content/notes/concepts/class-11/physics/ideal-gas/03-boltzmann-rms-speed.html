Summary

The Boltzmann constant k = R/N_A = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K links macroscopic temperature to microscopic molecular energy. RMS speed v_rms = √(3kT/m) = √(3RT/M) characterizes the typical molecular speed in a gas at temperature T. At the same temperature, lighter molecules move faster; v_rms determines diffusion rates, effusion, and kinetic energy.

Notes

  • Boltzmann Constant Definition: k = R/N_A where R = 8.314 J/(mol·K) and N_A = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. Value: k = 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K (exact since 2019 SI redefinition). It converts temperature (kelvin) into energy (joules) per particle.
  • Physical Significance of k: Average translational KE per molecule = (3/2)kT. At 300 K, ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)(1.38×10⁻²³)(300) ≈ 6.21 × 10⁻²¹ J ≈ 0.039 eV. This is the thermal energy scale governing molecular motion.
  • RMS Speed Formula: v_rms = √(⟨v²⟩) = √(3kT/m) = √(3RT/M), where m = molecular mass, M = molar mass. Derived from equating pressure expression P = (1/3)ρv²_rms with ideal gas law PV = nRT.
  • Speed Hierarchy: For Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution: v_mp = √(2kT/m) < v_avg = √(8kT/πm) < v_rms = √(3kT/m). Numerical ratio: v_mp : v_avg : v_rms ≈ 1.41 : 1.60 : 1.73 (or 1 : 1.13 : 1.22 normalized to v_mp).
  • Temperature Dependence: v_rms ∝ √T. Doubling absolute temperature increases v_rms by factor √2 ≈ 1.414. Cooling to 1/4 original T halves the rms speed.
  • Mass Dependence: v_rms ∝ 1/√M. At same T, hydrogen (M=2 g/mol) moves √(32/2) = 4 times faster than oxygen (M=32 g/mol). This explains why light gases escape planetary atmospheres more readily.
  • Typical Values at 300 K: H₂: 1920 m/s; He: 1370 m/s; N₂: 517 m/s; O₂: 483 m/s; CO₂: 412 m/s. All supersonic yet no bulk flow due to random directions.
  • Connection to Kinetic Energy: Total translational KE of n moles = (3/2)nRT = (3/2)NkT. Internal energy of monatomic ideal gas U = (3/2)nRT depends only on T, not V or P.
  • Effusion and Graham's Law: Rate of effusion ∝ v_rms ∝ 1/√M. Lighter gases effuse faster through small openings. Used in uranium enrichment (²³⁵UF₆ vs ²³⁸UF₆).

Specific Conditions

  • Ideal Gas Assumption: Formulas assume point particles with elastic collisions and no intermolecular forces. Real gases deviate at high P or low T.
  • Thermal Equilibrium Required: Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution and well-defined v_rms apply only at equilibrium. Non-equilibrium flows have different velocity distributions.
  • Translational Motion Only: v_rms refers to center-of-mass translational speed. Rotational and vibrational energies contribute to total internal energy but not to v_rms.
  • Classical Regime: Quantum effects become significant when de Broglie wavelength λ = h/(mv) approaches interparticle spacing. For most gases above ~10 K, classical treatment is valid.

Key Points

  • k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K bridges macroscopic T and microscopic energy.
  • v_rms = √(3RT/M): depends on T and M, not on P or V directly.
  • At same T, all gases have same ⟨KE⟩ but different v_rms.
  • v_rms > v_avg > v_mp always for Maxwellian distribution.
  • Lighter molecules move faster: v_rms ∝ 1/√M.
  • Graham's law of effusion follows directly from v_rms ∝ 1/√M.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Boltzmann constant relates temperature to average molecular kinetic energy via ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT.
  • Statement 2: RMS speed is the square root of the mean of squared molecular speeds, not the arithmetic mean speed.
  • Statement 3: At identical temperatures, gases with lower molar mass have higher rms speeds.
  • Statement 4: The three characteristic speeds (v_mp, v_avg, v_rms) are distinct and ordered for any Maxwellian gas.
  • Statement 5: Graham's law of effusion is a direct consequence of the mass dependence of rms speed.

Important Notes

  • Since 2019, k is defined exactly as 1.380649 × 10⁻²³ J/K, fixing the kelvin in terms of energy.
  • v_rms appears in pressure formula because pressure depends on ⟨v²⟩, not ⟨v⟩².
  • Escape velocity criterion: if v_rms > v_escape/6, significant atmospheric loss occurs over geological time.
  • NEB frequently asks numerical calculation of v_rms given T and M — ensure M in kg/mol.

Exam Short Tricks

  • v_rms = √(3RT/M): plug T in K, M in kg/mol, R = 8.314.
  • Same T → same ⟨KE⟩; lighter = faster.
  • Ratio v_mp:v_avg:v_rms = √2 : √(8/π) : √3 ≈ 1.41:1.60:1.73.
  • Double T → v_rms × √2; double M → v_rms ÷ √2.
  • Effusion rate ∝ 1/√M (Graham's law).

Exam Notes

  • Calculate v_rms numerically: convert M to kg/mol (e.g., O₂ = 0.032 kg/mol).
  • Compare speeds of two gases at same T using v₁/v₂ = √(M₂/M₁).
  • Explain why hydrogen escapes Earth's atmosphere but nitrogen does not.
  • Derive v_rms from P = (1/3)ρv²_rms and PV = nRT.

Examples

  • Oxygen at 27°C (300 K): v_rms = √(3×8.314×300/0.032) = √(233,812) ≈ 483 m/s.
  • Hydrogen at 300 K: v_rms = √(3×8.314×300/0.002) ≈ 1920 m/s. Ratio to O₂: √(32/2) = 4×.
  • At what T does N₂ have v_rms = 1000 m/s? T = Mv²/(3R) = 0.028×10⁶/(3×8.314) ≈ 1123 K.
  • Graham's law: H₂ effuses √(32/2) = 4 times faster than O₂ through a pinhole at same T and P.

Practice

  • Compute v_rms for various gases at standard and elevated temperatures.
  • Use speed ratios to find one speed given another.
  • Apply Graham's law to compare effusion rates.
  • Relate changes in T or M to changes in v_rms.
  • Convert between molecular mass m and molar mass M correctly.

Universal Facts

  • Ludwig Boltzmann (1844–1906) established statistical mechanics; his constant was named posthumously.
  • The 2019 SI redefinition fixed k exactly, making the kelvin dependent on the joule rather than water's triple point.
  • At room temperature, air molecules collide ~5 billion times per second with mean free path ~68 nm.
  • Neutron stars have surface temperatures ~10⁶ K; protons there have v_rms ~ 100 km/s.

Confusion

  • Confusing v_rms with average speed: v_rms = √(⟨v²⟩) ≠ ⟨v⟩. They differ by ~8% for Maxwellian gases.
  • Using M in g/mol instead of kg/mol: Must convert to SI (kg/mol) for v_rms in m/s.
  • Thinking heavier gases have higher KE at same T: All gases at same T have identical ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT regardless of mass.
  • Believing v_rms depends on pressure: v_rms depends only on T and M for ideal gases; P affects density, not individual molecular speeds.
  • Assuming all molecules move at v_rms: Individual speeds vary widely; v_rms is a statistical measure of the distribution.
