Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Gas Laws Combined

    Boyle, Charles and Gay-Lussac each froze one variable and watched two others. Combine their three laws and out falls PV = nRT — one equation that predicts the pressure, volume or temperature of any (ideal) gas from the other two plus the amount of gas.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2024)

    2.1 The three empirical laws

    LawStatementFormula (constant)
    Boyle's lawAt constant T, pressure ∝ 1/volumePV= const (T, n fixed)
    Charles's lawAt constant P, volume ∝ absolute temperatureV/T= const (P, n fixed)
    Pressure (Gay-Lussac) lawAt constant V, pressure ∝ absolute temperatureP/T= const (V, n fixed)

    2.2 Combining into the ideal gas equation (NEB 2024)

    From the three laws, P∝T/V for fixed n: T1​P1​V1​​=T2​P2​V2​​=const⇒PV=nRT​

    • n = number of moles; R=8.31 J mol⁻¹K⁻¹ = universal gas constant.
    • R=NA​kB​; NA​=6.022×1023 /mol, kB​=1.38×10−23 J/K.
    • In molecular form: PV=NkB​T.
    • STP: T = 273.15 K, P = 1.013×10⁵ Pa; molar volume 22.4 L.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (2m, 2024): Two moles of an ideal gas at 300 K occupy 0.05 m³. Calculate its pressure. (R = 8.31 J/mol·K) P=VnRT​=0.052×8.31×300​=9.97×104 Pa≈1.0×105 Pa

    Q (4m, 2022): A gas at 27 °C and pressure 1.0×10⁵ Pa occupies 0.5 m³. Find its volume at 100 °C if the pressure remains constant.

    • Charles's law: V2​=V1​T1​T2​​=0.5×300373​=0.62 m³

    Q (NEB-style): A gas occupies 2 L at 1 atm and 300 K. Find its volume at 2 atm and 450 K. V2​=V1​×P2​P1​​×T1​T2​​=2×21​×300450​=1.5 L.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 63/64: kinetic theory derives PV = ⅓Nmv̄² and matches it to PV = NkT — the microscopic origin of the gas laws.
    • To real gases: the equation holds only at low pressure and high temperature; real gases need van der Waals corrections.
    • To Avogadro: equal volumes of gases at same T, P contain equal numbers of molecules.

    4.2 Common examination mistakes

    1. Using Celsius temperature in gas laws — always kelvin.
    2. Forgetting to convert litres to m³ (1 m³ = 1000 L).
    3. Mixing n (moles) with N (molecules): PV = nRT vs PV = NkT.

  • n vs N: n = moles (use R); N = molecules (use k_B).

Key Points

  • Boyle (T): PV const; Charles (P): V/T const; pressure law (V): P/T const.

Summary

Three empirical laws anchor gas behaviour: Boyle (PV = const at fixed T), Charles (V/T = const at fixed P) and the pressure law (P/T = const at fixed V). Combined for a fixed amount of gas they give P1​V1​/T1​=P2​V2​/T2​, and with amount included the ideal gas equation PV=nRT (R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹K⁻¹) or, per molecule, PV=NkB​T. NEB 2024 asked for exactly this combination (3m) plus the numerical P=nRT/V=2×8.31×300/0.05≈1.0×105 Pa (2m); NEB 2022's Charles-law problem heats 0.5 m³ from 27 °C to 100 °C to 0.62 m³ (4m). Temperatures must always be kelvin.

Special Notes

  • Never substitute Celsius into PV=nRT.
  • Gauge pressure is not absolute pressure.

Important Statements

  • "The ideal gas equation combines Boyle's, Charles's and the pressure law: PV = nRT."

Exam Notes

Important Concepts

  • The combined-gas shortcut: P₁V₁/T₁ = P₂V₂/T₂ solves most numericals without finding n.

Important Tasks

  • A 2 mol gas occupies 0.050 m³ at 300 K. Find absolute pressure.
  • A gas at 100 kPa and 300 K is heated to 360 K at fixed volume. Find final pressure.
  • Why do real gases deviate near condensation?

Specific Conditions

  • Ideal Gas Assumptions: The equation PV=nRT assumes (1) molecules are point particles with zero volume, (2) no intermolecular forces except during elastic collisions, (3) motion is random and obeys Newton's laws. These hold best at high temperature and low pressure; real gases deviate near condensation.
  • Universal Gas Constant: R=8.314extJ/mol⋅K is the same for all ideal gases. It connects macroscopic (P,V,n,T) to microscopic (kB​,NA​) via R=NA​kB​. The value depends on units: R=0.0821extL⋅atm/mol⋅K in common chemistry units.
  • Temperature Must Be Absolute: T must be in Kelvin for PV=nRT to hold. Using Celsius gives incorrect results because T=0∘extCeq0@@KATEX10590@@P∝T@@KATEX10591@@V∝T only works with absolute temperature.

Exercises

1.

0.50 mol of ideal gas is at 300 K in a volume of 0.012 m^3. Take R = 8.3 J/mol K. Find the pressure.

Steps
  • P = n R T / V.
  • n R T = 0.50 × 8.3 × 300 = 1245.
  • P = 1245 / 0.012 = 1.04 × 10^5 Pa.
  • 300 K is 27°C, and the kelvin value is the one in the formula.
  • The gas is ideal, so there is no volume correction.
Answer

1.04 × 10^5 Pa.
