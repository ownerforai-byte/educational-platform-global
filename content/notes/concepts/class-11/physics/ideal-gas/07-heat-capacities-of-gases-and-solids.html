Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Why Gases Have Two Heat Capacities

    Heat a gas at constant pressure and it expands, doing work — so it needs more heat per kelvin than when heated at fixed volume. That single difference splits Cp from Cv, gives Cp − Cv = R, and the equipartition theorem predicts each value from counting degrees of freedom.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021, 2024)

    2.1 Equipartition of energy (NEB 2021, 2024)

    Statement: in thermal equilibrium, the total energy of a molecule is equally distributed among all its degrees of freedom, each receiving 21​kB​T on average.

    Degrees of freedom (DOF): independent modes of energy storage.

    • Monatomic (He, Ar): 3 translational → U=23​kB​T, CV​=23​R, CP​=25​R, γ=5/3≈1.67.
    • Diatomic (O₂, N₂): 3 translational + 2 rotational (+ vibration ignored at room T) → U=25​kB​T, CV​=25​R, CP​=27​R, γ=7/5=1.4.
    • Polyatomic (CO₂, NH₃): ≥ 6 DOF → CV​=3R, γ≈1.33.

    Q (2m, 2021 & 2024): What is the law of equipartition of energy? How many degrees of freedom does a monatomic gas molecule have? Answer: statement above; a monatomic molecule has 3 translational degrees of freedom.

    2.2 Cp and Cv

    • CV​: heat to raise 1 mol by 1 K at constant volume — all heat becomes internal energy: dQ=dU=CV​dT.
    • CP​: at constant pressure — heat both raises internal energy AND does expansion work PdV=RdT:

    CP​−CV​=R​γ=CV​CP​​=1+f2​

    • Gases have two heat capacities because their volume changes; solids and liquids barely expand, so they have effectively one.

    2.3 Heat capacities of solids (Dulong–Petit law)

    Atoms of a solid vibrate in 3D: 6 vibrational DOF → CV​=3R≈25 J mol⁻¹K⁻¹ — the Dulong–Petit law, good at room temperature for many elements.

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): For a diatomic gas, find C_V, C_P and γ. CV​=25​R=20.8 J/mol·K; CP​=27​R=29.1 J/mol·K; γ=29.1/20.8=1.4.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 65: equipartition generalises (3/2)k_BT to f degrees of freedom.
    • To first law (Unit 5): Cp − Cv = R is the first law applied to isobaric expansion.
    • To adiabatic processes: γ controls PV^γ = const (used in Physics XII thermodynamics).

Summary

The equipartition theorem states that in equilibrium a molecule's energy divides equally among all its degrees of freedom, 21​kB​T each (NEB 2021, 2024, 2m) — a monatomic molecule has 3 translational DOF. Counting f gives CV​=2f​R directly; heating at constant pressure must additionally supply expansion work RdT, giving Cp − Cv = R and γ=1+2/f: monatomic (f = 3) γ = 5/3, diatomic (f = 5) γ = 1.4, polyatomic γ ≈ 1.33. Gases need two heat capacities because they expand; solids, barely expanding, obey the Dulong–Petit law C≈3R≈25 J mol⁻¹K⁻¹.
