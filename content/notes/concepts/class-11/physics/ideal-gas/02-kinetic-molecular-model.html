Summary

The kinetic molecular model states that a gas is a vast number of identical molecules in rapid random motion, and that its pressure is the collective bombardment of the container walls while its temperature measures the average molecular kinetic energy. Its assumptions: (1) identical point molecules, (2) rapid random motion in all directions, (3) perfectly elastic collisions, (4) negligible intermolecular forces except during instantaneous collisions, (5) Newtonian straight-line motion between collisions, (6) enormous N making behaviour statistical (NEB 2023, 2024, 5m each). From these assumptions alone the model yields P = ⅓ρc̄², PV = NkT, the gas laws, and the rms speed — mechanics becoming thermodynamics.

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Pressure from a Billion Collisions

    A tyre wall feels steady pressure, yet it is being bombarded by ~10²³ molecules per second. The kinetic molecular model builds gas behaviour from pure mechanics: elastic collisions of point molecules with the walls. Its five assumptions and one postulate are a 5-mark NEB favourite.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024)

    2.1 The kinetic theory — statement and assumptions (5m)

    Statement: a gas consists of a very large number of molecules in rapid, random motion; the pressure exerted by the gas arises from the continuous bombardment of the container walls by these molecules, and temperature measures the average kinetic energy of molecular motion.

    Assumptions:

    1. A gas consists of a very large number N of identical molecules — point masses (volume negligible compared to the container).
    2. Molecules are in rapid, random motion, moving with all directions equally likely.
    3. Collisions (molecule–molecule and molecule–wall) are perfectly elastic; no energy is lost.
    4. Intermolecular forces are negligible except during collisions; the duration of a collision is negligible compared with the time between collisions.
    5. The molecules obey Newton's laws of motion; between collisions they move in straight lines at constant speed.
    6. (Often added) The number of molecules per unit volume is enormous, so statistically uniform; gravity's effect is negligible.

    2.2 What the model delivers

    • Pressure: P=31​ρc2ˉ=31​VNm​c2ˉ (derived in Topic 64).
    • Temperature: KEˉ=23​kB​T (Topic 65).
    • Gas laws: PV = NkT follows immediately — Boyle's, Charles's laws all emerge.
    • Speeds: rms speed vrms​=3kB​T/m​ (Topic 66).

    2.3 Model answers (5m)

    Q (5m, 2023 & 2024): State the kinetic theory of gases and its assumptions. Answer: statement as §2.1 + the six assumptions listed — each assumption carries roughly a mark, with the statement and the pressure origin completing the five.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 62: the model quantifies the molecular picture.
    • To Topic 64: the pressure derivation is the model's centrepiece calculation.
    • To elastic collisions (Topic 21): assumption 3 is the elastic-collision physics.
    • To ideal gas equation: the model derives PV = NkT rather than postulating it.

    4.2 Common examination mistakes

    1. Listing only 2–3 assumptions — the 5m question expects five or six.
    2. Forgetting "elastic collisions" and "negligible intermolecular forces" — the two most-marked assumptions.
    3. Omitting the pressure-from-bombardment statement.

Specific Conditions

  • Low Density Required: Model assumes molecular volume ≪ container volume. Valid when gas is dilute (low P, high T). Breaks down near condensation point.
  • Elastic Collisions Only: Energy conservation in collisions is essential. Inelastic collisions would cause temperature decay — not observed in equilibrium gases.
  • Large Number of Molecules: Statistical averaging requires N ≫ 1. Fluctuations become significant for very small systems (nanoscale).
  • Thermal Equilibrium Assumed: Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution applies only at equilibrium. Non-equilibrium flows require Boltzmann transport equation.

Formulas

    • P = ⅓ρc̄² = Nm c̄²/3V
    • KE_avg = (3/2)k_BT
    • v_rms = √(3k_BT/m)

Key Points

  • Statement: pressure = wall bombardment; temperature ∝ average KE.
  • Six assumptions: identical point molecules; random motion; elastic collisions; negligible intermolecular forces; Newton's laws; huge N.

Important Statements

  • "The pressure of a gas arises from the bombardment of the container walls by its molecules."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: statement + assumptions (2023, 2024 — 5m each).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Write the statement, then number the assumptions 1–6."

Exam Notes

  • The identical 5m question appeared in two consecutive years — near-certain to repeat.

Examples

  • Tyre wall — steady pressure from ~10²³ molecular collisions per second.

Practice Questions

  • Short (2m): Which two assumptions fail for a real gas at high pressure? — Point-molecule volume (molecules occupy finite volume) and negligible intermolecular forces (attractions become significant) — the van der Waals corrections.

Practice

  • Why does the model fail near condensation?

Universal Facts

  • The kinetic theory is one of physics' great reductions: Boyle's and Charles's laws fall out of pure mechanics.

Confusion

  • 'Ideal' means the assumptions hold: point molecules, elastic collisions, no forces — real gases approximate this at low P, high T.
  • Pressure is statistical: no single molecule sustains it; 10²³ collisions per second average into a steady push.

Mcs

1.

According to kinetic theory, gas pressure is caused by:

Options
  • Gravitational pull on molecules
  • Molecular collisions with container walls
  • Intermolecular repulsion
  • Thermal expansion
Answer

B

Explanation

Pressure = rate of momentum transfer per unit area from wall collisions.

2.

Average translational KE per molecule depends on:

Options
  • Pressure only
  • Volume only
  • Temperature only
  • Both P and V
Answer

C

Explanation

⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT — depends solely on absolute temperature.

3.

If temperature doubles, v_rms becomes:

Options
  • Double
  • Four times
  • √2 times
  • Unchanged
Answer

C

Explanation

v_rms ∝ √T, so doubling T multiplies v_rms by √2.

4.

Which speed is greatest for a Maxwellian gas?

Options
  • Most probable
  • Average
  • RMS
  • All equal
Answer

C

Explanation

v_rms > v_avg > v_mp always for Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution.

5.

Kinetic theory assumes intermolecular forces are:

Options
  • Always attractive
  • Always repulsive
  • Negligible except during collisions
  • Proportional to distance
Answer

C

Explanation

Ideal gas model ignores forces except instantaneous elastic collisions.
