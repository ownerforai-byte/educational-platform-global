Summary

The kinetic molecular model explains gas behavior through random motion of molecules. Key assumptions: point particles, elastic collisions, no intermolecular forces except during collision, negligible volume compared to container. Derives pressure P = (1/3)ρv²_rms and connects macroscopic properties (P, V, T) to microscopic molecular motion.

Notes

  • Basic Postulates: (1) Gas consists of large number of identical molecules in random motion. (2) Molecular size ≪ mean separation (point masses). (3) Collisions with walls and between molecules are perfectly elastic. (4) No intermolecular forces except during brief collisions. (5) Time of collision ≪ time between collisions. (6) Newton's laws apply to each molecule.
  • Origin of Pressure: Pressure arises from momentum transfer when molecules collide with container walls. Each collision imparts impulse Δp = 2mv_x (for wall perpendicular to x-axis). Summing over all molecules and averaging gives P = (1/3)(N/V)m⟨v²⟩ = (1/3)ρv²_rms.
  • Root Mean Square Speed: v_rms = √(⟨v²⟩) = √(3RT/M) = √(3kT/m), where M is molar mass, m is molecular mass, k is Boltzmann constant. At same T, lighter molecules move faster.
  • Connection to Temperature: Average translational KE per molecule = (3/2)kT. Total translational KE of N molecules = (3/2)NkT = (3/2)nRT. Temperature is a measure of average molecular kinetic energy.
  • Ideal Gas Law Derivation: From P = (1/3)(N/V)m⟨v²⟩ and ⟨KE⟩ = (1/2)m⟨v²⟩ = (3/2)kT, we get PV = NkT = nRT — the ideal gas equation derived from microscopic mechanics.
  • Maxwell-Boltzmann Distribution: Molecular speeds follow a statistical distribution f(v) = 4π(m/2πkT)^(3/2) v² exp(-mv²/2kT). Most probable speed v_mp = √(2kT/m), average speed v_avg = √(8kT/πm), rms speed v_rms = √(3kT/m). Ratio v_mp : v_avg : v_rms ≈ 1 : 1.13 : 1.22.
  • Degrees of Freedom: Monatomic gas has 3 translational DOF → C_v = (3/2)R. Diatomic adds 2 rotational DOF at room T → C_v = (5/2)R. Vibrational modes activate at high T.
  • Mean Free Path: Average distance between collisions λ = 1/(√2 πd² n), where d is molecular diameter and n = N/V number density. At STP, λ ≈ 68 nm for air.
  • Limitations of Model: Assumes point particles and no interactions — fails at high pressure (finite volume matters) and low temperature (intermolecular attraction matters). Real gases require van der Waals corrections.

Specific Conditions

  • Low Density Required: Model assumes molecular volume ≪ container volume. Valid when gas is dilute (low P, high T). Breaks down near condensation point.
  • Elastic Collisions Only: Energy conservation in collisions is essential. Inelastic collisions would cause temperature decay — not observed in equilibrium gases.
  • Large Number of Molecules: Statistical averaging requires N ≫ 1. Fluctuations become significant for very small systems (nanoscale).
  • Thermal Equilibrium Assumed: Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution applies only at equilibrium. Non-equilibrium flows require Boltzmann transport equation.

Key Points

  • Gas pressure results from molecular collisions with container walls.
  • Temperature measures average translational kinetic energy: ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT.
  • v_rms = √(3RT/M): lighter gases move faster at same temperature.
  • PV = nRT derives directly from kinetic theory postulates.
  • Speed distribution follows Maxwell-Boltzmann statistics.
  • Model valid for dilute gases; real gases need corrections at high P or low T.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Pressure of an ideal gas equals one-third the product of density and mean square speed.
  • Statement 2: Absolute temperature is proportional to average translational kinetic energy per molecule.
  • Statement 3: The ideal gas law PV = nRT is derivable from Newtonian mechanics applied to molecular motion.
  • Statement 4: Molecular speeds in equilibrium follow the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution.
  • Statement 5: Kinetic theory bridges macroscopic thermodynamics and microscopic mechanics.

Important Notes

  • Boltzmann constant k = R/N_A = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J/K links macroscopic and microscopic scales.
  • At 300 K, nitrogen molecules have v_rms ≈ 517 m/s — supersonic yet no wind because motion is random.
  • Equipartition theorem assigns (1/2)kT energy per quadratic degree of freedom.
  • NEB frequently asks derivation of P = (1/3)ρv²_rms and explanation of temperature-KE relationship.

Exam Short Tricks

  • P = (1/3)ρv²_rms — memorize as 'one-third rho v-squared'.
  • ⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT — temperature IS kinetic energy (per molecule).
  • v_rms ∝ √(T/M) — double T increases speed by √2; double M decreases by √2.
  • Three speeds: v_mp < v_avg < v_rms (always in this order).
  • Monatomic C_v = 3R/2 comes from 3 translational DOF × (1/2)R each.

Exam Notes

  • Derive pressure formula step-by-step: single molecule → wall collision → force → sum over N molecules → average.
  • Explain why real gases deviate from ideal behavior using kinetic theory limitations.
  • Calculate v_rms numerically given T and M — ensure units: M in kg/mol, R = 8.314 J/(mol·K).
  • Relate Brownian motion to kinetic theory evidence for molecular reality.

Examples

  • Nitrogen (M = 0.028 kg/mol) at 300 K: v_rms = √(3×8.314×300/0.028) ≈ 517 m/s.
  • Hydrogen vs Oxygen at same T: v_rms(H₂)/v_rms(O₂) = √(M_O₂/M_H₂) = √(32/2) = 4. Hydrogen moves 4× faster.
  • Pressure calculation: N = 10²³ molecules, m = 4.65×10⁻²⁶ kg (N₂), V = 0.0224 m³, v_rms = 517 m/s → P = (1/3)(10²³×4.65×10⁻²⁶/0.0224)(517)² ≈ 1.01×10⁵ Pa = 1 atm.
  • Mean free path of air at STP: d ≈ 3.7×10⁻¹⁰ m, n ≈ 2.69×10²⁵ m⁻³ → λ ≈ 6.8×10⁻⁸ m = 68 nm.

Universal Facts

  • Daniel Bernoulli first proposed kinetic theory in 1738, but it was ignored until Clausius, Maxwell, and Boltzmann developed it rigorously (1857–1870s).
  • Brownian motion (observed 1827, explained by Einstein 1905) provided direct experimental confirmation of molecular motion.
  • At absolute zero (0 K), classical kinetic theory predicts zero molecular motion — quantum mechanics shows zero-point energy persists.
  • The Sun's core plasma obeys kinetic theory at 15 million K with v_rms ~ 500 km/s for protons.

Confusion

  • Thinking temperature measures total energy: It measures AVERAGE translational KE per molecule, not total internal energy.
  • Confusing v_rms with average speed: v_rms = √(⟨v²⟩) ≠ ⟨v⟩. They differ by ~8% for Maxwell distribution.
  • Believing molecules stop at 0°C: 0°C = 273 K; molecules still have substantial KE. Only at 0 K would classical motion cease.
  • Assuming all molecules move at same speed: Speeds follow a broad distribution; individual molecules constantly change speed via collisions.
  • Thinking pressure comes from molecular weight: Pressure arises from momentum transfer in collisions, not gravitational force on molecules.

According to kinetic theory, gas pressure is caused by:

Options
  • Gravitational pull on molecules
  • Molecular collisions with container walls
  • Intermolecular repulsion
  • Thermal expansion
Answer

B

Explanation

Pressure = rate of momentum transfer per unit area from wall collisions.

Average translational KE per molecule depends on:

Options
  • Pressure only
  • Volume only
  • Temperature only
  • Both P and V
Answer

C

Explanation

⟨KE⟩ = (3/2)kT — depends solely on absolute temperature.

3.

If temperature doubles, v_rms becomes:

Options
  • Double
  • Four times
  • √2 times
  • Unchanged
Answer

C

Explanation

v_rms ∝ √T, so doubling T multiplies v_rms by √2.

C
