Notes

  • Dimensions express a quantity using base quantities. In mechanics, mass M, length L and time T give velocity [v]=LT⁻¹, acceleration [a]=LT⁻² and force [F]=MLT⁻².
  • Find dimensions from definitions: work W=Fs gives [W]=ML²T⁻²; power P=W/t gives [P]=ML²T⁻³. Pressure is force per area, [p]=ML⁻¹T⁻².
  • Dimensional homogeneity requires every term added in an equation to have the same dimensions. In s=ut+(1/2)at², both ut and at² have dimension L.
  • Worked check: in v²=u²+2as, v² and u² are L²T⁻²; as is (LT⁻²)L=L²T⁻². The equation is dimensionally possible, though this test cannot prove it is correct.
  • Dimensional analysis can check units and infer powers in a relation, but cannot determine dimensionless constants such as 1/2 or functions involving dimensionless variables.
  • A dimensional check can reject an impossible equation and can suggest the powers in a relation, but it cannot find a dimensionless number such as 1/2 or 2π. An equation that survives the check may still be wrong.

Confusion

  • Dimensions differ from units: length has dimension L and SI unit metre.
  • A dimensionally correct equation may still be physically wrong.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Exercises

1.

Check whether v^2 = u^2 + 2 a s is dimensionally possible.

Steps
  • [v^2] = L^2 T^−2.
  • [u^2] has the same dimensions.
  • [a s] = (L T^−2)(L) = L^2 T^−2.
  • Every term matches, so the equation is dimensionally possible.
  • The check does not prove the 2.
Answer

Dimensionally possible. The factor 2 is not tested.
