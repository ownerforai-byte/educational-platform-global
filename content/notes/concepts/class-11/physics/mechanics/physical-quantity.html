Notes

  • A physical quantity is a measurable property expressed as magnitude and unit, such as 2.5 m or 4.0 s. A number without a unit is incomplete unless the quantity is dimensionless.
  • SI base quantities include length (m), mass (kg), time (s), current (A), temperature (K), amount (mol) and luminous intensity (cd). Derived units combine these; 1 N=1 kg m s⁻².
  • Scalars have magnitude only (mass, time, energy); vectors have magnitude and direction (displacement, velocity, force). Their addition rules differ.
  • Example: 3 m east followed by 4 m north gives path length 7 m but displacement magnitude √(3²+4²)=5 m. Distance and displacement are different quantities.
  • Weight is gravitational force measured in newtons; mass is measured in kilograms. Near Earth W=mg, but mass does not change when the gravitational field changes.
  • A physical quantity needs a magnitude and a unit, unless it is a pure ratio. Saying the length is 2 is incomplete. Base quantities are measured directly. Derived quantities such as speed and force are defined from them.

Confusion

  • A negative vector component indicates direction, not negative magnitude.
  • Do not report weight in kilograms or mass in newtons.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Summary

Physics uses quantities, units and correct mathematical structure. Distinguishing scalar from vector quantities prevents errors in displacement, velocity and force calculations.

Exercises

1.

Express 72 km/h as metres per second, and name the base quantities inside the unit.

Steps
  • 72 km/h = 72 × 1000 m / 3600 s.
  • 72 × 5/18 = 20.
  • The speed is 20 m/s.
  • The unit uses length and time, which are base quantities.
  • Mass is not in this unit.
Answer

20 m/s, built from length and time.
