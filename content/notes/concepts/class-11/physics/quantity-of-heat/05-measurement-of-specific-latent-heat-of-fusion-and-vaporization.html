Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Measuring the Hidden Heat

    Latent heat cannot be read on a thermometer; it must be weighed out in energy balances. Drop melting ice into warm water, or pass steam through it, and calorimetry converts temperature changes into L_f and L_v — the numbers behind refrigeration, steam engines and weather.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021)

    2.1 Specific latent heat of fusion and vaporization

    • Specific latent heat of fusion (Lf​): heat required to convert 1 kg of a solid to liquid at its melting point, with no temperature change. Water: 3.34×105 J/kg.
    • Specific latent heat of vaporization (Lv​): heat required to convert 1 kg of liquid to vapour at its boiling point. Water: 2.26×106 J/kg.

    Q=mL

    2.2 Measurement of L_f (method of mixtures)

    A calorimeter (mass mc​, specific heat cc​) contains warm water (mw​, T2​). Dry ice (m, at 0 °C) is added; final temperature T: mLf​+mcw​(T−0)=(mw​cw​+mc​cc​)(T2​−T) Lf​=m(mw​cw​+mc​cc​)(T2​−T)−mcw​T​ (ice must be dry — any attached water adds unaccounted mc_w(T−0) on the wrong side).

    2.3 Measurement of L_v (condensation method)

    Steam at 100 °C is passed into cold water in a calorimeter; mass of steam condensed = m (weighed before/after): mLv​+mcw​(100−T)=(mw​cw​+mc​cc​)(T−T2​) Lv​=m(mw​cw​+mc​cc​)(T−T2​)−mcw​(100−T)​

    2.4 Worked numerical (NEB 2021, 5m)

    Q (5m, 2021): A mass of steam at 100 °C is passed into water at 30 °C. If 5 g of steam condenses to raise 50 g of water from 30 °C to 50 °C, calculate the specific latent heat of vaporization of water.

    • Heat gained by water: 0.05×4200×(50−30)=4200 J
    • Heat lost by steam: mLv​+mcw​(100−50)=0.005Lv​+0.005×4200×50=0.005Lv​+1050
    • Balance: 0.005Lv​+1050=4200⇒Lv​=0.0053150​=6.3×105 J/kg

    2.5 Second worked numerical (NEB-style)

    Q (NEB-style): 40 g of ice at 0 °C is added to 200 g of water at 30 °C in a 60 g copper calorimeter (c = 385). Final temperature 15 °C. Find L_f.

    • Gained: 0.04Lf​+0.04×4200×15
    • Lost: (0.2×4200+0.06×385)(30−15)=(840+23.1)×15=12947 J
    • 0.04Lf​+2520=12947⇒Lf​=260675≈2.6×105 J/kg (close to 3.34×10⁵; difference = experimental losses).

Key Points

  • Q = mL; L_f = 3.34×10⁵; L_v = 2.26×10⁶ J/kg (standard values).
  • Steam method: mL_v + mc(100−T) = gained; Ice method: mL_f + mcT = gained.

Summary

Specific latent heat L is the heat to convert 1 kg across a phase change at constant temperature: Q=mL — for water Lf​=3.34×105 J/kg (fusion) and Lv​=2.26×106 J/kg (vaporization). Both are measured by calorimetry. Ice method: mLf​+mcw​T=(mw​cw​+mc​cc​)(T2​−T). Steam method: mLv​+mcw​(100−T)=(mw​cw​+mc​cc​)(T−T2​) — the steam both condenses and cools, giving two heat terms. NEB 2021's 5m problem (5 g steam raising 50 g water from 30 to 50 °C) yields Lv​=6.3×105 J/kg from the given data; always mind dry ice, gram-kilogram conversion and the calorimeter term.

Exercises

1.

5.0 g of dry ice at 0°C is added to 100 g of water at 25°C in a light cup. The final temperature is 20°C. Take c = 4200 J kg^-1 K^-1. Find L_f.

Steps
  • Convert masses: ice 0.0050 kg, water 0.100 kg. Neglect the cup.
  • Heat gained by ice = 0.0050 L_f + 0.0050 × 4200 × 20.
  • Heat lost by water = 0.100 × 4200 × (25 − 20) = 2100 J.
  • 0.0050 L_f + 420 = 2100.
  • 0.0050 L_f = 1680, so L_f = 3.36 × 10^5 J/kg.
Answer

3.36 × 10^5 J/kg.
