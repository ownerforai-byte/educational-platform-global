Newton's Law of Cooling — Exponential Solution & Graph

Class 11PhysicsQuantity Of Heat

Notes

  • Start from −mc dθ/dt = K (θ − θ0), with K and θ0 constant. This is the rate form of Newton's law of cooling.
  • Separate the variables: dθ / (θ − θ0) = −(K/mc) dt. Every temperature term is on the left and time is on the right.
  • Integrate from the initial temperature θ1 at t = 0 to the temperature θ at time t. The left side becomes the natural log of the ratio of the excesses.
  • The result is ln[(θ − θ0)/(θ1 − θ0)] = −(K/mc) t. Exponentiating both sides gives θ − θ0 = (θ1 − θ0) e^(−Kt/mc).
  • The excess temperature therefore falls exponentially. It becomes half in a fixed time that depends on mc/K, and it is never exactly zero at a finite time.
  • A graph of excess against time is a falling curve that flattens toward the axis. A graph of ln(excess) against time is a straight line whose slope is −K/mc.

Confusion

  • The temperature does not fall linearly with time.
  • The body does not overshoot θ0 in this solution.
  • θ1 is the initial temperature, not the surroundings.
  • The exponent is negative for a hot body, so the excess shrinks.

Exercises

1.

The excess temperature falls to 1/e of its initial value in 5.0 min. How long does it take to fall to half the initial excess?

Steps
  • θ − θ0 = (θ1 − θ0) e^(−t/τ), where τ = mc/K = 5.0 min, because at t = τ the factor is 1/e.
  • Half the excess means e^(−t/τ) = 1/2.
  • −t/τ = ln(1/2) = −ln 2.
  • t = τ ln 2.
  • ln 2 is about 0.693, so t = 5.0 × 0.693 = 3.5 min.
Answer

About 3.5 min.
