Newton's Law of Cooling — Identical Cooling Conditions

Class 11PhysicsQuantity Of Heat

Notes

  • Two liquids can be used to compare specific heats by cooling them in the same way. Identical conditions mean the same container shape, the same surface, the same room temperature, and the same temperature interval.
  • Under those conditions the surface factor K is the same for both, and the excess temperature follows the same history. The time spent in that interval then depends on the heat capacity.
  • From dθ/dt = −(K/mc) (θ − θ0), the time to pass between two temperatures is proportional to mc when K and the temperature limits are the same.
  • Therefore m1 c1 / t1 = m2 c2 / t2. The liquid that takes longer to cool through the same interval has the larger heat capacity.
  • If the containers differ, K differs and the times cannot be compared by this relation. If the intervals differ, the excess temperatures differ and the relation again fails.
  • The calorimeter itself has a heat capacity. A careful comparison uses the same calorimeter and includes its water equivalent on both sides, or uses masses large enough that the question says to neglect it.

Confusion

  • Same room is not enough if the cups have different surfaces.
  • Equal times do not mean equal specific heats unless the masses and the intervals match.
  • The law compares rates, not the final temperatures after an unlimited wait. Both end at room temperature.
  • A lid or a fan changes K.

Exercises

1.

Liquid A, mass 0.20 kg, cools from 70°C to 60°C in 4.0 min. Liquid B, mass 0.10 kg, takes 3.0 min for the same interval in the same cup. Find c_B / c_A.

Steps
  • The cup, the room and the interval match, so m c / t is the same for both.
  • m_A c_A / t_A = m_B c_B / t_B.
  • 0.20 c_A / 4.0 = 0.10 c_B / 3.0.
  • 0.050 c_A = 0.0333 c_B.
  • c_B / c_A = 0.050 / (0.10/3.0) = 0.050 × 30 = 1.5.
Answer

c_B / c_A = 1.5.
