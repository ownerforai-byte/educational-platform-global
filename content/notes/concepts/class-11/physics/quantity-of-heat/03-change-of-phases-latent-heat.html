Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Heat That Changes State, Not Temperature

    Boil a kettle and the thermometer freezes at 100 °C while the burner pours in energy. During a phase change every joule goes into breaking molecular bonds, not raising temperature — that hidden energy is latent heat, the reason steam burns and ice cools.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2017, 2023)

    2.1 Change of phase

    Matter changes phase — solid ↔ liquid ↔ gas — at characteristic temperatures (melting point, boiling point) for a given pressure. During the change:

    • Temperature stays constant even though heat is continuously supplied.
    • The supplied energy goes into increasing intermolecular potential energy (breaking bonds/overcoming attractions), not into kinetic energy.

    2.2 Latent heat

    Latent heat is the quantity of heat absorbed or released during a change of phase, without any change in temperature.

    Specific latent heat (L): heat required to change the phase of 1 kg of a substance at constant temperature: Q=mLL in J/kg

    2.3 Latent heat of fusion vs vaporization (NEB 2023)

    Latent heat of fusion (Lf​)Latent heat of vaporization (Lv​)
    Changesolid ↔ liquidliquid ↔ gas
    For water3.34×10⁵ J/kg2.26×10⁶ J/kg
    Directionabsorbed on melting, released on freezingabsorbed on boiling, released on condensing
    Magnitudesmaller (few bonds broken)larger (all remaining attractions overcome)

    Lv​>Lf​ because vaporization must completely separate molecules, while melting only loosens the lattice.

    2.4 Why temperature stays constant during a phase change (NEB 2017)

    The kinetic energy of molecules (which is temperature) does not change; all the supplied heat raises the potential energy of the molecules as bonds loosen or break. The energy is "latent" — hidden in molecular configuration, recoverable on the reverse change.

    2.5 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): How much heat melts 500 g of ice at 0 °C? (Lf​ = 3.34×10⁵ J/kg) Q=mL=0.5×3.34×105=1.67×105 J.

    Q (NEB-style): 20 g of steam at 100 °C condenses on skin. How much energy is released beyond that of 20 g of water at 100 °C? Extra = mLv​=0.02×2.26×106=4.52×104 J — why steam scalds so badly.

    • To Topic 51: calorimetry problems add mL terms alongside mcΔT.
    • To Topic 53: measurement of L by mixture and electrical methods.
    • To pressure effects: melting point of ice drops under pressure (regelation); boiling point rises with pressure (pressure cooker).

Temperature stays constant during a phase change because the supplied energy increases the potential energy of molecules (breaking intermolecular bonds) rather than their kinetic energy, which is what temperature measures.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying temperature "pauses" because heating stops — heat supply continues.
    2. Swapping the values of Lf​ and Lv​.
    3. Writing L's unit as J (it is J/kg).

Confusion

  • Latent ≠ lost: the energy is stored in molecular configuration and returned on freezing/condensing.
  • Temperature measures average KE — phase changes spend heat on PE, so the thermometer holds still.

Practice

Universal Facts

  • Evaporation of sweat removes ~2.26×10⁶ J/kg from skin — the body's main cooling mechanism.

Summary

During a change of phase — melting, freezing, boiling, condensing — the temperature holds constant while heat flows: every joule raises the potential energy of molecules (loosening or breaking bonds), not their kinetic energy, which is what temperature measures (NEB 2017). That hidden heat is latent heat, quantified per kilogram as Q=mL. For water, the latent heat of fusion is 3.34×10⁵ J/kg (solid ↔ liquid) and the latent heat of vaporization is 2.26×10⁶ J/kg (liquid ↔ gas) — nearly seven times larger, because vaporization must fully separate molecules (NEB 2023). The reverse changes release the same heat: steam scalds, sweat cools, ice packs freeze injuries.
