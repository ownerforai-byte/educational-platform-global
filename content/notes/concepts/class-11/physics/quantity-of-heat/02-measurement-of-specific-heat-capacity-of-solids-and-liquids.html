Notes

  • Specific heat capacity is measured by supplying a known quantity of heat and measuring the temperature rise, or by the method of mixtures, where heat lost by the hot body equals heat gained by the cold body.
  • For a solid, heat the sample to a known temperature θ1, then drop it into a calorimeter of cold water at θ2. The mixture settles at θ. The solid loses m_s c_s (θ1 − θ). The water and the calorimeter gain that heat.
  • If the calorimeter has mass m_c and specific heat c_c, and the water has mass m_w and specific heat c_w, then m_s c_s (θ1 − θ) = m_w c_w (θ − θ2) + m_c c_c (θ − θ2).
  • Solve for c_s. Every temperature in a difference can stay in °C. The masses must be in the unit that matches c, normally kilograms if c is in J kg^-1 K^-1.
  • A liquid's specific heat can be found the same way, by heating a hot solid of known c and dropping it into the cold liquid, or by an electric heater. The electrical heat is I V t, or I^2 R t, if the heater is in the liquid and losses are small.
  • Stir so the thermometer reads the mixture, and keep a lid on to reduce loss to the room. A loss that is ignored makes the calculated specific heat too small for the solid, because the solid appears to have given less heat to the water than it really lost.

Universal Facts

  • Heat Q = mcΔT; specific heat c, thermal capacity C = mc, heat capacity per mole = C. molar.
  • Latent heat L: Q = mL (change of state at constant T). Calorimetry: heat lost = heat gained.
  • Newton's law of cooling: rate of heat loss ∝ (T − T₀); dθ/dt = −k(θ − θ₀).

Summary

Specific heat is found from a known heat and a measured temperature change. In the method of mixtures the hot body's heat loss equals the cold body's heat gain, including the calorimeter when it is not negligible. An electrical heater supplies Q = I V t.

Exercises

1.

A 0.10 kg metal piece at 100°C is dropped into 0.20 kg of water at 20°C. The mixture reaches 25°C. Neglect the calorimeter. Take c_w = 4200 J kg^-1 K^-1. Find c of the metal.

Steps
  • Heat lost by the metal equals heat gained by the water.
  • 0.10 × c × (100 − 25) = 0.20 × 4200 × (25 − 20).
  • 0.10 × c × 75 = 0.20 × 4200 × 5.
  • 7.5 c = 4200.
  • c = 560 J kg^-1 K^-1.
Answer

560 J kg^-1 K^-1.
