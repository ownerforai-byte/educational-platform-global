Notes

  • The triple point is the unique pressure and temperature at which solid, liquid and vapour of a pure substance coexist in equilibrium. It is a point on the phase diagram, not the same as a normal-pressure melting or boiling point.
  • Water’s triple point is approximately 273.16 K (0.01°C) and 611.7 Pa. At ordinary atmospheric pressure, ice and liquid water coexist near 0°C at a different pressure.
  • While all three phases coexist, supplied energy can change their relative amounts while pressure and temperature remain fixed. Equal quantities of each phase are not required.
  • On a pressure–temperature diagram, the solid–liquid, liquid–vapour and solid–vapour boundary curves meet at the triple point. It provides a reproducible reference state for thermometry.
  • A phase diagram shows why pressure must accompany temperature when describing a phase equilibrium; changing pressure can shift the phase boundaries.
  • The triple point is a single point on the phase diagram, not a line. Solid, liquid and vapour of a pure substance sit together there, and neither pressure nor temperature can be changed without losing one of the phases. Water's triple point is the fixed point of the kelvin scale, at 273.16 K.

Confusion

  • Do not quote 0°C and 1 atm as the triple-point conditions.
  • The definition applies to one pure substance in equilibrium.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Exercises

1.

Explain why a kelvin temperature can be defined from the water triple point, and state that temperature.

Steps
  • The triple point occurs at one temperature and one pressure.
  • It can be reproduced in a sealed cell of pure water.
  • That temperature is defined as 273.16 K.
  • A degree kelvin is then 1/273.16 of that temperature.
  • The ice point, 0°C, is slightly lower, at 273.15 K, because it is not the triple point.
Answer

273.16 K.
