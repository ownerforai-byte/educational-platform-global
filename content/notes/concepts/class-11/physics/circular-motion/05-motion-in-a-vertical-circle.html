Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Circle That Fights Gravity

    Whirl a bucket of water overhead and nothing spills — provided you spin fast enough. Motion in a vertical circle differs from every previous case because gravity's pull along the radius changes constantly: strongest at the bottom, weakest at the top. The minimum speeds at those two points decide whether the loop is completed.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 The changing tension

    A bob of mass m on a string of length r moves in a vertical circle with speed v. Tension must supply centripetal force together with (or against) gravity's radial component:

    • At the lowest point: tension and gravity oppose radially: Tbottom​−mg=rmvB2​​⇒Tbottom​=rmvB2​​+mg — maximum tension.
    • At the highest point: gravity and tension both point inward: Ttop​+mg=rmvT2​​⇒Ttop​=rmvT2​​−mg — minimum tension.

    2.2 Minimum speeds

    At the top the string can only pull, not push. The loop is completed only if Ttop​≥0: rmvT2​​≥mg⇒vT,min​=gr​​ At this speed Ttop​=0 — gravity alone provides the centripetal force.

    At the bottom, energy conservation between top and bottom (21​mvB2​=21​mvT2​+mg⋅2r) gives: vB,min​=5gr​ and the corresponding minimum bottom tension T=6mg.

    If the bob is on a light rod (or inside a tube), it cannot fall away — vT,min​=0; the rod can push.

    2.3 Worked numerical (NEB 2024)

    Q (3m, 2024): A stone is whirled in a vertical circle of radius 1 m. What is the minimum velocity it must have at the top of the circle to complete the loop? (g = 9.8 m/s²) vT,min​=gr​=9.8×1​=3.13 m/s

    Q (NEB-style): For that stone, find the minimum speed at the bottom. vB,min​=5gr​=5×9.8×1​=49​=7 m/s.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 24: Fc​ here is supplied by tension plus gravity's radial component — the general principle applied.
    • To Conservation of energy (Topic 19): speed differences between top and bottom come purely from the 2r height change.
    • To real rides: roller-coaster loops and vertical carousels obey the same vtop​≥gr​ condition (with normal force replacing tension).

  • 4. Exam Orientation and Previous Year Questions (NEB)

    4.1 Verified PYQs from the solved-paper bank

    YearUnitTopicQuestionMarksType
    2080/2024MechanicsCircular motionA stone is whirled in a vertical circle of radius 1 m. What is the minimum velocity it must have at the top of the circle to complete the loop? (g = 9.8 m/s²)3Numerical

    4.2 Model answer

    Q (3m, 2024): At the top, for the loop to be completed the string must remain taut: T+mg=mv2/r with T≥0. The limiting case T=0 gives mg=mv2/r, so vmin​=gr​=9.8×1​=3.13 m/s.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Quoting 5gr​ when the question asks for the top speed (that is the bottom speed).
    2. Forgetting that Ttop​=0 is the limiting condition, not T=mg.
    3. Missing the height difference 2r in the energy step.

Confusion

  • Top vs bottom minimum speeds: top needs √(gr); bottom needs √(5gr) — five times the energy scale.
  • String vs rod: a string can only pull (v_top ≥ √gr); a rod can push (v_top ≥ 0).

Practice

  • Why is tension greatest at the bottom of the swing?

Universal Facts

  • Water stays in a bucket swung overhead for the same reason: at √(gr) the water presses on the bucket bottom, not the other way.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (4m): A 0.2 kg bob completes a vertical loop of radius 0.5 m with the minimum possible top speed. Find v_top, v_bottom and the maximum tension. — v_top = √(9.8×0.5) = 2.21 m/s; v_bottom = √5 × 2.21 = 4.95 m/s; T_max = mv_B²/r + mg = 0.2×49/0.5 + 1.96 = 19.6 + 1.96 = 21.6 N (= 6mg ✓ since 6×1.96 = 11.8... check: mv_B²/r = 0.2×(4.95²)/0.5 = 9.8 N; +mg = 1.96 → T = 11.76 N = 6mg ✓).

Formulas

    • T_top = mv_T²/r − mg; T_bottom = mv_B²/r + mg
    • v_T,min = √(gr); v_B,min = √(5gr)
    • Energy: ½mv_B² = ½mv_T² + 2mgr

Key Points

  • Ttop​=mvT2​/r−mg; Tbottom​=mvB2​/r+mg.
  • vT,min​=gr​; vB,min​=5gr​; Tbottom,max​=6mg at the limit.

Summary

In a vertical circle gravity's radial pull changes with position, so tension varies: Tbottom​=mvB2​/r+mg (maximum) and Ttop​=mvT2​/r−mg (minimum). A string can only pull, so the loop completes only if Ttop​≥0, giving the minimum top speed vT​=gr​ — for r = 1 m that is 3.13 m/s (NEB 2024). Energy conservation across the 2r height difference then fixes the minimum bottom speed vB​=5gr​, where the bottom tension reaches 6mg. A rod or a roller-coaster track can push, so its minimum top speed is zero.

Special Notes

  • The factor √(5gr) for minimum bottom speed (string) is the single most tested result in NEB exams.
  • Always distinguish between string (T ≥ 0) and rod (T can be negative / pushing).
  • If the string goes slack, the body becomes a projectile — follow parabolic path.
  • T_bottom = 6mg only when v_bottom = √(5gr) — this is the minimum case.
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Find the minimum speed at the bottom to complete the vertical circle'.

Important Statements

  • "At the highest point, gravity itself acts as the centripetal force in the limiting case."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQ: minimum top velocity (2024, 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Top: √(gr). Bottom: √(5gr). Tension at bottom limit: 6mg."

Exam Notes

  • The 2024 question is the direct one-liner √(gr) — but know the full tension analysis for 5m variants.

Specific Conditions

  • Energy Conservation (No Non-Conservative Work): Total mechanical energy (K+U) is conserved if only gravity and tension act (tension does zero work). v2=u2−2gh where h=R(1−cosheta) from the lowest point.
  • String vs. Rod Constraint:

    • String: Can only pull (tension T≥0). If To0, the string goes slack and the particle follows a parabolic projectile path until the string tightens again.
    • Rigid Rod: Can push or pull (T can be negative/compressive). The particle completes the circle even if speed at top is zero (but vtop​≥0 is required).
  • Critical Speeds (String): vtop​≥gR​ (taut string at top); vbottom​≥5gR​ (minimum to complete circle). At the horizontal point (heta=90∘), minimum speed is 2gR​ for the string to remain taut.
