Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Gravity and Tension in Balance

    A bob whirled on a string so that the string traces a cone executes uniform circular motion in a horizontal plane. Two forces — tension along the string and gravity straight down — combine to provide exactly the centripetal force needed. The result is a clock-like period depending only on the vertical depth of the cone.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Setup and force resolution

    A bob of mass m on a string of length L revolves in a horizontal circle of radius r=Lsinθ, where θ is the angle the string makes with the vertical. Forces on the bob:

    • Weight mg downward.
    • Tension T along the string (at angle θ to the vertical).

    Resolving T:

    • Vertical: Tcosθ=mg (no vertical acceleration).
    • Horizontal: Tsinθ=rmv2​ (supplies centripetal force).

    2.2 Period derivation

    Dividing: tanθ=rgv2​. With v=T0​2πr​ (period T0​): tanθ=T02​g4π2r​=T02​g4π2Lsinθ​⇒T0​=2πgLcosθ​​

    Note Lcosθ = vertical depth h of the cone below the support: T0​=2πgh​​​

    Properties:

    • The period is independent of the mass of the bob.
    • It depends only on the vertical depth h, not on the string length or speed separately.
    • Faster spin → smaller θ... actually faster spin → larger θ (wider cone, but same depth? No — for fixed L, faster spin raises the bob, decreasing h and increasing the period demand; the system settles where tanθ=4π2r/T02​g).

    2.3 Worked numerical (NEB 2024)

    Q (3m, 2024): A conical pendulum has a string 1 m long making 30° with the vertical. Calculate its period of revolution. (g = 9.8 m/s²)

    • h=Lcosθ=1×cos30°=0.866 m
    • T0​=2π0.866/9.8​=2π0.0884​=2π×0.297=1.87 s

    Q (NEB-style): Find the tension for a 0.2 kg bob at θ = 30°. T=mg/cosθ=0.2×9.8/0.866=2.26 N.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 24: the horizontal component of tension is the centripetal force.
    • To Simple pendulum: the simple pendulum swings in a vertical arc with T=2πL/g​; the conical pendulum circles horizontally with T=2πLcosθ/g​ — always shorter for the same L.
    • To Banking (Topic 27): identical force-resolution pattern with the normal force replacing tension.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Using L instead of Lcosθ in the period formula.
    2. Using sinθ for the depth — the depth is Lcosθ.
    3. Treating it as a simple pendulum with 2πL/g​.

Confusion

  • Conical ≠ simple pendulum: the conical bob moves in a horizontal circle; its period uses L cos θ, not L.
  • θ is measured from the vertical, not from the horizontal.

Practice

Universal Facts

  • The conical pendulum was historically used as a gravity meter — measuring T₀ and h gives g.

Formulas

    • T cos θ = mg; T sin θ = mv²/r
    • tan θ = v²/rg
    • T₀ = 2π√(L cos θ/g) = 2π√(h/g)
    • r = L sin θ

Key Points

  • Tcosθ=mg; Tsinθ=mv2/r; period T0​=2πLcosθ/g​=2πh/g​.
  • Independent of bob mass.

Summary

A conical pendulum whirs its bob in a horizontal circle; tension splits into Tcosθ=mg (vertical balance) and Tsinθ=mv2/r (centripetal force). Dividing and inserting v=2πr/T0​ gives the period T0​=2πLcosθ/g​=2πh/g​ — set only by the vertical depth of the cone, independent of mass. A 1 m string at 30° has depth 0.866 m and period 1.87 s (NEB 2024). It differs from the simple pendulum, whose period 2πL/g​ is always longer for the same length, and it mirrors the banked-road analysis where the normal force replaces tension.

Special Notes

  • The period is independent of mass — this is a key result that NEB frequently tests.
  • The faster the rotation, the larger the angle θ (the cone becomes flatter).
  • The tension is always greater than mg: T = mg/cos θ (since cos θ < 1).
  • The minimum period (θ → 0) equals the period of a simple pendulum of the same length.
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Show that the period is independent of mass' — practice this derivation.

Specific Conditions

  • Vertical Angle Limit: For the conical pendulum to exist (cosheta≤1), the angular velocity must satisfy ω≥g/L​. Below this speed, the bob cannot maintain a horizontal circular path and will simply hang at the bottom.
  • String Tension Constraint: The tension in the string is always greater than the weight of the bob (T=mg/cosheta>mg) because it must support the weight and provide the centripetal force.
  • Small Angle Assumption: The formulas rely on the string remaining taut. If the bob rotates too slowly, the string goes slack, and the motion ceases to be a horizontal circle (conical pendulum) and becomes a vertical oscillation.
