Notes

  • Conical pendulum: a small bob of mass m attached to a string of length L, moving in a horizontal circle at constant speed such that the string traces out a cone. The string makes angle θ with the vertical and the bob moves in a circle of radius r = L sin θ.
  • Forces and geometry: two forces act on the bob: tension T along the string, and weight mg downward. The tension has vertical component T cos θ (balances weight) and horizontal component T sin θ (provides centripetal force).
  • Key equations: T cos θ = mg (vertical equilibrium) ... (1); T sin θ = mω²r = mω²L sin θ (centripetal) ... (2). From (2): T = mω²L. Substituting into (1): mω²L cos θ = mg → cos θ = g/(ω²L). Also: ω = √(g/L cos θ) and T_period = 2π√(L cos θ/g).
  • Key insight — period depends only on L cos θ: the period T = 2π√(L cos θ/g) is independent of the mass of the bob. A taller cone (larger θ, smaller cos θ) has a shorter period (faster rotation). The minimum period is when θ → 0: T_min = 2π√(L/g) (same as a simple pendulum of length L).
  • Worked example: a 0.2 kg bob on a 2 m string rotates with θ = 30°. cos 30° = 0.866. Angular speed: ω = √(9.8/(2 × 0.866)) = √5.66 = 2.38 rad/s. Period: T = 2π/2.38 = 2.64 s. Tension: T = mω²L = 0.2 × 5.66 × 2 = 2.26 N. Radius: r = 2 × sin 30° = 1 m.
  • Comparison with simple pendulum: both have period T = 2π√(L_eff/g). For a simple pendulum, L_eff = L. For a conical pendulum, L_eff = L cos θ (the vertical height of the cone). So a conical pendulum swings slower than a simple pendulum of the same string length.
  • NEB exam focus: deriving the relation cos θ = g/(ω²L); finding the period, angular speed, or tension; showing that the period is independent of mass; comparing conical and simple pendulums.
  • Practical examples: a tethered aeroplane model, a ball on a string swung in a horizontal circle, vehicles banking on a curved road (same geometry — the road provides the 'tension' component).
  • Deriving the key result step by step: from vertical equilibrium: T cos θ = mg → T = mg/cos θ. From horizontal force: T sin θ = mω²r = mω²L sin θ. Canceling sin θ (assuming θ ≠ 0): T = mω²L. Equating: mω²L = mg/cos θ → ω² = g/(L cos θ) → ω = √(g/L cos θ). This derivation is the standard NEB proof question.
  • Period formula insight: T_period = 2π/ω = 2π√(L cos θ/g). Note that L cos θ is the VERTICAL HEIGHT h of the cone. So T = 2π√(h/g) — the period depends only on the vertical height, not on the radius or the mass. A cone with height 1 m has the same period regardless of how wide it is.
  • Relation to simple pendulum: a simple pendulum of length L has period T = 2π√(L/g). A conical pendulum of string length L has period T = 2π√(L cos θ/g) = 2π√(h/g). Since cos θ < 1, the conical pendulum's period is LONGER than a simple pendulum of the same string length — it swings more slowly.
  • Connection to banking of roads: the conical pendulum and a banked road share identical force geometry. In the pendulum, tension provides both vertical support and centripetal force. In a banked road, the normal force plays the same dual role. The equation tan θ = v²/(rg) for banking is equivalent to the conical pendulum's geometry.
  • Watt's governor: the conical pendulum is the basis of the centrifugal governor used in steam engines. As the engine speeds up, the bobs rise (θ increases), which mechanically reduces the steam supply. This is a real-world application that NEB sometimes references.

  • Setup: a mass m attached to a string of length l rotates in a horizontal circle, making a constant angle θ with the vertical.
  • Forces: tension T has components Tcosθ upward balancing weight mg, and Tsinθ inward providing centripetal force.
  • Period: Tperiod​=2πglcosθ​​. The period depends on the string length, the angle, and gravity.
  • Special case — small angles: when θ is small, cosθ≈1, so Tperiod​≈2πl/g​, the same as a simple pendulum.
  • Speed: v=grtanθ​, where r=lsinθ is the radius of the horizontal circle. Faster spin means a larger angle θ.
  • Height of the pendulum: the vertical distance from the suspension point to the mass is h=lcosθ. The period depends only on this height: Tperiod​=2πh/g​.
  • Angular velocity: ω=g/(lcosθ)​=g/h​. As the mass spins faster, θ increases, h decreases, and the period decreases.
  • Tension: from Tcosθ=mg, we get T=mg/cosθ. As θ increases, tension increases without bound. At θ=90∘, tension would be infinite — physically impossible.
  • Maximum angle limit: as ω→∞, θ→90∘ but never reaches it. The mass rises toward the horizontal but cannot get there because the required tension would be infinite.
  • Analogy with a spinning conical top: the path swept by the string traces a cone, hence the name. The path on a horizontal plane is circular, but the full trajectory in 3D is the surface of a cone.
  • Relation to simple pendulum: a conical pendulum with very small θ behaves like a simple pendulum of the same length, oscillating back and forth rather than spinning around.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'The tension in the string equals mg.' ✅ The vertical component of tension equals mg. The total tension is greater: T = mg/cos θ (since cos θ < 1, T > mg).
  • ❌ 'The bob is in equilibrium.' ✅ The bob is in vertical equilibrium (T cos θ = mg) but NOT horizontal equilibrium — it accelerates toward the centre (centripetal acceleration).
  • ❌ 'The period depends on the mass of the bob.' ✅ The period T = 2π√(L cos θ/g) is independent of mass. A heavier bob rotates at the same period (for the same θ).
  • ❌ 'A faster rotation gives a smaller θ.' ✅ A FASTER rotation gives a LARGER θ (the cone becomes flatter). From cos θ = g/(ω²L), as ω increases, cos θ decreases, so θ increases.
  • ❌ 'The radius of the circle is L.' ✅ The radius is r = L sin θ, which is LESS than L. Only if θ = 90° would r = L (but then cos θ = 0 and the equations break down).
  • ❌ 'The centripetal force is a new force to add to the FBD.' ✅ The centripetal force IS the horizontal component of tension: T sin θ = mω²r. No new force is added.

  • The period of a conical pendulum Tperiod​=2πlcosθ/g​ is always less than that of a simple pendulum of the same length (2πl/g​) because cosθ<1.
  • Tension in a conical pendulum is always greater than mg because T=mg/cosθ and cosθ<1. The faster the rotation, the greater the tension.
  • The period of a conical pendulum decreases as it spins faster (larger θ), unlike a simple pendulum where period depends only on length and g.
  • The period depends on the vertical height h=lcosθ, not on the radius of the circle. Two conical pendulums with the same h have the same period regardless of their lengths.
  • The conical pendulum is not a 2D problem. The mass moves in a horizontal circle, but the string sweeps out a cone in 3D space.

Universal Facts

  • The period of a conical pendulum (T = 2π√(L cos θ/g)) is independent of the mass of the bob.
  • A conical pendulum's period depends only on the vertical height of the cone (L cos θ), not on the radius of the circle.
  • The faster the rotation, the larger the angle θ — the cone becomes flatter.
  • The minimum period of a conical pendulum (θ → 0) equals the period of a simple pendulum of the same length.
  • The geometry of the conical pendulum is the same as a banking vehicle — the 'tension' is replaced by the normal reaction.
  • The period of a conical pendulum (T=2πgLcosθ​​) is independent of the mass of the bob.
  • A conical pendulum's period depends only on the vertical height of the cone (Lcosθ), not on the radius of the circle.
  • The faster the rotation, the larger the angle θ — the cone becomes flatter.
  • The minimum period of a conical pendulum (θ→0) equals the period of a simple pendulum of the same length.
  • The period of a conical pendulum depends only on the vertical height from the suspension point to the mass's plane of rotation: T=2πh/g​.
  • As a conical pendulum spins faster, it rises higher (larger θ), reducing h and decreasing the period. This is the opposite of intuition for many students.
  • The tension in the string is always greater than mg because a component of tension must provide both the centripetal force and support the weight.
  • A conical pendulum cannot rotate with θ=90∘ because this would require infinite tension and infinite angular velocity.
  • The conical pendulum demonstrates that the period of circular motion depends on the effective height, not the string length alone, which connects it to the physics of satellites at different altitudes.

Specific Conditions

  • Vertical Angle Limit: For the conical pendulum to exist (cosheta≤1), the angular velocity must satisfy ω≥g/L​. Below this speed, the bob cannot maintain a horizontal circular path and will simply hang at the bottom.
  • String Tension Constraint: The tension in the string is always greater than the weight of the bob (T=mg/cosheta>mg) because it must support the weight and provide the centripetal force.
  • Small Angle Assumption: The formulas rely on the string remaining taut. If the bob rotates too slowly, the string goes slack, and the motion ceases to be a horizontal circle (conical pendulum) and becomes a vertical oscillation.
