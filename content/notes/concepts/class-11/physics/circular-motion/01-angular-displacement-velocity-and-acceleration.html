Notes

  • Angular displacement (Δθ): the angle through which a body rotates about a fixed axis. Measured in radians (rad). 1 rad = 57.3°, 2π rad = 360°. Angular displacement is a vector along the axis (right-hand rule): positive for anticlockwise rotation.
  • Angular velocity (ω): the rate of change of angular displacement: ω=dθ/dt (instantaneous), ω=Δθ/t (average). Unit: rad/s. Direction: along the axis (right-hand rule). For uniform circular motion, ω is constant.
  • Angular acceleration (α): the rate of change of angular velocity: α=dω/dt=d2θ/dt2. Unit: rad/s2. When α is constant, the kinematic equations mirror linear motion.
  • Relationship between linear and angular quantities (for circular motion): v=rω (linear speed = radius × angular speed), at​=rα (tangential acceleration), ac​=v2/r=rω2 (centripetal acceleration). These links connect rotational and translational motion.
  • Kinematic equations for constant angular acceleration (mirror linear v=u+at, etc.): ω=ω0​+αt; θ=ω0​t+21​αt2; ω2=ω02​+2αθ; θ=(ω+ω0​)t/2. The structure is identical to linear motion — just swap xightarrowθ, vightarrowω, aightarrowα.
  • Worked example — spinning wheel: a wheel starts from rest and accelerates uniformly at 2 rad/s2 for 5 s. Angular displacement: θ=0+21​×2×25=25 rad. Final angular velocity: ω=0+2×5=10 rad/s. Number of revolutions: 25/(2π)≈3.98 rev.
  • Worked example — decelerating fan: a fan blade rotating at 120 rpm (120×2π/60=12.57 rad/s) comes to rest in 8 s. Angular acceleration: α=(0−12.57)/8=−1.57 rad/s2. Angular displacement: θ=12.57×8/2=50.27 rad ≈8 rev.
  • NEB exam focus: converting between rpm and rad/s; applying the kinematic equations for constant angular acceleration; linking linear and angular quantities (v=rω, a=rω2); finding number of revolutions from angular displacement.
  • Right-hand rule for angular quantities: curl the fingers of your right hand in the direction of rotation; your thumb points along the angular velocity/acceleration vector. For anticlockwise rotation (as viewed from above), the vector points upward; for clockwise, it points downward.
  • Angular displacement is NOT a simple angle: while measured in radians, angular displacement has direction (along the axis). For rotations greater than 2π, the magnitude keeps increasing — unlike a simple angle which resets after 360°. This is why angular displacement is a true vector quantity.
  • Non-uniform circular motion: when angular acceleration α=0, both tangential acceleration (at​=rα) and centripetal acceleration (ac​=rω2) are present. The total acceleration is the vector sum: a=at2​+ac2​​. The direction of total acceleration is at angle ϕ=arctan(ac​/at​) from the tangential direction.
  • Small angle approximation: for oscillatory motion (pendulums, springs), when θ is small (θ<0.1 rad ≈6∘), we can approximate sinθ≈θ and cosθ≈1. This linearizes the equations and makes them solvable analytically.
  • Angular quantities as vectors — addition is NOT commutative for large angles: while infinitesimal angular displacements dθ are vectors (they commute), finite angular displacements do NOT commute. Rotating 90° about x then 90° about y gives a different result than rotating 90° about y then 90° about x. This is why angular velocity is a true vector but finite angular displacement is not.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Angular displacement and angle are the same.' ✅ Angular displacement is a vector (along the axis), while angle is a scalar. For a full revolution, angular displacement = 2π rad, but the body returns to its starting position.
  • ❌ 'Angular velocity is always constant in circular motion.' ✅ Only in UNIFORM circular motion is ω constant. In non-uniform circular motion, ω changes and there is angular acceleration.
  • ❌ 'The direction of angular velocity is tangential.' ❌ The direction of angular velocity is ALONG THE AXIS of rotation (right-hand rule), not tangential. Tangential velocity is v = rω, but ω itself points along the axis.
  • ❌ 'Angular acceleration means the body is speeding up.' ✅ Angular acceleration is the rate of change of angular velocity. If α and ω have the same sign, the body speeds up; if opposite signs, it slows down.
  • ❌ 'The kinematic equations for rotation are different from linear motion.' ✅ They are IDENTICAL in form: just replace x with θ, v with ω, and a with α. The same problem-solving approach works.
  • ❌ 'rpm and rad/s are the same.' ✅ 1 rpm = 2π/60 rad/s ≈ 0.105 rad/s. Always convert rpm to rad/s before using formulas.

Universal Facts

  • Angular velocity is a vector along the axis of rotation — its direction is given by the right-hand rule.
  • The kinematic equations for constant angular acceleration are identical in form to those for linear motion.
  • v = rω links linear speed to angular speed — this is why the outside of a rotating disc moves faster than the inside.
  • Angular acceleration is caused by torque (rotational analogue of force): τ = Iα, where I is the moment of inertia.
  • The number of revolutions is θ/(2π) — always divide angular displacement in radians by 2π to get revolutions.

Examples

  • rpm to rad/s: A wheel rotates at 180 rpm. ω = 180 × 2π/60 = 18.85 rad/s.
  • Kinematic equation: A wheel starts from rest and accelerates at 3 rad/s². After 4 s: ω = 12 rad/s, θ = 24 rad, revolutions = 24/2π ≈ 3.82 rev.
  • Linear-angular link: A disc of radius 0.5 m rotates at 10 rad/s. Linear speed at the edge: v = 0.5 × 10 = 5 m/s. Centripetal acceleration: a = 0.5 × 100 = 50 m/s².
  • Deceleration: A fan at 90 rpm stops in 6 s. ω₀ = 9.42 rad/s. α = −1.57 rad/s². θ = 9.42 × 6/2 = 28.27 rad ≈ 4.5 rev.
  • Non-uniform motion: A particle moves in a circle of radius 2 m with ω = 3t rad/s. At t = 2 s: v = 12 m/s, a_t = 6 m/s², a_c = 72 m/s².

Formulas

  • Angular velocity: ω = dθ/dt (instantaneous), ω = Δθ/t (average)
  • Angular acceleration: α = dω/dt = d²θ/dt²
  • Kinematic equations (constant α): ω = ω₀ + αt; θ = ω₀t + ½αt²; ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ
  • Linear-angular links: v = rω; a_t = rα; a_c = v²/r = rω²
  • rpm conversion: ω (rad/s) = rpm × 2π/60; revolutions = θ/(2π)

Key Points

  • Angular displacement is measured in radians; 2π rad = 360° = 1 revolution.
  • Angular velocity is a vector along the axis (right-hand rule), not tangential.
  • The kinematic equations for rotation mirror linear motion: x→θ, v→ω, a→α.
  • v = rω links linear and angular speed — the basis of all circular motion problems.
  • Always convert rpm to rad/s before using formulas: multiply by 2π/60.

Summary

Angular displacement (θ), angular velocity (ω = dθ/dt), and angular acceleration (α = dω/dt) describe rotational motion. For constant angular acceleration, the kinematic equations mirror linear motion. The links v = rω and a_c = rω² connect rotational and translational quantities. rpm is converted to rad/s by multiplying by 2π/60.

Special Notes

  • Angular velocity points ALONG THE AXIS (right-hand rule), not tangentially — this is the most common confusion.
  • The kinematic equations for rotation are IDENTICAL in form to linear motion — just swap the variables.
  • Always convert rpm to rad/s before using formulas — forgetting this is the most common calculation error.
  • The number of revolutions is θ/(2π) — divide angular displacement in radians by 2π.
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Find the number of revolutions' — use θ/(2π), not θ in radians.

Specific Conditions

  • Axis of Rotation Constraint: Angular quantities (heta, ω, α) are defined with respect to a specific fixed axis. If the axis of rotation changes orientation, the angular velocity becomes a time-dependent vector, and simple scalar kinematics no longer apply.
  • Kinematic Equations Applicability: The kinematic equations (analogs of v=u+at) for angular motion are strictly valid only for constant angular acceleration (α=extconstant). For time-varying α(t), direct integration of dω/dt is required.
  • Reference Frame: Angular velocity is defined in an inertial frame. If observing from a rotating reference frame, non-inertial (fictitious) forces like centrifugal or Coriolis forces appear, which must be accounted for if analyzing dynamics in that frame.
