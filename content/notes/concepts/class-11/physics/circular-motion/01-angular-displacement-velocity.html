Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Describing Spin

    A particle moving in a circle is best described by angle instead of distance. Angular displacement, angular velocity and angular acceleration are the rotational twins of displacement, velocity and acceleration — and the bridge between them is the radius.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    Linear (translational)Angular (rotational)Relation
    Displacement s (m)Angular displacement θ (rad)s=rθ
    Velocity v (m/s)Angular velocity ω (rad/s)v=rω
    Acceleration a (m/s²)Angular acceleration α (rad/s²)a=rα

    Angular displacement (θ): the angle swept by the position vector, measured in radians (dimensionless: 2π rad = 360°). Anticlockwise is positive by convention.

    Angular velocity (ω): rate of change of angular displacement, ω=dtdθ​; SI unit rad/s. For n revolutions per second, ω=2πn; for N rpm, ω=602πN​.

    Angular acceleration (α): rate of change of angular velocity, α=dtdω​; SI unit rad/s².

    Period and frequency: T=ω2π​, f=T1​=2πω​.

  • 3. Worked Numericals (NEB 2023, 2024)

    Q (4m, 2023): A flywheel of radius 0.5 m rotates at a constant speed of 300 revolutions per minute. Find its angular velocity and the linear speed of a point on its rim.

    • ω=602πN​=602π×300​=10π=31.4 rad/s
    • v=ωr=31.4×0.5=15.7 m/s
    • (bonus) ac​=ω2r=(31.4)2×0.5≈4.9×102 m/s²

    Q (3m, 2024): A wheel of diameter 0.8 m rotates at 300 revolutions per minute. Find its angular velocity and the linear speed of a point on its rim.

    • r=0.4 m; ω=602π×300​=31.4 rad/s
    • v=ωr=31.4×0.4=12.6 m/s

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Kinematics (Unit 3): identical structure — θ,ω,α play the roles of s,v,a; the rotational equations of motion (ω=ω0​+αt, etc.) mirror v=v0​+at.
    • To Topic 24: v=rω is the seed of centripetal acceleration ac​=ω2r=v2/r.
    • Radian: dimensionless because it is arc length ÷ radius.

Confusion

  • rpm vs rad/s: 300 rpm is 5 revolutions per second = 10π rad/s — always convert before using v=ωr.
  • Radian has no dimensions: angular quantities carry 'rad' as a label, not a base unit — v=ωr still works dimensionally.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A disc of radius 0.2 m spins at 1200 rpm. Find ω, the rim speed and the period. — ω = 2π(1200)/60 = 125.7 rad/s; v = ωr = 25.1 m/s; T = 2π/ω = 0.05 s.

Formulas

    • ω = dθ/dt; α = dω/dt
    • ω = 2πn = 2πN/60 (n rev/s, N rpm)
    • s = rθ; v = rω; a = rα
    • T = 2π/ω; f = ω/2π

Key Points

  • θ (rad), ω=dθ/dt (rad/s), α=dω/dt (rad/s²).
  • s=rθ, v=rω, a=rα; ω=2πN/60 for N rpm.
  • T=2π/ω, f=ω/2π.

Summary

Circular motion is described rotationally: angular displacement θ (radians), angular velocity ω=dθ/dt and angular acceleration α=dω/dt. They convert to linear quantities through the radius: s=rθ, v=rω, a=rα. A machine rated at N rpm spins at ω=2πN/60 rad/s — a 0.5 m flywheel at 300 rpm has ω=31.4 rad/s and rim speed 15.7 m/s (NEB 2023); a 0.8 m-diameter wheel at the same rate has rim speed 12.6 m/s (NEB 2024). Every point of a rigid body shares ω; only v scales with r. Period and frequency follow: T=2π/ω, f=ω/2π.

Special Notes

  • Angular velocity points ALONG THE AXIS (right-hand rule), not tangentially — this is the most common confusion.
  • The kinematic equations for rotation are IDENTICAL in form to linear motion — just swap the variables.
  • Always convert rpm to rad/s before using formulas — forgetting this is the most common calculation error.
  • The number of revolutions is θ/(2π) — divide angular displacement in radians by 2π.
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Find the number of revolutions' — use θ/(2π), not θ in radians.

Branches
1.
Topic

Angular Displacement (θ)

Details
  • Measured in radians: 2π rad = 360° = 1 rev
  • Vector quantity — direction along axis (RHR)
  • θ > 2π allowed (unlike plain angle)
  • θ = s/r where s is arc length, r is radius
2.
Topic

Angular Velocity (ω)

Details
  • ω = dθ/dt (instantaneous), ω = Δθ/Δt (average)
  • Unit: rad/s; also rpm (revolutions per minute)
  • Vector along axis via right-hand rule
  • Uniform circular motion: ω = constant
3.
Topic

Angular Acceleration (α)

Details
  • α = dω/dt = d²θ/dt²
  • Unit: rad/s²
  • Positive α: speeding up (if ω > 0)
  • Negative α: slowing down (if ω > 0)
4.
Topic

Linear-Angular Links

Details
  • v = rω (linear speed)
  • a_t = rα (tangential acceleration)
  • a_c = v²/r = rω² (centripetal acceleration)
  • s = rθ (arc length)
5.
Topic

Kinematic Equations

Details
  • ω = ω₀ + αt
  • θ = ω₀t + ½αt²
  • ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ
  • θ = (ω + ω₀)t/2
6.
Topic

Conversions

Details
  • rpm → rad/s: multiply by 2π/60
  • rad/s → rpm: multiply by 60/2π
  • Degrees → rad: multiply by π/180
  • Revolutions → rad: multiply by 2π

Specific Conditions

  • Axis of Rotation Constraint: Angular quantities (heta, ω, α) are defined with respect to a specific fixed axis. If the axis of rotation changes orientation, the angular velocity becomes a time-dependent vector, and simple scalar kinematics no longer apply.
  • Kinematic Equations Applicability: The kinematic equations (analogs of v=u+at) for angular motion are strictly valid only for constant angular acceleration (α=extconstant). For time-varying α(t), direct integration of dω/dt is required.
  • Reference Frame: Angular velocity is defined in an inertial frame. If observing from a rotating reference frame, non-inertial (fictitious) forces like centrifugal or Coriolis forces appear, which must be accounted for if analyzing dynamics in that frame.
