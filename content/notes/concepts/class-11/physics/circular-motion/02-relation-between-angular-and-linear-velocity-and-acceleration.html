Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — One Motion, Two Languages

    The same rotating wheel can be described in radians per second or metres per second. The relations s=rθ, v=rω, at​=rα translate between the two languages — and they explain why outer rim points travel faster than inner ones on the same rigid body.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Derivation of v=rω

    A particle on a circle of radius r sweeps arc length s=rθ. Differentiating with respect to time (r fixed): v=dtds​=rdtdθ​=rω Differentiating again gives the tangential acceleration at​=rα — the component that changes the speed along the circle.

    2.2 The two parts of acceleration in circular motion

    Even at constant speed the direction of v changes, producing centripetal acceleration ac​=rv2​=ω2r directed to the centre. Total acceleration: a=ac2​+at2​​

    • Uniform circular motion: at​=0, a=ac​.
    • Speeding up/slowing down: both components present.

  • 3. Worked Numericals

    Q (3m, NEB-style): A car takes a turn of radius 50 m at 10 m/s. Find the angular velocity and the centripetal acceleration.

    • ω=v/r=10/50=0.2 rad/s
    • ac​=v2/r=100/50=2 m/s²

    Q (4m, NEB-style): A flywheel starts from rest and reaches 300 rpm in 10 s. Find its angular acceleration and the tangential acceleration of a rim point at r = 0.5 m.

    • ω=31.4 rad/s; α=Δω/t=31.4/10=3.14 rad/s²
    • at​=rα=0.5×3.14=1.57 m/s²

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 22: this topic is the dictionary between angular and linear.
    • To Topic 24: ac​=v2/r=ω2r — two equivalent forms; use whichever data is given.
    • To Gravitation (Unit 7): satellite speed relates to angular velocity through the same v=rω.

Confusion

  • Tangential vs centripetal: at​=rα changes speed; ac​=v2/r changes direction — uniform circular motion has only the second.
  • ω same for all points of a rigid body; v is not — v scales with r.

  • Centrifuges separate particles because a_c = ω²r grows with r and with the square of spin rate.

Practice Questions

Formulas

    • v = rω; a_t = rα
    • a_c = v²/r = ω²r = 4π²r/T²
    • a = √(a_c² + a_t²)

Key Points

  • v=rω; at​=rα; ac​=v2/r=ω2r; total a=ac2​+at2​​.
  • Uniform circular motion: at​=0, acceleration is purely centripetal.

Summary

The radius is the exchange rate between rotational and linear descriptions: s=rθ, v=rω, at​=rα. Circular motion also carries a direction-changing acceleration ac​=v2/r=ω2r toward the centre; the tangential part rα changes speed, and total acceleration is ac2​+at2​​. A car rounding a 50 m turn at 10 m/s has ω=0.2 rad/s and ac​=2 m/s² (NEB 2022, 2024). On one rigid body all points share ω but outer points move faster and feel larger centripetal acceleration — the working principle of centrifuges.

Special Notes

  • Angular velocity points ALONG THE AXIS (right-hand rule), not tangentially — this is the most common confusion.
  • The kinematic equations for rotation are IDENTICAL in form to linear motion — just swap the variables.
  • Always convert rpm to rad/s before using formulas — forgetting this is the most common calculation error.
  • The number of revolutions is θ/(2π) — divide angular displacement in radians by 2π.
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Find the number of revolutions' — use θ/(2π), not θ in radians.

Branches
1.
Topic

Angular Displacement (θ)

Details
  • Measured in radians: 2π rad = 360° = 1 rev
  • Vector quantity — direction along axis (RHR)
  • θ > 2π allowed (unlike plain angle)
  • θ = s/r where s is arc length, r is radius
2.
Topic

Angular Velocity (ω)

Details
  • ω = dθ/dt (instantaneous), ω = Δθ/Δt (average)
  • Unit: rad/s; also rpm (revolutions per minute)
  • Vector along axis via right-hand rule
  • Uniform circular motion: ω = constant
3.
Topic

Angular Acceleration (α)

Details
  • α = dω/dt = d²θ/dt²
  • Unit: rad/s²
  • Positive α: speeding up (if ω > 0)
  • Negative α: slowing down (if ω > 0)
4.
Topic

Linear-Angular Links

Details
  • v = rω (linear speed)
  • a_t = rα (tangential acceleration)
  • a_c = v²/r = rω² (centripetal acceleration)
  • s = rθ (arc length)
5.
Topic

Kinematic Equations

Details
  • ω = ω₀ + αt
  • θ = ω₀t + ½αt²
  • ω² = ω₀² + 2αθ
  • θ = (ω + ω₀)t/2
6.
Topic

Conversions

Details
  • rpm → rad/s: multiply by 2π/60
  • rad/s → rpm: multiply by 60/2π
  • Degrees → rad: multiply by π/180
  • Revolutions → rad: multiply by 2π

Specific Conditions

  • Rigid Body Constraint: Relations like v=rω and at​=rα assume the particles are at a fixed distance r from the axis of rotation (e.g., points on a rotating rigid body).
  • Instantaneous Values: These relationships hold at any instant, even if ω or α are changing over time. They are local relations between linear and angular kinematics.
  • Vector Interpretation: These are scalar magnitudes of the cross-product relations v=ω×r and a=α×r+ω×(ω×r). The latter vector equation includes both tangential and centripetal terms.
