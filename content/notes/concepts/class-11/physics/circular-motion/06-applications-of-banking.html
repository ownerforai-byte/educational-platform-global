Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Roads That Lean Into Turns

    A car rounding a flat curve relies entirely on friction — fragile at speed. Bank the road, tilt the surface inward, and the normal force itself supplies most of the centripetal force. The same physics explains why cyclists lean and why railway tracks are superelevated.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Flat (unbanked) road

    Friction alone supplies the centripetal force: f=rmv2​, with f≤μN=μmg. Maximum safe speed: vmax​=μrg​

    2.2 Banked road — derivation of the banking angle (NEB 2024)

    For a road banked at angle θ (frictionless ideal case), forces on the car: weight mg down, normal force N perpendicular to the road surface.

    1. Vertical equilibrium: Ncosθ=mg.
    2. Horizontal (centripetal): Nsinθ=rmv2​.
    3. Dividing:

    tanθ=rgv2​⇒θ=tan−1(rgv2​)​

    At this design speed, no friction is needed — the normal force alone does everything.

    2.3 With friction (maximum speed on a banked road)

    vmax​=rg1−μtanθμ+tanθ​​

    2.4 Worked numericals

    Q (5m, 2024): Derive the expression for the angle of banking of a curved road, and find the banking angle for a 100 m radius curve taken at 20 m/s. (g = 10 m/s²)

    • Derivation as in §2.2.
    • tanθ=rgv2​=100×10400​=0.4⇒θ=tan−1(0.4)=21.8°

    Q (NEB-style): A 1000 kg car takes a 50 m curve at 15 m/s on a flat road. What friction force is required, and is it available if μ = 0.6?

    • f=mv2/r=1000×225/50=4500 N; fmax​=μmg=0.6×9800=5880 N > 4500 N ✓ safe.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 24: banking is centripetal force engineering — the normal force's horizontal component replaces friction.
    • To Conical pendulum (Topic 25): identical geometry: tanθ=v2/rg appears in both.
    • To Friction (Unit 4): the flat-road limit v=μrg​.
    • Aircraft turns: a plane banks so that lift's horizontal component supplies Fc​ — same equations with lift instead of N.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Resolving N along the vertical/horizontal with the wrong trig functions (cos is vertical).
    2. Using mgsinθ instead of Nsinθ for the centripetal component.
    3. Forgetting to state that the ideal case assumes no friction.
    4. Arithmetic: tan−1(0.4) is 21.8°, not 40°.

Confusion

  • N cos θ is vertical, N sin θ is horizontal — the angle θ is measured from the vertical, so cos pairs with mg.
  • Banking doesn't remove friction — it removes the need for friction only at the design speed v = √(rg tan θ).

  • Railway curves are superelevated (outer rail raised) by the same tan θ = v²/rg rule.

Examples

  • NEB 2024 — r = 100 m, v = 20 m/s, g = 10: tan θ = 0.4 → θ = 21.8°.

Practice Questions

  • Derivation + numerical (5m): Derive tan θ = v²/rg and find θ for r = 50 m at 15 m/s (g = 9.8). — Vertical: N cos θ = mg. Horizontal: N sin θ = mv²/r. Dividing: tan θ = v²/rg = 225/(50×9.8) = 0.459 → θ = 24.7°.

Formulas

    • Flat road: v_max = √(μrg)
    • Banked (frictionless): tan θ = v²/rg; v = √(rg tan θ)
    • Banked with friction: v_max = √[rg(μ + tan θ)/(1 − μ tan θ)]

Key Points

  • tanθ=v2/rg (frictionless design speed).
  • Flat road: vmax​=μrg​; banked with friction: vmax​=rg(μ+tanθ)/(1−μtanθ)​.

Summary

On a flat curve friction alone must supply Fc​, limiting speed to v=μrg​. Banking the road lets the normal force help: vertical equilibrium Ncosθ=mg and horizontal centripetal Nsinθ=mv2/r divide to give tanθ=v2/rg — the design angle at which no friction is needed (NEB 2024, 5m). For r = 100 m at 20 m/s with g = 10 m/s², tanθ=0.4 and θ=21.8°. With friction the same analysis yields vmax​=rg1−μtanθμ+tanθ​​. The identical geometry governs the conical pendulum, railway superelevation and banking aircraft.

Special Notes

  • The design speed is the speed at which no friction is required — the most important concept.
  • Always check: if v²/(rg) > tan θ, the vehicle needs additional friction downward (going too fast).
  • The formula v_max = √[rg(tan θ + μ)/(1 − μ tan θ)] requires μ tan θ < 1 for physical validity.
  • Railway superelevation problems: h = w tan θ where w is the standard gauge (1.5 m in Nepal).
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Why are roads banked?' — answer: to reduce friction dependence and prevent skidding.

Important Statements

  • "At the design speed, the horizontal component of the normal force supplies the entire centripetal force."
