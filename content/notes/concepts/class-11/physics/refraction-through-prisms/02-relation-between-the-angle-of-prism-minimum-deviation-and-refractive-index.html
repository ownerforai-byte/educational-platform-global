Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Prism Formula

    Combine prism geometry with minimum-deviation symmetry and a powerful result drops out: n=sin2A​sin2A+δm​​​. It is how spectrometers measure refractive index, and NEB 2024 carried it as a 5-mark derivation plus a numerical.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Deviation relation δ = i + e − A (NEB 2024, 4m)

    At the first face the ray deviates by (i − r₁); at the second by (e − r₂). Total deviation: δ=(i−r1​)+(e−r2​)=(i+e)−(r1​+r2​) From the quadrilateral geometry inside the prism (the two normals meet at the prism angle A): r1​+r2​=A⇒δ=i+e−A​

    2.2 The prism formula (NEB 2024, 5m)

    At minimum deviation: i = e and r₁ = r₂ = r = A/2. So δm = 2i − A → i = (A + δm)/2. Snell's law at the first face: n=sinrsini​=sin2A​sin2A+δm​​​ — the prism formula; a spectrometer measures A and δm to obtain n.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (3m, 2024): A prism of angle 60° has refractive index 1.5. Calculate its angle of minimum deviation. sin260°+δm​​=1.5×sin30°=0.75 260°+δm​​=sin−1(0.75)=48.6°⇒δm​≈37.2°

    Q (4m, 2022): A prism of refracting angle 60° has n = 1.5. If the angle of incidence is 40° and the internal angle of refraction at the first face is 30°, find the angle of emergence and the deviation.

    • r2​=A−r1​=60°−30°=30°
    • Emergence: sine=1.5×sin30°=0.75 → e=48.6°
    • δ=i+e−A=40°+48.6°−60°=28.6°

    Q (NEB-style): A 10° prism gives δm = 2°. Find n. n=sin(5°)sin(6°)​=0.08720.1045​≈1.20.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 74: the formula is built on the δm symmetry conditions.
    • To Topic 76: for small A, the formula linearises to δ = (n − 1)A.
    • To dispersion: repeating the measurement for different colours gives n(λ) — the spectrometer's real power.

Specific Conditions

  • Minimum Deviation Condition: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = A/2, ray inside parallel to base — only at this symmetric position is D = D_min.
  • Small Angle Approximation: D ≈ (μ-1)A valid only for small A (typically < 10°) and small angles of incidence.
  • Transparent Prism: Material must be transparent in the wavelength range used; absorption affects deviation measurement.

Formulas

    • δ = i + e − A; r₁ + r₂ = A
    • δm = 2i − A at minimum
    • n = sin((A+δm)/2)/sin(A/2)

Key Points

  • δ = i + e − A; n = sin((A+δm)/2)/sin(A/2).

Important Statements

  • "The refractive index of the prism material is given by the sine ratio of half the sum (A + δm) to half the prism angle."

Summary

Prism optics rest on two relations. First, the deviation relation: δ = (i − r₁) + (e − r₂) with the geometry identity r1​+r2​=A gives δ=i+e−A (NEB 2024, 4m). Second, at minimum deviation the symmetric passage (i = e, r₁ = r₂ = A/2) plus Snell's law yields the prism formula n=sin2A​sin2A+δm​​​ (NEB 2024, 5m). Numerically: a 60° prism with n = 1.5 has δm ≈ 37.2° (NEB 2024, 3m); with i = 40°, r₁ = 30°, the emergent angle is 48.6° and δ = 28.6° (NEB 2022, 4m). This formula is how spectrometers measure refractive index.
