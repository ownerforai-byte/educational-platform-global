Notes

  • Prism: A transparent optical element with flat, polished surfaces that refract light. Triangular prism has two refracting surfaces inclined at angle A (angle of prism).
  • Deviation: Angle between incident ray direction and emergent ray direction. Depends on angle of incidence i, prism angle A, and refractive index μ.
  • Minimum Deviation: At a particular angle of incidence, deviation D is minimum (D_min). At this position, ray passes symmetrically: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = r, and ray inside prism is parallel to base.
  • Prism Formula at Minimum Deviation: μ = sin((A + D_min)/2) / sin(A/2). This is the most important relation for determining refractive index of prism material.
  • Small Angle Prism: For thin prism (small A) and small angles: D_min ≈ (μ - 1)A. Deviation is independent of angle of incidence for small angles.
  • Relation r₁ + r₂ = A: At any incidence, sum of refraction angles at two faces equals prism angle.
  • Deviation Formula: D = i + e - A where i = incidence angle, e = emergence angle, A = prism angle.
  • Factors Affecting Deviation: Deviation depends on prism angle A, refractive index μ, wavelength of light (dispersion), and angle of incidence.

Specific Conditions

  • Minimum Deviation Condition: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = A/2, ray inside parallel to base — only at this symmetric position is D = D_min.
  • Small Angle Approximation: D ≈ (μ-1)A valid only for small A (typically < 10°) and small angles of incidence.
  • Transparent Prism: Material must be transparent in the wavelength range used; absorption affects deviation measurement.

Formulas

  • Deviation: D = i + e - A
  • Angle relation: r₁ + r₂ = A
  • Minimum deviation: μ = sin((A + D_min)/2) / sin(A/2)
  • Small angle prism: D_min ≈ (μ - 1)A
  • At minimum deviation: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = A/2

Key Points

  • Prism angle A = r₁ + r₂ at any incidence.
  • Deviation D = i + e - A.
  • At minimum deviation: i = e, r₁ = r₂ = A/2.
  • μ = sin((A + D_min)/2) / sin(A/2) — prism formula.
  • Small angle prism: D ≈ (μ - 1)A.
  • Deviation depends on μ, A, wavelength, and incidence angle.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: At minimum deviation, refracted ray passes symmetrically through prism (i = e, r₁ = r₂).
  • Statement 2: Refractive index μ = sin((A + D_min)/2) / sin(A/2).
  • Statement 3: For small-angled prism, D_min ≈ (μ - 1)A.
  • Statement 4: Deviation D = i + e - A where i and e are incidence and emergence angles.
  • Statement 5: r₁ + r₂ = A at the two refracting surfaces.

Important Notes

  • Minimum deviation position gives most accurate μ measurement.
  • D_min depends on wavelength — dispersion causes different colors to deviate differently.
  • The symmetric position (ray parallel to base) is easy to identify experimentally.
  • Small angle approximation simplifies calculations but loses accuracy for large A.

Exam Short Tricks

  • At D_min: i = e, r = A/2 — use this to simplify.
  • μ = sin((A+D_min)/2)/sin(A/2) — memorize this prism formula.
  • Small angle: D = (μ-1)A — quick estimate.
  • D = i + e - A — general deviation formula.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks to derive prism formula μ = sin((A+D_min)/2)/sin(A/2).
  • Experimental determination of μ using spectrometer and minimum deviation method.
  • Small angle prism numericals using D = (μ-1)A.
  • Distinguish deviation (angle through which ray turns) from D_min.

Examples

  • Glass prism A = 60°, D_min = 37°. μ = sin((60+37)/2)/sin(60/2) = sin(48.5°)/sin(30°) = 0.749/0.5 = 1.498 ≈ 1.5.
  • Small angle prism A = 5°, μ = 1.5: D_min ≈ (1.5-1)×5° = 2.5°.
  • Prism A = 45°, D_min = 30°: μ = sin(37.5°)/sin(22.5°) = 0.609/0.383 = 1.59.

Universal Facts

  • The spectrometer with prism is the classic instrument for measuring refractive index accurately.
  • Dispersion (μ varies with λ) causes white light to split into colors — violet deviates most, red least.
  • Rainbow formation involves refraction, internal reflection, and refraction again in water droplets.
  • Prism binoculars and periscopes use total internal reflection in prisms instead of mirrors.

Confusion

  • Confusing deviation angle D with prism angle A.
  • Thinking minimum deviation occurs at any incidence: Only at symmetric position (i = e).
  • Using small angle formula D = (μ-1)A for large A: Only valid for small A.
  • Confusing angle of refraction r with angle of deviation D.
  • Forgetting that D_min depends on wavelength (dispersion).
