Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Prism's Sweet Spot

    Turn a prism slowly and the emerging ray swings — the deviation shrinks, reaches a lowest value, then grows again. That lowest value, the minimum deviation, happens when the ray passes symmetrically, and it is the key to measuring refractive index in the spectrometer.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2020, 2021, 2023, 2024)

    2.1 What is a prism? (NEB 2021)

    A prism is a transparent refracting element with two plane refracting faces that are not parallel. Two types: triangular prism (spectrometers, dispersion) and right-angled (totally reflecting) prism (periscopes, binoculars). The angle of the prism A is the angle between the two refracting faces.

    2.2 Deviation and the i–δ curve (NEB 2024)

    A prism bends light towards its base by the angle of deviation δ = i + e − A. As i increases:

    • δ falls steeply, bottoms out at minimum deviation δm, then rises slowly — a U-shaped curve.
    • There is a single minimum because δ(i) is symmetric about the symmetric path (i = e); moving i either way increases δ (NEB 2024, 2m).

    Condition for minimum deviation (NEB 2023): the ray passes symmetrically through the prism: i=eandr1​=r2​=2A​ The ray inside the prism is then parallel to the base (for an isosceles prism).

    2.3 Minimum deviation and the parallel-to-base ray (NEB 2020, 4m)

    Definition: minimum deviation is the smallest angle of deviation produced by the prism for given material and light, obtained when the ray passes symmetrically (i = e).

    Derivation sketch: at minimum deviation the passage is reversible — reversing the emergent ray retraces the incident one. This symmetry forces r1​=r2​, and since r1​+r2​=A, each equals A/2. With the internal ray making equal angles with both faces, it runs parallel to the base of an isosceles prism.

    2.4 Factors affecting δm (NEB 2024, 2m)

    δm increases with (1) the prism angle A and (2) the refractive index n of the material; it is smaller for longer wavelengths (red deviates least).

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 75: δm feeds directly into the prism formula n = sin((A+δm)/2)/sin(A/2).
    • To Topic 76: for small A, δm ≈ (n − 1)A.
    • To dispersion: n depends on colour, so δm does too — violet bends more than red.

Summary

A prism is a transparent element with two non-parallel refracting faces (triangular or right-angled types), whose angle A is the angle between them (NEB 2021). The deviation it produces, δ = i + e − A, traces a U-shaped curve with the angle of incidence, dipping to a single minimum deviation δm (NEB 2024, 2m). The minimum occurs when the ray passes symmetrically: i = e and r₁ = r₂ = A/2, so the internal ray runs parallel to the base of an isosceles prism — proved by reversibility of the light path (NEB 2020, 4m). δm grows with A and with n, and is smaller for red than violet (NEB 2024, 2m) — the gateway to the prism formula and dispersion.
