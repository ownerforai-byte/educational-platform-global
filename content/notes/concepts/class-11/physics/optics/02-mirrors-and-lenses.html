Summary

Mirrors and lenses form images by reflection and refraction. Plane mirrors form virtual images; spherical mirrors (concave/convex) and lenses use the mirror/lens formula with sign conventions to determine image position, size, and nature.

Notes

  • Plane Mirror: Forms a virtual, erect, same-sized image behind the mirror. Image distance equals object distance. Lateral inversion occurs (left-right reversed).
  • Spherical Mirrors: Concave (converging) and convex (diverging) mirrors. Key terms: pole (P), center of curvature (C), radius of curvature (R), focus (F), focal length (f = R/2), principal axis.
  • Mirror Formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u, where u = object distance, v = image distance, f = focal length. Sign convention: distances measured from pole; positive in direction of incident light.
  • Magnification: m = -v/u = h_i/h_o, where h_i is image height and h_o is object height. Negative m means inverted image.
  • Convex Lens: Converging lens. Thicker at center. Forms real inverted images (except when object within focal length). Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u.
  • Concave Lens: Diverging lens. Thinner at center. Always forms virtual, erect, diminished images.
  • Power of Lens: P = 1/f (f in meters), unit: diopter (D). Convex lens: P > 0. Concave lens: P < 0. Lenses in contact: P = P₁ + P₂ + ...
  • Spherical Aberration: Marginal rays focus at different points than paraxial rays. Minimized by using small apertures or parabolic mirrors.

Specific Conditions

  • Thin Lens/Mirror Approximation: The mirror equation (1/f = 1/u + 1/v) and lens formula (1/f = 1/v - 1/u) assume thin optics where thickness is negligible. For thick lenses, principal planes must be used. Sign convention (Cartesian): distances measured from pole/lens center; positive in direction of incident light for u, opposite for v/f.
  • Spherical Aberration: Paraxial rays (close to axis) follow the simple formulas. Non-paraxial rays focus at different points, causing spherical aberration. The formulas assume paraxial approximation (small angles, sin θ ≈ θ).
  • Lens/Mirror Power: Power P = 1/f (meters) in diopters. Converging (convex lens, concave mirror): P > 0. Diverging (concave lens, convex mirror): P < 0. Powers add for thin lenses in contact: P_total = Σ P_i.

Formulas

  • Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u
  • Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u
  • Magnification: m = -v/u = h_i/h_o
  • Focal length and radius: f = R/2
  • Power of lens: P = 1/f (f in meters), unit: diopter
  • Lenses in contact: P = P₁ + P₂ + ...

Key Points

  • Plane mirror: virtual, erect, same-size image, image distance = object distance.
  • Concave mirror: converging, can form real or virtual images depending on object position.
  • Convex mirror: diverging, always forms virtual, erect, diminished image.
  • Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u with sign convention.
  • Lens formula: 1/f = 1/v - 1/u with sign convention.
  • Power P = 1/f in diopters; convex positive, concave negative.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: A plane mirror forms a virtual, erect, laterally inverted image of the same size as the object.
  • Statement 2: The mirror formula 1/f = 1/v + 1/u relates object distance, image distance, and focal length.
  • Statement 3: The lens formula 1/f = 1/v - 1/u relates object distance, image distance, and focal length for lenses.
  • Statement 4: Magnification m = -v/u = h_i/h_o; negative m indicates an inverted image.
  • Statement 5: Power of a lens P = 1/f (f in meters) measured in diopters.

Important Notes

  • Sign convention: distances measured from the optical center/pole; positive in the direction of incident light.
  • Real images are formed on the same side as the reflected/transmitted light; virtual images appear behind the mirror/lens.
  • Concave mirrors and convex lenses are converging; convex mirrors and concave lenses are diverging.
  • For a convex lens, when object is between F and the lens, the image is virtual, erect, and magnified (magnifying glass case).
  • Spherical aberration is reduced by using small apertures (paraxial approximation).

Exam Short Tricks

  • Concave mirror: object beyond C → real, inverted, diminished image.
  • Concave mirror: object at C → real, inverted, same-size image.
  • Concave mirror: object between C and F → real, inverted, magnified image.
  • Convex lens: object beyond 2F → real, inverted, diminished image on other side.
  • Convex mirror: always virtual, erect, diminished image regardless of object position.
  • m = -v/u: negative magnification = inverted image.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks for ray diagrams for concave mirror and convex lens at different object positions.
  • Mirror formula and lens formula numericals are standard exam questions.
  • Sign convention errors are common — always draw the diagram and mark signs carefully.
  • Power of lens and combination of lenses in contact are frequently tested.
  • Magnification formula connects image size to object size via distances.

Examples

  • Object 30 cm in front of concave mirror (f = 15 cm): 1/15 = 1/v + 1/(-30) → 1/v = 1/15 + 1/30 = 3/30 → v = 10 cm. Real image at 10 cm, m = -v/u = -10/(-30) = 1/3.
  • Convex lens (f = 20 cm), object 30 cm away: 1/20 = 1/v - 1/(-30) → 1/v = 1/20 - 1/30 = 1/60 → v = 60 cm. Real image, m = -60/(-30) = -2 (inverted, 2× magnified).
  • Convex lens (f = 10 cm), object 5 cm away (within focal length): 1/10 = 1/v - 1/(-5) → 1/v = 1/10 - 1/5 = -1/10 → v = -10 cm. Virtual image, m = -(-10)/(-5) = 2 (erect, 2× magnified).

Practice Questions

  • Q1. Derive the mirror formula 1/f = 1/v + 1/u with sign convention.
  • Q2. An object is placed 20 cm from a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find image position and magnification.
  • Q3. State the lens formula and explain the sign convention.
  • Q4. A convex lens of focal length 10 cm forms a real image 30 cm away. Find object distance and magnification.
  • Q5. Calculate the power of a convex lens with focal length 25 cm.
  • Q6. Draw ray diagrams for concave mirror when object is (a) beyond C, (b) at C, (c) between C and F.

Practice

  • Draw ray diagrams for concave mirror at all object positions.
  • Draw ray diagrams for convex lens at all object positions.
  • Solve mirror formula numericals with sign convention.
  • Solve lens formula numericals including power calculations.
  • Combine lenses in contact and compute equivalent power.

Universal Facts

  • The word 'focus' comes from Latin for 'hearth' — where sunlight converges to a point.
  • Concave mirrors are used in shaving mirrors, headlights, and reflecting telescopes.
  • Convex mirrors are used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles because they give a wider field of view.
  • The human eye uses a convex lens (crystalline lens) to focus light on the retina.
  • Galileo's telescope used a convex objective lens and concave eyepiece lens.

Confusion

  • Confusing mirror formula (1/f = 1/v + 1/u) with lens formula (1/f = 1/v - 1/u): The sign before 1/u differs.
  • Thinking convex mirrors always form real images: Convex mirrors always form virtual, erect, diminished images.
  • Believing magnification m = v/u without the negative sign: m = -v/u, the negative sign indicates inversion.
  • Confusing real and virtual images: Real images can be projected on a screen; virtual images cannot.
  • Using wrong sign convention: Always measure from pole/optical center and follow the Cartesian convention consistently.

1.

A plane mirror forms an image that is:

Options
  • Real and inverted
  • Virtual and erect
  • Real and magnified
  • Virtual and diminished
Answer

B

Explanation

Plane mirror forms a virtual, erect, same-sized image with lateral inversion.

2.

The mirror formula is:

Options
  • 1/f = 1/v - 1/u
  • 1/f = 1/v + 1/u
  • 1/f = u/v
  • f = u + v
Answer

B

Explanation

Mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u with proper sign convention.

3.

A convex lens is also called:

Options
  • Diverging lens
  • Converging lens
  • Plane lens
  • Cylindrical lens
Answer

B

Explanation

A convex lens converges light rays and is called a converging lens.

4.

Power of a lens is measured in:

Options
  • Meters
  • Diopters
  • Watts
  • Joules
Answer

B

Explanation

Power P = 1/f where f is in meters; unit is diopter (D).

5.

Magnification m = -v/u means negative m indicates:

Options
  • Erect image
  • Inverted image
  • Diminished image
  • Magnified image
Answer

B

Explanation

Negative magnification indicates the image is inverted relative to the object.
