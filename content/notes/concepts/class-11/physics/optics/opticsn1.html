Notes

  • Geometrical optics treats light as rays and predicts reflection and refraction without tracking wave phase. It is useful when optical elements are much larger than visible wavelengths.
  • At a reflecting boundary i=r; at a refracting boundary n1 sin i=n2 sin r. A higher-index medium has lower light speed, v=c/n.
  • When light travels from higher to lower refractive index, refraction bends away from the normal. Above the critical angle c, total internal reflection occurs: sin c=n2/n1.
  • Example: glass n1=1.50 to air n2=1.00 gives c=sin⁻¹(1/1.50)≈41.8°. At incidence 50° there is TIR; at 30° the ray refracts into air.
  • When an opening approaches the wavelength, diffraction spreads the wave and a ray-only model is insufficient; interference and polarisation also require wave optics.
  • Geometrical optics draws straight rays and ignores phase. That picture is good for lenses and mirrors whose openings are huge compared with the wavelength of light. When a slit or an obstacle is only tens or hundreds of wavelengths across, diffraction bends the energy out of the ray prediction.

Confusion

  • A low-to-high index ray cannot undergo total internal reflection.
  • At exactly the critical angle the refracted ray grazes the boundary.

Specific Conditions

  • Optical Principles: The behavior of light is governed by the laws of reflection (angle of incidence = angle of reflection) and refraction (Snell's law: n₁sinθ₁ = n₂sinθ₂). These laws derive from Fermat's principle of least time.
  • Wave Nature of Light: Light exhibits wave phenomena including interference, diffraction, and polarization. These cannot be explained by ray optics alone and require wave optics. The wavelength λ and frequency f are related by c = λf.
  • Approximations and Validity: Ray optics (geometrical optics) is valid when object sizes ≫ wavelength λ. Wave optics is required when apertures or obstacles are comparable to λ. Quantum optics is needed for single-photon phenomena.

Exercises

1.

A slit is 0.050 mm wide and the wavelength is 500 nm. Find how many wavelengths fit across the slit, and say whether diffraction can be ignored.

Steps
  • a = 5.0 × 10^−5 m and λ = 5.0 × 10^−7 m.
  • a/λ = 100.
  • One hundred wavelengths is not a huge number.
  • Diffraction will spread the beam by an angle of order λ/a = 0.01 rad.
  • A doorway, millions of wavelengths wide, shows no everyday diffraction of light.
Answer

100 wavelengths. Diffraction is not negligible. The spread is about 0.01 rad.
