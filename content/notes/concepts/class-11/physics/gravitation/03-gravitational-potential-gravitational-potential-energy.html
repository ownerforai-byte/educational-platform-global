Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Energy Ledger of Gravity

    Potential energy near Earth's surface (mgh) is only an approximation. The exact bookkeeping — valid to any distance — uses the inverse-square law: gravitational potential energy U=−GMm/r, negative because gravity binds. The potential per unit mass, V=−GM/r, extends this to any test mass.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Definitions

    Gravitational potential energy (U): the work done by an external agent to bring a mass m from infinity (zero reference) to a distance r from mass M: U=−rGMm​ Negative sign meaning: gravity does positive work as the mass approaches; the field is attractive, so the bound system's energy is below zero. Infinity is the zero of energy — free escape.

    Gravitational potential (V): potential energy per unit mass: V=−rGM​(J/kg) V at a point is the work done per unit mass bringing a test mass from infinity to that point.

    2.2 Derivation

    Work done moving m from infinity to r against gravity: U=∫∞r​x2GMm​dx(external work)=−∫∞r​x2GMm​dx=[xGMm​]∞r​=−rGMm​

    2.3 Relation between field and potential

    g=−drdV​ The field points down the potential gradient — masses "roll downhill" toward lower (more negative) potential.

    2.4 Worked numerical (NEB 2021)

    Q (3m, 2021): Find the gravitational potential energy of a satellite of mass 200 kg orbiting at a height of 400 km above Earth's surface. (R = 6.4×10⁶ m, M = 6×10²⁴ kg, G = 6.67×10⁻¹¹)

    • r=R+h=6.8×106 m
    • U=−rGMm​=−6.8×1066.67×10−11×6×1024×200​=−6.8×1068.00×1016​=−1.18×1010 J

    Q (NEB-style): Find the potential at that orbit. V=−GM/r=−6.8×1064.00×1014​=−5.9×107 J/kg.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 19: near Earth's surface U=mgh is the linear approximation of −GMm/r for h≪R.
    • To Topic 34: escape velocity follows from total energy ≥ 0: 21​mv2−RGMm​=0.
    • To Topic 35: satellite total energy E=−GMm/2r — half the PE.
    • To electrostatics: identical mathematics with 1/4πϵ0​ replacing G.

Specific Conditions

  • Reference at Infinity: Potential is defined with V(∞)=0. This makes all finite-distance potentials negative — work must be done to separate masses.
  • Near-Surface Approximation: U=mgh is valid only for h≪R where g is approximately constant. For large heights, use U=−GMm/r.
  • Potential is Not Field: Potential is scalar (can be negative), field is vector. g​=−∇V — field points toward lower potential.

Formulas

    • U = −GMm/r; V = −GM/r
    • g = −dV/dr
    • Near surface: U ≈ mgh

Key Points

  • U=−GMm/r; V=−GM/r (zero at infinity); g=−dV/dr.
  • Near the surface −GMm/r reduces to mgh (h ≪ R).

Important Statements

  • "Gravitational potential at a point is the work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to that point."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQ: satellite PE numerical (2021, 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "r = R + h first; then U = −GMm/r with the minus sign."

Exam Notes

  • The 2021 numerical is the pattern: orbital radius → one substitution.

Examples

  • NEB 2021 — 200 kg satellite at 400 km: r = 6.8×10⁶ m → U = −1.18×10¹⁰ J.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): Find the potential at Earth's surface (M = 6×10²⁴ kg, R = 6.4×10⁶ m). — V = −GM/R = −(6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 6×10²⁴)/6.4×10⁶ = −6.25×10⁷ J/kg.

Practice

  • Why is the zero of gravitational potential taken at infinity?

Universal Facts

  • The Moon's PE in Earth's field is about −7.6×10²⁸ J — bound bodies always carry negative PE.

Confusion

  • Potential vs potential energy: V = −GM/r (J/kg, per unit mass); U = −GMm/r (J, for mass m).
  • Why negative: with zero at infinity, bringing a mass closer against attraction releases energy — the bound system sits below zero.

Summary

Gravitational potential energy U=−GMm/r and potential V=−GM/r (J/kg) are measured from a zero at infinity; the minus sign encodes attraction — a bound system's energy lies below zero, and escape requires supplying energy. The integral ∫∞r​GMm/x2dx yields both, and the field is the potential gradient: g=−dV/dr. Near the surface the formula collapses to the familiar mgh. A 200 kg satellite orbiting 400 km up (r = 6.8×10⁶ m) has U=−1.18×1010 J (NEB 2021) and sits in a potential of −5.9×10⁷ J/kg. This mathematics transfers unchanged to electrostatics with 1/4πϵ0​ in place of G.
