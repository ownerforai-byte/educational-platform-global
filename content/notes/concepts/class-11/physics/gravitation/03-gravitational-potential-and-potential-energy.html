Enriched Content

Notes
  • Gravitational potential V: the work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to a point, without acceleration. For a point mass M: V=−rGM​. At infinity V=0, and everywhere else V is negative because gravity does positive work as the mass approaches — the field does the work, so potential decreases.
  • Gravitational potential energy U: the work done against gravity in assembling a system; for two masses separated by r: U=−rGMm​. It is the energy of the whole system, not of one mass alone. U is negative because the system is bound — you must supply GMm/r of energy to separate the masses to infinity.
  • Why the minus sign matters: potential energy is defined with zero at infinity. Since the force is attractive, energy is released (and PE decreases) as masses come together. A satellite in orbit at radius r has U=−rGMm​ — its total mechanical energy is E=K+U=−2rGMm​, which is negative, confirming a bound orbit.
  • Near Earth's surface: for small heights h above the surface, U=mgh. This is the constant-g approximation of −rGMm​: since Δ(1/r)≈−h/R2, the change matches mgh. Use mgh only for h≪R; use −rGMm​ for large altitudes (satellites, rockets).
  • Worked example — escape energy: to launch a mass m from Earth's surface (R) to infinity you must supply GMm/R. Numerically for m = 1 kg: 6.37×1066.67×10−11×5.97×1024​≈6.25×107 J≈62.5 MJ per kg. This energy corresponds to the escape speed 11.2 km/s.
  • Potential vs potential energy vs field: field g=−drdV​ (the force per unit mass, a vector); potential V is a scalar (J/kg); potential energy U=mV. The field is the negative gradient of the potential: g=−drdV​ radially.
  • Equipotential surfaces: surfaces of constant V. Moving along an equipotential surface requires zero work; the field is always perpendicular to these surfaces. For a point mass, equipotentials are concentric spheres.
  • Superposition: for multiple masses, V=∑(−ri​GMi​​) and U=∑(−ri​GMi​m​) — potentials add as scalars, which is much simpler than adding force vectors.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'Potential and potential energy are the same.' ✅ Potential V is work per unit mass (J/kg); potential energy U=mV (J). V belongs to a point in the field; U belongs to the mass–field system.
  • ❌ 'Negative energy is unphysical.' ✅ Negative potential energy simply means the system is BOUND — energy must be added to separate it. It is a matter of the reference at infinity.
  • ❌ 'U=mgh and U=−rGMm​ disagree.' ✅ They agree for small heights: mgh is the CHANGE in −rGMm​ near the surface. Use the right one for the régime.
  • ❌ 'Potential is maximum at Earth's surface.' ✅ Potential is most negative at the surface (−RGM​) and rises toward zero with altitude — at infinity it reaches the (higher) value 0.
  • ❌ 'Doubling the distance doubles the potential energy.' ✅ U=−rGMm​ — doubling r halves the magnitude, so U becomes −2rGMm​ (less negative).
  • ❌ 'A satellite far away has lots of potential energy.' ✅ A far-away satellite has potential energy near ZERO (least negative, but U≈0). It needs enormous kinetic energy (orbital speed) that comes from the launch energy.
Practice
  • Find V and g at a point 2R above Earth's centre.
  • Calculate the potential energy of a 1000 kg satellite at r=6.8×106 m.
  • Find the energy required to move a 500 kg mass from R to 3R.
  • Show that for a circular orbit Etotal​=U/2=−2rGMm​.
  • Derive the escape speed from setting K+U=0 at infinity.
  • Find the work needed to place a 10 kg mass at infinity from Earth's surface.
Universal Facts
  • Gravitational potential energy is exactly the 'missing' energy that binds systems — this is why the binding energy of Earth + satellite equals ∣U∣.
  • The concept of potential is useful because it is scalar: complex many-body problems reduce to addition rather than vector resolution.
  • Near a black hole, gravitational potential becomes so deep (−rGM​) that even light cannot escape — the origin of the event horizon.
  • Gravitational potential determines whether planets retain atmospheres: gas molecules with thermal speeds above the local escape speed leak away.
  • The Voyager probes were launched using the escape-energy budget — their final trajectory traced the potential wells of the Sun and giant planets.
Examples
  • V at Earth's surface: V=−RGM​=−6.25×107 J/kg.
  • U of 1000 kg satellite at r=6.8×106 m: U=−rGMm​=−5.9×1010 J.
  • Escape energy per kg: GM/R=6.25×107 J≈17 kWh per kg.
  • Near surface: a 2 kg mass lifted 10 m gains U=mgh=196 J.
  • Total energy of circular orbit: E=−2rGMm​ — negative, so the orbit is bound.
Practice Questions
  • Calculate the gravitational potential at a point 3×107 m from the centre of Earth (M=6×1024 kg).
  • Find the gravitational potential energy of a 1500 kg satellite at an altitude of 2000 km above Earth.
  • How much energy is required to move a 3000 kg spacecraft from Earth's surface to infinity?
  • A 700 kg probe is moved from r=8×106 m to r=5×106 m. Find the change in its potential energy.
  • Derive that for a circular orbit v2=rGM​ and hence E=−2rGMm​.
  • At what height is the gravitational potential of Earth equal to −3×107 J/kg?
Formulas
  • Gravitational potential: V=−rGM​ (J/kg)
  • Gravitational potential energy: U=mV=−rGMm​ (J)
  • Near the surface: U=mgh (h≪R)
  • Field from potential: g=−drdV​
  • Total orbital energy: E=−2rGMm​=U/2
Key Points
  • V=−rGM​ is negative, approaching zero at infinity.
  • U=−rGMm​ is the energy of the (bound) system, negative for attraction.
  • Near Earth's surface U=mgh is the appropriate approximation.
  • Potential and field connect: g=−drdV​.
  • Total energy of a circular orbit is negative: E=−2rGMm​.
Summary

Gravitational potential V=−rGM​ is the work per unit mass to bring a test mass from infinity; potential energy U=−rGMm​ is the negative binding energy of two masses. Near the surface U≈mgh. The field relates to the potential by g=−drdV​, and circular orbits have total energy E=−2rGMm​, confirming their bound nature.

Special Notes
  • Both V and U are defined with reference zero at infinity — that is why they are negative.
  • Use U=mgh only for small heights (h≪R); use −rGMm​ for space-scale problems.
  • Potential is a scalar — use scalar addition with multiple masses.
  • The escape energy equals ∣U∣ at the surface: GMm/R.
  • NEB may ask 'derive U=−rGMm​ starting from F=r2GMm​' — know the integration step.

Exam Short Tricks
  • Escape-energy shortcut: energy to infinity = GMm/R=mgR (since g=R2GM​).
  • Orbital energy memory: E=−2rGMm​=U/2 means K=−U/2.
  • V at surface ≈−6.25×107 J/kg for Earth — compare planets by this value.
  • When moving closer to a mass, U becomes MORE negative (energy is released).
  • Use mgh only when the height is small compared with Earth's radius — a quick sanity check.
Exam Notes
  • Derivation of U=−rGMm​ from integrating F=r2GMm​ is a standard question.
  • Numericals on satellite total energy and escape energy are frequently asked.
  • Conceptual MCQ: why is potential energy negative? → because zero is at infinity and gravity is attractive.
  • Linking g=−drdV​ to a V–r graph slope appears in higher-level questions.
  • Compare U=mgh with −rGMm​: know when each applies.

Specific Conditions
  • V vs U Distinction: Gravitational potential V=−GM/r is work per unit test mass (J/kg) — a property of the field at a point. Gravitational potential energy U=mV=−GMm/r is the energy of the system (J). They differ by a factor of the test mass m.
  • Zero at Infinity Convention: V=0 and U=0 at r=∞. Negative values mean the system is bound. Moving closer to a mass makes V and U more negative (energy released). The depth of the potential well is ∣U∣=GMm/r (binding energy).
  • Approximation Boundary: U=mgh is valid only for h≪R (constant g approximation). For h≳R, the exact U=−GMm/r must be used. The crossover is typically around h∼0.1R where the error exceeds 1%.

  • Gravitational potential at a point is the work done per unit mass in bringing a mass from infinity to that point: V=−rGM​.
  • Gravitational potential energy of two masses is U=−rGMm​, taking zero at infinity.
  • For small heights near Earth's surface, U=mgh.
  • The gravitational field is the negative gradient of the potential: g=−drdV​.
  • A satellite in a circular orbit has total energy E=−2rGMm​ (bound system).

Important Notes

  • V=−rGM​ (potential) and U=−rGMm​ (potential energy) both take zero at infinity — hence negative for attracted masses.
  • Near Earth's surface use U=mgh; for space-scale distances use the inverse-r form.
  • The field is the negative slope of the potential: g=−drdV​.
  • Binding energy of a two-mass system =∣U∣=GMm/r.
  • Total energy of a circular orbit (E=−2rGMm​) is negative — a signature of a bound state.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Escape-energy shortcut: energy to infinity = GMm/R=mgR (since g=R2GM​).
  • Orbital energy memory: E=−2rGMm​=U/2 means K=−U/2.
  • V at surface ≈−6.25×107 J/kg for Earth — compare planets by this value.
  • When moving closer to a mass, U becomes MORE negative (energy is released).
  • Use mgh only when the height is small compared with Earth's radius — a quick sanity check.
