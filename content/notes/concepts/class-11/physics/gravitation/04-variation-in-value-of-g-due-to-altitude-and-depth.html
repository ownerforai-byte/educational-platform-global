Notes

  • Acceleration Due to Gravity: At Earth's surface, g=GM/R2≈9.8 m/s2. It varies with location due to altitude, depth, Earth's rotation, and shape.
  • Variation with Altitude: At height h above surface: gh​=GM/(R+h)2=g(1+h/R)−2≈g(1−2h/R) for h≪R. Gravity decreases with altitude — inverse square law.
  • Variation with Depth: At depth d below surface (assuming uniform density): gd​=g(1−d/R)=g(R−d)/R. Gravity decreases linearly with depth, becoming zero at Earth's center.
  • Comparison: For small h and d, decrease at height h (Δg≈−2gh/R) is twice the decrease at depth d (Δg=−gd/R). Gravity decreases faster with altitude than with depth.
  • Earth's Rotation: Effective gravity at latitude λ: g′=g−ω2Rcos2λ. Maximum at poles (λ=90∘, no rotation effect), minimum at equator (λ=0∘, maximum centrifugal reduction).
  • Earth's Shape: Earth is oblate (equatorial radius > polar radius). Since g∝1/R2, g is higher at poles (closer to center) and lower at equator.
  • Combined Effects: The measured g varies from about 9.78 m/s2 at equator to 9.83 m/s2 at poles due to rotation + shape + altitude.

Specific Conditions

  • Uniform Density Assumption: The depth formula gd​=g(1−d/R) assumes Earth has uniform density. Real Earth has varying density, so actual variation is more complex.
  • Small Altitude Approximation: gh​≈g(1−2h/R) is valid only for h≪R. For large heights, use full inverse-square formula.
  • Latitude Effect: Rotation effect on g depends on latitude — zero at poles, maximum at equator.

Formulas

  • Surface gravity: g=GM/R2
  • At height h: gh​=g(R/(R+h))2≈g(1−2h/R)
  • At depth d: gd​=g(1−d/R)=g(R−d)/R
  • At center: g=0
  • Rotation effect: g′=g−ω2Rcos2λ
  • Comparison: Δgheight​≈2×Δgdepth​ for equal h and d

Key Points

  • Gravity decreases with altitude: gh​=g(R/(R+h))2.
  • Gravity decreases with depth: gd​=g(1−d/R), zero at center.
  • For equal h and d, decrease at height is approximately twice decrease at depth.
  • Rotation reduces effective gravity most at equator, none at poles.
  • Earth's oblateness makes g higher at poles, lower at equator.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Acceleration due to gravity at height h: gh​=GM/(R+h)2≈g(1−2h/R) for h≪R.
  • Statement 2: Acceleration due to gravity at depth d: gd​=g(1−d/R), assuming uniform Earth density.
  • Statement 3: Gravity decreases faster with altitude than with depth for equal displacements.
  • Statement 4: Earth's rotation reduces effective gravity: g′=g−ω2Rcos2λ.
  • Statement 5: g is maximum at poles and minimum at equator due to rotation and Earth's shape.

Important Notes

  • g decreases with both altitude and depth but at different rates.
  • At Earth's center, g = 0 (mass symmetrically distributed in all directions).
  • The depth formula assumes uniform density — real Earth has core, mantle, crust with different densities.
  • Rotation effect is a centrifugal effect in the rotating frame of Earth.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Height: gh​≈g(1−2h/R); Depth: gd​=g(1−d/R).
  • For same h = d: change at height is twice change at depth.
  • Zero at center, max at poles, min at equator.
  • Use binomial approximation (1+x)n≈1+nx for small h/R.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks to derive variation of g with altitude and depth.
  • Compare rate of decrease with height vs depth — a common conceptual question.
  • Numerical problems: find g at given height/depth, or find height/depth where g is a fraction of surface value.
  • Rotation effect: poles vs equator difference.

Examples

  • g at height 100 km: gh​=9.8(1−2×100/6400)=9.8(1−0.03125)=9.49 m/s2.
  • g at depth 100 km: gd​=9.8(1−100/6400)=9.8(1−0.015625)=9.65 m/s2.
  • g at height equal to R (h = R): gh​=g(R/2R)2=g/4=2.45 m/s2.
  • Depth where g = g/2: g/2=g(1−d/R)⇒d=R/2=3200 km.
  • Height where g = g/2: g/2=g(R/(R+h))2⇒R+h=R2​⇒h=0.414R≈2650 km.

Universal Facts

  • g varies from 9.78 m/s² at equator to 9.83 m/s² at poles — about 0.5% variation.
  • At Earth's center, a mass would be weightless because gravitational pulls from all directions cancel.
  • Satellites experience microgravity not because g is zero (g ≈ 8.7 m/s² at ISS altitude) but because they are in free fall.
  • The GRACE mission measures Earth's gravity field variations to study groundwater, ice sheets, and ocean currents.
  • Olympic records in jumping events are slightly easier at high altitude (lower g) and near equator.

Confusion

  • Thinking g decreases at same rate with height and depth: Height decrease (~2h/R) is approximately twice depth decrease (d/R).
  • Believing g is zero at Earth's center because no mass below: It's zero because mass surrounds symmetrically — pulls cancel.
  • Ignoring Earth's rotation in g variation: Rotation contributes significantly to equator-pole difference.
  • Using depth formula without uniform density assumption: Real Earth's density varies, so actual g(d) is not linear.
  • Confusing g variation with altitude vs gravitational potential variation.
