Enriched Content

Notes
  • Orbital velocity (v₀): a satellite stays in a circular orbit when the gravitational pull GMm/r2 provides the required centripetal force mv2/r. Equating: GMm/r2=mv02​/r→v0​=GM/r​. Near Earth's surface, v0​=gR​≈7.9 km/s.
  • Why v₀ matters: too slow → the satellite spirals in; too fast → it escapes to a higher ellipse. The orbital speed depends only on the radius (GM/r), not on the satellite's mass — heavy and light satellites share the same orbit speed.
  • Time period (T): T=v0​2πr​=2πGMr3​​ — Kepler's third law. Squaring: T2∝r3. A higher orbit has a longer period: the Moon (r ≈ 3.84×10⁸ m) takes ~27.3 days; the ISS (r ≈ 6.8×10⁶ m) takes ~92 minutes.
  • Energy of an orbiting satellite: K=21​mv02​=2rGMm​, U=−rGMm​, so Etotal​=−2rGMm​. Notice K=−U/2 and E=U/2 — a signature of circular orbits. To raise a satellite to a higher orbit you must ADD energy, but the KE actually decreases (higher orbit = slower speed).
  • Worked example — ISS orbit: at height 400 km, r = 6.8×10⁶ m. v0​=6.8×1066.67×10−11×5.97×1024​​=5.86×107​≈7.66 km/s. T=7.66×1032π×6.8×106​≈5570 s≈93 min.
  • Worked example — geostationary orbit: for T = 24 h, r=(4π2GMT2​)1/3≈4.22×107 m≈35,800 km above the equator with v0​≈3.08 km/s.
  • Low Earth vs geostationary comparison: LEO (200–2000 km): v ≈ 7.8 km/s, T ≈ 90 min, covers Earth rapidly (imaging, ISS). Geostationary (35,800 km): v ≈ 3.07 km/s, T = 24 h, appears fixed over one spot (communications, weather).
  • Orbit changes: to transfer between circular orbits, a Hohmann ellipse uses two engine burns; near perigee/apogee speeds change by Δv=v2​−v1​. Firing thrust ahead slows the satellite and lowers the orbit; firing backward raises it.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'A satellite must be above the atmosphere to feel no gravity.' ✅ A satellite feels gravity at every altitude — it is in continuous free fall. It never falls to Earth because its forward speed curves its fall into an orbit.
  • ❌ 'Weightlessness means g = 0 on the ISS.' ✅ g at 400 km is ≈ 8.7 m/s² (89% of surface). Astronauts float because both they and the station accelerate toward Earth together (free fall).
  • ❌ 'Heavier satellites orbit slower.' ✅ Orbital speed is independent of the satellite's mass: v0​=GM/r​.
  • ❌ 'Higher orbit → faster speed.' ✅ Higher orbit means SLOWER speed (v0​=GM/r​) but a LONGER period. To reach a higher orbit you must speed up at launch; once there, the circular speed is lower.
  • ❌ 'T is proportional to r.' ✅ T∝r3/2 — the period grows faster than the radius.
  • ❌ 'A satellite's kinetic energy increases as it moves to a higher orbit.' ✅ KE=2rGMm​ DECREASES with height; the added energy is stored as potential energy (+GmM⋅Δ(1/r)) making the total E=−2rGMm​ less negative.

Universal Facts
  • The first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, launched in 1957, orbited at ~8 km/s — exactly the value predicted by v0​=GM/r​.
  • Arthur C. Clarke proposed the geostationary orbit in 1945; it is now so crowded that slot allocation is internationally regulated.
  • Satellites in low orbit experience tiny atmospheric drag; station-keeping burns periodically restore their altitude.
  • GPS satellites orbit at 20,200 km altitude with a period of 12 hours (ground speed matched to Earth), giving precise timing signals.
  • The Moon is a natural satellite whose orbital period (27.3 days) follows the same T=2πr3/GM​ law as any spacecraft.
Examples
  • LEO speed: v=gR​=9.8×6.4×106​≈7.9 km/s.
  • ISS period ≈ 93 minutes at r = 6.8×10⁶ m.
  • Geostationary altitude ≈ 35,800 km; v ≈ 3.08 km/s.
  • Moon period ≈ 27.3 days from T=2πr3/GM​.
  • Energy check at r = 7×10⁶ m, m = 500 kg: E=−2rGMm​≈−1.42×1010 J.

  • Compare the speed, period and energy of a LEO satellite with a geostationary one.
Formulas
  • Orbital velocity: v0​=rGM​​
  • Near-surface orbital speed: v0​=gR​
  • Time period: T=v0​2πr​=2πGMr3​​
  • Kinetic energy: K=2rGMm​
  • Total energy: E=−2rGMm​ (E = U/2, K = −U/2)
Key Points
  • v0​=GM/r​ — orbital speed depends only on radius, not satellite mass.
  • T=2πr3/GM​ — Kepler's third law: T2∝r3.
  • Higher orbit → slower speed, longer period.
  • Etotal​=−2rGMm​: bound orbit, with K=−U/2.
  • Astronauts float because of free fall, not absence of gravity.
Summary

A satellite orbits when gravity supplies the centripetal force: v0​=GM/r​ and T=2πr3/GM​. Energy is K=2rGMm​, U=−rGMm​, E=−2rGMm​. Orbital speed is mass-independent; higher orbits are slower but longer-period. Weightlessness arises from continuous free fall.

Special Notes
  • r is measured from the centre of Earth: r=R+h, never forget to add the radius.
  • Orbital speed is ~7.9 km/s at the surface, 7.66 km/s at the ISS altitude, 3.08 km/s at geostationary.
  • E is negative everywhere in orbit — a positive total energy means escape.
  • To compare orbits always compute r=R+h first.
  • NEB asks to derive v₀ and T and to solve for altitude given T.

Exam Short Tricks
  • Near surface: v0​=gR​≈7.9 km/s — remember this anchor value.
  • T applet: for the ISS (≈ 90 min) confirm with T=2πr3/GM​.
  • To find height for a given T: first find r=(4π2GMT2​)1/3, then subtract R.
  • K=−U/2 and E=U/2 — instantly get E from U.
  • If E > 0 the orbit is unbound — the satellite will escape.
Exam Notes
  • Deriving v₀ and T from equating gravity to centripetal force is a year-on-year question.
  • Finding the altitude for a given period (e.g., 2 h, 24 h) is a standard numerical.
  • Energy numericals (K, U, E) for a given orbit are frequently asked.
  • Conceptual: 'why do astronauts float?' — answer free fall.
  • Geostationary orbit numbers (35,800 km, 3.08 km/s) are exam favourites.

Specific Conditions
  • Orbital Velocity Derivation: v0​=GM/r​ comes from equating gravitational force to centripetal force: GMm/r2=mv02​/r. This applies to circular orbits around a spherically symmetric mass. The satellite's mass cancels out — all satellites at the same radius have the same speed.
  • Period Law: T=2πr3/GM​ is Kepler's third law for circular orbits. Squaring gives T2∝r3, independent of satellite mass. This is valid only for bound circular/elliptical orbits — not for escape trajectories (E≥0).
  • Energy Relations: For circular orbits: K=GMm/2r, U=−GMm/r, E=−GMm/2r. Notice K=−U/2 and E=U/2. The negative total energy confirms a bound orbit. To raise an orbit, you ADD energy (thrust), but the orbital speed decreases because v0​∝1/r​.

  • Orbital speed is ~7.9 km/s at the surface, 7.66 km/s at the ISS altitude, 3.08 km/s at geostationary.
  • E is negative everywhere in orbit — a positive total energy means escape.
  • To compare orbits always compute r=R+h first.
  • NEB asks to derive v₀ and T and to solve for altitude given T.

Important Statements

  • The orbital velocity of a satellite is v0​=rGM​​.
  • The period of revolution is T=2πGMr3​​.
  • Orbital speed is independent of the satellite's mass.
  • The total energy of an orbiting satellite is E=−2rGMm​.
  • A satellite is in continuous free fall; weightlessness does not mean zero gravity.

Important Notes

  • r=R+h — always measure from the centre of Earth.
  • v0​=GM/r​ is mass-independent: all satellites at the same height have the same speed.
  • T=2πr3/GM​: higher orbit, longer period.
  • Etotal​=−2rGMm​ is negative — a signature of a bound orbit.
  • Astronauts float due to free fall, not zero gravity.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Near surface: v0​=gR​≈7.9 km/s — remember this anchor value.
  • T applet: for the ISS (≈ 90 min) confirm with T=2πr3/GM​.
  • To find height for a given T: first find r=(4π2GMT2​)1/3, then subtract R.
  • K=−U/2 and E=U/2 — instantly get E from U.
  • If E > 0 the orbit is unbound — the satellite will escape.

Exam Notes

  • Deriving v₀ and T from equating gravity to centripetal force is a year-on-year question.
  • Finding the altitude for a given period (e.g., 2 h, 24 h) is a standard numerical.
  • Energy numericals (K, U, E) for a given orbit are frequently asked.
  • Conceptual: 'why do astronauts float?' — answer free fall.
  • Geostationary orbit numbers (35,800 km, 3.08 km/s) are exam favourites.

Important Concepts

  • Orbital velocity v0​=GM/r​ follows from gravity = centripetal force.
  • Period T=2πr3/GM​ — Kepler's third law.
  • Orbital speed is independent of satellite mass.
  • Energy: K=2rGMm​, U=−rGMm​, E=−2rGMm​.
  • Weightlessness = free fall.

Important Tasks

  • Derive v0​=GM/r​ and T=2πr3/GM​.
  • Compute v and T for a satellite at a given altitude (R + h).
  • Compute K, U and E for a given orbit.
  • Find the geostationary altitude from T = 24 h.
  • Explain weightlessness in orbit in terms of free fall.

Summary

A satellite orbits when gravity supplies the centripetal force: v0​=GM/r​ and T=2πr3/GM​. Energy is K=2rGMm​, U=−rGMm​, E=−2rGMm​. Orbital speed is mass-independent; higher orbits are slower but longer-period. Weightlessness arises from continuous free fall.
