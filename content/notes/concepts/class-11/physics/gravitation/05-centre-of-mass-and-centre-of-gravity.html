Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Where an Object "Is"

    Every extended body behaves, under gravity and in collisions, as if all its mass were concentrated at one point. For most objects that point — the centre of mass — and the point where gravity effectively acts — the centre of gravity — coincide. Knowing where they sit explains balancing, stability and tumbling.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Centre of mass

    The centre of mass (COM) is the point where the total mass of a system may be assumed to be concentrated for translational motion: rCOM​=∑mi​∑mi​ri​​(continuous body: rCOM​=M1​∫rdm)

    Two-particle shortcut: the COM divides the line joining m1​,m2​ in the ratio m2​:m1​ (closer to the heavier mass).

    2.2 Centre of gravity

    The centre of gravity (COG) is the point where the total gravitational torque on the body is zero — the point at which the resultant weight may be considered to act: rCOG​=∑mi​gi​∑mi​gi​ri​​

    When do they coincide? When g is uniform over the body — true for everyday objects. They differ for very tall/large structures where g varies measurably with height (the COG sits slightly below the COM).

    2.3 Key properties

    • The COM of a uniform symmetric body is at its geometric centre (sphere, cube, rod, disc).
    • The COM may lie outside the material — a ring's COM is at its centre; a boomerang's is in the gap.
    • Motion of isolated systems: the COM of an isolated system moves with constant velocity (momentum conservation).
    • Stability: a body topples when the vertical line through its COG falls outside the base.

    2.4 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): Masses 2 kg and 6 kg sit at x = 0 and x = 2 m. Find the COM. xCOM​=82(0)+6(2)​=1.5 m — three-quarters of the way toward the heavier mass.

    Q (NEB-style): Where is the COM of a uniform L-shaped lamina made of two 20 cm rods (equal mass per length) joined at right angles? Treat each rod's mass at its midpoint: (10, 0) and (0, 10) cm with equal masses → COM at (5, 5) cm — inside the corner gap, outside both rods.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Unit 4 (torque and equilibrium): the COG is where weight's torque acts; equilibrium requires it above the base.
    • To momentum conservation (Topic 12): Fext​=MaCOM​ — Newton's second law for systems.
    • To satellites: two-body orbits are rotations about the common COM (barycentre).

Summary

The centre of mass rCOM​=∑mi​ri​/∑mi​ is the point where a system's mass acts concentrated for translation; the centre of gravity is where the resultant weight acts (zero net gravitational torque). They coincide in a uniform gravitational field — all everyday cases — and separate only when g varies across the body, the COG then sitting slightly below the COM. The COM divides the line between two masses in the ratio m2​:m1​, may lie outside the material (a ring), and obeys Fext​=MaCOM​, which is momentum conservation restated. Stability is a COG story: a body topples when the vertical through its COG leaves its base.

MCQS

1.

The centre of mass of a uniform ring lies:

Options
  • On the rim
  • At the centre (empty space)
  • At the bottom
  • Nowhere
Answer

B

Explanation

Symmetry places it at the geometric centre.

2.

COM and COG coincide when:

Options
  • Masses are equal
  • g is uniform over the body
  • The body is small only
  • Never
Answer

B

Explanation

Uniform field makes the g-weighting identical.

3.

Masses 2 kg and 6 kg, 2 m apart — COM from the 2 kg mass:

Options
  • 0.5 m
  • 1 m
  • 1.5 m
  • 2 m
Answer

C

Explanation

x = 6×2/8 = 1.5 m.

4.

The COM of an isolated system:

Options
  • Accelerates
  • Moves with constant velocity
  • Stays at origin
  • Oscillates
Answer

B

Explanation

No external force → constant COM velocity.

5.

A body topples when:

Options
  • Its COM is high
  • The vertical through COG leaves the base
  • Its mass is large
  • Friction is low only
Answer

B

Explanation

Torque about the base edge then overturns it.

6.

For a very tall building, the COG lies:

Options
  • Above the COM
  • Below the COM
  • At the COM
  • At the ground
Answer

B

Explanation

g is stronger lower down, weighting lower parts more.

7.

The equation of motion for a system's COM is:

Options
  • F = ma for each particle
  • F_ext = M a_COM
  • F = 0 always
  • F = mv
Answer

B

Explanation

Internal forces cancel in pairs.

8.

The COM of two particles divides their separation:

Options
  • Equally
  • In ratio m₁:m₂ from m₁
  • In ratio m₂:m₁ from m₁
  • Randomly
Answer

C

Explanation

Closer to the heavier mass.
