Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Speed That Leaves

    Throw a ball up and it returns; launch it fast enough and it never comes back. The boundary speed — the escape velocity — depends only on the planet's mass and radius, not on the projectile. It is the speed at which kinetic energy exactly cancels the gravitational binding.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2023)

    2.1 Definition

    Escape velocity is the minimum velocity with which a body must be projected from a planet's surface so that it escapes the planet's gravitational field completely (reaches infinity with zero speed).

    2.2 Derivation (NEB 2023, 5m)

    By conservation of mechanical energy (taking potential zero at infinity): total energy at surface21​mve2​−RGMm​​​=at infinity, just escaping0+0​​ 21​mve2​=RGMm​⇒ve​=R2GM​​​

    Using g=GM/R2: ve​=2gR​

    Value for Earth: ve​=2×9.8×6.4×106​=1.254×108​=1.12×104 m/s = 11.2 km/s.

    Key properties:

    • Independent of the mass of the escaping body and of the direction of projection (neglecting atmosphere).
    • ve​=2​vo​ — escape speed is only 41% more than orbital speed.
    • Smaller/denser bodies can have high escape speeds; the Moon's is 2.4 km/s (why it lost its atmosphere).

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (5m, 2023): Derive the expression for escape velocity of Earth. Hence calculate its value. (R = 6.4×10⁶ m, g = 9.8 m/s²) Derivation as §2.2; ve​=2gR​=2×9.8×6.4×106​=11.2 km/s.

    Q (3m, 2022): Calculate the escape velocity from the surface of a planet whose surface gravity is 5 m/s² and radius is 6×106 m. ve​=2gR​=2×5×6×106​=6×107​=7.75×103 m/s=7.75 km/s

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 30: the derivation is pure energy conservation with U=−GMm/r.
    • To Topic 33: ve​=2​vo​ at the same radius.
    • To atmospheres: gas molecules with rms speed comparable to ve​ leak away over time — why the Moon (2.4 km/s) is airless and Earth (11.2 km/s) kept its air.
    • To black holes: when ve​=c, nothing escapes — the Schwarzschild criterion.

Summary

Escape velocity is the minimum launch speed that lets a body leave a planet's gravitational field, reaching infinity with zero speed. Energy conservation between the surface and infinity, 21​mve2​−RGMm​=0, gives ve​=2GM/R​=2gR​ — independent of the projectile's mass and direction (NEB 2023, 5m). For Earth, 2×9.8×6.4×106​=11.2 km/s; for a planet with g = 5 m/s² and R = 6×10⁶ m, 7.75 km/s (NEB 2022). Escape speed is 2​ times orbital speed, and its ratio to molecular speeds decides whether a world keeps an atmosphere — the Moon's 2.4 km/s could not.

Specific Conditions

  • At altitude h, use r=R+h in v_e=√(2GM/r); do not reuse the surface radius in the general expression.
  • The derivation assumes no air resistance, spherical symmetry, negligible external bodies and no continuing thrust after launch.

Original Content

Notes
  • Escape velocity is the minimum speed at radius r that lets an object reach infinity with zero final speed when no further thrust or drag acts. With gravitational potential energy zero at infinity, energy conservation gives (1/2)mv²−GMm/r=0.
  • Solving gives ve=√(2GM/r). At the surface r=R, g=GM/R², so ve=√(2gR). The escaping object’s mass cancels, so the ideal threshold is independent of its mass.
  • Worked Earth estimate: g=9.81 m s⁻² and R=6.37×10⁶ m give ve=√(2gR)≈1.12×10⁴ m s⁻¹=11.2 km s⁻¹.
  • At altitude h, use r=R+h: ve(h)/ve(0)=√[R/(R+h)]. At h=R, local escape speed is 1/√2 of its surface value.
  • Circular orbital speed at the same radius is vc=√(GM/r), so ve=√2 vc. This is an ideal energy threshold; real launches must account for atmosphere, rotation, gravity losses and propulsion.
Confusion
  • The formula neglects air resistance, rotation, external bodies and continued thrust.
  • Escape speed is not the practical rocket delta-v from the launch pad.

Specific Conditions
  • Energy Conservation Derivation: Escape velocity ve​=2GM/R​ comes from setting total mechanical energy to zero at infinity: 21​mve2​−GMm/R=0. This is the minimum speed to reach infinity with zero residual kinetic energy.
  • Relation to Orbital Velocity: ve​=2​⋅v0​ where v0​=GM/R​ is the circular orbital velocity at the surface. The factor 2​≈1.414 means escape requires 41% more speed than a low circular orbit.
  • No Atmosphere Assumption: The formula assumes no air resistance. In reality, atmospheric drag requires higher initial speed for Earth; for the Moon (no atmosphere), the formula is exact. Also assumes a spherically symmetric, non-rotating body.

Confusion

  • Orbital vs escape: √(gR) = 7.9 km/s keeps you circling; √(2gR) = 11.2 km/s sets you free.
  • Mass of the projectile is irrelevant — a proton and a rocket share the same escape speed.
