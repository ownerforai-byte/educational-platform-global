Notes

  • Escape velocity is the minimum speed at radius r that lets an object reach infinity with zero final speed when no further thrust or drag acts. With gravitational potential energy zero at infinity, energy conservation gives (1/2)mv²−GMm/r=0.
  • Solving gives ve=√(2GM/r). At the surface r=R, g=GM/R², so ve=√(2gR). The escaping object’s mass cancels, so the ideal threshold is independent of its mass.
  • Worked Earth estimate: g=9.81 m s⁻² and R=6.37×10⁶ m give ve=√(2gR)≈1.12×10⁴ m s⁻¹=11.2 km s⁻¹.
  • At altitude h, use r=R+h: ve(h)/ve(0)=√[R/(R+h)]. At h=R, local escape speed is 1/√2 of its surface value.
  • Circular orbital speed at the same radius is vc=√(GM/r), so ve=√2 vc. This is an ideal energy threshold; real launches must account for atmosphere, rotation, gravity losses and propulsion.
  • Escape velocity is the speed that lets a projectile coast to infinity, where the gravitational potential is taken as zero. It does not depend on the mass of the projectile. From the Earth's surface it is about 11 km/s, which is √2 times the speed of a low circular orbit.

Formulas

  • At radius r: ve=√(2GM/r); at surface ve=√(2GM/R)=√(2gR).
  • At the same radius, ve=√2 vc where vc=√(GM/r).

Examples

  • Earth: √(2×9.81×6.37×10⁶)≈11.2 km s⁻¹.
  • At h=R, ve(h)/ve(0)=1/√2.
  • Escape velocity is the speed that lets a projectile coast to infinity, where the gravitational potential is taken as zero. It does not depend on the mass of the projectile. From the Earth's surface it is about 11 km/s, which is √2 times the speed of a low circular orbit.
  • The working relations are v_esc = √(2 g R) at the surface and v_esc = √(2 G M / R). Name each symbol, convert the data into SI units, and only then substitute.
  • Take g = 10 m/s^2 and R = 6.4 × 10^6 m. Estimate the escape speed from the surface.

Key Points

  • At escape threshold, total mechanical energy is zero at infinity.
  • Use r=R+h for altitude, not the surface radius.
  • The orbital-speed relation compares both speeds at the same radius.

  • v_esc = √(2 G M / R)

Summary

Energy conservation gives ve=√(2GM/R)=√(2gR) for an ideal surface launch. It is independent of projectile mass and √2 times circular speed at the same radius.

Specific Conditions

  • At altitude h, use r=R+h in v_e=√(2GM/r); do not reuse the surface radius in the general expression.
  • The derivation assumes no air resistance, spherical symmetry, negligible external bodies and no continuing thrust after launch.

Specific Conditions
  • Energy Conservation Derivation: Escape velocity ve​=2GM/R​ comes from setting total mechanical energy to zero at infinity: 21​mve2​−GMm/R=0. This is the minimum speed to reach infinity with zero residual kinetic energy.
  • Relation to Orbital Velocity: ve​=2​⋅v0​ where v0​=GM/R​ is the circular orbital velocity at the surface. The factor 2​≈1.414 means escape requires 41% more speed than a low circular orbit.
  • No Atmosphere Assumption: The formula assumes no air resistance. In reality, atmospheric drag requires higher initial speed for Earth; for the Moon (no atmosphere), the formula is exact. Also assumes a spherically symmetric, non-rotating body.

Confusion

  • The formula neglects air resistance, rotation, external bodies and continued thrust.
  • Escape speed is not the practical rocket delta-v from the launch pad.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Exercises

1.

Take g = 10 m/s^2 and R = 6.4 × 10^6 m. Estimate the escape speed from the surface.

Steps
  • v = √(2 g R).
  • 2 g R = 2 × 10 × 6.4 × 10^6 = 1.28 × 10^8.
  • √(1.28 × 10^8) = √1.28 × 10^4 ≈ 1.1 × 10^4 m/s.
  • The mass of the rocket does not appear.
  • Air resistance would mean a larger launch speed is needed.
Answer

About 1.1 × 10^4 m/s.
