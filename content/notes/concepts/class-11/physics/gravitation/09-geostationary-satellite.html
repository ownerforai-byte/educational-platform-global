Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Hanging Over One Spot

    A satellite that circles Earth in exactly 24 hours — moving with Earth's rotation — appears to hover over a fixed point on the equator. That demanding condition fixes its orbit precisely: one radius, one plane, one speed. Every TV dish and weather satellite image depends on it.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Conditions for a geostationary orbit

    1. Period = Earth's rotation period (23 h 56 min 4 s ≈ 24 h — sidereal day).
    2. Orbit in the equatorial plane (otherwise it would drift north–south).
    3. Circular orbit, moving west→east (same sense as Earth's rotation).
    4. Fixed longitude over the equator.

    2.2 Derivation of the orbital radius

    From T=2πr3/GM​: r3=4π2GMT2​⇒r=(4π2GMT2​)1/3 With M=6×1024 kg, T=86400 s: r3=39.486.67×10−11×6×1024×(8.64×104)2​=7.54×1022 m3⇒r=4.23×107 m

    Height above surface: h=r−R=4.23×107−6.4×106=3.59×107 m ≈ 36 000 km.

    Orbital speed: v=2πr/T=2π×4.23×107/86400=3.1 km/s.

    2.3 Uses

    • Communication: fixed antennas point once, forever (TV, telephone relays).
    • Weather watching: continuous images of the same hemisphere.
    • Broadcasting and military reconnaissance.

    Limitations: cannot serve polar regions (low elevation angle), signal delay ≈ 0.24 s round trip, and the orbit slot is a limited international resource.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 33: the geostationary radius is the answer to "what r gives T = 24 h?" — r=(GMT2/4π2)1/3.
    • To Kepler's third law: T2∝r3 fixes the radius once T is fixed.
    • To GPS (Topic 37): GPS satellites are NOT geostationary — they orbit at ~20 200 km with 12-hour periods, needing six orbital planes.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Writing the height as 42 300 km — that is the radius; the height is r − R ≈ 36 000 km.

    3. Omitting the equatorial-plane condition.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (4m): Calculate the radius and period-consistency of a geostationary orbit (M = 6×10²⁴ kg, T = 8.64×10⁴ s). — r³ = GMT²/4π² = (4.00×10¹⁴ × 7.46×10⁹)/39.48 = 7.56×10²² m³ → r = 4.23×10⁷ m; h = 3.59×10⁷ m ≈ 36 000 km.

Summary

A geostationary satellite keeps pace with Earth's rotation, hovering over a fixed equatorial point. Its conditions: period equal to the sidereal day (≈ 24 h), circular orbit in the equatorial plane, revolving west→east. Kepler's third law then fixes a unique radius: r=(GMT2/4π2)1/3=4.23×107 m — a height of about 36 000 km, with orbital speed ≈ 3.1 km/s. The fixed position lets ground antennas point permanently, powering communication, broadcasting and continuous weather imaging — though polar regions are poorly served and signal delay is ~0.24 s. GPS deliberately breaks all these conditions, using faster 12-hour orbits in six planes.
