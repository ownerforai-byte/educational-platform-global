  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Spark That Starts Electricity

    Rub a balloon on your hair and it sticks to the wall. That invisible "something" is electric charge — a fundamental property of matter, quantised in units of e, conserved in every interaction, and the protagonist of all of electrostatics.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021)

    2.1 Electric charge and its properties

    • Charge is the intrinsic property of matter responsible for electric forces; carried by protons (+e) and electrons (−e).
    • Two kinds: positive and negative — like charges repel, unlike attract.
    • Additivity: total charge is the algebraic sum of individual charges.
    • Quantisation (NEB 2021, 3m): all charges are integer multiples of the elementary charge:

    q=ne,e=1.6×10−19 C Possible charges: …, −2e, −e, 0, +e, +2e, … — a charge of 2.5e can never be observed, because charge is carried in discrete packets (electrons/protons; quarks carry fractional charge but are always confined inside whole hadrons).

    • Conservation: the total charge of an isolated system never changes — charging merely separates equal amounts of + and −.
    • Invariance: charge is the same in every reference frame.

    2.2 Charging methods

    • Friction (rubbing): transfer of electrons between surfaces (comb–hair, silk–glass).
    • Conduction (contact): touching a charged body to a neutral one shares the charge.
    • Induction: charging without contact by redistributing charge with a nearby charged body and earthing (Topic 84).

    2.3 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): How many electrons make a charge of −1 C? n=eq​=1.6×10−191​=6.25×1018 electrons.

    Q (NEB-style): A body gains 10⁹ electrons. Find its charge. q=ne=109×(−1.6×10−19)=−1.6×10−10 C.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 85: Coulomb's law quantifies the force between these charges.
    • To atomic structure: protons/electrons carry ±e; the neutron carries none.
    • To nuclear physics (Unit 26): charge conservation rules every nuclear reaction.

Summary

Electric charge is the fundamental property behind electric forces: two kinds (positive, negative), like repelling and unlike attracting. Its defining properties: additivity (charges sum algebraically), quantisation — free charge is always an integer multiple of e = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ C, so 2.5e can never be observed because charge travels in discrete electron/proton packets (NEB 2021, 3m) — and conservation, the total charge of an isolated system being fixed. Bodies charge by friction, conduction or induction; one coulomb equals 6.25×10¹⁸ elementary charges. These properties anchor all of electrostatics, from Coulomb's law to nuclear reactions.

Exercises

1.

A neutral body loses 2.0 × 10^12 electrons. Take e = 1.6 × 10^−19 C. Find the charge left on the body and its sign.

Steps
  • Electrons are negative, so removing them leaves the body positive.
  • Q = N e = 2.0 × 10^12 × 1.6 × 10^−19.
  • Q = 3.2 × 10^−7 C.
  • The cloth gains those electrons and becomes negative by the same amount.
  • Total charge of rod plus cloth is still zero.
Answer

+3.2 × 10^−7 C on the body.
