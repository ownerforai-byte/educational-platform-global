  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Inverse Square Law of Charge

    Two centuries before electrons were discovered, Coulomb measured with a torsion balance how charged bodies push and pull: a force proportional to each charge, inversely proportional to the square of the separation. That law is the electrostatic twin of Newton's gravitation.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Statement and formula

    Coulomb's law: the electrostatic force between two point charges acts along the line joining them, is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation: F=4πε0​1​r2q1​q2​​=kr2q1​q2​​

    • k=9×109 N m²C⁻² (in air/vacuum); ε0​=8.85×10−12 C²N⁻¹m⁻² (permittivity of free space).
    • Like charges repel, unlike attract — the sign of the product sets the direction.
    • In a dielectric medium: F=4πε0​εr​1​r2q1​q2​​ — the force shrinks by the relative permittivity.

Summary

Coulomb's law gives the force between point charges: F=4πε0​1​r2q1​q2​​, along the line joining them — repulsive for like charges, attractive for unlike — with k=9×109 N m²C⁻². NEB 2024's pair (2 μC, 3 μC, 0.1 m) repels at 5.4 N (3m); NEB 2022's MCQ (+2 μC, −3 μC, 0.6 m) gives 0.15 N attractive (1m). The law shares gravitation's inverse-square form but differs in sign behaviour, in being ~10³⁹ stronger for elementary particles, and in being weakened by dielectric media through ε_r.

Exercises

1.

Charges of 2.0 μC and 4.0 μC are 0.20 m apart in air. Find the force. Use k = 9.0 × 10^9.

Steps
  • q1 q2 = 8.0 × 10^−12.
  • r^2 = 0.040.
  • F = 9.0 × 10^9 × 8.0 × 10^−12 / 0.040.
  • 9e9 × 8e−12 = 0.072, and 0.072/0.040 = 1.8 N.
  • The charges repel.
Answer

1.8 N, repulsive.
