Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Charging Without Touching

    Bring a charged rod near a metal sphere and the sphere's own charges rearrange: the near side turns opposite, the far side same. Earth the sphere at the right moment and it keeps a permanent charge — all without contact. That is induction, the trick behind electrostatic generators and lightning safety.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 What is charging by induction?

    Induction charges a body without contact with a charged object: a nearby charge redistributes the free electrons of a conductor. The net charge of the pair is unchanged — induction separates charge, it never creates it.

    2.2 The four-step process

    Charging a metal sphere positively with a negatively charged rod:

    1. Bring the charged rod near (not touching) the sphere — electrons in the sphere are repelled to the far side; the near side becomes positive (charge polarisation).
    2. Earth the far side — repelled electrons flow away to the ground through the earth wire.
    3. Remove the earth connection first — the sphere is left electron-deficient.
    4. Remove the rod — the sphere is left with a uniform positive charge.

    Key sequencing: earth while the rod is near; disconnect the earth before removing the rod.

    2.3 Everyday evidence

    • A charged comb attracts neutral paper: the paper's charges polarise, the nearer opposite charge feels a stronger pull.
    • A charged balloon sticks to a neutral wall.
    • Electrostatic induction underlies the Wimshurst/van de Graaff generators and the gold-leaf electroscope's response.

    2.4 Worked reasoning

    Q (NEB-style): Why does a negatively charged rod attract a neutral conducting ball? The rod's negative charge repels free electrons to the ball's far side, leaving positive charge on the near side. The nearer positive charge feels a stronger attraction than the farther negative charge feels repulsion — net attraction.

    Q (NEB-style): Why must the earth wire be removed before the charged rod? Removing the rod first would let electrons flow back from the earth and neutralise the sphere — the induced charge exists only while the rod's field is present.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 83: induction conserves charge — it only separates it.
    • To conductors vs insulators: induction needs mobile electrons; insulators polarise only molecularly.
    • To lightning protection: earthing provides a charge reservoir — the same physics in reverse.

Summary

Charging by induction gives a conductor a permanent charge without contact: a nearby charged rod polarises the conductor's free electrons; earthing lets the repelled charge escape to ground; disconnecting the earth and then removing the rod leaves a net charge of the opposite sign to the rod. Charge is only redistributed — never created (conservation). The same polarisation explains why a charged comb attracts neutral paper and why electroscopes and electrostatic generators work. Order is everything: earth while the inducer is present, disconnect the earth before removing the inducer.
