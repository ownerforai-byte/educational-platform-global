Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Superposition: Adding Forces Vectorially

    With three or more charges, every pair interacts independently. Coulomb's law plus the principle of superposition — add all pairwise forces as vectors — handles any configuration, from electron clouds to charged spheres.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Principle of superposition

    The force on any charge in a system equals the vector sum of the forces each other charge would exert alone: F1​=F12​+F13​+F14​+⋯

    • Each pairwise force obeys Coulomb's law independently — the presence of other charges does not modify any single pair's force.
    • Forces add as vectors: resolve into components when directions differ.

    2.2 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): Charges q₁ = +2 μC and q₂ = +2 μC sit 0.2 m apart on the x-axis; q₃ = +1 μC sits at the midpoint. Find the net force on q₃.

    • Forces from q₁ and q₂ on q₃: equal magnitude, opposite directions → cancel.
    • F13​=F23​=(0.1)29×109×2×10−6×10−6​=1.8 N each, opposite → net force = 0.

    Q (NEB-style): Two charges +4 μC and −4 μC are 0.3 m apart. Find the field point force on a +1 μC test charge placed 0.1 m from +4 μC along the line beyond the −4 μC charge... simpler: find the net force on a charge placed at the midpoint of an unlike pair.

    • Unlike pair ±4 μC, 0.3 m apart, midpoint: both forces point the same way (toward the negative).
    • Feach​=(0.15)29×109×4×10−6×1×10−6​=1.6 N → net = 3.2 N toward the negative charge.

    Q (NEB-style): Three equal charges q at the corners of an equilateral triangle: force on each? Each charge feels two forces of magnitude a2kq2​ at 60° apart; the resultant has magnitude 3​a2kq2​ directed away from the triangle's centre (for like charges).

    • To vectors (Unit 2): component resolution is the practical tool.

  • Independence of pairs: other charges never "screen" a pairwise Coulomb force.

Summary

The principle of superposition extends Coulomb's law to many charges: the force on any one charge is the vector sum of the independent pairwise forces rij2​kqi​qj​​ exerted by all the others — no pair is affected by a third charge's presence. Symmetry simplifies everything: at the midpoint of a like pair the two forces cancel; at the midpoint of an unlike pair they add (3.2 N on 1 μC between ±4 μC, 0.3 m apart); each charge of an equilateral triangle of like charges feels 3​kq2/a2. Component resolution plus Coulomb's law solves any arrangement.

Exercises

1.

A 1.0 μC charge at the origin feels 3.0 × 10^−3 N east from one charge and 4.0 × 10^−3 N north from another. Find the net force.

Steps
  • The forces are perpendicular.
  • R = √(3.0^2 + 4.0^2) × 10^−3 = 5.0 × 10^−3 N.
  • tanθ = 4/3, so θ = 53° north of east.
  • Do not add 3 and 4 to get 7.
  • The third charge, if it sat on the test charge, is not a separate force on itself.
Answer

5.0 × 10^−3 N at 53° north of east.
