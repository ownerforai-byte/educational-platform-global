Notes

  • Electric Charge & Elementary Charge: Electric charge is an intrinsic physical property of elementary matter that governs all electromagnetic interactions. Charges exist in two polarities designated by Benjamin Franklin as positive (carried by protons, +e) and negative (carried by electrons, −e). The elementary charge quantum is e≈1.602×10−19C. Charge is a scalar quantity with SI unit Coulomb (C=A⋅s) and dimensions [IT].
  • Quantization of Charge: Electric charge is not continuous; it exists only in discrete integral multiples of the elementary electronic charge: q=±ne, where n∈{0,1,2,3,…}. Quantization was first demonstrated macroscopically by Robert Millikan's oil-drop experiment (1909). At macroscopic scales involving microcoulombs, the graininess is obscured (1μC≈6.24×1012 electrons), allowing charge to be treated as continuous.
  • Conservation & Additivity of Charge: In any isolated thermodynamic system, the algebraic sum of all electric charges remains strictly constant regardless of physical or chemical transformations: ∑qinitial​=∑qfinal​. In addition, charge is additive (total charge of an extended body is the scalar algebraic sum ∑qi​) and relativistically invariant (the magnitude of charge is independent of the reference frame speed, unlike mass).
  • Methods of Charging — Friction & Conduction: Neutral matter is charged by electron transfer: (1) Triboelectric effect (Friction): When two differing materials (e.g., glass and silk, or amber and wool) are rubbed, work function differences drive electron transfer from the lower to higher work function material; (2) Conduction (Contact): Direct contact between a charged conductor and a neutral conductor allows electron flow until both bodies achieve identical electrostatic potentials.
  • Charging by Electrostatic Induction: Charging by induction produces a net static charge on a conductor without physical contact with the charging source. Bringing a positively charged rod near an isolated neutral conductor repels positive ions conceptually while attracting mobile free conduction electrons toward the near face (polarization). Grounding the far face discharges positive potential by drawing electrons from Earth. Severing the ground connection before withdrawing the rod permanently traps an opposite negative charge on the conductor.
  • Gold-Leaf Electroscope & Charge Detection: The gold-leaf electroscope is a classical electrostatic apparatus comprising a brass rod, brass disc, and two ultra-thin gold leaves housed in an evacuated glass enclosure. Divergence of the leaves signals the presence of charge via mutual electrostatic repulsion. The electroscope can identify charge sign by observing leaf divergence when an unknown charge is brought near a pre-charged electroscope.

Confusion

  • Thinking induction creates new charge: Induction does not generate charge from nothing; it redistributes existing mobile valence electrons within the conductor while preserving net charge conservation of the combined system.
  • Believing grounding always pulls electrons down to Earth: If a positively charged rod is inducing negative charge near it, grounding pulls electrons UP from the Earth into the conductor. If a negative rod is near, electrons flow DOWN into the Earth.
  • Assuming insulators can be charged permanently by induction: Only materials with delocalized mobile charges (conductors) can be charged by induction and grounding. Insulators experience only molecular dielectric polarization.

Practice

  • A neutral metallic sphere on an insulating stand is to be charged with +3.2×10−10C using an inducing rod. (a) What sign of charge must the inducing rod carry? (b) How many electrons must be removed from the sphere? Solution: (a) To induce a net positive charge on the conductor, a negatively charged rod must be brought near it, driving electrons to ground when earthed. (b) Using quantization q=ne: n=eq​=1.602×10−19C3.2×10−10C​=2.0×109 electrons transferred to Earth.

Universal Facts

  • Charge is an absolute relativistic invariant: an electron's charge remains exactly 1.602176634×10−19C whether at rest in a laboratory or traveling at 99.999% the speed of light in the Large Hadron Collider.
  • Quarks carry fractional charges (+32​e and −31​e), but because of quantum chromodynamic color confinement, quarks never exist as isolated free particles; all observable free entities have integer charge multiples ne.

Examples

  • Electrostatic precipitators: Industrial smoke stacks ionize airborne fly ash and soot particles, which are attracted to grounded collecting plates, purifying factory exhaust by over 99%.
  • Photocopier (Xerography): An aluminum drum coated with photoconductive selenium is charged by corona wire in the dark; light from the document discharges illuminated areas, leaving an electrostatic latent image that attracts toner powder.
  • Electrostatic spray painting: Paint droplets are given a uniform negative charge as they leave the nozzle, while the metal car frame is grounded/positive. Mutual repulsion atomizes the spray into a fine mist that wraps uniformly around the car.

Key Points

  • Electric charge is quantized in integral multiples of e=1.602×10−19C.
  • Charge is an isolated invariant scalar conserved across all nuclear and electromagnetic interactions.
  • Induction charges a conductor with the sign opposite to that of the inducing charged body without contact loss.
  • Grounding neutralizes electrostatic potential by allowing electron exchange with Earth's infinite charge reservoir.
  • Electrostatic attraction occurs between unlike charges and between any charged body and a neutral conductor via polarization.

Summary

Electric charge is a fundamental, quantized (q=ne), and conserved property of matter. Objects can be charged by triboelectric friction, conduction, or electrostatic induction. Charging by induction polarizes mobile electrons in an insulated conductor; momentary grounding allows electron exchange with Earth, permanently trapping an opposite net charge on the conductor without losing inducing charge.

Special Notes

  • Grounding must be removed BEFORE withdrawing the inducing charged body; otherwise, mobile charges equalize back through ground, leaving the sphere neutral.
  • Repulsion is the only sure test of electrification because an uncharged body can be attracted by a charged body due to electrostatic polarization.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Electric charge is quantized, meaning it exists only in integral multiples of the fundamental charge e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.
  • Statement 2: The total electric charge of an isolated system remains constant over time regardless of physical or chemical reactions.
  • Statement 3: Electrostatic induction allows charging a conductor with a sign opposite to that of the inducing charged object without physical contact.
  • Statement 4: Repulsion is the definitive test of electrification, since neutral matter is attracted to any charged body by polarization.
  • Statement 5: Charge is invariant under relativistic transformations, independent of the relative velocity of the frame of reference.

Important Notes

  • Remember that in conductors, only negative charges (free conduction electrons) physically move; positive ionic cores remain fixed in the crystal lattice.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Quantization check: divide given charge Q by 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; if n is not an integer, the charge is physically impossible.
  • Induction charge sign: Induced net charge on conductor is ALWAYS opposite to the sign of the inducing rod.
  • Identical spheres contact trick: Touching identical spheres of charges q₁ and q₂ redistributes charge equally to (q₁ + q₂)/2 each.

1.

Which of the following charges cannot exist on an isolated physical body?

Options
  • 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
  • 4.8 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
  • 2.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
  • 6.4 × 10⁻¹⁹ C
Answer

C

Explanation

From quantization q = ne, n = (2.4 × 10⁻¹⁹) / (1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 1.5, which is not an integer. Therefore, this charge cannot exist.

2.

Charging a metallic sphere by induction using a positively charged glass rod leaves the sphere:

Options
  • positively charged
  • negatively charged
  • uncharged
  • either positive or negative depending on sphere size
Answer

B

Explanation

A positively charged rod attracts electrons from ground during the earthing phase, leaving the sphere with an excess of electrons (negatively charged).

3.

The surest test to confirm whether a body is electrified is:

Options
  • attraction
  • repulsion
  • polarization
  • earthing
Answer

B

Explanation

Repulsion occurs exclusively between two bodies carrying like charges. Attraction can also occur between a charged body and a neutral body via polarization.
