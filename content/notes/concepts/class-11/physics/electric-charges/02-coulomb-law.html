Notes

  • Coulomb's Law of Electrostatics: Formulated by Charles-Augustin de Coulomb in 1785, the electrostatic force between two stationary point charges is directly proportional to the product of the magnitudes of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them: F=kr2∣q1​q2​∣​. The electrostatic force acts along the line connecting the centers of the two charges, being repulsive for like charges (q1​q2​>0) and attractive for unlike charges (q1​q2​<0).
  • Electrostatic Constant & Permittivity of Free Space: The proportionality constant in vacuum/air is k=4πε0​1​≈8.98755×109N⋅m2/C2≈9×109N⋅m2/C2. Here ε0​ denotes the electric permittivity of free space, with value ε0​=8.854×10−12C2/(N⋅m2)=8.854×10−12F/m and dimensions [M−1L−3T4I2].
  • Vector Formulation & Newton's Third Law: In vector notation, the force F12​ exerted on charge q1​ by charge q2​ located at position vector r2​ is: F12​=4πε0​1​∣r1​−r2​∣3q1​q2​​(r1​−r2​)=4πε0​1​r2q1​q2​​r^21​. Because r^12​=−r^21​, it follows rigorously that F12​=−F21​. Thus, electrostatic forces between two charges constitute an action-reaction pair satisfying Newton's Third Law in its strong central-force form.
  • Dielectric Medium & Relative Permittivity: When point charges are embedded within an insulating medium of permittivity ε, dielectric polarization shields the charges, reducing the net force to Fm​=4πε1​r2∣q1​q2​∣​=εr​F0​​=κF0​​. The dimensionless quantity κ=εr​=ε0​ε​≥1 is the dielectric constant (relative permittivity) of the medium (for vacuum κ=1, dry air ≈1.0006, water ≈80, ideal conductor κ→∞).
  • Principle of Linear Superposition: Electrostatic interaction between any pair of charges is completely unaffected by the presence of other surrounding charges. Consequently, the net electrostatic force on a test charge q0​ due to a discrete distribution of N point charges q1​,q2​,…,qN​ is the vector sum: Fnet​=∑i=1N​F0i​=4πε0​q0​​∑i=1N​∣r0​−ri​∣3qi​​(r0​−ri​).
  • Electrostatic Equilibrium & Earnshaw's Theorem: A system of charges is in equilibrium if the net electrostatic force on each constituent charge vanishes (∑F=0). By Earnshaw's theorem (derived from Gauss's law ∇⋅E=0 in charge-free regions), a collection of stationary point charges cannot be maintained in a state of stable equilibrium solely under the influence of inverse-square electrostatic interactions.

Confusion

  • Adding electrostatic forces as scalar algebraic quantities: Electric forces are vectors! When calculating the net force exerted by multiple charges, one must resolve each pairwise force into Cartesian orthogonal components (Fx​,Fy​) before summing: Fnet​=(∑Fx​)2+(∑Fy​)2​.
  • Ignoring the medium's dielectric screening: In water (κ≈80), the electrostatic force between ions drops to 801​ of its vacuum value, explaining why table salt (NaCl) spontaneously dissociates into hydrated Na+ and Cl− ions in water.
  • Applying Coulomb's law to extended charge distributions at close range: Coulomb's inverse-square formula applies strictly to point charges or non-overlapping spherical charge distributions (via Shell Theorem). At close range, induction distorts charge distribution on real conductors.

Universal Facts

  • The electrostatic force between two protons inside a nucleus is approximately 1036 times stronger than the gravitational force between them: Fg​Fe​​=Gmp2​/r2e2/(4πε0​r2)​≈1.24×1036, showing the colossal dominance of electromagnetism over gravity at microscopic scales.
  • Coulomb's law has been tested experimentally to an accuracy of better than 1 part in 1016, establishing the inverse power r−(2+δ) with ∣delta∣<10−16, proving the photon has zero rest mass.

Examples

  • Ionic lattice cohesion in minerals: Table salt crystals (NaCl) are held together by Coulomb attraction between alternating Na+ cations and Cl− anions, providing a high melting point of 801 °C.
  • Atomic electron orbitals: In Bohr's model of the hydrogen atom, the electrostatic Coulomb attraction between the single proton nucleus and orbiting electron (F=4πε0​r2e2​) provides the required centripetal acceleration (mv2/r).
  • Dissolution in polar solvents: The high dielectric constant of liquid water (κ≈80) reduces ionic attraction by a factor of 80, permitting thermal agitation to break ionic bonds and dissolve salts.

Key Points

  • Coulomb's law is an inverse-square central force acting along the line connecting two stationary point charges.
  • Vector formulation demonstrates mutual symmetry: F12​=−F21​, fulfilling Newton's third law.
  • Presence of a dielectric medium with relative permittivity κ>1 reduces electrostatic force by a factor of κ.
  • The principle of superposition allows independent pairwise summation of forces in multi-charge configurations.
  • Earnshaw's theorem establishes that stable electrostatic equilibrium of stationary point charges is impossible without non-electrostatic constraints.

Summary

Coulomb's Law quantifies the electrostatic force between two stationary point charges as directly proportional to the product of charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation: F = k|q₁q₂|/r². In vector form, forces obey Newton's third law (F₁₂ = -F₂₁). When immersed in a dielectric medium of relative permittivity κ, the force reduces to F₀/κ. For multiple charges, the net force is determined via the principle of linear superposition by vector addition of all individual pairwise interactions.

Special Notes

  • Coulomb's law is strictly valid only for stationary point charges in an inertial reference frame. Accelerating charges radiate electromagnetic energy.
  • Definition of 1 Coulomb: That charge which, when placed in vacuum at a distance of 1 meter from an identical charge, repels it with a force of 9 × 10⁹ N.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: The electrostatic force between two stationary point charges varies inversely with the square of the distance between them.
  • Statement 2: Electrostatic forces are central forces acting strictly along the straight line joining the centers of two interacting charges.
  • Statement 3: Electrostatic forces satisfy Newton's third law: the force on charge 1 by charge 2 is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the force on charge 2 by charge 1.
  • Statement 4: The electrostatic force between two charges is independent of the presence of any other surrounding charges.
  • Statement 5: In any dielectric medium with relative permittivity κ, the electrostatic force between two charges is attenuated by a factor of κ.

Important Notes

  • Remember that the permittivity of free space ε₀ has SI units of C²/(N·m²) or F/m, with value 8.854 × 10⁻¹² F/m.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Equilibrium between like charges q₁ and q₂ at distance L: Third charge of opposite sign placed at x = L / (√(q₂/q₁) + 1) from q₁ achieves equilibrium.
  • Ratio of forces in dielectric medium: F_air / F_medium = κ.
  • Two identical charges in symmetrical polygons: Net electrostatic force at the geometric center of any regular polygon with equal vertices charges is identically ZERO.

Exam Notes

1.

Two point charges placed at a distance r in air experience a force F. If they are immersed in a medium of dielectric constant κ = 4 at the same distance, the new force is:

Options
  • 4F
  • 2F
  • F / 4
  • F / 2
Answer

C

Explanation

In a dielectric medium, the force is reduced by the relative permittivity: F_medium = F_air / κ = F / 4.

2.

Which of the following represents the correct vector form of Coulomb's law for force on q₁ due to q₂?

Options
  • F₁₂ = (1 / 4πε₀) · (q₁ q₂ / r²) r̂₁₂
  • F₁₂ = (1 / 4πε₀) · (q₁ q₂ / r³) r⃗₂₁
  • F₁₂ = (1 / 4πε₀) · (q₁ q₂ / r²) r̂₂₁
  • F₁₂ = (1 / 4πε₀) · (q₁ q₂ / r) r̂₂₁
Answer

C

Explanation

The force on charge 1 due to charge 2 acts along the unit vector from charge 2 to charge 1 (r̂₂₁): F⃗₁₂ = (1/4πε₀) · (q₁q₂/r²) r̂₂₁.

3.

Four equal positive charges +q are fixed at the four corners of a square of side a. The net force on a test charge placed at the center of the square is:

Options
  • 4kq² / a²
  • 2kq² / a²
  • Zero
  • √2 kq² / a²
Answer

C

Explanation

By diagonal symmetry, forces exerted by pairs of charges located at opposite corners are equal in magnitude and oppositely directed, canceling to exactly zero.

Important Concepts

  • Inverse-square law and electrostatic proportionality constant k = 1/(4πε₀).
  • Coulomb's law in vector form satisfying Newton's third law (central force).
  • Dielectric screening effect and definition of relative permittivity κ = ε/ε₀.
  • Principle of linear superposition for discrete charge distributions.
