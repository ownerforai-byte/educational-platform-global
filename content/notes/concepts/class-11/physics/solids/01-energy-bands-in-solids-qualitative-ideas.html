Notes

  • Atomic Energy Levels: An isolated atom has discrete energy levels (1s, 2s, 2p, etc.). Electrons occupy these levels according to Pauli exclusion principle.
  • Formation of Energy Bands: When N atoms come together to form a solid, their wavefunctions overlap. Each atomic level splits into N closely spaced levels, forming an energy band. For macroscopic N (~10²³), levels form quasi-continuous bands.
  • Valence Band: The highest energy band containing electrons at 0 K. It may be fully or partially filled. Electrons in this band are bound to atoms.
  • Conduction Band: The next higher energy band, typically empty or partially filled at 0 K. Electrons here are free to move and conduct electricity.
  • Band Gap (E_g): The energy gap between valence band and conduction band where no electron states exist. Size determines electrical classification.
  • Energy vs Momentum (E-k diagram): In solids, electron energy depends on wavevector k. Bands form allowed ranges; gaps are forbidden ranges.
  • Pauli Exclusion: Each quantum state can hold max 2 electrons (spin up/down). Band filling determines whether material conducts.

Specific Conditions

  • Isolated Atom Limit: Discrete levels only for isolated atoms. Bands form only when atoms are close (interatomic interaction).
  • Temperature Effect: At T > 0, electrons can be thermally excited across band gap if k_BT is comparable to E_g.
  • Macroscopic Solid: Band concept requires large N — small clusters have discrete levels (quantum dots).

Formulas

  • Band gap: E_g = E_conduction_bottom - E_valence_top
  • Number of states in band: ~2N (from N atoms, 2 electrons per state)
  • Fermi-Dirac: f(E) = 1/(exp((E-E_F)/k_BT) + 1)
  • Thermal energy at 300 K: k_BT ≈ 0.026 eV

Key Points

  • Isolated atoms have discrete levels; solids have energy bands.
  • N atomic levels split into N closely spaced levels forming a band.
  • Valence band: highest occupied band at 0 K.
  • Conduction band: band above valence band where electrons conduct.
  • Band gap E_g: forbidden energy region between valence and conduction bands.
  • Band structure determines whether solid is metal, insulator, or semiconductor.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: In solids, discrete atomic energy levels broaden into energy bands due to interatomic interaction.
  • Statement 2: The valence band is the highest energy band occupied by electrons at 0 K.
  • Statement 3: The conduction band is the band above the valence band where electrons can move freely.
  • Statement 4: The band gap E_g is the forbidden energy region between valence and conduction bands.
  • Statement 5: Electrical classification (metal/insulator/semiconductor) depends on band structure and E_g.

Important Notes

  • Band gap determines conductivity: metals (no gap or overlap), semiconductors (small gap ~1 eV), insulators (large gap > 3 eV).
  • Fermi level E_F is the energy at which occupation probability is 1/2 at T = 0 K.
  • Only electrons near Fermi level participate in conduction — deep band electrons are too tightly bound.
  • Temperature excites electrons across gap if thermal energy k_BT is sufficient.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Metal: valence band partially filled or overlaps conduction band.
  • Insulator: full valence band, large gap (> 3 eV), empty conduction band.
  • Semiconductor: full valence band, small gap (~1 eV), some thermal excitation.
  • Band gap E_g determines conductivity category.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks for qualitative explanation of band formation and classification.
  • Distinguish valence band, conduction band, and band gap clearly.
  • Explain why metals conduct and insulators don't using band picture.
  • Draw schematic band diagrams for metal, insulator, semiconductor.

Examples

  • Sodium: 3s band half-filled → metal (one valence electron per atom, band holds 2).
  • Diamond: valence band full, gap 5.5 eV → insulator.
  • Silicon: valence band full, gap 1.1 eV → semiconductor.
  • Magnesium: 3s band overlaps 3p band → metal despite even valence electrons.

Universal Facts

  • Band theory was developed by Bloch (1928), Wilson (1931), and Peierls using quantum mechanics.
  • The band gap of silicon (1.1 eV) corresponds to infrared wavelength (~1.1 μm) — Si is opaque to visible but transparent to IR.
  • Quantum dots (nanoscale clusters) have discrete levels instead of bands — size-tunable optical properties.
  • The Pauli exclusion principle is essential — without it, all electrons would collapse to the lowest state and no bands would form.

Confusion

  • Thinking bands are like atomic levels: Bands contain N closely spaced levels, not one level.
  • Believing valence electrons are the only ones in valence band: Core electrons also in bands but too tightly bound to conduct.
  • Confusing band gap with work function: Band gap is internal to material; work function is energy to remove electron from surface to vacuum.
  • Thinking all electrons in conduction band conduct: Only electrons near Fermi level participate significantly.
  • Believing bands exist in isolated atoms: Bands require interatomic overlap in solids.
