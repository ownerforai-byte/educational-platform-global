Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Three Classes, One Diagram

    Whether a material conducts copper-like, blocks diamond-like or sits between like silicon is decided entirely by its band picture: overlap, wide gap or narrow gap. This topic turns that classification into a comparison table you can reproduce under exam pressure.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024)

    2.1 The three classes by band structure

    • Metals (conductors): valence and conduction bands overlap, or the conduction band is partially filled. Electrons find empty states immediately above and drift freely under tiny fields. Examples: Cu, Ag, Al. Resistivity ~10⁻⁸ Ω·m.
    • Insulators: valence band completely full, conduction band empty, forbidden gap E_g > 3 eV (diamond ≈ 6 eV). Ordinary fields cannot supply enough energy; resistivity ~10¹²–10²⁰ Ω·m.
    • Semiconductors: narrow gap (Si 1.1 eV, Ge 0.7 eV). At 0 K they behave as insulators; at room temperature thermal energy promotes some electrons across, creating electron–hole pairs. Resistivity intermediate (~10⁻⁵–10² Ω·m) and falls steeply with temperature.

    2.2 Temperature behaviour (NEB 2024, 2m)

    Heating a semiconductor breaks more covalent bonds, creating extra electron–hole pairs — more carriers, higher conductivity. Heating a metal only agitates the lattice ions, which scatter the already-free electrons more — same carriers, more collisions, lower conductivity. Opposite temperature signatures are the quickest experimental test of the class.

    2.3 Worked reasoning

    Q (NEB-style): Why does a completely full band carry no current? For a net current, electrons must gain momentum in one direction — which requires empty states to move into. A full band has no empty states, so field-driven shifts cancel pairwise and the net current is zero. Only partly-empty bands conduct.

    Q (NEB-style): Classify carbon (diamond, E_g = 6 eV), silicon (1.1 eV) and copper (overlap). Diamond: insulator. Silicon: semiconductor. Copper: metal.

Summary

Band theory sorts solids into three classes. Metals (Cu, Ag): overlapping or partly-filled bands — abundant free electrons, ρ ~ 10⁻⁸ Ω·m. Insulators (diamond, E_g ≈ 6 eV): full valence band, empty conduction band, gap > 3 eV — no carriers, ρ up to 10²⁰ Ω·m. Semiconductors (Si 1.1 eV, Ge 0.7 eV): narrow gap — thermal energy creates electron–hole pairs, giving moderate conductivity that rises with temperature, while a metal's falls (NEB 2024, 2m). A full band carries no current (no empty states), which is why insulators block and semiconductors need excitation — NEB 2023 and 2024 both asked the full 5m differentiation.
