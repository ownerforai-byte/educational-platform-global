Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Engineering Conductivity Atom by Atom

    Pure silicon conducts feebly. Add one phosphorus atom per million silicon atoms and conductivity jumps a thousandfold. Doping — the deliberate addition of impurities — turns a passive crystal into the raw material of every diode, transistor and solar cell.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Intrinsic semiconductors (NEB 2022, 2024, 2m)

    An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure crystal (e.g. pure Si or Ge) with no added impurities. Thermal energy breaks covalent bonds, promoting electrons to the conduction band:

    • Electron: a negatively charged charge carrier promoted to the conduction band.
    • Hole: the positively charged vacancy left in the valence band, which drifts along the field as neighbouring electrons fill it — behaving as a mobile +e charge.
    • Carriers come in equal pairs: n = p = n_i. Conductivity rises steeply with temperature.

    2.2 Doping and extrinsic semiconductors (NEB 2024, 3m)

    Doping is adding trace impurities to boost conductivity enormously:

    • n-type: pentavalent impurity (P, As, Sb) donates an extra (fifth) electron loosely bound — electrons are the majority carriers, holes the minority. Donor level lies just below the conduction band.
    • p-type: trivalent impurity (B, Al, In) leaves one covalent bond unfilled, creating an extra hole — holes are the majority carriers, electrons the minority. Acceptor level lies just above the valence band.

    The crystal as a whole remains electrically neutral in both cases.

    2.3 Worked reasoning

    Q (NEB-style): Why does doping one atom in a million change conductivity so dramatically? The intrinsic carrier density n_i in Si at room temperature is ~10¹⁶ /m³, while the atomic density is ~10²⁸ /m³. Adding donors at 10²² /m³ multiplies the electron population a millionfold — conductivity, proportional to carrier density, rises accordingly.

    Q (NEB-style): In an n-type semiconductor, what happens to the hole concentration? The mass-action law n·p = n_i² holds: raising n by doping forces p below its intrinsic value — majority up, minority down.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topics 112–113: doping populates the conduction band without waiting for thermal excitation.
    • To p-n junctions: joining p-type and n-type creates the diode — the next step beyond this syllabus.
    • To drift (Topic 98): both electrons and holes drift under fields, contributing to current.

Summary

An intrinsic semiconductor is a pure crystal (Si, Ge) whose carriers come only from thermal bond-breaking: promoted electrons (negative carriers) and the holes they leave — positive vacancies that drift with the field (NEB 2022/2024, 2m); n = p = n_i. Doping adds trace impurities: n-type uses pentavalent donors (P, As) whose fifth electron becomes a conduction electron (electrons majority); p-type uses trivalent acceptors (B, Al) that create extra holes (holes majority) (NEB 2024, 3m). One atom per million multiplies conductivity a thousandfold, the mass-action law n·p = n_i² keeps the product fixed, and every doped crystal remains electrically neutral.
