Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — From Levels to Bands

    An isolated atom has sharp energy levels. Bring 10²³ atoms together and each level splits into a near-continuous band. Which bands are full, empty or partly filled decides whether a solid conducts, insulates or sits in between — the whole story of electronics.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024)

    2.1 How bands form

    In a crystal, neighbouring atoms' overlapping wavefunctions make each discrete atomic level split into N closely spaced levels — a band. Between bands lie forbidden gaps (no allowed states).

    • Valence band: the highest band filled by valence electrons at 0 K.
    • Conduction band: the next higher band; electrons here move freely and conduct.
    • Forbidden gap (E_g): the energy interval between them; electrons must acquire E_g to cross.

    2.2 Band theory of solids (NEB 2023/2024, 5m)

    In solids, atomic energy levels split into bands due to overlapping wavefunctions:

    • Metals: valence band overlaps the conduction band (no gap), or the conduction band is partially filled — electrons move freely. Example: Cu, Ag.
    • Insulators: a large forbidden gap (E_g > 3 eV) between a full valence band and an empty conduction band blocks electrons from reaching conduction states. Example: diamond (E_g ≈ 6 eV).
    • Semiconductors: a small gap (E_g ≈ 1 eV; Si 1.1 eV, Ge 0.7 eV) — thermal energy promotes some electrons across, leaving holes behind; conductivity is intermediate and strongly temperature-dependent.

    2.3 Worked reasoning

    Q (NEB-style): Why does the conductivity of a semiconductor increase with temperature while that of a metal decreases? Heating a semiconductor breaks more covalent bonds, creating extra electron–hole pairs — more carriers, higher conductivity. Heating a metal only agitates the lattice ions, which scatter the already-free electrons more — the same carriers, more collisions, lower conductivity.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 113: the gap size classifies metals, insulators and semiconductors.
    • To Topic 114: doping and intrinsic excitation both populate the conduction band.
    • To drift velocity (Topic 98): carriers in bands drift under fields just as free electrons do.

Summary

In a crystal, overlapping atomic orbitals split each atomic level into a near-continuous band; the highest filled one is the valence band, the next the conduction band, separated by the forbidden gap E_g. Metals (Cu, Ag) have overlapping or partly-filled bands — free conduction. Insulators (diamond, E_g ≈ 6 eV) have a wide gap (> 3 eV) blocking excitation. Semiconductors (Si 1.1 eV, Ge 0.7 eV) have a narrow gap — thermal energy creates electron–hole pairs. Hence heating raises a semiconductor's conductivity (more carriers) but lowers a metal's (more scattering) — NEB 2023 and 2024 both asked the 5m band-theory explanation.
