Notes

  • When many atoms form a solid, interactions split atomic energy levels into closely spaced allowed bands separated by forbidden energy gaps. Electrons cannot occupy energies in an ideal band gap.
  • The valence band contains bonding electrons; the conduction band contains mobile carriers. The gap Eg=Ec−Ev and the band filling help explain conductivity.
  • Metals have partially filled bands or overlapping bands. Insulators have a large gap and very few room-temperature carriers. Semiconductors have a smaller gap, so thermal or optical excitation can promote electrons into the conduction band.
  • In an intrinsic semiconductor, promoting an electron leaves a hole in the valence band. Both the electron and the hole can carry current; heating can therefore increase carrier concentration.
  • A photon can excite an electron across the gap if hν≥Eg. This underlies photoconductivity and semiconductor photodetectors.
  • In a solid the atomic levels spread into bands. A conductor has a partly filled band, or overlapping bands, so electrons can take a little extra energy and move. An insulator has a full valence band and a large gap. A semiconductor has a smaller gap, so heat or light can lift a few electrons across it.

Confusion

  • A band gap is a forbidden energy range, not a physical gap in the material.
  • Band gap alone does not determine conductivity without carrier concentration and mobility.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Summary

Band theory explains conductivity through allowed energy bands, gaps and carrier availability. Thermal and optical excitation can create mobile electron–hole pairs.

Exercises

1.

Silicon's gap is about 1.1 eV. A photon of wavelength 800 nm has energy 1240/800 = 1.55 eV. Can it lift an electron across the gap?

Steps
  • The gap is 1.1 eV.
  • The photon energy is 1.55 eV.
  • 1.55 is greater than 1.1, so the photon can be absorbed and create a pair.
  • A photon of energy 0.9 eV could not.
  • Temperature can also free a few electrons without a photon.
Answer

Yes. 1.55 eV is above the 1.1 eV gap.
