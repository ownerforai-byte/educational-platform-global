Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Counting Field Lines Through a Surface

    Gauss's law links the electric flux through any closed surface to the charge it encloses. With symmetry — a sphere, a line, a sheet — it turns hard integrations into one-line answers. NEB 2023 and 2024 both carried it as a 5-mark question.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024)

    2.1 Electric flux

    Electric flux through an area A in a uniform field E: Φ=EAcosθ=E⋅Aunit: N m2C−1 θ is the angle between E and the area's normal. Flux measures "how many field lines pierce" the surface.

    2.2 Gauss's law (NEB 2023, 2024, 5m)

    Statement: the total electric flux through any closed surface equals ε0​1​ times the net charge enclosed: ∮E⋅dA=ε0​qenclosed​​

    • The Gaussian surface is imaginary and chosen to exploit symmetry.
    • The law holds for any closed surface; only flux leaving the surface counts — charges outside contribute zero net flux.
    • Gauss's law is equivalent to Coulomb's law plus the inverse-square geometry.

    2.3 Application: infinite plane sheet of charge (NEB 2023, 2024)

    For a sheet of surface charge density σ, take a cylindrical Gaussian pillbox piercing the sheet:

    • Flux leaves through both flat faces: Φ=EA+EA=2EA
    • Enclosed charge: q=σA

    2EA=ε0​σA​⇒E=2ε0​σ​​ — uniform, independent of distance from the sheet, pointing away on both sides.

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): A spherical Gaussian surface of radius 0.2 m encloses 2 μC. Find the flux through it. Φ=ε0​q​=8.85×10−122×10−6​≈2.26×105 N m2C−1

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 87: flux counts field lines through a surface.
    • To Topic 89: the sphere, line and sheet results are Gauss's law's standard applications.
    • To conductors: zero field inside a conductor follows immediately from Gauss's law.

Summary

Electric flux through area A in field E is Φ=EAcosθ (N m²C⁻¹) — the field-line count through a surface. Gauss's law states that the total flux through any closed surface equals qenclosed​/ε0​ (NEB 2023, 2024, 5m). Applied to an infinite plane sheet of charge with a cylindrical pillbox: flux 2EA = enclosed σA/ε0​, giving the uniform field E=σ/2ε0​, independent of distance. The law works only cleanly when symmetry makes E constant over the Gaussian surface — spheres, lines, sheets — and it immediately explains the absence of field inside conductors.

Special Notes

  • If a closed surface encloses an electric dipole (charges +q and -q), the net flux through the surface is identically zero because Q_enc = +q - q = 0.
  • For an area vector of a closed surface, dA points outward by standard mathematical convention, so exiting flux is positive and entering flux is negative.

Important Statements

  • "The total electric flux through any closed surface is equal to 1/ε₀ times the net charge enclosed by the surface."

Mcs

1.

If an electric dipole of moment p is enclosed within a closed spherical surface of radius R, the total electric flux emerging from the surface is:

Options
  • p / ε₀
  • p / (4πε₀R²)
  • Zero
  • 2p / ε₀
Answer

C

Explanation

An electric dipole consists of equal and opposite charges (+q and -q). The net enclosed charge is Q_enc = +q - q = 0. By Gauss's Law, total flux Φ = Q_enc / ε₀ = 0.

2.

A point charge q is placed at the center of an imaginary sphere of radius R. If the radius of the sphere is doubled, the total electric flux passing through the sphere will:

Options
  • be halved
  • be doubled
  • be quadrupled
  • remain unchanged
Answer

D

Explanation

According to Gauss's Law, Φ = Q_enc / ε₀, which depends only on the enclosed charge, not on the surface radius or geometry.

3.

The SI unit of electric flux is:

Options
  • N / C
  • N·m² / C
  • N·m / C
  • C / (N·m²)
Answer

B

Explanation

Flux is E · A, so units are (N/C) · m² = N·m²/C, which is also equivalent to Volt-meters (V·m).
