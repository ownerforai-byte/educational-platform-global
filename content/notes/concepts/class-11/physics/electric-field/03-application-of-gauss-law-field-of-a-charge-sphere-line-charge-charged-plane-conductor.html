Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Three Results Every Physicist Knows

    Gauss's law plus symmetry delivers the three canonical fields: a charged sphere (acts like a point outside, zero inside), an infinite line (falls as 1/r), and a charged conducting plane (σ/ε₀, constant). These are the 5-mark derivations NEB keeps returning to.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Uniformly charged spherical shell (NEB 2024, 5m)

    Shell of radius R carrying charge Q:

    • Outside (r > R): spherical Gaussian surface encloses all Q:

    E⋅4πr2=ε0​Q​⇒E=4πε0​r2Q​ — the shell acts as if all charge sat at its centre.

    • Inside (r < R): the Gaussian surface encloses no charge → E=0 everywhere inside.
    • On the surface: E = Q/4πε₀R² (maximum).

    2.2 Infinite line charge

    Linear density λ, cylindrical Gaussian surface of radius r, length l: E⋅2πrl=ε0​λl​⇒E=2πε0​rλ​ — falls as 1/r, radial direction.

    2.3 Charged conducting plane (conductor surface)

    For a conductor's surface with charge density σ, the field just outside: E=ε0​σ​ — twice the infinite-sheet value, because all flux leaves through the outer face only (no field inside the conductor).

    2.4 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): A shell of radius 10 cm carries 4 μC. Find E at r = 20 cm and at the centre.

    • Outside: E=0.049×109×4×10−6​=9×105 N/C
    • Inside: E = 0.

    Q (NEB-style): A long wire has λ = 2 μC/m. Find E at 5 cm. E=2πε0​rλ​=2π×8.85×10−12×0.052×10−6​≈7.2×105 N/C.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 88: each result is the flux identity ∮E·dA = q/ε₀ with a symmetry-matched surface.
    • To conductors: E = 0 inside any conductor in equilibrium — the basis of electrostatic shielding.
    • To Topic 87: outside a sphere, the field matches a point charge — Coulomb's law recovered.

Specific Conditions

  • Symmetry Required: Gauss's law is always true but practically useful only for spherical, cylindrical, or planar symmetry where E is constant over the Gaussian surface.
  • Conductor vs Insulator: For conducting sphere, charge resides on surface → E = 0 inside. For insulating sphere with uniform volume charge, E grows linearly from center.
  • Infinite Approximation: Line charge formula E=λ/(2πε0​r) assumes infinite length — valid near middle of long wire, not near ends.

Formulas

    • Shell: E = Q/4πε₀r² (r > R); E = 0 (r < R)
    • Line: E = λ/2πε₀r
    • Conductor surface: E = σ/ε₀

Key Points

  • Shell: E = Q/4πε₀r² outside, 0 inside. Line: λ/2πε₀r. Conductor surface: σ/ε₀.

Important Statements

  • "The electric field inside a charged conducting shell is zero at every point."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: shell inside/outside 2024 5m; zero field inside conductor 2024 2m.

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Write the flux identity first, then solve for E — marks for the setup."

Exam Notes

  • State directions (radial outward for Q > 0) and the r-dependence in words.

Examples

  • Faraday cage — E = 0 inside a conductor shields whatever is inside.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A shell of radius 5 cm carries 2 μC. Find E at 10 cm from the centre. — Outside: E = 9×10⁹ × 2×10⁻⁶/0.01 = 1.8×10⁶ N/C, radial outward.

Practice

  • How does a car protect occupants from lightning?

Universal Facts

  • Outside a uniformly charged shell the field is exactly that of a point charge at the centre — the electrostatic analogue of Newton's shell theorem.

Confusion

  • Sheet vs conductor: infinite sheet gives σ/2ε₀ (both sides); a conducting surface gives σ/ε₀ (one side only).
  • Inside a shell E = 0 — the shell theorem's most surprising half.

Mcs

1.

Electric field inside a charged conducting spherical shell is:

Options
  • kQ/r²
  • Zero
  • kQ/R²
  • Depends on position
Answer

B

Explanation

Charge resides on surface; by Gauss's law with Gaussian surface inside, Q_enc = 0, so E = 0.

2.

Electric field due to infinite line charge at distance r is proportional to:

Options
  • 1/r
  • 1/r²
  • 1/r³
  • r
Answer

A

Explanation

Cylindrical symmetry gives E = λ/(2πε₀r) — inverse first power.

3.

Electric field near infinite plane sheet with charge density σ is:

Options
  • σ/ε₀
  • σ/(2ε₀)
  • 2σ/ε₀
  • Zero
Answer

B

Explanation

Pillbox Gaussian surface gives E = σ/(2ε₀) on each side of the sheet.

4.

For a uniformly charged solid insulating sphere, field inside at distance r from center:

Options
  • Zero
  • kQ/r²
  • kQr/R³
  • kQ/R²
Answer

C

Explanation

Inside uniform sphere, Q_enc ∝ r³ and area ∝ r², so E ∝ r: E = kQr/R³.

5.

Gauss's law is most useful when charge distribution has:

Options
  • No symmetry
  • Spherical/cylindrical/planar symmetry
  • Only spherical symmetry
  • Only planar symmetry
Answer

B

Explanation

High symmetry lets us assume E is constant over Gaussian surface and pull it outside the integral.

Summary

Gauss's law's standard applications: a uniformly charged spherical shell (Q, radius R) gives E=Q/4πε0​r2 outside — as if the charge were a point at the centre — and E = 0 everywhere inside (NEB 2024, 5m); free electrons in a conductor in equilibrium move until the internal field vanishes, which is why conductors shield their interiors and charge resides on the outer surface (NEB 2024, 2m). An infinite line charge (λ) gives E=λ/2πε0​r (1/r falloff), and a charged conducting plane gives E=σ/ε0​ just outside — double the infinite-sheet value because flux leaves through one face only. Symmetry plus one flux identity replaces pages of integration.
