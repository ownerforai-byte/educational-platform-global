Notes

  • Electric Field Definition: The electric field E at a point is the force F experienced per unit positive test charge q0​ placed at that point: E=F/q0​. It is a vector quantity with SI unit N/C (equivalently V/m).
  • Field of a Point Charge: By Coulomb's law, the field due to a point charge q at distance r is E=4πε0​1​r2q​r^=kr2q​r^, directed radially outward for positive q and inward for negative q. k=8.99×109 N⋅m2/C2.
  • Field Lines: Imaginary curves whose tangent gives field direction; density of lines represents field strength. Lines start on positive charges, end on negative charges, never cross, never form closed loops in electrostatics.
  • Superposition Principle: The net field from multiple charges is the vector sum of individual fields: Enet​=∑i​Ei​. This follows from linearity of Coulomb's law.
  • Uniform Field: Between two parallel oppositely charged plates, the field is uniform: E=V/d or E=σ/ε0​ (for infinite plates). A charge experiences constant force everywhere between plates.
  • Dipole Field: For an electric dipole (+q,−q separated by 2a), axial field: Eaxial​=4πε0​1​(r2−a2)22pr​≈r32kp​ (for r≫a). Equatorial field: Eeq​=4πε0​1​(r2+a2)3/2p​≈r3kp​. Dipole moment p=q(2a).
  • Field Inside Conductor: At electrostatic equilibrium, E=0 inside a conductor. All excess charge resides on the surface. The surface is an equipotential.

Specific Conditions

  • Test Charge Limit: The test charge q0​ must be infinitesimally small so it does not disturb the source charge distribution being measured.
  • Electrostatics Only: Field lines never form closed loops only in electrostatics. With changing magnetic fields (Faraday's law), induced electric fields form closed loops.
  • Point Charge Approximation: The formula E=kq/r2 assumes a point charge or spherically symmetric distribution measured outside the sphere.

Key Points

  • Electric field E=F/q0​ — force per unit positive test charge.
  • Point charge field: E=kq/r2, radially outward (+) or inward (-).
  • Field lines: start (+), end (-), never cross, density = strength.
  • Superposition: net field is vector sum of individual fields.
  • Uniform field between parallel plates: E=V/d.
  • Inside conductor at equilibrium: E=0.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Electric field at a point is force per unit positive test charge: E=F/q0​.
  • Statement 2: Field due to point charge q at distance r: E=4πε0​1​r2q​.
  • Statement 3: Field lines start on positive charges and end on negative charges; they never cross.
  • Statement 4: Superposition principle — net field is vector sum of fields from individual charges.
  • Statement 5: Electric field inside a conductor at electrostatic equilibrium is zero.

Important Notes

  • The test charge must be small enough not to alter the source charge distribution.
  • Field direction = force direction on positive test charge; opposite for negative charge.
  • Electric field is a vector — always add fields vectorially, not by magnitudes.
  • Near a point charge, field varies as 1/r2 — strong close to charge, weak far away.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Field due to +q points away; due to -q points toward.
  • Superposition: add vectors, draw diagram first, resolve components.
  • Between parallel plates: field is uniform (E=V/d) except near edges.
  • Inside conductor: E = 0 — all charge on surface.
  • Dipole field falls as 1/r3 (faster than point charge's 1/r2).

Examples

  • Two charges +3μC and −3μC are 10 cm apart. Find field at midpoint. Solution: Both fields point same way (toward - charge). E=2×(9×109)(3×10−6)/(0.05)2=2.16×107 N/C.
  • A +5μC charge creates field at 2 m distance. E=(9×109)(5×10−6)/4=11250 N/C, radially outward.
  • Between plates 2 cm apart with 100 V potential difference: E=V/d=100/0.02=5000 V/m, uniform.
  • Electron in field 1000 N/C: Force F=eE=(1.6×10−19)(1000)=1.6×10−16 N, opposite to field direction.

Universal Facts

  • The electric field concept was introduced by Michael Faraday to explain action-at-a-distance without instantaneous action.
  • Electric field lines are a visualization tool — they are not physical entities but help understand field structure.
  • The field inside a conductor being zero is why cars and airplanes are safe during lightning strikes (Faraday cage effect).
  • Electric field can exist in vacuum — it does not require a medium, unlike sound waves.
  • The superposition principle is exact for electric fields because Coulomb's law is linear.

Confusion

  • Confusing field direction for positive vs negative test charge: Field points in force direction on + charge, opposite on - charge.
  • Thinking field lines are real physical lines: They are imaginary visualization tools; the field exists continuously in space.
  • Assuming field strength is constant near a point charge: E∝1/r2 — it decreases rapidly with distance.
  • Adding field magnitudes instead of vectors: Electric field is a vector; use vector addition for multiple charges.
  • Believing field exists inside a charged conductor: At electrostatic equilibrium, E = 0 inside conductor material.

Summary

The electric field E=F/q0​ describes force per unit positive test charge. A point charge produces E=kq/r2 radially. Field lines visualize field (start +, end -, never cross). Superposition gives net field from multiple charges. Field is uniform between parallel plates (E=V/d) and zero inside a conductor at equilibrium.
