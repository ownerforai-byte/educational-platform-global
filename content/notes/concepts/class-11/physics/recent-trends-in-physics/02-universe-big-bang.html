Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Expanding Universe

    Every distant galaxy recedes, and the farther it is, the faster it flees — Hubble's law. Run the film backwards and everything converges ~13.8 billion years ago: the Big Bang, whose echo still fills the sky as the cosmic microwave background.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Hubble's law

    Recession speed is proportional to distance: v=H0​dH0​≈70 km s−1/Mpc

    • Measured from galactic redshift: light stretched by expansion shifts to longer wavelengths; z = Δλ/λ ≈ v/c for small speeds.
    • Hubble's law does not mean we sit at the centre — every observer sees the same recession pattern: space itself expands.

    2.2 The Big Bang

    The universe began ~13.8 billion years ago from an extremely hot, dense state and has been expanding and cooling ever since:

    • 10⁻⁶ s: quarks and leptons; pairs annihilate, leaving a slight matter excess.
    • 3 min: nuclei form (mostly H and He) — nucleosynthesis.
    • 380,000 yr: atoms form; the universe turns transparent — the released radiation cools into today's cosmic microwave background (CMB) at 2.7 K.
    • Galaxies form within the first billion years; expansion continues and, observations say, accelerates (dark energy).

    2.3 Evidence for the Big Bang

    1. Hubble's recession law — the expanding universe.
    2. CMB radiation — the cooled primordial glow, uniform to ~1 part in 10⁵.
    3. Light-element abundances — ~75% H, ~25% He, exactly as nucleosynthesis predicts.

    2.4 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): A galaxy 100 Mpc away recedes at what speed? v=H0​d=70×100=7000 km/s — its light is redshifted by z ≈ v/c ≈ 0.023.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 115: the Big Bang's first second produced the particle families.
    • To Topic 117: dark matter and dark energy dominate the universe's fate.
    • To Doppler effect (Unit 4): redshift is the same physics applied to light.

    4.1 Verified PYQs from the solved-paper bank

    4.2 Likely question forms

    2. Describe the Big Bang and its evidence (3m).
    3. Recession-speed numerical (2m).

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Interpreting Hubble's law as an explosion into space rather than an expansion of space.
    2. Forgetting the CMB as evidence.
    3. Mixing up Mpc and km in Hubble numericals.

Confusion

  • The Big Bang is an expansion OF space, not an explosion INTO space — there is no centre.
  • The CMB is the Big Bang's afterglow, cooled to 2.7 K over 13.8 billion years.

Universal Facts

  • Expansion is accelerating — attributed to dark energy, ~70% of the universe's content.

Summary

Hubble's law, v = H₀d with H₀ ≈ 70 km s⁻¹/Mpc, makes every galaxy recede with speed proportional to distance — evidence that space itself expands (measured via redshift, z ≈ v/c). Running expansion backwards gives the Big Bang ~13.8 billion years ago: quarks and leptons in the first microsecond, nucleosynthesis of H and He by 3 minutes, and at 380,000 years the release of radiation that cools into today's cosmic microwave background at 2.7 K. Three pillars support the model: Hubble's law, the CMB, and the ~75% H / ~25% He primordial abundances; current observations show the expansion accelerating.

Exercises

1.

Take H = 70 km/s per megaparsec. Estimate the recession speed of a galaxy 10 megaparsecs away.

Steps
  • v ≈ H d.
  • d = 10 Mpc.
  • v = 70 × 10 = 700 km/s.
  • A more distant galaxy recedes faster.
  • This is the expansion of space, not a motion through a fixed background from an explosion in one place.
Answer

700 km/s.
