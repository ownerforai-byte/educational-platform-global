Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Invisible 95%

    Everything we see — stars, gas, dust, us — is only ~5% of the universe. Dark matter holds galaxies together without shining; dark energy pushes the expansion faster; black holes trap light itself; and gravitational waves let us hear spacetime ring. Modern physics is mostly a search for the missing.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Dark matter

    Galaxy rotation curves refuse to fall off with distance as visible mass allows — the outer stars orbit as fast as the inner ones. An unseen mass component, dark matter, must supply the extra gravity:

    • ~5× more mass than ordinary (baryonic) matter — about 27% of the universe.
    • Interacts gravitationally but not electromagnetically: invisible, yet its lensing and rotation effects are measured.
    • Candidates: WIMPs, axions; detected so far only indirectly.

    2.2 Dark energy

    Distant supernovae (1998) show the expansion is accelerating. Dark energy — a uniform negative-pressure component, ~68% of the universe — drives that acceleration. Together with dark matter, the unseen is ~95% of everything.

    2.3 Black holes

    When a massive star's core collapses beyond the point of no return, gravity wins completely: a black hole, where escape velocity exceeds c. Key ideas:

    • Event horizon (Schwarzschild radius): Rs​=c22GM​ — for the Sun, ~3 km.
    • Nothing, not even light, escapes from inside the horizon; time dilation becomes extreme near it.
    • Evidence: stellar orbits around Sagittarius A, X-rays from accretion discs, the Event Horizon Telescope image of M87 (2019).

    2.4 Gravitational waves

    Accelerating masses ripple spacetime itself — gravitational waves travelling at c, squeezing and stretching space transversely. LIGO's 2015 detection of two merging black holes (~30 solar masses each, ~1.3 billion light-years away) confirmed a 1916 prediction, winning the 2017 Nobel Prize. The waveform's "chirp" encodes the masses and the merger.

    2.5 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): Find the Schwarzschild radius of a 10-solar-mass black hole (M☉ = 2×10³⁰ kg). Rs​=c22GM​=9×10162×6.67×10−11×2×1031​≈3×104 m=30 km

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 116: dark energy accelerates the expansion Hubble's law measures; dark matter shaped galaxy formation after the Big Bang.
    • To gravitation (Unit 8): black holes are the extreme limit of Newtonian and Einsteinian gravity.
    • To Topic 115: dark-matter candidates are new particles beyond the Standard Model.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Confusing dark matter (gravity, clumps) with dark energy (uniform, accelerates).
    2. Omitting the 2GM/c² formula in black-hole numericals.
    3. Saying gravitational waves are electromagnetic — they are spacetime ripples.

  • A black hole's horizon for the Sun is only 3 km — gravity compressed to a city's size.

Summary

Dark matter (~27% of the universe) supplies the unseen gravity that keeps galaxy rotation curves flat and bends light via lensing — it interacts gravitationally but not electromagnetically. Dark energy (~68%) is the uniform component making the expansion accelerate (1998 supernova surveys). Together the unseen is ~95% of everything. A black hole forms when collapse passes the event horizon Rs​=2GM/c2 (≈ 3 km per solar mass) — inside, even light cannot escape; evidence includes stellar orbits around Sagittarius A, X-ray binaries and the 2019 EHT image of M87. Gravitational waves — spacetime ripples from accelerating masses travelling at c — were first detected by LIGO in 2015 from a merger of two ~30-solar-mass black holes, confirming Einstein's 1916 prediction.

Exercises

1.

An outer star orbits as if the galaxy's mass inside its orbit is five times the mass of the visible stars inside that orbit. What does that imply?

Steps
  • Visible mass alone would give a slower orbit.
  • The observed speed needs about five times that mass.
  • Most of the mass inside the orbit does not shine.
  • That unseen mass is what is meant by dark matter in this argument.
  • The argument is dynamical. It does not photograph the dark matter.
Answer

About four fifths of the interior mass is unseen.
