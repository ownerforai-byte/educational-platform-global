Enriched Content

Notes
  • Law of Conservation of Linear Momentum: In an isolated system (no external net force), the total linear momentum remains constant: ∑p​i​=∑p​f​. This is a fundamental law derived from the homogeneity of space (Noether's theorem). It is a direct consequence of the fact that the laws of physics are the same at all points in space.
  • Isolated System: A system where the net external force is zero. Internal forces (like collision forces) do not change the total momentum because they act in equal and opposite pairs (Newton's 3rd law). The system can be as small as two colliding particles or as large as the entire universe.
  • Coefficient of Restitution (e): A measure of elasticity in collisions: e=(v2​−v1​)/(u1​−u2​). e=1 (perfectly elastic), 0<e<1 (inelastic), e=0 (perfectly inelastic). This coefficient depends on the materials of the colliding objects and their surface properties.
  • Collisions: Events where momentum is conserved. Elastic collisions also conserve kinetic energy; inelastic collisions do not (energy is lost to heat, sound, deformation).
  • Explosions: A reverse collision where an object breaks apart. Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy increases (internal energy is converted to motion).
  • Recoil: A classic application. A gun firing a bullet: Mg​Vg​+mb​vb​=0 (initially at rest).
  • NEB exam focus: State the law; apply it to 1D and 2D collisions; distinguish between elastic and inelastic collisions; solve recoil and explosion problems.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'Momentum is conserved only in elastic collisions.' ✅ Momentum is conserved in ALL collisions (elastic or inelastic) as long as the system is isolated.
  • ❌ 'Kinetic energy is always conserved in collisions.' ✅ Only in perfectly elastic collisions. In inelastic collisions, KE is lost.
  • ❌ 'Internal forces change the total momentum.' ✅ Internal forces (like collision forces) cancel out in pairs (Newton's 3rd law) and do not change the total momentum of the system.
  • ❌ 'Conservation of momentum applies to individual objects.' ✅ It applies to the SYSTEM of objects.
Practice
  • A 2 kg ball at 5 m/s hits a 3 kg ball at rest. If they stick together, find the final velocity. — v=2 m/s.
  • A 5 kg rifle fires a 20 g bullet at 400 m/s. Find the recoil velocity. — v=1.6 m/s.
  • A 1000 kg car at 10 m/s hits a 2000 kg truck at rest. If they stick, find the final speed. — v=3.33 m/s.
Universal Facts
  • The law of conservation of momentum is one of the fundamental laws of physics, derived from the homogeneity of space (Noether's theorem).
  • It holds true from subatomic particle collisions in accelerators to the motion of galaxies.
  • Rocket propulsion is a direct application: expelling mass backward to gain forward momentum. The thrust force is F=ve​(dm/dt). This is the Tsiolkovsky rocket equation principle.
  • In a system of particles, the center of mass moves with constant velocity if the net external force is zero, even if the particles themselves are colliding and changing velocities. The total momentum of the system is P=MVcm​, where M is the total mass and Vcm​ is the velocity of the center of mass.
  • Center of Mass (CM) Frame: In the CM frame, the total momentum is always zero, which simplifies the analysis of complex collisions. In this frame, the collision is seen as a symmetric event where the particles approach and recede from the CM with equal and opposite momenta.
  • Collisions in 2D: Momentum is conserved in both x and y directions independently: ∑pix​=∑pfx​ and ∑piy​=∑pfy​. This is essential for analyzing oblique collisions.
Examples
  • Example 1 (1D collision): m1​u1​+m2​u2​=(m1​+m2​)v for perfectly inelastic.
  • Example 2 (Explosion): A shell at rest breaks into two pieces m1​,m2​ with velocities v1​,v2​ such that m1​v1​+m2​v2​=0.

Specific Conditions
  • Isolated System Requirement: Momentum conservation (∑p​i​=∑p​f​) holds strictly only when the net external force on the system is zero. External forces like gravity, friction, or applied pushes must be absent or balanced.
  • Collisions vs. Continuous Interaction: In a collision, the interaction is brief and impulsive; internal forces dominate. In continuous interactions (e.g., rocket propulsion), momentum is conserved at each instant, but mass changes must be tracked using the variable-mass form F=dp/dt.
  • Kinetic Energy Distinction: Momentum is conserved in all isolated collisions (elastic or inelastic). Kinetic energy is conserved only in perfectly elastic collisions. In inelastic collisions, some KE is converted to heat, sound, or deformation energy.

  • Type 2 — alternate treatment: this version derives the conservation law from Newton's third law and the impulse picture, then applies it to recoil, explosions and two-body systems — the application chain NEB numericals follow.
  • The law: if the net EXTERNAL force on a system is zero, its total linear momentum stays constant: m1​u1​+m2​u2​=m1​v1​+m2​v2​. Internal forces (the pair between the two bodies) can change individual momenta but never the total.
  • Why it holds (third-law derivation): during contact, body 1 pushes body 2 with F12​ and body 2 pushes back F21​=−F12​ for the SAME time Δt. Impulses: Δp​1​=F21​Δt and Δp​2​=F12​Δt; adding gives Δ(p​1​+p​2​)=(F21​+F12​)Δt=0 — the total momentum cannot change.
  • When it applies: always for INTERNAL interactions; for the whole system even during explosions and collisions (internal forces are huge compared to external ones over the short interaction). Gravity/friction are external — momentum is conserved only along directions with zero net external force.
  • Recoil (from rest): initial momentum zero → MV+mv=0, so V=−Mm​v. Cannon–ball, rifle–bullet, rocket–exhaust, boat–passenger: the lighter body carries proportionally more speed.
  • Explosions and separations: a body of mass (m1​+m2​) at rest splits into pieces moving in opposite directions: m1​v1​=m2​v2​. Kinetic energy is NOT conserved here (chemical energy converts to motion) — momentum alone is the reliable bookkeeper.
  • Worked example: a 60 kg skater at rest throws a 3 kg ball at 8 m/s. Recoil: V=603×8​=0.4 m/s backward. If thrown at 30° above horizontal, only the horizontal component (8cos30°) enters the horizontal recoil balance.
  • Collision bookkeeping: in ANY collision (elastic or not) total momentum is conserved: m1​u1​+m2​u2​=m1​v1​+m2​v2​ (1-D). Two unknowns need a second equation — kinetic energy (elastic) or the coefficient of restitution e=u1​−u2​v2​−v1​​.
  • NEB exam focus: state the law with its condition (no net external force); derive it from the third law; solve recoil and explosion numericals; explain rocket propulsion and the gun recoil by momentum conservation.

Important Statements

  • The total linear momentum of an isolated system (zero net external force) remains constant in both magnitude and direction.
  • Newton's third law implies conservation of momentum: every action produces an equal and opposite reaction, so internal forces cannot change total momentum.
  • In a system of interacting bodies, the sum of individual momentum changes is zero: Δp₁ + Δp₂ + ... + Δpₙ = 0.
  • Momentum is conserved component-wise: if ΣF_x = 0, then Σp_x is constant even if forces act in other directions.
  • The law applies universally — from subatomic particles to galaxies — as long as the system is properly defined and isolated.

Important Notes

  • Note 1: Isolated system is key.
  • Note 2: Elastic vs Inelastic distinction.
  • Always define your system first — conservation applies to the chosen system, not to individual bodies within it.
  • Internal forces (collisions, explosions, attractions) can redistribute momentum among parts but never change the total.
  • External forces (gravity, friction, applied pushes) change the total momentum — account for them before applying conservation.
  • The law is derived from Newton's third law and is more fundamental than Newton's laws themselves (it holds in quantum mechanics and relativity too).
  • In NEB exams, the derivation from the third law is a standard 3-4 mark question — memorize the exact steps.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: If objects stick, use m1​u1​+m2​u2​=(m1​+m2​)v.
  • Trick 2: Recoil: V=−mv/M.
  • Trick 3: Explosion: m1​v1​=−m2​v2​.
  • Recoil shortcut: system starts at rest → m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ → speeds are inversely proportional to masses.
  • Sign convention: pick +x direction first; velocities in the opposite direction carry a negative sign — this is the most common error.
  • Explosion check: stationary object splits → fragments MUST move in opposite directions (total p was zero, stays zero).
  • Component trick: in 2-D, apply Σp_x = const and Σp_y = const separately — two equations for two unknowns.
  • Perfectly inelastic: bodies stick together → one final velocity, maximum KE loss, but momentum still conserved.
