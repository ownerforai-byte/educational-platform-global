Moment, Torque & Conditions of Equilibrium

Class 11PhysicsDynamics

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Why Handles Are Long and Seesaws Balance

    Force alone does not describe turning: the same 20 N opens a door easily at the handle and barely at the hinge. The turning effect is the moment (torque), and its balance — the principle of moments — explains seesaws, levers, beam balances and every statics problem.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    TermDefinitionFormulaUnit
    Moment of force (torque)Turning effect = force × perpendicular distance from the axis to the line of actionτ=F×d (vector: τ=r×F)N·m
    Moment arm (lever arm)Perpendicular distance from the pivot to the force's line of actiondm
    CoupleTwo equal, opposite, parallel forces whose lines of action differτ=F× separationN·m
    Translational equilibriumNet force zero: ∑F=0 (no acceleration of centre of mass)——
    Rotational equilibriumNet torque zero: ∑τ=0 (no angular acceleration)——
    Complete (static) equilibriumBoth conditions hold——

    Principle of moments: for a body in rotational equilibrium about a pivot, ∑clockwise moments=∑anticlockwise moments

    Sign convention: forces tending to rotate anticlockwise are positive (or vice versa — be consistent).

    Worked numerical: A uniform metre rule pivoted at its centre (50 cm) has a 2 N weight hung at 20 cm. Where must a 3 N weight hang to balance? Anticlockwise moment: 2×(50−20)=60 N·cm. Clockwise: 3×(x−50)=60→x=70 cm. The 3 N weight hangs at the 70 cm mark.

    Worked numerical (non-uniform support): A uniform rod of weight 10 N and length 2 m is carried by two people at its ends. Find the force each bears. By symmetry, each bears 5 N. If one person holds at 0.5 m from one end: taking moments about the far end — F1​×2=10×1→F1​=5 N, and F2​=5 N. For asymmetric holds, both equilibrium conditions (∑F=0, ∑τ=0) are needed.

  • 3. Why the Position of Force Matters

    Torque depends on the perpendicular distance, not the actual distance: a force applied at an angle contributes Fdsinθ. This is why:

    • Door handles sit far from hinges (maximum moment arm).
    • Spanners are long; cheater bars extend them.
    • A force along the line through the pivot (d = 0) produces no torque.

    Couple: equal, opposite, parallel forces (like turning a steering wheel) produce pure rotation — zero net force, non-zero torque independent of the pivot chosen.

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 13: equilibrium conditions are Newton's first and second laws specialized to zero acceleration (force and torque versions).
    • To Circular Motion: torque causes angular acceleration — τ=Iα (Class 12 extension).
    • To Gravitation: centre of gravity is where the total weight's moment acts; centre of mass and centre of gravity coincide in uniform g.
    • To real life: beam balances, wheelbarrows, bottle openers and crane loads are all principle-of-moments problems.

  • 5. Exam Orientation

    5.1 Previous year questions

    No moment/torque question exists in the authored bank. Questions below are NEB-style practice, clearly labelled.

    5.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Define moment of a force and state the principle of moments. Answer: The moment of a force about an axis is the product of the force and the perpendicular distance from the axis to its line of action: τ=Fd (unit N·m) — the turning effect. The principle of moments states that for a body in rotational equilibrium about a pivot, the sum of clockwise moments equals the sum of anticlockwise moments.

    Q2 (3m) — A uniform metre rule pivoted at 50 cm balances a 4 N weight at the 20 cm mark with an unknown weight W at the 80 cm mark. Find W. Anticlockwise: 4×30=120 N·cm; clockwise: W×30. Balance: W=4 N.

    Q3 (2m) — What is a couple? Answer: A pair of equal, opposite, parallel forces whose lines of action do not coincide; it produces pure rotation (no translation), with torque F× separation, independent of the pivot.

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Using the actual distance instead of the perpendicular distance (moment arm).
    2. Ignoring the rule's own weight when it is not pivoted at its centre of gravity.
    3. Mixing clockwise and anticlockwise signs inconsistently.
    4. Confusing N·m (torque) with joules (N·m = J for work) — same dimensions, different quantities.
    5. Forgetting the second equilibrium condition (∑F=0) when asked for support reactions.

Examples

  • Metre rule balance — 2 N at 20 cm vs 3 N at 70 cm about the 50 cm pivot.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A uniform 200 N ladder of 5 m rests against a smooth wall at 60° to the ground. Find the wall's reaction. — Taking moments about the foot: N_wall × 5 sin 60° = 200 × 2.5 cos 60° → N_wall = (500 × 0.5)/(5 × 0.866) = 57.7 N; the ground supplies the friction balancing it.

Formulas

    • τ = F × d (d = perpendicular distance); vector τ⃗ = r⃗ × F⃗
    • Equilibrium: ΣF⃗ = 0 and Στ⃗ = 0
    • Couple torque: F × separation

Key Points

  • Torque τ = F × d (perpendicular distance); unit N·m.
  • Equilibrium: ΣF = 0 (translational) and Στ = 0 (rotational).
  • Principle of moments: clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments.
  • Couple: equal/opposite/parallel forces → pure rotation, pivot-independent torque.

Summary

The turning effect of a force is its moment (torque): τ = F × d, with d the perpendicular distance from the axis to the force's line of action — the reason door handles sit far from hinges and a force through the pivot turns nothing. A body is in translational equilibrium when ∑F=0 and in rotational equilibrium when ∑τ=0; both together give static equilibrium, expressed as the principle of moments: clockwise moments equal anticlockwise moments — 2 N at 20 cm of a centre-pivoted metre rule balances 3 N at 70 cm. A couple — equal, opposite, parallel forces — produces pure rotation whose torque is pivot-independent. Every lever, balance and crane leans on this arithmetic.

Special Notes

  • A light beam means its weight is neglected.
  • Couple: two equal opposite forces with different lines of action, net force zero, net torque not zero.

Important Statements

  • "It is not the force but where you apply it that turns the world."

  • 50 cm

A couple produces:

Options
  • Pure translation
  • Pure rotation
  • Both
  • Neither
Answer

B

Explanation

The torque of a 10 N force applied at 30° to a 2 m spanner arm is:

Options
  • 20 N·m
  • 10 N·m
  • 17.3 N·m
  • 5 N·m

B

Explanation

The principle of moments states:

Options
  • Energy is conserved
  • Clockwise moments equal anticlockwise moments in equilibrium
  • Momentum is conserved
  • Force equals mass times acceleration

B

The balance condition about any pivot.

A longer spanner loosens a bolt more easily because it:

  • Increases friction
  • Increases the moment arm
  • Increases the force
  • Reduces the bolt's mass
Answer

B

Explanation

Same force, longer arm → larger torque.

Exercises

1.

A light rod 2.0 m long is supported at its ends. A 40 N weight hangs 0.50 m from the left end. Find the two support forces.

Steps
  • The rod is light, so it has no weight of its own.
  • Vertical equilibrium: N_left + N_right = 40 N.
  • Take torques about the left end. The left support has zero moment arm.
  • Clockwise moment of the weight is 40 × 0.50. Anticlockwise moment of the right support is N_right × 2.0.
  • N_right × 2.0 = 20, so N_right = 10 N. Then N_left = 30 N.
Answer

Left support 30 N upward, right support 10 N upward.
