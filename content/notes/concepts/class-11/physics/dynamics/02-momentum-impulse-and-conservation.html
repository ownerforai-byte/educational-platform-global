Linear Momentum, Impulse & Conservation of Linear Momentum

Class 11PhysicsDynamics

Notes

  • Linear momentum is p = mv. It is a vector, so a sign convention is part of the answer. The usual choice is to take one direction as positive and the opposite direction as negative.
  • Impulse is the product of a force and the time it acts, when the force is constant. The impulse–momentum relation says F Δt = Δp = mv − mu. A large force for a short time can change the momentum as much as a small force acting longer.
  • If the net external force on a system is zero, the total momentum of the system stays constant. Internal forces come in third-law pairs and do not change the total momentum.
  • For two bodies along one line, m1 u1 + m2 u2 = m1 v1 + m2 v2. This is conservation of momentum. It is true for collisions even when kinetic energy is not conserved.
  • An explosion or a gun firing is the same law. Before the event the total momentum can be zero. After it, the pieces have equal and opposite momenta so the vector sum is still zero.
  • Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision. Momentum conservation does not need that extra condition. In a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies stick, and you still use momentum conservation, with a shared final velocity.

Confusion

  • Momentum conservation does not require an elastic collision.
  • A wall can change a ball's momentum because the wall is an external body.
  • Impulse is not the same as momentum. Impulse is the change of momentum.
  • Speed is not momentum. Direction and mass both matter.

Exercises

1.

A 2.0 kg body moving at 4.0 m/s collides with and sticks to a 2.0 kg body at rest. Find the common speed.

Steps
  • Take the initial direction of the moving body as positive.
  • Initial momentum is 2.0 × 4.0 + 2.0 × 0 = 8.0 kg m/s.
  • The bodies stick, so there is one final velocity v.
  • Final momentum is (2.0 + 2.0) v = 4.0 v.
  • Conservation gives 4.0 v = 8.0, so v = 2.0 m/s.
Answer

2.0 m/s in the original direction.
