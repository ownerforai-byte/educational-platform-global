Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Adding Quantities That Refuse to Add

    Masses add: 3 kg + 4 kg = 7 kg. Displacements do not: 3 m east then 4 m north is not 7 m of anything. Quantities that need a direction — displacement, force, velocity — are vectors, and they obey geometric laws of addition. This topic covers scalars vs vectors, and the triangle, parallelogram and polygon laws.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2020)

    ScalarVector
    Magnitude (number + unit) onlyMagnitude and direction; obeys vector addition
    Adds arithmeticallyAdds geometrically (laws of vectors)
    Examples: mass, distance, speed, time, temperature, work, energy, power, densityExamples: displacement, velocity, acceleration, force, momentum, torque, electric field

    Representation: an arrow — length proportional to magnitude, head giving direction. Notation: A or bold A; magnitude written ∣A∣ or A.

    Types: equal vectors (same magnitude and direction), negative vector (same magnitude, opposite direction), null vector (zero magnitude, arbitrary direction), unit vector (A^=A/A, magnitude 1, gives direction), position vector, collinear/coplanar vectors.

  • 3. The Laws of Vector Addition

    3.1 Triangle law

    If two vectors are drawn head to tail, the resultant is drawn from the tail of the first to the head of the second (closing side of the triangle).

    R=A+B — the order does not matter (commutative: A+B=B+A).

    3.2 Parallelogram law (NEB 2021 — 5m)

    If two vectors are drawn from a common point as adjacent sides of a parallelogram, their resultant is the diagonal from the common point.

    Magnitude: R=A2+B2+2ABcosθ​ where θ is the angle between A and B.

    Direction: the resultant makes angle α with A: tanα=A+BcosθBsinθ​

    Special cases:

    θResultant
    0°R=A+B (maximum)
    90°R=A2+B2​
    180°R=∣A−B∣ (minimum)
    120°, A = BR=A

    3.3 Polygon law

    For more than two vectors drawn head to tail in any order, the resultant is the vector closing the polygon from the tail of the first to the head of the last. If the polygon closes, the resultant is zero (equilibrium).

  • 4. Worked Numericals (NEB 2021)

    Q (5m, 2021): Two forces P = 10 N and Q = 20 N act at a point with 60° between them. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant.

    Given: P = 10 N, Q = 20 N, θ = 60°. Formula: R=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ​; tanα=P+QcosθQsinθ​. Substitution: R=100+400+2(10)(20)(0.5)​=700​=26.5 N. tanα=10+20×0.520×0.866​=2017.32​=0.866→α=40.9°. Answer: R ≈ 26.5 N, directed at ≈ 40.9° from the 10 N force (toward the 20 N force).

    Q (3m, 2021): Add (3i^+4j^​) m and (5i^−12j^​) m. Substitution: R=(3+5)i^+(4−12)j^​=8i^−8j^​ m. Magnitude: R=64+64​=82​≈11.3 m. Direction: tanα=−8/8=−1→α=45° below the +x axis (fourth quadrant).

  • 5. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 4 (resolution): the parallelogram law and resolution are the same idea run in opposite directions — combining vs splitting.
    • To Topic 5 (products): the dot and cross products give compact formulas for the angle between vectors.
    • To Dynamics: forces add as vectors — equilibrium (polygon closing) is Newton's first law applied to a point.
    • To Kinematics: relative velocity is vector subtraction; projectile motion is vector addition of horizontal and vertical components.

Details

Summary

Scalars — mass, time, energy — carry magnitude only and add arithmetically; vectors — displacement, force, velocity — carry direction and add geometrically. The triangle law places vectors head to tail; the parallelogram law draws them from a common point and takes the diagonal: R=A2+B2+2ABcosθ​ at angle α with tanα=Bsinθ/(A+Bcosθ) — giving A + B when parallel, √(A²+B²) when perpendicular, |A − B| when antiparallel. The polygon law extends this to many vectors; a closed polygon means equilibrium. NEB's favourite numericals: 10 N and 20 N at 60° give R = 26.5 N at 40.9° (2021), and component addition of 3i^+4j^​ and 5i^−12j^​ gives 82​ m at 45° below the x-axis (2021).

  • 12 N

R = √(9 + 16) = 5 N for perpendicular vectors.

Options
  • 2 N
  • 10 N
  • 14 N
  • 0 N
Answer

A

Explanation

Minimum occurs at 180°: |8 − 6| = 2 N.

3.

If two equal forces A act at 120°, their resultant is:

Options
  • A/2
  • A
  • A√2
  • 2A
Answer

B

Explanation

R = √(A² + A² + 2A²cos120°) = √(2A² − A²) = A.

The angle α that R makes with A satisfies:

  • tan α = A sin θ/(B + A cos θ)
  • sin α = B/A
  • tan α = B/A
Answer

A

Explanation

The standard parallelogram direction formula measures α from A toward B.

5.

A closed force polygon means:

Options
  • Forces are maximum
  • The resultant is zero — equilibrium
  • Forces are perpendicular
  • Forces are equal
Answer

B

Explanation

A closed head-to-tail polygon leaves no closing side — zero resultant.

Options

  • 8î + 16ĵ
  • 2î − 8ĵ
  • 15î − 48ĵ

8.

Vector addition is:

Options
  • Commutative but not associative
  • Associative but not commutative
  • Both commutative and associative
  • Neither
Answer

C

Explanation

Vector addition obeys both A + B = B + A and (A + B) + C = A + (B + C).
