  • Four forces of 10 N, 20 N, 25 N, and 15 N act along the sides of a quadrilateral taken in order. If the quadrilateral closes, show the system is in equilibrium. [Solution: A closed quadrilateral means the vector polygon closes, so R=0. The system is in equilibrium by the polygon law.]
  • Three forces of 5 N, 7 N, and 8 N act at a point and keep it in equilibrium. Find the angles between the forces. [Solution: The forces form a triangle with sides 5, 7, 8. Using cosine rule: cosA=(49+64−25)/(2⋅7⋅8)=88/112=0.7857, so angle opposite 5 N is ≈38.2°. Similarly, angle opposite 7 N ≈61.0°, angle opposite 8 N ≈80.8°. The angles BETWEEN the forces are the supplements: 180°−38.2°=141.8°, 180°−61.0°=119.0°, 180°−80.8°=99.2°.]

Fy​=0+4+5sin(180°−53°)(−1)=4−5(0.7986)=4−3.993≈0. Both components ≈0, so the body is in equilibrium.]
  • Five equal forces act at a point with consecutive angles of 60° between them. Find the resultant. [Solution: The forces are at 0°, 60°, 120°, 180°, 240°. Rx​=F(cos0°+cos60°+cos120°+cos180°+cos240°)=F(1+0.5−0.5−1−0.5)=−0.5F. Ry​=F(sin0°+sin60°+sin120°+sin180°+sin240°)=F(0+0.866+0.866+0−0.866)=0.866F. R=F0.25+0.75​=F. The resultant has magnitude F.]
  • Using Lami's theorem, find the tension in two strings supporting a 100 N weight, if the strings make angles of 30° and 60° with the vertical. [Solution: The angles between forces: between T1 and T2 is 180°−30°−60°=90°, between T1 and W is 180°−60°=120°, between T2 and W is 180°−30°=150°. By Lami: T1​/sin120°=T2​/sin150°=100/sin90°. T1​=100⋅sin120°/1=86.6N. T2​=100⋅sin150°/1=50N.]
  • Three concurrent forces are in equilibrium. The angles between them are 150°, 120°, and 90°. If the smallest force is 20 N, find the other two. [Solution: Smallest force is opposite the largest angle (150°). By Lami's theorem: F1​/sin150°=F2​/sin120°=F3​/sin90°. With F1​=20: 20/0.5=40. So F2​=40sin120°=34.64N, F3​=40sin90°=40N.]
  • A force polygon has sides representing forces of 5 N, 12 N, and 13 N. Show that these forces can be in equilibrium and find the angles between them. [Solution: 52+122=25+144=169=132. This is a right triangle. For equilibrium, the angles between forces are the supplements of the triangle angles: 90°→90°, tan−1(12/5)≈67.4°→112.6°, tan−1(5/12)≈22.6°→157.4°.]
  • Four forces of magnitudes 1 N, 2 N, 3 N, and 4 N act along the sides of a rectangle taken in order. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant. [Solution: Along the rectangle sides (say, 2 units by 1 unit): F1​=1i^, F2​=2j^​, F3​=−3i^, F4​=−4j^​. R=(1−3)i^+(2−4)j^​=−2i^−2j^​. R=4+4​=8​=22​≈2.83N. Direction: 225° from positive x-axis (southwest).]
  • Five forces of 1 N, 2 N, 3 N, 4 N, and 5 N act along the five sides of a regular pentagon taken in order. Find the resultant. [Solution: For a regular pentagon, the exterior angle is 72°. Taking the first side along the x-axis, the five forces are at angles 0°, 72°, 144°, 216°, 288°. Rx​=1+2cos72°+3cos144°+4cos216°+5cos288°=1+0.618−2.427−3.236+1.545=−2.5. Ry​=0+2sin72°+3sin144°+4sin216°+5sin288°=0+1.902+1.754−2.351−4.755=−3.45. R=6.25+11.9​≈4.26N. Direction: tan−1(3.45/2.5)≈54.1° below negative x-axis, or 234.1° from positive x-axis.]
  • Three forces of 3 N, 4 N, and 5 N act at a point. The angle between the 3 N and 4 N forces is 90°. Find the angle between the 5 N force and the resultant of the other two. [Solution: Resultant of 3 N and 4 N at 90° is R34​=5N. Since the third force is also 5 N and the three are in equilibrium, the 5 N force must be opposite to the resultant of the other two. Therefore the angle between the 5 N force and the resultant of 3 N and 4 N is 180° — they are anti-parallel.]
