Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Grammar of Physical Equations

    Dimensions express every physical quantity in terms of the base quantities M, L, T (and I, Θ, N, J when needed). Dimensional analysis is the grammar check of physics: it tests whether an equation can be correct, derives relations, converts units and finds the units of unknown constants — the working tool behind every formula in this course.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    TermDefinition
    Dimensional formulaA quantity written as powers of base dimensions, e.g. force = [MLT−2]
    Dimensional equationThe equation equating a quantity to its dimensional formula: [F]=MLT−2
    Dimensional vs dimensionlessAngle, strain, relative density, refractive index, coefficient of friction have no dimensions
    Principle of homogeneityA physically correct equation must have the same dimensions in every term — you cannot add length to time

    2.1 Dimensional formulas to memorise

    QuantityFormulaSI unit
    VelocityLT−1m/s
    AccelerationLT−2m/s²
    ForceMLT−2N
    Work / energy / torque*ML2T−2J (torque: N·m, same dimensions)
    PowerML2T−3W
    Pressure / stressML−1T−2Pa
    Momentum / impulseMLT−1kg·m/s
    Gravitational constant GM−1L3T−2N·m²/kg²
    Planck's constant hML2T−1J·s
    Surface tensionMT−2N/m

    *Work and torque share dimensions but are different quantities — dimensional analysis cannot distinguish them.

  • 3. Uses of Dimensional Analysis

    1. Checking (verifying) equations — homogeneity test. Every term must share dimensions.
    2. Deriving relations. If T∝lambgc for a pendulum, dimensional balance gives a=1/2, b=0, c=−1/2 → T=kl/g​.
    3. Converting units between systems: n2​=n1​(M1​/M2​)a(L1​/L2​)b(T1​/T2​)c for a quantity with dimensions MaLbTc.
    4. Finding dimensions/units of unknown constants in given equations.
    5. Order-of-magnitude checks of formulas.

    3.1 Limitations

    • Cannot determine dimensionless constants (k, 1/2, 2π).
    • Cannot derive equations containing added dimensionally different terms or trigonometric/exponential/log functions of angles.
    • Cannot distinguish quantities with identical dimensions (work vs torque; speed vs emf-like ratios).

    3.2 Verifying equations (NEB 2017, 2023)

    Check s=ut+21​at2:

    • LHS: [s]=L.
    • [ut]=LT−1⋅T=L ✓
    • [21​at2]=LT−2⋅T2=L ✓ (the factor ½ is dimensionless)

    All terms have dimension L — dimensionally correct.

    Check v2=u2+2as:

    • LHS: [v2]=L2T−2.
    • [u2]=L2T−2 ✓; [2as]=LT−2⋅L=L2T−2 ✓ — dimensionally correct.

    Convert 5 kg into grams by dimensional analysis (NEB 2015): Mass has dimension M. With n1​=5 kg, M1​=1 kg, M2​=1 g: n2​=n1​(M1​/M2​)1=5×(1000g/1g)=5000 g. The principle of homogeneity/consistency of dimensions — the physical quantity is unchanged when its unit changes; only the numerical value compensates.

    NEB 2022 MCQ: kg m² s⁻² = ML2T−2 = work (also energy/torque's dimensions — of the options, work).

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 1: significant figures and errors quantify how well; dimensions check what the equation says.
    • To every later unit: before substituting numbers into any formula (pendulum, escape velocity, capacitor), check dimensional consistency — the fastest exam self-check.
    • To Kinematics: the homogeneity checks used at NEB (s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as) are the kinematic equations themselves.
    • To Gravitation: deriving T∝r3/GM​ style relations is a classic dimensional-derivation exercise.

    5.2 Model answers

    Q1 (3m, 2023) — Homogeneity; check v² = u² + 2as. Answer: The principle of homogeneity states that a physically meaningful equation must have the same dimensions in every term — quantities of different dimensions cannot be added or equated. Checking v2=u2+2as: LHS [v2]=(LT−1)2=L2T−2; [u2]=L2T−2; [2as]=(LT−2)(L)=L2T−2. Every term has dimensions L2T−2, so the equation is dimensionally consistent (the factor 2 is dimensionless).

    Q2 (3m, 2017) — Homogeneity; verify s = ut + ½at². Answer: Same principle as above. LHS [s]=L; [ut]=(LT−1)(T)=L; [21​at2]=(LT−2)(T2)=L. All three terms have dimension L — the equation is dimensionally correct.

    Q3 (2m, 2015) — Convert 5 kg to grams; state the principle. Answer: Mass has dimension M. The numerical value of a quantity is inversely proportional to the size of its unit (principle of homogeneity/consistency): n2​=n1​(M1​/M2​)1. With 1 kg = 1000 g: n2​=5×1000/1=5000 g. So 5 kg = 5000 g.

    Q4 (1m, 2022) — kg m² s⁻² represents? Answer: (a) work — ML2T−2 is the dimensional formula of work/energy (and torque).

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Forgetting that dimensionless factors (½, 2, π, sin θ of a pure angle) are ignored in checks.
    2. Declaring an equation "correct" when it is only dimensionally correct — homogeneity is necessary, not sufficient.
    3. Wrong exponents in conversions — the powers a,b,c come from the quantity's dimensions, not from memory.
    4. Confusing work (ML2T−2) with power (ML2T−3) or momentum (MLT−1).
    5. Adding terms of different dimensions in a derivation without noticing the violation.

Confusion

  • Dimensionally correct vs physically correct: homogeneity is necessary but not sufficient — s=ut+31​at2 is dimensionally correct yet physically wrong (the ⅓ should be ½).
  • Work vs torque: both have dimensions ML2T−2; dimensional analysis cannot tell them apart.

Practice

  • Why can dimensional analysis never fix the numerical constant in a derived formula?

Universal Facts

  • The seven base dimensions are M, L, T, I (current), Θ (temperature), N (amount), J (luminous intensity) — most mechanics problems need only the first three.

Examples

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (3m): Derive by dimensional analysis the period of a simple pendulum as T = k√(l/g). — Assume T ∝ lᵃ gᵇ: dimensions give [T]=La(LT−2)b=La+bT−2b. Equating with T: −2b=1→b=−1/2; a+b=0→a=1/2. So T=kl/g​, where k is a dimensionless constant dimensional analysis cannot determine (in fact 2π).

Formulas

    • Unit conversion: n₂ = n₁ (M₁/M₂)ᵃ (L₁/L₂)ᵇ (T₁/T₂)ᶜ for quantity MᵃLᵇTᶜ
    • Force = MLT⁻²; Work = ML²T⁻²; Power = ML²T⁻³; Pressure = ML⁻¹T⁻²
    • G = M⁻¹L³T⁻²; h = ML²T⁻¹; Surface tension = MT⁻²

Key Points

  • Principle of homogeneity: every term of a correct equation has the same dimensions.
  • Uses: check equations, derive relations, convert units (n2​=n1​(M1​/M2​)a(L1​/L2​)b(T1​/T2​)c), find units of constants.
  • Limitations: no dimensionless constants; no added different-dimension terms; no trig/exp/log arguments; identical dimensions not distinguished.

Summary

Dimensions are the grammar of physics: every quantity reduces to powers of M, L, T — force MLT−2, work ML2T−2, power ML2T−3, pressure ML−1T−2. The principle of homogeneity demands the same dimensions in every term of a correct equation, which powers the four uses of dimensional analysis: verifying equations (s=ut+21​at2 and v2=u2+2as both check out), deriving relations (T=kl/g​ for the pendulum), converting units (n2​=n1​(M1​/M2​)a(L1​/L2​)b(T1​/T2​)c, e.g. 5 kg = 5000 g), and finding the units of constants. Its limits: dimensionless constants stay hidden, added terms of different dimensions and transcendental functions cannot be handled, and identical dimensions (work vs torque) are indistinguishable.

Exercises

1.

Forces of 12 N east and 5 N west act at a point. Find the resultant.

Steps
  • Take east as positive.
  • The vectors are opposite, so R = 12 − 5 = 7 N.
  • The larger force is eastward.
  • The resultant is therefore 7 N east.
  • Check: 12 + (−5) = +7, the same result.
Answer

7 N east.

Central Concept

Resolution of Vectors & Unit Vectors

Branches
1.
Topic

Rectangular components

Details
  • A vector splits into perpendicular components along chosen axes
  • A_x = A cos θ, A_y = A sin θ
  • Recombine with A = √(A_x² + A_y²)
  • Direction: tan θ = A_y / A_x
2.
Topic

Unit vectors

Details
  • A unit vector has magnitude 1 and shows direction only
  • Â = A / |A|
  • Cartesian basis: î, ĵ, k̂ along x, y, z
  • A = A_x î + A_y ĵ + A_z k̂, and |A| = √(A_x² + A_y² + A_z²)
3.
Topic

Why resolve?

Details
  • Turns a 2D/3D problem into independent 1D problems
  • Essential for inclined planes and force analysis
  • Components along different axes are independent
  • Choose axes to minimise the number of component vectors
4.
Topic

Traps

Details
  • Components are not the original vector — never add them as scalars without direction
  • Resolution is not unique; it depends on the chosen axes
  • cos/sin swap if the angle is measured from the other axis
  • A component can exceed the magnitude only if signs are mishandled
