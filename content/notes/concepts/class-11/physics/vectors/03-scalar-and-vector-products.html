Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Two Ways to Multiply Vectors

    Vectors refuse ordinary multiplication: A×B is ambiguous until you decide what you want. The dot (scalar) product answers "how much of A lies along B?" — yielding a number. The cross (vector) product answers "how much do A and B twist around each other?" — yielding a perpendicular vector. Each has its own physics.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023)

    2.1 Scalar (dot) product

    A⋅B=ABcosθ

    • Result: a scalar; maximum when parallel (cos0=1), zero when perpendicular, negative when θ > 90°.
    • Component extraction: Ax​=A⋅i^.
    • Commutative: A⋅B=B⋅A.
    • In components: A⋅B=Ax​Bx​+Ay​By​+Az​Bz​.
    • Angle between vectors: cosθ=ABA⋅B​.

    Physics examples: work W=F⋅s; power P=F⋅v; magnetic flux ϕ=B⋅A.

    2.2 Vector (cross) product

    A×B=ABsinθ n^

    • Result: a vector of magnitude ABsinθ, direction n^ perpendicular to both by the right-hand rule (curl from A to B; thumb gives n^).
    • Anti-commutative: A×B=−B×A.
    • Zero when parallel (sin0=0); maximum when perpendicular.
    • Magnitude equals the area of the parallelogram formed by A and B.

    Physics examples: torque τ=r×F; angular momentum L=r×p​; magnetic force F=qv×B.

  • 3. Worked Numericals

    Q1: Find the angle between A=2i^+3j^​ and B=4i^−j^​. A⋅B=8−3=5; A=13​=3.61; B=17​=4.12. cosθ=5/(3.61×4.12)=5/14.9=0.336→θ≈70.4°.

    Q2: Find ∣A×B∣ for A=5, B=3, θ = 30°. ∣A×B∣=5×3×sin30°=7.5 (direction perpendicular to both by the right-hand rule).

    Q3: Show i^×j^​=k^, j^​×k^=i^, k^×i^=j^​ — cyclic; reversing the order flips the sign (j^​×i^=−k^). Dot products: i^⋅i^=1, i^⋅j^​=0.

  • 4. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 3: the dot product generalises "component of A along B"; the cross product's parallelogram area echoes the parallelogram law.
    • To Topic 4: Ax​=A⋅i^ — resolution is dot products with unit vectors.
    • To Work/Energy (Unit 5): work is a dot product — only the force component along displacement counts.
    • To Circular Motion/Rotation: torque and angular momentum are cross products — the reason their directions follow the right-hand rule.

    5.2 Model answer

    Q (3m, 2023): The scalar (dot) product of two vectors A and B is A⋅B=ABcosθ — a scalar, maximum when the vectors are parallel and zero when perpendicular. Example: work done by a force, W=F⋅s=Fscosθ. The vector (cross) product is A×B=ABsinθ n^ — a vector of magnitude ABsinθ directed perpendicular to both vectors by the right-hand rule, and it anticommutes (A×B=−B×A). Example: torque about a point, τ=r×F.

    5.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Using cos for the cross product or sin for the dot product — cos pairs with dot, sin with cross.
    2. Saying the cross product "commutes" — it anticommutes; the order fixes the direction.
    3. Forgetting the right-hand-rule direction when only the magnitude is asked — state both.
    4. Treating the dot product's result as a vector.
    5. Mixing component formulas: dot adds products of like components; cross needs the determinant form.

Summary

Vectors multiply in two ways. The dot product, A⋅B=ABcosθ, yields a scalar measuring alignment — zero for perpendicular vectors, maximal for parallel ones; it computes work (W=F⋅s), power and flux, extracts components, and gives the angle between vectors via cosθ=A⋅B/AB. The cross product, A×B=ABsinθn^, yields a vector perpendicular to both by the right-hand rule — zero for parallel vectors, maximal for perpendicular ones, equal in size to the parallelogram area, and anticommutative; it computes torque (τ=r×F), angular momentum and magnetic force. Dot asks "how much along"; cross asks "how much around" — cos and sin, scalar and vector, commute and anticommute.
