Enriched Content

Notes
  • Elastic Potential Energy (U): The energy stored in a deformed elastic body (like a spring or stretched wire) due to the work done against internal restoring forces. For a spring, U=21​kx2.
  • Energy Density (u): The elastic potential energy stored per unit volume. For a wire under tension, u=21​×stress×strain=21​σϵ=21​Eσ2​=21​Eϵ2.
  • Work Done: The work done to stretch a spring by x is the area under the F−x graph: W=∫0x​Fdx=∫0x​kxdx=21​kx2.
  • Hysteresis: In some materials, the loading and unloading stress-strain curves do not coincide. The area enclosed by the loop represents energy dissipated as heat per unit volume per cycle. This is crucial for shock absorbers and rubber components.
  • NEB exam focus: Derive U=21​kx2; calculate energy stored in springs and wires; understand energy density; explain hysteresis loops.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'Energy stored is kx2.' ✅ It is 21​kx2 — the factor of 1/2 comes from the average force Fˉ=(0+kx)/2=kx/2.
  • ❌ 'Energy density is σϵ.' ✅ It is 21​σϵ — again, the 1/2 factor from the triangular area under the stress-strain curve.
  • ❌ 'Hysteresis loop area is energy stored.' ✅ It is energy DISSIPATED (lost as heat) per cycle.
  • ❌ 'Elastic potential energy is only for springs.' ✅ It applies to any elastic deformation (wires, beams, bulk compression) where work is done against restoring forces.
Practice
  • A spring (k = 500 N/m) is stretched by 10 cm. Find the stored energy.
  • A steel wire (L = 2 m, A = 1 mm², E = 2×10¹¹ Pa) is stretched by 1 mm. Find the stored energy and energy density.
  • A rubber band is stretched and released. Explain why it gets warm (hysteresis).
  • Calculate the work done to stretch a spring from x = 5 cm to x = 15 cm (k = 200 N/m).
  • A load of 10 kg is hung from a wire (E = 2×10¹¹ Pa, L = 2 m, A = 1 mm²). Find the energy stored.
Universal Facts
  • The 21​kx2 formula is a specific case of the general potential energy U=∫Fdx.
  • Hysteresis is the reason rubber bands heat up when stretched and released rapidly.
  • Energy density is a key parameter in material selection for applications requiring high energy storage (e.g., bow strings, catapults).
  • The area under the stress-strain curve up to the fracture point is a measure of the material's toughness.
Examples
  • Spring: k = 400 N/m, x = 0.05 m → U=0.5×400×(0.05)2=0.5 J.
  • Wire: σ=108 Pa, ϵ=5×10−4→u=0.5×108×5×10−4=2.5×104 J/m³.
  • Work: Stretching from 0.1 m to 0.2 m (k=100) → W=0.5×100×(0.22−0.12)=1.5 J.

Key Points
  • Elastic potential energy is the work done against restoring forces.

  • Energy density u=21​σϵ.
  • Hysteresis loop area = energy dissipated per unit volume per cycle.

Summary

Elastic potential energy is stored in deformed bodies. For a spring, U=21​kx2. For a material under stress, energy density u=21​σϵ. Hysteresis loops represent energy dissipation, while the area under the stress-strain curve represents toughness.

Special Notes
  • Always use consistent SI units (N, m, J, Pa).
  • The 1/2 factor is crucial — it arises from the linear force-displacement relationship.
  • Hysteresis is important for materials subjected to cyclic loading (e.g., tires, engine mounts).
  • NEB often asks for the derivation of U=21​kx2 or the energy density formula.
Important Statements
  • Elastic potential energy is the work done to deform an elastic body.
  • Energy density is the energy stored per unit volume.
  • Hysteresis loop area represents energy dissipated as heat.
Important Notes
  • Energy density u=21​σϵ is valid only within the elastic limit.
  • Hysteresis is a characteristic of non-linear, dissipative elastic materials.
  • Toughness is the total energy a material can absorb before fracture.
Exam Short Tricks
  • Energy shortcut: U=21​Fx — if you know force and extension, no need for k.
  • Energy density: u=21​σ2/E — useful if you know stress and Young's modulus.
  • Hysteresis: loop area = energy lost — larger loop means more damping.
Exam Notes
  • Be prepared to derive U=21​kx2 using integration.
  • Understand the difference between energy stored (elastic) and energy dissipated (hysteresis).

Specific Conditions
  • Energy Density Validity: The formula u = rac{1}{2}\sigma\varepsilon = rac{1}{2}E\varepsilon^2 = rac{\sigma^2}{2E} holds only within the linear elastic region (Hooke's law). Beyond the proportional limit, energy density must be found by integrating the actual stress-strain curve: u=∫σdε.
  • Spring vs Wire Energy: For a spring, U = rac{1}{2}kx^2 where k=EA/L is the spring constant. For a wire, U = rac{1}{2} rac{EA}{L} x^2 = rac{1}{2} \sigma \varepsilon (AL) = u imes ext{volume}. Both formulas are equivalent when geometry is accounted for.
  • Hysteresis = Energy Dissipation: The area of a hysteresis loop represents energy dissipated (lost as heat) per cycle, not energy stored. For conservative elastic materials (ideal springs, metals below yield), loading and unloading paths coincide, and the loop area is zero.

Exercises

1.

A wire is stretched 2.0 mm by a force that grows from 0 to 100 N. Find the elastic energy.

Steps
  • The loading is elastic, so the graph is a triangle.
  • Energy = (1/2) F x.
  • x = 2.0 × 10^−3 m.
  • Energy = 0.5 × 100 × 2.0 × 10^−3 = 0.10 J.
  • Beyond the elastic limit the triangle formula no longer holds.
Answer

0.10 J.
