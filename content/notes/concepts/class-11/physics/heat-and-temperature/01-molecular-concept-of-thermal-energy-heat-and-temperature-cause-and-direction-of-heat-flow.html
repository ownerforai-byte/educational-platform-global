  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021, 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Molecular concept of thermal energy

    Matter is made of particles in ceaseless random motion. Thermal energy is the total internal energy of these particles — kinetic (translation, rotation, vibration) plus potential (intermolecular). Raising temperature increases the average kinetic energy; heating can also change potential energy (as during melting, with no temperature change).

    2.2 Temperature

    Temperature is the degree of hotness — a measure of the average translational kinetic energy of the molecules: KEˉ=23​kB​T. It determines the direction of spontaneous heat flow. SI unit: kelvin (K); common scale °C (TK​=TC​+273.15).

    2.3 Heat

    Heat is the energy transferred between bodies (or systems) because of a temperature difference, flowing spontaneously from hotter to colder until equilibrium. SI unit: joule (J); historical unit calorie (1 cal = 4.186 J). Heat is energy in transit — a body does not "contain heat".

    2.4 Cause and direction of heat flow

    • Cause: temperature difference (drives the transfer); molecular collision/diffusion mechanisms (conduction), bulk motion (convection), radiation.
    • Direction: always from the body at higher temperature to the body at lower temperature — never spontaneously reversed (second law).
    • Thermal contact: energy exchange continues until thermal equilibrium — equal temperatures, no net flow.

Summary

Molecules of every body are in ceaseless random motion; their total kinetic and potential energies make up thermal energy. Temperature measures the average translational kinetic energy (KEˉ=23​kB​T) — an intensive quantity in kelvin that decides the direction of heat flow. Heat is the energy in transit between bodies because of a temperature difference — an extensive quantity in joules, flowing spontaneously from hotter to colder until thermal equilibrium. A sparkler's spark outranks a bathtub in temperature but not in energy. NEB asks the distinction repeatedly (2021, 2022, 2024, 2m): definition, unit, mass-dependence, measuring instrument.

Exercises

1.

A 1 kg block of iron at 80°C is placed against a 5 kg block of iron at 30°C, with no heat lost to the room. Which way does heat flow, and when does the net flow stop?

Steps
  • Compare the temperatures, not the masses or the stored energies.
  • 80°C is higher than 30°C, so heat flows from the 1 kg block to the 5 kg block.
  • The small block cools and the large block warms.
  • Net flow stops when both reach the same final temperature.
  • That final temperature is closer to 30°C because the receiving block has the larger heat capacity, but the direction of flow does not depend on that.
Answer

Heat flows from the 80°C block to the 30°C block until the temperatures are equal.
