Notes

  • Matter is made of molecules in motion. In a solid they vibrate about fixed positions. In a liquid they move through the liquid. In a gas they move almost freely and collide. The energy of this motion, and of the molecular forces, is the internal energy of the body.
  • Temperature is the quantity that indicates how hot a body is. For an ideal gas it is proportional to the average translational kinetic energy of the molecules. A higher temperature means faster molecular motion, not a larger object.
  • Heat is energy that flows from one body to another because their temperatures differ. It is not a substance stored in a tank. Once the energy has entered a body it is part of that body's internal energy, and it is no longer called heat.
  • The direction of the flow is from the higher temperature to the lower temperature. The flow stops, in the sense that net transfer ends, when the two bodies reach the same temperature. That shared state is thermal equilibrium.
  • A large mass at a moderate temperature can hold more internal energy than a small mass at a higher temperature. Heat flow does not follow the amount of energy stored. It follows the temperature difference.
  • Putting a warm hand on a metal rail feels colder than putting it on wood at the same temperature because the metal conducts heat away faster. The temperatures were equal. The rates of flow were not.

Key Points

  • Temperature marks the average kinetic energy of molecules.
  • Heat is energy in transit.
  • Internal energy is stored in the body.
  • Flow continues until the temperatures match.

Summary

Molecules in motion store internal energy. Temperature indicates how hot a body is and, for an ideal gas, tracks the average kinetic energy. Heat is energy flowing from the hotter body to the cooler one until the temperatures are equal.

Special Notes

  • The zeroth law says that if each of two bodies is in equilibrium with a third, they are in equilibrium with each other. That is what makes a thermometer possible.
  • Absolute zero is 0 K, about −273°C, where the classical molecular picture reaches its lowest energy.

Exercises

1.

A 1 kg block of iron at 80°C is placed against a 5 kg block of iron at 30°C, with no heat lost to the room. Which way does heat flow, and when does the net flow stop?

Steps
  • Compare the temperatures, not the masses or the stored energies.
  • 80°C is higher than 30°C, so heat flows from the 1 kg block to the 5 kg block.
  • The small block cools and the large block warms.
  • Net flow stops when both reach the same final temperature.
  • That final temperature is closer to 30°C because the receiving block has the larger heat capacity, but the direction of flow does not depend on that.
Answer

Heat flows from the 80°C block to the 30°C block until the temperatures are equal.
