Summary

Thermal equilibrium occurs when two bodies in contact have no net heat flow — they share the same temperature. The Zeroth Law states that if A is in thermal equilibrium with B, and B with C, then A is in equilibrium with C. This transitive property justifies temperature as a measurable state variable and validates thermometer use.

Notes

  • Thermal Contact: Two systems are in thermal contact when heat can flow between them through a diathermal (heat-conducting) wall. An adiabatic wall prevents heat transfer.
  • Thermal Equilibrium Definition: Two systems in thermal contact are in thermal equilibrium when there is no net flow of heat between them. Their macroscopic properties (pressure, volume, temperature) remain constant over time. This implies they have the same temperature.
  • Zeroth Law Statement: If system A is in thermal equilibrium with system B, and system B is in thermal equilibrium with system C, then system A is in thermal equilibrium with system C. This is the transitive property of thermal equilibrium.
  • Significance of Zeroth Law: It establishes temperature as a fundamental, measurable property of matter. Without this law, we could not define a universal temperature scale or trust thermometer readings across different systems.
  • Temperature as State Variable: The Zeroth Law allows us to assign a numerical value (temperature) to every system in equilibrium. All systems in mutual equilibrium share the same temperature value.
  • Thermometer Principle: A thermometer is system B. When placed in contact with system A, it reaches equilibrium and displays a reading. If two systems give the same reading on the same thermometer, they are in equilibrium with each other — guaranteed by the Zeroth Law.
  • Historical Context: Though logically prior to the First and Second Laws, the Zeroth Law was formulated last (Fowler, 1931). It was named 'Zeroth' because it underpins all thermodynamics but was recognized after the other laws were established.
  • Equilibrium vs Steady State: Thermal equilibrium requires NO net heat flow AND uniform temperature throughout. A metal rod with ends at different temperatures may have steady-state heat flow but is NOT in thermal equilibrium.
  • Practical Applications: Calibration of thermometers, defining fixed points (ice point, steam point), ensuring consistent temperature measurements across laboratories worldwide.

Specific Conditions

  • Diathermal Wall Required: Systems must be able to exchange heat to reach equilibrium. Adiabatic isolation prevents equilibrium establishment.
  • Time to Reach Equilibrium: Equilibrium is not instantaneous. Poor conductors take longer. The Zeroth Law applies only AFTER equilibrium is achieved.
  • Macroscopic Scale: Temperature and equilibrium are statistical concepts valid for large ensembles of particles. They lose meaning for single molecules.
  • No Phase Change During Measurement: If phase change occurs during thermometer contact, latent heat absorption/release affects the reading until phase transition completes.

Key Points

  • Thermal equilibrium = no net heat flow = same temperature.
  • Zeroth Law provides transitivity: A~B and B~C implies A~C.
  • Temperature is a measurable state variable defined by Zeroth Law.
  • Thermometers work because of the Zeroth Law.
  • Equilibrium requires both thermal contact and sufficient time.
  • Zeroth Law was named last but is logically first in thermodynamics.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Two systems in thermal equilibrium have identical temperatures.
  • Statement 2: The Zeroth Law establishes temperature as a valid physical quantity.
  • Statement 3: Thermal equilibrium is a transitive relation among systems.
  • Statement 4: Thermometer calibration relies fundamentally on the Zeroth Law.
  • Statement 5: No net heat flow characterizes thermal equilibrium, not equal internal energies.

Important Notes

  • Equal temperature does NOT imply equal internal energy — different substances at same T can have vastly different U.
  • The Zeroth Law is empirical — it cannot be derived from more fundamental principles within classical thermodynamics.
  • In statistical mechanics, temperature emerges from the derivative of entropy with respect to energy: 1/T = ∂S/∂U.
  • NEB exams frequently ask to state and explain the Zeroth Law with its significance for temperature measurement.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Zeroth Law → Transitivity → Temperature definition → Thermometer validity.
  • Same T = equilibrium; different T = heat flows hot→cold.
  • Thermometer is the middle system (B) linking unknown (A) to scale (C).
  • Equilibrium ≠ steady state: equilibrium has zero net heat flow everywhere.
  • Zeroth came last historically but is foundational logically.

Exam Notes

  • Define thermal equilibrium precisely: no net heat flow between systems in thermal contact.
  • State Zeroth Law verbatim and explain its role in defining temperature.
  • Explain why thermometers give reliable readings using Zeroth Law reasoning.
  • Distinguish thermal equilibrium from mechanical equilibrium (no net force) and chemical equilibrium (no net reaction).

Examples

  • Hot coffee (80°C) in room (25°C): Heat flows from coffee to air until both reach ~25°C. At that point, thermal equilibrium is reached.
  • Clinical thermometer under tongue: Body (37°C) heats mercury until equilibrium. Reading stabilizes when T_mercury = T_body.
  • Three blocks A, B, C: A touches B until equilibrium (T_A=T_B). B touches C until equilibrium (T_B=T_C). Then A and C brought together — no heat flows, confirming T_A=T_C.
  • Ice-water mixture at 0°C: Ice and water coexist in thermal equilibrium. Adding heat melts ice without raising temperature until all ice melts.

Universal Facts

  • The Zeroth Law was coined by Ralph H. Fowler in 1931, decades after the First and Second Laws.
  • Temperature is one of the seven base quantities in the SI system (kelvin).
  • Absolute zero (0 K) is the theoretical limit where all thermal motion ceases — unattainable per Third Law.
  • The International Temperature Scale (ITS-90) defines temperature based on reproducible fixed points traceable to the Zeroth Law.

Confusion

  • Thinking equal temperature means equal heat content: Temperature measures average kinetic energy per degree of freedom, not total energy.
  • Confusing equilibrium with no heat transfer ever: Equilibrium means no NET flow; microscopic exchanges continue constantly.
  • Believing Zeroth Law is trivial: Without it, temperature would lack mathematical foundation and thermometers would be unjustified.
  • Assuming equilibrium happens instantly: Real systems require finite time depending on conductivity and heat capacity.
  • Mixing up Zeroth and First Law: Zeroth defines temperature; First conserves energy. They address different concepts.
