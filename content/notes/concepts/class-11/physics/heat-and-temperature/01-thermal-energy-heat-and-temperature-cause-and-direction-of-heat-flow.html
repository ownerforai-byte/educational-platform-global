Notes

  • Thermal energy is the total kinetic energy of the molecules in a substance, which depends on the temperature and the number of molecules.
  • Heat is the energy transferred between two systems due to a temperature difference. It is a form of energy in transit, not a property of a system.
  • Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of the molecules in a substance. It determines the direction of heat flow.
  • The direction of heat flow is always from a region of higher temperature to a region of lower temperature.
  • Heat flow occurs when there is a temperature difference between two systems in thermal contact.
  • The Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics establishes that if two systems are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, they are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
  • Thermal equilibrium is a state where there is no net heat flow between systems, indicating they have the same temperature.
  • Thermal expansion is the increase in dimensions of a substance when its temperature is increased, which is often accompanied by heat flow.
  • The cause of heat flow is the temperature difference between two systems, which creates a driving force for energy transfer.
  • Heat transfer mechanisms include conduction, convection, and radiation, each with its own characteristics and applications.
  • Conduction is the transfer of heat through a material without the movement of the material itself.
  • Convection is the transfer of heat by the movement of the heated fluid itself.
  • Radiation is the transfer of heat through electromagnetic waves, which does not require a medium.
  • The direction of heat flow is determined by the temperature gradient between two systems.
  • In a closed system, heat flow continues until thermal equilibrium is reached, at which point the temperatures of all parts of the system are equal.
  • Understanding the cause and direction of heat flow is essential for designing and analyzing thermal systems and devices.

Specific Conditions

  • Heat Flow Direction: Heat always flows from a region of higher temperature to a region of lower temperature. This is a fundamental principle of thermodynamics.
  • Thermal Equilibrium: When two systems are in thermal equilibrium, there is no net heat flow between them, indicating that their temperatures are equal.
  • Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics: If system A is in thermal equilibrium with system C, and system B is in thermal equilibrium with system C, then system A is in thermal equilibrium with system B. This establishes temperature as a well-defined property.
  • Thermal Expansion: Most substances expand when heated and contract when cooled. The change in length (ΔL) of a substance due to thermal expansion is given by ΔL = L₀ α ΔT, where L₀ is the original length, α is the coefficient of linear expansion, and ΔT is the change in temperature.
  • Heat Transfer Mechanisms:
    • Conduction: Heat transfer through conduction is given by Fourier's law: Q = -k A (dT/dx), where k is the thermal conductivity, A is the area, and dT/dx is the temperature gradient.
    • Convection: Heat transfer through convection is given by Newton's law of cooling: Q = h A ΔT, where h is the convective heat transfer coefficient, A is the area, and ΔT is the temperature difference.
    • Radiation: Heat transfer through radiation is given by the Stefan-Boltzmann law: Q = ε σ A (T⁴ - T_s⁴), where ε is the emissivity, σ is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant, A is the area, and T and T_s are the temperatures of the body and its surroundings.
  • Thermal Energy and Internal Energy: Thermal energy is related to the internal energy of a system, which includes the kinetic and potential energy of the molecules. The internal energy of a system depends on its temperature, pressure, volume, and the phase of the substance.
  • Heat Transfer in Real Systems: In real systems, heat transfer is often a combination of conduction, convection, and radiation. The overall heat transfer coefficient U combines these effects: 1/(U A) = 1/(h₁ A₁) + R_th + 1/(h₂ A₂), where R_th is thermal resistance.
  • Temperature Gradient: The temperature gradient is the rate of change of temperature with distance and is the driving force for heat conduction.
  • Thermal Contact: For heat transfer to occur between two systems, they must be in thermal contact. This means they must be able to exchange heat energy.
  • Diathermic and Adiabatic Walls: Diathermic walls allow heat to pass through, while adiabatic walls do not. Systems separated by adiabatic walls cannot exchange heat.

Formulas

  • Heat transfer: Q = m c ΔT
  • Thermal expansion: ΔL = L₀ α ΔT
  • Thermal expansion (area): ΔA = A₀ β ΔT
  • Thermal expansion (volume): ΔV = V₀ γ ΔT
  • Conduction: Q = -k A (dT/dx)
  • Convection: Q = h A ΔT
  • Radiation: Q = ε σ A (T⁴ - T_s⁴)
  • Specific heat capacity: c = Q / (m ΔT)
  • Molar heat capacity: C_m = Q / (n ΔT)
  • Thermal conductivity: k = -Q d / (A ΔT)
  • Convective heat transfer coefficient: h = Q / (A ΔT)
  • Emissivity: ε = Q / (σ A (T⁴ - T_s⁴))
  • Overall heat transfer coefficient: 1/(U A) = 1/(h₁ A₁) + R_th + 1/(h₂ A₂)

Examples

  • Consider two cups of water at different temperatures. When they are placed in thermal contact, heat flows from the hotter cup to the cooler cup until they reach thermal equilibrium at the same temperature.
  • A metal rod is placed between two blocks of ice and steam. Heat flows from the steam to the ice until all three systems reach thermal equilibrium at the same temperature.
  • A thermometer is placed in a beaker of water. The thermometer reaches thermal equilibrium with the water, and its reading indicates the temperature of the water.
  • Two systems, A and B, are each in thermal equilibrium with a third system, C. According to the Zeroth Law, systems A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other.
  • A metal rod expands when heated, demonstrating thermal expansion. The change in length can be calculated using ΔL = L₀ α ΔT.
  • A black body at 500 K emits radiation according to the Stefan-Boltzmann law: Q = ε σ A (T⁴ - T_s⁴).

  • Distinguish between thermal energy and heat. Thermal energy is the total kinetic energy of the molecules in a substance, while heat is the energy transferred between two systems due to a temperature difference.
