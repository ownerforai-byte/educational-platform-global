Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Law That Makes Thermometers Possible

    Touch a hot stove and heat flows; touch a table and nothing happens. "Nothing happening" is thermal equilibrium — and the zeroth law turns it into a definition of temperature. Without it, comparing temperatures with a thermometer would have no logical basis.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2023, 2024)

    2.1 Thermal equilibrium

    Two bodies in thermal contact are in thermal equilibrium when no net heat flows between them — they share the same temperature. Equilibrium is reached as energy transfers from the hotter to the colder body until temperatures equalise.

    2.2 The zeroth law — statement and explanation

    Statement: If two bodies A and B are separately in thermal equilibrium with a third body C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other.

    Explanation: "Being in thermal equilibrium" is a transitive relation. Body C (a thermometer) reading the same for A and for B means A and B share the same temperature — they would exchange no heat if brought into contact. This is what allows a thermometer to define and compare temperatures: the thermometer's own state (its mercury column length) tags a set of bodies all mutually in equilibrium.

    2.3 Why is it called the "zeroth" law? (NEB 2023, 2024)

    The first and second laws of thermodynamics were already established when physicists (Fowler, 1930s) realised this more basic principle underlies the very definition of temperature used in those laws. Since it is logically prior to the first law, it could not be called "third" — it was given the number zero.

    2.4 Worked reasoning (NEB 2024)

    Q (2m, 2024): Body A is in thermal equilibrium with body C, and body B is also in thermal equilibrium with body C. What can you conclude about A and B? A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other (zeroth law) — they have the same temperature and no heat would flow between them on contact.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 43: equilibrium is the endpoint of heat flow.
    • To Topic 45: a mercury thermometer works by the zeroth law — it reaches equilibrium with the body and reports its shared temperature.
    • To first law (Unit 11): energy bookkeeping presupposes a well-defined temperature — supplied by the zeroth law.

    2. Saying "so A and B have equal heat" — they have equal temperature.

Confusion

Summary

Bodies in thermal contact exchange energy until thermal equilibrium — equal temperature, no net heat flow. The zeroth law states that if A and B are each separately in thermal equilibrium with C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other: equilibrium is transitive, and this transitivity is precisely what licenses thermometers to compare temperatures (NEB 2023, 2024). It is called the zeroth law because although formulated after the first and second laws, it is logically prior to them — the definition of temperature itself rests on it. A~C and B~C therefore means A and B share a temperature and would exchange no heat on contact (NEB 2024).

Special Notes

  • Some books define work on the system as positive and write ΔU=Q+Won; do not mix conventions.
  • At constant volume, Q=ΔU only if no other work is present.

Specific Conditions

  • Energy Conservation Foundation: The first law (ΔU=Q−W) is energy conservation applied to thermodynamic systems. Internal energy U is a state function; heat Q and work W are path-dependent energy transfers. The sign convention: Q>0 when heat enters the system, W>0 when work is done BY the system.
  • Process Dependence: Q and W depend on the thermodynamic path (isothermal, adiabatic, isobaric, isochoric), but ΔU depends only on initial and final states. For a cyclic process, ΔU=0 and Q=W — net heat in equals net work out.
  • Work in Thermodynamics: Only PV-work is considered in the basic first law: W=∫Pext​dV. Reversible work uses the system pressure W=∫PdV. Other work forms (electrical, surface tension, magnetic) must be added as extra terms if present.

Exercises

1.

A gas absorbs 200 J of heat and does 50 J of work on the piston. Find the change in internal energy, taking work done on the gas as negative when the gas expands.

Steps
  • Heat added to the gas is Q = +200 J.
  • Work done on the gas is W = −50 J.
  • ΔU = Q + W = 200 − 50.
  • ΔU = 150 J.
  • The gas is warmer in the sense of a higher internal energy.
Answer

ΔU = 150 J.
