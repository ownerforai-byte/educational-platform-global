Notes

  • The first law is energy conservation for a thermodynamic system. With heat Q positive into the system and work W positive when done by the system, ΔU=Q−W. Internal energy U is a state function; heat and work are energy transfers across the boundary.
  • For a gas expanding against constant pressure, W=PΔV. If 500 J of heat enters and the gas does 180 J of work, then ΔU=500−180=320 J.
  • At constant volume, boundary work is zero, so Q=ΔU if no other work is present. In an adiabatic process Q=0 and ΔU=−W. Over a full cycle ΔU=0, hence net Q=net W.
  • For a monatomic ideal gas ΔU=(3/2)nRΔT. For 2 mol heated by 10 K, ΔU≈(3/2)(2)(8.314)(10)=249 J, independent of the path between the states.
  • A p–V diagram makes work visible: work done by a gas is the area under the process curve, W=∫P dV. Expansion gives positive work under this convention; compression gives negative work.
  • The first law says the rise in internal energy equals the heat added to the system plus the work done on the system. A common school form writes ΔQ = ΔU + ΔW with ΔW the work done by the gas. The two forms agree if you keep the sign of W consistent. This note uses work done on the gas.

Confusion

  • Some books define work on the system as positive and write ΔU=Q+Won; do not mix conventions.
  • At constant volume, Q=ΔU only if no other work is present.
  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Summary

The first law accounts for heat and work as energy transfers: ΔU=Q−W when work by the system is positive. Apply the convention consistently to expansion, compression, adiabatic and cyclic processes.

Specific Conditions

  • Energy Conservation Foundation: The first law (ΔU=Q−W) is energy conservation applied to thermodynamic systems. Internal energy U is a state function; heat Q and work W are path-dependent energy transfers. The sign convention: Q>0 when heat enters the system, W>0 when work is done BY the system.
  • Process Dependence: Q and W depend on the thermodynamic path (isothermal, adiabatic, isobaric, isochoric), but ΔU depends only on initial and final states. For a cyclic process, ΔU=0 and Q=W — net heat in equals net work out.
  • Work in Thermodynamics: Only PV-work is considered in the basic first law: W=∫Pext​dV. Reversible work uses the system pressure W=∫PdV. Other work forms (electrical, surface tension, magnetic) must be added as extra terms if present.

Steps
  • Heat added to the gas is Q = +200 J.
  • Work done on the gas is W = −50 J.
  • ΔU = Q + W = 200 − 50.
  • ΔU = 150 J.
  • The gas is warmer in the sense of a higher internal energy.
Answer

ΔU = 150 J.
