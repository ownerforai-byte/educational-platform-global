Summary

Quantity of heat is the energy transferred due to temperature difference. Specific heat capacity, heat capacity, latent heat, and calorimetry principle govern heat transfer calculations. Q = mcΔT for temperature change and Q = mL for phase change.

Notes

  • Heat: Energy transferred between bodies due to temperature difference. SI unit: joule (J). Heat flows from higher temperature to lower temperature.
  • Heat Capacity: C = Q/ΔT is the heat required to raise the temperature of a body by 1 K (or 1°C). Unit: J/K. Depends on mass and material of the body.
  • Specific Heat Capacity: c = C/m = Q/(mΔT) is the heat required to raise unit mass of a substance by 1 K. Unit: J/(kg·K). Characteristic property of the material.
  • Molar Heat Capacity: Cₘ = C/n = Q/(nΔT) is heat per mole. Unit: J/(mol·K). Used in thermodynamics and kinetic theory.
  • Calorimetry Principle: In an isolated system, heat lost by hot bodies equals heat gained by cold bodies. Assumes no heat loss to surroundings.
  • Latent Heat: Heat absorbed or released during phase change at constant temperature. Q = mL. L_f = latent heat of fusion (solid↔liquid), L_v = latent heat of vaporization (liquid↔gas).
  • Specific Latent Heat: L = Q/m is latent heat per unit mass. Unit: J/kg. Different for each substance and each phase change.

Specific Conditions

  • Heat Capacity Definition: Heat capacity C = ΔQ/ΔT is the heat required to raise temperature by 1 K. Specific heat capacity c = C/m is per unit mass (J/kg·K). Molar heat capacity Cₘ = C/n is per mole (J/mol·K).
  • Phase Change Latent Heat: During phase change (melting, boiling), temperature is constant while heat is absorbed/released as latent heat: Q = mL. Latent heat of fusion L_f (solid↔liquid) and vaporization L_v (liquid↔gas) are material properties.
  • Calorimetry Principle: In an isolated system, heat lost by hot bodies = heat gained by cold bodies. This assumes no heat loss to surroundings and no phase changes unless accounted for. The final equilibrium temperature is found by equating m₁c₁(T_f - T₁) = m₂c₂(T₂ - T_f).

Formulas

  • Heat transfer: Q = mcΔT
  • Heat capacity: C = Q/ΔT
  • Specific heat capacity: c = Q/(mΔT)
  • Molar heat capacity: Cₘ = Q/(nΔT)
  • Latent heat: Q = mL
  • Specific latent heat: L = Q/m
  • Calorimetry: m₁c₁(T_f - T₁) = m₂c₂(T₂ - T_f) (heat lost = heat gained)

Key Points

  • Heat flows from higher temperature to lower temperature.
  • Specific heat capacity c = Q/(mΔT) is a characteristic property of material.
  • Water has high specific heat (4186 J/kg·K) — moderates climate.
  • During phase change, temperature remains constant despite heat absorption.
  • Calorimetry: heat lost = heat gained in an isolated system.
  • Latent heat of fusion L_f for melting; latent heat of vaporization L_v for boiling.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Heat is energy transferred due to temperature difference; SI unit is joule.
  • Statement 2: Specific heat capacity c = Q/(mΔT) is the heat per unit mass per degree temperature rise.
  • Statement 3: Latent heat Q = mL is heat absorbed/released during phase change at constant temperature.
  • Statement 4: Calorimetry principle states heat lost by hot body = heat gained by cold body in an isolated system.
  • Statement 5: Water's high specific heat (4186 J/kg·K) makes it an effective coolant.

Important Notes

  • Specific heat capacity is independent of mass — it depends only on the material.
  • Heat capacity C depends on both mass and material of the body.
  • During melting or boiling, temperature stays constant while heat is being absorbed.
  • Latent heat of vaporization is much larger than latent heat of fusion for most substances.
  • Calorimetry assumes perfect insulation — no heat exchange with surroundings.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Q = mcΔT — use for temperature change (no phase change).
  • Q = mL — use for phase change (constant temperature).
  • Heat lost = heat gained — calorimetry equation.
  • Water: c = 4186 J/kg·K, L_f = 3.34 × 10⁵ J/kg, L_v = 2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg.
  • Units: c in J/(kg·K), L in J/kg, Q in joules.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks calorimetry numericals: mixing hot and cold bodies to find equilibrium temperature.
  • Specific heat determination by method of mixtures is a standard experiment.
  • Latent heat numericals: ice-steam mixtures with phase changes are common.
  • Distinguish between specific heat (per unit mass) and heat capacity (for the whole body).
  • Always check if phase change occurs — if yes, include Q = mL term.

Examples

  • Heat needed to raise 2 kg water from 20°C to 80°C: Q = mcΔT = 2 × 4186 × 60 = 502,320 J ≈ 502 kJ.
  • Heat to melt 0.5 kg ice at 0°C: Q = mL_f = 0.5 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 167,000 J = 167 kJ.
  • 200 g copper (c = 385 J/kg·K) at 100°C mixed with 300 g water at 20°C: equilibrium T found by m_cu c_cu (100-T) = m_w c_w (T-20).
  • Heat to vaporize 0.1 kg water at 100°C: Q = mL_v = 0.1 × 2.26 × 10⁶ = 226,000 J = 226 kJ.

Practice Questions

  • Q1. Define specific heat capacity and give its SI unit.
  • Q2. Calculate heat required to raise 3 kg of water from 25°C to 75°C.
  • Q3. State the principle of calorimetry and its assumptions.
  • Q4. What is latent heat of fusion? How does it differ from latent heat of vaporization?
  • Q5. A 500 g metal block at 100°C is placed in 200 g water at 20°C. Find equilibrium temperature.
  • Q6. Calculate heat needed to convert 0.2 kg ice at 0°C to water at 0°C.

Practice

  • Solve Q = mcΔT numericals for various substances.
  • Solve Q = mL numericals for melting and vaporization.
  • Apply calorimetry principle to mixing problems.
  • Determine specific heat by method of mixtures.
  • Solve mixed problems with both temperature change and phase change.

Universal Facts

  • Water has one of the highest specific heat capacities among common substances (4186 J/kg·K).
  • James Prescott Joule established the mechanical equivalent of heat: 1 cal = 4.186 J.
  • Joseph Black discovered latent heat and specific heat in the 18th century.
  • The high specific heat of water moderates coastal climates and helps regulate body temperature.
  • Latent heat of vaporization of water (2.26 × 10⁶ J/kg) is about 7 times the latent heat of fusion.

Confusion

  • Confusing heat capacity (for whole body) with specific heat capacity (per unit mass).
  • Thinking temperature rises during phase change: Temperature stays constant during melting/boiling.
  • Believing heat and temperature are the same: Heat is energy; temperature is degree of hotness.
  • Forgetting to convert units: mass to kg, temperature to K or °C (ΔT same in both), energy to J.
  • Assuming calorimetry works without insulation: Heat loss to surroundings causes error.
