Summary

A mercury thermometer works on the principle of thermal equilibrium and thermal expansion. When the thermometer bulb contacts a body, heat flows until both reach the same temperature (thermal equilibrium). Mercury expands uniformly and the level in the capillary tube indicates temperature.

Notes

  • Thermal Equilibrium: Two bodies in thermal contact are in thermal equilibrium when they are at the same temperature and no net heat flows between them.
  • Zeroth Law of Thermodynamics: If bodies A and B are each in thermal equilibrium with body C, then A and B are in thermal equilibrium with each other. This establishes temperature as a measurable property.
  • Mercury Thermometer Working: The bulb contains mercury. When placed in contact with a body, mercury absorbs heat and expands. The expansion moves the mercury column up the capillary tube. The height is calibrated to read temperature.
  • Why Mercury: Mercury has uniform thermal expansion, high boiling point (357°C), low freezing point (-39°C), does not wet glass, and is easily visible due to its shiny surface.
  • Thermometer Calibration: Two fixed points are used — lower fixed point (ice point, 0°C) and upper fixed point (steam point, 100°C). The interval is divided into 100 equal parts (Celsius scale).
  • Temperature Reading: Temperature T = ((L_t - L_0)/(L_100 - L_0)) × 100°C, where L_t is length at temperature T, L_0 at ice point, L_100 at steam point.

Specific Conditions

  • Thermal Equilibrium Condition: The thermometer must be in good thermal contact with the body and left undisturbed until the mercury column becomes steady (no further change in level).
  • Uniform Bore Assumption: The capillary tube must have uniform cross-section so that equal temperature changes produce equal changes in mercury column length.
  • No Heat Loss: The thermometer should not exchange significant heat with the surroundings during measurement — ideally it measures only the body's temperature.
  • Range Limitation: Mercury thermometers are suitable for approximately -39°C to 357°C. Outside this range, mercury freezes or boils.

Formulas

  • Temperature from length: T = ((L_t - L_0)/(L_100 - L_0)) × 100°C
  • Linear expansion: ΔL = L₀ α ΔT, where α is coefficient of linear expansion
  • Zeroth Law: If T_A = T_C and T_B = T_C, then T_A = T_B (thermal equilibrium transitive property)

Key Points

  • Thermal equilibrium means equal temperature and no net heat flow.
  • Zeroth law establishes temperature as a valid measurable quantity.
  • Mercury thermometer works by thermal expansion at thermal equilibrium.
  • Calibration uses ice point (0°C) and steam point (100°C) as fixed points.
  • Temperature reading is proportional to mercury column length change.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Two bodies are in thermal equilibrium when they are at the same temperature.
  • Statement 2: The zeroth law of thermodynamics states that thermal equilibrium is transitive.
  • Statement 3: A mercury thermometer measures temperature through thermal expansion of mercury at equilibrium.
  • Statement 4: Thermometer calibration requires two fixed points: ice point (0°C) and steam point (100°C).
  • Statement 5: Temperature is proportional to the length of mercury column above the ice-point mark.

Important Notes

  • Thermal equilibrium takes time — the thermometer must remain in contact until readings stabilize.
  • Mercury does not wet glass, ensuring smooth movement in the capillary tube.
  • The stem of the thermometer should be at the same temperature as the bulb for accurate readings.
  • Mercury's uniform expansion makes the temperature scale linear.
  • Parallax error can occur — read the meniscus at eye level.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Thermal equilibrium = same temperature, no heat flow.
  • Zeroth law = temperature is transitive (A=C, B=C → A=B).
  • Mercury expands uniformly — scale is linear.
  • Two fixed points: ice point (0°C) and steam point (100°C).
  • Read meniscus at eye level to avoid parallax error.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks to explain the working of a mercury thermometer using thermal equilibrium.
  • Zeroth law questions often ask why temperature is a fundamental property.
  • Thermometer calibration formula T = ((L_t - L_0)/(L_100 - L_0)) × 100 is a standard numerical.
  • Advantages of mercury: uniform expansion, visible, doesn't wet glass, wide liquid range.

Examples

  • Mercury column length at ice point L_0 = 10 cm, at steam point L_100 = 25 cm. At body temperature L_t = 16 cm: T = ((16-10)/(25-10)) × 100 = (6/15) × 100 = 40°C.
  • If L_t = 15 cm, L_0 = 10 cm, L_100 = 30 cm: T = ((15-10)/(30-10)) × 100 = (5/20) × 100 = 25°C.
  • A mercury thermometer reads 37°C for human body temperature — the normal temperature at thermal equilibrium with the body.

Universal Facts

  • Daniel Gabriel Fahrenheit invented the mercury thermometer in 1714.
  • The Celsius scale was defined by Anders Celsius in 1742, originally with 0° as boiling point and 100° as freezing point (later inverted).
  • Mercury freezes at -38.83°C and boils at 356.7°C, giving a useful liquid range for thermometers.
  • Galileo's thermoscope (c. 1593) was an early temperature-measuring device without a scale.
  • The triple point of water (0.01°C) is now used as the fundamental fixed point for precise temperature measurement.

Confusion

  • Confusing thermal equilibrium with thermal expansion: Equilibrium = same temperature; Expansion = volume change due to temperature.
  • Thinking thermometer measures the body's temperature instantly: It takes time to reach thermal equilibrium.
  • Believing all liquids expand uniformly: Mercury's expansion is nearly uniform; water's is not (anomalous expansion near 4°C).
  • Confusing the two fixed points: Ice point = 0°C (lower fixed point), Steam point = 100°C (upper fixed point).
  • Thinking thermometer stem temperature doesn't matter: Stem conduction can affect accuracy.

B

Ice point (0°C) and steam point (100°C) are the two fixed points.
