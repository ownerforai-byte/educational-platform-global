Summary

A mercury thermometer works by thermal equilibrium: bulb in contact with the body, heat flows until temperatures match, and the zeroth law guarantees the thermometer's temperature then equals the body's. Mercury's uniform expansion in a capillary is the thermometric property, calibrated at the ice and steam points (0 °C, 100 °C). Mercury is chosen for its wide liquid range (−39 to 357 °C), opacity, non-wetting and low specific heat. A faulty thermometer is rescued by linear interpolation: reading R=R1​+100(R2​−R1​)T​ — for one reading −5 °C at ice and 105 °C at steam, true 40 °C shows as 39 °C (NEB 2023).

Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Reading Temperature by Equilibrium

    A mercury thermometer does not "measure" temperature directly — it joins the body it touches, reaches thermal equilibrium with it, and then displays its own temperature. The zeroth law guarantees that this shared temperature equals the body's. The scale calibration then converts mercury length to degrees.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Working principle

    1. Thermal contact: the bulb is placed in contact with the body; heat flows between them until thermal equilibrium (zeroth law: the thermometer then reads the body's temperature).
    2. Thermometric property: mercury expands uniformly with temperature — its length in a fine capillary is the thermometric property. (Other choices: electrical resistance, gas pressure, thermo-emf.)
    3. Scale calibration: two fixed points — ice point (0 °C) and steam point (100 °C) at normal pressure — divided into equal divisions on the assumption of uniform expansion.

    2.2 Properties that make mercury suitable

    • Uniform expansion over a wide range (−39 °C to 357 °C — liquid across the whole Celsius range).
    • Opaque and shiny — easily read.
    • Does not wet glass (clean meniscus).
    • Good thermal conductor — quick equilibrium; small specific heat capacity — responds fast with minimal heat drain.
    • Low vapour pressure at high temperatures.

    Why not water? Water wets glass, expands non-uniformly (anomalous below 4 °C), and freezes at 0 °C.

    2.3 Non-ideal thermometer calibration (NEB 2023)

    A real thermometer may read R1​ at the ice point (true 0 °C) and R2​ at the steam point (true 100 °C). Assuming linear behaviour between the fixed points, the true temperature T maps to reading R: R=R1​+100R2​−R1​​T⟺T=R2​−R1​100(R−R1​)​

    2.4 Worked numerical (NEB 2023)

    Q (3m, 2023): A thermometer reads −5 °C when placed in ice water and 105 °C in boiling water. What reading does it give when placed in a liquid at 40 °C?

    • Scale factor: 100105−(−5)​=100110​=1.1 °C reading per °C true
    • R=−5+1.1×40=−5+44=39 °C​

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 44: the zeroth law is the logical foundation of the reading.
    • To Thermal expansion (Unit 10): mercury's expansion coefficient sets the scale spacing.
    • To Temperature scales: the same fixed-point logic builds kelvin and Fahrenheit scales.

Specific Conditions

  • Thermal Equilibrium Condition: The thermometer must be in good thermal contact with the body and left undisturbed until the mercury column becomes steady (no further change in level).
  • Uniform Bore Assumption: The capillary tube must have uniform cross-section so that equal temperature changes produce equal changes in mercury column length.
  • No Heat Loss: The thermometer should not exchange significant heat with the surroundings during measurement — ideally it measures only the body's temperature.
  • Range Limitation: Mercury thermometers are suitable for approximately -39°C to 357°C. Outside this range, mercury freezes or boils.

Formulas

    • R = R₁ + (R₂ − R₁)T/100
    • T = 100(R − R₁)/(R₂ − R₁)

Key Points

  • Principle: thermal contact → equilibrium → mercury length = thermometric property.
  • Faulty scale: R = R₁ + (R₂−R₁)T/100; NEB 2023: −5, 105 → 39 °C at true 40 °C.

Important Statements

  • "A thermometer measures its own temperature at equilibrium, which the zeroth law equates with the body's."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQ: faulty-thermometer numerical (2023, 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Scale factor = (R₂−R₁)/100; then R = R₁ + factor × T."

Exam Notes

  • The 2023 numerical is the pattern: two fixed points, linear map, one substitution.

Examples

  • NEB 2023 — reads −5 at 0 °C and 105 at 100 °C → 39 °C reading at true 40 °C.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A faulty thermometer reads 4 °C in melting ice and 98 °C in steam. What is the true temperature when it reads 60 °C? — T = 100(60−4)/(98−4) = 5600/94 = 59.6 °C.

Practice

  • Why must a clinical thermometer be shaken down before use?

Universal Facts

  • Mercury is liquid from −39 °C to 357 °C — spanning every ordinary temperature; alcohol thermometers extend to −115 °C.

Confusion

  • The thermometer reads its own temperature — trusted only because equilibrium makes it equal to the body's.
  • True range is 100 °C even when the faulty scale spans 110 reading-degrees.

Mcs

1.

A mercury thermometer works on the principle of:

Options
  • Thermal conduction
  • Thermal equilibrium and expansion
  • Thermal radiation
  • Thermal convection
Answer

B

Explanation

Mercury reaches thermal equilibrium with the body and expands, moving the column.

2.

The zeroth law of thermodynamics establishes:

Options
  • Energy conservation
  • Temperature as measurable
  • Entropy increases
  • Heat flows hot to cold
Answer

B

Explanation

Zeroth law establishes that temperature is a valid, transitive measurable property.

3.

Mercury is preferred in thermometers because:

Options
  • It wets glass
  • Uniform expansion
  • Expands when cooled
  • Freezes at 0°C
Answer

B

Explanation

Mercury has uniform thermal expansion, doesn't wet glass, and has a wide liquid range.

4.

Two fixed points for calibrating a Celsius thermometer:

Options
  • 0°C and 50°C
  • 0°C and 100°C
  • 32°F and 212°F
  • -273°C and 100°C
Answer

B

Explanation

Ice point (0°C) and steam point (100°C) are the two fixed points.

5.

Parallax error in thermometer reading:

Options
  • Reading from an angle
  • Mercury sticking to glass
  • Thermometer too short
  • Wrong scale
Answer

A

Explanation

Parallax error occurs when the scale is viewed from an angle instead of at eye level.
