Change of Phases and Latent Heat

Notes

  • Phase change is the transition of a substance from one state of matter to another (solid, liquid, or gas).
  • Latent heat is the amount of heat required to change the phase of a substance without changing its temperature.
  • During a phase change, the temperature of the substance remains constant even though heat is being added or removed.
  • The heat required for a phase change is given by Q = mL, where m is the mass of the substance and L is the latent heat.
  • There are six common phase changes: melting, freezing, vaporization, condensation, sublimation, and deposition.
  • Melting: the process of a solid turning into a liquid (e.g., ice melting into water).
  • Freezing: the process of a liquid turning into a solid (e.g., water freezing into ice).
  • Vaporization: the process of a liquid turning into a gas (e.g., water evaporating into steam).
  • Condensation: the process of a gas turning into a liquid (e.g., steam condensing into water).
  • Sublimation: the process of a solid turning directly into a gas (e.g., dry ice turning into carbon dioxide gas).
  • Deposition: the process of a gas turning directly into a solid (e.g., frost forming on a cold surface).
  • Latent heat of fusion (L_f): the heat required to change 1 kg of a substance from solid to liquid (or vice versa) at its melting point.
  • Latent heat of vaporization (L_v): the heat required to change 1 kg of a substance from liquid to gas (or vice versa) at its boiling point.
  • Latent heat of sublimation (L_s): the heat required to change 1 kg of a substance from solid to gas (or vice versa).
  • The latent heat values depend on the substance and the phase change involved.
  • For water at 1 atm: L_f = 334 × 10³ J/kg, L_v = 2260 × 10³ J/kg.
  • The latent heat of vaporization is generally much larger than the latent heat of fusion for a given substance.
  • The latent heat of sublimation is approximately equal to the sum of the latent heat of fusion and the latent heat of vaporization.
  • Phase diagrams show the conditions of temperature and pressure under which a substance exists in different phases.
  • The triple point is the unique temperature and pressure at which all three phases (solid, liquid, gas) of a substance coexist in equilibrium.
  • The critical point is the temperature and pressure above which a substance cannot exist as a liquid, no matter how much pressure is applied.
  • Phase changes are important in many natural phenomena and technological applications, such as weather patterns, climate change, and refrigeration.
  • The latent heat of vaporization decreases with increasing temperature and becomes zero at the critical temperature.
  • The latent heat of fusion is generally less than the latent heat of vaporization.
  • Phase changes are accompanied by changes in volume and density.
  • The energy required for a phase change is used to overcome intermolecular forces and to do work against external pressure.

Specific Conditions

  • Phase Change Definition: Phase change is the transition of a substance from one state of matter to another without a change in temperature. The heat required or released during a phase change is called latent heat.
  • Latent Heat: Latent heat is the amount of heat required to change the phase of a unit mass of a substance without changing its temperature. It is given by Q = mL, where m is the mass and L is the latent heat.
  • Common Phase Changes:
    • Melting: Solid to liquid (e.g., ice to water), L_f = 334 × 10³ J/kg for water
    • Freezing: Liquid to solid (e.g., water to ice), L_f = 334 × 10³ J/kg for water
    • Vaporization: Liquid to gas (e.g., water to steam), L_v = 2260 × 10³ J/kg for water
    • Condensation: Gas to liquid (e.g., steam to water), L_v = 2260 × 10³ J/kg for water
    • Sublimation: Solid to gas (e.g., dry ice to CO₂ gas), L_s ≈ L_f + L_v
    • Deposition: Gas to solid (e.g., frost formation), L_s ≈ L_f + L_v
  • Phase Diagrams: A phase diagram shows the conditions of temperature and pressure under which a substance exists in different phases. It includes the triple point and critical point.
  • Triple Point: The unique temperature and pressure at which all three phases (solid, liquid, gas) coexist in equilibrium. For water, it is 273.16 K (0.01°C) at 611.657 Pa.
  • Critical Point: The temperature and pressure above which a substance cannot exist as a liquid, no matter how much pressure is applied. For water, it is 647 K (374°C) at 21.8 MPa.
  • Energy Considerations: During a phase change, the heat added or removed is used to overcome intermolecular forces and to do work against external pressure. The temperature remains constant during a phase change.
  • Latent Heat Values: Typical latent heat values: Water L_f = 334 kJ/kg, L_v = 2260 kJ/kg; Ice L_f = 334 kJ/kg; Steam L_v = 2260 kJ/kg; Copper L_f = 205 kJ/kg; Aluminum L_f = 397 kJ/kg.
  • Phase Change and Volume: Phase changes are often accompanied by changes in volume and density. For example, water expands when it freezes, which is why ice floats on water.
  • Applications of Phase Changes: Phase changes are important in many natural phenomena and technological applications, such as weather patterns, climate change, refrigeration, and air conditioning.
  • Heat Transfer During Phase Changes: During a phase change, the heat transfer rate is given by Q = mL/t, where t is the time taken for the phase change.
  • Phase Change and Energy: The energy required for a phase change is much larger than the energy required to change the temperature of a substance. This is why phase changes are important in energy storage and transfer systems.
  • Phase Change and Temperature: The temperature of a substance remains constant during a phase change, even though heat is being added or removed. This is a key characteristic of phase changes.

Formulas

  • Heat for phase change: Q = mL
  • Latent heat of fusion: L_f
  • Latent heat of vaporization: L_v
  • Latent heat of sublimation: L_s ≈ L_f + L_v
  • Heat transfer rate during phase change: Q = mL/t
  • Energy required for phase change: ΔU = mL
  • Phase change and temperature: ΔT = 0 during phase change
  • Specific latent heat: L = Q/m
  • Molar latent heat: L_m = Q/n
  • Triple point of water: T = 273.16 K, P = 611.657 Pa
  • Critical point of water: T = 647 K, P = 21.8 MPa
  • Phase change energy: ΔU = Q = mL

Examples

  • Consider 1 kg of ice at 0°C that is melted into water at 0°C. The heat required is Q = mL_f = 1 kg × 334 × 10³ J/kg = 334 × 10³ J.
  • When 1 kg of water at 100°C is boiled into steam at 100°C, the heat required is Q = mL_v = 1 kg × 2260 × 10³ J/kg = 2260 × 10³ J.
  • A phase diagram for water shows the triple point at 273.16 K (0.01°C) and the critical point at 647 K (374°C).
  • Dry ice (solid CO₂) sublimates into CO₂ gas at room temperature, demonstrating a phase change from solid to gas without passing through the liquid phase.
  • Frost forms on a cold surface when water vapor in the air deposits directly into ice, demonstrating the phase change from gas to solid.
  • The energy required to melt 1 kg of ice at 0°C is 334 kJ, which is much larger than the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of ice by 1°C (approximately 2.1 kJ).
