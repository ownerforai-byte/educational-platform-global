Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Forces That Keep Receipts vs Forces That Burn Them

    Lift a book and put it back: gravity's net work is zero — you can recover the stored energy. Slide a book around a loop on a rough table: friction has drained energy you cannot recover from position. The first kind of force is conservative, the second non-conservative — the distinction that decides whether potential energy can even be defined.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    PropertyConservative forceNon-conservative force
    Work over a closed pathZeroNon-zero (dissipative)
    Work between two pointsIndependent of the pathDepends on the path taken
    Mechanical energy (KE + PE)ConservedDegrades to heat/sound
    Potential energy definable?Yes — PE = −(work by the force)No position-based PE exists
    ExamplesGravity, spring (elastic) force, electrostatic forceFriction, air resistance, viscosity, tension in inelastic processes

    2.1 Two equivalent definitions

    1. A force is conservative if the work done over any closed path is zero.
    2. Equivalently, the work done between two points is independent of the path.

    These are the same condition: if path A and path B between the same points gave different works, going A and returning by B would yield net work — a perpetual-motion absurdity.

    2.2 Gravity's path independence

    Lift a mass m by height h — work mgh whether you go straight up, up a staircase, or along a zigzag ending at the same height. Slide it back down and gravity returns exactly mgh. Gravity keeps perfect receipts: Wclosed​=0.

    2.3 Friction's path dependence

    Drag a box 5 m directly: friction work −μmg(5). Drag it along a 13 m zigzag to the same point: −μmg(13). Different works for the same endpoints — friction's work is path-dependent, closed-loop work is negative, and the lost energy becomes heat. No friction-PE exists.

    2.4 Consequences

    • For conservative forces, define PE: Wconservative​=−(PEf​−PEi​) — the work done by gravity equals the loss in gravitational PE.
    • Mechanical energy conservation (KE+PE= const) holds only when all acting forces are conservative; with friction, KE1​+PE1​=KE2​+PE2​+Eheat​.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 19: conservation of mechanical energy assumes conservative forces — this topic supplies the fine print.
    • To Topic 16/18: gravity's and the spring's works become mgh and ½kx² — stored, recoverable.
    • To Topic 15 (friction): friction is the canonical non-conservative force.
    • To Class 12 (electrostatics): the electrostatic force is conservative — electric potential exists for the same reason gravitational PE does.

  • 4. Exam Orientation

    4.1 Previous year questions

    No conservative/non-conservative question exists in the authored bank. Questions below are NEB-style practice, clearly labelled.

    4.2 NEB-style practice questions with model answers

    Q1 (3m) — Distinguish conservative and non-conservative forces with one example each. Answer: A conservative force does work between two points independent of the path (equivalently, zero work over a closed loop), so mechanical energy is conserved and a potential energy can be defined — e.g. gravity: lifting a mass m by h costs mgh by any route. A non-conservative force does path-dependent work and dissipates mechanical energy as heat — e.g. friction: dragging a box along longer paths does proportionally more negative work, and no friction-PE exists.

    Q2 (2m) — Why can no potential energy be defined for friction? Answer: PE is defined as minus the work of a force, and is meaningful only if that work is path-independent. Friction's work depends on the path length, so assigning a unique PE to a position is impossible — friction has no receipt to store.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Saying gravity's work depends on the path — it depends only on the height change.
    2. Claiming mechanical energy is conserved when friction acts.
    3. Confusing "closed path" with "closed orbit under gravity" subtleties — the test is zero net work around any loop.
    4. Forgetting the sign: conservative work = −ΔPE.
    5. Listing spring force as non-conservative — the ideal spring is conservative.

Confusion

  • Gravity vs friction receipts: gravity returns exactly what it took (path-independent); friction keeps everything (path-dependent, dissipative).
  • Conservative ≠ 'always helpful': gravity can do negative work on the way up — conservativeness is about bookkeeping, not direction.

Practice

Universal Facts

  • Perpetual-motion machines fail exactly here: any force returning a system to its start with net work gained would violate energy conservation.

Practice Questions

  • Short answer (3m): Show that if a force's work depended on the path, energy could be created. — Take two paths A and B between the same points with different works W_A > W_B. Go out by A and return by B: net work W_A − W_B > 0 on a closed loop, repeatable indefinitely — free energy from nowhere, violating conservation. Hence work must be path-independent for a conservative force.

Formulas

    • W_conservative (closed path) = 0
    • W_conservative = −(PE_f − PE_i)
    • With friction: KE₁ + PE₁ = KE₂ + PE₂ + E_heat

Key Points

  • Conservative: path-independent work, zero closed-loop work, PE definable, mechanical energy conserved — gravity, spring, electrostatic.
  • Non-conservative: path-dependent, dissipative — friction, air resistance, viscosity.
  • W_conservative = −ΔPE.

Summary

Forces split by their bookkeeping. Conservative forces — gravity, the spring, electrostatic — do work independent of path and zero work around any closed loop; their work can be stored as potential energy (W=−ΔPE), and mechanical energy is conserved. Non-conservative forces — friction, air resistance, viscosity — do path-dependent, dissipative work: dragging a box 13 m zigzag costs more than 5 m direct, the closed-loop work is negative, and no friction-PE can exist. If a force's work were path-dependent, an out-and-back loop would create energy from nothing — impossible. Hence mechanical energy conservation (KE+PE= const) is exactly the statement that all acting forces are conservative; friction rewrites the ledger with a heat term.

Special Notes

  • Friction does NOT have a potential energy — the energy becomes heat, not stored PE.
  • A conservative force does NOT guarantee energy conservation if non-conservative forces are also present.
  • The closed-loop test is the definitive way to classify a force as conservative or not.
  • Normal force is conservative (work = 0 when displacement is perpendicular).
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Is this force conservative? Justify using the closed-loop test.'

Notes
  • रूढिगत बल (Conservative force) को परिभाषा: एउटा बल रूढिगत हुन्छ यदि यसले गरेको कार्य केवल प्रारम्भिक र अन्तिम अवस्थामा निर्भर गर्छ, पथमा निर्भर गर्दैन। यस्तै, कुनै पनि बन्द लूपमा गरेको कार्य शून्य हुन्छ: ∮F·dr = 0।
  • पथ स्वतन्त्रता (Path independence): रूढिगत बलको लागि, W = U_i - U_f = -ΔU। कार्य स्थितिज ऊर्जाको परिवर्तनको नकारात्मक हुन्छ। यसले हामीले स्थितिज ऊर्जा फलन U(r) परिभाषा गर्न सकिन्छ जहाँ F = -∇U।
  • रूढिगत बलका उदाहरणहरू: गुरुत्वाकर्षण (पृथ्वीको नजिक: F = -mg, U = mgh), स्प्रिङ बल (F = -kx, U = ½kx²), स्थिरविद्युत बल (F = kQq/r², U = kQq/r)।
  • अरूढिगत बल (Non-conservative force): एउटा बल अरूढिगत हुन्छ यदि यसले गरेको कार्य पथमा निर्भर गर्छ। बन्द लूपमा गरेको कार्य शून्य होइन: ∮F·dr ≠ 0। ऊर्जा ताप वा शब्दको रूपमा ह्रास हुन्छ।
Important Statements
  • गुरुत्वाकर्षण, स्प्रिङ, र स्थिरविद्युत बलहरू सधैं रूढिगत हुन्छन्।
  • घर्षण र हवा प्रतिरोध सधैं अरूढिगत हुन्छन् — यी यान्त्रिक ऊर्जालाई तापमा परिवर्तित गर्छन्।
  • सामान्य ऊर्जा समीकरण: W_nc = (K_f + U_f) − (K_i + U_i)।

Grammar

Notes
  • Conservative (adjective): preserving; in physics, path-independent.
  • Non-conservative (adjective): not preserving; path-dependent.
  • Potential energy (noun phrase): energy stored due to position in a conservative field.
Important Statements
  • In physics, 'conservative' does not mean 'cautious' — it means the force conserves mechanical energy.
  • The term 'dissipative' is often used as a synonym for 'non-conservative'.
