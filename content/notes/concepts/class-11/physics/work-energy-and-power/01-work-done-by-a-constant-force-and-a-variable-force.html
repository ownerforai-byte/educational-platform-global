Notes

  • Work by Constant Force: W = F·d·cosθ = Fd cosθ where F is force magnitude, d is displacement, θ is angle between force and displacement vectors. SI unit: joule (J = N·m). Scalar quantity — can be positive, negative, or zero.
  • Work by Variable Force: When force varies with position, W = ∫F(x)·dx from x₁ to x₂. Graphically, W = area under F-x curve between limits. For F = kx (spring): W = ½kx².
  • Positive Work: Force component in direction of displacement (0° ≤ θ < 90°). Example: gravity on falling object, applied force pushing object forward.
  • Negative Work: Force component opposite to displacement (90° < θ ≤ 180°). Example: friction on sliding object, gravity on object thrown upward.
  • Zero Work: θ = 90° (force perpendicular to displacement) or d = 0 or F = 0. Example: centripetal force in circular motion, carrying object while walking horizontally.
  • Work-Energy Theorem: Net work on object equals change in kinetic energy: W_net = ΔK = ½mv_f² - ½mv_i². Valid for constant and variable forces, conservative and non-conservative.
  • Units: 1 J = 1 N·m = 1 kg·m²/s². Erg (CGS): 1 erg = 10⁻⁷ J. Electronvolt: 1 eV = 1.602×10⁻¹⁹ J.

Specific Conditions

  • Dot Product: W = F·d·cosθ — only force component parallel to displacement does work.
  • Variable Force Integration: Must integrate F(x)·dx; cannot use W = Fd unless F constant.
  • Angle Definition: θ is angle between force and displacement vectors (tail-to-tail).

Formulas

  • Constant force: W = Fd cosθ
  • Variable force: W = ∫F(x)·dx
  • Spring work: W = ½kx²
  • Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔK = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i²
  • Dot product: W = F→ · d→
  • 1 eV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ J

Key Points

  • W = Fd cosθ for constant force — scalar, can be +/-/zero.
  • Variable force: W = area under F-x curve = ∫F dx.
  • Spring: W = ½kx².
  • Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔK (always valid).
  • Zero work when force ⟂ displacement (θ = 90°).

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Work done by constant force W = Fd cosθ where θ is angle between force and displacement.
  • Statement 2: Work by variable force W = ∫F(x)·dx — area under F-x graph.
  • Statement 3: Work-energy theorem W_net = ΔK is valid for all forces.
  • Statement 4: Spring work W = ½kx² from Hooke's law integration.
  • Statement 5: Zero work when force perpendicular to displacement.

Important Notes

  • Work is scalar — use dot product, not simple multiplication unless force ∥ displacement.
  • Friction always does negative work (opposes motion).
  • Work-energy theorem works even with non-conservative forces (friction).
  • Holding a heavy object stationary does no work on it (d = 0) despite physiological effort.

Exam Short Tricks

  • W = Fd cosθ — check angle between force and displacement.
  • Area under F-x graph = work for variable force.
  • Spring: W = ½kx² (area under linear F-x triangle).
  • W_net = ΔK — works with friction too.
  • Perpendicular force = zero work.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks for work-energy theorem derivation and numericals on constant/variable force.
  • Distinguish work done by individual forces vs net work.
  • Calculate work from F-x graph area.
  • Spring compression/extension work problems.

Examples

  • Force 10 N at 30° to displacement 5 m: W = 10 × 5 × cos30° = 43.3 J.
  • Friction μk = 0.2, N = 50 N, distance 10 m: W_f = -0.2 × 50 × 10 = -100 J.
  • Spring k = 200 N/m compressed 0.1 m: W = ½ × 200 × 0.01 = 1 J.
  • Gravity on 2 kg falling 5 m: W = mgh = 2 × 9.8 × 5 = 98 J (force ∥ displacement).
  • Centripetal force on circular motion: W = 0 (force ⟂ velocity).

Universal Facts

  • James Joule established the mechanical equivalent of heat: 1 cal = 4.186 J, linking work and energy.
  • No work is done by centripetal force — yet it's essential for circular motion.
  • Human body does work even when holding a weight stationary (muscle fibers contract/release internally), but mechanical work on the weight is zero.
  • The electronvolt (eV) is natural unit in atomic/nuclear physics — energy of one electron through 1 V potential.

Confusion

  • Confusing force component parallel vs perpendicular to displacement: Only parallel component does work.
  • Thinking holding a weight stationary does work: d = 0 → W = 0 mechanically.
  • Using W = Fd without cosθ when force not parallel to displacement.
  • Believing friction always does negative work: Yes, kinetic friction always opposes relative motion → negative work.
  • Confusing work done by net force vs individual forces: W_net = ΔK; individual works sum to W_net.
