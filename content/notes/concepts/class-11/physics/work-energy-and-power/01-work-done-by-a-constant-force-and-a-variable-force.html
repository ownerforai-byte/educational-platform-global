Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Force That Transfers Energy

    Work is force's energy invoice: W=F⋅s. Pushing a wall does no work; lifting a bucket does. This topic covers work by constant and variable forces, the spring as the standard variable-force example, and the definitions of work, energy and power (NEB 2021).

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2021)

    QuantityDefinitionFormulaSI unit
    WorkEnergy transferred when a force moves its point of applicationW=F⋅s=Fscosθjoule (J) = N·m
    EnergyCapacity to do work—joule
    PowerRate of doing workP=W/twatt (W) = J/s

    Key points about work:

    • Work is a scalar; it can be positive (force along displacement), negative (force against displacement — friction), or zero (force perpendicular to displacement: circular motion, carrying a load horizontally).
    • 1joule = work done by 1 N over 1 m along the force.

    2.1 Work by a variable force

    For a force changing with position, sum infinitesimal contributions: W=∫xi​xf​​Fdx Graphically, work = area under the force–displacement graph.

    Spring (Hooke's law): restoring force F=kx; external force to stretch by x is kx: W=∫0x​kx′dx′=21​kx2

    Worked numerical (3m, 2024): Calculate the work done in stretching a spring of force constant 200 N/m by 0.1 m. W=21​kx2=21​(200)(0.1)2=21​(200)(0.01)= 1 J.

    2.2 Work by a constant force at an angle

    W=Fscosθ: pulling a 20 kg crate 10 m with 50 N at 60° above horizontal gives W=50×10×0.5=250 J — only the horizontal component works.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 17: power divides work by time — the delivery rate.
    • To Topic 18: the work–energy theorem connects net work to kinetic energy; spring work becomes elastic PE.
    • To Topic 20: gravity and springs are conservative (work path-independent, stored as PE); friction is not.
    • To Elasticity (Unit 9): the spring's 21​kx2 reappears as elastic potential energy and Young's modulus material.

    4.2 Model answers

    Q1 (3m, 2024): For a spring obeying Hooke's law, the external force to stretch it by x is F=kx, so the work is W=∫0x​kxdx=21​kx2=21​(200)(0.1)2=1 J. This work is stored as elastic potential energy in the spring.

    Q2 (3m, 2021): Work is the energy transferred when a force displaces its point of application — W=Fscosθ, SI unit joule (J). Energy is the capacity to do work, also measured in joules. Power is the rate of doing work — P=W/t, SI unit watt (W = J/s).

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Counting work when force is perpendicular to displacement (carrying a bag horizontally does no work against gravity).
    2. Writing W=Fx for a spring instead of 21​kx2 — the force grows with x.
    3. Ignoring the angle in Fscosθ.
    4. Calling work a vector.
    5. Sign errors: friction does negative work; the net work can be negative.

Specific Conditions

  • Dot Product: W = F·d·cosθ — only force component parallel to displacement does work.
  • Variable Force Integration: Must integrate F(x)·dx; cannot use W = Fd unless F constant.
  • Angle Definition: θ is angle between force and displacement vectors (tail-to-tail).

Formulas

    • W = Fs cos θ
    • Variable force: W = ∫F dx
    • Spring: W = ½kx²
    • P = W/t

Key Points

  • W = F⃗·s⃗ = Fs cos θ — scalar; joule = N·m.
  • Variable force: W = ∫F dx = area under F–x graph.
  • Spring: W = ½kx² (200 N/m stretched 0.1 m → 1 J).

Important Statements

  • "No displacement, no work — no matter how hard you push."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: spring work (2024, 3m); work/energy/power definitions (2021, 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Perpendicular force does zero work; spring work is half k x squared."

Exam Notes

  • Most-tested: spring numerical (2024) and the definition triplet (2021). Area under F–x graphs is the variable-force tool.

Examples

  • Spring stretch — ½kx² with k = 200 N/m, x = 0.1 m gives 1 J (NEB 2024).

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A 20 kg crate is dragged 10 m by a 50 N force at 60° above the horizontal. Find the work done by the force. — W = Fs cos θ = 50 × 10 × cos 60° = 250 J; only the horizontal 25 N component acts along the motion.

Practice

  • Why does the work done stretching a spring grow as x²?

Universal Facts

  • The joule is a derived unit: 1 J = 1 kg·m²/s² — energy's dimensions are mass × velocity².

Confusion

  • Work vs energy: work is energy in transit (a process); energy is the capacity (a state) — both measured in joules.
  • Zero work cases: force perpendicular to displacement (circular motion) or zero displacement (pushing a wall) — no transfer either way.

Summary

Work is the energy a force transfers along a displacement: W=F⋅s=Fscosθ — a scalar, zero for perpendicular forces, negative for friction. Work by a variable force integrates: W=∫Fdx, read as the area under the force–displacement graph; the spring is the standard case, W=21​kx2 — stretching k = 200 N/m by 0.1 m stores 1 J (NEB 2024). Energy is the capacity to do work (joules); power is its rate (watts) — the definition triplet NEB asked in 2021. Every later topic — kinetic energy, potential energy, collisions — prices energy through this definition.

  • Unit vector

5.

For a variable force, work equals:

Options
  • F × x always
  • The area under the F–x graph
  • The slope of the F–x graph
  • F/x
Answer

B

Explanation

W = ∫F dx — graphical area.

6.

A 50 N force at 60° moves a crate 10 m. Work done is:

Options
  • 500 J
  • 250 J
  • 433 J
  • 0 J
Answer

B

Explanation

W = 50 × 10 × cos 60° = 250 J.

7.

Negative work is done when:

Options
  • Force aids motion
  • Force opposes displacement
  • Displacement is zero
  • Angle is 0°
Answer

B

Explanation

cos θ < 0 for θ > 90° — friction does negative work.

8.

Power is defined as:

Options
  • Work × time
  • Work per unit time

  • Energy per unit mass

B

Explanation

P = W/t, in watts (NEB 2021).
