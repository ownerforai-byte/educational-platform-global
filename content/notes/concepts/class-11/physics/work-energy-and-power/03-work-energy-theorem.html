Enriched Content

Notes
  • Work done by a constant force: W=Fdcosθ, where F is the magnitude of the force, d is the displacement, and θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors. Work is a scalar quantity (SI unit: joule, J=N⋅m). Work is positive when the force has a component in the direction of displacement (θ<90∘), negative when opposite (θ>90∘), and zero when perpendicular (θ=90∘).
  • Work done by a variable force: when force varies with position, work is the area under the F−x graph, or mathematically, W=∫x1​x2​​F(x)dx. For a spring force F=−kx, the work done by the spring when stretched from x1​ to x2​ is W=21​k(x12​−x22​).
  • Work-energy theorem: the net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy: Wnet​=ΔK=21​mv2−21​mu2. This is one of the most powerful results in mechanics — it connects forces (through work) to motion (through kinetic energy).
  • Work done by gravity: Wg​=mgΔh=mg(hi​−hf​), where Δh is the change in height. Gravity does positive work when the body falls (h decreases) and negative work when it rises (h increases). The work done by gravity is independent of the path — only the vertical displacement matters.
  • Work done by a spring: Ws​=21​kx2 (work done ON the spring to stretch/compress it by x). The spring does work W=21​k(xi2​−xf2​) when it returns from xi​ to xf​. At the natural length (x=0), the spring does maximum work.
  • Work done by friction: Wf​=−fk​⋅d=−μk​⋅N⋅d. Friction always does negative work (it opposes motion), converting mechanical energy into heat. The work depends on the path length d, not just the displacement — friction is non-conservative.
  • Zero work situations: (1) Force perpendicular to displacement (e.g., normal force on a horizontal surface, centripetal force in circular motion). (2) No displacement (pushing a wall). (3) Force applied to a body that doesn't move.
  • NEB exam focus: work done by constant force (W=Fdcosθ), work done by a spring (W=21​kx2), work-energy theorem (Wnet​=ΔK), area under F−x graph for variable forces, and identifying zero-work situations.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'Work and energy are different concepts.' ✅ Work is a TRANSFER of energy. When you do work on a body, you transfer energy to it. The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) makes this precise.
  • ❌ 'Work is a vector because force and displacement are vectors.' ✅ Work is a SCALAR — it is the dot product of force and displacement (W = F·d·cos θ). It has magnitude but no direction.
  • ❌ 'If a body moves, work must be done.' ✅ Work is done only if the force has a component along the displacement. Centripetal force does zero work in circular motion (always perpendicular to velocity).
  • ❌ 'Work done by gravity depends on the path.' ✅ Gravity is a conservative force — work depends only on the vertical displacement (mgΔh), not on the path taken.
  • ❌ 'A spring does work W = ½kx² when stretched by x.' ✅ The work done ON the spring to stretch it is ½kx². The work done BY the spring when it returns is also ½kx² (energy is recovered).
  • ❌ 'Friction always does negative work.' ✅ Static friction can do positive work — e.g., the static friction on your feet pushes you forward when walking. Kinetic friction always does negative work.

Universal Facts
  • Work is the dot product of force and displacement — a scalar quantity measured in joules.
  • The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) connects forces to motion and is one of the most powerful results in mechanics.
  • Gravity is a conservative force — work depends only on vertical displacement, not on the path.
  • Friction is a non-conservative force — work depends on the path length and is always negative (for kinetic friction).
  • A spring stores elastic potential energy U = ½kx² when stretched or compressed — this energy is fully recoverable.
Examples
  • Constant force: A 20 N force pushes a block 5 m at 30° to the horizontal. W = 20 × 5 × cos 30° = 86.6 J.
  • Variable force (spring): A spring (k = 100 N/m) is stretched from 0 to 0.2 m. W = ½ × 100 × 0.2² = 2 J.
  • Work-energy theorem: A 2 kg block accelerates from 3 m/s to 7 m/s. W_net = ½ × 2 × (49 − 9) = 40 J.
  • Gravity: A 5 kg object falls 10 m. W_g = 5 × 9.8 × 10 = 490 J (positive, body speeds up).
  • Zero work: A body moves in a circle at constant speed. Centripetal force is perpendicular to velocity → W = 0.

Summary

Work (W = Fd cos θ for constant force, W = ∫F dx for variable force) is the transfer of energy by a force. The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) connects forces to motion. Gravity (conservative) and friction (non-conservative) are the two most important forces in work problems. Spring work (W = ½kx²) is a standard NEB topic.

Special Notes
  • Work is a scalar — it has magnitude but no direction. Do not assign a direction to work.
  • The angle θ in W = Fd cos θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors.
  • Zero work occurs when: (1) force ⊥ displacement, (2) no displacement, (3) no force.
  • The work-energy theorem works for ALL forces — it is more general than energy conservation.
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Find the work done by...' — identify the force, the displacement, and the angle between them.

Formulas

  • Work by constant force: W = Fd cos θ (positive when θ < 90°, negative when θ > 90°, zero when θ = 90°)
  • Work by variable force: W = ∫ F(x) dx (area under F-x graph)
  • Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔK = ½mv² − ½mu²
  • Work by gravity: W_g = mgΔh = mg(h_i − h_f) (path-independent)
  • Work by spring: W = ½k(x_i² − x_f²) (spring force F = −kx)

Key Points

  • Work is a scalar — the dot product of force and displacement (W = Fd cos θ).
  • Work is positive when the force helps motion, negative when it opposes motion, zero when perpendicular.
  • The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) connects forces to motion — one of the most powerful results in mechanics.
  • Gravity is conservative — work depends only on vertical displacement, not on the path.
  • Friction is non-conservative — work depends on the path length and is always negative (for kinetic friction).

Special Notes

  • Work is a scalar — it has magnitude but no direction. Do not assign a direction to work.
  • The angle θ in W = Fd cos θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors.
  • Zero work occurs when: (1) force ⊥ displacement, (2) no displacement, (3) no force.
  • The work-energy theorem works for ALL forces — it is more general than energy conservation.
