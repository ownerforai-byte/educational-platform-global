Notes

  • Power is the rate of energy transfer: average P=ΔW/Δt and instantaneous P=dW/dt. Its SI unit is watt, 1 W=1 J s⁻¹.
  • For translation, dW=F⃗·dr⃗ gives P=F⃗·v⃗=Fv cosθ. Only the force component parallel to velocity transfers energy; a perpendicular centripetal force does no work in uniform circular motion.
  • Worked example: a 200 kg load rises steadily at 0.50 m s⁻¹. The lifting force is mg=1960 N for g=9.8 m s⁻², so useful power is Fv=980 W. In 10 s the load rises 5 m and gains 9800 J, confirming P=W/t=980 W.
  • At constant speed, net power is zero because engine power balances resistive forces. For rotation, P=τ⃗·ω⃗; efficiency η=Pout/Pin distinguishes useful output from input.
  • A 50 N force at 60° to a crate moving at 3.0 m s⁻¹ transfers energy at P=50×3×cos60°=75 W, not 150 W.
  • Power is the rate of doing work. When a force stays along the velocity, power is the force times the speed. A larger engine can do the same work in less time, which is a larger power, not a larger store of energy.

Formulas

  • Average P=ΔW/Δt; instantaneous P=dW/dt.
  • Translation P=F⃗·v⃗; rotation P=τ⃗·ω⃗; efficiency η=Pout/Pin.

Examples

  • Raising 200 kg at 0.50 m s⁻¹ requires 980 W of useful power.
  • A 50 N force at 60° while moving at 3 m s⁻¹ gives 75 W.
  • Power is the rate of doing work. When a force stays along the velocity, power is the force times the speed. A larger engine can do the same work in less time, which is a larger power, not a larger store of energy.
  • The working relations are P = W/t and P = F v when force and velocity are parallel. Name each symbol, convert the data into SI units, and only then substitute.
  • A force of 200 N pushes a crate at a steady 3.0 m/s along the force. Find the power.

Practice Questions

  • A 1200 kg car moves at 20 m s⁻¹ against 600 N drag. Find power to maintain speed.
  • Why does centripetal force do zero work in uniform circular motion?
  • An 80% efficient motor takes 2 kW. Find useful output.

Key Points

  • Use the force component parallel to velocity.
  • At constant speed net power is zero though a motor may deliver positive power.
  • State whether input or output power is requested.
  • P = W/t
  • P = F v when force and velocity are parallel
  • Convert units before substituting.
  • Quote the unit with the result.

Summary

Power measures energy-transfer rate. For translation it is the force–velocity dot product; practical machines distinguish useful output from losses.

Specific Conditions

  • P=F⃗·v⃗ uses instantaneous force and velocity; it applies even when force changes with time.
  • For efficiency, state whether the requested power is input or useful output.

Exercises

1.

A force of 200 N pushes a crate at a steady 3.0 m/s along the force. Find the power.

Steps
  • The force and the velocity are parallel.
  • P = F v = 200 × 3.0.
  • P = 600 W.
  • In one second the work is 600 J.
  • If the force were perpendicular to the velocity, the power would be zero.
Answer

600 W.
