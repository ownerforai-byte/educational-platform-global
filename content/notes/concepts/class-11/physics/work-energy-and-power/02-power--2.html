Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Rate of Energy Delivery

    Two cranes can lift the same car, but only the powerful one lifts it quickly. Power measures work per unit time — the billing unit of the electricity grid (the kilowatt-hour) and the performance metric of engines, pumps and athletes.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    QuantityDefinitionFormulaUnit
    PowerWork done per unit timeP=W/twatt (W) = J/s
    Instantaneous powerRate at an instantP=F⋅v=FvcosθW
    Kilowatt-hourCommercial energy unit: energy delivered by 1 kW for 1 hour1kWh=3.6×106 J—

    NEB 2024 (2m): 1 kWh = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6×106 J — the unit on every electricity bill.

    2.1 Worked numericals

    Q1 (3m, 2022/2024): An electric motor has a power output of 2 kW. How much time will it take to lift a 500 kg mass through 20 m? (g = 10 m/s²) Work needed: W=mgh=500×10×20=105 J. Time: t=W/P=105/2000= 50 s.

    Q2 (3m, 2022): A person of mass 60 kg climbs a staircase of vertical height 8 m in 20 s (g = 9.8). Calculate the power developed. Work: W=mgh=60×9.8×8=4704 J. Power: P=4704/20= 235.2 W (about 0.24 kW — a healthy human output).

    Q3 (3m, 2024): A car of mass 1000 kg moves at a uniform speed of 20 m/s against a total resistive force of 500 N. What power must the engine develop? Uniform speed → engine force equals resistance: F=500 N. Power: P=Fv=500×20= 10,000 W = 10 kW.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 16: power is work's time derivative — same energy, different delivery rate.
    • To Topic 18: P=Fv comes from differentiating the work–energy theorem: P=dW/dt=Fds/dt.
    • To electrical circuits (Unit 25): P=VI=I2R=V2/R — the same watt in electrical dress.
    • To real life: electricity bills (kWh), engine horsepower (1 hp ≈ 746 W) and human sprint outputs (~1 kW briefly) are all this topic.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Forgetting P=Fv for uniform-speed vehicles — the engine force equals the resistance, not ma.
    2. Using g = 9.8 in one numerical and 10 in another inconsistently.
    3. Confusing kWh (energy) with kW (power).
    4. Ignoring the vertical height in staircase problems — only mgh counts.
    5. Arithmetic slips with 3.6×106 J per kWh.

Formulas

    • P = W/t; P = Fv cos θ
    • 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J; 1 hp ≈ 746 W
    • Lifting: t = mgh/P; climbing: P = mgh/t

Examples

  • 2 kW motor, 500 kg, 20 m — t = 50 s (NEB 2022 & 2024).

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A pump lifts 200 kg of water per minute to a height of 10 m. Calculate the power of the pump (g = 10 m/s²). — Work per minute = mgh = 200 × 10 × 10 = 20,000 J; P = 20,000/60 ≈ 333 W (NEB 2024).

Key Points

  • P = W/t = Fv cos θ; watt = J/s.
  • 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J (NEB 2024).
  • Motor lifting: t = mgh/P. Human climbing: P = mgh/t. Vehicle: P = F_resistance × v.

Summary

Power is the rate of energy delivery: P=W/t, or instantaneously P=F⋅v — for a vehicle at uniform speed the engine force just balances resistance, so P=Fres​v (a 1000 kg car at 20 m/s against 500 N needs 10 kW — NEB 2024). Lifting numericals divide the work by the power: a 2 kW motor raises 500 kg through 20 m (10⁵ J) in 50 s (NEB 2022 & 2024); a 60 kg climber ascending 8 m in 20 s develops 235 W (NEB 2022). The commercial energy unit is the kilowatt-hour: 1 kWh = 3.6 × 10⁶ J (NEB 2024). Power prices speed of delivery, not the size of the job.

Specific Conditions

  • P=F⃗·v⃗ uses instantaneous force and velocity; it applies even when force changes with time.
  • For efficiency, state whether the requested power is input or useful output.

Original Content

Notes
  • Power is the rate of energy transfer: average P=ΔW/Δt and instantaneous P=dW/dt. Its SI unit is watt, 1 W=1 J s⁻¹.
  • For translation, dW=F⃗·dr⃗ gives P=F⃗·v⃗=Fv cosθ. Only the force component parallel to velocity transfers energy; a perpendicular centripetal force does no work in uniform circular motion.
  • Worked example: a 200 kg load rises steadily at 0.50 m s⁻¹. The lifting force is mg=1960 N for g=9.8 m s⁻², so useful power is Fv=980 W. In 10 s the load rises 5 m and gains 9800 J, confirming P=W/t=980 W.
  • At constant speed, net power is zero because engine power balances resistive forces. For rotation, P=τ⃗·ω⃗; efficiency η=Pout/Pin distinguishes useful output from input.
  • A 50 N force at 60° to a crate moving at 3.0 m s⁻¹ transfers energy at P=50×3×cos60°=75 W, not 150 W.
Confusion
  • Do not use P=Fv unless force and velocity are parallel or include cosθ.
  • Motor output is not the net power on a constant-speed vehicle.

Exercises

1.

A force of 200 N pushes a crate at a steady 3.0 m/s along the force. Find the power.

Steps
  • The force and the velocity are parallel.
  • P = F v = 200 × 3.0.
  • P = 600 W.
  • In one second the work is 600 J.
  • If the force were perpendicular to the velocity, the power would be zero.
Answer

600 W.
