Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — What Survives a Crash

    Every collision conserves momentum; only some conserve kinetic energy. The classification — elastic, inelastic, perfectly inelastic — decides how the bodies separate, and powers billiards, crash engineering and particle detectors.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2020)

    TypeMomentumKinetic energySeparationExample
    ElasticConservedConservedBodies separateBilliard balls, gas molecules, atomic collisions
    InelasticConservedPartly lost (heat, sound, deformation)Bodies separateMost real collisions; a dropped ball that bounces lower
    Perfectly inelasticConservedMaximum possible KE lossBodies stick togetherBullet embedding in a block; clay lumps

    Momentum is conserved in all three (isolated system); kinetic energy only in the first.

    2.1 Elastic collision in one dimension

    For masses m1​,m2​ with initial velocities u1​,u2​ (m_2 at rest is the standard case), solving momentum and KE conservation together gives:

    v1​=m1​+m2​(m1​−m2​)u1​+2m2​u2​​,v2​=m1​+m2​(m1​−m2​)u2​+2m1​u1​​

    With m2​ initially at rest (u2​=0): v1​=m1​+m2​m1​−m2​​u1​,v2​=m1​+m2​2m1​​u1​

    Special cases (u₂ = 0):

    MassesResult
    m1​=m2​Velocities exchange: v1​=0, v2​=u1​
    m1​≫m2​m1​ continues nearly unchanged; m2​ shoots off at ≈ 2u1​
    m1​≪m2​m1​ rebounds at ≈ −u1​; m2​ barely moves

    2.2 Perfectly inelastic collision

    Bodies stick: common final velocity v=m1​+m2​m1​u1​+m2​u2​​. KE lost = initial KE − final KE — the maximum possible consistent with momentum conservation.

    Worked numerical (4m, 2022): A bullet of mass 0.02 kg moving at 500 m/s embeds in a block of mass 4.98 kg at rest. Find the velocity of the system and the kinetic energy lost. v=5.000.02×500​=2 m/s. KEi​=21​(0.02)(5002)=2500 J; KEf​=21​(5)(4)=10 J. KE lost = 2490 J (99.6%) — momentum kept, energy mostly gone.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topics 11–12: momentum conservation is the universal collision law; this topic adds the energy bookkeeping.
    • To Topic 20: lost KE becomes heat — non-conservative dissipation inside the collision.
    • To Kinetic Theory (Unit 13): gas molecules collide elastically — the model's foundation.
    • To real life: car crumple zones maximise KE loss safely; neutron moderation in reactors exploits m1​=m2​ velocity exchange (hydrogen moderators).

  • 4. Exam Orientation and Previous Year Questions (NEB)

    4.1 Verified PYQs from the solved-paper bank

    4.2 Model answers

    Q1 (3m, 2020): In an elastic collision both total momentum and total kinetic energy are conserved and the bodies separate after impact — e.g. collisions between billiard balls or gas molecules. In an inelastic collision momentum is conserved but part of the kinetic energy converts to heat, sound or deformation, and the bodies may or may not separate — e.g. a ball bouncing to a lower height. In the limiting perfectly inelastic case the bodies stick together and move with a common velocity — e.g. a bullet embedding in a block — with the maximum possible loss of kinetic energy.

    Q2 (4m, 2022): see worked numerical in §2.2 — common velocity 2 m/s; 2490 J of the original 2500 J becomes heat and deformation.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Claiming momentum is conserved only in elastic collisions — it is conserved in all of them.
    2. Using the elastic-collision formulas for a sticking collision.
    3. Forgetting the sign of v1​ when m1​<m2​ (the projectile rebounds).
    4. Computing "energy lost" as KEi​ instead of KEi​−KEf​.
    5. Ignoring that the final mass in embedding problems is the sum.

Confusion

  • Momentum vs KE conservation: momentum survives every isolated collision; KE survives only elastic ones — the lost KE becomes heat and deformation.
  • Elastic vs 'bouncy': a real bouncy ball's collision is inelastic (it rebounds lower); only idealised collisions are elastic.

Practice

  • Why does a neutron slow down fastest colliding with hydrogen?

Universal Facts

  • Gas molecules collide almost perfectly elastically — without that, the kinetic theory's pressure predictions would fail.

Examples

  • Bullet in block — 0.02 kg at 500 m/s into 4.98 kg: v = 2 m/s, 2490 J lost (NEB 2022).

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (4m): A 2 kg ball at 6 m/s collides elastically head-on with a 2 kg ball at rest. Find the final velocities. — Equal masses exchange velocities: the first stops (v₁ = 0), the second moves off at 6 m/s. Momentum (12 kg·m/s) and KE (36 J) both conserved.

Formulas

    • Elastic: v₁ = [(m₁−m₂)u₁ + 2m₂u₂]/(m₁+m₂); v₂ = [(m₁−m₂)u₂ + 2m₁u₁]/(m₁+m₂)
    • u₂ = 0: v₁ = (m₁−m₂)u₁/(m₁+m₂); v₂ = 2m₁u₁/(m₁+m₂)
    • Perfectly inelastic: v = (m₁u₁+m₂u₂)/(m₁+m₂)
    • KE lost = KE_i − KE_f

Key Points

  • All collisions conserve momentum; only elastic ones conserve KE.
  • Elastic (u₂ = 0): v₁ = (m₁−m₂)u₁/(m₁+m₂); v₂ = 2m₁u₁/(m₁+m₂); equal masses exchange velocities.
  • Perfectly inelastic: v = (m₁u₁+m₂u₂)/(m₁+m₂); maximum KE loss.

Summary

Collisions divide by what survives. Momentum is always conserved in an isolated collision; kinetic energy only in elastic ones. Elastic collisions (billiard balls, gas molecules) obey v1​=m1​+m2​(m1​−m2​)u1​+2m2​u2​​ — with equal masses the velocities simply exchange, a light projectile rebounds off a heavy target, and a heavy one ploughs through sending the light target off at nearly double speed. Inelastic collisions lose part of the KE to heat, sound and deformation; in the perfectly inelastic limit the bodies stick and move at v=m1​+m2​m1​u1​+m2​u2​​ — a 0.02 kg bullet embedding in a 4.98 kg block leaves the pair at 2 m/s, having converted 2490 J of its 2500 J into heat (NEB 2022).

Special Notes

  • In a perfectly inelastic collision, the objects do NOT lose ALL their kinetic energy — only the KE relative to the center of mass can be lost.
  • The coefficient of restitution e depends on the materials, not on the velocities of the colliding bodies.
  • In an elastic collision between equal masses where one is initially at rest, the masses exchange velocities.
  • Momentum is conserved in ALL collisions (elastic and inelastic) as long as no external force acts.
  • NEB collision problems: always check whether the collision is elastic (use both conservation laws) or inelastic (use only momentum conservation).

Important Statements

  • "Momentum always balances; kinetic energy only sometimes."

Important Notes

Exam Notes

Nepali

Notes
  • प्रत्यास्थ टक्कर (Elastic collision): एउटी टक्कर जहाँ कुल संवेग (momentum) र कुल गतिज ऊर्जा (kinetic energy) दुवै संरक्षित रहन्छ। पिण्डहरू स्थायी विकृति बिना प्रतिघात गर्छन्, शब्द/ताप उत्पादन हुन्न। सापेक्ष वेगको पहुँच = सापेक्ष वेगको अलगाव: v₂ - v₁ = u₁ - u₂।
  • अप्रत्यास्थ टक्कर (Inelastic collision): एउटी टक्कर जहाँ संवेग संरक्षित हुन्छ तर गतिज ऊर्जा संरक्षित हुँदैन। केही गतिज ऊर्जा आन्तरिक ऊर्जामा (ताप, शब्द, विकृति) परिवर्तित हुन्छ। प्रत्यास्थता गुणांक e < 1।
  • पूर्ण अप्रत्यास्थ टक्कर (Perfectly inelastic collision): चरम अवस्था जहाँ टक्कर खाने पिण्डहरू टक्करपछि एकसाथ रहन्छन् र सामान्य वेगसँग चल्छन्। संवेग संरक्षित हुन्छ; गतिज ऊर्जाको नोक्सान अधिकतम (तर १००% होइन किनकि संवेग संरक्षित हुनुपर्छ)।
Important Statements
  • प्रत्यास्थ टक्करमा संवेग र गतिज ऊर्जा दुवै संरक्षित रहन्छ।
  • अप्रत्यास्थ टक्करमा संवेग मात्र संरक्षित हुन्छ, गतिज ऊर्जा हुँदैन।
  • प्रत्यास्थता गुणांक e = (अलगावको सापेक्ष वेग) / (पहुँचको सापेक्ष वेग)।
  • केन्द्रको द्रव्यमान फ्रेममा कुल संवेग शून्य हुन्छ — यो 2D टक्कर विश्लेषण सरल बनाउँछ।

Grammar

Notes
  • Collision (noun): an instance of objects striking together. Plural: collisions.
  • Elastic (adjective): able to resume shape after deformation; in physics, KE-conserving.
  • Inelastic (adjective): not elastic; in physics, KE not conserved.
  • Coefficient of restitution (noun phrase): ratio of separation speed to approach speed.
Important Statements
  • In physics, 'elastic' means kinetic energy is conserved, not just that objects bounce back.
  • The term 'perfectly inelastic' means maximum kinetic energy loss consistent with momentum conservation.

MCQS

1.

In which collisions is momentum conserved?

Options
  • Elastic only
  • Inelastic only
  • All isolated collisions
  • None
Answer

C

Explanation

Momentum conservation needs only isolation — not elasticity.

2.

In an elastic collision between equal masses (one at rest), the projectile:

Options
  • Rebounds
  • Stops and transfers all velocity
  • Continues at half speed
  • Doubles speed
Answer

B

Explanation

Equal masses exchange velocities.

3.

A 0.02 kg bullet at 500 m/s embeds in a 4.98 kg block. Common velocity:

Options
  • 1 m/s
  • 2 m/s
  • 5 m/s
  • 10 m/s
Answer

B

Explanation

v = (0.02×500)/5 = 2 m/s (NEB 2022).

4.

The KE lost in that collision is about:

Options
  • 10 J
  • 490 J
  • 2490 J
  • 2500 J
Answer

C

Explanation

2500 − 10 = 2490 J — 99.6% dissipated.

5.

A very light body elastically hits a very heavy one at rest. It:

Options
  • Continues forward
  • Rebounds with nearly the same speed
  • Stops
  • Carries the heavy body with it
Answer

B

Explanation

m₁ ≪ m₂ → v₁ ≈ −u₁.

6.

In a perfectly inelastic collision the bodies:

Options
  • Separate elastically
  • Stick together
  • Exchange velocities
  • Lose all momentum
Answer

B

Explanation

Common final velocity — maximum KE loss.

7.

Which is approximately elastic in practice?

Options
  • Car crash
  • Clay balls colliding
  • Gas molecules colliding
  • Bullet in sand
Answer

C

Explanation

Molecular collisions are nearly perfectly elastic.

8.

The maximum KE loss consistent with momentum conservation occurs when:

Options
  • The collision is elastic
  • The bodies stick together
  • Masses are equal
  • The collision is one-dimensional
Answer

B

Explanation

Perfectly inelastic — common velocity minimises final KE.
