Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Work Becomes Motion, Position Becomes Stored Work

    The work–energy theorem is mechanics' accounting identity: net work = change in kinetic energy. Kinetic energy is motion's energy; potential energy is position's stored work. Together they set up conservation (next topic).

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    QuantityDefinitionFormula
    Kinetic energyEnergy of motionKE=21​mv2
    Potential energy (gravitational)Energy of position in a gravity fieldPE=mgh
    Potential energy (elastic)Energy stored in a deformed springPE=21​kx2
    Work–energy theoremNet work on a body equals its change in kinetic energyWnet​=ΔKE=21​mv2−21​mu2

    2.1 Proof of the work–energy theorem for a constant force (NEB 2023, 2024 — 5m)

    Consider a body of mass m accelerated from u to v over displacement s by a constant net force F:

    1. Newton's second law: F=ma.
    2. Kinematic equation (no time): v2=u2+2as→a=2sv2−u2​.
    3. Substitute: F=m2sv2−u2​.
    4. Work done: W=Fs=m2sv2−u2​⋅s=21​mv2−21​mu2.

    Wnet​=ΔKE​ The net work done on a body equals the change in its kinetic energy. ∎

    2.2 Worked numericals

    Q (4m, 2023): A body of mass 4 kg is released from rest and falls freely through 10 m (g = 9.8). Find its kinetic energy and speed on reaching the ground. Speed: v=2gh​=2×9.8×10​=196​=14 m/s. KE: 21​mv2=21​×4×196=392 J (= mgh = 4 × 9.8 × 10 = 392 J ✓). KE = 392 J, v = 14 m/s.

    Q: A 1000 kg car brakes from 20 m/s to rest. Find the braking work. W=ΔKE=0−21​(1000)(400)=−2×105 J — negative work by the brakes (energy leaves as heat).

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 16: the theorem is the definition of work applied to the net force.
    • To Topic 19: PE is work stored reversibly; conservation equates mgh to ½mv².
    • To Topic 9 (free fall): v=2gh​ is the energy result wearing kinematics' clothes.
    • To real life: braking distances, crash energies and spring-loaded devices all price through 21​mv2 and 21​kx2.

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Using W=mas with a from the wrong kinematic equation.
    2. Forgetting "net" work — gravity plus friction plus applied forces all enter.
    3. Mixing up KE's mass dependence: doubling speed quadruples KE.
    4. Dropping the squared terms in the derivation's final step.
    5. In the falling-body numerical, forgetting to check KE = mgh as a cross-check.

Confusion

  • KE vs momentum: KE = p²/2m — doubling v quadruples KE but only doubles p; the theorem uses KE because work is a scalar.
  • PE reference level: mgh depends on the chosen zero of height — only changes in PE are physical.

Practice

  • Why does the theorem use kinetic energy rather than momentum?

Universal Facts

  • A 1-tonne car at 30 m/s carries 450 kJ — the reason crash barriers are engineered like energy absorbers.

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (4m): A 2 kg ball at 10 m/s is stopped by a net force over 5 m. Find the work done and the average force. — ΔKE = 0 − ½(2)(100) = −100 J (the force does −100 J); F = W/s = −100/5 = −20 N (opposing motion).

Formulas

    • W_net = ΔKE = ½mv² − ½mu²
    • KE = ½mv²; PE = mgh; PE_spring = ½kx²
    • v = √(2gh)

Key Points

  • WET: W_net = ΔKE = ½mv² − ½mu² (constant force proof via v² = u² + 2as).
  • KE = ½mv²; PE = mgh; elastic PE = ½kx².
  • Falling body: v = √(2gh), KE = mgh — energy cross-check.

Summary

The work–energy theorem states that the net work done on a body equals its change in kinetic energy: Wnet​=21​mv2−21​mu2. The constant-force proof is four lines: F=ma; the kinematic relation v2=u2+2as; substitute to get F=m(v2−u2)/2s; multiply by s (NEB 2023, 2024 — 5m). Kinetic energy 21​mv2 prices motion — doubling speed quadruples it; gravitational potential energy mgh and elastic 21​kx2 store work reversibly. A 4 kg body falling 10 m lands at 14 m/s carrying 392 J — exactly the mgh it started with (NEB 2023). Braking a 1000 kg car from 20 m/s extracts 2 × 10⁵ J of negative work.

MCQS

1.

The work-energy theorem states:

Options
  • W = Δp
  • W_net = ΔKE
  • W = ΔPE only
  • W = F/t
Answer

B

Explanation

Net work equals the change in kinetic energy (NEB 2020, 2023, 2024).

2.

A 4 kg body falls freely 10 m (g = 9.8). Its KE at the ground is:

Options
  • 196 J
  • 392 J
  • 400 J
  • 98 J
Answer

B

Explanation

KE = mgh = 4 × 9.8 × 10 = 392 J (NEB 2023).

3.

Its speed on impact is:

Options
  • 10 m/s
  • 14 m/s
  • 19.6 m/s
  • 28 m/s
Answer

B

Explanation

v = √(2gh) = √196 = 14 m/s.

4.

Doubling a body's speed multiplies its KE by:

Options
  • 2
  • 4
  • 8
  • √2
Answer

B

Explanation

KE ∝ v² — doubling v gives 4× the energy.

5.

In the WET proof, the kinematic equation used is:

Options
  • v = u + at
  • s = ut + ½at²
  • v² = u² + 2as
  • s = (u+v)t/2
Answer

C

Explanation

The time-free equation connects v, u, a, s.

6.

The elastic PE of a spring stretched by x is:

Options
  • kx
  • kx²
  • ½kx²
  • ½kx
Answer

C

Explanation

∫kx dx = ½kx².

7.

Braking work for a 1000 kg car from 20 m/s to rest is:

Options
  • +2×10⁵ J
  • −2×10⁵ J
  • +4×10⁵ J
  • −10⁴ J
Answer

B

Explanation

ΔKE = 0 − ½(1000)(400) = −2×10⁵ J.

8.

PE depends on the chosen reference level, so only:

Options
  • Absolute PE matters
  • Changes in PE are physical
  • PE is always positive
  • PE is a vector
Answer

B

Explanation

The zero of height is arbitrary; ΔPE is invariant.
