Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Walking Uphill Without Effort

    Move a charge along an equipotential surface and you do no work — like walking along a contour line at constant altitude. Equipotentials turn energy thinking into geometry: where surfaces crowd, fields are strong; where they are flat, nothing pushes.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Definition

    Equipotential surfaces are surfaces (real or imaginary) over which the electric potential is constant. Every point on one has the same V — a contour map of the electric landscape.

    2.2 Properties (NEB 2024, 3m)

    1. No work is done in moving a charge along an equipotential surface (W=qΔV=0).
    2. Field lines always cross equipotential surfaces at right angles — the field has no component along the surface.
    3. Two equipotential surfaces never intersect — one point cannot have two potential values.
    4. Surfaces are crowded where the field is strong, widely spaced where weak.
    5. A conductor's surface in equilibrium is an equipotential; the whole interior is at one potential.

    2.3 Standard pictures

    • Point charge: concentric spheres (V = kq/r), field radial.
    • Uniform field (parallel plates): equally spaced parallel planes perpendicular to E.
    • Dipole: lobed surfaces symmetric about the axis.

    2.4 Worked reasoning

    Q (3m, 2024): What are equipotential surfaces? Write any three of their properties. — Definition + any three properties from §2.2.

    Q (NEB-style): Why must field lines meet equipotential surfaces at 90°? If a field line had a component along the surface, moving a charge along that component would change potential — contradicting the surface being equipotential. Hence E ⊥ surface.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 90: the surface is the locus V = constant of the potential field.
    • To Topic 92: E = −dV/dr — zero component along the surface is the same statement geometrically.
    • To conductors: the entire conductor in equilibrium is one equipotential region.

Key Points

Summary

Equipotential surfaces are loci of constant potential. Their properties: moving a charge along one does no work (W=qΔV=0); field lines cross them at right angles (any tangential field component would change V); two surfaces never intersect (one point, two potentials — impossible); crowding marks strong fields; and a conductor's whole interior is a single equipotential region (NEB 2024, 3m). Point charges give concentric spherical equipotentials; parallel plates give evenly spaced planes. The geometry is the visual form of E = −dV/dr.

Special Notes

  • Inside a charged conductor there is no field direction, so the concept of perpendicularity does not apply there; the whole cavity is simply at the constant potential of the conductor's surface.
  • For a dipole, the V = 0 equipotential surface is the perpendicular bisector plane of the axis — the only region in the dipole's field where V is exactly zero, though E is non-zero there.

Mcs

1.

The work done in moving a charge q from one equipotential surface to another surface at a higher potential is:

Options
  • zero
  • qΔV
  • negative qΔV
  • independent of q
Answer

B

Explanation

W = qΔV where ΔV is the potential difference between the two surfaces. This is non-zero because the surfaces are not equipotential with each other.

2.

Equipotential surfaces of a point charge are:

Options
  • planes
  • concentric spheres
  • cylinders
  • paraboloids
Answer

B

Explanation

Since V = kQ/r, V is constant for a fixed value of r, which is the equation of a sphere centred on the charge. Hence equipotentials are concentric spheres.

3.

The spacing between two consecutive equipotential surfaces in a region is small. This indicates that the electric field in that region is:

Options
  • zero
  • very large
  • moderate and constant
  • perpendicular to the surfaces
Answer

B

Explanation

A small separation for a fixed potential difference means a large gradient, E = -ΔV/Δs, so the field is very large in that region.

Important Concepts

  • Equipotentials are contour lines of the electric landscape — closer spacing means steeper potential, stronger field.

  • Calculate work and potential energy differences between two equipotential surfaces.
