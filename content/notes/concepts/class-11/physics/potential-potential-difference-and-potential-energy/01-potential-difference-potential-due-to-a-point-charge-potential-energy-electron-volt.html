  • 1. Topic Introduction — The Energy Landscape of Charge

    Electric potential is electrostatics' energy language: instead of asking how strongly a charge is pushed, we ask how much work it takes to move it. Volts, electron-volts and the kq/r formula make field problems as easy as bookkeeping.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2020, 2021, 2022, 2023, 2024)

    2.1 Potential and potential difference

    • Electric potential at a point: the work done per unit positive charge in bringing it from infinity to that point without acceleration:

    V=qW​SI unit: volt (V) = J C−1

    • Potential difference between two points: work per unit charge moving between them — 1 volt = 1 joule per coulomb (NEB 2023, 1m MCQ).
    • Work–charge relation: W=qV; for n electrons, W=neV (NEB 2022, 4m).

    2.2 Potential due to a point charge

    V=rkq​=4πε0​rq​ — a scalar, so potentials from several charges add algebraically (signs included). Zero taken at infinity.

    2.3 Potential energy of a pair of charges

    U=rkq1​q2​​ — negative for unlike charges (bound system), positive for like charges (work needed to assemble).

    2.4 The electron volt

    1 eV = the kinetic energy gained by an electron crossing a potential difference of 1 V: 1 eV=e×1 V=1.6×10−19 J

    2.5 Worked numericals

    Q (2m, 2024): Calculate the electric potential at 0.5 m from a point charge of 2 μC in air. V=rkq​=0.59×109×2×10−6​=3.6×104 V=36 kV

    Q (3m, 2020): Find the work done in moving a charge of 2×10⁻¹⁹ C through 300 V; express in electron volts.

    • W=qV=2×10−19×300=6.0×10−17 J
    • W=1.6×10−196.0×10−17​=375 eV

    Q (3m, 2024): Two charges +3 μC and −2 μC are 0.3 m apart. Calculate the potential energy. U=rkq1​q2​​=0.39×109×3×10−6×(−2×10−6)​=−0.18 J — negative: work must be done to separate them; opposite charges bind.

    Q (2m, 2024): Define the electron-volt; convert 1 eV into joules. — see §2.4.

    4.2 Common examination mistakes

    1. Adding potentials as vectors — potential is a scalar; add algebraically with signs.
    2. Confusing eV with joules: 1 eV = 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J.
    3. Writing V = kq/r² — that is E; potential goes as 1/r.

Confusion

  • Potential is a scalar: add kq/r values with signs, no directions needed.
  • E goes as 1/r²; V goes as 1/r — don't mix the two formulas.

Summary

Electric potential is work per unit positive charge brought from infinity: V=W/q, in volts (1 V = 1 J/C — NEB 2023 MCQ). The potential difference moves charge between points: W=qV, or W=neV for n electrons (NEB 2022, 4m). A point charge gives V=kq/r — a scalar — so 2 μC at 0.5 m produces 36 kV (NEB 2024, 2m). The potential energy of a pair is U=kq1​q2​/r: −0.18 J for +3 μC and −2 μC at 0.3 m, negative because opposite charges bind (NEB 2024, 3m). The electron-volt, the energy an electron gains crossing 1 V, equals 1.6×10⁻¹⁹ J (NEB 2024, 2m) — the bridge between circuit volts and atomic energies.

Exercises

1.

Find the potential 0.30 m from a point charge of 2.0 μC. Use k = 9.0 × 10^9. Then find the potential energy of an electron there. Use e = 1.6 × 10^−19 C.

Steps
  • V = k Q / r = 9.0 × 10^9 × 2.0 × 10^−6 / 0.30.
  • k Q = 1.8 × 10^4, so V = 6.0 × 10^4 V.
  • The electron charge is negative.
  • U = (−1.6 × 10^−19)(6.0 × 10^4) = −9.6 × 10^−15 J.
  • Potential has no direction. The force on the electron points toward the positive charge.
Answer

V = 6.0 × 10^4 V. The electron's potential energy is −9.6 × 10^−15 J.
