Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — From Potential to Field

    The electric field and the potential are two views of one thing: E is the slope of V. Where potential drops fastest, the field is strongest — a relation that turns every potential map into a force map, and explains why the inside of a charged shell is field-free.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 The relation E = −dV/dr

    The potential gradient is the rate of change of potential with distance: E=−drdV​unit: V/m≡N/C

    • The minus sign: the field points in the direction of decreasing potential — from high V to low V.
    • Magnitude: the steepest slope of the potential landscape.
    • For a uniform field between plates: E=dV​ — potential difference per unit separation.

    2.2 Statement form (NEB 2024, 3m)

    Electric potential is work per unit charge from infinity (SI unit volt); the relation between field and potential is E=−dV/dr — the field points down the steepest potential slope. NEB 2024 asked exactly this trio: definition, unit, relation.

    2.3 Worked reasoning and numericals

    Q (3m, 2024): Show that the electric potential everywhere inside a uniformly charged hollow sphere equals its surface value.

    • Inside the shell E = 0 (Gauss's law — no enclosed charge).
    • Since E=−dV/dr: zero field means dV/dr=0 → V is constant throughout the interior.
    • Continuity at the surface forces that constant to be V=4πε0​RQ​ — the surface value. The interior is one equipotential region.

    Q (NEB-style): Between plates 2 cm apart at 500 V, find E. E=dV​=0.02500​=2.5×104 V/m, directed from the positive to the negative plate.

    Q (NEB-style): Potential varies as V = 2x² volt along x. Find E at x = 1 m. E=−dxdV​=−4x → at x = 1: E = −4 V/m (4 V/m in the −x direction).

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 90: integrating E gives V; differentiating V recovers E.
    • To Topic 91: perpendicularity of field lines and equipotentials is E = −dV/dr drawn as geometry.
    • To capacitors (Unit 24): E = V/d links plate voltage to field.

Summary

The potential gradient links the two electric maps: E=−drdV​ (V/m ≡ N/C) — the field points in the direction of decreasing potential, with magnitude equal to the steepest slope. NEB 2024 asked for the definition of potential, its unit and this relation together (3m), and used it to show that inside a charged hollow sphere E = 0 ⇒ dV/dr = 0 ⇒ V is constant at its surface value Q/4πε₀R (3m). For uniform fields the relation reduces to E = V/d — 2.5×10⁴ V/m between plates 2 cm apart at 500 V. Field lines ⊥ equipotentials, sparks at sharp points, and shielding all follow from this one derivative.

Special Notes

  • The relation E = -∇V holds only for electrostatic fields. In a time-varying magnetic field, the generalized relation is E = -∇V - dA/dt, so potential alone no longer determines the field (Faraday's law).
  • Wherever the potential is constant, the potential gradient and hence the electric field are exactly zero. This is why the interior of a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is field-free even though its potential is finite.

Important Statements

  • "The electric field is equal to the negative gradient of the electric potential."

Important Notes

  • Verified PYQs: potential + unit + E–V relation (2024, 3m); hollow sphere interior potential (2024, 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "E = V/d for plates; E = −dV/dr in general."

Exam Notes

  • Quote the minus sign's meaning — 'from high to low potential'.

Exercises

1.

The potential falls by 20 V over 5.0 cm in the direction of the field. Find E.

Steps
  • The fall is along the field, so the magnitude is ΔV/Δx.
  • Δx = 0.050 m.
  • E = 20 / 0.050 = 400 V/m.
  • The direction is the direction of the falling potential.
  • 1 V/m is the same as 1 N/C.
Answer

400 V/m, toward lower potential.
