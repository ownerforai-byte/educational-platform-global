Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Solids Grow When Warm

    Railway tracks buckle in summer, bridges have toothed expansion joints, and a pendulum clock keeps different time in winter and summer. All trace to one simple law: a solid's length grows in proportion to its original length and its temperature rise.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2019, 2021, 2022, 2024)

    2.1 Coefficient of linear expansion

    Definition: the fractional increase in length per degree rise in temperature: α=ΔTΔL/L​⇒ΔL=αLΔT,LT​=L0​(1+αΔT) SI unit: K⁻¹ (or °C⁻¹ — the same size interval). Typical values: steel 12×10⁻⁶, aluminium 24×10⁻⁶, invar 1.2×10⁻⁶ K⁻¹.

    2.2 Measurement of α (comparison method)

    A rod is supported on a horizontal frame with one end fixed; a micrometer screw / optical lever at the other end measures the tiny extension ΔL when the rod's temperature rises by ΔT (measured with a thermocouple or thermometer). Then α=ΔL/(LΔT).

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (2m, 2019): Define the coefficient of linear expansion. A metal rod is 1 m long at 0 °C and 1.0017 m at 100 °C. Find α.

    • Definition as above. α=1×1001.0017−1​=1.7×10−5 K⁻¹

    Q (3m, 2021): A metal rod of length 1 m at 20 °C is heated to 100 °C. If α = 1.2×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, find the increase in length and the final length.

    • ΔL=αLΔT=1.2×10−5×1×80=9.6×10−4 m = 0.96 mm
    • L=1.00096 m

    Q (2m, 2024): A steel rod 2 m long at 20 °C is heated to 120 °C. By how much does it expand? (α = 12×10⁻⁶ K⁻¹)

    • ΔL=12×10−6×2×100=2.4×10−3 m = 2.4 mm

    2.4 Pendulum clock (NEB 2024)

    Q (2m, 2024): A pendulum clock keeps correct time in winter but loses time in summer. Explain why. The pendulum's length increases in summer (LT​=L0​(1+αΔT)), so its period T=2πL/g​ increases — each swing takes longer and the clock runs slow (loses time). In winter L decreases and it gains.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 47: β=2α (superficial), γ=3α (cubical) — one coefficient generates all three.
    • To SHM/pendulum: period depends on length — thermal expansion perturbs timekeeping.
    • To engineering: expansion joints, bimetallic strips (thermostats), gaps in rails.

Confusion

  • α's unit is K⁻¹, not K — it is per-degree fractional change.
  • Clocks lose time in summer because longer pendulum → longer period → slower ticking.

Formulas

    • ΔL = αLΔT; L_T = L₀(1 + αΔT)
    • α = ΔL/(LΔT)
    • T_pendulum = 2π√(L/g)

Summary

Solids expand on heating according to ΔL=αLΔT, where the coefficient of linear expansion α (K⁻¹) is the fractional length increase per degree (NEB 2019, 2022 definitions). It is measured by fixing one end of a rod and reading the tiny extension at the other with a micrometer screw or optical lever while the temperature rises by ΔT. NEB's numericals: α = 1.7×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹ from a 1 m rod growing 1.7 mm per 100 °C (2019); a 1 m rod over 80 °C gains 0.96 mm (2021); a 2 m steel rod over 100 °C gains 2.4 mm (2024). Consequences are everywhere — and a pendulum clock loses time in summer because T=2πL/g​ lengthens with the rod (NEB 2024).
