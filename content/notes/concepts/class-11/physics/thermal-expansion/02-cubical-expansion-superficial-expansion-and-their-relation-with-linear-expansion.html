Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Expanding in Two and Three Dimensions

    A heated plate grows in area; a heated block grows in volume. Because every edge obeys the same linear law, the area and volume coefficients follow automatically: β = 2α and γ = 3α. One measurement of α pins down the whole thermal fingerprint.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Superficial (area) expansion

    A plate of area A0​ heated by ΔT: AT​=A0​(1+βΔT),β=ΔTΔA/A0​​ β = coefficient of superficial expansion, unit K⁻¹.

    Relation β = 2α: a square of side L0​ expands to L=L0​(1+αΔT), so AT​=L2=L02​(1+αΔT)2≈A0​(1+2αΔT) (neglecting the tiny α²ΔT² term) — hence β = 2α.

    2.2 Cubical (volume) expansion

    A solid of volume V0​ heated by ΔT: VT​=V0​(1+γΔT),γ=ΔTΔV/V0​​

    Relation γ = 3α: a cube of side L0​: VT​=L03​(1+αΔT)3≈V0​(1+3αΔT)⇒γ=3α

    Ratio chain: α:β:γ=1:2:3.

    2.3 Worked numericals

    Q (NEB-style): A steel plate has area 2 m² at 20 °C (α = 12×10⁻⁶ K⁻¹). Find its area at 120 °C.

    • β=2α=2.4×10−5 K⁻¹; ΔT = 100
    • ΔA=βAΔT=2.4×10−5×2×100=4.8×10−3 m²
    • A=2.0048 m²

    Q (NEB-style): The volume of a lead sphere is 1 dm³ at 0 °C (α = 2.9×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹). Find its volume at 100 °C.

    • γ=3α=8.7×10−5 K⁻¹
    • ΔV=γVΔT=8.7×10−5×10−3×100=8.7×10−6 m³
    • V=1.0000087 dm³ ≈ 1.0000087×10⁻³ m³

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 46: everything derives from ΔL = αLΔT.
    • To Topic 48: liquids have only cubical expansion (no fixed shape).
    • To density: heated solid's density falls as ρT​=ρ0​/(1+γΔT).

  • 4. Exam Orientation and Previous Year Questions (NEB)

    4.1 Verified PYQs from the solved-paper bank

    No verified PYQ for this exact topic in the bank; the related linear-expansion questions (2019–2024) belong to Topic 46. Practise the β = 2α, γ = 3α derivations and area/volume numericals as NEB-style practice.

    4.2 Likely question forms

    1. Show that β = 2α (3m).
    2. Show that γ = 3α (3m).
    3. Area or volume numerical (3m).

    4.3 Common examination mistakes

    1. Forgetting to drop the α²ΔT² (and α³ΔT³) terms in the binomial expansion.
    2. Using α where β or γ is needed.
    3. Writing the ratio as 1:3:2.

Specific Conditions

  • Isotropic Material: γ = 3α and β = 2α only for isotropic solids. Crystalline anisotropic materials have different α along different axes.
  • Small ΔT: Formulas assume constant α over temperature range. For large ΔT, α may vary.
  • Solid Container: For liquid expansion, container expansion must be accounted for to get real expansion.

Formulas

    • A = A₀(1+βΔT); β = 2α
    • V = V₀(1+γΔT); γ = 3α
    • α : β : γ = 1 : 2 : 3

Key Points

  • β = 2α; γ = 3α; A = A₀(1+βΔT); V = V₀(1+γΔT).

Important Statements

  • "The coefficient of cubical expansion is three times the coefficient of linear expansion."

Important Notes

  • No verified PYQ; NEB-style practice (derivations 3m, numericals 3m).

Exam Short Tricks

  • "Need ΔA? Use 2α. Need ΔV? Use 3α."

Exam Notes

  • The β = 2α and γ = 3α derivations are the likely 3m questions.

Examples

  • Steel plate — 2 m² over 100 °C: ΔA = 4.8×10⁻³ m².

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): A copper cube of volume 500 cm³ at 0 °C is heated to 200 °C (α = 1.7×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹). Find ΔV. — γ = 5.1×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹; ΔV = 5.1×10⁻⁵ × 500×10⁻⁶ × 200 = 5.1×10⁻⁹ m³ = 5.1 cm³... check: 5.1×10⁻⁵×5×10⁻⁴×200 = 5.1×10⁻⁶ m³ = 5.1 cm³.

Practice

  • Why does a tight metal lid loosen under hot water?

Universal Facts

  • A heated hole in a plate expands too — the hole behaves as if made of the surrounding material.

Confusion

  • The 1:2:3 chain: α : β : γ = 1 : 2 : 3 — memorise it and half the numericals solve themselves.
  • Binomial truncation: (1+αΔT)² ≈ 1+2αΔT only because αΔT is tiny.

Water shows anomalous expansion:

Options
  • Expands on heating from 0°C
  • Contracts on heating from 0°C to 4°C
  • Always expands
  • Never contracts

Water contracts from 0°C to 4°C (density maximum at 4°C).

B

Valid for isotropic solids where expansion is same in all directions.

Summary

Every edge of a heated solid obeys ΔL=αLΔT, so area and volume follow automatically: A=A0​(1+2αΔT) and V=V0​(1+3αΔT) — the binomial's higher terms vanish because αΔT is minuscule. Hence the superficial coefficient β = 2α and the cubical coefficient γ = 3α, with the ratio chain α:β:γ=1:2:3. A 2 m² steel plate over 100 °C gains 4.8×10⁻³ m² of area; a 1 dm³ lead sphere over 100 °C gains 8.7 mm³ of volume. Even holes expand as if filled with surrounding material — a hot lid loosens, never tightens.
