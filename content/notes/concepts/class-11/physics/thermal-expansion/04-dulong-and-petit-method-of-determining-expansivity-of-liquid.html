Notes

  • Dulong and Petit's Method: Experimental method to determine absolute coefficient of expansion of a liquid using a U-tube with two limbs at different temperatures. One limb (hot) and one limb (cold) contain the same liquid; columns balance when pressures equal.
  • Principle: Pressure at same horizontal level in continuous liquid is equal. For two columns of heights h₁ (cold, t₁) and h₂ (hot, t₂) with densities ρ₁, ρ₂: h₁ρ₁g = h₂ρ₂g. Using ρ₂ = ρ₁/(1 + γΔt): derive γ = (h₁ - h₂)/(h₂·t₂ - h₁·t₁).
  • Formula: γ = (h₁ - h₂)/(h₂t₂ - h₁t₁) where h₁ = cold column height, h₂ = hot column height, t₁ = cold temperature, t₂ = hot temperature.
  • Absolute vs Apparent: Dulong-Petit gives absolute expansivity (liquid relative to vacuum) since the glass expansion cancels out in the pressure balance method.
  • Experimental Setup: U-tube with one limb in ice bath (0°C) and other in steam bath or heated bath. Adjust levels until balance, measure heights and temperatures.
  • Corrections: Account for vapor pressure of liquid, capillary effects, and non-uniform temperature along columns.
  • Applications: Used for liquids like water, mercury, alcohol where accurate expansivity needed for thermometer design and thermal engineering.

Specific Conditions

  • Liquid Columns in Communication: Two limbs must contain same liquid connected at bottom — pressure equality at bottom.
  • Different Temperatures: One limb maintained at reference (e.g., 0°C), other at known higher temperature.
  • No Flow: Static equilibrium — liquid not flowing, columns stationary.

Formulas

  • Pressure balance: h₁ρ₁g = h₂ρ₂g
  • Density relation: ρ₂ = ρ₁/(1 + γΔt)
  • Expansivity: γ = (h₁ - h₂)/(h₂t₂ - h₁t₁)
  • Absolute expansivity: γ_absolute = γ_apparent + γ_glass

Key Points

  • Dulong-Petit determines absolute liquid expansivity using U-tube at two temperatures.
  • Pressure equality: h₁ρ₁ = h₂ρ₂ at same level.
  • Formula: γ = (h₁ - h₂)/(h₂t₂ - h₁t₁).
  • Method eliminates container expansion effect.
  • Requires two temperature baths and height measurements.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: Dulong-Petit method uses a U-tube with liquid columns at two different temperatures.
  • Statement 2: Pressure balance at bottom: h₁ρ₁g = h₂ρ₂g.
  • Statement 3: Absolute expansivity γ = (h₁ - h₂)/(h₂t₂ - h₁t₁).
  • Statement 4: Method gives absolute expansivity independent of container expansion.
  • Statement 5: One limb at reference temperature (0°C), other at measured higher temperature.

Important Notes

  • The method determines absolute expansivity — glass expansion doesn't enter the formula.
  • Accuracy depends on precise height and temperature measurements.
  • Vapor pressure and capillary effects must be minimized or corrected.
  • Commonly used for mercury, water, alcohol expansivity determination.

Exam Short Tricks

  • γ = (h₁ - h₂)/(h₂t₂ - h₁t₁) — memorize formula.
  • h₁ = cold (denser, taller column), h₂ = hot (less dense, shorter column).
  • Denominator can be written h₂t₂ - h₁t₁ — check signs.
  • Method gives absolute expansivity directly.

Exam Notes

  • NEB frequently asks to describe Dulong-Petit method with diagram and derive formula.
  • Derive from pressure balance h₁ρ₁ = h₂ρ₂.
  • Explain why method gives absolute (not apparent) expansivity.
  • State experimental procedure and precautions.

Examples

  • h₁ = 100 cm at t₁ = 0°C, h₂ = 96 cm at t₂ = 80°C: γ = (100-96)/(96×80 - 100×0) = 4/7680 = 5.2×10⁻⁴ /°C.
  • Water expansivity ~2.1×10⁻⁴ /°C (20-50°C range), mercury ~1.8×10⁻⁴ /°C.
  • If h₁ = h₂ (equal heights), γ = 0 — no expansion (or temperatures equal).

Universal Facts

  • Dulong and Petit (French physicists, early 19th century) also known for Dulong-Petit law of specific heat (c ≈ 3R for solids).
  • Mercury expansivity ~1.8×10⁻⁴ /°C makes it suitable for thermometers (uniform expansion).
  • Water expansivity varies with temperature — negative below 4°C, positive above.
  • Absolute expansivity needed for designing accurate liquid thermometers.

Confusion

  • Confusing absolute with apparent expansivity: Dulong-Petit gives absolute (no container correction needed).
  • Swapping h₁ and h₂ in formula: h₁ is colder/denser/taller column.
  • Thinking container expansion matters: It cancels in this method.
  • Forgetting temperatures must be different for measurement.
  • Using Celsius temperatures directly — fine since ΔT same in °C and K.
