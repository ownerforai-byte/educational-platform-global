Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Weighing an Invisible Expansion

    Liquids expand far too little to measure directly by eye. Dulong and Petit's genius was to cancel the vessel's contribution: the same liquid in two different vessels shows level rises that differ by exactly the liquid's true expansion. The apparatus turns an apparent effect into an absolute measurement.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts

    2.1 Principle

    A liquid's apparent expansion in a vessel = real expansion − vessel's expansion: γa​=γr​−γg​ In two vessels of cubical coefficients γ1​ and γ2​ (same liquid, same ΔT): γa1​=γr​−γ1​,γa2​=γr​−γ2​ Subtracting: γ2​−γ1​=γa1​−γa2​​ — the difference of apparent coefficients equals the difference of vessel coefficients, independent of the liquid! Measuring the level changes gives the vessel coefficients; then γr​=γa​+γg​ for either vessel.

    2.2 Apparatus and procedure

    1. Two identical bulbs (glass and metal, e.g. iron and copper) joined by a U-tube containing the liquid (historically mercury).
    2. The whole assembly is heated uniformly through ΔT.
    3. Each vessel's liquid column height changes according to its apparent expansion: the difference in column heights directly reads γa1​−γa2​.
    4. With vessel coefficients known (from α of the materials, γ = 3α), the liquid's real coefficient follows: γr​=γa​+γg​.

    2.3 Worked numerical

    Q (NEB-style): In a Dulong–Petit experiment, mercury's apparent coefficients in a glass vessel (γ_g = 2.5×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹) and a copper vessel (γ_Cu = 5.1×10⁻⁵ K⁻¹) are measured. If the apparent coefficient in glass is 1.8×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹, find (a) the real coefficient of mercury, (b) the apparent coefficient in copper.

    • (a) γr​=γa​+γg​=1.8×10−4+2.5×10−5=2.05×10−4 K⁻¹
    • (b) γa,Cu​=γr​−γCu​=2.05×10−4−5.1×10−5=1.54×10−4 K⁻¹

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 48: the experiment operationalises γ_r = γ_a + γ_g.
    • To Topic 47: vessel coefficients come from γ = 3α.
    • To history: Dulong and Petit (1818) also discovered the law of constant atomic heat — a different, famous result.

Specific Conditions

  • Liquid Columns in Communication: Two limbs must contain same liquid connected at bottom — pressure equality at bottom.
  • Different Temperatures: One limb maintained at reference (e.g., 0°C), other at known higher temperature.
  • No Flow: Static equilibrium — liquid not flowing, columns stationary.

Formulas

    • γ_a = γ_r − γ_g
    • γ₂ − γ₁ = γ_{a1} − γ_{a2}
    • γ_g = 3α (vessel material)

Key Points

  • γ_a = γ_r − γ_g; two vessels ⇒ γ₂ − γ₁ = γ_{a1} − γ_{a2}.
  • Apparatus: two bulbs (different materials) + U-tube, heated uniformly.

Important Statements

Practice Questions

  • Numerical (3m): In a Dulong–Petit experiment the liquid levels in glass (γ_g = 2.7×10⁻⁵) and iron (γ_Fe = 3.6×10⁻⁵) vessels differ such that γ_{a1} − γ_{a2} = 9×10⁻⁶ K⁻¹. If γ_a(glass) = 1.75×10⁻⁴, find γ_r. — γ_r = γ_a + γ_g = 1.75×10⁻⁴ + 2.7×10⁻⁵ = 2.02×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹ (consistency: γ_a(Fe) = 2.02×10⁻⁴ − 3.6×10⁻⁵ = 1.66×10⁻⁴; difference 9×10⁻⁶ ✓).

Universal Facts

  • Dulong and Petit also gave the law of constant atomic heat capacity (1818) — unrelated but bearing their name.

Confusion

  • The trick is cancellation: the liquid's expansion difference between two vessels equals the vessels' expansion difference — no direct measurement of tiny expansions needed.
  • Apparent = real − vessel: keep the signs straight.

Summary

Dulong and Petit's method measures a liquid's real cubical expansivity by cancelling the vessel effect. The apparent expansion in any vessel obeys γa​=γr​−γg​; using the same liquid in two vessels of coefficients γ₁ and γ₂ gives γ2​−γ1​=γa1​−γa2​ — a relation independent of the liquid. The apparatus is two bulbs of different materials (glass and metal) joined by a U-tube, heated uniformly through ΔT; the liquid columns shift by the difference of apparent expansions, the vessel coefficients follow from γ = 3α of their materials, and the liquid's real coefficient is then γr​=γa​+γg​. For mercury in glass (γ_g = 2.5×10⁻⁵, γ_a = 1.8×10⁻⁴), γ_r = 2.05×10⁻⁴ K⁻¹.
