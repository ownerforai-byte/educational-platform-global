Notes

  • Why liquids need special treatment: Unlike solids, liquids cannot maintain a fixed shape and must be contained. When heated, BOTH the liquid and the container expand, making direct measurement of liquid expansion impossible without correction.
  • Absolute (real) expansion: The actual increase in volume of the liquid itself, independent of the container. ΔVabs​=V0​γliq​ΔT, where γliq​ is the coefficient of cubical expansion of the liquid.
  • Apparent expansion: The observed increase in volume of the liquid as read from the container's scale. Since the container also expands, the scale marks move apart, making the liquid level appear to rise LESS than it actually does.
  • Formula: γapp​=γliq​−γcontainer​. Therefore γliq​=γapp​+γcontainer​. The absolute expansion equals the apparent expansion plus the container's expansion.
  • Physical picture: Imagine a flask marked with graduated lines. When heated, the glass expands and the marks move farther apart. The liquid also expands and rises. The net rise on the (now-spreading) scale is the apparent expansion.
  • Mercury thermometer principle: Mercury has γ≈182×10−6 K⁻¹ while glass has γ≈27×10−6 K⁻¹. The apparent expansion coefficient is γapp​=155×10−6 K⁻¹, large enough to give readable scale divisions.
  • Anomalous expansion of water: Water CONTRACTS from 0°C to 4°C and expands above 4°C. Its density is maximum at 4°C (ρ=1.0000 g/cm³). Below 4°C, γ is negative — water behaves opposite to normal liquids.
  • Consequence for lakes: In winter, surface water cools to 4°C and sinks (maximum density). Water below 4°C is less dense and stays at the bottom. Ice forms at 0°C on the surface, insulating the water below and allowing aquatic life to survive.
  • Measuring γliq​ — the weight thermometer: A glass bulb with a capillary is filled with mercury or the liquid. It is weighed empty, filled, then heated. The mass of liquid expelled is measured. γapp​=mremaining​×ΔTmexpelled​​.
  • Correction for glass expansion: Since the glass bulb also expands, the true coefficient is γliq​=γapp​+γglass​. For mercury in glass: γliq​=155×10−6+27×10−6=182×10−6 K⁻¹.
  • NEB exam focus: Distinguish between absolute and apparent expansion. Derive γliq​=γapp​+γglass​. Explain the anomalous expansion of water and its ecological significance. Solve weight thermometer problems.
  • Ethanol vs. mercury: Ethanol (γ≈750×10−6 K⁻¹) expands much more than mercury, making ethanol thermometers more sensitive. However, ethanol's boiling point (78°C) limits its range, while mercury works to 357°C.
  • Practical application — liquid-in-glass thermometers: The scale is calibrated using fixed points (ice point 0°C, steam point 100°C). The apparent expansion determines the distance between divisions. Different liquids give different scales.
  • Common values: Mercury: γ=182×10−6 K⁻¹. Ethanol: γ=750×10−6 K⁻¹. Glycerin: γ=500×10−6 K⁻¹. Water (above 4°C): γ≈210×10−6 K⁻¹ at 20°C.
  • Why γliq​>γsolid​ always: In liquids, molecules are less tightly bound than in solids. Heating gives molecules more kinetic energy to overcome intermolecular forces, leading to larger volume increases per degree.

Confusion

  • ❌ Apparent expansion equals absolute expansion. ✅ Apparent expansion is LESS than absolute expansion because the container also expands. γapp​=γliq​−γcontainer​<γliq​.
  • ❌ The apparent expansion of a liquid is independent of the container material. ✅ The apparent expansion DEPENDS on the container. A liquid in a copper container (γCu​≈51×10−6) shows less apparent expansion than in glass (γglass​≈27×10−6).
  • ❌ Water expands uniformly when cooled from 10°C to 0°C. ✅ Water CONTRACTS when cooled from 10°C to 4°C (reaches maximum density at 4°C), then EXPANDS from 4°C to 0°C. This anomalous behavior is unique among common liquids.
  • ❌ A liquid's absolute expansion can be measured directly with any container. ✅ It CANNOT — the container always expands. You must either use a container with negligible expansion (impossible) or apply the correction γliq​=γapp​+γcontainer​.
  • ❌ The weight of the liquid changes when heated. ✅ The MASS is constant. Only the volume changes. The weight thermometer measures the mass of liquid expelled, not its weight changing.
  • ❌ All liquids expand when heated. ✅ Most liquids expand, but water between 0°C and 4°C CONTRACTS. Some alloys (like invar) also show anomalous expansion behavior over certain temperature ranges.
  • ❌ Ice floating is due to water's density being the same as liquid water. ✅ Ice is LESS dense than liquid water (about 9% less) because water EXPANDS upon freezing. This is a direct consequence of the anomalous expansion below 4°C.
  • Carefully distinguish between similar-looking concepts in liquid expansion.

Practice

  • A glass flask of 200 cm³ capacity is filled with mercury at 20°C. Both are heated to 80°C. Find the amount of mercury that overflows. γglass​=27×10−6, γHg​=182×10−6, ΔT=60 K. Overflow = V0​(γHg​−γglass​)ΔT=200×155×10−6×60=1.86 cm³.
  • A weight thermometer contains 50 g of mercury at 10°C. When heated to 60°C, 0.75 g of mercury is expelled. Find γapp​ and γliq​ of mercury. γapp​=50−0.750.75​×501​=49.25×500.75​=3.05×10−4 K⁻¹. γliq​=3.05×10−4+2.7×10−5=3.32×10−4 K⁻¹ ≈ 182 × 10⁻⁶ (reasonable for Hg).
  • Why does a fully filled glass bottle of water crack when frozen? Water expands by about 9% when it freezes (0°C liquid → 0°C ice). The container cannot accommodate this volume increase, creating enormous pressure that exceeds glass's tensile strength.
  • A liquid has γapp​=1.5×10−4 K⁻¹ in a glass container (γglass​=2.7×10−5 K⁻¹). What is its absolute expansion coefficient? γliq​=γapp​+γglass​=1.5×10−4+0.27×10−4=1.77×10−4 K⁻¹.
  • A 100 cm³ py Volum flask is filled with water at 4°C (density max). If heated to 100°C, how much water overflows? γwater​≈2.1×10−4 K⁻¹ (average). γglass​=2.7×10−5 K⁻¹. ΔV=100×(2.1×10−4−0.27×10−4)×96=100×1.83×10−4×96=1.76 cm³.
  • Explain why a thermometer gives a false reading if the glass bulb expands differently. If the glass bulb's expansion is not uniform (e.g., due to manufacturing defects), the scale calibration is wrong. The apparent expansion γapp​ varies across the scale, giving inaccurate readings at different temperatures.

Universal Facts

  • The anomalous expansion of water was first studied by Daniel Bernoulli in the 18th century. It remains one of the most important anomalies in nature, making life on Earth possible.
  • The weight thermometer method for measuring liquid expansion was devised by Dulong and Petit in 1816. It remains the standard laboratory method for determining coefficients of cubical expansion.
  • Mercury's relatively low expansion coefficient (γ=182×10−6) compared to organic liquids makes mercury thermometers stable over a wide range (−39°C to 357°C), which is why they were the standard for over a century.
  • The fact that ice floats (density 0.917 g/cm³ vs. water's 1.000 g/cm³) is directly responsible for the survival of aquatic ecosystems in polar regions. Without this anomaly, Earth's climate would be dramatically different.
  • Modern digital thermometers have largely replaced liquid-in-glass thermometers in many applications, but the principle of differential expansion between liquid and container remains fundamental to thermal measurement.
