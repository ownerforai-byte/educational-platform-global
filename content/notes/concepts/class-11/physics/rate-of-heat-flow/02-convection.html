Notes

  • 1. Topic Introduction — Heat That Rides the Current

    Convection moves heat by moving matter itself: warmed fluid expands, becomes lighter, and rises while cooler fluid sinks to replace it. The loop powers boiling pots, sea breezes, room heaters and monsoons — and it cannot exist in a solid or in free fall.

  • 2. Fundamental Concepts (NEB 2024)

    2.1 Definition and mechanism

    Convection is the transfer of heat by the actual motion (bulk flow) of the heated material — possible only in fluids. Heated fluid expands (density falls), buoyancy lifts it, and denser cooler fluid takes its place, setting up a convection current.

    Natural (free) convection: driven by buoyancy from density differences (sea breeze, boiling water, chimney draught). Forced convection: driven by an external pump/fan (car radiator, blood circulation, air conditioning).

    2.2 Why the sea breeze blows

    Day: land (low specific heat) heats faster than the sea → warm air over land rises → cool sea air flows landward = sea breeze. Night: land cools faster → flow reverses = land breeze. (Water's huge specific heat sets the temperature contrast.)

    2.3 Applications

    • Ventilation of rooms (warm air escapes through top vents, fresh air enters below).
    • Boiling water: hot water rises from the bottom, cool descends.
    • Trade winds and monsoons: planetary-scale convection cells.
    • Car radiators and domestic "radiators" (which actually work mostly by convection).

    2.4 Worked reasoning (NEB 2024 context)

    Q (NEB-style): Why does the flame heat the water throughout the kettle rather than only near the bottom? Water in contact with the bottom expands (density ~0.958 g/cm³ at 100 °C vs 1.000 at 4–20 °C), becomes buoyant and rises; cooler denser water sinks to the bottom — a convection current distributes heat through the whole volume. Conduction alone through water (k = 0.6 W/m·K) would be far too slow.

    Q (NEB-style): Why is the freezer compartment at the top of a refrigerator? Cooling makes air denser: cold air sinks from the top-freezer, warm air rises to replace it — a natural convection loop that circulates the whole cabinet without a fan.

  • 3. Conceptual Relationships

    • To Topic 56: convection replaces conduction's diffusive transport with advective transport — vastly faster in fluids.
    • To Topic 51: the sea breeze is specific heat capacity at planetary scale.
    • To Topic 59/60: radiation is the only mode through vacuum — convection needs matter.
    • To gravity: no buoyancy in free fall — flames on the ISS are spherical and convection ceases.

Summary

Convection transfers heat by the bulk motion of the fluid itself: heated fluid expands, loses density, rises by buoyancy, and cooler fluid sinks to replace it — a convection current. Natural convection is buoyancy-driven (sea breeze, boiling, chimney draught); forced convection uses pumps and fans (radiators, blood flow). The sea breeze exists because land's lower specific heat makes it heat faster by day and cool faster by night, reversing the flow daily. Convection needs matter and gravity — in free fall flames go spherical — and it transfers heat orders of magnitude faster than conduction through the same fluid.

Sea breeze blows:

Options
  • Land→sea by day
  • Sea→land by day
  • Always seaward
  • Only at night

Land heats faster; warm air rises over land.

7.

A convection current forms because heated fluid:

Options
  • Contracts
  • Becomes less dense and rises
  • Freezes
  • Evaporates always

B

On the ISS, convection:

Options

  • Speeds up
