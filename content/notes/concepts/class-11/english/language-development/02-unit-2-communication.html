Notes

  • 1. What is Communication?

  • Communication is the exchange of information, ideas, feelings, and meaning between two or more people through symbols, signs, or behavior.

  • 2. Elements of Communication

    • Sender: The person who initiates the message
    • Message: The content being communicated
    • Channel/Medium: The method of transmission (verbal, written, digital)
    • Receiver: The person who gets the message
    • Feedback: The response from the receiver
    • Noise: Any interference that distorts the message

  • 3. Types of Communication

    • Verbal: Spoken or written words
    • Non-verbal: Body language, gestures, facial expressions
    • Visual: Images, graphs, symbols
    • Active Listening: Fully concentrating on what is being said

  • 4. Communication Barriers

  • Physical (distance, noise), Psychological (prejudice, emotion), Semantic (language differences), Cultural (norms, values).

  • 5. Effective Communication Skills

  • Clarity, conciseness, correctness, courtesy, and confidence are the 5 Cs of effective communication.

  • 6. Entrance Exam Focus

  • MCQs: Identify elements of communication; Differentiate verbal and non-verbal; List barriers to communication.

Summary

Unit 2 defines communication as the exchange of information, ideas, feelings and meaning between two or more people through symbols, signs or behaviour. It sets out the six elements - sender, message, channel, receiver, feedback and noise - and the types, including verbal, non-verbal, visual and active listening. The unit then groups the barriers into physical, psychological, semantic and cultural, and closes with the 5 Cs of effective communication: clarity, conciseness, correctness, courtesy and confidence.

Important Concepts

  • Communication as the exchange of information, ideas, feelings and meaning through symbols, signs or behaviour
  • The six elements: sender, message, channel or medium, receiver, feedback and noise
  • Types of communication: verbal, non-verbal, visual and active listening
  • Four barriers: physical, psychological, semantic and cultural
  • The 5 Cs of effective communication: clarity, conciseness, correctness, courtesy and confidence
  • Active listening as fully concentrating on what is being said

Important Statements

  • Communication is not only talking; it also includes listening, observing and interpreting.
  • Noise is any interference that distorts the message.
  • Verbal communication uses spoken or written words, while non-verbal communication uses body language, gestures and facial expressions.
  • Visual communication uses images, graphs and symbols.
  • More words do not make communication better; clarity and conciseness matter more.
  • Physical barriers include distance and noise, and semantic barriers arise from differences in language.

Important Tasks

  • Explain the elements of communication with a diagram.
  • Differentiate between verbal and non-verbal communication with examples.
  • List the barriers to effective communication and explain how they can be overcome.
  • Explain the 5 Cs of effective communication with a short example for each.
  • Describe active listening and its importance in a face-to-face conversation.

Key Points

  • Six elements in order: sender, message, channel or medium, receiver, feedback and noise.
  • The channel or medium is the method of transmission (verbal, written, digital), not the person who sends the message.
  • Non-verbal communication covers body language, gestures and facial expressions.
  • Four barriers: physical (distance, noise), psychological (prejudice, emotion), semantic (language differences), cultural (norms, values).
  • The 5 Cs of effective communication are clarity, conciseness, correctness, courtesy and confidence.
  • Feedback is the receiver's response and is what completes the communication process.

Special Notes

  • Do not answer 'Communication means talking only' - the notes explicitly reject this.
  • In a diagram, label all six elements and show noise interfering with the channel.
  • Do not write that more words make communication better; clarity and conciseness are what matter.
  • Keep the answers themselves clear and concise - the 5 Cs apply to your writing too.
  • Active listening belongs with the types in these notes; it is not one of the six elements and not a barrier.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Mnemonic for the six elements: 'Some Messages Change Hands, Not Fully' - Sender, Message, Channel, Receiver, Feedback, Noise.
  • Reject any option that defines communication as only speaking.
  • Barrier keyword test: distance or noise = physical; prejudice or emotion = psychological; language difference = semantic; norms or values = cultural.
  • For 5 Cs questions, write the five words in a single line first, then explain each - this prevents forgetting one.
  • If a question asks which element completes the process, the answer is feedback.

Exam Notes

  • Elements with a diagram, barriers and types are all common question types in this unit.
  • MCQ traps: treating the medium as the person who sends the message, or treating noise as the feedback.
  • Open a long answer with a one-line definition of communication, then list the elements, then the barriers.
  • This unit is content and vocabulary based; do not add grammar rules that are not in these notes.

Mcs

1.

In the communication process, the person who gets the message is the:

Options
  • Sender
  • Channel
  • Receiver
  • Noise
Answer

C

Explanation

The receiver is the person who gets the message. The sender initiates it, the channel transmits it and noise is any interference that distorts it.

2.

Which of the following is one of the six elements of communication?

Options
  • Feedback
  • Ignorance
  • Silence
  • Memory
Answer

A

Explanation

The six elements are sender, message, channel, receiver, feedback and noise. Feedback is the response from the receiver.

3.

Physical distance between two people is an example of:

Options
  • A psychological barrier
  • A semantic barrier
  • A cultural barrier
  • A physical barrier
Answer

D

Explanation

Distance and noise are listed as physical barriers. Prejudice and emotion are psychological, language differences are semantic, and norms and values are cultural.

4.

Active listening means:

Options
  • Listening while preparing a reply
  • Fully concentrating on what is being said
  • Listening only to music
  • Repeating the speaker's words aloud
Answer

B

Explanation

The notes define active listening as fully concentrating on what is being said. It is also used to show that communication includes listening, observing and interpreting, not only talking.

5.

Which of the following is NOT one of the 5 Cs of effective communication?

Options
  • Clarity
  • Conciseness
  • Courtesy
  • Complexity
Answer

D

Explanation

The 5 Cs are clarity, conciseness, correctness, courtesy and confidence. Complexity is the opposite of conciseness and clarity.
