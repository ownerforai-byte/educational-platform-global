Notes

  • 1. Types of Essays

    • Narrative: Tells a story with a sequence of events
    • Descriptive: Paints a picture with words
    • Expository: Explains or informs objectively
    • Argumentative: Presents a claim with evidence
    • Persuasive: Convinces the reader to accept a viewpoint

  • 2. Essay Structure

    • Introduction: Hook + thesis statement
    • Body Paragraphs: Topic sentence + evidence + analysis
    • Conclusion: Restate thesis + summary + final thought

  • 3. Argumentative Essay Tips

  • State a clear thesis, use credible evidence, address counterarguments, and conclude forcefully.

  • 4. Descriptive Essay Tips

  • Use sensory details (sight, sound, smell, touch, taste). Show, don't tell.

  • 5. Common Essay Topics

  • Education, Technology, Environment, Globalization, Youth Role, Health, Corruption, Tourism in Nepal.

  • 6. Entrance Exam Focus

  • Write an essay on any one: 'Technology has made life easier' / 'Environmental protection is our duty'. Structure: 5-7 paragraphs, 250-300 words.

Summary

This chapter sets out the five types of essay - narrative, descriptive, expository, argumentative and persuasive - and the three-part structure common to all of them: an introduction with a hook and a thesis statement, body paragraphs built on a topic sentence with evidence and analysis, and a conclusion that restates the thesis and adds a final thought. It also gives type-specific technique, such as using credible evidence and answering counterarguments in argumentative writing, and appealing to the five senses in descriptive writing. For the entrance exam, expect an essay of 5-7 paragraphs and roughly 250-300 words on a familiar topic.

Important Concepts

  • Five essay types: narrative, descriptive, expository, argumentative and persuasive
  • Three-part structure: introduction, body paragraphs and conclusion
  • Introduction: a hook plus a thesis statement
  • Body paragraph formula: topic sentence + evidence + analysis
  • Conclusion: restate the thesis + summary + final thought
  • Descriptive technique: sensory details of sight, sound, smell, touch and taste

Important Statements

  • An argumentative essay must be backed by evidence and logic; it is not mere personal opinion.
  • A descriptive essay should engage all five senses rather than only list facts.
  • Every body paragraph needs a topic sentence that states the paragraph's main idea.
  • The introduction needs a hook to attract the reader and a clear thesis statement.
  • The conclusion restates the thesis, summarizes the body and ends with a final thought.
  • An expository essay explains or informs objectively.

Important Tasks

  • Write an argumentative essay on 'Is technology beneficial or harmful?'
  • Describe your favourite place using sensory details.
  • Write an expository essay on 'The importance of education'.
  • Write an essay of 250-300 words in 5-7 paragraphs on a prescribed topic.
  • Write a persuasive essay that convinces the reader to accept a viewpoint.

Key Points

  • Types: narrative tells a story with a sequence of events; descriptive paints a picture with words; expository explains objectively; argumentative presents a claim with evidence; persuasive convinces the reader.
  • Introduction = hook + thesis statement.
  • Body = topic sentence + evidence + analysis, with one main idea per paragraph.
  • Conclusion = restate thesis + summary + final thought.
  • Descriptive technique: use sensory details and show rather than tell.
  • Argumentative technique: state a clear thesis, use credible evidence, address counterarguments and conclude forcefully.

Special Notes

  • Argumentative does NOT mean personal opinion - it must be backed by evidence and logic.
  • Descriptive does NOT mean listing facts - it should engage all five senses.
  • Check paragraph unity: every body paragraph must serve the thesis, so delete any paragraph that drifts from it.
  • Before finishing a descriptive essay, confirm that all five senses appear.
  • Underline the topic sentence of each paragraph when you finish; a missing one usually costs a mark.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Structure memory hook: 'Hook-Thesis, Topic-Evidence-Analysis, Restate-Summary-Final thought'.
  • Word-count shortcut: 5-7 paragraphs of about 40-50 words each lands safely inside 250-300 words.
  • Type test: does it tell a story (narrative), paint a picture (descriptive), explain (expository), argue with evidence (argumentative) or convince (persuasive)?
  • To handle a counterargument, add one short paragraph that raises the opposite view and then answers it - this is what lifts an argumentative essay.
  • Never introduce new information in the last sentence; conclude forcefully on the point already made.

Exam Notes

  • The exam task is 5-7 paragraphs of 250-300 words on a common topic such as education, technology, environment, globalization, the role of youth, health, corruption or tourism in Nepal.
  • Marks follow the structure, so never skip the introduction or the conclusion.
  • MCQ traps: 'an argumentative essay needs only personal opinion' and 'a descriptive essay is a list of facts' are both false.
  • Keep one consistent register and check spelling and grammar; errors cost marks even when the content is correct.
  • This chapter is about structure and technique; do not add grammar rules that are not in these notes.

B

Explanation

The essay structure given in the notes is introduction (hook + thesis statement), body paragraphs, then conclusion.

2.

What is the correct order of elements in a body paragraph?

Options
  • Evidence, then analysis, then topic sentence
  • Topic sentence, then evidence, then analysis
  • Analysis, then topic sentence, then evidence
  • Conclusion, then introduction, then body
Answer

B

Explanation

A body paragraph is built as topic sentence + evidence + analysis.

3.

An essay that explains a topic or informs the reader objectively is a(n):

Options
  • Narrative essay
  • Descriptive essay
  • Persuasive essay
  • Expository essay
Answer

D

Explanation

The expository essay explains or informs objectively. Narrative tells a story, descriptive paints a picture with words, and persuasive convinces the reader.

4.

Sensory details of sight, sound, smell, touch and taste are most important in a:

Options
  • Descriptive essay
  • Argumentative essay
  • Expository essay
  • Narrative essay
Answer

A

Explanation

Descriptive essays use sensory details and show rather than tell; listing facts without sensory detail is a common weakness.
