Notes

  • What this covers: the complete mechanics of notices (the shortest high-mark format) and the full contrast between formal and informal writing — so that after reading, you can produce ANY piece of English writing with the right register, structure and tone.
  • NOTICE — the 5 locked components in order: 1) the word NOTICE (top-centre, or the issuing body's name above it) 2) DATE of issue 3) HEADING (what the notice is about, centred, bold) 4) BODY (answers What/When/Where/Who in 3-4 sentences, max ~50 words) 5) SIGNATURE block (name, designation, issuing body — bottom-left).
  • NOTICE — the 50-word law: examiners deduct for length. Every word must carry information: 'The school is organising...' ❌ → 'An inter-house debate will be held...' ✅
  • NOTICE — no personal touches: no 'Dear students', no first/second person. Passive voice and third person only: 'Students are informed that...'. Signature = name + designation only.
  • NOTICE — tense logic: upcoming events → future (will be held); results → present perfect (has been selected).
  • FORMAL WRITING — definition: writing for institutions and strangers: letters to editors/principals/offices, reports, articles, notices, essays. Traits: no contractions, no slang, full sentences, objective tone, 'Dear Sir/Madam' → 'Yours faithfully/sincerely'.
  • INFORMAL WRITING — definition: writing to people you know: letters to friends/family, diary entries. Traits: contractions welcome, casual connectors (well, anyway, by the way), warmth, 'Dear Ravi' → 'With love'.
  • THE REGISTER SWITCH TABLE (memorise): Formal 'I would like to inform you' ↔ Informal 'Just so you know'; 'I am writing to inquire about' ↔ 'I wanted to ask'; 'Please find enclosed' ↔ 'I'm sending'; 'We regret to inform you' ↔ 'Bad news —'.
  • FORMAL LETTER skeleton: sender's address (top-right, no name) → date → receiver's designation + address (left) → Subject line (one line, factual) → 'Dear Sir/Madam' → 3 paragraphs (WHY you write / DETAILS / ACTION you expect) → 'Yours faithfully' + full name.
  • Faithfully vs Sincerely — the rule everyone forgets: 'Dear Sir/Madam' (no name) → Yours faithfully. 'Dear Mr Sharma' (named) → Yours sincerely.
  • INFORMAL LETTER skeleton: address (top-right) → date → 'Dear Name,' → opening (acknowledge their last letter: 'It was great to hear from you') → middle (the news, chatty, 2-3 paragraphs) → close ('Write back soon / Give my regards to...') → 'With love' + first name.
  • HOW PARAGRAPHS WORK — the architecture every format shares: 1) TOPIC SENTENCE (the paragraph's claim in one line) 2) SUPPORTING SENTENCES (evidence, example, reason) 3) LINK SENTENCE (ties back or bridges forward). One idea per paragraph — mixing ideas is the #1 structural error.
  • COHESION — how paragraphs connect: reference chains (this policy, such measures — don't repeat the noun), connectors by function (addition: moreover; contrast: however; result: therefore; sequence: firstly/finally), and one consistent tense per time-frame.
  • THE COMPOSITION MATRIX: Notice = announce an event (50 words). Formal letter = complain/apply/request an institution. Informal letter = share news with a person. Article = inform/persuade readers (title + byline + intro-body-conclusion). Report = record facts for an authority (past tense, passive-heavy). Essay = argue a thesis (intro → 3-4 body paras → conclusion). Speech = greeting + hook + body + thank-you. Story = setting → conflict → climax → resolution.
  • TENSE MAP BY FORMAT: notice → future; report → past (+passive); article/essay → present for general truths; story → past narrative (past perfect for flashbacks); informal letter → any tense (natural).
  • AFTER-READING CHECKLIST (can you do all 5?): write a compliant 50-word notice · choose faithfully vs sincerely correctly · convert formal ↔ informal · build a topic+support+link paragraph · pick the right format and tense for any prompt.

Confusion

  • ❌ Writing 'Dear Sir' in a notice. ✅ Notices have no salutation — they are impersonal announcements.
  • ❌ Signing a notice with a first name only. ✅ Full name + designation + issuing body.
  • ❌ 'Yours sincerely' after 'Dear Sir/Madam'. ✅ Unnamed → faithfully; named → sincerely.
  • ❌ Contractions in formal writing. ✅ 'cannot', 'will not' — contractions mark informality.
  • ❌ A 90-word notice. ✅ Stay ~50 words; extra sentences risk deduction.

Practice

  • Draft a notice (≤50 words): Eco Club tree-plantation drive next Saturday; register by Thursday.
  • Write the opening and closing paragraphs of a letter to the editor about reckless honking near a school.
  • Rewrite informally: 'I regret to inform you that I shall be unable to attend.' ('So sorry — I can't make it.')
  • Identify the format: past tense, passive voice, heading, findings — (report.)
  • Build one paragraph on 'morning walks': topic sentence + two supports + link.

Universal Facts

  • All English prose formats share one skeleton: opening (purpose) → middle (detail) → closing (action/feeling).
  • Register is signalled by four levers: contractions, vocabulary, sentence length, person.
  • Examiners grade structure first (format components), then language, then content.

Examples

  • Specimen notice: NOTICE / 15 March 2026 / INTER-HOUSE DEBATE / An inter-house debate will be held on 22 March 2026 at 10 a.m. in the school hall. Two participants per house. — Anita Rai, Secretary, Literary Club
  • Same news, two registers: Formal — 'The Literary Club cordially invites...' / Informal — 'Hey! The debate is finally happening...'
  • Paragraph dissected: topic ('Morning walks transform health') → supports (air, routine, sunlight) → link ('The mind benefits just as much...')

Formulas

  • NOTICE = body name + NOTICE + date + heading + body(what/when/where/who) + name + designation; ≤50 words; no salutation.
  • FORMAL LETTER = address → date → receiver → subject → salutation → why → details → action → faithfully + full name.
  • INFORMAL LETTER = address → date → Dear Name → ack → news → warm close → With love + first name.
  • PARAGRAPH = Topic + 2-3 Support + Link; ONE idea per paragraph.
  • Register levers: no contractions + Latinate vocab + long sentences + 3rd person = formal.

Key Points

  • Notices: 5 components, ≤50 words, third person, future tense for events.
  • Faithfully (unnamed) vs Sincerely (named) — an automatic mark.
  • Formal = no contractions, objective; Informal = contractions, warmth.
  • Every paragraph: topic → support → link.
  • Tense by format: report=past, notice=future, essay=present, story=past.

Summary

Notices are 50-word impersonal announcements with five locked components; formal and informal writing differ across contractions, vocabulary, sentence length and person; and every format is the same purpose-driven skeleton in different clothes. Master the skeleton, the register switches and the paragraph architecture, and any writing prompt becomes executable.

Special Notes

  • In the exam, write the format skeleton first — format marks are the cheapest marks available.
  • Keep a personal register-switch table of 10 formal↔informal pairs; revise before every exam.

Important Statements

  • A notice never addresses the reader directly — it informs in the third person.
  • One paragraph = one idea, developed with support and closed with a link.
  • Formal writing persuades with structure; informal writing connects with warmth.
