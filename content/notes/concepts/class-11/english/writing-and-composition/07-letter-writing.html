Summary

This chapter separates formal from informal letters and gives the part-by-part order of each. A formal letter runs sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation (Sir/Madam), body, closing (Yours faithfully) and signature, while an informal letter runs date, salutation (Dear Name), opening, body, closing (Love or Yours) and signature. It lists the common formal types - application, complaint, inquiry and editor's letter - and the informal types - friendly, thank-you and congratulatory letters - and ends with email conventions. In the entrance exam, expect either a formal letter to the editor or a scholarship application.

Important Concepts

  • Formal letter format: sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation, body, closing and signature
  • Informal letter format: date, salutation, opening, body, closing and signature
  • Formal letter types: application, complaint, inquiry and editor's letter
  • Informal letter types: friendly letter, thank-you letter and congratulatory letter
  • Salutation rules: 'Dear Sir/Madam' when the name is unknown, 'Dear Name' for a known person
  • Standard layouts: block style with every part on a new line, and semi-block style with indented body paragraphs

Important Statements

  • A formal letter closes with 'Yours faithfully' and carries a signature, while an informal letter closes with 'Love' or 'Yours'.
  • Use 'Dear Sir/Madam' when the name of the receiver is unknown.
  • A formal letter requires a subject line, placed between the receiver's address and the salutation.
  • Professional emails require a formal tone even though email is a digital medium.
  • An email subject line must be clear and concise, and its paragraphs should be kept short.
  • Applications are used for jobs, admissions and leaves; inquiry letters ask for information; complaint letters concern products, services or conditions.

Key Points

  • Formal order: sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation (Sir/Madam), body, closing (Yours faithfully), signature.
  • Informal order: date, salutation (Dear Name), opening, body, closing (Love or Yours), signature.
  • Formal types: application for jobs, admissions and leaves; complaint; inquiry; and editor's letter expressing opinion on a public issue.
  • Informal types: friendly letter for catching up, thank-you letter for gratitude, and congratulatory letter for celebrating achievements.
  • Email writing: a clear concise subject line, a formal tone for professional mail, and short paragraphs.
  • Exam tasks: a formal letter to the editor about pollution in Kathmandu, and an application for a scholarship.

Special Notes

  • Formal letters need 'Dear Sir/Madam' when no name is known, not a bare 'Dear Sir' with a missing name.
  • Emails are not automatically informal; a professional email requires a formal tone.
  • Never leave out the subject line of a formal letter - it is among the first things an examiner checks.
  • In a complaint or editor's letter, state the problem first, then the cause, then a suggested remedy, then a courteous request.
  • In an application, give your qualifications and the reason for applying before you make the request, and keep one clear request per letter.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Parts memory hook for a formal letter: 'Send Date Receive Subject Salute Body Close Sign'.
  • Layout shortcut: block style puts every part flush left on a new line; semi-block style indents the body paragraphs within the same arrangement.
  • If the receiver is named, such as the principal, write 'Dear Sir'; if the name is unknown, write 'Dear Sir/Madam'.
  • Time-saving formula for an editor's letter: problem, cause, suggestion, request - one paragraph each.
  • For an informal letter, open warmly and close with 'Love' or 'Yours'; 'Yours faithfully' belongs to formal letters only.

Exam Notes

  • Format carries marks: address, date, subject, salutation, body, closing and signature must appear in the correct order.
  • MCQ traps: treating emails as informal, and writing 'Dear Sir/Madam' when a name has been given.
  • Keep the tone consistent from the salutation to the closing - formal letters stay formal, informal letters stay warm and conversational.
  • Block style is the safest layout in the exam because it is the hardest to mark incorrectly.
  • This chapter is about format, type and tone; do not add grammar rules that are not in these notes.

Mcs

1.

In a formal letter, the subject line is written:

Options
  • After the salutation
  • In the second paragraph
  • Before the salutation, below the receiver's address
  • After the signature
Answer

C

Explanation

The formal order given in the notes is sender's address, date, receiver's address, subject, salutation, body, closing and signature, so the subject comes before the salutation.

2.

If the name of the receiver is unknown, the correct salutation is:

Options
  • Dear Sir/Madam
  • My dear friend
  • Hello everyone
  • Good day to you all
Answer

A

Explanation

The notes instruct using 'Dear Sir/Madam' when the name is unknown. A named receiver is addressed directly instead.

3.

Which of the following is a part of a formal letter but not of an informal letter?

Options
  • Date
  • Salutation
  • Subject
  • Closing
Answer

C

Explanation

Date, salutation and closing appear in both formats, but the subject line is specific to the formal letter format.

4.

A letter written to a newspaper to express an opinion on a public issue is a(n):

Options
  • Editor's letter
  • Thank-you letter
  • Congratulatory letter
  • Friendly letter
Answer

A

Explanation

The editor's letter is the formal type used for expressing an opinion on a public issue. Thank-you, congratulatory and friendly letters are all informal types.

5.

According to the notes, a professional email requires:

Options
  • A clear, concise subject line and a formal tone
  • Slang and no subject line
  • Very long unbroken paragraphs
  • An informal greeting such as hi
Answer

A

Explanation

Even though email is digital, professional emails require a formal tone, a clear and concise subject line and short paragraphs.
