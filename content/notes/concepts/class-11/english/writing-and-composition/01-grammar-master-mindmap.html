Notes

  • How to use this: grammar is one connected system, not 30 separate chapters. This mindmap is the central trunk (SENTENCE) with six branches — every exam question hangs off one of them. Revise by walking the branches, not by re-reading chapters.
  • CENTRAL NODE — THE SENTENCE: every English sentence = SUBJECT + VERB + (OBJECT/COMPLEMENT) + (modifiers). Everything below is either how these parts CHANGE FORM (morphology) or how they COMBINE (syntax). If you can locate the subject and verb of any sentence, you can solve any grammar question.
  • BRANCH 1 — PARTS OF SPEECH (the 8 word classes): Noun (common, proper, collective, abstract, material; countable vs uncountable), Pronoun (personal I/we/they, possessive mine/ours, reflexive myself, relative who/which/that, demonstrative this/those, indefinite each/everyone, interrogative who?/what?), Verb (transitive vs intransitive; state vs action; regular/irregular), Adjective (quality, quantity, number, demonstrative, possessive, interrogative; degrees: big-bigger-biggest), Adverb (manner -ly, time, place, frequency, degree; also modifies adjectives and other adverbs), Preposition (in/on/at/of/by/with/for — position and direction words), Conjunction (coordinating FANBOYS: for-and-nor-but-or-yet-so; subordinating: because/although/when/if/unless; correlative pairs: either...or, not only...but also), Interjection (ouch! hurrah! — punctuation, not grammar).
  • BRANCH 1 sub-node — determiners: articles a/an/the, quantifiers some/any/much/many/few/little, and the demonstrative/possessive words that sit before nouns. The article rules and quantifier choices generate a large share of fill-in-the-blank questions.
  • BRANCH 2 — TENSES (12-cell grid): 3 times (past/present/future) × 4 aspects (simple/continuous/perfect/perfect continuous). Key cells: Simple Present = habits & universal truths ('The sun rises in the east'); Present Perfect = past action, present relevance ('I have finished'); Present Perfect Continuous = since/for duration ('has been raining since morning'); Past Perfect = the EARLIER of two past actions ('had left before I arrived'); Simple Future = will + V1. Memory hook: aspect answers HOW the action sits in time, time answers WHEN.
  • BRANCH 3 — SUBJECT-VERB AGREEMENT: the verb agrees with the HEAD of the subject. Rules cascade: of-phrases don't count ('the bouquet of roses IS'); each/every/either/neither/everyone = singular; collective nouns usually singular (team IS) but plural when members act individually; pair nouns (scissors, trousers) plural; 'one of + plural noun + singular verb'; subjects joined by and = plural (except single ideas: bread and butter IS); either...or / neither...nor → verb agrees with the NEARER subject; distances/amounts as one unit = singular ('Ten miles IS a long walk').
  • BRANCH 3 sub-node — concord traps: 'There/Here' sentences hide the subject after the verb; relative pronouns take the number of their antecedent ('one of the boys WHO play'); 'a number of' = plural, 'the number of' = singular; 'news/mathematics/physics' = singular though they look plural.
  • BRANCH 4 — VOICE (active ↔ passive): only TRANSITIVE verbs passivise. Machine: object → subject + be (in the tense of the original verb) + past participle + by + agent. Present: is/am/are + V3. Past: was/were + V3. Perfect: has/have been + V3. Future: will be + V3. Modals: can/must be + V3. Skip the agent when it's obvious/unimportant ('My bicycle has been stolen' — not 'by someone'). State verbs (have, resemble, suit, lack) have no passive.
  • BRANCH 5 — NARRATION (direct ↔ indirect speech): two operations: (a) remove quotes, add 'that'/to-infinitive/connector; (b) BACKSHIFT — reporting verb in past shifts everything one step back: present→past, past→past perfect, will/can/may→would/could/might, this→that, now→then, today→that day, ago→before, here→there, tomorrow→the next day. Statements → that +; yes-no questions → if/whether with statement word-order; wh-questions → wh-word + statement order; commands → to + V1 ('told him to go'); requests → requested + to; exclamations → exclaimed with joy/sorrow that.
  • BRANCH 5 sub-node — no-backshift cases: universal truths stay present ('the earth revolves...'); if the reporting verb is present/future ('says'), nothing shifts.
  • BRANCH 6 — MODALS & CONDITIONS: modals (can, could, may, might, shall, should, will, would, must, ought to) never take to/s/-ing and are followed by V1. Meanings: can=ability, may=permission/possibility, must=obligation/certainty, should=advice, would=past habit/politeness/unreal. The conditional ladder: Type 1 (if + present, will + V1) real future; Type 2 (if + past, would + V1) unreal present — 'were' for all persons; Type 3 (if + had + V3, would have + V3) unreal past — regret/replay.
  • CROSS-BRANCH MICRO-ZONES (the marks-savers): ARTICLES (a/an by SOUND — 'an hour', 'a university'; the = specific/second mention/unique; zero before meals, languages, games). PREPOSITIONS (IN/ON/AT for place and time scales). QUESTION TAGS (copy the auxiliary, flip polarity; none → do/does/did). NON-FINITES (infinitive = purpose; gerund = noun; participle = adjective). DEGREES (comparative + than; superlative + the; 'than that of' for like-with-like). FIXED PAIRS (no sooner...than; hardly...when; not only...but also — never mixed).
  • EXAM EXECUTION LOOP: 1) identify the branch the question lives on; 2) apply that branch's machine (the rules are mechanical); 3) in error-spotting walk the scan order: agreement → tense → preposition → article → pronoun; 4) after writing, re-check the five most-tested zones.

Confusion

  • ❌ Memorising 30 disconnected 'chapters'. ✅ All of grammar is 6 branches off one trunk: the sentence.
  • ❌ 'My team are winning' (institution sense). ✅ Collective noun as one unit → singular 'is'.
  • ❌ 'He told that he was ill.' ✅ 'told' needs a person: 'told ME that' / 'said that'.
  • ❌ 'a university' / 'an hour' feels backwards. ✅ Sound decides: /yuː/ → 'a'; silent h → 'an'.
  • ❌ 'If I would have known...'. ✅ Type 3: 'If I HAD known, I would have come.'

Practice

  • Label any newspaper sentence: subject, verb, object + word class of every remaining word.
  • Write the 12-cell tense grid empty and fill one example per cell — from memory.
  • Convert: She said, 'I may visit tomorrow.' → (She said that she might visit the next day.)
  • Passivise in three tenses: 'The chef prepares the meal.' → present/past/present perfect passive.
  • Walk the error-scan on: 'One of my friend have gone to America last year.' (friends; has/went — agreement + tense).

Universal Facts

  • Every grammar question is either morphology (form changes) or syntax (combination rules).
  • The 12 tense cells are generated by two questions — WHEN (time) and HOW it sits in time (aspect).
  • Error-spotting is not reading: it is a fixed scan order walked mechanically.

Examples

  • One sentence, all six branches: 'Yesterday, each of the players, who had trained hard, was given a medal by the captain.' — agreement (each...was), relative pronoun, tense (had trained), voice (was given), narration, modifiers.
  • The tense grid in one breath: I write / I am writing / I have written / I have been writing — shift all four into past and future for 12.
  • A mindmap walk for 'spot the error': agreement ✓ → tense ✓ → preposition ✓ → article ✗ (found it).

Practice Questions

  • Q2. Where does a question-tag error live on the map? (Micro-zones.)
  • Q3. Which two branches dominate NEB error-spotting? (Agreement + Tense.)

Formulas

  • SENTENCE = SUBJECT + VERB + rest. Find the head pair first.
  • PASSIVE = object + be (same tense) + V3 (+ by agent).
  • BACKSHIFT = everything one step back (time, place, certainty).
  • CONDITIONALS: 1) if+present→will; 2) if+past→would; 3) if+had V3→would have V3.
  • AGREEMENT: verb follows the HEAD noun; each/every = singular; or/nor → nearest subject.

Key Points

  • Six branches: parts of speech, tenses, agreement, voice, narration, modals/conditions.
  • Micro-zones decide fill-in-the-blanks: articles, prepositions, tags, non-finites, pairs.
  • Scan order for errors: agreement → tense → preposition → article → pronoun.
  • Voice needs a transitive verb; state verbs never passivise.
  • Universal truths don't backshift.

Summary

English grammar is one sentence-mechanics system with six branches — word classes, the 12-tense grid, agreement, voice, narration, modals/conditionals — plus a micro-zone layer of articles, prepositions, tags and fixed pairs that supplies most exam questions. Learn each branch as a machine, walk the error-scan in fixed order, and the whole syllabus compresses onto one revisable page.
