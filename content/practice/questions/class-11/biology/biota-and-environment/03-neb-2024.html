Questions

1.

What are biotic and abiotic components of the environment? Give two examples of each.

Marks

2

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Biotic (living): plants, animals, microbes — producers, consumers, decomposers.

Abiotic (non-living): light, temperature, water, soil, air, minerals — the stage every organism performs on.

Knowledge Note

Liebig's law of the minimum: the scarcest factor limits growth, however abundant the rest.

2.

What are hydrophytes and xerophytes? Write two adaptive features of each.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Hydrophytes (water plants, e.g. lotus): aerenchyma air spaces for buoyancy, waxy waterproof leaves, reduced roots.

Xerophytes (dry-land plants, e.g. cactus): fleshy water-storing stems, spines instead of leaves, sunken stomata and thick cuticle against transpiration.

Knowledge Note

Opposite water problems, opposite engineering — environment sculpts anatomy predictably.

3.

Define natality, mortality, and population density. How are they related to population growth?

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Natality (birth rate) adds; mortality (death rate) subtracts; density (individuals per unit area) is the current stock.

Growth = (natality + immigration) − (mortality + emigration) — density rises only when arrivals beat departures.

Knowledge Note

J-shaped (exponential) vs S-shaped (logistic, carrying capacity K) — the two growth curves behind every population question.

4.

What are the major causes and effects of air pollution?

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Causes: vehicle exhaust (CO, NOₓ), brick kilns and industry (SPM, SO₂), biomass burning, dust from construction.

Effects: respiratory disease, acid rain, smog cutting visibility, greenhouse warming; Kathmandu's winter inversion traps it all into hazardous AQI weeks.

Knowledge Note

Kathmandu Valley's bowl topography + winter inversion = Nepal's own air-pollution case study.

5.

What is BOD? Why is high BOD harmful for aquatic life?

Marks

2

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Biochemical Oxygen Demand is the oxygen microbes consume decomposing organic matter in water (mg/L over 5 days at 20°C).

High BOD means the water's oxygen is being eaten — fish suffocate, clean-water species vanish, and only pollution-tolerant worms survive.

Knowledge Note

BOD < 5 ppm is clean; Bagmati sewage stretches run far above — a number that tells a river's whole story.

6.

What is soil erosion? Suggest any three conservation measures practised in Nepal.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Soil erosion is the removal of topsoil by water and wind — Nepal's hills lose tonnes per hectare yearly.

Measures: (1) terrace farming on slopes; (2) community-forest afforestation stabilising catchments; (3) check dams and contour bunding slowing runoff.

Knowledge Note

Community forestry now covers a third of Nepal's forest — devolved conservation that reversed Himalayan deforestation.
