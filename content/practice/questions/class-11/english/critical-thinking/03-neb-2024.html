NEB Grade 11 Model Questions / NEB Past Papers 2020-2024

Questions

1.

What is critical thinking? Explain any three of its key skills with examples.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Critical thinking is disciplined, evidence-based reasoning instead of accepting claims at face value.

Skills: (1) Analysis — breaking an argument into premises and conclusion. (2) Evaluation — testing evidence quality (a viral health claim vs a peer-reviewed trial). (3) Inference — drawing warranted conclusions (wet streets → it rained, barring alternatives).

Knowledge Note

Bloom's ladder (remember → create) places evaluation and creation at the top — exams increasingly target those rungs.

2.

Distinguish between fact and opinion with two examples of each.

Marks

2

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Fact is verifiable: 'Mt. Everest is 8,848.86 m high.' 'Water boils at 100°C at sea level.'

Opinion expresses judgement: 'Pokhara is Nepal's most beautiful city.' 'Online classes are better than physical ones.'

Test: can evidence settle it? Yes → fact; no → opinion.

Knowledge Note

Editorials blend both — spotting which sentence is which is the core critical-reading move.

3.

What are premises and conclusions in an argument? Identify them in: 'All students must wear uniforms since uniforms reduce economic discrimination.'

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Premises are the supporting reasons; the conclusion is the claim they support (flagged by 'therefore, so, since, because').

Premise: 'uniforms reduce economic discrimination.' Conclusion: 'All students must wear uniforms.'

Critical check: does the premise really entail the conclusion, or are cheaper alternatives (dress codes, subsidies) ignored?

Knowledge Note

'Since/because' introduce premises; 'therefore/so' introduce conclusions — indicator words map arguments instantly.

4.

What is a logical fallacy? Explain ad hominem and hasty generalisation with one example of each.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

A fallacy is reasoning that looks valid but isn't.

Ad hominem: attacking the person, not the claim — 'Don't trust his tax plan; he failed math.' Hasty generalisation: leaping from tiny samples — 'Two tourists were rude, so all tourists are rude.'

Knowledge Note

Strawman (distorting the opponent's view) completes the big-three fallacy set for this unit.

5.

Write any four criteria for evaluating the credibility of an information source.

Marks

2

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

(1) Authority — qualified author/publisher? (2) Evidence — data and citations, or bare claims? (3) Currency — recent enough for the topic? (4) Objectivity — balanced or pushing an agenda? (+ Purpose: inform vs sell.)

Knowledge Note

Lateral reading — checking what OTHER reputable sites say about a source — beats judging any site by its own design.

6.

Distinguish between observation and inference with an example.

Marks

2

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Observation reports direct senses: 'The classroom floor is wet.' Inference explains it: '…so the roof must be leaking (or someone spilled water).'

Observations are checkable facts; inferences are best-guess explanations — science advances by testing inferences against new observations.

Knowledge Note

Multiple inferences can fit one observation — listing alternatives before concluding is the critical habit.
