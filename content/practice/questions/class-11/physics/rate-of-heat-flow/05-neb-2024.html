Questions

1.

Define conduction, convection, and radiation, giving one example of each.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Conduction: heat flows through a material by molecular collisions, no bulk motion — e.g. a metal spoon heating in tea.

Convection: heat carried by the bulk movement of fluid — e.g. hot air rising in a room.

Radiation: heat transferred as electromagnetic waves, needing no medium — e.g. warmth from the Sun.

Knowledge Note

Solids conduct; fluids convect; everything radiates — most real situations mix at least two.

2.

State Fourier's law of heat conduction and explain each term.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Fourier's law: Q/t = kA(ΔT/d).

Q/t = rate of heat flow (J/s); k = thermal conductivity of the material; A = cross-sectional area; ΔT = temperature difference between the ends; d = length along the flow.

Heat flows faster through short, fat, highly conducting bars with a steep temperature drop.

Knowledge Note

k has units W/m·K — copper (~400) vs air (~0.026) explains why metals feel cold and wool feels warm.

3.

A metal rod 0.5 m long with cross-section 4 cm² has its ends at 100°C and 0°C. If k = 400 W/m·K, find the rate of heat flow through the rod.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

A = 4 cm² = 4 × 10⁻⁴ m².

Q/t = kAΔT/d = (400 × 4 × 10⁻⁴ × 100)/0.5 = 16/0.5 = 32 W.

Knowledge Note

Steady state is assumed — the ends held at fixed temperatures so no heat accumulates in the rod.

4.

State Stefan's law of black-body radiation. If the absolute temperature of a body is doubled, by what factor does its radiated power increase?

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Stefan's law: the total energy radiated per unit area per second is E = σT⁴ (σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/m²K⁴).

Since E ∝ T⁴, doubling T multiplies the power by 2⁴ = 16 times.

Knowledge Note

T⁴ sensitivity is why a small rise in filament temperature makes a bulb dramatically brighter.

5.

Why are cooking utensils blackened at the bottom, and why do people prefer white clothes in summer?

Marks

2

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Black surfaces are the best absorbers (and emitters) of radiation: a blackened bottom absorbs the flame's radiant heat fastest, cooking food quicker.

White clothes reflect most of the Sun's radiation instead of absorbing it, keeping the wearer cooler — the same reason houses in hot regions are painted white.

Knowledge Note

Good absorber = good emitter (Kirchhoff's law) — which is also why blackened utensils cool faster once off the flame.

6.

State Newton's law of cooling and give one practical application.

Marks

2

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Newton's law of cooling: the rate of loss of heat of a body is directly proportional to the temperature difference between the body and its surroundings (for small differences).

Application: forensic estimation of time of death from body temperature, or judging how long tea has been standing from its temperature.

Knowledge Note

The law holds only for small ΔT and still air — forced draughts (a fan on hot tea) cool faster than it predicts.
