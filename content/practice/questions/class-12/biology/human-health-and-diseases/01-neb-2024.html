Questions

1.

Describe malaria with respect to its pathogen, vector, mode of transmission, symptoms and preventive measures.

Marks

5

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Pathogen: Plasmodium species (P. vivax, P. falciparum, P. malariae), a protozoan.

Vector: the female Anopheles mosquito, which transmits the infective sporozoites when it bites; the parasite develops in the mosquito's gut and salivary glands.

Symptoms: cyclic fever with chills and rigors every 48–72 h, headache, vomiting, anaemia and enlarged spleen as red cells are destroyed.

Prevention: destroy breeding sites (stagnant water), use mosquito nets and repellents, screen houses, and treat patients promptly with antimalarials (chloroquine/artemisinin combination).

Knowledge Note

The periodic fever corresponds to the synchronous rupture of infected red cells; P. falciparum causes the most dangerous cerebral malaria.

2.

Differentiate between innate and acquired immunity with one example of each.

Marks

4

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Innate (non-specific) immunity is present from birth and acts against any foreign agent: physical barriers (skin, mucous membranes), physiological barriers (acid in the stomach, lysozyme in tears), and cellular barriers (phagocytes, natural killer cells). It is not specific and has no memory — e.g. sneezing expelling dust and microbes.

Acquired (specific) immunity develops after exposure to an antigen; it is highly specific and shows memory. It is mediated by B lymphocytes (humoral, antibodies) and T lymphocytes (cell-mediated). Example: immunity to measles after an attack or after vaccination.

Knowledge Note

Antibody-mediated (humoral) immunity handles extracellular pathogens; cell-mediated immunity handles virus-infected cells and grafts.

3.

What is AIDS? Name the causative agent, state the modes of transmission and explain its effect on the body's defence.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

AIDS (Acquired Immuno Deficiency Syndrome) is a fatal viral disease in which the immune system is progressively destroyed.

Agent: Human Immunodeficiency Virus (HIV), a retrovirus with RNA, which uses reverse transcriptase to make DNA and integrates into the host genome.

Transmission: unprotected sexual contact, transfusion of infected blood, sharing needles, and from mother to child through the placenta or milk.

Effect: HIV infects and destroys helper T lymphocytes (CD4⁺), so the body loses cell-mediated immunity and succumbs to opportunistic infections such as tuberculosis and pneumonia.

Knowledge Note

Death is usually from an opportunistic infection, not from HIV itself — hence 'syndrome' rather than a single disease.
