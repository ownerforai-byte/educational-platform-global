Questions

1.

Define drift velocity and derive the relation I = neAv_d for a conductor.

Marks

4

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Drift velocity is the average velocity with which free electrons move opposite the field.

In time t, electrons travel v_d·t, so all electrons in a cylinder of volume A·v_d·t cross the section. Number = n·A·v_d·t carrying charge q = ne·A·v_d·t.

I = q/t = neAv_d. Since v_d = eEτ/m, also I = ne²AτE/m.

Knowledge Note

Drift speed is only ~10⁻⁴ m s⁻¹ while the signal travels near light speed — the field, not the electron, carries the message.

2.

State Kirchhoff's laws for electric circuits and use them to obtain the balance condition of a Wheatstone bridge.

Marks

5

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Junction rule: sum of currents entering = sum leaving (charge conservation). Loop rule: algebraic sum of potential changes round any closed loop is zero (energy conservation).

For the bridge, at balance no current flows through the galvanometer, so current divides into P,Q and R,S branches: I₁P = I₂R and I₁Q = I₂S. Dividing,

P/Q = R/S — the balance condition.

Knowledge Note

At balance the galvanometer branch may be removed or replaced by any resistance without changing the currents — the basis of the metre bridge.

3.

A cell of emf 2 V and internal resistance 0.5 Ω is connected to a 3.5 Ω resistor. Calculate the current in the circuit and the terminal potential difference across the cell.

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

I = E/(R + r) = 2/(3.5 + 0.5) = 0.5 A.

Terminal p.d. V = IR = 0.5 × 3.5 = 1.75 V (equivalently V = E − Ir = 2 − 0.5×0.5).

Knowledge Note

Terminal voltage is always less than emf while discharging; it equals emf only in an open circuit (I = 0).
