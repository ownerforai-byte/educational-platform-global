Questions

1.

State Markovnikov's rule and explain it on the basis of the carbocation mechanism; also state the peroxide (Kharasch) effect.

Marks

4

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

Markovnikov's rule: in the addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene, the negative part goes to the carbon carrying the smaller number of hydrogens.

Reason: the first step is protonation of the double bond, and the more stable carbocation decides the product. Addition of H⁺ to propene gives CH₃CH⁺CH₃ (2°, more stable) rather than CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺ (1°), so Br⁻ attaches to C-2 and the product is 2-bromopropane.

Peroxide effect: with peroxides the mechanism becomes free-radical, giving the anti-Markovnikov product (1-bromopropane) because the more stable secondary radical forms.

Knowledge Note

Only HBr shows the peroxide effect; HCl and HI add by the ionic path regardless, since their radical steps are unfavourable.

2.

Write the reactions of benzene with (a) conc. HNO₃/H₂SO₄, (b) Cl₂/FeCl₃, (c) CH₃Cl/anhydrous AlCl₃, and name the type of mechanism involved.

Marks

4

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution

All three are electrophilic substitution on the aromatic ring, though the ring's delocalised π electrons are preserved:

(a) nitration — the nitronium ion NO₂⁺ attacks, giving nitrobenzene;

(b) chlorination — the Cl⁺/FeCl₄⁻ complex gives chlorobenzene;

(c) Friedel–Crafts alkylation — the methyl cation gives toluene (with polyalkylation and rearrangement as side reactions).

Mechanism: electrophile attacks → arenium (σ-complex) intermediate → loss of H⁺ restores aromaticity.

Knowledge Note

Benzene prefers substitution to addition precisely because addition would destroy its aromatic stabilisation (about 150 kJ mol⁻¹).

3.

How will you distinguish between ethane, ethene and ethyne using simple chemical tests?

Marks

3

Year

2024

Source

NEB Model

Solution
  • Bromine water: decolourised by ethene and ethyne (addition), unchanged by ethane.
  • Ammoniacal AgNO₃ or Cu₂Cl₂: a white (Ag) or red-brown (Cu) precipitate identifies ethyne (terminal ≡CH, acidic hydrogen); ethene gives no precipitate.
  • Alkaline KMnO₄ (Baeyer's reagent): decolourised by both unsaturated gases, giving a diol — confirms unsaturation while distinguishing them from ethane.
Knowledge Note

Terminal alkynes are acidic because the sp-carbon holds the electron pair tightly — the basis of the silver/copper acetylide tests.
