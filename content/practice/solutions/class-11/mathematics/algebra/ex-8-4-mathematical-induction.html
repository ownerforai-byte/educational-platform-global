Questions

1.

Q1(a). If P(n) is '(n+1)(n+2) is an odd number', find P(1), P(2), P(3). Are they true?

Knowledge Note

Evaluate P(1), P(2), P(3) directly: (n+1)(n+2) is even for n = 1, 2, 3.

Marks

2

Solution

P(1): 2×3 = 6 (even, not odd — false). P(2): 3×4 = 12 (even — false). P(3): 4×5 = 20 (even — false). None is true: (n+1)(n+2) is always the product of consecutive integers, hence always even.

4.

Q2(i). By induction: 1+2+3+...+n = n(n+1)/2 for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

Base n = 1: 1 = 1·2/2; step adds (m+1) to both sides and factors (m+1)(m+2)/2.

Marks

3

Solution

Base: n = 1 gives 1 = 1·2/2 ✓. Assume true for m; add (m+1): LHS = m(m+1)/2+(m+1) = (m+1)(m+2)/2, the formula at m+1. Hence true for all n by induction.

Q2(v). By induction: 1^2+3^2+5^2+...+(2n-1)^2 = n(2n-1)(2n+1)/3 for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

4

Q3(ii). By induction: 1/(1.2)+1/(2.3)+...+1/(n(n+1)) = n/(n+1) for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

14.

Q4(i). Prove by induction that n^2+n is an even number for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

(m+1)^2+(m+1)-(m^2+m) = 2(m+1): the increment is visibly even.

Marks

3

Solution

Base: 1+1 = 2, even ✓. Assume m^2+m even; then (m+1)^2+(m+1) = (m^2+m)+2(m+1), even+even = even. Hence true for all n.

15.

Q4(ii). Prove by induction that n^3+2n is divisible by 3 for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

Increment: 3m^2+3m+3 = 3(m^2+m+1), a multiple of 3 on top of the hypothesis.

Marks

3

Solution

Base: 1+2 = 3 ✓. Assume 3 | m^3+2m; then (m+1)^3+2(m+1) = (m^3+2m)+3m^2+3m+3 = (m^3+2m)+3(m^2+m+1), divisible by 3. Hence true for all n.

16.

Q4(iii). Prove by induction that n(n+1)(n+2) is a multiple of 6 for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

Of three consecutive integers one is a multiple of 3 (and one even): use (m+1)(m+2)(m+3)-(previous) = 3(m+1)(m+2).

Marks

4

Solution

Base: 1·2·3 = 6 ✓. Assume 6 | m(m+1)(m+2); then (m+1)(m+2)(m+3) = m(m+1)(m+2)+3(m+1)(m+2). Now (m+1)(m+2) is even (consecutive integers), so 3(m+1)(m+2) is a multiple of 6; adding the hypothesis keeps a multiple of 6. Hence true for all n.

Source

Sukunda Basic Mathematics Grade XI, Ch.8 Ex 8.4 Q4(iii); book p237 = PDF p264 (vision read)

Q5(i). By induction prove that 3^{2n}-1 is divisible by 8 for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

Marks

4

Solution

Base: 3^2-1 = 8 ✓. Assume 8 | 3^{2m}-1; then 3^{2(m+1)}-1 = 9·3^{2m}-1 = 9(3^{2m}-1)+8, divisible by 8. Hence true for all n.

18.

Q5(ii). By induction prove that x^n-y^n is divisible by x-y for all natural n.

Knowledge Note

x^{m+1}-y^{m+1} = x(x^m-y^m)+y^m(x-y): both pieces carry (x-y).

Marks

4

Solution

Base: x-y divides itself ✓. Assume (x-y) | x^m-y^m; then x^{m+1}-y^{m+1} = x(x^m-y^m)+y^m(x-y), and both terms are multiples of (x-y). Hence true for all n.
