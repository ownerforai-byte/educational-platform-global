Questions

1.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve x^2 = 4by and the ordinates x = a and x = b.

Knowledge Note

Solve for y first: the only y on a parabola of this form is the positive square-root-free branch, so the area is a plain power integral.

Marks

2

Solution

From x^2 = 4by, y = x^2/(4b). Area = integral from a to b of x^2/(4b) dx = (1/(4b))[x^3/3]_a^b = (b^3 - a^3)/(12b). Boxed (printed key p605: (b^3 - a^3)/12b). Positive when b > a > 0, otherwise the integral is negative and its modulus is the area.

2.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve y = 4x^3 and the ordinates x = 2 and x = 4.

Knowledge Note

Pure power rule; the whole curve is above the x-axis on [2,4] so no sign correction is needed.

Marks

2

Solution

Area = integral from 2 to 4 of 4x^3 dx = [x^4]_2^4 = 256 - 16 = 240. Boxed (printed key p605).

3.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve y = 3x^2 - 2 and the ordinates x = 1 and x = 4.

Knowledge Note

A parabola whose root is at x = sqrt(2/3) < 1, so the whole interval [1,4] lies above the axis and the integral is the area directly.

Marks

2

Solution

Area = [x^3 - 2x]_1^4 = (64 - 8) - (1 - 2) = 56 - (-1) = 57. Boxed (printed key p605).

4.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve y^2 - x - 4 = 0 and the ordinates x = 2 and x = 5.

Knowledge Note

Take the positive branch y = sqrt(x+4); the radical integrates by the power rule with exponent 1/2.

Marks

3

Solution

The curve is y^2 = x + 4, so above the axis y = sqrt(x+4). Area = integral from 2 to 5 of (x+4)^(1/2) dx = (2/3)[(x+4)^(3/2)]_2^5 = (2/3)(9^(3/2) - 6^(3/2)) = (2/3)(27 - 6 sqrt 6) = 18 - 4 sqrt 6 = 2(9 - 2 sqrt 6). Boxed (printed key p605).

5.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve y = e^(ax) and the ordinates x = b and x = c. (HSEB 2050, 2052)

Knowledge Note

Exponential with a linear exponent: the antiderivative is e^(ax)/a.

Marks

2

Solution

Area = integral from b to c of e^(ax) dx = (1/a)[e^(ax)]_b^c = (1/a)(e^(ca) - e^(ba)). Boxed (printed key p605).

6.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve y = log(1 + x) and the ordinates x = 0 and x = 1.

Knowledge Note

Integral of a logarithm is a by-parts standard form: integral log u du = u log u - u.

Marks

3

Solution

Substitute u = 1 + x (du = dx, limits 1 to 2): area = integral from 1 to 2 of log u du = [u log u - u]_1^2 = (2 log 2 - 2) - (0 - 1) = 2 log 2 - 1. Boxed (printed key p605).

7.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve y^2 = 8ax and the ordinate at the point (4a, 0).

Knowledge Note

The 'ordinate at the point' means the x-coordinate of that point, x = 4a; take the upper branch y = sqrt(8ax).

Marks

3

Solution

Since (4a, 0) lies on the curve, the ordinate is x = 4a. Area = integral from 0 to 4a of sqrt(8ax) dx = sqrt(8a) (2/3)[x^(3/2)]_0^{4a} = sqrt(8a) (2/3)(4a)^(3/2) = sqrt(8a) (2/3) 8a^(3/2) = (2/3) 8 2 sqrt 2 * a^2 = (32 sqrt 2/3) a^2. Boxed (printed key p605).

8.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve y^2 = 4a(x - a) and the ordinate at the point (h, k).

Knowledge Note

The parabola has vertex (a, 0), so the integration starts at a - the width of the region is (h - a).

Marks

4

Solution

The point (h, k) satisfies k^2 = 4a(h - a), so its ordinate is x = h and h >= a. Area = integral from a to h of sqrt(4a(x-a)) dx = 2 sqrt a * (2/3)[(x-a)^(3/2)]_a^h = (4/3) sqrt a (h - a)^(3/2). Boxed (printed key p605).

9.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve x^2 = 4by and the ordinate at the point (b, 0).

Knowledge Note

Same parabola as Q1(a) with the upper limit fixed at x = b.

Marks

2

Solution

From x^2 = 4by, y = x^2/(4b); the ordinate through (b,0) is x = b. Area = integral from 0 to b of x^2/(4b) dx = (1/(4b))(b^3/3) = b^2/12. Boxed (printed key p605).

10.

Find the area enclosed by the x-axis and the curve y = 3x - 5x^2. (HSEB 2055)

Knowledge Note

Factor to find the limits: x(3 - 5x) = 0 gives 0 and 3/5, the two points where the curve meets the axis.

Marks

3

Solution

Solving 3x - 5x^2 = 0 gives x = 0 and x = 3/5. Area = integral from 0 to 3/5 of (3x - 5x^2) dx = [3x^2/2 - 5x^3/3]_0^{3/5} = 27/50 - 27/75 = 27/50 - 18/50 = 9/50. Boxed (printed key p605).

11.

Find the area enclosed by the x-axis and the curve y = x^2 - 8x + 15.

Knowledge Note

Between its roots an upward parabola lies below the axis, so the integral must be taken with a minus sign.

Marks

3

Solution

x^2 - 8x + 15 = (x - 3)(x - 5), so the curve cuts the axis at 3 and 5. Area = integral from 3 to 5 of -(x^2 - 8x + 15) dx = -[x^3/3 - 4x^2 + 15x]_3^5 = -{(125/3 - 100 + 75) - (9 - 36 + 45)} = -{(125/3 - 25) - 18} = -{50/3 - 18} = -(50/3 - 54/3) = 4/3. Boxed (printed key p605).

12.

Find the area enclosed by the x-axis and the curve y = x^2 - 10x + 24.

Knowledge Note

Same shape as Q3(ii) with roots 4 and 6; a useful check is that shifting the parabola does not change the area.

Marks

3

Solution

x^2 - 10x + 24 = (x - 4)(x - 6), roots 4 and 6, parabola above the axis outside [4,6]. Area = integral from 4 to 6 of -(x^2 - 10x + 24) dx = -[x^3/3 - 5x^2 + 24x]_4^6 = -{(72 - 180 + 144) - (64/3 - 80 + 96)} = -{36 - (64/3 + 16)} = -{36 - 112/3} = -(108/3 - 112/3) = 4/3. Boxed (printed key p605).

13.

Find the area enclosed by the x-axis and the curve y = x^2 - 25.

Knowledge Note

Symmetric interval: integrate from 0 to 5 and double, since the integrand is even.

Marks

3

Solution

Roots at x = +-5, and the parabola lies below the axis between them. Area = integral from -5 to 5 of (25 - x^2) dx = 2[25x - x^3/3]_0^5 = 2(125 - 125/3) = 2(250/3) = 500/3 = 166 2/3. Boxed (printed key p605: 166 2/3).

14.

Find the area enclosed by the x-axis and the curve y = (x - 1)(x - 2)(x - 3).

Knowledge Note

A cubic with three simple roots changes sign at each: the total enclosed area is the sum of the absolute values of the pieces.

Marks

4

Solution

Roots 1, 2, 3. On (1,2) the product of three factors is positive; on (2,3) it is negative. Expand: y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 11x - 6, antiderivative x^4/4 - 2x^3 + 11x^2/2 - 6x. Piece (1,2): F(2) - F(1) = (4 - 16 + 22 - 12) - (1/4 - 2 + 11/2 - 6) = (-2) - (-1/4 - 2 + 5.5 - 6) = -2 - (-2.75) = 0.75 = 3/4. Piece (2,3): F(3) - F(2) = (81/4 - 54 + 49.5 - 18) - (-2) = (20.25 - 22.5) + 2 = -2.25 + 2 = -0.25 = -1/4, whose modulus is 1/4. Total area = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1/2. Boxed (printed key p605: 1/2).

Source

Sukunda Basic Mathematics Grade XI, Ch.19 Exercise 19.7 Q3(v); book p604-605 = PDF p631-632; key book p605 = PDF p632

15.

Find the area of the region between the curve y^2 = 16x and the line y = 2x. (HSEB 2053, 056)

Knowledge Note

Subtract the two y-values between the intersection abscissae; the parabola wins near 0, the line near 1, and they cross at 4.

Marks

3

Solution

Intersections: (2x)^2 = 16x gives 4x^2 = 16x, so x = 0 and x = 4 (y = 0 and 8). Between them the parabola's upper branch y = 4 sqrt x lies above y = 2x. Area = integral from 0 to 4 of (4 sqrt x - 2x) dx = [4(2/3)x^(3/2) - x^2]_0^4 = (8/3)(8) - 16 = 64/3 - 16 = 16/3 = 5 1/3. Boxed (printed key p605: 5 1/3).

16.

Find the area of the region between the curve y^2 = 4x and the line x = y.

Knowledge Note

Integrating with respect to y avoids splitting the parabola into branches: right curve minus left curve, dy.

Marks

3

Solution

x = y is the same as y = x, and y^2 = 4x gives x = y^2/4. They meet where y = y^2/4, i.e. y = 0 and y = 4. Area = integral from 0 to 4 of (y - y^2/4) dy = [y^2/2 - y^3/12]_0^4 = 8 - 64/12 = 8 - 16/3 = 8/3 = 2 2/3. Boxed (printed key p605: 2 2/3).

17.

Find the area of the region between the curve y^2 = 4ax and the line x = a.

Knowledge Note

The chord is vertical, so y is the natural variable; the region is symmetric about the x-axis.

Marks

3

Solution

At x = a the parabola gives y^2 = 4a^2, so y = +-2a. For a fixed y the region runs from x = y^2/(4a) to x = a. Area = integral from -2a to 2a of (a - y^2/(4a)) dy = [ay - y^3/(12a)]_{-2a}^{2a} = (2a^2 - 8a^3/(12a)) - (-2a^2 + 8a^3/(12a)) = (2a^2 - 2a^2/3) + (2a^2 - 2a^2/3) = 8a^2/3. Boxed (printed key p605).

18.

Find the area of the region between the curve y = x^3 and the line x = y lying in the first quadrant.

Knowledge Note

x = y is the line y = x; two intersections 0 and 1, and a quick test point (x = 1/2) shows which curve is on top.

Marks

2

Solution

Intersections: x^3 = x, so x = 0 and x = 1 (first quadrant). At x = 1/2 the line gives 1/2 and the cubic 1/8, so the line is above. Area = integral from 0 to 1 of (x - x^3) dx = [x^2/2 - x^4/4]_0^1 = 1/2 - 1/4 = 1/4. Boxed (printed key p605).

19.

Find the area of the region between the curve y^2 = x^3 and the line x = 4.

Knowledge Note

y^2 = x^3 gives the two branches y = +-x^(3/2) for x >= 0; the region between them is symmetric.

Marks

3

Solution

For x in [0,4] the curve has y = +-x^(3/2), meeting x = 4 at y = +-8. Area = 2 integral from 0 to 4 of x^(3/2) dx = 2 * (2/5)[x^(5/2)]_0^4 = (4/5)(32) = 128/5 = 25 3/5. Boxed (printed key p605: 25 3/5).

20.

Find the area of the region between the curves y^2 = 4ax and x^2 = 4ay. (HSEB 2058)

Knowledge Note

Two parabolas, one opening sideways and one upwards; solve simultaneously to get the second intersection x = 4a.

Marks

4

Solution

Substituting x = y^2/(4a) into x^2 = 4ay gives y^4/(16a^2) = 4ay, so y^3 = 64a^3 and y = 4a; the points are (0,0) and (4a, 4a). On (0, 4a) the upper curve is y = 2 sqrt(ax). Area = integral from 0 to 4a of (2 sqrt(ax) - x^2/(4a)) dx = [2 sqrt a (2/3)x^(3/2) - x^3/(12a)]_0^{4a} = (4/3) sqrt a (4a)^(3/2) - (64a^3)/(12a) = (4/3)(8a^2) - 16a^2/3 = (32/3)a^2 - (16/3)a^2 = 16a^2/3. Boxed (printed key p605).

21.

Find the area of the circle x^2 + y^2 = 16.

Knowledge Note

For a circle the integral reproduces pi r^2; the substitution x = r sin theta is the clean way to see the pi appear.

Marks

3

Solution

Radius r = 4, centre the origin, so the area is pi r^2 = 16 pi. Derivation: area = 2 integral from -4 to 4 of sqrt(16 - x^2) dx; with x = 4 sin theta, dx = 4 cos theta d theta and limits +-pi/2, this is 2 16 integral cos^2 theta d theta = 32 (pi/2) = 16 pi. Boxed (printed key p605).

22.

Find the area of the circle x^2 + y^2 = a^2.

Knowledge Note

The same computation with r = a: the answer is the standard circle-area formula pi a^2.

Marks

2

Solution

Radius a. Area = 2 integral from -a to a of sqrt(a^2 - x^2) dx = 2 * (pi a^2/2) = pi a^2 (using x = a sin theta, integral of cos^2 over [-pi/2, pi/2] = pi/2). Boxed (printed key p605).

23.

Find the area of the ellipse x^2/a^2 + y^2/b^2 = 1.

Knowledge Note

Four quarters by symmetry, then the circle-type substitution x = a sin theta: the result is the classic pi ab.

Marks

4

Solution

By symmetry the area is 4 times the first-quadrant part, where y = (b/a) sqrt(a^2 - x^2). So area = 4(b/a) integral from 0 to a of sqrt(a^2 - x^2) dx. With x = a sin theta, dx = a cos theta d theta and limits 0 to pi/2: integral = a^2 integral cos^2 theta d theta = a^2 (pi/4). Therefore area = 4(b/a)(a^2 pi/4) = pi ab. Boxed (printed key p605).

24.

Find the area bounded by the axes of coordinates, the curve x^2 = 4a(y - 2a) and the ordinate at the point (h, k).

Knowledge Note

The point (h,k) on the curve fixes the vertical line x = h; the lower boundary is the x-axis, the upper boundary the shifted parabola.

Marks

4

Solution

From x^2 = 4a(y - 2a), y = x^2/(4a) + 2a: a parabola with vertex (0, 2a) opening upwards. The region is bounded by the x-axis, the y-axis (x = 0) and the ordinate x = h through (h, k). Area = integral from 0 to h of (x^2/(4a) + 2a) dx = h^3/(12a) + 2ah = (h/(12a))(h^2 + 24a^2). Boxed (printed key p605).

25.

Find the area bounded by the y-axis, the curve x^2 = 4by and y = b.

Knowledge Note

The y-axis is one boundary, so integrate x as a function of y: x = 2 sqrt(by).

Marks

3

Solution

From x^2 = 4by, x = 2 sqrt(by) for x >= 0. The region lies between the y-axis (x = 0) and the curve, from y = 0 to y = b. Area = integral from 0 to b of 2 sqrt(by) dy = 2 sqrt b (2/3)[y^(3/2)]_0^b = (4/3) sqrt b b^(3/2) = 4b^2/3. Boxed (printed key p605).

26.

Find the area bounded by the y-axis, the curve x^2 = 4a(y - 2a) and y = 6a.

Knowledge Note

The parabola's vertex is at y = 2a, so the region starts there and runs up to y = 6a; x = 2 sqrt(a(y-2a)).

Marks

3

Solution

From x^2 = 4a(y - 2a), x = 2 sqrt(a(y - 2a)) with vertex (0, 2a). Area = integral from 2a to 6a of 2 sqrt(a(y - 2a)) dy = 2 sqrt a (2/3)[(y - 2a)^(3/2)]_{2a}^{6a} = (4/3) sqrt a (4a)^(3/2) = (4/3) sqrt a 8a^(3/2) = 32a^2/3. Boxed (printed key p605).

27.

Find the area bounded by the x-axis, the curve x = at^2, y = 2at and the ordinates corresponding to t = 0 to t = 1.

Knowledge Note

For a parametric curve, area = integral y (dx/dt) dt between the parameter values - here the limits are given directly as t = 0 and t = 1.

Marks

3

Solution

x = at^2 gives dx = 2at dt, and y = 2at. Area = integral from t = 0 to 1 of y dx = integral from 0 to 1 of (2at)(2at) dt = 4a^2 integral t^2 dt = 4a^2/3. Boxed (printed key p605).
