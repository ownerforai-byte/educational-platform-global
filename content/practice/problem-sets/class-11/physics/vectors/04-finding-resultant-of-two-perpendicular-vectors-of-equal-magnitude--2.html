Finding the Resultant of Two Perpendicular Vectors of Equal Magnitude

Class 11PhysicsVectors

Notes

  • If two vectors of equal magnitude act perpendicular to each other, find the resultant. Let ∣A∣=∣B∣=a and θ=90°. Using R=A2+B2+2ABcosθ​=a2+a2+2a2cos90°​=2a2​=a2​. Direction: tanα=a+acos90°asin90°​=aa​=1⇒α=45° — the resultant bisects the angle between the two equal perpendicular vectors.
  • Q. If two equal vectors act perpendicular to each other and their resultant is 1.414 (≈2​) units, find the magnitude of each vector. Using R=a2​ from above: a=2​R​=2​2​​=1 unit — each vector has magnitude 1 unit.
  • For two vectors of equal magnitude a at right angles, the parallelogram formula loses the middle term because cos90° = 0. R = sqrt(a^2 + a^2) = a√2.
  • The direction from either vector satisfies tanα = a/a = 1, so α = 45°. The resultant is exactly between them.
  • This is the same statement as adding aî and aĵ to get aî + aĵ, whose magnitude is a√2 and whose direction is 45° to each axis.
  • If a measured resultant of two such vectors is 1.414 units, which is √2 to three decimals, then a√2 = √2 and each vector has magnitude 1.

Confusion

  • a√2 is not 2a.
  • 45° is from each vector, so the angle between the vectors is still 90°.
  • √2 ≈ 1.414, not 1.732.
  • Parallel vectors of magnitude a give 2a, a larger resultant.

Key Points

  • cos90° = 0.
  • R = sqrt(2) times either vector.
  • The resultant bisects the right angle.
  • If R is √2, each vector has magnitude 1.

Exercises

1.

Two equal vectors act at 90°. Their resultant is 1.414 units. Find each magnitude and the direction of the resultant.

Steps
  • For equal perpendicular vectors, R = a√2.
  • 1.414 is √2 to three decimals, so a√2 = √2.
  • Therefore a = 1.
  • The resultant bisects the 90° angle.
  • It lies at 45° to each vector.
Answer

Each vector has magnitude 1. The resultant has magnitude √2 and lies at 45° to each.

Summary

Equal vectors at right angles have resultant a√2 directed halfway between them. The cosine term drops out because cos90° is zero. A resultant of √2 means each vector has magnitude 1.
