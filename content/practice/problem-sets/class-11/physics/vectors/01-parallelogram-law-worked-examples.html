Notes

  • Q1. Two equal vectors have a resultant equal in magnitude to either vector. Find the angle between them. Let P=Q=x and R=x. Then x2=x2+x2+2x2cosθ⇒cosθ=−21​⇒θ=120A^°.
  • Q2. Two equal vectors are inclined at angle θ to each other. Show that the angle α between the resultant and each vector is θ/2. With P=Q: tanα=Q+QcosθQsinθ​=1+cosθsinθ​. Using half-angle identities sinθ=2sin(θ/2)cos(θ/2) and 1+cosθ=2cos2(θ/2), this simplifies to tanα=tan(θ/2), so α=θ/2.
  • The parallelogram law gives the resultant of two vectors P and Q with angle θ between them: R^2 = P^2 + Q^2 + 2PQ cosθ. The direction from P satisfies tanα = Q sinθ / (P + Q cosθ).
  • If the vectors are equal and the resultant equals either of them, set P = Q = R = x. Then x^2 = x^2 + x^2 + 2x^2 cosθ, so cosθ = −1/2 and θ = 120°.
  • That angle is obtuse. The two vectors are not opposite, because opposite vectors would give a zero resultant, and they are not perpendicular, because perpendicular equal vectors give x√2.
  • For the direction of the resultant of two equal vectors, put P = Q in the tanα formula. tanα = sinθ / (1 + cosθ).

Confusion

  • 120° is not 60°. The cosine is negative.
  • α is the angle between the resultant and one vector, not the angle between the two vectors.
  • Equal magnitudes are required for the bisector result.
  • R = P does not mean the second vector is zero.

Exercises

1.

Two equal vectors have a resultant equal in magnitude to either. Find the angle between them, then show that the resultant bisects that angle.

Steps
  • Let each magnitude be x, and let the resultant also be x.
  • x^2 = x^2 + x^2 + 2x^2 cosθ.
  • cosθ = −1/2, so θ = 120°.
  • For equal vectors, tanα = sinθ / (1 + cosθ) = tan(θ/2).
  • So α = θ/2 = 60°. The resultant bisects the 120° angle.
Answer

120° between the vectors, and 60° between the resultant and either vector.

Summary

The parallelogram law gives both the size and the direction of a resultant. Two equal vectors whose resultant equals either of them stand at 120°. The resultant of any two equal vectors lies on the angle bisector.
