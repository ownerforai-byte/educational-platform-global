Q: State the assumptions behind Newton's Law of Cooling

Notes

  • The temperature difference between the body and surroundings is small
  • Heat is lost mainly by convection and radiation, not conduction
  • The surrounding temperature remains constant throughout cooling
  • No phase change occurs in the body during cooling
  • Newton's law of cooling, that the rate of fall of temperature is proportional to the excess over the surroundings, is an approximation. It needs a small excess, a steady room, no change of state, and heat loss dominated by convection and radiation rather than by a changing conduction path.
  • The working relations are The excess temperature must be small and The surroundings stay at one temperature. Name each symbol, convert the data into SI units, and only then substitute.
  • List the four assumptions and say which one fails if the body is 300 K above the room. The excess temperature is supposed to be small. The surroundings stay at one temperature.
  • There is no change of state. Heat leaves mainly by convection and radiation. A 300 K excess is not small, so the linear law fails and the full radiation difference, proportional to T^4, is needed.

Confusion

  • A symbol is not a number until the unit is named.
  • A proportional relation is not an equality until the constant is included.
  • A vector answer needs a direction as well as a magnitude.
  • A special case, such as a right angle or a steady temperature, has to be stated before it is used.

Exercises

1.

List the four assumptions and say which one fails if the body is 300 K above the room.

Steps
  • The excess temperature is supposed to be small.
  • The surroundings stay at one temperature.
  • There is no change of state.
  • Heat leaves mainly by convection and radiation.
  • A 300 K excess is not small, so the linear law fails and the full radiation difference, proportional to T^4, is needed.
Answer

The small-excess assumption fails for a 300 K difference.

Summary

Newton's law of cooling assumes a small excess temperature, steady surroundings, no change of state, and loss mainly by convection and radiation. A body 300 K above the room breaks the small-excess assumption.
