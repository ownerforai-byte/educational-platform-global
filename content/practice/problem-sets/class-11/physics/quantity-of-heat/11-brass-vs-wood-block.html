Notes

  • Setup: a brass block (100 g) and a wooden block (100 g) are both initially at 100Â°C and left in the same room
  • Wood has a much lower thermal conductivity and typically a lower specific heat capacity than brass, but under Newton's Law of Cooling the key factor is mÂ·c: a lower mÂ·c means a faster rate of temperature fall for the same rate of heat loss
  • This is exactly why 'water equivalent' is useful â€” instead of tracking each material's own c, every object can be converted to 'an equivalent mass of water' (W = mc/c_w), letting heat calculations for mixtures of different materials be done as if everything were water
  • A 100 g brass block and a 100 g wooden block, both at 100°C in the same room, do not cool at the same rate. Newton's law makes the rate of fall proportional to K/(mc) times the excess.
  • If the surfaces were identical, the block with the smaller specific heat would have the smaller mc and would lose temperature faster. Wood's specific heat and its poor conductivity both matter in a real block, because the inside of the wood does not stay at one temperature as easily as brass.
  • For a body that is all at one temperature, the mc factor is the one the law names. A smaller heat capacity means each joule that leaves causes a larger drop in temperature.

Confusion

  • The blocks start at the same temperature. The one that feels as if it 'holds heat' longer has the larger mc or the poorer coupling to the air.
  • Conductivity and specific heat are different properties.
  • Water equivalent is not the mass of the block.
  • Equal masses do not mean equal cooling rates.

Exercises

1.

A metal block has m = 0.10 kg and c = 400 J kg^-1 K^-1. Find its water equivalent. Take c_water = 4200 J kg^-1 K^-1.

Steps
  • W = mc / c_water.
  • mc = 0.10 × 400 = 40 J/K.
  • W = 40 / 4200 kg.
  • W = 0.0095 kg = 9.5 g.
  • The block stores heat like 9.5 g of water.
Answer

9.5 g of water.

Summary

Under the same surface conditions the block with the smaller heat capacity loses temperature faster. Brass stays closer to one temperature because it conducts well. Water equivalent rewrites a heat capacity as the mass of water with the same mc.
