State Faraday's first and second laws of electrolysis. A current of 2A passed for 30 minutes deposits 2.22g of a metal (valency 2). Calculate the metal's atomic mass. (F=96500C/mol)

Solution

First law: mass deposited is proportional to charge passed, w=ZQ. Second law: masses deposited by the same charge are proportional to chemical equivalents E=A/valency. Charge Q=It=2×1800=3600C. Moles of electrons =Q/F=3600/96500=0.0373mol. M2++2e−→M, so moles of metal =0.0373/2=0.01865mol. Atomic mass =2.22/0.01865≈119g/mol (tin).

4.

Explain rusting of iron in terms of electrochemistry (anodic and cathodic regions). Give two methods of preventing corrosion with the principle behind each.

ID

che-electro-2022-4

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

hard

Solution

Rusting is an electrochemical cell on the iron surface. At anodic spots iron oxidises: Fe→Fe2++2e−. Electrons flow through the metal to cathodic spots where oxygen is reduced: O2​+2H2​O+4e−→4OH−. Fe2+ and OH− diffuse together and precipitate as Fe(OH)2​, which oxidises further to hydrated ferric oxide Fe2​O3​⋅xH2​O (rust). Water with dissolved CO2​/salts acts as the electrolyte. Prevention: (1) Barrier methods — painting, oiling or galvanising (zinc coating) keep water and oxygen away from iron. (2) Sacrificial protection — connecting iron to a more reactive metal (Mg/Zn) makes iron the cathode of the cell so it cannot oxidise; the sacrificial metal corrodes instead.
