Questions

1.

What is plant breeding? Describe its main steps.

ID

bio-foo-2025-1

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Plant breeding is the deliberate genetic improvement of crop plants to raise yield and quality, and to add resistance to disease, pests and stress. Steps: (1) collection and evaluation of genetic variability — germplasm collection from wild relatives and landraces; (2) selection of parents with the desired characters; (3) hybridisation — crossing them to combine traits; (4) selection in the segregating generations, often by pedigree or bulk method, until the line is uniform; (5) testing for yield, quality and resistance in multi-location trials; (6) release and seed multiplication. Mutation breeding, polyploidy and, more recently, marker-assisted selection and genetic engineering speed up or widen the process.

2.

Explain the types of hybridisation used in plant breeding and give two achievements of the green revolution in India and Nepal.

Type

short

Marks

Difficulty

Solution

Hybridisation may be intraspecific (crossing two varieties of the same species), interspecific (two species of a genus) or intergeneric (two genera, used to transfer disease resistance from wild relatives). It may be a simple cross, a back-cross to recover the recurrent parent, or a three-way and double cross in self-pollinated crops. Achievements: semi-dwarf, fertiliser-responsive Mexican wheat (Sonora-64, Lerma Rojo) and IR8 rice raised yields several-fold and made the green revolution possible; in Nepal improved rice (Radha-4, Sabitri) and wheat varieties spread widely, and hybrid maize and disease-resistant varieties raised production — though at the cost of heavier input use.

3.

What is single cell protein? Name two organisms used and give its advantages.

Type

short

Marks

Solution

Single cell protein (SCP) is protein extracted from the cells of microorganisms — algae, yeasts, fungi or bacteria — grown in bulk culture and used as a protein-rich food or feed supplement. Organisms: the alga Spirulina and the yeast Saccharomyces cerevisiae; also Methylophilus methylotrophus (bacterium) and the fungus Fusarium. Advantages: fast growth gives high yield on cheap substrates including waste (molasses, whey, methanol, straw hydrolysate); it is rich in protein and B vitamins and can be produced in a small area independent of weather; it recycles waste and reduces pollution. Limits are the cost of processing, possible nucleic acid excess and consumer acceptance.

Solution

Animal husbandry is the scientific management of farm animals — feeding, breeding, housing, health care and record keeping — to obtain milk, meat, eggs, wool and draught power. Cattle: indigenous breeds such as Sahiwal and Red Sindhi (disease-resistant, good for hot climates) and exotic Jersey and Holstein-Friesian (high milk yield) are crossed for combined merit; breeding is by artificial insemination, with attention to balanced feed, clean water, housing and vaccination against foot-and-mouth and haemorrhagic septicaemia. Poultry: layers such as Leghorn and broilers such as Plymouth Rock are raised with controlled temperature, balanced feed, clean litter, vaccination against Ranikhet and culling of diseased birds.

What are biofertilisers? Name three types and state their advantages.

Marks

Solution

Biofertilisers are preparations of living microorganisms that enrich the soil with nutrients, mainly nitrogen or phosphorus, when applied to seed or soil. Types: nitrogen-fixing bacteria — Rhizobium (symbiotic in legume root nodules) and Azotobacter and Azospirillum (free-living); cyanobacteria such as Anabaena and Azolla (used in paddy fields); phosphate-solubilising bacteria and mycorrhizal fungi, which make insoluble phosphorus available. Advantages: they are cheap, renewable and non-polluting, reduce the need for chemical nitrogen, improve soil structure and water holding, and are suited to small farmers; they have no harmful residues and often suppress soil-borne pathogens.

What is sustainable agriculture? Give four practices that make agriculture sustainable.

Solution

Sustainable agriculture is farming that meets present food needs without damaging the soil, water, biodiversity or climate on which future production depends — it balances productivity with ecological and social costs. Practices: crop rotation and mixed cropping with legumes to restore nitrogen; use of organic manure, compost and biofertilisers instead of only chemical fertiliser; integrated pest management with biological control and resistant varieties; conservation of soil and water by terracing, contour bunding and mulching; and agroforestry, drip irrigation and use of local seed to reduce inputs and risk.
