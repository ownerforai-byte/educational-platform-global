Questions

1.

Name the microorganisms used in four household products and state what each does.

ID

bio-mhw-2025-1

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

easy

Solution

Curd: Lactobacillus bulgaricus and Streptococcus thermophilus ferment milk sugar to lactic acid, which curdles the casein and gives curd its taste; vitamin B12 is also increased. Bread: Saccharomyces cerevisiae (baker's yeast) ferments sugar, and the CO2 released makes the dough rise while alcohol evaporates during baking. Cheese: Lactococcus and Streptococcus with Propionibacterium shermanii give the flavour and the holes of Swiss cheese; Penicillium roqueforti gives blue cheese. Fermented batter and beverages: Leuconostoc and yeasts ferment rice and black gram batter for idli and dosa, while yeast ferments palm sap for toddy. These microbes also preserve food by producing acid that inhibits spoilage organisms.

2.

Describe the industrial production of an antibiotic, of alcohol and of citric acid.

ID

bio-mhw-2025-2

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Antibiotic: Penicillium notatum or P. chrysogenum is grown in a stirred, aerated fermenter on a medium containing lactose and corn steep liquor; penicillin is secreted, extracted into an organic solvent at acid pH and purified as a salt. Antibiotics are also made by Streptomyces (streptomycin) and Cephalosporium. Alcohol: Saccharomyces cerevisiae ferments molasses or fruit juice anaerobically to ethanol, which is distilled to the required strength for beverages or fuel. Citric acid: Aspergillus niger ferments a sucrose or molasses medium in aerobic submerged culture; the acid is precipitated as calcium citrate and recovered. These products are made in large bioreactors with controlled pH, temperature and aeration.

3.

How are microbes used as biocontrol agents and as biofertilisers?

ID

bio-mhw-2025-3

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Biocontrol: Bacillus thuringiensis sprays kill caterpillars of moths and butterflies because its toxin paralyses the gut, while leaving other insects untouched; Trichoderma harzianum controls soil-borne fungal pathogens; the nuclear polyhedrosis virus attacks insect larvae; and ladybirds and Trichogramma are released to eat or parasitise pests. These agents reduce chemical pesticide load and do not leave residues. Biofertilisers: Rhizobium in legume root nodules, free-living Azotobacter and Azospirillum, and cyanobacteria such as Anabaena and Azolla fix atmospheric nitrogen; phosphate-solubilising bacteria release bound phosphorus and mycorrhizal fungi extend the root's reach for phosphorus and water.

4.

Explain bioremediation and the production of biogas by microbes.

ID

bio-mhw-2025-4

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Bioremediation is the use of microorganisms to remove or neutralise pollutants: Pseudomonas and other bacteria break down oil spills and petroleum hydrocarbons, some strains degrade polychlorinated compounds and pesticides, and others remove heavy metals or cyanide from effluent. Fungi such as Aspergillus help compost organic waste, and phytoremediation uses plants together with rhizosphere microbes. Biogas: methanogens — Methanobacterium in anaerobic digestion — decompose cattle dung, sewage sludge and crop waste in a closed digester, producing biogas (about 60–70% methane and rest CO2) that is used for cooking, lighting or running engines. The spent slurry is a rich organic manure, so the process gives clean fuel and fertiliser while disposing of waste.

5.

Describe the stages of sewage water treatment.

ID

bio-mhw-2025-5

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Primary treatment (physical): bar screens and grit chambers remove solids, and the sewage passes to a settling tank where suspended matter settles as primary sludge and grease is skimmed off; the liquid still carries dissolved organic matter. Secondary or biological treatment: the effluent is aerated in an aeration tank where aerobic bacteria and fungi, forming flocs, digest the dissolved organic matter, and the biomass then settles in another tank — activated sludge. The clarified water has a much lower BOD and can be discharged. Anaerobic digestion of the sludge with methanogens produces biogas and digested sludge used as manure; sometimes a tertiary step of chemical treatment or filtration removes phosphorus, nitrogen or pathogens.

6.

Why do some cured and fermented foods keep better than fresh ones?

ID

bio-mhw-2025-6

Type

mcq

Marks

2

Difficulty

easy

Solution

Fermenting microbes convert sugars into acids or alcohol — lactic acid in curd, pickles and sauerkraut, ethanol in wines — and so lower the pH or raise the alcohol level until most spoilage and pathogenic bacteria cannot grow. The food is therefore protected by the very process that gives it its flavour, and the useful microbes also outcompete spoilers for nutrients. This is why curd, cheese, fermented batter and pickles last much longer than raw milk, vegetables or batter.
