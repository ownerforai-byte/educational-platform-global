1.

In Young's double-slit experiment, if the distance between the slits and the screen is doubled while everything else is unchanged, the fringe width: (A) halves (B) doubles (C) stays the same (D) becomes four times

ID

phy-wav-2025-1

Type

mcq

Marks

1

Difficulty

easy

Solution

Fringe width β=dλD​, so β∝D.

Doubling D doubles β. Answer (B). (Doubling the slit separation d would halve it; changing the source intensity affects brightness, not spacing.)

3.

In Young's experiment, light of wavelength 600nm falls on slits 0.5mm apart and the screen is 1m away. Calculate the fringe width and the distance of the third bright fringe from the centre.

ID

phy-wav-2025-3

Type

short

Marks

5

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Fringe width: β=dλD​=0.5×10−3(600×10−9)(1.0)​=5×10−46×10−7​=1.2×10−3m=1.2mm.

The nth bright fringe is at yn​=nβ, so the third bright fringe is at 3×1.2=3.6mm from the central maximum.

If the whole apparatus were immersed in water (n=1.33), λ would fall and the fringes would close up to 1.2/1.33≈0.9mm — a standard way to confirm that the effect depends on wavelength in the medium.

4.

Two coherent waves, each of intensity I, produce an interference pattern. Find the resultant intensity at (i) a point of constructive interference and (ii) a point of destructive interference.

ID

phy-wav-2025-4

Type

short

Marks

5

Difficulty

medium

Solution

For two waves of equal amplitude a with phase difference ϕ, the resultant amplitude is A=2acos2ϕ​, so the intensity is IR​=4Icos22ϕ​ (since I∝a2).

(i) Constructive: ϕ=0,2π,… so cos22ϕ​=1 and IR​=4I — four times a single wave, not twice.

(ii) Destructive: ϕ=π,3π,… so cos2ϕ​=0 and IR​=0 — the energy is redistributed to the bright fringes, which is why energy is still conserved.

5.

A monochromatic light of wavelength 5890A˚ falls normally on a diffraction grating with 6000 lines per centimetre. At what angle is the second-order image seen?

Solution

Grating spacing: d=60001cm​=60001×10−2​=1.667×10−6m.

Grating equation: dsinθ=nλ with n=2 and λ=5890A˚=5.89×10−7m.

sinθ=1.667×10−62×5.89×10−7​=0.707, so θ≈45∘.

A third-order image would need sinθ=1.06>1, so it does not exist for this grating and wavelength — only orders up to n=2 are visible.

6.

(a) With a labelled diagram, derive the expression for the fringe width in Young's double-slit experiment, β=λD/d. (b) State two conditions for sustained interference and explain why the two slits must be close together.

ID

phy-wav-2025-6

Type

long

Marks

8

Difficulty

hard

Solution

(a) Let the two slits be d apart and the screen at distance D≫d. For a point P at height y from the centre, the path difference is Δ=S2​P−S1​P≈dsinθ≈dDy​.

Bright fringes occur where Δ=nλ, so yn​=dnλD​; dark fringes where Δ=(n+21​)λ, so yn′​=d(n+21​)λD​.

The separation between consecutive bright (or dark) fringes is the fringe width: β=yn+1​−yn​=dλD​. All fringes are equally spaced and equally bright, which is what makes the pattern recognisable.

(b) Conditions: the sources must be coherent (constant phase difference) and of comparable amplitude; they must also be monochromatic and close enough for the fringe width to be observable.

The slits must be close because β∝1/d: if d is large the fringes become too narrow to resolve, and the single-slit envelope also narrows until the pattern is lost. Keeping D large and d small is the practical recipe.
