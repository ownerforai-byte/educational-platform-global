2.

A charge is carried from one point to another on the same equipotential surface. The work done by the external agent is: (A) maximum (B) zero (C) equal to qV (D) negative

ID

phy-est-2025-2

Type

mcq

Marks

1

Difficulty

easy

Solution

Work done between two points is W=q(VB​−VA​).

On an equipotential surface VA​=VB​, so W=0. The displacement is everywhere perpendicular to the field, so no force component acts along the path. Answer (B).

Solution

Take charges +q and −q separated by 2a, and a point P on the axial line at distance r from the centre (r>a).

Field due to +q: E+​=4πε0​1​(r−a)2q​ away from the dipole. Field due to −q: E−​=4πε0​1​(r+a)2q​ towards the dipole. Since E+​>E−​, the resultant acts along the dipole axis away from it:

5.

Using Gauss's law, find the electric field at a point (i) outside and (ii) inside a uniformly charged thin spherical shell. Sketch how the field varies with distance from the centre.

ID

phy-est-2025-5

Type

short

Marks

5

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Let the shell have radius R and surface charge density σ, so total charge Q=4πR2σ. Take a spherical Gaussian surface of radius r concentric with the shell; by symmetry E is radial and has the same magnitude everywhere on it, so ∮E⋅dA=E(4πr2).

(i) Outside (r>R): enclosed charge is Q, so E(4πr2)=Q/ε0​ and E=4πε0​1​r2Q​ — the shell behaves as if all its charge were at the centre.

(ii) Inside (r<R): the Gaussian surface encloses no charge, so E(4πr2)=0 and E=0 everywhere inside.

Graph: E=0 for r<R, jumps to E=σ/ε0​ at the surface, then falls as 1/r2 outside. This is why a charged conductor's charge resides on its outer surface and why a hollow conductor shields its interior (Faraday cage).
