Questions

1.

State Kirchhoff's laws. Use Kirchhoff's voltage law to derive the balance condition of a Wheatstone bridge with resistances P,Q,R,S (galvanometer between the junctions carries no current at balance).

ID

phy-cur-2022-1

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Kirchhoff's current law: the algebraic sum of currents at a junction is zero (∑I=0, charge conservation). Kirchhoff's voltage law: the algebraic sum of voltages round any closed loop is zero (∑V=0, energy conservation). Label the bridge ABCD with battery across A–C and galvanometer across B–D. At balance Ig​=0, so the current I1​ through P also flows through Q, and I2​ through R also flows through S. Loop ABD: −I1​P+I2​R=0, so I1​P=I2​R. Loop BCD: −I1​Q+I2​S=0, so I1​Q=I2​S. Dividing gives P/Q=R/S — the balance condition. Equivalently P/Q=R/S lets the unknown resistance be found from three known ones.

2.

A wire of resistance 12Ω is bent into a circle. What is the effective resistance between the ends of any diameter?

ID

phy-cur-2022-2

Type

mcq

Marks

2

Difficulty

easy

Solution

Bending into a circle splits the wire into two semicircular halves, each of resistance 12/2=6Ω. The ends of a diameter connect these two halves in parallel, so 1/Req​=1/6+1/6=1/3, giving Req​=3Ω.

3.

Define drift velocity. Derive the relation I=neAvd​ between current and drift velocity, and explain why the drift speed is small even though electric signals travel fast.

ID

phy-cur-2022-3

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Drift velocity vd​ is the slow average velocity superimposed on electrons' random thermal motion by an applied electric field. In time t, electrons within length vd​t of a cross-section A pass through it: volume Avd​t, number nAvd​t (with n = free electrons per unit volume), charge neAvd​t. Hence I=Q/t=neAvd​. vd​ is of order 10−4m/s because electrons collide constantly with lattice ions. Signals travel near light speed because the electric field propagates through the wire almost instantly and pushes every free electron simultaneously — individual electrons still drift slowly.

4.

A cell of emf E=6V and internal resistance r=1Ω drives current through an external resistor R. (a) Find R for maximum power delivered to it and the value of that power. (b) Show the efficiency at maximum power transfer is 50%.

ID

phy-cur-2022-4

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

hard

Solution

(a) Current I=E/(R+r). Power in R: P=I2R=E2R/(R+r)2. Maximising over R: dP/dR=E2[(R+r)2−2R(R+r)]/(R+r)4=0 gives R+r−2R=0, i.e. R=r=1Ω (maximum power transfer theorem). Pmax​=E2r/(2r)2=E2/4r=36/4=9W. (b) Power supplied by the cell: Ptotal​=EI=E2/(R+r)=36/2=18W. Efficiency η=P/Ptotal​=9/18=0.5, i.e. 50% — half the power is wasted heating the internal resistance.
