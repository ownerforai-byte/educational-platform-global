3.

Define drift velocity and derive the relation I=neAvd​. A copper wire of cross-section 1.0mm2 carries 1.0A; if n=8.5×1028m−3, find the drift speed and explain why it is so small.

Type

short

Marks

5

Difficulty

Solution

Drift velocity is the average velocity with which free electrons move along the wire under an applied field, opposite to the field.

In time t every electron in a length vd​t crosses a section, so the charge crossing is q=neAvd​t, giving I=q/t=neAvd​.

vd​=neAI​=(8.5×1028)(1.6×10−19)(1.0×10−6)1.0​=1.36×1041.0​≈7.4×10−5ms−1.

The drift speed is tiny because electrons collide constantly with the lattice (mean free path ~10−8m) and their motion is a slow random walk with a small superimposed drift. The lamp still lights instantly because the electric field — and hence the push on every electron — is set up along the wire at nearly the speed of light.

4.

State the balance condition of a Wheatstone bridge and derive it. Why is the bridge unsuitable for measuring very low resistances?

ID

phy-cur-2025-4

Type

short

Marks

5

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Four resistances P,Q,R,S form a quadrilateral; a cell is connected across one diagonal and a galvanometer across the other. The bridge is balanced when the galvanometer shows no deflection, and then QP​=SR​.

At balance no current flows through the galvanometer, so B and D are at the same potential: VA​−VB​=VA​−VD​ and VB​−VC​=VD​−VC​. With current I1​ through P and Q, and I2​ through R and S: I1​P=I2​R and I1​Q=I2​S. Dividing, QP​=SR​; the unknown arm is found from the three known ones.

For very low resistances the resistance of the connecting wires and contact resistance become comparable with the unknown, so the ratio is no longer reliable. A metre bridge or a Kelvin double bridge is used instead.

5.

A potentiometer wire of length 10m has a resistance of 20Ω and carries 0.1A. A cell balances against 60cm of the wire. Find the emf of the cell, and state one advantage of a potentiometer over a voltmeter.

Type

Marks

Solution

Potential gradient: k=LIR​=100.1×20​=0.2Vm−1.

Balancing length l=0.60m, so ε=kl=0.2×0.60=0.12V.

Advantage: at balance the cell draws no current, so the potentiometer measures the true emf; a voltmeter always draws some current and reads the terminal voltage ε−Ir, which is less than the emf.

6.

(a) State Kirchhoff's laws for an electrical network. (b) Two cells of emf 12V (internal resistance 1Ω) and 6V (0.5Ω) are connected in parallel across a 4Ω resistor. Find the current through the resistor and through each cell.

ID

phy-cur-2025-6

Type

long

Marks

8

Difficulty

hard

Solution

(a) Junction rule: the algebraic sum of currents at a junction is zero (conservation of charge). Loop rule: around any closed loop the algebraic sum of potential differences is zero (conservation of energy).

(b) Let I1​ flow from the 12V cell and I2​ from the 6V cell into the resistor, so the resistor current is I=I1​+I2​.

Loop with 12V cell and the resistor: 12−1I1​−4(I1​+I2​)=0⇒5I1​+4I2​=12.

Loop with 6V cell: 6−0.5I2​−4(I1​+I2​)=0⇒4I1​+4.5I2​=6.

Solving: from the first, I1​=(12−4I2​)/5. Substituting, 4(12−4I2​)/5+4.5I2​=6⇒48−16I2​+22.5I2​=30⇒6.5I2​=−18, so I2​=−2.77A (the 6V cell is being charged, current driven into it). Then I1​=(12+11.08)/5=4.62A and I=I1​+I2​=1.85A.

Check with the terminal voltage: V=4×1.85=7.4V; the 12V cell gives 12−4.62=7.4V and the 6V cell gives 6+2.77×0.5=7.4V, confirming the result.
