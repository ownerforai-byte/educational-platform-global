1.

Total internal reflection can occur when light travels from: (A) air to glass (B) glass to air at an angle greater than the critical angle (C) air to water (D) vacuum to glass

ID

phy-ray-2025-1

Type

mcq

Marks

1

Difficulty

easy

Solution

Total internal reflection needs the ray to travel from a denser to a rarer medium, and the angle of incidence must exceed the critical angle θc​=sin−1(1/n).

Going from air to glass the ray bends towards the normal and can never be totally reflected. Answer (B).

4.

Solution

Refraction at the first surface (radius R1​): v′n2​​−un1​​=R1​n2​−n1​​. At the second surface (radius R2​), the image v′ acts as object: vn1​​−v′n2​​=R2​n1​−n2​​.

Adding and taking the lens in air (n1​=1, n2​=n): v1​−u1​=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​). For an object at infinity v=f, so f1​=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​).

Numerical: f1​=(1.5−1)(0.201​−−0.201​)=0.5×10=5m−1, so f=0.20m=20cm.

Check with the symmetric form: for equal radii, R1​1​−R2​1​=R2​, so f=2(n−1)R​=120​=20cm — the two surfaces contribute equally, which is why a biconvex lens of this glass has f equal to its radius of curvature.

6.

(a) Derive the relation vn2​​−un1​​=Rn2​−n1​​ for refraction at a single spherical surface. (b) A point object in air is 30cm from a convex surface of radius 10cm bounding glass of refractive index 1.5. Locate the image.

Solution

(a) A ray from an object O on the axis meets the surface at P and is refracted towards I. For small apertures the angles are small, so Snell's law n1​sini=n2​sinr becomes n1​i=n2​r.

In the triangle OPC, the exterior angle is i=α+γ (angles measured from the axis, with γ at the centre of curvature C); in triangle IPC, r=γ−β. For small angles α≈h/u, β≈h/v, γ≈h/R.

Substituting: n1​(uh​+Rh​)=n2​(Rh​−vh​), and cancelling h with signs applied by the Cartesian convention gives vn2​​−un1​​=Rn2​−n1​​.

(b) n1​=1, n2​=1.5, u=−30cm, R=+10cm: v1.5​−−301​=101.5−1​.

v1.5​+0.0333=0.05, so v1.5​=0.0167 and v=+90cm.

The image is real, on the far side of the surface, 90cm from it — the glass surface converges the ray because it is convex towards the incident light.
