4.

Derive an expression for the force per unit length between two infinitely long parallel straight conductors carrying currents I1​ and I2​. Hence define one ampere.

ID

Type

short

Marks

5

Difficulty

Solution

The first wire produces at the second (distance d) a field B1​=2πdμ0​I1​​, perpendicular to both the wire and the separation.

Force on length l of the second wire: F=B1​I2​l, so the force per unit length is lF​=2πdμ0​I1​I2​​.

By Newton's third law the first wire experiences an equal and opposite force. Currents in the same direction attract; in opposite directions they repel.

Definition: one ampere is that steady current which, flowing in each of two infinitely long parallel conductors of negligible cross-section placed one metre apart in vacuum, produces between them a force of 2×10−7N per metre of length.

5.

Show that the torque on a rectangular current loop in a uniform magnetic field is τ=NIABsinθ. Why is the magnetic field in a moving-coil galvanometer made radial?

ID

phy-mag-2025-5

Type

short

Marks

5

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Take a rectangular coil with N turns, sides l and b, carrying current I in a field B, with the normal to the coil at angle θ to B. The two sides of length l carry opposite currents, so the forces F=NBIl on them are equal, opposite and separated by bsinθ; they form a couple.

Torque: τ=F× perpendicular distance =NBIl(bsinθ)=NIABsinθ, with A=lb. In vector form τ=NIA×B.

A radial field keeps the plane of the coil always parallel to the field lines, so θ=90∘ for every deflection and the torque NIAB is constant. Then the deflection is directly proportional to the current, giving a uniformly divided linear scale.

Solution

(a) Two hollow D-shaped electrodes (dees) are placed in a uniform magnetic field perpendicular to their plane, with a high-frequency alternating voltage across the gap. A particle injected at the centre is bent into a semicircle inside a dee; crossing the gap it is accelerated, and the larger radius keeps the period the same, so the same frequency continues to work.

Derivation: the magnetic force supplies the centripetal force, qvB=rmv2​, so v=mqBr​ and the period is T=v2πr​=qB2πm​ — independent of speed and radius. Cyclotron frequency f=T1​=2πmqB​.

(b) f=2π(1.67×10−27)(1.6×10−19)(1.5)​=1.05×10−262.4×10−19​≈2.3×107Hz=23MHz.
