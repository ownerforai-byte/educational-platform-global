Questions

1.

Differentiate primary from secondary aquatic adaptations with two examples each.

ID

bio-bio-2025-1

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Primary aquatic animals live in water throughout their life and have never left it: streamlined body, gills for respiration, fins or cilia for locomotion, no eyelids or external ears, and kidneys that excrete ammonia. Examples: fish (Rohu), prawn. Secondary aquatic animals are originally land animals that have returned to water but retain land adaptations: they breathe by lungs, have hairs or feathers, four limbs modified into flippers or paddles, external ears may be reduced and they must surface to breathe. Examples: whale and dolphin (mammals), turtle and duck (reptile and bird).

2.

Describe cursorial, fossorial, arboreal and volant adaptations with one example each.

ID

bio-bio-2025-2

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Cursorial (running): long, strong hind limbs with reduced digits and light body; horse, hare. Fossorial (burrowing): cylindrical body, short powerful forelimbs with claws, reduced eyes and ears, and a burrow habit; mole rat, earthworm-like mole. Arboreal (tree-dwelling): prehensile hands or tail, opposable thumb, long limbs, claws or adhesive pads, forward-facing eyes for judging distance; monkey, tree frog. Volant (flying): wings of feathers or membrane, hollow or light bones, keeled sternum with strong flight muscles and acute vision; bird, bat. Gliding animals such as the flying squirrel use a patagium instead of true wings.

3.

Define reflex action, taxes and dominance–leadership in animal behaviour, with one example each.

ID

bio-bio-2025-3

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Reflex action is an automatic, involuntary and rapid response to a stimulus, mediated by a reflex arc — receptor, sensory neurone, spinal cord centre, motor neurone and effector — without waiting for the brain, as when the pupil narrows in bright light or the hand is pulled back from a hot object. Taxes are directed, whole-body movements towards or away from a stimulus: phototaxis in Euglena moving to light and geotaxis in roots growing downwards. Dominance and leadership: in a group one individual asserts priority over food, space or mates (the dominant bull) and others follow it, as the leading hen in a pecking order or the lead animal in a herd moving to water.

4.

Explain fish and bird migration with their causes and one method used to study migration.

ID

bio-bio-2025-4

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Migration is the regular seasonal movement of animals between breeding and feeding or wintering grounds. Fish such as salmon and the Himalayan mahseer move upstream to spawn in cool, oxygen-rich water and return to feed in larger rivers or the sea (anadromous in salmon, catadromous in eel). Birds move between breeding grounds and warmer wintering areas when food and daylight fall in the north. Causes: shortage of food and water, cold, need for a safe spawning or nesting site and day length acting as the trigger. Methods of study: ringing or banding with numbered metal or colour rings, radio and satellite telemetry, and geolocators that record light levels; observations of flock direction and timing are recorded alongside.

5.

Write the sources, effects and control measures of air pollution.

ID

bio-bio-2025-5

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Sources: burning of fossil fuels in industry, vehicles and brick kilns; smoke from biomass stoves; dust from roads, quarries and construction; sulphur and nitrogen oxides, carbon monoxide, hydrocarbons and particulate matter; also CFCs and methane. Effects: respiratory disease and asthma, eye irritation, acid rain, damage to crops and lichens, reduced visibility, corrosion of buildings, and contribution to global warming and ozone depletion. Control: shift to clean and renewable energy, fit catalytic converters and scrubbers, improve public transport, pave and water roads, ban open burning, plant green belts and enforce vehicle emission and industrial standards.

6.

Explain the chief sources and effects of water pollution and soil pollution, and suggest one control measure for each.

ID

bio-bio-2025-6

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Water pollution: untreated sewage and industrial effluent, agricultural fertilisers and pesticides, hospital waste, bathing and washing, and solid waste dumping. Effects: oxygen depletion kills fish, nutrient enrichment causes blooms and eutrophication, heavy metals accumulate in the food chain, and pathogens spread cholera and typhoid. Control: treat sewage and industrial effluent before discharge and stop dumping waste into rivers. Soil pollution: excess chemical fertilisers and pesticides, plastic and polythene waste, industrial sludge, mine spoil and acid rain. Effects: loss of fertility and soil structure, death of soil organisms, crop contamination and groundwater pollution. Control: use organic manure and biofertilisers, adopt integrated pest management, and separate, compost and recycle solid waste.

7.

What are pesticides? Describe their effects and state how their harm can be reduced.

ID

bio-bio-2025-7

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Pesticides are chemicals used to kill pests — insecticides (organochlorines such as DDT, organophosphates), herbicides, fungicides and rodenticides. Effects: they kill non-target beneficial insects and pollinators, and persistent chlorinated compounds accumulate in soil, water and living tissue, increasing at each trophic level through biomagnification — the reason birds of prey suffered eggshell thinning. Exposure causes poisoning in farm workers, residues in food and milk, and development of resistant pests; runoff pollutes rivers and groundwater. Reduction: integrated pest management with biological control, resistant varieties and careful timing; use of biopesticides such as neem and Bacillus thuringiensis; correct dose, protective clothing and safe disposal; and legal restriction or ban of persistent organochlorines.
