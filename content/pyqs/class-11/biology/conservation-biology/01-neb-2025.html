Questions

1.

Define biodiversity and describe its three levels with an example of each.

ID

bio-con-2025-1

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

easy

Solution

Biodiversity is the variety and variability of living organisms at all levels of organisation, together with the ecological complexes they form. Genetic diversity: variation of genes within a species — different landraces of rice or varieties of apple. Species diversity: the number and relative abundance of species in an area — the many tree species of a Chitwan sal forest versus a single-species plantation. Ecosystem or ecological diversity: the variety of habitats and communities — forests, grasslands, wetlands, rivers and alpine meadows within one country. Nepal, with all these within 147,000 km², ranks high in ecosystem diversity.

2.

Name the categories of protected areas in Nepal with one example each and explain what a Ramsar site is.

ID

bio-con-2025-2

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Nepal's protected area system includes national parks (Chitwan, Sagarmatha, Bardia), wildlife reserves (Koshi Tappu, Parsa), conservation areas (Annapurna, Manaslu), hunting reserve (Dhorpatan) and buffer zones around parks; some areas are also designated as Ramsar sites. A Ramsar site is a wetland of international importance listed under the Ramsar Convention of 1971, on the basis of its waterfowl, fish, plant life or ecological role. Nepal has listed sites including Koshi Tappu, Beeshazar Tal, Ghodaghodi Tal, Jagadishpur Reservoir, Rara Lake, Phoksundo Lake and the Mai Pokhari, which obliges the country to conserve them and use them wisely.

3.

Why does wildlife matter, what causes extinction, and what strategies conserve threatened species?

ID

bio-con-2025-3

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Wildlife matters for ecological balance — predators control prey, pollinators and seed dispersers keep forests regenerating, and each species is part of a food web; it also provides direct economic value through tourism, genetic resources and medicine, and has cultural and ethical value. Causes of extinction: habitat loss and fragmentation, overhunting and poaching, pollution, invasive species, climate change, disease and small population effects such as inbreeding. Strategies: protect and connect habitats through parks, corridors and community forests; enforce anti-poaching law and control trade; breed endangered species in captivity and reintroduce them; restore degraded habitat; involve local people through buffer zones and ecotourism; and monitor populations with research.

4.

Explain the IUCN Red List categories of threatened species.

ID

bio-con-2025-4

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

The IUCN Red List ranks the extinction risk of species. Extinct (EX): no individual remains. Extinct in the wild (EW): survives only in captivity. Critically endangered (CR): extremely high risk in the wild. Endangered (EN): very high risk. Vulnerable (VU): high risk of extinction. Near threatened (NT): close to qualifying. Least concern (LC): widespread and not currently at risk. Data deficient (DD): too little information to assess. CR, EN and VU together are 'threatened'. The categories are assigned from quantitative criteria — population size, rate of decline, range area and fragmentation — and are reviewed periodically, so they guide national law and conservation priority.

5.

Name any five endangered or threatened animal species of Nepal and state the main threat to each.

ID

bio-con-2025-5

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Greater one-horned rhinoceros — poaching for horn and loss of grassland habitat. Royal Bengal tiger — poaching for skin and bone and habitat fragmentation. Red panda — loss of bamboo forest and fur trade. Snow leopard — retaliatory killing over livestock and loss of prey. Gharial — loss of river habitat, fishing nets and sand mining. Other threatened species include the wild elephant, musk deer, pangolin, Chinese pangolin, hispid hare, Bengal florican and the spiny babbler. Conservation relies on anti-poaching units, habitat protection, prey recovery, captive breeding and community participation.

6.

Differentiate in-situ and ex-situ conservation and give two advantages and one limitation of each.

ID

bio-con-2025-6

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

In-situ conservation keeps a species inside its natural habitat — national parks, reserves, conservation areas, community forests and wetlands. Advantages: the whole community and its interactions are preserved, and populations keep evolving under natural selection; it protects watersheds and supports tourism. Limitation: it needs large areas and can fail if the habitat is degraded or the population is already very small. Ex-situ conservation keeps the species outside its habitat — botanical gardens, zoos, seed and gene banks, tissue culture. Advantages: it can save species whose habitat is gone and supplies material for reintroduction; it allows research and breeding. Limitation: only a few individuals are kept, genetic diversity is narrow and behaviour needed in the wild may be lost, so it cannot replace habitat protection.

7.

What does 'endangered species' mean, and how does it differ from a 'rare' species?

ID

bio-con-2025-7

Type

mcq

Marks

2

Difficulty

easy

Solution

An endangered species is one whose population has fallen so low, or is declining so fast, that it faces a very high risk of extinction in the wild in the near future — for example the greater one-horned rhinoceros in Nepal. A rare species has a naturally small population or a very restricted range and may never have been abundant; it is not necessarily declining. Rare species are vulnerable because a single local disturbance can wipe out the whole population, so both categories need protection but for different reasons.
