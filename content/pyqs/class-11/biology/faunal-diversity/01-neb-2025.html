Questions

1.

Give an outline classification of Protista and the diagnostic features of Protozoa up to class with one example of each class.

ID

bio-fau-2025-1

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Kingdom Protista is divided into protozoans (animal-like), algae (plant-like) and slime moulds (fungus-like). Protozoa are unicellular eukaryotic heterotrophs without a cell wall, moving by pseudopodia, flagella or cilia and often forming cysts. Classes: Rhizopoda or Sarcodina — move by pseudopodia, e.g. Amoeba; Mastigophora or Flagellata — move by flagella, e.g. Trypanosoma, Euglena; Ciliophora — move by cilia, e.g. Paramecium; Sporozoa — no locomotory organelle, parasitic with spore-like stages, e.g. Plasmodium.

2.

Describe the structure and reproduction of Paramecium caudatum.

ID

bio-fau-2025-2

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Paramecium is a slipper-shaped ciliate covered by a pellicle with cilia; it has two nuclei — a large macronucleus for metabolism and a small micronucleus for reproduction — a contractile vacuole for osmoregulation at each end, food vacuoles and a cytostome with an oral groove. Asexual reproduction is by transverse binary fission, sometimes preceded by conjugation. Sexual reproduction is conjugation: two individuals join at the oral grooves, their macronuclei degenerate and micronuclei undergo meiosis; a haploid nucleus from each migrates into the other, fuses to form a synkaryon, and the cells separate and divide — a way of exchanging genetic material and restoring vigour.

3.

Describe the life cycle of Plasmodium vivax and name the vector.

ID

bio-fau-2025-3

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

hard

Solution

The cycle has two hosts. In the female Anopheles mosquito (definitive host) gametocytes taken up with a blood meal fuse in the gut to form a zygote, which becomes a motile ookinete and then an oocyst full of sporozoites; these migrate to the salivary glands. When the mosquito bites, sporozoites enter the human liver and multiply asexually in liver cells (pre-erythrocytic phase), releasing merozoites into the blood. There they invade red cells, grow as trophozoites and divide into more merozoites, bursting the cells and causing the fever cycle; some become gametocytes, which are picked up by the next mosquito. Relapse follows from hypnozoites left in the liver.

4.

State the economic importance of Plasmodium falciparum.

ID

bio-fau-2025-4

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

P. falciparum causes falciparum malaria, the most severe and often fatal form, with cerebral malaria, severe anaemia, kidney failure and pulmonary oedema. Its economic importance is entirely destructive: it removes productive days from adults, keeps children out of school, and reduces work capacity and learning; it drains household income for treatment and travel, and national budgets for drugs, nets and spraying. In Nepal it threatens the Terai and inner-Terai districts and complicates irrigation and settlement projects, so malaria control is a public-health and development priority rather than a resource.

5.

Explain the levels of body organisation, body symmetry, body cavity and segmentation in animals with examples.

ID

bio-fau-2025-5

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Organisation: cellular (Porifera), tissue (Cnidaria), organ (Platyhelminthes), organ system (Nematoda onwards). Symmetry: asymmetrical (sponges), radial (Hydra, starfish) and bilateral, where the body has a right and left half, a dorsal and ventral side and anterior and posterior ends — typical of active animals. Body cavity: acoelomate, with no cavity between gut and body wall (Platyhelminthes); pseudocoelomate, a cavity not lined by mesoderm (Nematoda); coelomate, a true cavity lined by mesoderm (Annelida onwards). Segmentation: metameric repetition of body parts along the axis, seen clearly in the earthworm, and as jointed segments in arthropods.

6.

Give the diagnostic features of any five animal phyla with one example each.

ID

bio-fau-2025-6

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Porifera: cellular level, no organs, body wall perforated by ostia and a spongocoel with collar cells; Sycon. Cnidaria: radial symmetry, tissue level, one opening surrounded by tentacles bearing nematocysts; Hydra, Aurelia. Platyhelminthes: flat, bilaterally symmetrical, acoelomate, with flame cells and often a parasitic life cycle; Fasciola, Taenia. Nematoda: unsegmented, cylindrical, pseudocoelomate, covered by a cuticle, sexes usually separate; Ascaris. Annelida: metamerically segmented coelomate worms with setae and a closed circulatory system; Pheretima, Hirudinaria. Arthropoda: jointed appendages, chitinous exoskeleton, segmented body and open circulation; Palaemon, butterfly. Mollusca: soft body with a fleshy foot and mantle, often a calcareous shell and a radula; Pila, Octopus.

7.

Describe the digestive system of earthworm.

ID

bio-fau-2025-7

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

The alimentary canal is a straight tube: mouth in the first segment, buccal cavity, muscular pharynx that pumps in soil, oesophagus, muscular gizzard that grinds the food, and stomach, followed by the intestine which runs to the anus. The intestine has an internal fold, the typhlosole, that increases absorptive surface, and its wall secretes enzymes; chloragogen cells store food and neutralise toxins. Digestion is extracellular: proteases, amylase and lipase act in the intestine at pH favourable for the enzymes. The worm feeds on soil rich in decayed organic matter and the undigested soil is egested as a cast — an albuminous soil deposit valuable to farmers.

8.

Describe the excretory and nervous systems of earthworm.

ID

bio-fau-2025-8

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Excretion is by nephridia of three kinds: pharyngeal micronephridia, integumentary nephridia scattered on the body wall, and septal nephridia attached to the septa behind the clitellum. A nephridium has a nephrostome ciliated funnel, a coiled tubule and an external nephridiopore; it removes nitrogenous waste as urea, and the chloragogen cells help by converting ammonia into urea. The nervous system is a dorsal cerebral ganglion in the third segment, a circumpharyngeal connectives ring and a ventral nerve cord with a ganglion in each segment; sense organs include photoreceptor cells, taste buds and epidermal receptor cells, and giant nerve fibres allow rapid withdrawal.

9.

Describe the reproductive system of earthworm and state four of its economic benefits.

ID

bio-fau-2025-9

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Earthworm is hermaphrodite: male organs are two pairs of testes in segments 10 and 11, with seminal vesicles and vasa deferentia opening at the prostatic pores of segment 18; female organs are a pair of ovaries in segment 13 with oviducts opening at segment 14. Four pairs of spermathecae in segments 6–9 store sperm during mutual cross-fertilisation; two worms lie head to tail, exchange sperm, and later the clitellum secretes a cocoon into which eggs and stored sperm are shed, so fertilisation is external in the cocoon. Benefits: casts improve soil fertility and structure, burrows aerate and drain soil, worms are protein feed for poultry and fish, and they help degrade organic waste in vermicomposting; they also serve as bait and in the food chain.

10.

Describe the digestive and respiratory systems of frog.

ID

bio-fau-2025-10

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Digestive: the mouth has a sticky tongue attached in front and numerous small, backwardly directed teeth on the upper jaw, with the internal nares opening into the buccal cavity. The oesophagus leads to a stomach with cardiac and pyloric parts, then the small intestine (duodenum, ileum) and large intestine opening into the cloaca. The liver with gall bladder, pancreas and salivary glands pour in secretions; digestion is completed in the intestine with absorption through villi. Respiratory: in water by moist skin and buccal lining; on land by lungs — a pair of simple sacs with internal folds, linked to the buccal cavity by the larynx and bronchi. Breathing is a buccal pump: air is drawn into the buccal cavity, the nares close, the floor rises and air is forced into the lungs, which is why a frog throbs even when resting.

11.

Describe the structure of a frog's heart and explain the working of the circulation.

ID

bio-fau-2025-11

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

hard

Solution

The heart is three-chambered — two atria and one ventricle — lying in the pericardial cavity. The sinus venosus receives venous blood and opens into the right atrium; the left atrium receives oxygenated blood from the lungs; the ventricle is thin-walled with internal ridges that reduce mixing, and the truncus arteriosus leaves it and divides into carotid, systemic and pulmocutaneous arches. Working: the sinus venosus pumps deoxygenated blood into the right atrium while pulmonary veins fill the left; both atria contract, sending blood into the ventricle. During ventricular contraction the blood is directed so that the pulmocutaneous arches receive the deoxygenated part (to lungs and skin) and the carotids receive the oxygenated part (to head), so mixing is partly avoided. Blood returns through the venae cavae and pulmonary veins, and the flow is unidirectional because of valves at each chamber junction.

12.

Describe the male and female reproductive systems of frog.

ID

bio-fau-2025-12

Type

short

Marks

4

Difficulty

medium

Solution

Male: a pair of yellowish oval testes attached to the kidney by the mesorchium, with vasa efferentia that run through the kidney into the ureter, which becomes the urinogenital duct opening into the cloaca; a vocal sac and nuptial thumb pad develop in breeding season. Female: a pair of triangular ovaries above the kidneys with long, much-coiled oviducts, each opening into the coelom by a ciliated funnel and opening into the cloaca, plus a swollen oviduct during breeding. During amplexus, the male clasps the female and she releases eggs into the water, which the male fertilises externally; the zygotes hatch into tadpoles.
