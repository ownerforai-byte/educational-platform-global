Notes

  • Boltzmann constant: kB​=R/NA​=1.38×10−23 J/K. It connects microscopic and macroscopic thermodynamics. Named after Ludwig Boltzmann (1844-1906).
  • RMS speed: vrms​=3RT/M​, where M is molar mass in kg/mol. At STP (273 K), vrms​ for air is about 479 m/s. At 300 K, about 484 m/s. Lighter gases move faster on average.
  • Most probable speed: vp​=2RT/M​. This is the speed at which the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution peaks. At 300 K for air: vp​≈364 m/s. At 300 K for Hâ‚‚: vp​≈1650 m/s.
  • Mean speed: vmean​=8RT/πM​. The average of all molecular speeds. At 300 K for air: vmean​≈464 m/s. At 300 K for Hâ‚‚: vmean​≈1760 m/s.
  • Heat capacities - monatomic gases: CV​=23​R, CP​=25​R. The ratio γ=CP​/CV​=5/3≈1.67. Monatomic gases (He, Ne, Ar) only have translational degrees of freedom.
  • Heat capacities - diatomic gases: CV​=25​R, CP​=27​R at ordinary temperatures (γ=7/5=1.4). Includes 3 translational + 2 rotational DOF. At high temperature, vibration adds more, so CV​→4R, CP​→5R, γ→9/7≈1.29.
  • Dulong-Petit law for solids: Molar heat capacity of most solid elements at room temperature ≈3R≈25 J/(molÂ·K). This comes from 3 quadratic degrees of freedom per atom (one for each spatial axis), each contributing R per mole of atoms.
  • Why heat capacities differ: The ratio CP​/CV​=γ matters for adiabatic processes (PVγ=const). This affects everything from engine cycles to atmospheric science. γ≈1.4 for air is crucial in meteorology.
  • Experimental verification: Heat capacities were measured by Regnault and others in the 19th century. The agreement with kinetic theory predictions was one of the great successes of molecular physics.
  • Units: kB​=1.38×10−23 J/K. CP​,CV​ have units J/(molÂ·K). Gas constant R has the same units.
  • Connection to energy: Each degree of freedom contributes 21​R per mole or 21​kB​ per molecule. 3 translational DOF ightarrow3×21​R=23​R for CV​

Confusion

  • The ratio γ=CP​/CV​ is NOT always 5/3 or 7/5. It depends on temperature and molecular structure. At very high temperatures, vibrational modes activate, changing γ.
  • The formula CV​=23​R applies ONLY to monatomic ideal gases. For diatomic or polyatomic gases, rotational (and sometimes vibrational) modes contribute.
  • Dulong's law (C≈3R) for solids works well at room temperature but fails at very low temperatures (quantum effects freeze out degrees of freedom). Einstein and Debye developed low-temperature models.
  • The Boltzmann constant kB​ is NOT the same as the gas constant R. kB​=1.38×10−23 J/K per molecule. R = 8.314 J/(molÂ·K) per mole. R=NA​kB​.
  • Most probable speed is NOT the average speed. For the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution: vp​⋅2​<vmean​<vrms​. The order is always vp​<vmean​<vrms​ at the same temperature.

Universal Facts

  • The Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution was first derived by James Clerk Maxwell in 1860 and later generalized by Ludwig Boltzmann. It describes the distribution of speeds in an ideal gas and is one of the most important results of kinetic theory.
  • At 300 K, the three speed measures for nitrogen (M=28 g/mol) are: most probable vp​≈422 m/s, mean speed vmean​≈477 m/s, and rms speed vrms​≈517 m/s. The ratios are always vp​:vmean​:vrms​=2​:8/π​:3​≈1.414:1.596:1.732.
  • The Boltzmann constant kB​ was first introduced by Max Planck in his derivation of the blackbody radiation formula (1900). He used it to connect the energy of oscillators to temperature: E=kB​T.
  • Dulong and Petit's law (C≈3R for solids) was discovered in 1819. It took nearly 150 years to understand why it works - the kinetic theory's 3 degrees of freedom per atom (1859-1870s) provided the explanation.
  • The ratio γ=CP​/CV​ determines whether sound speed is faster in an adiabatic process: vsound​=γRT/M​. For air (γ≈1.4), vsound​≈343 m/s at 300 K. For helium (γ=5/3≈1.67), vsound​≈965 m/s at 300 K.
