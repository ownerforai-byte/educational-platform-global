Notes

  • Dispersion: splitting of white light into its constituent colours because the refractive index of a material depends on wavelength. This occurs because the speed of light in a medium varies with wavelength — violet light (shorter wavelength) slows down more than red light (longer wavelength), so it bends more.
  • Pure spectrum: a spectrum in which colours do not overlap. Achieved with a narrow slit and a single prism so that each wavelength emerges at a distinct angle. The narrow slit ensures each colour comes from a single point source, eliminating colour blending.
  • Dispersive power ω: ω=ny​−1nv​−nr​​, where nv​, nr​, ny​ are refractive indices for violet, red, and yellow (sodium D line). Dispersive power is a dimensionless quantity — it is the ratio of angular dispersion to the mean deviation.
  • Crown vs flint glass: crown glass has ω≈0.016, flint glass has ω≈0.031. Flint glass disperses about twice as much, which is why it is used in spectroscope prisms where a wide angular spread is desired.
  • Angular dispersion: δv​−δr​=(nv​−nr​)A for a small-angle prism, or equivalently (nv​−nr​)A=ω(ny​−1)A. The spread of colours is proportional to both the refractive-index difference and the prism angle A.
  • Deviation: for a small-angle prism, the mean deviation is δy​=(ny​−1)A. The prism bends all wavelengths, but different amounts — this differential bending is what creates the spectrum.
  • Why dispersion occurs: the refractive index follows Cauchy's relation n(λ)=A+λ2B​+λ4C​, so shorter wavelengths have higher n and bend more. This wavelength dependence is a property of the electronic response of the material.
  • Unit analysis: dispersive power is dimensionless (ratio of two quantities with the same units). Angular dispersion has units of radians. The prism angle A is in radians or degrees.
  • Rainbow formation: a natural example of dispersion — sunlight undergoes refraction, internal reflection, and a second refraction inside water droplets, separating into the seven colours of the spectrum.

Confusion

  • Dispersive power is NOT the same as angular dispersion — dispersive power is dimensionless (a ratio), while angular dispersion has units of angle and depends on the prism angle.
  • A larger refractive index does not necessarily mean a larger dispersive power — flint glass has both a higher n and a higher ω than crown glass, but the two properties are independent.
  • Dispersion is NOT caused by the prism absorbing different colours — all colours pass through; they simply bend by different amounts because n depends on wavelength.
  • The colours in a spectrum do not have sharp boundaries — the seven colours (VIBGYOR) are a convention. The spectrum is a continuous gradient of wavelengths.
  • For a large-angle prism, the small-angle approximation δ=(n−1)A is no longer valid — one must use Snell's law at each surface separately.

Solution: ω=(ny​−1)Aangular dispersion​=(1.62−1)×5°2.76°​=3.102.76​≈0.890 — wait, that gives ω≈0.890 which is unreasonably large. Recalculating: angular dispersion =(nv​−nr​)A, so nv​−nr​=2.76/5=0.552. Then ω=0.620.552​≈0.890 — this indicates a problem with the numbers; realistic flint glass has ω≈0.031. With corrected data: if ω=0.031 and ny​=1.62, then angular dispersion =0.031×0.62×5°=0.096°≈5.8′.
  • White light passes through a prism of angle 4° and refractive index 1.54 for red light and 1.58 for violet light. Find the angular dispersion and the mean deviation. Solution: Angular dispersion =(1.58−1.54)×4°=0.16°=9.6′. Mean refractive index ny​≈1.56, so mean deviation δy​=(1.56−1)×4°=2.24°.
  • A crown glass prism (ω=0.016) and a flint glass prism (ω=0.031) are to be combined to produce angular dispersion without deviation. If the crown prism angle is 5°, what must be the flint prism angle? Solution: For no deviation, (ny,c​−1)Ac​=(ny,f​−1)Af​. For crown ny​≈1.515, for flint ny​≈1.62. Net deviation =0, so Af​=(1.62−1)(1.515−1)×5°​=0.620.515×5​≈4.15°. Net angular dispersion =(0.031)(0.62)(4.15°)−(0.016)(0.515)(5°)≈0.0799°−0.0412°≈0.0387°.
  • Explain why a parallel-sided glass slab does not produce a visible spectrum from white light, even though refraction occurs at both surfaces. Solution: Although refraction occurs at the first surface, the two surfaces are parallel, so the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray. The dispersion produced at the first surface is exactly undone at the second surface, and the net result is a laterally displaced white beam with no angular separation of colours.

Universal Facts

  • The refractive index of glass for violet light is typically about 1–2% higher than for red light, which is enough to produce a clearly visible spectrum.
  • Newton's prism experiments (1666) showed that dispersion is a property of light itself, not of the prism — he used a second prism to recombine the spectrum back into white light.
  • The human eye is most sensitive to yellow-green light (λ≈555 nm), which is why the sodium D line at 589 nm is commonly used as the reference wavelength.
  • Dispersive power depends only on the material, not on the prism angle — it is an intrinsic property of the glass.
  • Optical fibres rely on dispersion management — different wavelengths travel at different speeds, which limits the bandwidth of long-distance communication.
