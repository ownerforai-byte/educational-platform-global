Notes

  • Gravitational potential V: the work done per unit mass in bringing a test mass from infinity to a point, without acceleration. For a point mass M: V = −GM/r. At infinity V = 0, and everywhere else V is negative because gravity does positive work as the mass approaches — the field does the work, so potential decreases.
  • Gravitational potential energy U: the work done against gravity in assembling a system; for two masses separated by r: U = −GMm/r. It is the energy of the whole system, not of one mass alone. U is negative because the system is bound — you must supply GMm/r of energy to separate the masses to infinity.
  • Why the minus sign matters: potential energy is defined with zero at infinity. Since the force is attractive, energy is released (and PE decreases) as masses come together. A satellite in orbit at radius r has U = −GMm/r — its total mechanical energy is E = K + U = −GMm/2r, which is negative, confirming a bound orbit.
  • Near Earth's surface: for small heights h above the surface, U = mgh. This is the constant-g approximation of −GMm/r: since Δ(1/r) ≈ −h/R², the change matches mgh. Use mgh only for h ≪ R; use −GMm/r for large altitudes (satellites, rockets).
  • Worked example — escape energy: to launch a mass m from Earth's surface (R) to infinity you must supply GMm/R. Numerically for m = 1 kg: (6.67×10⁻¹¹)(5.97×10²⁴)/6.37×10⁶ ≈ 6.25×10⁷ J ≈ 62.5 MJ per kg. This energy corresponds to the escape speed 11.2 km/s.
  • Potential vs potential energy vs field: field g = −dV/dr (the force per unit mass, a vector); potential V is a scalar (J/kg); potential energy U = mV. The field is the negative gradient of the potential: g = −dV/dr radially.
  • Equipotential surfaces: surfaces of constant V. Moving along an equipotential surface requires zero work; the field is always perpendicular to these surfaces. For a point mass, equipotentials are concentric spheres.
  • Superposition: for multiple masses, V = Σ(−GMᵢ/rᵢ) and U = Σ(−GMᵢm/rᵢ) — potentials add as scalars, which is much simpler than adding force vectors.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Potential and potential energy are the same.' ✅ Potential V is work per unit mass (J/kg); potential energy U = mV (J). V belongs to a point in the field; U belongs to the mass–field system.
  • ❌ 'Negative energy is unphysical.' ✅ Negative potential energy simply means the system is BOUND — energy must be added to separate it. It is a matter of the reference at infinity.
  • ❌ 'U = mgh and U = −GMm/r disagree.' ✅ They agree for small heights: mgh is the CHANGE in −GMm/r near the surface. Use the right one for the régime.
  • ❌ 'Potential is maximum at Earth's surface.' ✅ Potential is most negative at the surface (−GM/R) and rises toward zero with altitude — at infinity it reaches the (higher) value 0.
  • ❌ 'Doubling the distance doubles the potential energy.' ✅ U = −GMm/r — doubling r halves the magnitude, so U becomes −GMm/2r (less negative).
  • ❌ 'A satellite far away has lots of potential energy.' ✅ A far-away satellite has potential energy near ZERO (least negative, but U ≈ 0). It needs enormous kinetic energy (orbital speed) that comes from the launch energy.

Universal Facts

  • Gravitational potential energy is exactly the 'missing' energy that binds systems — this is why the binding energy of Earth + satellite equals |U|.
  • The concept of potential is useful because it is scalar: complex many-body problems reduce to addition rather than vector resolution.
  • Near a black hole, gravitational potential becomes so deep (−GM/r) that even light cannot escape — the origin of the event horizon.
  • Gravitational potential determines whether planets retain atmospheres: gas molecules with thermal speeds above the local escape speed leak away.
  • The Voyager probes were launched using the escape-energy budget — their final trajectory traced the potential wells of the Sun and giant planets.

Examples

  • V at Earth's surface: V = −GM/R = −6.25×10⁷ J/kg.
  • U of 1000 kg satellite at r = 6.8×10⁶ m: U = −GMm/r = −5.9×10¹⁰ J.
  • Escape energy per kg: GM/R = 6.25×10⁷ J ≈ 17 kWh per kg.
  • Near surface: a 2 kg mass lifted 10 m gains U = mgh = 196 J.
  • Total energy of circular orbit: E = −GMm/2r — negative, so the orbit is bound.

Formulas

  • Gravitational potential: V = −GM/r (J/kg)
  • Gravitational potential energy: U = mV = −GMm/r (J)
  • Near the surface: U = mgh (h ≪ R)
  • Field from potential: g = −dV/dr
  • Total orbital energy: E = −GMm/2r = U/2

Key Points

  • V = −GM/r is negative, approaching zero at infinity.
  • U = −GMm/r is the energy of the (bound) system, negative for attraction.
  • Near Earth's surface U = mgh is the appropriate approximation.
  • Potential and field connect: g = −dV/dr.
  • Total energy of a circular orbit is negative: E = −GMm/2r.

Summary

Gravitational potential V = −GM/r is the work per unit mass to bring a test mass from infinity; potential energy U = −GMm/r is the negative binding energy of two masses. Near the surface U ≈ mgh. The field relates to the potential by g = −dV/dr, and circular orbits have total energy E = −GMm/2r, confirming their bound nature.

Special Notes

  • Both V and U are defined with reference zero at infinity — that is why they are negative.
  • Use U = mgh only for small heights (h ≪ R); use −GMm/r for space-scale problems.
  • Potential is a scalar — use scalar addition with multiple masses.
  • The escape energy equals |U| at the surface: GMm/R.
  • NEB may ask 'derive U = −GMm/r starting from F = GMm/r²' — know the integration step.

Important Statements

  • Gravitational potential at a point is the work done per unit mass in bringing a mass from infinity to that point: V = −GM/r.
  • Gravitational potential energy of two masses is U = −GMm/r, taking zero at infinity.
  • For small heights near Earth's surface, U = mgh.
  • The gravitational field is the negative gradient of the potential: g = −dV/dr.
  • A satellite in a circular orbit has total energy E = −GMm/2r (bound system).

Important Notes

  • V = −GM/r (potential) and U = −GMm/r (potential energy) both take zero at infinity — hence negative for attracted masses.
  • Near Earth's surface use U = mgh; for space-scale distances use the inverse-r form.
  • The field is the negative slope of the potential: g = −dV/dr.
  • Binding energy of a two-mass system = |U| = GMm/r.
  • Total energy of a circular orbit (E = −GMm/2r) is negative — a signature of a bound state.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Escape-energy shortcut: energy to infinity = GMm/R = mgR (since g = GM/R²).
  • Orbital energy memory: E = −GMm/2r = U/2 means K = −U/2.
  • V at surface ≈ −6.25×10⁷ J/kg for Earth — compare planets by this value.
  • When moving closer to a mass, U becomes MORE negative (energy is released).
  • Use mgh only when the height is small compared with Earth's radius — a quick sanity check.

Exam Notes

  • Derivation of U = −GMm/r from integrating F = GMm/r² is a standard question.
  • Numericals on satellite total energy and escape energy are frequently asked.
  • Conceptual MCQ: why is potential energy negative? → because zero is at infinity and gravity is attractive.
  • Linking g = −dV/dr to a V–r graph slope appears in higher-level questions.
  • Compare U = mgh with −GMm/r: know when each applies.
