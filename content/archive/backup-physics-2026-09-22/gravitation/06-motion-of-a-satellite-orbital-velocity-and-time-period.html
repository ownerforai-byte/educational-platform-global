Enriched Content

Notes
  • Orbital velocity (v₀): a satellite stays in a circular orbit when the gravitational pull GMm/r² provides the required centripetal force mv²/r. Equating: GMm/r² = mv₀²/r → v₀ = √(GM/r). Near Earth's surface, v₀ = √(gR) ≈ 7.9 km/s.
  • Why v₀ matters: too slow → the satellite spirals in; too fast → it escapes to a higher ellipse. The orbital speed depends only on the radius (GM/r), not on the satellite's mass — heavy and light satellites share the same orbit speed.
  • Time period (T): T = circumference/velocity = 2πr/v₀ = 2π√(r³/GM) — Kepler's third law. Squaring: T² ∝ r³. A higher orbit has a longer period: the Moon (r ≈ 3.84×10⁸ m) takes ~27.3 days; the ISS (r ≈ 6.8×10⁶ m) takes ~92 minutes.
  • Energy of an orbiting satellite: K = ½mv₀² = GMm/2r, U = −GMm/r, so E_total = −GMm/2r. Notice K = −U/2 and E = U/2 — a signature of circular orbits. To raise a satellite to a higher orbit you must ADD energy, but the KE actually decreases (higher orbit = slower speed).
  • Worked example — ISS orbit: at height 400 km, r = 6.8×10⁶ m. v₀ = √(6.67×10⁻¹¹ × 5.97×10²⁴/6.8×10⁶) = √(5.86×10⁷) ≈ 7.66 km/s. T = 2π×6.8×10⁶/7.66×10³ ≈ 5570 s ≈ 93 min.
  • Worked example — geostationary orbit: for T = 24 h, r = (GMT²/4π²)^(1/3) ≈ 4.22×10⁷ m ≈ 35,800 km above the equator with v₀ ≈ 3.08 km/s.
  • Low Earth vs geostationary comparison: LEO (200–2000 km): v ≈ 7.8 km/s, T ≈ 90 min, covers Earth rapidly (imaging, ISS). Geostationary (35,800 km): v ≈ 3.07 km/s, T = 24 h, appears fixed over one spot (communications, weather).
  • Orbit changes: to transfer between circular orbits, a Hohmann ellipse uses two engine burns; near perigee/apogee speeds change by Δv = v₂ − v₁. Firing thrust ahead slows the satellite and lowers the orbit; firing backward raises it.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'A satellite must be above the atmosphere to feel no gravity.' ✅ A satellite feels gravity at every altitude — it is in continuous free fall. It never falls to Earth because its forward speed curves its fall into an orbit.
  • ❌ 'Weightlessness means g = 0 on the ISS.' ✅ g at 400 km is ≈ 8.7 m/s² (89% of surface). Astronauts float because both they and the station accelerate toward Earth together (free fall).
  • ❌ 'Heavier satellites orbit slower.' ✅ Orbital speed is independent of the satellite's mass: v₀ = √(GM/r).
  • ❌ 'Higher orbit → faster speed.' ✅ Higher orbit means SLOWER speed (v₀ = √(GM/r)) but a LONGER period. To reach a higher orbit you must speed up at launch; once there, the circular speed is lower.
  • ❌ 'T is proportional to r.' ✅ T ∝ r^{3/2} — the period grows faster than the radius.
  • ❌ 'A satellite's kinetic energy increases as it moves to a higher orbit.' ✅ KE = GMm/2r DECREASES with height; the added energy is stored as potential energy (+GmM·Δ(1/r)) making the total E = −GMm/2r less negative.

  • Explain why astronauts float even though gravity at 400 km is strong.
Universal Facts
  • The first artificial satellite, Sputnik 1, launched in 1957, orbited at ~8 km/s — exactly the value predicted by v₀ = √(GM/r).
  • Arthur C. Clarke proposed the geostationary orbit in 1945; it is now so crowded that slot allocation is internationally regulated.
  • Satellites in low orbit experience tiny atmospheric drag; station-keeping burns periodically restore their altitude.
  • GPS satellites orbit at 20,200 km altitude with a period of 12 hours (ground speed matched to Earth), giving precise timing signals.
  • The Moon is a natural satellite whose orbital period (27.3 days) follows the same T = 2π√(r³/GM) law as any spacecraft.
Examples
  • LEO speed: v = √(gR) = √(9.8×6.4×10⁶) ≈ 7.9 km/s.
  • ISS period ≈ 93 minutes at r = 6.8×10⁶ m.
  • Geostationary altitude ≈ 35,800 km; v ≈ 3.08 km/s.
  • Moon period ≈ 27.3 days from T = 2π√(r³/GM).
  • Energy check at r = 7×10⁶ m, m = 500 kg: E = −GMm/2r ≈ −1.42×10¹⁰ J.

Formulas
  • Orbital velocity: v₀ = √(GM/r)
  • Near-surface orbital speed: v₀ = √(gR)
  • Time period: T = 2πr/v₀ = 2π√(r³/GM)
  • Kinetic energy: K = GMm/2r
  • Total energy: E = −GMm/2r (E = U/2, K = −U/2)
Key Points
  • v₀ = √(GM/r) — orbital speed depends only on radius, not satellite mass.
  • T = 2π√(r³/GM) — Kepler's third law: T² ∝ r³.
  • Higher orbit → slower speed, longer period.
  • E_total = −GMm/2r: bound orbit, with K = −U/2.
  • Astronauts float because of free fall, not absence of gravity.
Summary

  • Finding the altitude for a given period (e.g., 2 h, 24 h) is a standard numerical.
