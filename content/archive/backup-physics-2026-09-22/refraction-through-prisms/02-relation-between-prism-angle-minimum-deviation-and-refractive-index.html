Relation Between Prism Angle, Minimum Deviation and Refractive Index

Notes

  • The prism formula: n=sin(2A​)sin(2A+δmin​​)​​ where n is the refractive index of the prism material, A is the prism angle, and δmin​ is the angle of minimum deviation.
  • Derivation at minimum deviation: At minimum deviation, i=e and r1​=r2​=r=A/2. From Snell's law at the first face: n=sinrsini​=sin(A/2)sini​.
  • Finding i at minimum deviation: Since δmin​=i+e−A=2i−A, we get i=2A+δmin​​. Substituting into Snell's law: n=sin2A​sin2A+δmin​​​.
  • Measuring n experimentally: (1) Place the prism on a spectrometer table. (2) Direct a parallel beam of monochromatic light at one face. (3) Rotate the prism to find the position of minimum deviation. (4) Measure δmin​. (5) Measure A separately. (6) Calculate n using the formula.
  • Thin prism approximation: For small A (typically A<10°), sin2A​≈2A​ and sin2A+δmin​​≈2A+δmin​​. The formula simplifies to: n≈1+Aδmin​​, or δmin​≈(n−1)A.
  • Dispersion and the prism formula: Since n varies with wavelength (normal dispersion: nviolet​>nred​), each color has a different δmin​. This is why a prism separates white light into a spectrum.
  • Refractive index from deviation: If you know A and measure δmin​, you can find n. Conversely, if you know n and measure δmin​, you can find A (useful for identifying unknown prism materials).
  • Wavelength dependence: For crown glass: nred​≈1.514, nyellow​≈1.517, nviolet​≈1.532. For flint glass: nred​≈1.620, nviolet​≈1.662. Flint glass produces more dispersion (wider spectrum).
  • Deviation for different colors: At minimum deviation, δmin​=2i−A. Since i depends on n (via Snell's law), and n depends on wavelength, each color has a different δmin​. Violet deviates most, red deviates least.
  • Angular dispersion: The angular separation between two colors is δmin​(λ1​)−δmin​(λ2​). For a prism with A=60° and Δn=0.018 (flint glass), the angular dispersion is approximately Δδ≈dndδ​Δn≈cos((A+δ)/2)2sin(A/2)​Δn.
  • Mean deviation: The deviation for yellow (sodium D line, 589 nm) is often taken as the mean deviation. This is the wavelength most sensitive to the human eye and is used as the reference in many measurements.
  • Practical application — gemology: Gemologists use the refractive index (measured via minimum deviation) to identify gemstones. Different gem materials have characteristic refractive indices: quartz (n≈1.54), ruby (n≈1.76), diamond (n≈2.42).
  • Limit of the formula: The formula assumes the prism is surrounded by air (nair​≈1). If the prism is in another medium, replace n with nprism​/nmedium​. For example, in water: nrel​=nglass​/nwater​≈1.5/1.33=1.13.
  • NEB exam focus: Derive the prism formula from Snell's law and the minimum deviation condition. Use it to calculate n, A, or δmin​ when two are known. Distinguish between the general deviation formula and the minimum deviation formula.
  • Common values to remember: For common glass (n≈1.5) and A=60°: δmin​≈37°. For flint glass (n≈1.65) and A=60°: δmin​≈50°.

Confusion

  • ❌ The prism formula n=sin2A+δ​/sin2A​ works for ANY angle of incidence. ✅ This formula is ONLY valid at minimum deviation. For arbitrary incidence, you must use the general formulas: δ=i+e−A and Snell's law at each face.
  • ❌ All colors deviate by the same amount through a prism. ✅ Different wavelengths have different refractive indices (dispersion). Violet light (λ≈400 nm) has a higher n and deviates MORE than red light (λ≈700 nm).
  • ❌ The prism formula gives n directly without measuring δmin​. ✅ You must measure BOTH A and δmin​ experimentally. The formula relates them to n, but you need at least two of the three quantities to find the third.
  • ❌ A larger prism angle always gives larger minimum deviation. ✅ Generally yes, but the relationship is not linear. For a fixed n, as A increases, δmin​ increases, but the rate of increase depends on the trigonometric functions involved.
  • ❌ The thin prism approximation δmin​=(n−1)A is exact for all prism angles. ✅ It is an approximation valid only for small A (typically <10°). For larger angles, use the full formula with sine functions.
  • ❌ The refractive index is the same for all wavelengths. ✅ Refractive index depends on wavelength (Cauchy's equation: n(λ)=A+B/λ2+C/λ4). This wavelength dependence is what causes dispersion in prisms.
  • ❌ You can use the prism formula when the prism is immersed in water. ✅ The formula assumes the prism is in air. In water, use the RELATIVE refractive index: nrel​=nprism​/nwater​. The deviation will be smaller because nrel​ is closer to 1.

  • For a prism with n=1.6 and A=50°, find δmin​. 1.6=sin25°sin250+δmin​​​. sin250+δmin​​=1.6×0.4226=0.6762. 250+δmin​​=arcsin(0.6762)=42.55°. δmin​=85.1−50=35.1°.
  • A thin prism produces a deviation of 4° for yellow light. If n=1.5, find the prism angle. Using δmin​=(n−1)A: 4°=(1.5−1)A=0.5A. A=8°. Check: n=1+4/8=1.5. ✓
  • Red and violet light pass through a prism with A=60°. nred​=1.514, nviolet​=1.532. Find the angular dispersion. For red: sin260+δr​​=1.514×0.5=0.757⇒260+δr​​=49.2°⇒δr​=38.4°. For violet: sin260+δv​​=1.532×0.5=0.766⇒260+δv​​=50.0°⇒δv​=40.0°. Angular dispersion =40.0−38.4=1.6°.
  • A prism in water (nwater​=1.33) has nglass​=1.5. Find the relative refractive index and the minimum deviation for A=60°. nrel​=1.5/1.33=1.128. sin260+δmin​​=1.128×0.5=0.564. 260+δmin​​=34.3°. δmin​=68.6−60=8.6°. Much smaller than in air (37°) because the index contrast is smaller.
  • Show that for a given prism material, the minimum deviation increases with prism angle. Taking the derivative of δmin​ with respect to A: from nsin(A/2)=sin((A+δmin​)/2), implicit differentiation shows dAdδmin​​>0 for all A<90°.

Universal Facts

  • The prism formula was derived by W. Thomson (Lord Kelvin) in the 19th century, though the underlying principles of Snell's law and minimum deviation were known since the 17th century.
  • Diamond's high refractive index (n≈2.42) is why it produces such brilliant dispersion (fire). A diamond prism would separate white light much more effectively than a glass prism.
  • The Cauchy equation n(λ)=A+B/λ2+C/λ4 describes how refractive index varies with wavelength. This is why blue light always bends more than red light in any dispersive medium.
  • In spectroscopy, prisms were replaced by diffraction gratings for high-precision work because gratings provide linear dispersion (angle proportional to wavelength), while prisms have nonlinear dispersion.
  • The minimum deviation method is so accurate that it can determine refractive index to 5 significant figures. This precision is essential in optics design for cameras, microscopes, and telescopes.
