Relation Between Prism Angle, Minimum Deviation and Refractive Index

Notes

  • Small-angle prism definition: A prism with a refracting angle A≤10° (typically 2°–10°) is called a thin or small-angle prism. The approximation sinθ≈θ (radians) is valid for such angles.
  • Derivation of δ=(n−1)A: From the prism formula at minimum deviation: n=sin2A​sin2A+δ​​. For small angles: sinx≈x, so n≈A/2(A+δ)/2​=AA+δ​=1+Aδ​. Therefore: δ≈(n−1)A​.
  • Independent of angle of incidence: Unlike the general prism formula, the thin-prism deviation δ=(n−1)A does NOT depend on the angle of incidence i. This is because for small A, the ray passes nearly symmetrically regardless of i.
  • Why independence matters: In a thin prism, even if the incident ray is not at the minimum deviation position, the deviation is approximately the same. This makes thin prisms useful in optical instruments where precise alignment is difficult.
  • Dispersion by a thin prism: Since n varies with wavelength: δv​=(nv​−1)A and δr​=(nr​−1)A. The angular dispersion (separation between violet and red) is: δv​−δr​=(nv​−nr​)A​.
  • Dispersive power: The dispersive power of the prism material is ω=ny​−1nv​−nr​​, where ny​ is the refractive index for yellow (sodium D line). The mean deviation is δy​=(ny​−1)A. Therefore: ω=δy​δv​−δr​​​.
  • Combined thin prisms — achromatic combination: Two thin prisms of different materials can be combined to produce deviation WITHOUT dispersion (achromatic): (nv1​−nr1​)A1​=(nv2​−nr2​)A2​. The deviations cancel for dispersion while the mean deviations add.
  • Combined thin prisms — direct-vision prism: Two thin prisms can be arranged to produce dispersion WITHOUT net deviation (direct vision): (ny1​−1)A1​=(ny2​−1)A2​ but (nv1​−nr1​)A1​=(nv2​−nr2​)A2​. This is used in spectrometers to view a spectrum without deviating the central ray.
  • Practical applications: Thin prisms are used in (1) binoculars for image erection, (2) spectrometers for approximate dispersion measurements, (3) optical compensators in interferometers, (4) prism goggles for vision correction (prismatic lenses).
  • Deviation is small but measurable: For crown glass (n=1.52) and A=5°: δ=(1.52−1)×5°=2.6°. This small deviation is easily measured with a spectrometer and used to find n accurately.
  • Limitation of the approximation: The formula δ=(n−1)A has about 1% error for A=10° and n=1.5. For A=60° (a typical large prism), the error is ~15% — the thin-prism formula is completely inapplicable.
  • Connection to lenses: A thin prism deflects light by a constant angle. A thin lens can be thought of as many thin prisms arranged base-to-base (convex) or tip-to-tip (concave). The deviation at each "prism" varies with distance from the axis, producing focusing.
  • NEB exam focus: Derive δ=(n−1)A from the general prism formula using the small-angle approximation. Calculate angular dispersion and dispersive power for thin prisms. Solve combination problems (achromatic and direct-vision).
  • Numerical example — crown glass prism: A=6°, nr​=1.514, ny​=1.517, nv​=1.523. δr​=(1.514−1)×6=3.084°. δy​=3.102°. δv​=3.138°. Angular dispersion =3.138−3.084=0.054°. Dispersive power ω=0.054/3.102=0.0174.
  • Reverse calculation — finding A from known δ: If a thin prism produces a deviation of 3° for light of wavelength 589 nm and the material has n=1.52: A=δ/(n−1)=3°/0.52=5.77°≈5°46′. This is a typical prism angle for measuring refractive index.

Confusion

  • ❌ The thin-prism formula δ=(n−1)A works for ANY prism angle. ✅ It is only valid for small A (typically A≤10°). For larger angles, use the full formula n=sin2A+δ​/sin2A​.
  • ❌ The deviation through a thin prism depends on the angle of incidence. ✅ For a thin prism, the deviation is approximately INDEPENDENT of the angle of incidence. This is a key distinguishing feature from a thick prism.
  • ❌ A thin prism produces no dispersion. ✅ A thin prism DOES produce dispersion — different wavelengths deviate by slightly different amounts. The dispersion is smaller than for a thick prism, but it is nonzero.
  • ❌ Two thin prisms always cancel each other's deviation. ✅ Two thin prisms can be arranged to cancel deviation (direct-vision) or cancel dispersion (achromatic), but not both simultaneously with the same materials.
  • ❌ The dispersive power ω depends on the prism angle A. ✅ Dispersive power is a PROPERTY OF THE MATERIAL, not the prism geometry. ω=(nv​−nr​)/(ny​−1) depends only on refractive indices.
  • ❌ For a thin prism, δmin​=(n−1)A. ✅ For a thin prism, δmin​≈δ≈(n−1)A. The minimum deviation is approximately the same as the deviation at any angle of incidence.
  • ❌ A combination of two thin prisms can eliminate both deviation and dispersion. ✅ This is impossible with two prisms of different materials. You can eliminate dispersion (achromatic) OR deviation (direct-vision), but not both simultaneously.

Practice

  • A thin prism of angle A=4° produces a deviation of 2.2°. Find the refractive index. δ=(n−1)A⇒n−1=δ/A=2.2/4=0.55. n=1.55. This is typical of flint glass.
  • A crown glass thin prism (nr​=1.514, nv​=1.524) has A=5°. Find the angular dispersion and dispersive power. δv​−δr​=(nv​−nr​)A=(1.524−1.514)×5=0.05°. Using ny​≈1.519: ω=0.5190.010​=0.0193.
  • Two thin prisms are combined to form an achromatic system. Prism 1 (crown glass: nr​=1.514, nv​=1.524, ny​=1.519) has A1​=4°. Prism 2 (flint glass: nr​=1.613, nv​=1.632, ny​=1.620) has unknown A2​. For achromatism: (nv1​−nr1​)A1​=(nv2​−nr2​)A2​. (0.010)(4°)=(0.019)A2​. A2​=0.040/0.019=2.11°≈2°7′.
  • A direct-vision prism is made by combining a crown glass prism (A1​=5°, ny​=1.517) with a flint glass prism (ny​=1.620). Find the flint prism angle for zero net deviation. For zero deviation: (ny1​−1)A1​=(ny2​−1)A2​. (0.517)(5°)=(0.620)A2​. A2​=2.585/0.620=4.17°≈4°10′.
  • A thin prism deviates a ray by 3.5°. If the prism angle is 7°, find n. n=1+δ/A=1+3.5/7=1.50. The material is crown glass or similar.
  • Show that for a thin prism, the deviation is nearly independent of the angle of incidence. At small A, the internal angles r1​ and r2​ are both small. By Snell's law: i≈nr1​ and e≈nr2​. Since r1​+r2​=A (approximately), i+e≈nA. Therefore δ=i+e−A≈(n−1)A, independent of i.

Universal Facts

  • The thin-prism approximation δ=(n−1)A is one of the simplest formulas in geometric optics. Despite its simplicity, it is accurate enough for many practical applications in optical instrument design.
  • Achromatic prism combinations were invented by Chester Moore Hall in the 1730s. He discovered that two prisms of different glass types could cancel dispersion while preserving deviation, enabling the first true color-corrected telescopes.
  • The dispersive power ω is a dimensionless quantity that characterizes the "rainbow strength" of a glass. Crown glasses have ω≈0.016–0.018; flint glasses have ω≈0.028–0.040.
  • Thin prisms are used in ophthalmology as prismatic lenses to correct double vision (diplopia). A prism of deviation 2–10° bends light to align the images seen by each eye.
  • In fiber optics, a thin prism can be used as a wavelength splitter because different colors are deviated by different amounts. This principle is used in dense wavelength-division multiplexing (DWDM) systems.
