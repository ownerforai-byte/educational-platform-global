Minimum Deviation Condition

Notes

  • Deviation angle (δ): When a ray of light passes through a prism, it bends at both faces. The total angle between the incident ray's original direction and the emergent ray's direction is called the angle of deviation, δ.
  • Formula for deviation: δ=i+e−A, where i is the angle of incidence, e is the angle of emergence, and A is the prism angle (refracting angle).
  • Minimum deviation (δ_min): As you vary the angle of incidence i, the deviation δ changes. There is a unique value of i that gives the SMALLEST possible deviation, called the angle of minimum deviation, δmin​.
  • Symmetric path condition: At minimum deviation, the ray passes SYMMETRICALLY through the prism: i=e (angle of incidence equals angle of emergence) and r1​=r2​=r (angles of refraction at both faces are equal).
  • Geometric consequence: At minimum deviation, the ray inside the prism is parallel to the base of the prism (for an isosceles prism). This is the shortest path through the prism.
  • Finding δ_min experimentally: Rotate the prism on a spectrometer table while viewing the emergent ray. The deviation angle decreases to a minimum and then increases. The minimum point is marked by the ray momentarily stopping and reversing direction.
  • Why minimum deviation matters: At δmin​, the prism formula n=sin2A​sin2A+δmin​​​ is exact and doesn't require knowing the internal ray path. This makes it the most accurate way to measure refractive index.
  • Curve of δ vs. i: Plotting deviation δ against angle of incidence i gives a U-shaped curve. The lowest point is δmin​. The curve is symmetric about the minimum point (since swapping i and e gives the same deviation).
  • Physical intuition: At minimum deviation, the light takes the "path of least resistance" through the prism. Any deviation from the symmetric path increases the total bending because one face does more bending while the other does less, and the net effect is larger.
  • Small prism approximation: For a thin prism (small A), the minimum deviation is approximately δmin​≈(n−1)A. This is useful for estimating deviation without precise measurements.
  • Dispersion at minimum deviation: Since refractive index n depends on wavelength (nviolet​>nred​), each color has a different δmin​. Violet light deviates more than red light, producing a spectrum.
  • Spectrometer measurement: In a laboratory spectrometer, δmin​ is found by rotating the prism table until the image of the slit stops moving and reverses direction. The angle between the incident and emergent ray at this point is δmin​.
  • NEB exam focus: State the condition for minimum deviation (i=e, r1​=r2​). Draw the ray diagram showing the symmetric path. Explain why minimum deviation gives the most accurate refractive index measurement.
  • Connection to prisms in optics: Minimum deviation is used in spectrometers, monochromators, and prism-based optical instruments. It provides the most well-defined relationship between angle and refractive index.

Confusion

  • ❌ Minimum deviation occurs when the ray passes straight through the prism without bending. ✅ Minimum deviation still involves bending — it's the SMALLEST possible total deviation, not zero deviation. The ray still refracts at both surfaces.
  • ❌ At minimum deviation, the ray is perpendicular to both prism faces. ✅ At minimum deviation, the ray inside the prism is parallel to the BASE (for an isosceles prism), not perpendicular to the faces. The angles of incidence and emergence are equal but not necessarily 90°.
  • ❌ Any angle of incidence gives the same deviation. ✅ Deviation varies with angle of incidence. Only at one specific value of i does the deviation reach its minimum. For other values of i, the deviation is larger.
  • ❌ The angle of minimum deviation is zero for a prism. ✅ For a prism with A>0 and n>1, the minimum deviation is always positive. Zero deviation would require no refraction, which is impossible for a prism in air.
  • ❌ Minimum deviation means the ray doesn't bend at all. ✅ "Minimum" means the smallest possible, not zero. The ray still bends — it's just the configuration that produces the least total bending.
  • ❌ At minimum deviation, r1​=r2​. ✅ At minimum deviation, r1​=r2​=A/2. This is one of the key conditions that defines minimum deviation.
  • ❌ You can find δ_min without a spectrometer. ✅ While you can approximate δ_min with a simple setup, precise measurement requires a spectrometer to accurately measure the angle between incident and emergent rays.

Practice

  • A prism has angle A=60° and refractive index n=1.5. Find the angle of minimum deviation. At minimum deviation: n=sin2A​sin2A+δmin​​​. 1.5=sin30°sin260+δmin​​​=0.5sin260+δmin​​​. So sin260+δmin​​=0.75. 260+δmin​​=arcsin(0.75)=48.59°. δmin​=97.18−60=37.18°.
  • Show that at minimum deviation, r=A/2. At minimum deviation, i=e and by symmetry r1​=r2​. From the prism geometry: A=r1​+r2​=2r. Therefore r=A/2. For A=60°, r=30°.
  • A glass prism (n=1.6) has minimum deviation δmin​=40°. Find the prism angle A. From n=sin2A​sin2A+δmin​​​: 1.6=sin2A​sin2A+40​​. Let x=A/2: 1.6sinx=sin(x+20°). Solving numerically: x≈30°, so A≈60°.
  • Why is the minimum deviation method more accurate than measuring deviation at arbitrary incidence? At minimum deviation, the ray path is symmetric, making the measurement less sensitive to small errors in reading the angles. Also, the deviation is at a stationary point (minimum), so small errors in prism orientation have minimal effect on the measured angle.
  • A ray passes through a prism with A=50° and n=1.52. Find the angle of incidence that produces minimum deviation. At minimum deviation: r=A/2=25°. By Snell's law at the first face: sini=nsinr=1.52×sin25°=1.52×0.4226=0.6424. i=arcsin(0.6424)=40.0°.
  • Prove that for a given prism, the angle of incidence that gives minimum deviation is unique. The deviation function δ(i) is convex (U-shaped) with a single minimum. This can be shown by taking the derivative dδ/di and showing it equals zero at exactly one value of i (where i=e).

Universal Facts

  • The minimum deviation condition was first studied systematically by Isaac Newton in his optical experiments. He used a prism to separate white light into colors and measured the deviation of each color.
  • Spectrometers used in chemistry and physics labs are designed to measure angles of minimum deviation with high precision (to within 1 arcminute or better). This allows determination of refractive index to 4-5 significant figures.
  • The concept of minimum deviation applies not just to prisms but to any refracting or reflecting system. Mirrors, lenses, and diffraction gratings all have similar minimum-deflection configurations.
  • In fiber optics, the concept of minimum deviation is related to the critical angle and total internal reflection. Light entering an optical fiber at certain angles experiences minimum loss.
  • The minimum deviation angle depends on the wavelength of light (dispersion). This is why prisms produce rainbows — different colors have different refractive indices and therefore different minimum deviation angles.
