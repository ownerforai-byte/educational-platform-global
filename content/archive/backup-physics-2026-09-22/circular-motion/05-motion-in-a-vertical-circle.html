Motion in a Vertical Circle

Notes

  • Critical case — lowest point: tension is maximum because it must support weight and provide centripetal force: Tmax​=mg+mv2/r.
  • Critical case — highest point: tension is minimum. The body stays on the circle if v≥gr​. If v<gr​, the string goes slack and the body falls.
  • Minimum speed at top: vtop​=gr​. Below this, gravity alone provides more inward force than needed, so the body falls inward.
  • Energy conservation: since only gravity does work, mechanical energy is conserved. 21​mvbottom2​=21​mvtop2​+mg(2r).
  • Practical example: a bucket of water swung in a vertical circle does not spill at the top if the speed is high enough, because the water's inertia keeps it pressed against the bucket.
  • Speed at bottom for minimum condition: using energy conservation with vtop​=gr​: vbottom2​=vtop2​+4gr=gr+4gr=5gr, so vbottom​=5gr​.
  • Tension at bottom (minimum case): Tbottom​=mg+m(5gr)/r=mg+5mg=6mg. The tension at the bottom is 6 times the weight when the object barely completes the loop.
  • Tension at top (minimum case): Ttop​=mvtop2​/r−mg=mg−mg=0. The tension is zero — the string is just about to go slack.
  • Tension difference: the difference between bottom and top tension is always Tbottom​−Ttop​=6mg, regardless of the actual speed, as long as the motion is circular.
  • Rod vs string: a light rod (unlike a string) can push. For a rod, the minimum speed at the top can be zero (vtop​=0). The corresponding minimum speed at the bottom becomes vbottom​=4gr​=2gr​.
  • Vertical circular motion is non-uniform: the speed changes continuously because gravity does work. At the top, speed is minimum; at the bottom, it is maximum.

Confusion

  • For a string, the minimum speed at the top is gr​ (not zero). For a rigid rod, it can be zero because the rod can push outward to support the weight.
  • At the lowest point, tension exceeds weight because it must both support the weight and provide centripetal force: T=mg+mv2/r>mg.
  • At the highest point, tension can be less than weight. If v=2gr​, then T=mv2/r−mg=2mg−mg=mg= weight. For v<2gr​, tension is less than weight.
  • The condition vtop​≥gr​ ensures the string remains taut. Below this speed, the string goes slack and the object leaves the circular path — it does not continue in a smaller circle.
  • In vertical circular motion, the speed is not constant. The kinetic energy converts to potential energy and back, so v varies around the circle even though the radius is fixed.

Practice

  • A 0.5 kg ball is swung in a vertical circle of radius 2 m on a string. What is the minimum speed at the bottom? Solution: vbottom​=5gr​=5×9.8×2​=98​=9.9 m/s.
  • A 1 kg ball is swung in a vertical circle of radius 1.5 m. At the top, the speed is 5 m/s. Find the tension at the top and the speed at the bottom. Solution: At top: Ttop​=mv2/r−mg=1×25/1.5−1×9.8=16.67−9.8=6.87 N. At bottom: 21​mvb2​=21​mvt2​+mg(2r)=21​(25)+9.8×3=12.5+29.4=41.9. vb​=83.8​=9.15 m/s.
  • A 200 g ball on a 0.6 m string just completes a vertical circle. Find the tension at the bottom. Solution: vbottom​=5gr​=5×9.8×0.6​=29.4​=5.42 m/s. Tbottom​=mg+mv2/r=0.2×9.8+0.2×29.4/0.6=1.96+9.8=11.76 N =6mg.
  • A ball on a string has vbottom​=7 m/s with r=1 m. Find the tension at the bottom and at the top, and the speed at the top. Solution: Tbottom​=mg+mv2/r. Using energy: vtop2​=vbottom2​−4gr=49−39.2=9.8. vtop​=3.13 m/s. Ttop​=mvtop2​/r−mg=m(9.8−9.8)=0. The ball just barely reaches the top with tension zero.
  • A light rod carries a 1 kg ball at its end and rotates in a vertical circle of radius 0.8 m. Find the minimum speed at the bottom and the tension at the bottom and top for this minimum case. Solution: For a rod, vtop​=0. vbottom​=4gr​=4×9.8×0.8​=5.6 m/s. At top: Ttop​=mg−mvtop2​/r=9.8−0=9.8 N (rod pushes up). At bottom: Tbottom​=mg+mvbottom2​/r=9.8+1×31.36/0.8=9.8+39.2=49 N =5mg.

Universal Facts

  • For a string in vertical circular motion, the minimum speed at the bottom is 5gr​, and the minimum speed at the top is gr​. These conditions ensure the string never goes slack.
  • The tension at the bottom exceeds the tension at the top by exactly 6mg for any speed that completes the loop, as long as the string remains taut.
  • A ball on a rigid rod can reach the top with zero speed (unlike a string), so the minimum speed at the bottom is 2gr​ for a rod vs 5gr​ for a string.
  • In a vertical loop, the ball is fastest at the bottom and slowest at the top. The speed difference depends on the loop radius: vbottom2​−vtop2​=4gr.
  • The centripetal force is not constant in vertical circular motion — it varies because the speed changes. Only the radius remains constant.
