Centripetal Acceleration and Centripetal Force

Relevance

Notes

  • Centripetal acceleration: ac​=v2/r=rω2. It points toward the centre of the circle and changes the direction of velocity without changing its magnitude.
  • Why 'centripetal' means centre-seeking: without this inward acceleration, the object would move in a straight line by Newton's first law.
  • Centripetal force: Fc​=mv2/r=mrω2. This is not a new type of force; it is the net inward force required for circular motion.
  • Examples of centripetal force: tension in a string for a conical pendulum, friction between tyres and road for a turning car, gravity for a satellite.
  • Centrifugal force: in a rotating frame, an outward pseudo force appears. It is not a real force but a consequence of inertia in a non-inertial frame.
  • Derivation of ac​: for a particle moving at speed v in a circle, the velocity changes direction but not magnitude. For a small angle dθ, the change in velocity ∣dv∣=vdθ. Since dθ=ds/r=vdt/r, the acceleration ac​=∣dv∣/dt=v2/r.
  • No work done by centripetal force: since Fc​ is perpendicular to v, the power P=F⋅v=0. Centripetal force changes direction but never the kinetic energy.
  • Banking and centripetal force: on a banked road, the horizontal component of the normal reaction provides Fc​=Nsinθ. On an unbanked road, static friction provides it: Fc​=μs​N.
  • Satellite orbit: for a satellite in circular orbit at radius R from Earth's centre, mv2/R=GMm/R2, giving v=GM/R​. Larger orbits mean lower orbital speeds.
  • Conical pendulum: tension T provides centripetal force Tsinθ=mv2/r, while its vertical component Tcosθ balances the weight.
  • Unit analysis: [mv2/r]=kg⋅(m/s)2/m=kg⋅m/s2=N. The centripetal force formula is dimensionally consistent.

Confusion

  • Centripetal force is not a new or special type of force. It is always one of the existing forces (tension, friction, gravity, normal reaction, or their resultant) directed toward the centre.
  • Centrifugal force is a fictitious (pseudo) force that appears only in a rotating reference frame. In an inertial frame, it does not exist — the object merely tends to move in a straight line.
  • Centripetal force does no work because it is perpendicular to the velocity. An object in uniform circular motion has constant speed and constant kinetic energy.
  • The centripetal force mv2/r does not appear in free body diagrams as a separate force. The net of all real forces in the radial direction must equal mv2/r.
  • As radius increases for a given speed, less centripetal force is needed (Fc​∝1/r). But for a given angular velocity, more force is needed (Fc​∝r). Always check which variable is held constant.

Practice

  • A 2 kg stone is swung in a vertical circle of radius 1.5 m at 6 m/s. Find the centripetal acceleration and centripetal force. Solution: ac​=v2/r=36/1.5=24 m/s2. Fc​=mac​=2×24=48 N.
  • A car of mass 1200 kg turns a corner of radius 50 m at 20 m/s on a flat road. Find the friction force needed and the minimum coefficient of friction. Solution: Fc​=mv2/r=1200×400/50=9600 N. f=μs​mg, so μs​=9600/(1200×9.8)=0.816.
  • A satellite orbits Earth at R=7000 km from the centre. Earth's mass M=5.97×1024 kg, G=6.67×10−11 Nm2/kg2. Find the orbital speed. Solution: v=GM/R​=6.67×10−11×5.97×1024/7×106​=5.69×107​=7543 m/s ≈7.5 km/s.
  • A 0.5 kg mass is attached to a 1 m string and swings in a horizontal circle with the string at 60∘ to the vertical. Find the tension, speed, and period. Solution: Tcos60∘=mg=4.9, so T=9.8 N. r=lsin60∘=0.866 m. Tsin60∘=mv2/r, so v=Trsin60∘/m​=9.8×0.866×0.866/0.5​=3.83 m/s. Tperiod​=2πr/v=2π×0.866/3.83=1.42 s.
  • A washing machine drum of radius 0.25 m spins at 1200 RPM. Find the centripetal acceleration at the rim. Solution: ω=1200×2π/60=40π≈125.66 rad/s. ac​=rω2=0.25×15791=3948 m/s2≈403g. This enormous acceleration is how water is extracted from clothes.

Universal Facts

  • Centripetal force is always perpendicular to velocity, so it never changes the kinetic energy or speed of the object — it only changes the direction of motion.
  • For any object in uniform circular motion, there must be a net inward force. If this force disappears, the object flies off tangentially in a straight line, not radially outward.
  • The fastest a planet can orbit at a given radius is the speed of light. For a black hole, the event horizon radius is where the required centripetal acceleration equals the speed of light divided by light-crossing time.
  • In the rotating reference frame of a carnival ride, passengers feel pushed outward (centrifugal force). In the inertial frame, they are simply being constrained to circular motion by the wall of the ride.
  • On Earth's surface, objects at the equator experience a centripetal acceleration of about 0.034 m/s2 due to Earth's rotation — about 0.3% of g.
