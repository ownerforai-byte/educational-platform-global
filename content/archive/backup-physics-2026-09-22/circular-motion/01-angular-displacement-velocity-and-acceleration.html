Angular Displacement, Velocity and Acceleration

Notes

  • Angular displacement θ: the angle through which a point on a rotating body moves, measured in radians. θ=s/r, where s is arc length and r is radius.
  • Angular velocity ω: rate of change of angular displacement. ω=dθ/dt. Units: rad/s.
  • Angular acceleration α: rate of change of angular velocity. α=dω/dt. Units: rad/s2.
  • Connection to linear motion: v=rω and at​=rα, where v is tangential speed and at​ is tangential acceleration.
  • Direction: angular velocity and acceleration are vectors along the axis of rotation, given by the right-hand rule.
  • Uniform circular motion: when ω is constant, angular displacement θ=ωt, angular acceleration α=0, but centripetal acceleration ac​=rω2 is nonzero.
  • Constant angular acceleration: rotational kinematic equations mirror translational ones: ω2=ω02​+2αθ, θ=ω0​t+21​αt2, ω=ω0​+αt.
  • Radian measure: one complete revolution =2π radians =360∘. All circular motion formulas require angles in radians, not degrees.
  • Frequency and period: frequency f=ω/(2π) in Hz (revolutions per second). Period T=1/f=2π/ω is the time for one revolution.
  • Angular velocity in RPM: to convert from revolutions per minute (RPM) to rad/s: ω=2πN/60, where N is RPM.
  • Physical significance: angular acceleration tells you how quickly the rotation speeds up or slows down. A spinning bicycle wheel with constant ω has α=0 but nonzero centripetal acceleration.

Confusion

  • Angular velocity ω is in rad/s, not m/s. The linear speed v=rω converts angular velocity to linear speed at distance r from the axis.
  • A body can have angular velocity without any linear velocity at all — every point on a body rotating about its own centre has zero linear velocity of the centre.
  • Angular acceleration α is zero in uniform circular motion, even though centripetal acceleration ac​=rω2 is not. α changes the speed; ac​ changes the direction.
  • One full revolution =2π rad =360∘. When using formulas like θ=ωt, angles must be in radians, not degrees.
  • The sign of ω or α indicates direction of rotation: positive for counterclockwise, negative for clockwise (by convention in the standard coordinate system).

Practice

  • A flywheel spinning at 300 RPM accelerates uniformly to 900 RPM in 10 seconds. Find the angular acceleration and total angular displacement. Solution: ω0​=300×2π/60=10π rad/s, ω=900×2π/60=30π rad/s. α=(30π−10π)/10=2π rad/s2. θ=ω0​t+21​αt2=10π×10+21​×2π×100=100π+100π=200π rad ≈628 rad.
  • A wheel of radius 0.5 m rotates with ω=10 rad/s and α=4 rad/s2. Find the tangential speed, centripetal acceleration, and tangential acceleration. Solution: v=rω=0.5×10=5 m/s. ac​=rω2=0.5×100=50 m/s2. at​=rα=0.5×4=2 m/s2.
  • The period of a rotating fan is 0.25 s. Find its angular velocity and frequency. Solution: ω=2π/T=2π/0.25=8π≈25.13 rad/s. f=1/T=4 Hz.
  • A disc starts from rest and reaches 1200 RPM in 4 s with uniform angular acceleration. Find α and the number of revolutions. Solution: ω=1200×2π/60=40π rad/s. α=40π/4=10π rad/s2. θ=21​×10π×16=80π rad =40 revolutions.
  • A point on a rim is at radius 0.3 m from the axis. After rotating through 6π radians, how far has this point traveled? Solution: s=rθ=0.3×6π=1.8π≈5.65 m.

Universal Facts

  • The radian is the natural unit for angular measurements in physics because it directly relates arc length to radius: s=rθ. Using degrees in formulas would introduce extra conversion factors.
  • Angular velocity is a vector directed along the rotation axis by the right-hand rule. Two gears meshing in opposite directions have angular velocities of opposite signs.
  • In uniform circular motion, angular velocity is constant (α=0) but the object still has centripetal acceleration because the direction of velocity continuously changes.
  • The period T and frequency f are inversely related: f=1/T. The SI unit of frequency is the hertz (Hz), where 1Hz=1s−1.
  • Rotational kinematic equations have exact analogs to translational kinematics: replace x with θ, v with ω, and a with α.
