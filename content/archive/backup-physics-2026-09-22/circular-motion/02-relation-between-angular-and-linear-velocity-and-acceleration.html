Relation Between Angular and Linear Velocity and Acceleration

Notes

  • Linear to angular: ω=v/r. A point at the rim of a wheel moves faster in linear speed but has the same angular velocity as a point near the centre.
  • Angular to linear: v=rω. If ω is constant, linear speed increases with radius — which is why the outer edge of a spinning disc has higher speed.
  • Tangential acceleration: at​=rα. This is the acceleration tangent to the circle, changing the speed along the path.
  • Centripetal acceleration: ac​=v2/r=rω2. This is the inward acceleration required to keep the object moving in a circle.
  • Total acceleration: when both speed and direction change, a=at​+ac​. The two components are perpendicular.
  • Direction of v: the linear velocity is always tangential to the circular path. The direction changes continuously even when speed is constant.
  • Instantaneous vs average: v=rω relates instantaneous values. For average values, vˉ=rωˉ holds only if angular acceleration is constant.
  • Vector form: v=ω×r. This cross product automatically gives the correct direction (tangential) and magnitude (rω).
  • Multiple radii on one body: all points on a rigid rotating body share the same ω and α, but their tangential speed and tangential acceleration differ proportionally to their distance from the axis.
  • Practical example: a grinding wheel at 3000 RPM with diameter 0.2 m: ω=3000×2π/60=100π≈314 rad/s. Rim speed v=0.1×314=31.4 m/s, fast enough to be dangerous.

Confusion

  • All points on a rigid body have the same angular velocity ω, but points farther from the axis have greater linear speed v=rω. The relation v=rω does not mean larger radius means larger ω.
  • Centripetal acceleration ac​=v2/r depends on v at that radius. Since v=rω, substituting gives ac​=rω2, which increases with r. Do not confuse with v2/r appearing to decrease with r — that is for fixed v, not fixed ω.
  • Tangential acceleration at​=rα changes the speed. Centripetal acceleration ac​=rω2 changes the direction. They are perpendicular and independent — you can have one without the other.
  • The formula v=ω×r is the vector relationship. In one dimension, it simplifies to v=rω. But the vector form is essential for 3D problems.
  • For a rolling wheel, the velocity of the top is v+rω (which equals 2v) and the bottom is v−rω=0 (instantaneous rest for rolling without slipping).

Practice

  • A point on a disc of radius 0.1 m is at 5 m/s tangential speed. Find the angular velocity and the centripetal acceleration. Solution: ω=v/r=5/0.1=50 rad/s. ac​=v2/r=25/0.1=250 m/s2. Alternatively ac​=rω2=0.1×2500=250 m/s2.
  • A spinning disc has angular acceleration α=10 rad/s2 and angular velocity ω=20 rad/s at a point 0.15 m from the axis. Find the total acceleration. Solution: at​=rα=0.15×10=1.5 m/s2. ac​=rω2=0.15×400=60 m/s2. a=1.52+602​≈60.02 m/s2. Direction: θ=tan−1(1.5/60)=1.43∘ from the radial direction.
  • A merry-go-round rotates at 20 RPM. Two children sit at 1 m and 2 m from the centre. Find their linear speeds and the difference. Solution: ω=20×2π/60=2π/3 rad/s. v1​=1×2π/3=2.09 m/s. v2​=2×2π/3=4.19 m/s. Difference =2.10 m/s.
  • A point 0.4 m from the axis has ω=15 rad/s and α=5 rad/s2. Find the angle between the total acceleration vector and the radius. Solution: at​=2 m/s2, ac​=0.4×225=90 m/s2. θ=tan−1(2/90)=1.27∘.
  • A bicycle wheel of diameter 0.7 m rolls without slipping at 10 m/s. Find the angular velocity. Solution: r=0.35 m. ω=v/r=10/0.35=28.6 rad/s. RPM =28.6×60/(2π)=273 RPM.

Universal Facts

  • For a rigid body rotating about a fixed axis, every point shares the same angular velocity ω and angular acceleration α, regardless of its distance from the axis.
  • Linear speed v=rω increases with radius. This is why the tips of helicopter rotor blades can exceed the speed of sound even when the hub rotates relatively slowly.
  • Centripetal acceleration ac​=rω2 also increases with radius for a given angular velocity, meaning outer points experience greater centripetal acceleration.
  • The vector relation v=ω×r always gives the tangential velocity direction. The cross product means v is perpendicular to both ω and r.
  • In rolling without slipping, the condition v=rω connects the translational and rotational motions, reducing the degrees of freedom by one.
