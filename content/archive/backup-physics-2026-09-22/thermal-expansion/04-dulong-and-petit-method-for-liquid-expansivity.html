Dulong and Petit Method for Liquid Expansivity

Notes

  • Apparatus: A U-tube made of glass with two vertical limbs and a horizontal base. One limb is kept in ice (0°C) and the other in steam (100°C) or hot water. The liquid in the tube reaches a steady state with different temperatures in each limb.
  • Principle: At the horizontal base of the U-tube, the pressure from both limbs must be equal (Pascal's law). The pressure at the base from each limb is P=ρgh, where ρ is the liquid density at that temperature and h is the height of the liquid column.
  • Equating pressures: ρ0​gh0​=ρ100​gh100​, where h0​ is the height in the cold limb (0°C) and h100​ is the height in the hot limb (100°C). Therefore: h0​h100​​=ρ100​ρ0​​.
  • Connecting density to expansion: Since ρ=Vm​ and mass is constant: ρ100​ρ0​​=V0​V100​​=1+γabs​ΔT. Therefore: h0​h100​​=1+γabs​ΔT1​.
  • Solving for γabs​: 1+γabs​ΔT=h100​h0​​. Therefore: γabs​=h100​⋅ΔTh0​−h100​​​.
  • Apparent vs absolute: The heights h0​ and h100​ are read from the glass scale, which itself expands with temperature. The measured heights give γapp​. To get γabs​: γabs​=γapp​+γglass​.
  • Why this method is elegant: No knowledge of the liquid's mass or volume is needed. Only the difference in heights is measured. The U-tube eliminates errors from container expansion because both limbs are made of the same glass.
  • Experimental procedure: (1) Fill the U-tube with the liquid. (2) Place one limb in an ice bath and the other in a steam bath or hot water. (3) Wait for steady state (15–20 minutes). (4) Measure h0​ and h100​ from the scale. (5) Calculate γabs​.
  • Source of error — scale expansion: The glass scale expands with temperature, so the measured heights are slightly smaller than the true heights. The correction is γglass​≈2.7×10−5 K⁻¹ for common glass.
  • Source of error — temperature gradient: The liquid in each limb may not be at a uniform temperature. The cold limb may warm slightly from the surroundings, and the hot limb may cool. Careful insulation minimizes this.
  • NEB exam focus: Draw and label the Dulong-Petit apparatus. Derive the formula for γabs​. Explain the distinction between apparent and absolute expansion. State sources of error and how to minimize them.
  • Sample calculation: In a U-tube experiment, h0​=50.0 cm (cold limb at 0°C) and h100​=51.5 cm (hot limb at 100°C). γapp​=50.0×10051.5−50.0​=50001.5​=3.0×10−4 K⁻¹. γabs​=3.0×10−4+2.7×10−5=3.27×10−4 K⁻¹. This is close to the value for ethanol (7.5×10−4) — the liquid may be a different substance or the measurement has error.
  • Comparison with other methods: The Dulong-Petit method is more accurate than the weight thermometer for liquids with small expansion coefficients. However, it requires a long U-tube and careful temperature control.
  • Historical note: Pierre Dulong and Alexis Thérèse Petit published this method in 1816. They also discovered the Dulong-Petit law for the molar heat capacity of solids (Cm​≈3R≈25 J/(mol·K)), which was one of the first links between thermal and atomic properties.
  • Why the hot limb is taller: The liquid in the hot limb is less dense, so a taller column is needed to produce the same pressure at the base. This is why h100​>h0​ — it confirms that heating reduces density.

Confusion

  • ❌ The formula is γ=h0​ΔTh0​−h100​​. ✅ The correct formula is γabs​=h100​ΔTh0​−h100​​. The denominator uses h100​ (the hot limb height), not h0​.
  • ❌ The apparent expansion is greater than the absolute expansion. ✅ Apparent expansion is LESS than absolute expansion because the glass container also expands. γapp​=γabs​−γglass​<γabs​.
  • ❌ The U-tube method measures absolute expansion directly. ✅ It measures APPARENT expansion (read from the glass scale). The glass expansion must be added to get the absolute expansion.
  • ❌ The hot limb has a shorter column because the liquid expands. ✅ The hot limb has a TALLER column because the liquid is LESS dense. A longer column of less-dense liquid produces the same pressure as a shorter column of denser liquid.
  • ❌ Both limbs must have the same height for equal pressure. ✅ The pressures are equal, but the heights are DIFFERENT because the densities differ. ρ0​h0​=ρ100​h100​, not h0​=h100​.
  • ❌ The method works for any temperature difference. ✅ The method is most accurate when ΔT is large (0°C to 100°C is ideal). Small ΔT gives small height differences that are hard to measure accurately.
  • ❌ The glass tube's expansion affects only the scale, not the liquid columns. ✅ The glass tube expands in all dimensions. The inner diameter increases (affecting volume readings) and the scale markings move apart (affecting height readings). Both effects are small for glass but must be corrected for precision.

Practice

  • In a Dulong-Petit experiment, h0​=60.0 cm and h100​=61.8 cm. Find γapp​ and γabs​ for the liquid. γapp​=60.0×10061.8−60.0​=60001.8​=3.0×10−4 K⁻¹. γabs​=3.0×10−4+2.7×10−5=3.27×10−4 K⁻¹. This is close to water's expansion coefficient.
  • A liquid gives hcold​=45.0 cm and hhot​=46.35 cm at ΔT=100 K. If γglass​=2.4×10−5 K⁻¹, find the absolute expansion coefficient. γapp​=45.0×10046.35−45.0​=45001.35​=3.0×10−4 K⁻¹. γabs​=3.0×10−4+0.24×10−4=3.24×10−4 K⁻¹.
  • Show that if the liquid were incompressible and had no expansion (γ=0), both limbs would have equal height. If γ=0, then ρ0​=ρ100​, so h0​=h100​ from the pressure equation. The heights are equal, confirming no expansion.
  • Why must the U-tube be uniform in cross-section? If the limbs have different diameters, the height difference still correctly reflects the density difference (pressure depends only on height, not area). However, capillary action becomes significant in narrow tubes, causing the liquid to rise or fall due to surface tension. A uniform, moderately wide tube minimizes capillary errors.
  • A student measures h0​=55.0 cm and h100​=56.1 cm. Calculate the apparent expansion coefficient. What liquid might this be? γapp​=55.0×10056.1−55.0​=55001.1​=2.0×10−4 K⁻¹. This is close to water (γ≈2.1×10−4 K⁻¹ at 20°C).
  • Explain why the Dulong-Petit method is preferred over the weight thermometer for comparing liquids. The U-tube method directly compares densities at two temperatures without needing to weigh the liquid. It is faster, less prone to spillage errors, and the height measurement is more precise than mass measurement for small expansion coefficients.

Universal Facts

  • The Dulong-Petit U-tube method is still used in teaching laboratories because it visually demonstrates the relationship between density, temperature, and pressure in a simple, elegant way.
  • Pierre Dulong and Alexis Petit also discovered that the molar heat capacity of most solid elements is approximately 25 J/(mol·K) at room temperature. This was one of the first pieces of evidence linking thermal properties to atomic structure.
  • The method works because hydrostatic pressure depends only on the height of the liquid column, not on the shape or width of the container. This is a direct application of Pascal's law.
  • Mercury has a very small expansion coefficient (γ≈182×10−6 K⁻¹), which is why it was the standard liquid in thermometers for over 200 years. Its small, regular expansion makes for a linear, predictable scale.
  • The apparent expansion of a liquid in a glass container is always less than its absolute expansion. This is why a thermometer calibrated in glass will read slightly low if the glass expands more than expected — a consideration in precision thermometry.
