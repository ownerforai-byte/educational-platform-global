Notes

  • Elementary particles: Particles with no known internal structure or substructure. They are the fundamental constituents of matter and radiation under the Standard Model of particle physics. Elementary particles fall into two classes: fermions (matter) and bosons (force carriers).
  • Fermions (spin-21​): Obey the Pauli exclusion principle. They include quarks and leptons. Quarks combine to form hadrons (baryons, mesons); leptons do not participate in the strong interaction. There are three generations of each.
  • Bosons (integer spin): Do NOT obey the exclusion principle; they can occupy the same quantum state (lasers, BEC). Force-carrying bosons: photon (gamma, EM), gluon (g, strong), W^\\pm/Z^0 (weak), and the Higgs boson (H0, scalar, spin-0).
  • Quarks: Six flavors: up (u), down (d), charm (c), strange (s), top (t), bottom (b). Charges in units of e: u,c,t have +2/3; d,s,b have −1/3. They combine via the strong force (color charge) into color-neutral bound states: baryons (qqq, e.g., proton =udd, neutron =udd) and mesons (qqˉ​, e.g., p=udˉ, K+=usˉ).
  • Leptons: Six flavors: electron (e−), muon (mu−), tau (τ−), electron neutrino (νe​), muon neutrino (\nu_\\mu), tau neutrino (ντ​). Electron and its neutrino form one generation; muon and its neutrino another; tau and its neutrino the third. Unlike quarks, leptons do not experience the strong force.
  • Antiparticles: Every elementary particle has a corresponding antiparticle with the same mass (or, for the neutrino, at most a very slightly different one) but opposite quantum numbers (charge, baryon number, lepton number, strangeness, charm, bottomness, topness). The positron e+=(e−)c has charge +e.
  • Conservation laws in particle reactions: For any reaction to occur, the following must be conserved: (1) Energy, (2) Momentum, (3) Electric charge, (4) Baryon number B (in strong/EM interactions), (5) Lepton number L (in EM/weak), (6) Strangeness, charm, bottomness, topness in strong interactions, (7) Color charge.
  • Higgs mechanism: The Higgs field permeates all space. Particles that interact more strongly with it (top quark, W/Z bosons) are heavier; those that interact weakly (electron, photon) are lighter. The Higgs boson is the quantum excitation of this field, discovered at CERN in 2012.
  • Neutrino oscillations: The 2015 Nobel Prize in Physics was awarded to Kajita and McDonald for discovering that neutrinos have mass and change flavor (oscillate) during propagation. This required extending the Standard Model beyond its minimal form.

Confusion

  • Confusion: The proton is made of three quarks (uud), but its mass is 938,MeV/c2 while the sum of quark masses is only sim10,MeV/c2. The missing mass (99%) comes from the strong-field interaction energy within the proton - mass is NOT just the sum of parts.
  • Confusion: There are only TWO stable hadrons found in nature: protons and electrons. Free neutrons decay (τ1/2​approx14.6,min). Free quarks are never found - color confinement means they only exist inside composite particles.
  • Confusion: A neutrino has almost no mass, but NOT zero mass. Oscillation experiments show Deltam2=0, so neutrinos must have mass. The absolute mass is unknown but <2,eV (normal hierarchy).
  • Confusion: Antimatter is NOT just a sci-fi plot device. Positron emission tomography (PET) uses e+e− annihilation (gamma-pair detection). Antiprotons are stored in the AMS-02 detector on the ISS to study cosmic rays.
  • Confusion: Not all particles with the same quantum numbers can annihilate. An electron and proton (both fermions, opposite charge) can form positroniumium but cannot annihilate into pure photons - charge conservation forbids e−+p→gamma+gamma, so they form Hydrogen instead.
  • Confusion: Top quark (t) and bottom quark (b) are also called truth (t) and beauty (b) in older literature. The IUPAP standard names (top/bottom) are now universally used. Charmonium (ccˉ) and bottomonium (bbˉ) are real quarkonium states.
  • Confusion: Color charge is an abstract SU(3) gauge charge analogous to electric charge, but with THREE types (+red, +green, +blue) instead of two. It's called "color" because it produces a color-force law analogous to electromagnetism - not because quarks have visible color.
  • Confusion: The Standard Model explains 3 of 4 fundamental forces (EM, strong, weak) but NOT gravity. Neutrino masses require a BSM (Beyond Standard Model) extension (e.g., Majorana masses, seesaw mechanism).

Universal Facts

  • Fact (Historical): The quark model was proposed by Murray Gell-Mann and George Zweig in 1964 to explain the "particle zoo". Gell-Mann named them "quarks" after the line "Three quarks for Muster Mark" in James Joyce's novel Finnegans Wake.
  • Fact (Application): PET scans create 64Cu or 18F radioisotopes that decay by positron (e+) emission. The emitted e+ annihilates with a tissue electron, producing two 511,keV gamma photons detected in coincidence - a medical application of antiparticle physics.
  • Fact (Exam tip): For NEB particle-physics questions, know: (1) proton =uud, neutron =udd, pion =qqˉ​; (2) mp​=938,MeV; (3) top quark is the heaviest; (4) neutrinos oscillate and have mass.
  • Fact (Connection): The total mass of ordinary (baryonic) matter in the universe is only sim5 of the cosmic energy budget. Dark matter (sim27) and dark energy (sim68) dominate - this is from the Planck satellite CMB measurements. Standard Model particles are a tiny fraction.
  • Fact (Everyday): The cosmic microwave background (CMB) radiation we observe today - relic radiation from 380,000 years after the Big Bang - tells us the early universe was a hot quark-gluon plasma where quarks and gluons moved freely before confinement set in.
  • Fact (Scale): The Planck mass mP​=sqrthbarc/Gapprox21.2,mug is the scale where quantum gravity dominates. All particles in the Standard Model have mllmP​. The hierarchy problem - why mH​llmP​ - is a major unsolved question in physics.
  • Fact (Origin): The Standard Model's 3×3 structure (three generations of quarks and leptons) is not predicted by the theory - it's an experimental input. Why three generations? Each generation mirrors the first with increasing mass: (u,d,s,c,b,t) and (e, \nu_e, \\mu, \nu_\\mu, \tau, \nu_\tau).
