Confusion

  • Temperature and heat are NOT the same. Temperature is an intensive property (per unit mass); heat is energy transferred (extensive). A bathtub of 30Â°C water has more heat than a cup of 80Â°C water, but lower temperature.

  • Kelvin is NOT written as 'Â°K' - it is simply 'K' (e.g., 300 K, not 300Â°K). There is no 'degree Kelvin.' The word 'kelvin' is not capitalized when spelled out.
  • Absolute zero is NOT just 'really cold' - it is the state where classical thermal motion ceases entirely. At 0 K, only quantum zero-point energy remains, and all real substances have condensed to their ground states.
  • The triple point of water (273.16 K) and the freezing point of water (273.15 K) differ by exactly 0.01 K. Confusing these is a common source of error in metrology.

Universal Facts

  • Temperature is a measure of the average kinetic energy of particles: ⟨KE⟩=(3/2)kT, where k=1.38×10−23J/K is Boltzmann's constant. This is why temperature must be in Kelvin - kinetic energy cannot be negative.
  • There is no upper limit to temperature - particle speeds can increase without bound. The Planck temperature (1.417×1032K) is a theoretical limit where quantum gravity effects become important, not a physical maximum.
  • The Kelvin scale was originally defined by the triple point of water (273.16 K) and absolute zero (0 K). Since 2019, it is defined by fixing the Boltzmann constant k to exactly 1.380649×10−23J/K.
  • Different thermometric substances give slightly different scales because their expansion is not perfectly linear with temperature. Gas thermometers are most accurate because ideal gases have perfectly linear behavior in the limit of zero pressure.
  • The Rankine scale (Â°R) is to Fahrenheit as Kelvin is to Celsius - it is the absolute temperature scale. NASA uses Rankine in some engineering calculations alongside SI.
