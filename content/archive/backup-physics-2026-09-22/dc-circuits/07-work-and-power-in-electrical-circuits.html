Notes

  • Electrical work: W=VIt=I2Rt=V2t/R. Work done by a current is the energy transferred.
  • Electrical power: P=VI=I2R=V2/R. Power is the rate of energy transfer.
  • Joule heating: P=I2R is the power dissipated as heat in a resistor. This is also called I2R loss.
  • Kilowatt-hour: unit of electrical energy. 1, extkWh=3.6 imes106, extJ. This is what electricity meters measure.
  • Efficiency: useful power output divided by total power input. Transmission lines use high voltage to reduce I2R losses because Ploss​proptoI2.
  • Power in different elements: in a resistor, all electrical energy is converted to heat: P=I2R. In a motor, electrical energy converts to mechanical work plus heat: Pinput​=VI=I2R+Pmech​. In a battery being charged, electrical energy is stored as chemical energy: Pinput​=mathcalEI+I2r.
  • Energy conservation in circuits: the total electrical energy supplied equals the sum of energy dissipated as heat and energy converted to other forms: Vsource​cdotIcdott=I2Rt+ extotherforms.
  • Power rating of resistors: resistors are rated by the maximum power they can dissipate without damage. Common ratings are 0.25 W, 0.5 W, and 1 W. Exceeding this causes overheating and failure.
  • Domestic electrical cost: the cost of running an appliance is  extCost=P( extkW) imest( exth) imes extrate(perkWh). A 100 W bulb running for 5 hours uses 0.5 kWh.
  • Power factor: for AC circuits, P=VIcosphi, where cosphi is the power factor. For DC circuits, cosphi=1, so P=VI always.
  • Heat energy in calories: H=0.24I2Rt calories (mechanical equivalent of heat). In SI units, H=I2Rt joules.

Confusion

  • Power P=I2R and P=V2/R are both correct but apply in different contexts. Use I2R when current is known (series circuits). Use V2/R when voltage is known (parallel circuits). Using the wrong one gives a wrong answer because V and I are not independent.
  • Electrical energy is measured in joules (J) or kilowatt-hours (kWh), NOT in watts (W). Watt is a unit of power (energy per time). 1, extkWh=3.6 imes106 J.
  • A 100 W bulb connected to 230 V draws less current than a 60 W bulb (because I=P/V, so the 100 W bulb has I=0.43 A vs I=0.26 A for the 60 W bulb). The 100 W bulb actually has LOWER resistance.
  • In a series circuit, the higher-wattage bulb glows dimmer because it has lower resistance, and in series the same current flows through both: P=I2R, so the higher resistance (60 W bulb) gets more power.
  • Power dissipated in internal resistance (I2r) is wasted energy. This is why battery-powered devices get warm - the internal resistance converts some chemical energy to heat instead of useful work.

Practice

  • A 60 W, 230 V bulb is connected to a 230 V supply. Find the current, resistance, and energy consumed in 5 hours. Solution: I=P/V=60/230=0.261 A. R=V/I=230/0.261=881,Omega. W=Pt=60 imes5=300, extWh=0.3, extkWh=1.08 imes106 J.
  • A 12 V car headlamp draws 5 A. Find the power, resistance, and cost of running it for 4 hours at 8 pence per kWh. Solution: P=VI=12 imes5=60 W. R=V/I=12/5=2.4,Omega. Energy =60 imes4/1000=0.24, extkWh. Cost =0.24 imes8=1.92 pence.
  • A 100 Omega resistor carries a current of 2 A for 5 minutes. Find the heat produced in joules and calories. Solution: H=I2Rt=4 imes100 imes300=120000 J. In calories: H=120000/4.184=28681 cal approx28.7 kcal.
  • A transmission line carries 100 A at 11 kV over a total resistance of 5 Omega. Find the power loss and the percentage of power lost. Solution: Ploss​=I2R=10000 imes5=50000 W =50 kW. Power transmitted =VI=11000 imes100=1.1 imes106 W =1100 kW. Percentage loss =50/1100=4.5.

Universal Facts

  • A 100 W incandescent bulb is only about 5% efficient - most electrical energy is converted to heat rather than visible light. LEDs are about 15-20% efficient but produce far more lumens per watt.
  • Electric transmission lines carry power at very high voltages (up to 765 kV) to minimize I2R losses. Halving the voltage doubles the current and quadruples the power loss, which is why high-voltage transmission is essential.
  • The unit of electrical energy consumption, the kilowatt-hour, is not an SI unit. The SI unit of energy is the joule: 1, extkWh=3.6 imes106 J.
  • A typical household circuit breaker is rated for 15-20 A at 120-240 V, giving a maximum power of 1.8-4.8 kW. Connecting too many high-power appliances can trip the breaker or cause a fire.
  • The heating effect of current was discovered by James Prescott Joule in 1841. His law H=I2Rt is a fundamental result of energy conservation in electrical circuits.
