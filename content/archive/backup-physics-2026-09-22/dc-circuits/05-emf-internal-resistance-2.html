EMF and Internal Resistance

Backup Physics 2026 09 22

Relevance

Notes

  • EMF (mathcalE): the open-circuit voltage of a source. It is the work done per unit charge in moving charge around the complete circuit.
  • Internal resistance r: the resistance inside the cell due to electrolyte and electrodes. It causes the terminal voltage to drop when current flows.
  • Terminal voltage: V=mathcalE−Ir. When no current flows (I=0), V=mathcalE. Under load, V<mathcalE.
  • Cells in series: mathcalEtotal​=mathcalE1​+mathcalE2​+ldots, rtotal​=r1​+r2​+ldots. Series adds both EMF and internal resistance.
  • Cells in parallel: mathcalEtotal​=mathcalE (if all equal), rtotal​=r/n for n identical cells. Parallel reduces internal resistance, allowing higher current delivery.
  • Maximum power transfer: the power delivered to an external load R is maximum when R=r. The maximum power is Pmax​=mathcalE2/(4r).
  • Current in a simple circuit: I=mathcalE/(R+r), where R is the external resistance and r is the internal resistance.
  • Efficiency: the efficiency of power transfer is eta=R/(R+r). Maximum efficiency approaches 100% when Rggr, but maximum power requires R=r.
  • Short circuit current: when R=0, Isc​=mathcalE/r. This can be very large and dangerous, causing heating and damage.
  • Cells with different EMFs: when cells with different EMFs are connected in parallel, circulating currents flow between them. This can damage cells and should be avoided.
  • Real cell behavior: as a cell discharges, its EMF slowly decreases and its internal resistance increases. An old battery reads lower than its rated voltage even when not loaded.

Confusion

  • EMF is not a force - it is a voltage (energy per unit charge). The name is historical. EMF is measured in volts, not newtons.
  • Terminal voltage V=mathcalE−Ir is less than EMF when current flows out of the cell. But when charging the cell, current flows into the positive terminal, so V=mathcalE+Ir - the terminal voltage is greater than EMF.
  • In series, both EMF and internal resistance add. In parallel, the equivalent EMF is the same as one cell, but the equivalent internal resistance is reduced, allowing more current.
  • Maximum power transfer (R=r) occurs at 50% efficiency. For efficient power delivery, you want Rggr (high efficiency, low power). For maximum power, you sacrifice efficiency.
  • A fresh battery has a high open-circuit voltage (close to its EMF) but drops significantly under load if its internal resistance has increased with age. That is why old batteries seem weak.

Practice

  • A cell with EMF 1.5 V and internal resistance 0.5 Omega is connected to a 7 Omega resistor. Find the current, terminal voltage, and power delivered to the resistor. Solution: I=1.5/(7+0.5)=0.2 A. V=1.5−0.2 imes0.5=1.4 V. P=I2R=0.04 imes7=0.28 W.
  • A cell gives 2.0 V when no current flows and 1.8 V when delivering 1 A. Find the EMF and internal resistance. Solution: EMF mathcalE=2.0 V (open circuit). Under load: 1.8=2.0−1 imesr, so r=0.2,Omega.
  • Three identical cells of EMF 1.5 V and internal resistance 0.3 Omega each are connected in series across a 6 Omega load. Find the current. Solution: mathcalEtotal​=4.5 V, rtotal​=0.9,Omega. I=4.5/(6+0.9)=0.652 A.
  • The same three cells above are connected in parallel across the same 6 Omega load. Find the current. Solution: mathcalEtotal​=1.5 V, rtotal​=0.3/3=0.1,Omega. I=1.5/(6+0.1)=0.246 A.
  • Find the external resistance for maximum power transfer to a cell with EMF 12 V and internal resistance 2 Omega. Find the maximum power. Solution: R=r=2,Omega. I=12/(2+2)=3 A. Pmax​=I2R=9 imes2=18 W. Alternatively Pmax​=mathcalE2/(4r)=144/8=18 W.

Universal Facts

  • The internal resistance of a typical AA alkaline cell is about 0.15 Omega when fresh, but increases to 0.5 Omega or more as it discharges.
  • Connecting cells in series increases the available voltage while keeping the same current capacity. Connecting in parallel increases the current capacity while keeping the same voltage.
  • A short-circuited 12 V car battery with internal resistance 0.01 Omega can deliver 1200 A, which is why it can melt tools and cause severe burns.
  • The maximum power transfer theorem (R=r) is a fundamental design principle for matching loads to sources, but it is inefficient for power distribution systems where Rggr is desired.
  • In a battery pack, series strings are often used to increase voltage, and parallel strings within each series group are used to increase capacity and reduce internal resistance.
