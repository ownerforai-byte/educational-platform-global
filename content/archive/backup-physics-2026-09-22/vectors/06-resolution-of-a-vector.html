Resolution of a Vector

Notes

  • Definition: the process of splitting (breaking) a vector into its constituent parts is known as resolution of a vector. Each constituent is called a component of the vector along the chosen direction.
  • Rectangular (perpendicular) resolution: when a vector is resolved along two mutually perpendicular directions (typically x and y axes), the components are Rx​=Rcosθ and Ry​=Rsinθ, where θ is the angle the vector makes with the x-axis.
  • Inverse operation of addition: resolution is the reverse of vector addition. If two perpendicular components Rx​ and Ry​ add to give R, then resolving R recovers these components.
  • Derivation setup: let R act at origin O making angle θ with the x-axis. Construct parallelogram OACB where OA lies on the x-axis and OC lies on the y-axis. The diagonal OB represents R.
  • By the parallelogram law: OA+OC=OB, i.e., Rx​+Ry​=R. This means R is completely determined by its two perpendicular components.
  • Deriving the components: in right triangle OAB, cosθ=OA/OB=Rx​/R, so Rx​=Rcosθ. Similarly, sinθ=OC/OB=Ry​/R, so Ry​=Rsinθ.
  • Combining components: R=Rx2​+Ry2​​ (magnitude) and tanθ=Ry​/Rx​ (direction). The original vector is R=Rx​i^+Ry​j^​ in unit vector notation.
  • Sign convention: Rx​ is positive if Rx​ is along +x direction, negative if along −x. Same for Ry​. This sign convention allows vectors in any quadrant to be handled systematically.
  • Vector form: R=Rx​i^+Ry​j^​+Rz​k^ for a 3D vector, where Rx​=Rcosα, Ry​=Rcosβ, Rz​=Rcosγ and α,β,γ are the angles with x, y, z axes.
  • Direction cosines: for 3D vectors, cosα, cosβ, cosγ are called direction cosines, and they satisfy cos2α+cos2β+cos2γ=1.
  • Why resolution is useful: a single vector makes solving equilibrium problems difficult. Resolving into components turns a vector problem into multiple scalar problems, which are easier to solve.
  • Resolution in any two directions: a vector can be resolved along ANY two non-parallel directions, not just perpendicular ones. Rectangular resolution is the most common because it gives independent components.
  • Resolution and addition are inverse operations: Rx​+Ry​=R (addition) and R=Rx​+Ry​ (resolution). The same equation, viewed in two directions.
  • Applications: inclined plane problems (gravity resolved into parallel and perpendicular components), projectile motion (initial velocity into horizontal and vertical), forces at angles (along and perpendicular to surfaces).
  • On an inclined plane at angle ϕ: weight mg is resolved into mgsinϕ parallel to plane (causing sliding) and mgcosϕ perpendicular to plane (causing normal reaction). This is a classic NEB application.
  • For a vector along an arbitrary direction u^: the component of A along u^ is Au​=A⋅u^=∣A∣cosα, where α is the angle between A and u^.
  • Resolution in 3D: R=Rx​i^+Ry​j^​+Rz​k^ where Rx​=Rcosα, Ry​=Rcosβ, Rz​=Rcosγ, and α,β,γ are angles with x, y, z axes respectively.
  • Equivalence with triangle law: the resolution of R into Rx​ and Ry​ is essentially constructing a right triangle with hypotenuse R and legs Rx​,Ry​. The Pythagorean theorem gives R=Rx2​+Ry2​​.
  • Inverse problem: given components Rx​ and Ry​, the original vector is R=Rx​i^+Ry​j^​ with R=Rx2​+Ry2​​ and θ=tan−1(Ry​/Rx​).
  • The angle in the formula θ must be measured from the axis the component is along. For Rx​=Rcosθ, θ is measured from the x-axis. The component along any other axis at angle ϕ is Rcos(θ−ϕ).
  • Oblique resolution: a vector can be resolved along ANY two non-parallel directions, not just perpendicular ones. If resolved along directions making angles α and β with the vector, the components are found using the sine rule: sin(180°−(α+β))R​=sinβR1​​=sinαR2​​.
  • Resolution along a non-perpendicular axis: the component of R along a direction making angle ϕ with the x-axis is Rcos(θ−ϕ), where θ is the angle of R with the x-axis.
  • Projection vs component: the projection of A on B is the scalar A⋅B^=Acosα. The component of A along B is the vector (A⋅B^)B^. The projection is the magnitude (with sign); the component is the full vector.
  • Resolution in physics problems: in projectile motion, v0​ is resolved into v0x​=v0​cosθ and v0y​=v0​sinθ. In inclined planes, weight is resolved into mgsinθ (parallel) and mgcosθ (perpendicular). The choice of axes determines the complexity of the problem.

Confusion

  • Resolution is the same as addition — Resolution BREAKS a vector INTO components; addition COMBINES components INTO a vector. They are inverse operations. Rx​+Ry​=R is addition; given R, find Rx​,Ry​ is resolution.
  • Components can be in any two directions — Components can be in ANY two non-parallel directions, but rectangular (perpendicular) resolution is most common because the components are independent. Oblique components can be converted to rectangular using transformation formulas.
  • The angle in Rx​=Rcosθ is the angle between R and Ry​ — No, it is the angle between R and the x-axis (the direction of Rx​). The component Ry​=Rsinθ uses the same θ because θx​+θy​=90° for perpendicular components.
  • A larger component means a larger vector — A larger component does not mean a larger vector if the components are along different directions. R=Rx2​+Ry2​​ is the actual magnitude, considering both components.
  • Resolution gives the magnitudes of sub-vectors only — Resolution gives the FULL components as vectors: Rx​ (with direction) and Ry​ (with direction). Magnitudes alone are Rx​ and Ry​, but each is along a specific axis.
  • The angle in the formulas must be from the x-axis always — The angle can be measured from any axis, but you must use the correct trigonometric function: Rx​=Rcosθ if θ is from x-axis, Ry​=Rsinθ. If θ is from y-axis, swap the roles.
  • Resolution into more than two components is impossible — A vector can be resolved into any number of components, as long as their vector sum equals the original. In 3D, we typically use three perpendicular components.
  • You can resolve a vector into components without specifying axes — To resolve a vector, you must specify the directions of resolution. Without axes, there are infinitely many possible resolutions.
  • Resolution changes the physical meaning of the vector — No. Resolution gives an EQUIVALENT description. The vector's physical effect is unchanged; we've just broken it into parts that are easier to work with.
  • A vector can have more than one set of components — Yes! Any vector has infinitely many component representations depending on the choice of axes. However, the rectangular (perpendicular) components are unique for a given choice of axes.

Practice

  • A force of 100 N acts at 30° to the horizontal. Find its horizontal and vertical components. [Solution: Fx​=100cos30°=100⋅(0.866)=86.6N. Fy​=100sin30°=100⋅(0.5)=50N.]
  • A block of 10 kg rests on a smooth inclined plane at 30° to the horizontal. Find the components of weight along and perpendicular to the plane. [Solution: W=mg=10⋅9.8=98N. Along plane: Wsin30°=98⋅0.5=49N (down the slope). Perpendicular: Wcos30°=98⋅0.866=84.87N (into the plane).]
  • Resolve a vector of magnitude 20 N along two perpendicular axes such that the component along one axis is 12 N. Find the other component and the angle. [Solution: R2=Rx2​+Ry2​, so Ry​=202−122​=400−144​=256​=16N. θ=cos−1(12/20)=53.1°.]
  • A body moves with velocity 50 m/s at 60° north of east. Find its eastward and northward components. [Solution: vx​=50cos60°=25m/s (east). vy​=50sin60°=50⋅0.866=43.3m/s (north).]
  • If A=4i^+3j^​ and B=5i^−12j^​, find the resultant. [Solution: R=A+B=9i^−9j^​. R=81+81​=162​=92​≈12.73. α=tan−1(−9/9)=−45° (below x-axis).]
  • Show that the resultant of two perpendicular vectors of magnitudes 5 and 12 is 13, and find the angle it makes with the 12-unit vector. [Solution: R=25+144​=169​=13. θ=tan−1(5/12)=22.6° from the 12-unit vector. By the 5-12-13 right triangle, this is the famous 3-4-5 triple scaled up.]
  • A force has components Fx​=−6N and Fy​=8N. Find the magnitude of the force and the angle it makes with the positive x-axis. [Solution: F=36+64​=10N. θ=tan−1(8/(−6))=180°−53.1°=126.9° (second quadrant).]
  • A vector of magnitude 10 units makes equal angles with the x and y axes. Find its components. [Solution: If θx​=θy​=α, then since the components are along the axes, the angle between them is 90°. The vector is along the angle bisector, so α=45°. Components: Rx​=Ry​=10cos45°=10/2​=52​≈7.07.]
  • A force of 50 N is resolved into two components along directions making angles of 30° and 45° with the force. Find the magnitudes of the components. [Solution: Using the sine rule in the resolution triangle: sin(180°−30°−45°)50​=sin45°F1​​=sin30°F2​​. The angle between the components is 180°−30°−45°=105°. F1​=50sin45°/sin105°=50(0.7071)/0.9659=36.6N. F2​=50sin30°/sin105°=50(0.5)/0.9659=25.9N.]
  • A particle is acted upon by three forces: 10 N along +x, 15 N at 60° from +x, and 20 N at 150° from +x. Find the resultant by resolution. [Solution: F1x​=10, F1y​=0. F2x​=15cos60°=7.5, F2y​=15sin60°=12.99. F3x​=20cos150°=−17.32, F3y​=20sin150°=10. Rx​=10+7.5−17.32=0.18, Ry​=0+12.99+10=22.99. R=0.03+528.54​=528.57​≈23.0N. α=tan−1(22.99/0.18)≈89.6° from +x.]

Universal Facts

  • Resolution of vectors is the inverse operation of vector addition. Addition combines two or more vectors into a single resultant; resolution decomposes a single vector into two or more components.
  • NEB exam frequency: resolution problems appear in nearly every physics paper, especially in inclined plane mechanics, projectile motion, and statics. Mastery of the formulas Rx​=Rcosθ and Ry​=Rsinθ is essential.
  • In structural engineering, forces in trusses are resolved into components along the truss members to determine tensions and compressions. The resolution allows solving for multiple unknowns using equilibrium equations.
  • The choice of coordinate axes in resolution is a free choice that affects the simplicity of the solution. For inclined plane problems, axes along and perpendicular to the plane minimize the work.
  • Galileo's inclined plane experiments for studying falling bodies relied on resolution. He resolved gravity into components along and perpendicular to the plane, making the analysis tractable.
  • Resolution of vectors in 2D uses two components; in 3D, three. The number of components equals the dimension of the space — but for any practical purpose, three components suffice for our world.
  • The result of resolving a vector into components depends on the choice of axes. Different axes give different components, but the resultant is the same. Always choose axes that simplify the problem.
  • In computer graphics and game development, every 3D object is manipulated using resolved vector components along x, y, z axes. This allows efficient calculations for rotation, translation, and lighting.
  • The resolution of weight on an inclined plane is one of the most important applications in Class 11 physics. The component mgsinθ causes acceleration down the plane; mgcosθ is balanced by the normal reaction.
  • In statics, resolution of forces into components leads to the two equilibrium equations: ∑Fx​=0 and ∑Fy​=0. These are the algebraic form of the polygon law for coplanar forces.
