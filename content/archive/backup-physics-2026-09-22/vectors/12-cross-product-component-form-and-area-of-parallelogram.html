Notes

  • Component form of cross product: For A=A1​i^+A2​j^​+A3​k^ and B=B1​i^+B2​j^​+B3​k^: A×B=​i^​j^​​k^ A1​​A2​​A3​ B1​​B2​​B3​​​.
  • Expanded form: A×B=(A2​B3​−A3​B2​)i^+(A3​B1​−A1​B3​)j^​+(A1​B2​−A2​B1​)k^. Note the cyclic pattern and the negative sign on the j^​ component.
  • Memory trick (Sarrus' rule): Write the first two columns again to the right: A1​​A2​​A3​​A1​​A2​ B1​​B2​​B3​​B1​​B2​​. The i^ component comes from the diagonal A2​B3​−A3​B2​. The j^​ component uses the middle diagonal with a minus sign: −(A1​B3​−A3​B1​). The k^ component uses A1​B2​−A2​B1​.
  • Area of parallelogram: The magnitude of the cross product equals the AREA of the parallelogram formed by the two vectors: Area=∣A×B∣=ABsinθ. This is because the base is A and the height is Bsinθ.
  • Area of triangle: The area of the triangle formed by two vectors is HALF the parallelogram area: Area△​=21​∣A×B∣. This is because the triangle is exactly half of the parallelogram.
  • Worked example: Find A×B for A=2i^+j^​−3k^ and B=i^−2j^​+4k^. A×B=i^(4−6)−j^​(8+3)+k^(−4−1)=−2i^−11j^​−5k^.
  • Area calculation example: For A=i^+2j^​+3k^ and B=2i^−3j^​+4k^: A×B=i^(8+9)−j^​(4−6)+k^(−3−4)=17i^+2j^​−7k^. Area of parallelogram =289+4+49​=342​≈18.5 sq units. Area of triangle =342​/2≈9.25 sq units.
  • Perpendicularity property: The cross product A×B is perpendicular to BOTH A and B. This can be verified: A⋅(A×B)=0 and B⋅(A×B)=0. This property is used to find normal vectors to planes.
  • Normal to a plane: If two non-parallel vectors A and B lie in a plane, then A×B is normal (perpendicular) to that plane. This is used to find the equation of a plane: n⋅(r−r0​​)=0, where n=A×B.
  • NEB exam focus: Compute cross products using the determinant method. Calculate the area of parallelograms and triangles using cross products. Find normal vectors to planes. Verify perpendicularity using dot products.
  • Physical application — torque: Torque τ=r×F. The magnitude τ=rFsinθ gives the turning effect. The direction (along the axis of rotation) follows the right-hand rule. A wrench applying force perpendicular to its handle (θ=90°) gives maximum torque.
  • Physical application — magnetic force: The magnetic force on a moving charge is F=qv×B. The force is perpendicular to both velocity and magnetic field. This causes charged particles to move in circular or helical paths in magnetic fields.
  • Physical application — angular momentum: L=r×p​=m(r×v). Angular momentum is conserved in the absence of external torque. This conservation explains why ice skaters spin faster when they pull their arms in.
  • Physical application — linear velocity in rotation: For a rigid body rotating with angular velocity ω, the linear velocity of a point at position r is v=ω×r. This relates rotational motion to translational motion.
  • Cross product in 2D: In 2D, the cross product of A=Ax​i^+Ay​j^​ and B=Bx​i^+By​j^​ gives A×B=(Ax​By​−Ay​Bx​)k^. The scalar Ax​By​−Ay​Bx​ is called the 2D "perp dot product" and equals the signed area of the parallelogram.

Confusion

  • ❌ The cross product of two vectors is a scalar. ✅ The cross product is a VECTOR. Its magnitude is a scalar (the area), but the result itself is a vector perpendicular to both input vectors.
  • ❌ A×B=B×A. ✅ The cross product is anti-commutative: A×B=−(B×A). The two results have the same magnitude but opposite directions.
  • ❌ The area of the parallelogram is ∣A∣⋅∣B∣. ✅ The area is ∣A×B∣=ABsinθ. The dot product A⋅B=ABcosθ gives a different quantity (projection-related, not area).
  • ❌ You can cancel vectors in a cross product: if A×B=A×C, then B=C. ✅ You CANNOT cancel. A×(B−C)=0 means B−C is parallel to A, not necessarily zero. B and C could be different vectors both parallel to A.
  • ❌ The j^​ component of the cross product has a positive sign. ✅ The j^​ component has a NEGATIVE sign: −(A1​B3​−A3​B1​)=A3​B1​−A1​B3​. This is a common source of sign errors.
  • ❌ The cross product is associative: (A×B)×C=A×(B×C). ✅ The cross product is NOT associative. The two expressions give different results. Always use parentheses to specify the order.
  • ❌ If ∣A×B∣=0, then both vectors must be zero. ✅ If the cross product magnitude is zero, the vectors are PARALLEL (or one is zero). Non-zero parallel vectors have zero cross product.

Universal Facts

  • The determinant form of the cross product is a mnemonic device — it is not a true determinant (since the entries include vectors). It is a convenient shorthand for remembering the component formula.
  • The cross product's geometric interpretation as an area is used extensively in computer graphics for computing surface normals, which determine how light interacts with 3D objects.
  • In electrical engineering, the cross product appears in the Lorentz force law F=q(E+v×B), which describes the force on a charged particle in electromagnetic fields.
  • The vector triple product expansion A×(B×C)=(A⋅C)B−(A⋅B)C (BAC-CAB rule) is essential in proving that the cross product is not associative.
  • The cross product was formalized by Josiah Willard Gibbs in his 1881 notes on vector analysis. Before this, quaternions (developed by William Rowan Hamilton) were used, but Gibbs's vector approach proved far more practical for physics applications.
