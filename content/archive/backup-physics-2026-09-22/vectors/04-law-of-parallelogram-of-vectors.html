Law of Parallelogram of Vectors

Notes

  • Statement: If two vectors acting at a point are represented in magnitude and direction by the two adjacent sides of a parallelogram drawn from that point, their resultant is represented in magnitude and direction by the diagonal of the parallelogram passing through the same point.
  • Setup: let P and Q​ be two vectors acting at point A, represented by adjacent sides AB and AD of parallelogram ABCD. The diagonal AC represents the resultant R. The angle between P and Q​ is θ.
  • Derivation of magnitude: draw CE perpendicular to AB extended. In right triangle BCE: CE=Qsinθ and BE=Qcosθ. Then AE=AB+BE=P+Qcosθ.
  • In right triangle ACE: AC2=AE2+CE2=(P+Qcosθ)2+(Qsinθ)2=P2+2PQcosθ+Q2cos2θ+Q2sin2θ.
  • Simplifying with sin2θ+cos2θ=1: R2=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ, so R=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ​.
  • Derivation of direction: in triangle ACE, tanα=AECE​=P+QcosθQsinθ​, giving α=tan−1(P+QcosθQsinθ​), where α is the angle between R and P.
  • Special case θ=0°: R=P2+Q2+2PQ​=P+Q, α=0°. Maximum resultant — vectors reinforce each other completely.
  • Special case θ=90°: R=P2+Q2​, α=tan−1(Q/P). The Pythagorean result — the most frequently tested case.
  • Special case θ=180°: R=P2+Q2−2PQ​=∣P−Q∣, α=0° or 180°. Minimum resultant — vectors oppose each other.
  • Special case P=Q: R=2Pcos(θ/2) and α=θ/2. The resultant bisects the angle between two equal vectors. This is a key result for equilibrium problems.
  • Special case P=Q and R=P: this occurs when θ=120°, since R=2Pcos(60°)=2P(1/2)=P. Three equal forces at 120° to each other are in equilibrium.
  • Equivalence to triangle law: the parallelogram and triangle laws are mathematically equivalent. The diagonal AC of the parallelogram is also the third side of triangle ABC, confirming both methods yield identical results.
  • Diagonals of a parallelogram: the two diagonals represent P+Q​ and P−Q​. Their magnitudes satisfy: ∣P+Q​∣2+∣P−Q​∣2=2(P2+Q2) — this is the parallelogram law of geometry.
  • The parallelogram law is the foundation of statics — the branch of mechanics dealing with forces in equilibrium. Every force analysis in engineering begins with constructing a parallelogram of forces.
  • Graphical method: to find the resultant graphically, draw the two vectors to scale from a common point, complete the parallelogram, and measure the diagonal. This is how structural engineers historically solved problems before calculators.
  • The parallelogram law applies to all vector quantities equally: forces, velocities, displacements, accelerations, electric fields, magnetic fields, and momenta. The mathematical form is universal.
  • Verification experiment: suspend two spring balances from a ring, attach a third balance downward. The readings on the two upper balances (vectors P and Q​) and the lower balance (weight W) should satisfy the parallelogram law with R=−W.
  • In the parallelogram construction, the angle θ must be measured between the vectors when they are tail-to-tail (at the common origin point), not the interior angle of the parallelogram.
  • The parallelogram law was formalized by Isaac Newton in his Principia (1687) as the 'parallelogram of forces', making it one of the foundational principles of classical mechanics.
  • If P⊥Q​, the parallelogram becomes a rectangle, and the diagonal is easily found using Pythagoras: R=P2+Q2​. This simplification is heavily tested.
  • Parallelogram law with unequal sides: when P=Q, the diagonal does not bisect the angle. The angle α from P is α=tan−1(P+QcosθQsinθ​), which equals θ/2 only when P=Q.
  • The second diagonal: the diagonal from B to D (the other diagonal) represents P−Q​ (or Q​−P depending on orientation). Its magnitude is d2​=P2+Q2−2PQcosθ​.
  • Sum of squares of diagonals: d12​+d22​=(P2+Q2+2PQcosθ)+(P2+Q2−2PQcosθ)=2(P2+Q2). This identity is useful in many geometry and physics problems.
  • When the resultant equals one component: if R=P, then P2=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ, giving cosθ=−Q/(2P). This requires Q≤2P. A special case: if P=Q and R=P, then θ=120°.
  • Graphical precision: the graphical method is limited by drawing accuracy. For NEB exams, analytical methods using the formula are preferred. Graphical methods are for verification and intuition only.

Confusion

  • The parallelogram law gives a different result from the triangle law — They give identical results. The triangle is simply half the parallelogram. The diagonal of the parallelogram is the third side of the triangle.
  • The angle in the formula is the interior angle of the parallelogram — The angle θ in R=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ​ is the angle between the two vectors when placed tail-to-tail, which equals the interior angle at the common vertex.
  • Only forces obey the parallelogram law — All vectors obey it: velocity, displacement, acceleration, electric field, magnetic field, momentum. Any quantity with magnitude and direction that follows vector addition rules obeys this law.
  • The resultant is always the longer diagonal — No. The resultant is specifically the diagonal drawn from the common origin point (where the two vectors start). The other diagonal represents P−Q​.
  • If θ=90°, the parallelogram becomes a square — It becomes a rectangle, not necessarily a square. It is a square only if P=Q as well.
  • The direction formula changes between the two laws — The direction formula α=tan−1(Qsinθ/(P+Qcosθ)) is identical for both the triangle and parallelogram laws, since they are geometrically equivalent.
  • You need to draw a full parallelogram to use the law — No. You can use just the triangle (half the parallelogram) for calculations. The full parallelogram is helpful for visualization but not required for computation.
  • The parallelogram law and triangle law are different — They are mathematically identical. The triangle is exactly half the parallelogram. Both give the same R and α.
  • The parallelogram law fails for three or more vectors — It extends naturally: for three vectors, first find R12​=P+Q​ using the parallelogram, then combine R12​ with R using the law again. Or use the polygon law for 3+ vectors directly.

Practice

  • Two forces of 5 N and 12 N act at a point with an angle of 90° between them. Find the resultant using the parallelogram law. [Solution: R=25+144​=169​=13N. α=tan−1(12/5)≈67.4° from the 5 N force.]
  • Two forces of 10 N and 8 N act at a point with an angle of 60° between them. Find the resultant and its direction. [Solution: R=100+64+160cos60°​=164+80​=244​≈15.62N. α=tan−1(8sin60°/(10+8cos60°))=tan−1(6.928/14)≈26.3°.]
  • Show that if two equal forces each of magnitude F have a resultant of magnitude F, then the angle between them is 120°. [Solution: F=F2+F2+2F2cosθ​=F2+2cosθ​. So 1=2+2cosθ​, giving 1=2+2cosθ, hence cosθ=−1/2 and θ=120°.]
  • Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at a point. What angle between them gives a resultant of 5 N? [Solution: 25=9+16+24cosθ, so 25=25+24cosθ, giving cosθ=0 and θ=90°. The forces are perpendicular.]
  • Find the resultant of two vectors A=6i^+8j^​ and B=3i^−4j^​ using component method. [Solution: R=9i^+4j^​. R=81+16​=97​≈9.85. α=tan−1(4/9)≈24.0°.]
  • Two forces P and Q​ have a resultant of 26 N. If Q​ is doubled, the resultant becomes 54 N. Find P and Q. [Solution: P2+Q2+2PQcosθ=676 and P2+4Q2+4PQcosθ=2916. Subtracting: 3Q2+2PQcosθ=2240. This requires additional info; assume θ=90°: then P2+Q2=676 and P2+4Q2=2916. Subtracting: 3Q2=2240, Q2=746.67, Q≈27.3. Then P2=676−746.67<0 — inconsistent. Try solving simultaneously with cosθ: need third equation.]
  • Prove that the sum of the squares of the diagonals of a parallelogram equals the sum of the squares of its four sides. [Solution: Diagonals: d12​=P2+Q2+2PQcosθ, d22​=P2+Q2−2PQcosθ. Sum: d12​+d22​=2P2+2Q2=2(P2+Q2). Four sides: 2P2+2Q2. Hence d12​+d22​=2P2+2Q2. Proven.]
  • Two forces act at a point: one is twice the other. If the resultant is perpendicular to the smaller force, find the angle between them. [Solution: Let forces be F and 2F. Resultant R⊥F means α=90°. From tanα=F+2Fcosθ2Fsinθ​=∞, the denominator must be zero: F+2Fcosθ=0, so cosθ=−1/2, giving θ=120°.]
  • Two forces of 10 N and 15 N act at a point with an angle of 90° between them. Find the resultant and the angle it makes with the 15 N force. [Solution: R=100+225​=325​≈18.03N. α=tan−1(10/15)=tan−1(2/3)≈33.7° from the 15 N force.]
  • Two vectors A and B with ∣A∣=6, ∣B∣=8, and angle 60° between them. Find the magnitude of their resultant and the angle it makes with A. [Solution: R=36+64+96cos60°​=100+48​=148​≈12.17. α=tan−1(8sin60°/(6+8cos60°))=tan−1(6.928/10)≈34.7°.]

Universal Facts

  • Newton's second law of motion (Fnet​=ma) implicitly relies on the parallelogram law: the net force is the vector sum of all individual forces, found by successive application of the parallelogram law.
  • The parallelogram identity (∣A+B∣2+∣A−B∣2=2∣A∣2+2∣B∣2) is a fundamental property of inner product spaces and holds in any number of dimensions, not just 2D.
  • NEB exam frequency: parallelogram law numerical problems appear in almost every year's physics paper, typically 3-5 marks. The 90° special case (Pythagorean) is the most common variant.
  • The parallelogram law of forces was experimentally verified by Pierre Varignon (1654-1722), who showed that the moment of a resultant force equals the sum of the moments of its components — a direct consequence of the parallelogram law.
  • In aerodynamics, the parallelogram law explains how wind affects an aircraft's ground track. The airspeed vector plus the wind vector gives the ground velocity vector (the resultant).
  • The term 'resultant' comes from the Latin resultare meaning 'to leap back' or 'to result'. In physics, it specifically means the single vector that has the same effect as all the original vectors combined.
  • Engineering structures like bridge trusses are analyzed using the parallelogram law at each joint. Every joint must be in equilibrium, so the force vectors at each joint form closed polygons.
  • When θ=120° and P=Q, the resultant equals each component. This is why tripods are stable: the three leg forces are typically arranged at approximately 120° to each other, giving balanced resultant forces.
  • The parallelogram law is the geometric foundation for the component method. Resolving vectors into x and y components and adding them is mathematically equivalent to repeatedly applying the parallelogram law.
  • In electrostatics, the net force on a charge due to multiple other charges is found by applying the parallelogram law successively (or using components), since electric forces are vectors.
