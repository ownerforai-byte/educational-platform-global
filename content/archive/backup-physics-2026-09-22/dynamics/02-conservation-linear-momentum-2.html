Notes

  • Type 2 — alternate treatment: this version derives the conservation law from Newton's third law and the impulse picture, then applies it to recoil, explosions and two-body systems — the application chain NEB numericals follow.
  • The law: if the net EXTERNAL force on a system is zero, its total linear momentum stays constant: m1​u1​+m2​u2​=m1​v1​+m2​v2​. Internal forces (the pair between the two bodies) can change individual momenta but never the total.
  • Why it holds (third-law derivation): during contact, body 1 pushes body 2 with F12​ and body 2 pushes back F21​=−F12​ for the SAME time Δt. Impulses: Δp​1​=F21​Δt and Δp​2​=F12​Δt; adding gives Δ(p​1​+p​2​)=(F21​+F12​)Δt=0 — the total momentum cannot change.
  • When it applies: always for INTERNAL interactions; for the whole system even during explosions and collisions (internal forces are huge compared to external ones over the short interaction). Gravity/friction are external — momentum is conserved only along directions with zero net external force.
  • Recoil (from rest): initial momentum zero → MV+mv=0, so V=−Mm​v. Cannon–ball, rifle–bullet, rocket–exhaust, boat–passenger: the lighter body carries proportionally more speed.
  • Explosions and separations: a body of mass (m1​+m2​) at rest splits into pieces moving in opposite directions: m1​v1​=m2​v2​. Kinetic energy is NOT conserved here (chemical energy converts to motion) — momentum alone is the reliable bookkeeper.
  • Worked example: a 60 kg skater at rest throws a 3 kg ball at 8 m/s. Recoil: V=603×8​=0.4 m/s backward. If thrown at 30° above horizontal, only the horizontal component (8cos30°) enters the horizontal recoil balance.
  • Collision bookkeeping: in ANY collision (elastic or not) total momentum is conserved: m1​u1​+m2​u2​=m1​v1​+m2​v2​ (1-D). Two unknowns need a second equation — kinetic energy (elastic) or the coefficient of restitution e=u1​−u2​v2​−v1​​.
  • NEB exam focus: state the law with its condition (no net external force); derive it from the third law; solve recoil and explosion numericals; explain rocket propulsion and the gun recoil by momentum conservation.

Important Statements

  • The total linear momentum of an isolated system (zero net external force) remains constant in both magnitude and direction.
  • Newton's third law implies conservation of momentum: every action produces an equal and opposite reaction, so internal forces cannot change total momentum.
  • In a system of interacting bodies, the sum of individual momentum changes is zero: Δp₁ + Δp₂ + ... + Δpₙ = 0.
  • Momentum is conserved component-wise: if ΣF_x = 0, then Σp_x is constant even if forces act in other directions.
  • The law applies universally — from subatomic particles to galaxies — as long as the system is properly defined and isolated.

Important Notes

  • Always define your system first — conservation applies to the chosen system, not to individual bodies within it.
  • Internal forces (collisions, explosions, attractions) can redistribute momentum among parts but never change the total.
  • External forces (gravity, friction, applied pushes) change the total momentum — account for them before applying conservation.
  • The law is derived from Newton's third law and is more fundamental than Newton's laws themselves (it holds in quantum mechanics and relativity too).
  • In NEB exams, the derivation from the third law is a standard 3-4 mark question — memorize the exact steps.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Recoil shortcut: system starts at rest → m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ → speeds are inversely proportional to masses.
  • Sign convention: pick +x direction first; velocities in the opposite direction carry a negative sign — this is the most common error.
  • Explosion check: stationary object splits → fragments MUST move in opposite directions (total p was zero, stays zero).
  • Component trick: in 2-D, apply Σp_x = const and Σp_y = const separately — two equations for two unknowns.
  • Perfectly inelastic: bodies stick together → one final velocity, maximum KE loss, but momentum still conserved.

Exam Notes

  • State the law precisely: 'If the net external force on a system is zero, its total linear momentum remains constant.'
  • The third-law derivation (F₁₂ = −F₂₁ → Δp₁ + Δp₂ = 0) is asked almost every year — practice it.
  • Recoil and explosion numericals appear frequently — master m₁v₁ = m₂v₂ and its vector form.
  • Rocket propulsion and jet propulsion are explained by momentum conservation — be ready with the mechanism.
  • In collision problems, always write the momentum conservation equation first; add energy or restitution only if the problem requires it.

Important Concepts

  • The law of conservation of linear momentum: Σp = constant when ΣF_ext = 0 — one of the most fundamental laws in physics.
  • Newton's third law implies momentum conservation: action-reaction pairs produce equal and opposite momentum changes.
  • Recoil: a special case where a system initially at rest separates into parts with equal and opposite momenta.
  • Rocket propulsion: continuous ejection of mass produces continuous thrust — the variable-mass momentum problem.
  • Momentum conservation applies component-wise: a direction with zero net external force conserves momentum along that direction regardless of other directions.
