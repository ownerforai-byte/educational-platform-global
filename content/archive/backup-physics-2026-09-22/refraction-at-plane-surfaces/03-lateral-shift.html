Lateral Shift

Backup Physics 2026 09 22

Unit SLUG

Relevance

Notes

  • Definition: When a ray of light passes through a parallel-sided transparent slab (like a glass slab), it emerges parallel to the original direction but displaced sideways. This perpendicular displacement is called the lateral shift (or lateral displacement).
  • Formula: d=t⋅cosrsin(i−r)​, where t is the thickness of the slab, i is the angle of incidence, and r is the angle of refraction.
  • Derivation: Inside the slab, the ray travels at angle r to the normal. The horizontal distance traveled inside the slab is t/cosr. The lateral shift is this distance multiplied by sin(i−r): d=(t/cosr)⋅sin(i−r).
  • Special case — normal incidence: When i=0°, d=0. The ray passes straight through without any lateral shift. This is consistent with Snell's law: r=0 when i=0.
  • Dependence on thickness: The lateral shift is directly proportional to the slab thickness t. A thicker slab produces a larger shift. Doubling the thickness doubles the shift.
  • Dependence on angle of incidence: As i increases from 0°, the lateral shift increases. At grazing incidence (i→90°), the shift approaches the thickness t (the ray travels almost parallel to the slab faces).
  • Dependence on refractive index: For a fixed angle of incidence, a higher refractive index means a larger refraction angle r is smaller, so (i−r) is larger, and the shift is larger. Denser materials produce more lateral shift.
  • No angular deviation: The emergent ray is PARALLEL to the incident ray. The slab causes a lateral displacement but no change in direction. This is because the two refractions (entry and exit) cancel each other's angular effect.
  • Apparent shift of objects: When you look through a glass window at an angle, objects appear slightly displaced from their true positions. This is due to lateral shift. The effect is small for thin windows but noticeable with thick glass blocks.
  • Multiple slabs: If light passes through multiple parallel slabs of different materials, the total lateral shift is the sum of individual shifts: dtotal​=∑tk​⋅cosrk​sin(ik​−rk​)​, where each ik​ and rk​ is determined by Snell's law at each interface.
  • NEB exam focus: Derive the lateral shift formula. Calculate the shift for given values of t, i, and n. Explain why the emergent ray is parallel to the incident ray. Draw ray diagrams showing the shift.
  • Numerical example: A glass slab (n=1.5) of thickness 10 cm is illuminated by a ray at i=60°. First find r: sinr=sin60°/1.5=0.866/1.5=0.5774, so r=35.3°. Then d=10⋅cos35.3°sin(60°−35.3°)​=10⋅0.816sin24.7°​=10⋅0.8160.418​=5.12 cm.
  • Why the emergent ray is parallel: At the first interface, the ray bends toward the normal by angle (i−r). At the second interface, it bends away from the normal by the same angle (i−r) because the surfaces are parallel and the geometry is symmetric. Net angular change = 0.
  • Application — periscopes and binoculars: Parallel-sided glass blocks are used in optical instruments to displace the light path without changing its direction. This allows compact instrument design while maintaining the line of sight.
  • Connection to prism: A prism has non-parallel faces, so the emergent ray is NOT parallel to the incident ray — it is deviated by angle δ=i+e−A. The lateral shift formula does not apply to prisms.

Confusion

  • ❌ The emergent ray is deviated from the original direction. ✅ The emergent ray is PARALLEL to the incident ray. A parallel-sided slab causes lateral shift but NO angular deviation.
  • ❌ Lateral shift depends on the refractive index of the slab material only. ✅ Lateral shift depends on ALL THREE: thickness t, angle of incidence i, AND refractive index n (through the refraction angle r).
  • ❌ At normal incidence, there is a lateral shift. ✅ At normal incidence (i=0°), the lateral shift is ZERO. The ray passes straight through without any displacement.
  • ❌ The lateral shift formula d=tsin(i−r)/cosr works for prisms. ✅ This formula is ONLY for parallel-sided slabs. Prisms have non-parallel faces, so the emergent ray is deviated, not just shifted.
  • ❌ A thicker slab always produces a proportionally larger shift for the same angle. ✅ Yes, the shift IS directly proportional to thickness: d∝t. But the proportionality constant depends on i and n.
  • ❌ The lateral shift is the same as the deviation angle. ✅ Lateral shift is a DISTANCE (measured in cm or m). Deviation angle is an ANGLE (measured in degrees). They are completely different quantities.
  • ❌ Light passing through a glass slab changes its frequency. ✅ The frequency of light does NOT change when it passes through any medium. Only the speed and wavelength change: v=c/n and λ=λ0​/n.

Practice

  • A glass slab of thickness 5 cm (n=1.5) is struck by a ray at i=45°. Find the lateral shift. sinr=sin45°/1.5=0.7071/1.5=0.4714, so r=28.1°. d=5⋅cos28.1°sin(45°−28.1°)​=5⋅0.883sin16.9°​=5⋅0.8830.291​=1.65 cm.
  • Show that for small angles of incidence, the lateral shift is approximately d≈t⋅i⋅(1−1/n). For small i and r: sin(i−r)≈i−r and cosr≈1. From Snell's law: r≈i/n. Therefore d≈t(i−i/n)=t⋅i⋅(1−1/n). This linear approximation is useful for paraxial optics.
  • A ray passes through a glass slab and emerges with a lateral shift of 2 cm. If the slab thickness is doubled to 10 cm (same material, same angle of incidence), what is the new lateral shift? Since d∝t, doubling the thickness doubles the shift: dnew​=2×2=4 cm.
  • A transparent slab of thickness 3 cm produces a lateral shift of 1 cm for a ray incident at 60°. Find the refractive index of the slab. d=tsin(i−r)/cosr. 1=3sin(60°−r)/cosr. sin(60°−r)=cosr/3. Using the identity sin(60°−r)=sin60°cosr−cos60°sinr: 0.866cosr−0.5sinr=cosr/3. Solving: r≈30°. Then n=sin60°/sin30°=0.866/0.5=1.73. This is a high-index glass or crystal.
  • Why does a fish in water appear displaced when viewed from above at an angle? Light from the fish refracts at the water-air interface. The emergent ray appears to come from a point closer to the surface (apparent depth effect) AND laterally shifted. The combined effect makes the fish appear at a different position than its actual location.
  • A ray enters a parallel-sided slab of refractive index n at angle i. Show that the lateral shift is maximum when i→90° (grazing incidence). As i→90°, r→arcsin(1/n). The shift d=tsin(i−r)/cosr→tsin(90°−r)/cosr=tcosr/cosr=t. The maximum lateral shift equals the slab thickness.

Universal Facts

  • The lateral shift through a parallel-sided slab is a fundamental result in geometrical optics. It demonstrates that refraction at parallel interfaces produces displacement without deviation — a principle used in many optical instruments.
  • In architectural glass, lateral shift is usually negligible for thin panes (a few mm). But in thick glass blocks used in art installations or optical tables, the shift can be several centimeters and must be accounted for in precise alignments.
  • The concept of lateral shift generalizes to any number of parallel interfaces. In a multi-layer coating (anti-reflective coatings on lenses), each layer contributes a small lateral shift, and the total effect determines the phase relationship between reflected waves.
  • In fiber optics, the concept of lateral shift is related to the "beam wander" that occurs when light enters a fiber at an angle. Proper coupling requires minimizing the lateral displacement at the fiber end face.
  • Leonhard Euler studied the optics of parallel-sided plates in the 1740s, deriving the lateral shift formula and showing its relationship to the delay introduced by the plate — a precursor to modern optical path length calculations.
