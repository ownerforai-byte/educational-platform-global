Laws of Refraction and Refractive Index

Relevance

Notes

  • Absolute refractive index: The refractive index of a medium measured relative to vacuum (or air, approximately). n=vc​, where c=3.00×108 m/s is the speed of light in vacuum and v is the speed in the medium.
  • Relative refractive index n21​: When light passes from medium 1 to medium 2, the relative refractive index is n21​=n1​n2​​=v2​v1​​. It tells how much slower (or faster) light travels in medium 2 compared to medium 1.
  • Snell's law with relative index: n21​=sinrsini​, where i is the angle of incidence in medium 1 and r is the angle of refraction in medium 2. This is the original form of Snell's law.
  • Reciprocity relation: n12​=n21​1​. The refractive index of medium 1 relative to medium 2 is the reciprocal of the refractive index of medium 2 relative to medium 1. This follows directly from n12​=n1​/n2​.
  • Multiplicative chain rule: For light passing through multiple media: n31​=n32​×n21​. For example, air → water → glass: nglass/air​=nglass/water​×nwater/air​. This is useful for calculating the overall deviation through a stack of materials.
  • Speed of light in different media: v=c/n. In water (n=1.33): v=2.26×108 m/s. In glass (n=1.52): v=1.97×108 m/s. In diamond (n=2.42): v=1.24×108 m/s. Light slows down in denser media.
  • Wavelength changes, frequency stays constant: When light enters a medium, its speed changes but its frequency remains the same (determined by the source). Therefore: λmedium​=nλvacuum​​. The wavelength shortens in denser media.
  • Refractive index and optical density: A medium with higher n is optically denser. Light travels slower in optically denser media. Note: optical density is NOT the same as mass density (e.g., oil has lower mass density than water but can have a higher refractive index).
  • Practical example — diving board apparent depth: A diver looking down at a pool floor sees it at apparent depth d′=d/n, where d is the real depth. For water (n=1.33), a 3 m deep pool appears to be 3/1.33=2.25 m deep.
  • NEB exam focus: Distinguish between absolute and relative refractive index. Derive n31​=n32​×n21​. Solve problems involving light passing through multiple media. Calculate apparent depth and real depth.
  • Why relative index matters: In compound lens systems (camera lenses, microscopes), light passes through multiple glass-air interfaces. The relative refractive index at each interface determines the bending angle and overall focal length.
  • Refractive index and color: The refractive index depends on wavelength (dispersion). Blue light has a slightly higher n than red light in most glasses. This causes chromatic aberration in lenses — different colors focus at different points.
  • Fresnel equations connection: The reflection coefficient at an interface depends on the relative refractive index. For normal incidence: R=(n2​+n1​n2​−n1​​)2. At an air-glass interface (n1​=1,n2​=1.52): R=(0.52/2.52)2=0.043 (4.3% reflection).
  • Index matching: When two media have the same refractive index (n1​=n2​), there is no refraction at the interface — light passes straight through as if the boundary doesn't exist. This principle is used in immersion oil microscopy to increase resolution.

Confusion

  • ❌ The refractive index can be less than 1. ✅ For normal materials, n≥1 because light travels slowest in vacuum. However, in specialized materials (plasmas, metamaterials), the phase velocity can exceed c, giving n<1 — but information still travels at v≤c.
  • ❌ A higher refractive index means a denser material (more mass per volume). ✅ Optical density and mass density are different. Oil (ρ≈0.9 g/cm³) can have n≈1.47, while water (ρ=1.0 g/cm³) has n=1.33. Oil is optically denser but mass-wise lighter.
  • ❌ n21​=n2​−n1​. ✅ The relative refractive index is a RATIO, not a difference: n21​=n2​/n1​. This is crucial for correct application of Snell's law.
  • ❌ The frequency of light changes when it enters a new medium. ✅ The FREQUENCY remains constant. Only the speed and wavelength change. f=c/λvacuum​=v/λmedium​.
  • ❌ Light always bends toward the normal when entering a new medium. ✅ Light bends TOWARD the normal only when entering a denser medium (n2​>n1​). When entering a rarer medium (n2​<n1​), it bends AWAY from the normal.
  • ❌ The chain rule n31​=n32​×n21​ only works for three media. ✅ The chain rule generalizes to any number of media: nnm​=nn,n−1​×nn−1,n−2​×⋯×n21​.
  • ❌ The refractive index is the same for all colors of light. ✅ The refractive index decreases with increasing wavelength (normal dispersion). Blue light (λ≈450 nm) has a higher n than red light (λ≈700 nm) in most glasses.

Practice

  • Light passes from water (n=1.33) into glass (n=1.52). Find the relative refractive index nglass/water​ and the angle of refraction if the angle of incidence is 30°. ngw​=1.52/1.33=1.143. Snell's law: 1.33sin30°=1.52sinr. sinr=0.665/1.52=0.4375. r=25.9°.
  • A ray passes from air into water (n=1.33) then into glass (n=1.52). If the angle of incidence in air is 45°, find the angle in glass. Method 1 (step-by-step): In water: sinrw​=sin45°/1.33=0.7071/1.33=0.5317, rw​=32.1°. In glass: 1.33sin32.1°=1.52sinrg​, sinrg​=1.33×0.5317/1.52=0.4654, rg​=27.7°. Method 2 (chain rule): nga​=1.52, sinrg​=sin45°/1.52=0.4654, rg​=27.7°. Same result.
  • Show that n12​×n23​×n31​=1. n12​=n1​/n2​, n23​=n2​/n3​, n31​=n3​/n1​. Product: (n1​/n2​)(n2​/n3​)(n3​/n1​)=1. ✓ This is a useful consistency check.
  • The speed of light in a certain liquid is 2.0×108 m/s. Find its refractive index. n=c/v=3.0×108/2.0×108=1.5. This is typical of crown glass or some oils.
  • A coin at the bottom of a pool 2.0 m deep appears to be at what depth when viewed from above? Apparent depth d′=d/n=2.0/1.33=1.50 m. The coin appears 50 cm closer than it actually is.
  • Light travels from diamond (n=2.42) into water (n=1.33). Find the critical angle. sinθc​=n2​/n1​=1.33/2.42=0.5496. θc​=arcsin(0.5496)=33.4°. Light entering water from diamond at angles greater than 33.4° undergoes total internal reflection.

Universal Facts

  • The concept of refractive index was formalized by Willebrord Snellius (Snell) in 1621, though the law was first correctly stated by Ibn Sahl (940–1000 CE) in his treatise on burning mirrors and lenses.
  • The refractive index of air at STP is 1.000293, so for most practical purposes we treat air as having n=1. This is why we use "absolute" refractive indices measured relative to vacuum but report them as if measured in air.
  • Diamond's exceptionally high refractive index (n=2.42) is why it sparkles so brilliantly. The small critical angle (24.4°) means most light entering a well-cut diamond undergoes total internal reflection before exiting, creating the characteristic fire.
  • The refractive index is dimensionless — it is a ratio of two speeds. This makes it convenient for calculations because no unit conversions are needed when using Snell's law with relative indices.
  • In fiber optics, the core has a slightly higher refractive index than the cladding (ncore​≈1.48, nclad​≈1.46). This small difference (about 1.4%) is enough to trap light by total internal reflection over kilometers of cable.
