Electric Charges and Charging by Induction

Enriched Content

Notes
  • Electric Charge: An intrinsic property of matter causing electromagnetic force. Quantized: q=ne (e≈1.6×10−19 C). Conserved in isolated systems.
  • Charging by Induction: A method to charge a conductor without contact. A charged object brought near a neutral conductor polarizes it; grounding the far side allows charge to flow, leaving the conductor with an opposite charge.
  • Properties: Additivity of charges, conservation of charge, quantization of charge.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'Induction creates charge.' ✅ No, it redistributes existing charge; the total charge of the system is conserved.
  • ❌ 'Grounding always removes electrons.' ✅ Grounding allows electrons to flow to/from the earth to neutralize the potential, depending on the inducing charge.
Practice
  • Calculate the number of electrons in 1 C of charge.
  • Describe the steps to charge a metal sphere positively using a negatively charged rod.
Universal Facts
  • Millikan's oil-drop experiment proved the quantization of charge.
  • Charge is invariant under relativistic motion (a charge's value doesn't change with speed).
  • Induction is the principle behind electrostatic precipitators and photocopiers.
Examples
  • Rubbing a glass rod with silk (glass becomes positive, silk negative).
  • Attracting small paper bits with a charged comb (polarization/induction).
  • Lightning as a massive discharge of accumulated charge.
Practice Questions
  • If a body has 10^19 excess electrons, what is its charge?
  • Explain why a charged balloon sticks to a wall.
  • Can a body have a charge of 3.2 x 10^-18 C? Why?
Formulas
  • Quantization: q = ±ne
  • Total charge conservation: Σq_initial = Σq_final
Key Points
  • Charge is quantized (integer multiples of e).
  • Charge is conserved in isolated systems.
  • Induction requires a conductor and grounding.
  • Polarization occurs in both conductors and insulators.
Summary

Electric charge is a fundamental, quantized, and conserved property. Charging by induction allows creating opposite charges on conductors without physical contact, relying on polarization and grounding.

Special Notes
  • Always specify the sign of the charge.
  • Induction requires a conducting path to ground for permanent charging.
  • Insulators can be polarized but not easily charged by induction.
Important Statements
  • The total charge of an isolated system is constant.
  • Electric charge is quantized: q = ne.
  • Charging by induction results in a charge opposite to the inducing charge.
  • Like charges repel, unlike charges attract.
  • A neutral object can be attracted to a charged object due to polarization.
Important Notes
  • Quantization is negligible at macroscopic scales.
  • Induction is a temporary effect unless grounding is used.
  • Electrostatic force is much stronger than gravitational force.
Exam Short Tricks
  • For quantization problems, always use n = q/e.
  • Induction: inducing charge and induced charge are always opposite.
  • Grounding: electrons flow to/from the earth to neutralize the potential.
Exam Notes
  • Understand the difference between conduction (contact) and induction (no contact).
  • Be able to draw the steps of charging by induction.
  • Quantization problems are frequent.
