Conservation of Energy

Notes

  • Law of conservation of energy: Energy can neither be created nor destroyed; it can only be transformed from one form to another or transferred between systems. The total energy of an isolated system remains constant over time.
  • Mechanical energy conservation: When only conservative forces (gravity, spring force, electrostatic force) act on a system, mechanical energy E = K + U is conserved: ΔK + ΔU = 0, or K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f.
  • Potential energy types: Gravitational PE near Earth: U_g = mgh (h = height above reference). Elastic PE of a spring: U_s = ½kx² (x = compression/extension from natural length). For a universal gravitational field: U = -GMm/r.
  • Non-conservative forces and energy loss: Friction, air resistance, and viscous drag are non-conservative. They convert mechanical energy into thermal energy (heat). Work done by non-conservative forces: W_nc = ΔE_mech = E_final - E_initial.
  • Total energy conservation including heat: Even with friction, total energy is conserved if you include thermal energy: ΔK + ΔU + ΔE_thermal = 0. The first law of thermodynamics: ΔU_internal = Q - W, where Q is heat added, W is work done BY the system.
  • Pendulum energy conservation: For a simple pendulum (mass m, length L, angle θ): At the bottom, K = ½mv², U = 0. At the extremes, v = 0, K = 0, U = mgL(1-cosθ). With no air resistance: ½mv² + mgL(1-cosθ) = constant.
  • Spring-mass system: For a mass m on a spring with constant k: At equilibrium, K = ½mv², U = 0. At max extension x = A, v = 0, K = 0, U = ½kA². Energy oscillates: ½mv² + ½kx² = ½kA² = constant.
  • Roller coaster physics: At the top of a hill, the car has maximum PE (mgh) and minimum KE. At the bottom, maximum KE and minimum PE. Total energy constant (neglecting friction). Minimum speed at top of vertical loop: v ≥ √(gr) to stay on track.
  • Energy in collisions: In elastic collisions, both momentum and KE are conserved. In inelastic collisions, momentum is conserved but KE is not (some converts to heat/sound). In perfectly inelastic collisions, objects stick together and KE loss is maximum.
  • Conservation of energy in nuclear reactions: Mass-energy equivalence E = mc² shows mass is another form of energy. In nuclear fission/fusion, the total mass of products is less than reactants — the mass defect Δm appears as kinetic energy of products: ΔE = Δmc².
  • Power and energy relationship: Power is the rate of energy transfer: P = dE/dt. If power is constant, E = Pt. If power varies, E = ∫P dt. Energy delivered is the area under the P-t curve.
  • Real-world energy transformations: Chemical energy (fuel) → thermal (burning) → mechanical (engine) → electrical (generator) → electromagnetic (light) → thermal (absorber). At each step, some energy becomes heat (second law).
  • Reference point for potential energy: Gravitational PE is defined relative to a chosen zero level. The choice is arbitrary — only differences in PE matter. Usually we set U = 0 at the lowest point in the problem or at ground level.

Confusion

  • Energy is NOT created or destroyed — it only transforms. When a pendulum stops swinging due to friction, the energy doesn't 'disappear'; it becomes thermal energy in the air and the pivot. The FIRST law of thermodynamics says energy is conserved in all processes.
  • Conservation of mechanical energy (K + U = constant) ONLY holds when ALL forces are conservative. If there is ANY friction, air resistance, or non-conservative force, mechanical energy is NOT conserved. You must include W_nc or track thermal energy.
  • Potential energy is NOT absolute — it is defined relative to an arbitrary zero point. A ball on a table has U = mgh relative to the floor, but U = mg(h+H) relative to the basement. Only PE differences matter physically.
  • In a collision, momentum is ALWAYS conserved (if no external forces). Kinetic energy is ONLY conserved in perfectly ELASTIC collisions. Many students think KE is always conserved in collisions — it is NOT. Real collisions are usually somewhat inelastic.
  • The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) and energy conservation (ΔK + ΔU = -W_nc) are DIFFERENT. The first is about NET work changing kinetic energy. The second is about total mechanical energy. With conservative forces, W_net = -ΔU, so W_net + ΔU = ΔK, which is the work-energy theorem.

Practice

  • A 2 kg block slides down a frictionless incline of height 5 m. Find its speed at the bottom. Solution: U_i = mgh = 2 × 9.8 × 5 = 98 J. K_i = 0. At bottom: U_f = 0. K_f = U_i → ½mv² = 98 → v² = 196/2 = 98 → v = √98 = 9.9 m/s.
  • A 0.5 kg block on a spring (k = 200 N/m) is compressed 0.1 m and released. Find max speed. Solution: U_s = ½kx² = ½×200×0.01 = 1 J. At equilibrium, all U_s → K. ½mv² = 1 → v² = 4 → v = 2 m/s. Amplitude of oscillation = 0.1 m.
  • A 1000 kg car moving at 20 m/s brakes to a stop. The brakes heat up with 80% of the KE going to thermal energy in the brakes. How much heat is generated? Solution: KE_initial = ½×1000×400 = 200,000 J = 200 kJ. Heat = 0.8 × 200 kJ = 160 kJ.
  • A ball is dropped from height h = 10 m and bounces back to height 7.5 m. Find the coefficient of restitution e and the fraction of energy lost. Solution: Speed before impact = √(2gh) = √(196) = 14 m/s. Speed after = √(2g×7.5) = √(147) = 12.1 m/s. e = v_after/v_before = 12.1/14 = 0.86. Energy lost = 1 - e² = 1 - 0.74 = 26%.
  • A 5 kg block slides down a rough 30° incline of length 4 m with μ = 0.2. Find its speed at the bottom. Solution: Height drop = 4 sin 30° = 2 m. U_i = mgh = 5×9.8×2 = 98 J. Work by friction: W_f = -μmg cosθ × s = -0.2×5×9.8×cos30°×4 = -0.2×5×9.8×0.866×4 = -34 J. ΔK = U_i + W_f = 98 - 34 = 64 J. v = √(2×64/5) = √(128/5) = 5.06 m/s.

Universal Facts

  • The law of conservation of energy is the first law of thermodynamics and applies to ALL physical processes — mechanics, electromagnetism, thermodynamics, quantum mechanics, general relativity. It is the most fundamental and universal law in physics.
  • Emmy Noether proved (1918) that conservation of energy is a consequence of time translation symmetry: the laws of physics are the same at all times. This is Noether's theorem — every continuous symmetry implies a conserved quantity.
  • Perpetual motion machines of the first kind (producing energy without input) are IMPOSSIBLE because they violate energy conservation. No known process in the universe creates net energy from nothing.
  • The total energy of the universe is a subtle concept in cosmology. In an expanding universe, energy conservation is not globally well-defined in the same way. However, in local physics, energy conservation is exact and tested to incredible precision.
  • Energy exists in many forms: kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, chemical, nuclear, electromagnetic, thermal, rest mass energy (mc²), etc. All can be converted into each other, subject to the laws of physics.

Examples

  • Pendulum: A 2 kg bob is released from 0.5 m above the lowest point. Speed at bottom: mgh = ½mv² → v = √(2×9.8×0.5) = 3.13 m/s.
  • Spring-mass: A 0.5 kg mass on a spring (k = 200 N/m) compressed 0.1 m. Max speed: ½kA² = ½mv² → v = √(200×0.01/0.5) = 2 m/s.
  • Roller coaster: A 500 kg car starts at 20 m height. Speed at bottom: v = √(2×9.8×20) = 19.8 m/s.
  • Free fall: A 1 kg stone dropped from 45 m. Speed at ground: v = √(2×9.8×45) = 29.7 m/s.
  • Inclined plane: A 3 kg block slides down a smooth 30° slope of length 10 m. Speed at bottom: v = √(2×9.8×5) = 9.9 m/s (h = 10 sin 30° = 5 m).

Formulas

  • Mechanical energy conservation: K_i + U_i = K_f + U_f (only conservative forces)
  • Work-energy theorem: W_net = ΔK = ½mv_f² − ½mv_i²
  • Work by non-conservative forces: W_nc = ΔE_mech = (K_f + U_f) − (K_i + U_i)
  • Gravitational PE: U_g = mgh (near Earth); U = −GMm/r (universal)
  • Elastic PE: U_s = ½kx²; total spring energy: ½mv² + ½kx² = ½kA²

Key Points

  • Mechanical energy (K + U) is conserved ONLY when all forces are conservative.
  • Friction and air resistance are non-conservative — they convert mechanical energy to heat.
  • The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) holds for ALL forces, conservative or not.
  • Total energy (including thermal) is always conserved — this is the first law of thermodynamics.
  • Choose the reference level for PE at the lowest point to simplify calculations.

Summary

Energy cannot be created or destroyed — only transformed. When only conservative forces act, mechanical energy (K + U) is conserved. Non-conservative forces (friction) convert mechanical energy to heat, but total energy is always conserved. The work-energy theorem (W_net = ΔK) is the key tool for solving problems.

Special Notes

  • Mechanical energy conservation requires ALL forces to be conservative — one friction force breaks it.
  • The reference level for gravitational PE is arbitrary — only differences matter.
  • In a spring-mass system, energy oscillates between kinetic and potential: ½mv² + ½kx² = constant.
  • The work-energy theorem is more general than mechanical energy conservation — it works with friction.
  • NEB frequently asks: 'Find the speed at the bottom' — use mgh = ½mv² for smooth surfaces.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Smooth surface shortcut: mgh = ½mv² — mass cancels, so v = √(2gh) independent of mass.
  • Spring shortcut: ½kA² = ½mv² — max speed v = A√(k/m).
  • Pendulum shortcut: v_max = √(2gL(1−cosθ)) — depends on L and θ, not mass.
  • Rough slope shortcut: mgh − μmg cos θ × d = ½mv² — friction does negative work.
  • Work-energy shortcut: W_net = ΔK — always works, even with friction.
