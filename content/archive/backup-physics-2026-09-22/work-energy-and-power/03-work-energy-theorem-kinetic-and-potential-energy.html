Work-Energy Theorem — Kinetic and Potential Energy

Notes

  • Work-Energy Theorem (statement): The net work done by all forces acting on a particle equals the change in its kinetic energy: Wnet​=ΔK=Kf​−Ki​=21​mvf2​−21​mvi2​. This is a scalar equation — direction is handled through the dot product in work.
  • Derivation from Newton's Second Law: F=ma=mdtdv​. Multiply both sides by v=dx/dt: Fv=mvdtdv​=mdtd​(21​v2). Integrate over time: ∫Fvdt=∫Fdx=W=Δ(21​mv2)=ΔK. This shows the theorem is mathematically equivalent to F=ma.
  • Kinetic energy definition: K=21​mv2. Always positive (since v2≥0). Scalar quantity — depends on speed, not velocity direction. A 1 kg mass at 10 m/s has K=50 J. Doubling speed quadruples KE (v² dependence).
  • Potential energy — general concept: Potential energy U is energy stored due to the position or configuration of a system in a conservative force field. It is defined ONLY for conservative forces. The change in potential energy is the negative of the work done by the conservative force: ΔU=−Wconservative​.
  • Gravitational potential energy (near Earth): Ug​=mgh, where h is height above an arbitrarily chosen reference level (usually ground or lowest point in problem). ΔUg​=mgΔh. Work by gravity: Wg​=−ΔUg​=mg(hi​−hf​). Only HEIGHT difference matters, not the path taken.
  • Elastic potential energy (spring): For a spring obeying Hooke's law F=−kx, Us​=21​kx2 where x is displacement from natural length. Work by spring: Ws​=−ΔUs​=21​kxi2​−21​kxf2​. Us​ is always ≥0 (minimum at x=0).
  • Mechanical energy: E=K+U=K+Ug​+Us​+…. When ONLY conservative forces do work, Wnet​=Wconservative​=−ΔU. Then Wnet​=ΔK=−ΔU, so ΔK+ΔU=0, meaning E=constant. This is conservation of mechanical energy.
  • Non-conservative forces and the general work-energy relation: When non-conservative forces (friction, air resistance, applied forces) are present: Wnc​=ΔE=ΔK+ΔU. This is the most general form. Wnc​ is the work done by ALL non-conservative forces. If Wnc​=0, mechanical energy is conserved.
  • Kinetic energy in different frames: KE is frame-dependent. In lab frame: K=21​mv2. In CM frame: KCM​=21​μvrel2​ where μ is reduced mass. The total KE = KCM​ (motion of CM) + Krel​ (internal motion). Only internal KE can be converted to heat in inelastic collisions.
  • Potential energy reference point is arbitrary: U=mgh uses h measured from any reference. Changing reference adds a constant to all U values, which cancels in ΔU. Always choose the reference to simplify the problem (e.g., U=0 at ground level, or at equilibrium of spring).
  • Energy approach vs force approach: Force approach: F=ma, solve for acceleration, integrate for velocity/position. Energy approach: Wnc​=ΔE, solve directly for speed or height. Energy is often simpler when forces vary or path is curved, but it gives speeds, not trajectories or times.
  • Work done by gravity in a closed loop: Zero. Wg​=−ΔUg​, and for a closed loop ΔUg​=0 (you return to the same height). This is why gravity is conservative. Work done by friction in a closed loop: NEGATIVE (energy lost to heat). Friction is non-conservative.
  • Power and the work-energy theorem: P=dtdW​=dtdK​=Fv (when F and v are parallel). The rate of change of kinetic energy equals the power delivered by the net force. For a particle moving in a circle at constant speed, ΔK=0, so net work = 0, and centripetal force does zero work (F⊥v).

Confusion

  • The work-energy theorem uses NET work (sum of work by ALL forces). A common error is using work by just one force. If you push a block at constant speed on a rough floor, your work is positive, friction's work is negative, NET work = 0, ΔK=0 — the theorem holds perfectly.
  • Potential energy is NOT defined for non-conservative forces. You CANNOT write Ufriction​=μmgx. Friction dissipates energy as heat; there is no 'friction potential' to store and recover energy. This is a fundamental distinction.
  • Mechanical energy conservation (K+U=constant) ONLY applies when Wnc​=0. If there is ANY friction, air resistance, or external pushing/pulling, mechanical energy is NOT conserved. You MUST use Wnc​=ΔE instead.
  • The reference point for potential energy does NOT affect the physics. ΔU is what matters. If you set U=0 at the table vs floor, the KE at the bottom is the same. Students often waste time 'finding the right' reference — any reference works if used consistently.
  • Kinetic energy can NEVER be negative (v2≥0). Potential energy CAN be negative (e.g., U=−GMm/r for universal gravity). Total mechanical energy can be negative (bound systems like planets in orbit). Only ENERGY DIFFERENCES are physically meaningful.

Practice

  • A 0.5 kg ball is dropped from height 20 m. Find its speed at 5 m above ground using energy conservation. Solution: Ui​=mghi​=0.5×9.8×20=98 J. Uf​=0.5×9.8×5=24.5 J. ΔU=−73.5 J. ΔK=+73.5 J. 21​mv2=73.5→v=147/0.5​=294​=17.1 m/s.
  • A 2 kg block compresses a spring (k=400 N/m) by 0.1 m on a frictionless surface and is released. Find its max speed. Solution: Initial: Us​=21​kx2=200×0.01=2 J, K=0. Final: Us​=0, Kmax​=2 J. 21​×2×v2=2→v2=2→v=1.41 m/s.
  • A 1 kg block slides on a rough horizontal surface (μk​=0.3) with initial speed 10 m/s. How far does it go before stopping? Solution: Wnc​=Wf​=−μk​mgd. ΔK=0−21​×1×100=−50 J. Wnc​=ΔK→−0.3×1×9.8×d=−50→d=50/2.94=17 m.
  • A 3 kg mass is attached to a vertical spring (k=200 N/m) and released from the spring's natural length. Find the max extension. Solution: Let x be extension. Initial: Ug​=0 (reference), Us​=0, K=0. Final: Ug​=−mgx, Us​=21​kx2, K=0. Energy: 0=−mgx+21​kx2→x=2mg/k=2×3×9.8/200=0.294 m.
  • A 1000 kg car goes up a 10° hill at constant 20 m/s. Engine power? Assume 20% losses. Solution: Force to overcome gravity: Fg​=mgsin10∘=1000×9.8×0.174=1705 N. Useful power P=Fg​v=1705×20=34,100 W. With 20% loss, input power = 34,100/0.8=42,625 W ≈ 57 hp.

Universal Facts

  • The work-energy theorem was developed by Leibniz, Bernoulli, and Coriolis in the 17th-19th centuries. The concept of 'vis viva' (living force, mv2) by Leibniz (1686) was a precursor — he argued mv2 (not mv) was the conserved quantity in mechanical systems.
  • Noether's theorem (1918) proves that conservation of energy is a consequence of time translation symmetry: the laws of physics don't change with time. If physics were different tomorrow, energy wouldn't be conserved. This is one of the deepest connections in physics.
  • Kinetic energy 21​mv2 is NOT a relativistic formula — it's the low-speed approximation of (γ−1)mc2. At v=0.1c, the error is about 0.5%. At everyday speeds, Newtonian KE is exact for all practical purposes.
  • Potential energy is a property of the SYSTEM, not a single object. A ball alone doesn't have gravitational PE; the ball-EARTH system does. The energy is stored in the gravitational field. Similarly, spring PE is stored in the spring's deformed configuration.
  • The total energy of the universe (if well-defined) is constant. In general relativity, energy conservation is subtle — in an expanding universe, energy of photons decreases (redshift), and there's no global energy conservation law. But locally, energy conservation is exact and tested to extraordinary precision.
