Enriched Content

Notes
  • Poisson's ratio (σ): when a body is stretched (or compressed), it becomes thinner (or thicker) in the perpendicular directions. Poisson's ratio is the ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain: σ = −(lateral strain)/(longitudinal strain) = −(β/α). The minus sign keeps σ positive because lateral strain is opposite in sign to longitudinal strain.
  • Why it is dimensionless: Poisson's ratio divides one strain by another — strain is dimensionless, so σ has no units and no dimensions. Its value for most materials lies between 0 and 0.5.
  • Typical values: cork ≈ 0 (no lateral change), concrete ≈ 0.1–0.2, steel ≈ 0.25–0.3, copper ≈ 0.33, aluminium ≈ 0.34, rubber ≈ 0.5 (nearly incompressible). A hypothetical material with σ = 0.5 would conserve volume exactly.
  • Worked example — wire stretch: a wire of length 2 m and diameter 1 mm is stretched by 2 mm. Longitudinal strain = 2×10⁻³/2 = 10⁻³. If σ = 0.3, lateral strain = −0.3×10⁻³, so diameter change = 0.3×10⁻³ × 1 mm = 3×10⁻⁴ mm = 0.3 μm (a fractional diameter change of 3×10⁻⁴).
  • Relation with the moduli: σ links Young's modulus E, bulk modulus B and shear modulus G: E = 2G(1 + σ) and E = 3B(1 − 2σ). From these: 1/σ can be used to check material consistency, and σ = (3B − E)/(6B).
  • Connection to volume change: the fractional volume change under uniaxial stretch is ΔV/V = ε(1 − 2σ). For a perfectly incompressible material (σ = 0.5), ΔV/V = 0 — the volume is unchanged.
  • Limits on σ: the thermodynamic bound is −1 < σ < 0.5. Nearly all common materials have 0 < σ < 0.5. Materials with negative Poisson's ratio (auxetics) actually thicken when stretched — rare and exotic.
  • Applications: σ matters in designing cables, stretching films, and geological materials; cork (σ≈0) is chosen for bottle stoppers because it doesn't change width when squeezed, so it seals without expanding.
Confusion
  • ❌ 'Poisson's ratio has units of strain.' ✅ It is a RATIO of two strains — dimensionless.
  • ❌ 'Poisson's ratio can be greater than 1.' ✅ No — the thermodynamic limit is 0.5 for isotropic materials (a few exotic anisotropic materials exceed 0.5 but never 1).
  • ❌ 'Lateral strain equals longitudinal strain.' ✅ Lateral strain = −σ × longitudinal strain, with σ typically between 0 and 0.5.
  • ❌ 'When stretched, volume always increases.' ✅ If σ = 0.5 the volume is constant; for σ < 0.5 the volume increases slightly (ΔV/V = ε(1−2σ)).
  • ❌ 'Cork and rubber have similar σ.' ✅ Cork ≈ 0, rubber ≈ 0.5 — at opposite ends of the range.
  • ❌ 'Poisson's ratio is a force.' ✅ It is a pure, dimensionless ratio of deformation (strain) — not a force or modulus.
Practice
  • A 1 mm diameter wire stretches 1 mm over 2 m length. The diameter decreases to 0.9997 mm. Find Poisson's ratio.
  • For steel, E = 2×10¹¹ and G = 8×10¹⁰. Find σ.
  • A material has σ = 0.25 and longitudinal strain 2×10⁻³. Find the lateral strain.
  • Compute the fractional volume change for ε = 10⁻³ and σ = 0.3.
  • Under what condition is the volume unchanged during stretching? (ΔV/V = 0).
  • Why is cork used for bottle stoppers? Answer using its Poisson's ratio.
Universal Facts
  • Poisson's ratio is named after Siméon Denis Poisson (1829) who derived it theoretically for isotropic materials.
  • The majority of materials have σ between 0.25 and 0.35 — metals cluster around 0.3.
  • Auxetic (negative σ) materials — like some foams — thicken perpendicular to the applied stretch, useful in impact protection. They are being explored for bulletproof vests and smart textiles.
  • Rubber's σ ≈ 0.5 means it is nearly incompressible — stretching a rubber band thinning preserves volume.
  • Poisson's ratio plays a central role in stress analysis in civil and mechanical engineering. It is essential for finite element analysis (FEA) software used in modern engineering design.
  • Thermoelastic Effect: When a material is stretched, it cools (Joule effect); when compressed, it heats. This is due to the change in entropy associated with molecular alignment.
Examples
  • Wire: L = 2 m, d = 1 mm, ΔL = 2 mm, σ = 0.3 → diameter change = σ × ε × d = 0.3×10⁻³×1 = 3×10⁻⁴ mm.
  • From moduli: E = 2×10¹¹, G = 8×10¹⁰ → σ = E/(2G) − 1 = 1.25 − 1 = 0.25.
  • Lateral strain: ε_long = 2×10⁻³, σ = 0.25 → ε_lat = −5×10⁻⁴.
  • Volume: ε = 10⁻³, σ = 0.3 → ΔV/V = 10⁻³(1−0.6) = 4×10⁻⁴.
  • Cork: σ ≈ 0 → bottle stopper keeps its width when squeezed.
Practice Questions
  • A wire of diameter 1 mm is stretched so its length increases by 0.5%. The diameter reduces by 0.15%. Find Poisson's ratio.
  • Steel has E = 2×10¹¹ N/m² and B = 1.6×10¹¹ N/m². Find σ from E = 3B(1−2σ).
  • A rubber cord (σ = 0.5) is stretched. Show that its volume stays constant.
  • Find the lateral strain on a 2 mm square bar stretched with longitudinal strain 10⁻³ (σ = 0.3).
  • A concrete block (σ = 0.2) is compressed by 0.1%. Find the lateral expansion.
  • Explain why materials with σ = 0.5 are called incompressible.
Formulas
  • Poisson's ratio: σ = −(lateral strain)/(longitudinal strain)
  • Relation with moduli: E = 2G(1 + σ); E = 3B(1 − 2σ)
  • σ from E and B: σ = (3B − E)/(6B)
  • σ from E and G: σ = E/(2G) − 1
  • Fractional volume change: ΔV/V = ε(1 − 2σ)
Key Points
  • Poisson's ratio is the (negative) ratio of lateral strain to longitudinal strain — always dimensionless.
  • σ typically lies between 0 and 0.5 for isotropic materials.
  • σ links E, B and G: E = 2G(1+σ) = 3B(1−2σ).
  • σ = 0.5 means incompressible (volume is conserved); σ = 0 means no lateral change (cork).
  • ΔV/V = ε(1 − 2σ) — volume change during stretching depends only on σ and strain.
Summary

Poisson's ratio σ = −(lateral strain)/(longitudinal strain) is a dimensionless material constant between 0 and 0.5 describing how a body thins when stretched. It links the elastic moduli through E = 2G(1+σ) = 3B(1−2σ). σ = 0.5 corresponds to an incompressible material, while cork (σ ≈ 0) barely changes width.

Special Notes
  • Remember the minus sign in σ = −(lateral)/(longitudinal) — it makes typical σ positive.
  • σ is dimensionless — do not attach units.
  • For steel, σ ≈ 0.25–0.3; for rubber, σ ≈ 0.5; for cork, σ ≈ 0.
  • Volume is conserved when σ = 0.5 (ΔV/V = 0).
  • NEB often asks to compute σ using E = 2G(1+σ) given E and G.
