Newton's Law of Cooling — Exponential Solution & Graph

Notes

  • Differential equation: dtdθ​=−K′(θ−θ0​), where K′=k/(mc) is the cooling constant.
  • Separation of variables: θ−θ0​dθ​=−K′dt. Integrate both sides: ∫θ−θ0​dθ​=−K′∫dt.
  • Integration result: ln(θ−θ0​)=−K′t+C, where C is the integration constant.
  • Applying initial condition: At t=0, θ=θi​. So C=ln(θi​−θ0​). Therefore: ln(θ−θ0​)=−K′t+ln(θi​−θ0​).
  • Exponential solution: θ(t)−θ0​=(θi​−θ0​)e−K′t​. This is the key result — temperature decays exponentially toward the surrounding temperature.
  • Temperature excess: Let Δθ(t)=θ(t)−θ0​ be the temperature excess. Then Δθ(t)=Δθi​⋅e−K′t. The excess decays exponentially.
  • Half-life of cooling: The time for the temperature excess to halve is t1/2​=K′ln2​≈K′0.693​. After each half-life, the excess is halved again.
  • Time constant: τ=K′1​=kmc​. After time τ, the excess is 1/e≈36.8% of its initial value. This is the characteristic timescale of cooling.
  • Graph of θ vs. t: The curve starts at θi​, decreases rapidly at first (steep slope = large dθ/dt), and gradually flattens as it approaches θ0​ asymptotically. The curve NEVER reaches θ0​ in finite time.
  • Graph of dθ/dt vs. (θ - θ₀): This is a straight line through the origin with slope −K′. It shows the linear relationship at the heart of Newton's law.
  • Graph of ln(θ - θ₀) vs. t: This is a straight line with slope −K′ and intercept ln(θi​−θ0​). This linear form is useful for experimental verification — plot the data and check for linearity.
  • Asymptotic behavior: As t→∞, e−K′t→0, so θ→θ0​. The body approaches thermal equilibrium with its surroundings but never quite reaches it in finite time.
  • Reversibility for heating: If θi​<θ0​ (body starts colder than surroundings), the same equation applies: θ(t)−θ0​=(θi​−θ0​)e−K′t. Since (θi​−θ0​) is negative, θ(t) increases toward θ0​.
  • Numerical example: A body at 80°C in a 20°C room with K′=0.05 min−1. After 10 min: θ=20+60×e−0.5=20+60×0.607=56.4°C. After 20 min: θ=20+60×e−1=20+22.1=42.1°C. After 40 min: θ=20+60×e−2=20+8.1=28.1°C.
  • NEB exam focus: Derive the exponential solution from the differential equation. Sketch the cooling curve. Identify the half-life and time constant. Use the formula to calculate temperatures at given times.

Confusion

  • ❌ The body reaches room temperature in finite time. ✅ The exponential function e−K′t approaches zero asymptotically but never reaches zero. The body gets arbitrarily close to θ0​ but never exactly reaches it.
  • ❌ The cooling curve is a straight line. ✅ The cooling curve θ(t) is an exponential decay, not a straight line. The slope dθ/dt decreases with time. Only the plot of ln(θ−θ0​) vs. t is a straight line.
  • ❌ The half-life depends on the initial temperature. ✅ The half-life t1/2​=ln2/K′ depends ONLY on the cooling constant, not on θi​. Each half-life reduces the excess by half, regardless of the starting excess.
  • ❌ A larger K′ means slower cooling. ✅ A larger K′ means FASTER cooling. K′=k/(mc) — larger k (better heat transfer) or smaller mc (less thermal inertia) gives faster cooling.
  • ❌ The exponential solution only works for cooling, not heating. ✅ The same equation θ(t)−θ0​=(θi​−θ0​)e−K′t works for both. For heating, (θi​−θ0​) is negative, so θ(t) increases toward θ0​.
  • ❌ After one time constant, the body has cooled halfway. ✅ After one time constant τ, the excess is 1/e≈36.8% of its initial value, not 50%. The half-life is t1/2​=τln2≈0.693τ.
  • ❌ You can use the approximate method (average rate) for large temperature differences. ✅ The approximate method ΔtΔθ​=K′(θavg​−θ0​) is only accurate for small temperature intervals. For large intervals, use the exact exponential formula.

Practice

  • A body cools from 80°C to 50°C in 3 min in a 20°C room. Find the time to cool from 50°C to 35°C. First: 30=60e−3K′⇒e−3K′=0.5⇒K′=3ln2​=0.231 min−1. Second interval: 15=30e−0.231t⇒e−0.231t=0.5⇒t=0.231ln2​=3 min. Same time because the ratio of excesses is the same (2:1).
  • A cup of tea at 90°C cools to 70°C in 5 min in a 20°C room. How long to reach 40°C? K′=51​ln5070​=5ln1.4​=0.0673 min−1. Time to 40°C: t=0.06731​ln2070​=14.86×1.253=18.6 min from start.
  • Show that equal successive temperature drops take progressively longer. From 80→60°C: t1​=K′1​ln4060​=K′ln1.5​. From 60→40°C: t2​=K′1​ln2040​=K′ln2​. Since ln2>ln1.5, t2​>t1​. Each equal drop takes longer as the body approaches room temperature.
  • A body takes 10 min to cool from 60°C to 40°C in a 10°C room. Find its temperature after 20 min. K′=101​ln3050​=10ln(5/3)​=0.0511 min−1. After 20 min: θ=10+50×e−0.0511×20=10+50×e−1.022=10+50×0.360=28.0°C.
  • Plot the cooling curve for a body with θi​=90°C, θ0​=25°C, K′=0.1 min−1. Calculate at t = 0, 5, 10, 15, 20 min. t=0: 90°C. t=5: 25+65e−0.5=25+39.5=64.5°C. t=10: 25+65e−1=25+23.9=48.9°C. t=15: 25+65e−1.5=25+14.5=39.5°C. t=20: 25+65e−2=25+8.8=33.8°C.
  • A liquid cools from 75°C to 65°C in 2 min. If K′=0.08 min−1 and θ0​=25°C, verify the result using the exact formula. Exact: 50=10×e−0.08×2=10×e−0.16=10×0.852=8.52. Universal Facts

  • The exponential decay form N=N0​e−λt appears in radioactive decay, RC circuits, population dynamics, and Newton's law of cooling. They are all solutions to the same differential equation: dtdy​=−ky.
  • The half-life concept from radioactive decay is directly analogous to the cooling half-life. In both cases, the "half-life" is independent of the initial amount — it depends only on the decay constant.
  • Leonhard Euler first solved the differential equation of Newton's law of cooling in the 1740s. His work on differential equations laid the foundation for much of classical physics.
  • The exponential function is unique in that its derivative is proportional to itself. This property makes it the natural solution to any process where the rate of change is proportional to the current value.
  • In computing, the "rule of 72" for exponential decay has an analogue for cooling: the time to halve the temperature excess is approximately 0.693/K′. For quick estimates, use t1/2​≈0.7/K′.
