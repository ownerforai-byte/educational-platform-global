Newton's Law of Cooling — Identical Cooling Conditions

Notes

  • Identical conditions defined: Two bodies are cooled under identical conditions when they have the same surface area, same surface texture/emissivity, same shape, and are placed in the same surroundings (same θ0​, same air movement).
  • Equal rate of heat loss: Under identical conditions, the heat-loss constant k is the same for both bodies. Therefore, at the same temperature excess (θ−θ0​): dtdH1​​=dtdH2​​=k(θ−θ0​).
  • Different rate of temperature fall: Since dtdH​=mcdtdθ​, equal heat loss rates give: m1​c1​dtdθ1​​=m2​c2​dtdθ2​​. Therefore: (dθ/dt)2​(dθ/dt)1​​=m1​c1​m2​c2​​​.
  • Key insight: Equal heat loss does NOT mean equal temperature change. The body with the SMALLER heat capacity (mc) experiences a LARGER temperature change for the same heat loss.
  • Practical application — comparing specific heats: If two liquids are cooled under identical conditions and you measure their rates of temperature fall at the same temperature, you can find the ratio of their specific heats: c2​c1​​=(dθ/dt)1​(dθ/dt)2​​×m1​m2​​.
  • Equal temperature fall: If two bodies show the SAME rate of temperature fall under identical conditions, their heat capacities must be equal: m1​c1​=m2​c2​. This is the basis for comparing substances.
  • Method of cooling experiment: (1) Take equal masses of two liquids (water and an unknown liquid) in identical calorimeters. (2) Heat both to the same initial temperature. (3) Record the time for each to cool through the same temperature interval. (4) Use t1​(m1​c1​+W)c1​​=t2​(m2​c2​+W)c2​​ to find the unknown c2​.
  • Water as reference: Since water's specific heat is known (cw​=4186 J/kg⋅K), it serves as the reference substance. If liquid X takes time tx​ and water takes time tw​ to cool through the same interval: cw​cx​​=tx​tw​​ (ignoring calorimeter).
  • Calorimeter correction: Always include the water equivalent W of the calorimeter: tx​(mx​cx​+W)cw​​=tw​(mw​cw​+W)cw​​. If W is neglected, the result has systematic error.
  • Why identical conditions matter: If one calorimeter has a larger opening or a different surface finish, k will differ, and the comparison is invalid. The experiment must control for all geometric and surface factors.
  • Surface area effect: A wider calorimeter cools faster (larger A → larger k). If the two calorimeters have different shapes, even with the same material, the cooling rates won't be comparable.
  • NEB exam focus: Understand that identical conditions → equal k. Use t1​m1​c1​​=t2​m2​c2​​ (with calorimeter correction) to compare specific heats. Know why equal heat loss doesn't mean equal temperature change.
  • Real-world example: A copper cup and an aluminum cup of the same size, filled with equal masses of hot water, will cool at the same RATE OF HEAT LOSS (same k, same excess temperature) but the aluminum cup's contents may cool faster in temperature if the cup itself absorbs more heat.

Confusion

  • ❌ Identical cooling conditions mean the bodies cool at the same rate of temperature fall. ✅ Identical conditions mean equal heat-loss constant k. The temperature fall rates can differ if the heat capacities (mc) differ.
  • ❌ If two bodies cool at the same temperature rate, they must be the same material. ✅ They must have the same heat capacity (mc), but different combinations are possible: a small mass of high-c material can equal a large mass of low-c material.
  • ❌ The calorimeter's heat capacity can be ignored in the method of cooling. ✅ The calorimeter absorbs/releases the same heat as the liquid. For accurate results, include W (water equivalent) in the equation.
  • ❌ Two bodies with the same mass always cool at the same rate under identical conditions. ✅ Same mass is not enough — they must also have the same specific heat (same mc). Different materials with the same mass cool at different rates.
  • ❌ The method of cooling requires equal masses of the two liquids. ✅ It requires equal volumes in identical calorimeters. The masses should be measured and used in the calculation. Equal volumes don't guarantee equal masses if densities differ.
  • ❌ Newton's law of cooling applies to the method of cooling experiment without restriction. ✅ The experiment works best when the temperature excess is small (< 30°C). For large temperature ranges, the law is approximate and results may be inaccurate.
  • ❌ A larger calorimeter (more surface area) always cools faster. ✅ For the SAME liquid and same initial temperature, a larger surface area increases k, leading to faster cooling. But if the larger calorimeter also holds more liquid, the effect on temperature rate depends on the ratio k/(mc).

Practice

  • Equal masses of water and liquid X are cooled under identical conditions. Water cools through 10°C in 3 min; liquid X in 5 min. Find cX​. cw​cX​​=tx​tw​​=53​. cX​=53​×4186=2512 J/kg⋅K. (Close to ethanol: 2440 J/kg·K).

Solving gives co​≈2000 J/kg⋅K (more reasonable).
  • A body cools from 60°C to 40°C in 8 min. Another body of half the heat capacity cools under identical conditions through the same interval. How long does it take? Since K′∝1/(mc), the body with half mc has double K′. The cooling time is halved: t=4 min. (Using exact formula: t=K′1​ln2040​=K′ln2​. Half mc → double K′ → half time.)
  • In a method of cooling experiment, water (c=4200) and an unknown liquid both take 5 min to cool from 65°C to 55°C in a 10 g calorimeter (W=8 g) with 100 g of liquid. Find cliquid​. (mw​cw​+Wcw​)/tw​=(ml​cl​+Wcw​)/tl​. Since times are equal and masses equal: cl​=cw​=4200 J/kg⋅K. The liquids have the same specific heat.
  • Why must the initial temperatures of both liquids be the same in a method of cooling experiment? Because the cooling rate depends on (θ−θ0​). If initial temperatures differ, the rate of heat loss differs even under identical conditions, making comparison invalid. Both must be at the same starting point.
  • Two spheres of the same material but different radii are heated to the same temperature and cooled. Which cools faster in temperature? The smaller sphere has smaller mass (∝r3) but smaller surface area (∝r2). K′=k/(mc)∝r2/r3=1/r. Smaller radius → larger K′ → faster cooling. The smaller sphere cools faster.

Universal Facts

  • The method of cooling was historically one of the first quantitative techniques for comparing specific heats. Before precise thermometers were available, timing cooling rates was a practical way to compare thermal properties.
  • In industrial settings, the method of cooling is used to compare the thermal properties of different materials quickly. For example, quality control in metallurgy uses cooling curves to verify alloy composition.
  • The assumption of "identical conditions" is crucial — in practice, slight differences in calorimeter shape, surface finish, or air current can introduce significant errors. Careful experimental design minimizes these.
  • The relationship c2​c1​​=t1​t2​​ (for equal masses) is a direct consequence of energy conservation. It shows that the product mc⋅(dθ/dt) is constant under identical cooling conditions.
  • Modern thermal analyzers (DSC — differential scanning calorimeters) use a refined version of this principle: they measure the heat flow difference between a sample and a reference as both are heated/cooled at a controlled rate.
