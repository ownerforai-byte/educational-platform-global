Relative Velocity

Notes

  • Definition of relative velocity: The velocity of an object B as measured from the reference frame of another moving object A. It is the velocity of B relative to A.
  • Same direction (one-dimensional): If car A moves at velocity v_A and car B at v_B in the same direction, the velocity of B relative to A is v_{B/A} = v_B - v_A. If v_B > v_A, B moves ahead relative to A; if v_B < v_A, B falls behind.
  • Opposite directions (one-dimensional): If A and B move toward each other with speeds v_A and v_B, the relative speed (magnitude of relative velocity) is v_{rel} = v_A + v_B. The direction of v_{B/A} is toward A if B is moving toward A.
  • Vector form (two/three dimensions): \vec{v}_{B/A} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A. This is the velocity of B in the ground frame minus the velocity of A in the ground frame. In components: (v_{B/A})_x = v_{Bx} - v_{Ax}, etc.
  • Relative velocity in 2D — Rain example: If rain falls vertically downward at speed v_r and a person runs horizontally at speed v_p, the rain appears to come at the person at an angle heta where tan heta = v_p / v_r. The apparent speed is \sqrt{v_r^2 + v_p^2}.
  • River crossing (boat relative to water vs. ground): Let \vec{v}_{bw} be the boat's velocity relative to the water (its heading and speed), and \vec{v}_{wg} be the water's velocity relative to ground (current). Then the boat's ground velocity is \vec{v}_{bg} = \vec{v}_{bw} + \vec{v}_{wg}. To reach a point directly across, the boat must angle upstream.
  • Relative velocity of approach/separation: For two objects approaching, the rate at which their separation decreases is v_{rel} = v_A + v_B (if opposite directions) or |v_B - v_A| (if same direction). For collision problems, relative velocity is crucial.
  • Frame of reference transformation: To transform from frame A to frame B, subtract the velocity of frame A: \vec{v}' = \vec{v} - \vec{v}_A. The laws of physics (Newton's laws) are the same in all inertial frames (Galilean relativity).
  • Galilean velocity addition: In classical mechanics, velocities add vectorially: \vec{v}_{B/C} = \vec{v}_{B/A} + \vec{v}_{A/C}. This chain rule allows finding velocity of B relative to C via an intermediate frame A.
  • Relative motion on an incline: If a block slides down an incline at speed v relative to the incline, and the incline itself moves (e.g., on a truck), the block's ground velocity is the vector sum of its velocity relative to the incline and the incline's velocity relative to ground.
  • Closing speed vs. relative velocity: Closing speed is the rate at which the distance between two objects decreases. For objects moving directly toward each other, closing speed = |v_A| + |v_B|. This is the magnitude of the relative velocity only if they are on a collision course.

Confusion

  • Relative velocity is NOT the same as the difference in speeds. For two cars moving at 60 km/h and 40 km/h in the SAME direction, the relative speed is 20 km/h, NOT 100 km/h. For opposite directions, the relative speed is the SUM (100 km/h).
  • When subtracting vectors, the ORDER MATTERS: \vec{v}_{B/A} = \vec{v}_B - \vec{v}_A. This is the velocity of B AS SEEN BY A. \vec{v}_{A/B} = \vec{v}_A - \vec{v}_B = -\vec{v}_{B/A}. They are equal in magnitude but opposite in direction.
  • A common mistake in river problems: students add the boat's speed to the current speed as scalars. You must add them as VECTORS: the boat's velocity relative to water PLUS water's velocity relative to ground. The angle matters!
  • In the rain problem, the rain does NOT change its actual velocity — it still falls vertically. Only the APPARENT velocity (relative to the moving person) is angled. The person tilts their umbrella forward to block the apparent rain.
  • Galilean relativity breaks down at speeds close to the speed of light (c). Einstein's special relativity replaces it with the Lorentz velocity addition formula: v = (v1 + v2)/(1 + v1v2/c^2). For everyday speeds (<< c), this reduces to Galilean addition.

Practice

  • Two trains A and B are moving on parallel tracks. Train A moves east at 60 km/h. Train B moves east at 80 km/h. Find the velocity of B relative to A, and the velocity of A relative to B. Solution: v_{B/A} = 80 - 60 = 20 km/h east (B moves ahead of A at 20 km/h). v_{A/B} = 60 - 80 = -20 km/h (20 km/h west, meaning A falls behind B).
  • Rain is falling vertically at 30 m/s. A man runs horizontally at 10 m/s. At what angle from vertical does the rain appear to fall? What is the apparent speed? Solution: tan heta = v_man/v_rain = 10/30 = 1/3. heta = an^{-1}(1/3) = 18.4° from vertical. Apparent speed = \sqrt{30^2 + 10^2} = \sqrt{1000} = 31.6 m/s.
  • A boat's speed in still water is 5 m/s. The river flows at 3 m/s east. The boat wants to reach a point directly across the river (north). (a) At what angle must the boat head? (b) What is its ground speed? (c) Time to cross a 100 m wide river? Solution: (a) Boat must cancel eastward current: 5 sin heta = 3 → heta = 36.9° west of north. (b) v_ground = 5 cos heta = 5 × 0.8 = 4 m/s north. (c) t = width/v_ground = 100/4 = 25 s.
  • Two particles move on the x-axis. Particle A: x_A(t) = 2t + 1. Particle B: x_B(t) = -3t + 10. Find their relative velocity at t = 2 s. Solution: v_A = dx_A/dt = 2 m/s. v_B = dx_B/dt = -3 m/s. v_{B/A} = v_B - v_A = -3 - 2 = -5 m/s. The distance between them decreases at 5 m/s.
  • An airplane flies at 400 km/h relative to air. The wind blows at 100 km/h from the west (toward east). What is the plane's ground speed and direction if it heads (a) north? (b) northeast? Solution: (a) v_ground = \sqrt{400^2 + 100^2} = 412 km/h. Direction: tan heta = 100/400 = 1/4 → heta = 14° east of north. (b) v_plane = 400/\sqrt{2} north + 400/\sqrt{2} east. v_wind = 100 east. v_ground = (400/\sqrt{2}) north + (400/\sqrt{2} + 100) east = 283 north + 383 east. Speed = \sqrt{283^2 + 383^2} = 476 km/h.

Universal Facts

  • Galilean relativity (velocity addition v' = v - u) was the standard until Einstein's special relativity (1905) showed it is only an approximation valid for v ≪ c. At everyday speeds, the error is completely negligible.
  • Relative velocity is a fundamental concept in all of physics — from classical mechanics to electromagnetism (magnetic fields are electric fields viewed from a moving frame) to special relativity.
  • The principle of Galilean relativity: the laws of motion are identical in all inertial frames. If you toss a ball up in a moving train (constant velocity), it lands back in your hand — the ball has the train's forward velocity added to its motion.
  • In collision problems, the relative velocity of approach is often more useful than individual velocities. For elastic collisions, the relative velocity of separation equals the relative velocity of approach (in magnitude).
  • The concept of relative velocity explains why we don't feel Earth's orbital speed (30 km/s) or rotation speed (0.46 km/s at equator) — we move with the Earth, so our velocity relative to Earth is zero. Only in non-inertial frames (acceleration) do we feel fictitious forces.
