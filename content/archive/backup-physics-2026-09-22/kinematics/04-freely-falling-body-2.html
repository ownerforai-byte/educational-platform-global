Notes

  • Type 2 — alternate treatment: this version works free fall entirely through the equations of motion with sign conventions and the up-and-down symmetry argument — the pattern NEB numericals follow.
  • Definition: a freely falling body moves under gravity ALONE — no air resistance, no push after release. All bodies fall with the same acceleration g≈9.8 m/s² (take 10 m/s² in numericals) independent of mass — Galileo's famous result.
  • Sign convention method: choose upward positive. Then for a body projected up at u: v=u−gt, h=ut−21​gt2, v2=u2−2gh. The SAME equations with u=0 describe a dropped body; with the throw downward, u enters negative.
  • Up–down symmetry: a body thrown up with speed u returns to the launch point with the same speed (downward); time up = time down =u/g; total flight T=2u/g. Air resistance would break this symmetry — its absence is the point of 'free' fall.
  • Key instants for a body thrown up: at the top, v=0 but a=g downward (never zero); height reached hmax​=u2/2g; velocities at equal heights going up and down are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
  • Dropped from a height h: v=gt, h=21​gt2, v=2gh​. Time to fall: t=2h/g​. Falling 1:3:5:7 distances each second (Galileo's odd-number rule).
  • Bodies with initial downward or upward motion from a height (e.g. a ball thrown down at 5 m/s): v2=u2+2gh with BOTH terms positive when down is the direction of travel — the third equation handles every case once signs are set.
  • Relative fall problems: a ball dropped from a descending/ascending lift, or two balls released with a time gap — write each body's position yi​(t) with the same origin, then use their difference. E.g. separation between a dropped ball and one thrown down 1 s later grows as Δy=21​g+(u)t.
  • NEB exam focus: state that g is mass-independent (with the Galileo/feather-coin argument); compute maximum height, time of flight, velocity on impact; apply up–down symmetry; solve dropped/thrown-from-lift problems with one consistent sign convention.

Confusion

  • ❌ 'Heavier bodies fall faster.' ✅ In free fall ALL bodies have the same acceleration g; observed differences come from air resistance — demonstrated by the Apollo 15 hammer–feather drop on the Moon.
  • ❌ 'At the top of the flight, acceleration is zero because the body stops.' ✅ v=0 but a=g downward at ALL points of the flight. If a were zero at the top, the body would stay there!
  • ❌ Using g=−10 m/s² AND taking h positive upward in h=21​gt2. ✅ Put the sign in ONCE: either g negative with up positive, or down positive with g positive — never both conventions mixed.
  • ❌ 'A body thrown up takes longer to rise than to fall.' ✅ Without air resistance the times are EXACTLY equal (u/g each way); with air resistance the fall takes LONGER, not the rise.
  • ❌ 'Velocity on returning to the thrower is less than the launch speed.' ✅ Equal in magnitude (symmetry). Only air resistance or bounce changes it.
  • ❌ Treating 'dropped' and 'thrown down' identically. ✅ Dropped means u=0; thrown down means u=0 downward — the equations differ by that initial term.

Practice

  • A stone is thrown up at 29.4 m/s. Find (a) max height, (b) time to top, (c) total flight time (g=9.8). — (a) 44.1 m; (b) 3 s; (c) 6 s.
  • A ball dropped from a tower takes 2.5 s to land. Find the tower height and impact speed (g=10). — h=31.25 m; v=25 m/s.
  • A body is thrown down at 4 m/s from a 50 m building. When does it land and at what speed? — 50=4t+5t2⇒t=2.78 s; v=4+10(2.78)≈31.8 m/s.
  • A ball thrown up passes a window at 10 m/s going up. When does it pass the window again (same speed downward)? — By symmetry it returns with 10 m/s; time gap =2u/g=2 s.
  • From a lift descending at 4 m/s, a coin is dropped. Relative to the lift floor, describe its motion; relative to the ground, find its acceleration. — Relative to lift: falls with g from rest; relative to ground: u=4 m/s down, a=g down.

Universal Facts

  • Apollo 15 commander David Scott dropped a hammer and falcon feather on the Moon (1971): both hit the dust together — the most-watched verification that g is mass-independent.
  • Vacuum-tube 'Guinea and feather' demonstrations in classrooms reproduce the same result; air resistance, not gravity, distinguishes everyday falls.
  • g varies over Earth: about 9.78 m/s² at the equator and 9.83 m/s² at the poles — precise free-fall experiments are used by geophysicists to survey underground density (gravimetry).
  • Skydivers reach terminal velocity (~55 m/s belly-down) only because air resistance grows with speed; in free fall proper, speed would grow by 9.8 m/s every second without limit.

Examples

  • Example 1 (dropped): a mango falls from a 20 m tree — t=2h/g​=4​=2 s; strikes at v=2(10)(20)​=20 m/s.
  • Example 2 (thrown up): a ball goes up at 15 m/s — top after 1.5 s, hmax​=11.25 m, back in hand after 3 s at 15 m/s (symmetry).
  • Example 3 (through a window): a ball dropped past a 2 m window takes 0.1 s — speed at window top ≈0.12​−21​(10)(0.1)≈19.5 m/s, hence fall height ≈19 m.
  • Example 4 (two balls): one dropped and one thrown down at 10 m/s from the same tower stay 10t apart — both gain speed at g; the throw only adds a constant offset.
  • Example 5 (lift): a lift falls freely — objects inside float relative to the cabin because both cabin and objects have a=g (weightlessness in free fall).

Practice Questions

  • Q1. Prove that the time of ascent equals the time of descent for a body projected vertically upward (neglect air resistance).
  • Q2. A stone dropped from a bridge strikes the water in 3 s. Find the bridge height and the striking velocity (g=10).
  • Q3. A ball is thrown upward and rises 45 m. Find its initial speed and total time of flight.
  • Q4. Show that a body thrown up returns to the starting point with the same speed, using v2=u2−2gh.
  • Q5. A coin is dropped from a lift moving up at constant velocity 6 m/s. Find its acceleration relative to (a) the lift, (b) the ground.

Formulas

  • Dropped from rest: v=gt, h=21​gt2, v=2gh​
  • Thrown up (up positive): v=u−gt, h=ut−21​gt2, v2=u2−2gh
  • Maximum height: hmax​=2gu2​ (at v=0)
  • Times: tup​=tdown​=gu​, total flight T=g2u​
  • n-th second fall distance: sn​=2g​(2n−1) — ratio 1:3:5:7…

Key Points

  • Free fall acceleration g is independent of mass, size and material.
  • At every instant of the flight, a=g downward — including the top (v=0, a=0).
  • Up–down symmetry: equal speeds at equal heights, equal times up and down.
  • Use ONE sign convention throughout a problem; put g's sign in consistently.
  • 'Dropped' = u=0; 'thrown' = u=0 — the equations differ by the ut term.
  • Air resistance is neglected in free fall; it is what produces terminal velocity in reality.

Summary

Free fall is uniform acceleration g with no air resistance: all bodies fall alike. With one consistent sign convention, the three equations of motion give every free-fall result — dropped bodies (h=21​gt2, v=2gh​), bodies thrown up (hmax​=u2/2g, T=2u/g), and mixed cases. The up–down symmetry (equal times and speeds) is the key structural insight, and v=0 with a=g at the top is the classic conceptual test point.

Special Notes

  • Note 1: Take g=9.8 m/s² unless the question says 10 — and say which you used.
  • Note 2: For a body thrown up, do not put t=T/2 anywhere except the top; the position equation is valid for the whole flight.
  • Note 3: Impact-speed questions: v=2gh​ works even when t is unknown — fastest route.
  • Note 4: Two-body fall problems: write y1​(t) and y2​(t) with one origin; separation is their difference — accelerations add no relative change if both are g.

Important Statements

  • Statement 1: In free fall, every body accelerates at g regardless of its mass — gravity acts identically on all masses.
  • Statement 2: At the highest point of vertical flight the velocity is zero but the acceleration is still g downward.
  • Statement 3: Neglecting air resistance, the time of ascent equals the time of descent, and the body returns with its launch speed.
  • Statement 4: A body falling from rest covers distances in the ratio 1 : 3 : 5 : 7 in successive equal time intervals.

Important Notes

  • Note 1: Fix one positive direction per problem; the sign of g follows from it (up positive ⇒ a=−g).
  • Note 2: v=2gh​ gives impact speed without needing time — quickest route for drop problems.
  • Note 3: Inside a freely falling lift, objects appear weightless — relative acceleration between cabin and objects is zero.
  • Note 4: Real falls approach terminal velocity because air resistance grows with speed; the free-fall model ignores this.

Exam Short Tricks

  • Trick 1: 'Maximum height' ⇒ remember u2/2g; 'time to top' ⇒ u/g; both come from v=0 in the first and third equations.
  • Trick 2: Total flight time of a return throw: T=2u/g — read the word 'returns' and write it instantly.
  • Trick 3: For drops, ratio the times: heights fallen in t, 2t, 3t are in ratio 1:4:9; per-second distances 1:3:5.
  • Trick 4: Sign shortcut: throw up ⇒ a enters negative; throw down or drop ⇒ a and motion share sign — no algebra mistakes.
  • Trick 5: Ball dropped from a moving lift: relative to the lift it simply falls from rest (g) — use the lift frame to halve the work.

Exam Notes

  • Exam Tip 1: NEB loves the ascent = descent proof — quote v=u−gt at top and bottom points and use symmetry.
  • Exam Tip 2: In 'velocity on reaching ground' questions, prefer v2=u2+2gh; finding t first wastes a step and invites rounding error.
  • Exam Tip 3: Always sketch the motion (up, top, down) and mark v=0 at the top — diagrams earn marks and prevent sign slips.
  • Exam Tip 4: State 'neglecting air resistance' in theory answers — it defines the free-fall model.

Important Concepts

  • Concept 2: Sign-convention discipline — one axis, one consistent g sign per problem.

  • Concept 4: Up–down symmetry — equal times and speeds at equal heights.
  • Concept 5: Odd-number/1:4:9 patterns for falls from rest — quick checks on fall distances.
