Power of a Lens

Backup Physics 2026 09 22

Relevance

Notes

  • Definition: P=1/f, where f is in metres. Units: dioptres (D).
  • Convex lens: f is positive, so P is positive. A +2.0 D lens has f=0.5m=50cm.
  • Concave lens: f is negative, so P is negative. A -4.0 D lens has f=−0.25m=−25cm.
  • Combination in contact: Ptotal​=P1​+P2​+P3​+…. Powers add algebraically because thin lenses in contact have a combined focal length given by 1/feq​=1/f1​+1/f2​.
  • Optician's prescription: a prescription of +3.0 D means a converging lens of focal length 33.3 cm for hyperopia (farsightedness); -2.5 D means a diverging lens of focal length -40 cm for myopia (nearsightedness).
  • Dioptre definition: One dioptre (1 D) is the power of a lens with a focal length of exactly 1 metre. The dioptre is a reciprocal metre (extm−1).
  • Lensmaker's formula and power: For a thin lens in air, P=(n−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​), where n is the refractive index of lens material relative to air, and R1​, R2​ are radii of curvature (positive if centre of curvature is on the outgoing side). This shows power depends on both material and surface curvatures.
  • Power in a medium other than air: If a lens of refractive index nlens​ is immersed in a medium of refractive index nmedium​, its power becomes Pmedium​=(nmedium​nlens​​−1)(R1​1​−R2​1​). For example, a glass lens (n=1.5) in water (n=1.33) has only about 15% of its power in air — it becomes much weaker.
  • Equivalent power of separated thin lenses: For two thin lenses separated by distance d (in metres), Peq​=P1​+P2​−dP1​P2​. The term −dP1​P2​ accounts for the separation; when d→0, this reduces to simple algebraic sum.
  • Afocal systems (telescopes): When Peq​=0, the combination has infinite focal length — it produces parallel output for parallel input. This occurs when d=1/P1​+1/P2​=f1​+f2​ for two converging lenses (astronomical telescope) or when a converging and diverging lens are separated appropriately (Galilean telescope).
  • Accommodation of the eye: The human eye changes its power by altering the curvature of the crystalline lens. Total power ranges from approximately +58 D (relaxed, focused at infinity) to +70 D (maximum accommodation, focused at near point ~25 cm). The cornea contributes ~+43 D and the lens ~+15 D to +27 D.
  • Presbyopia: Age-related loss of accommodation. The near point recedes with age (10 cm at age 10, 25 cm at 40, 50 cm at 50, 100 cm at 60). Corrected by adding a converging lens (reading glasses) to supply the missing accommodation power.
  • Contact lenses vs spectacles: Contact lenses sit on the cornea (vertex distance ~0), while spectacles are ~12-15 mm in front. The effective power differs: Pspectacle​=1−dPcontact​Pcontact​​ where d is vertex distance in metres. For high prescriptions (>4 D), this difference is clinically significant.
  • Cylindrical power and astigmatism: Astigmatism requires different powers in two perpendicular meridians. Prescription written as sphere/cylinder × axis, e.g., −2.00/−0.75×180. Total power varies with meridian: P(θ)=Psphere​+Pcylinder​sin2(θ−axis).

Confusion

  • Power is the reciprocal of focal length — Confusing power with focal length is common. Remember: P=1/f (with f in metres). A lens of power +2 D has f=0.5 m, not 2 m. Always convert cm to m before taking the reciprocal.
  • Sign of power for concave lens — A concave (diverging) lens has negative focal length and therefore negative power. Students often forget the minus sign. A prescription of -2.0 D means a diverging lens of focal length -50 cm.
  • Adding powers vs adding focal lengths — For lenses in contact, powers add algebraically (P=P1​+P2​), but focal lengths do NOT add (f=f1​+f2​). The correct relation for focal lengths is 1/f=1/f1​+1/f2​.
  • Lens power in different media — A lens's power changes when immersed in a medium other than air. A glass lens in water has much lower power because the relative refractive index nglass​/nwater​ is much smaller than nglass​/nair​. It does NOT retain its air power.
  • Vertex distance effect — The power of a spectacle lens is not the same as a contact lens of the same prescription if vertex distance is non-zero. For high powers, the effective power at the cornea differs significantly. Contact lens prescriptions are typically weaker (less minus / more plus) than spectacle prescriptions for myopia.

Practice

  • A convex lens has a focal length of 25 cm. What is its power in dioptres? — Solution: f=0.25 m, so P=1/0.25=+4.0 D.
  • A concave lens of power -6.0 D is placed in contact with a convex lens of power +4.0 D. Find the focal length of the combination. — Solution: Ptotal​=−6.0+4.0=−2.0 D. f=1/P=1/(−2.0)=−0.5 m = -50 cm. The combination behaves as a diverging lens.
  • A thin glass lens (n=1.5) has radii of curvature R1​=+20 cm and R2​=−20 cm. Find its power in air and in water (n=4/3). — Solution: In air, P=(1.5−1)(1/0.20−1/(−0.20))=0.5(5+5)=+5.0 D. In water, nrel​=1.5/1.333=1.125. Pwater​=(1.125−1)(5+5)=0.125×10=+1.25 D. Power in water is 1/4 of power in air.
  • Two thin lenses of powers +3.0 D and +5.0 D are separated by 10 cm. Find the equivalent power. — Solution: d=0.10 m. Peq​=3.0+5.0−(0.10)(3.0)(5.0)=8.0−1.5=+6.5 D.
  • A myopic person has a far point of 40 cm. What power spectacle lens (at vertex distance 15 mm) is needed to see distant objects clearly? — Solution: The lens must form a virtual image at the far point. For object at infinity, f=−0.40 m (spectacle plane). P=−2.50 D. At cornea (15 mm closer), the effective power needed is Pcornea​=P/(1−dP)=−2.50/(1−0.015(−2.50))=−2.50/1.0375≈−2.41 D. So contact lens prescription would be about -2.41 D.

Universal Facts

  • The dioptre is the only SI-derived unit named after a person (Johannes Kepler's 'dioptrice'), though the unit itself is simply m−1.
  • The human eye's total optical power is approximately +60 D, with the cornea providing ~+43 D (fixed) and the crystalline lens providing ~+17 D (variable via accommodation).
  • In optometry, the 'spherical equivalent' of a prescription with cylinder is SE=sphere+21​cylinder. This represents the average power across all meridians.
  • A Fresnel lens achieves the same optical power as a conventional lens but with a fraction of the thickness by breaking the surface into concentric annular sections — each section provides the required refraction at its local slope.
  • The power of a lens is additive for thin lenses in contact, but for thick lenses or separated lenses, the concept of 'principal planes' must be used. The effective power is then P=n/feff​ where feff​ is measured from the principal planes, not the lens vertices.
