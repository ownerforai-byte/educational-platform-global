Notes

  • Series combination: same charge Q on each capacitor. 1/Ceq​=1/C1​+1/C2​+…. Equivalent capacitance is less than the smallest individual capacitance.
  • Parallel combination: same voltage V across each capacitor. Ceq​=C1​+C2​+…. Equivalent capacitance is greater than the largest individual capacitance.
  • Why series reduces capacitance: the effective plate separation increases because the dielectrics are stacked, weakening the overall field.
  • Why parallel increases capacitance: the effective plate area increases because the plates are side by side, allowing more charge storage at the same voltage.
  • Mixed combinations: reduce step by step. Identify simple series or parallel groups, find their equivalent, and repeat.
  • Voltage division in series: for two capacitors in series, V1​=V⋅C2​/(C1​+C2​) and V2​=V⋅C1​/(C1​+C2​). The smaller capacitor gets the larger voltage.
  • Charge division in parallel: for two capacitors in parallel, Q1​=Q⋅C1​/(C1​+C2​) and Q2​=Q⋅C2​/(C1​+C2​). The larger capacitor stores more charge.
  • Energy in combinations: total energy is conserved but may redistribute. In series, the smaller capacitor stores more energy (U=Q2/(2C)). In parallel, the larger capacitor stores more energy (U=CV2/2).
  • Three equal capacitors C in series: Ceq​=C/3. Three equal capacitors in parallel: Ceq​=3C. These are common benchmark results.
  • Star-to-delta transformation: for complex networks that are neither purely series nor parallel, star-delta transformations can simplify the circuit, analogous to resistor networks.

Confusion

  • In series, the equivalent capacitance is always less than any individual capacitor. Do not confuse this with resistors in series, where resistance adds up. Capacitors in series combine like resistors in parallel.
  • In parallel, the equivalent capacitance is always greater than any individual capacitor. This is the same rule as resistors in parallel — the rules are reversed between the two.
  • In a series combination, charge is the same on every capacitor, but voltage divides inversely with capacitance. The smaller capacitor has the larger voltage drop.
  • In a parallel combination, voltage is the same across every capacitor, but charge divides directly with capacitance. The larger capacitor stores more charge.
  • When capacitors with different initial charges are connected, charge redistributes until voltages equalize. Energy is not conserved in this process — some is lost as heat or radiation.

Practice

  • Three capacitors 2μF, 4μF, and 6μF are connected in series across a 12 V battery. Find the equivalent capacitance, charge on each, and voltage across each. Solution: 1/Ceq​=1/2+1/4+1/6=6/12+3/12+2/12=11/12, so Ceq​=12/11≈1.09μF. Charge Q=Ceq​V=1.09×12=13.09μC. V1​=13.09/2=6.55 V, V2​=13.09/4=3.27 V, V3​=13.09/6=2.18 V. Check: 6.55+3.27+2.18=12 V.
  • Two capacitors 3μF and 6μF are connected in parallel across 12 V. Find the total capacitance, total charge, and charge on each. Solution: Ceq​=3+6=9μF. Total charge Q=9×12=108μC. Q1​=3×12=36μC, Q2​=6×12=72μC.
  • Find the equivalent capacitance between points A and B when a 2μF capacitor is in series with a parallel combination of 3μF and 6μF. Solution: Parallel part: Cp​=3+6=9μF. Series with 2μF: 1/Ceq​=1/2+1/9=9/18+2/18=11/18, so Ceq​=18/11≈1.64μF.
  • Two capacitors C1​=4μF and C2​=12μF are charged separately to 12 V, then disconnected. They are connected positive-to-negative. Find the final voltage and energy loss. Solution: Initial charges: Q1​=48μC, Q2​=144μC. Net charge =144−48=96μC. Final V=96/(4+12)=6 V. Initial energy: 21​(4×144+12×144)×10−6=1.152×10−3 J. Final energy: 21​(16)×36×10−6=0.288×10−3 J. Energy loss: 0.864 mJ.
  • Five identical capacitors each of capacitance C are connected in a bridge configuration between points A and B. Find the equivalent capacitance if the bridge is balanced. Solution: In a balanced bridge, the middle capacitor carries no current and can be ignored. The equivalent is Ceq​=C.

Universal Facts

  • Capacitors in series combine inversely, just like resistors in parallel. Capacitors in parallel combine directly, just like resistors in series. The rules are always opposite.
  • In a series combination, the same charge resides on every capacitor, but the voltage across each is inversely proportional to its capacitance.
  • In a parallel combination, the same voltage appears across every capacitor, but the charge on each is directly proportional to its capacitance.
  • When connecting capacitors of different initial voltages, always check polarity. Connecting positive to positive is safe; connecting positive to negative causes charge cancellation and energy loss.
  • The energy stored in a capacitor network is the sum of energies in individual capacitors: Utotal​=∑21​Ci​Vi2​.
