Capacitance and Parallel Plate Capacitor

Notes

  • Structure: two parallel conducting plates separated by a small distance, with air or dielectric between them.
  • Capacitance: C=ϵ0​A/d for air; with dielectric, C=κϵ0​A/d, where κ is the dielectric constant.
  • Effect of dielectric: inserting a dielectric increases capacitance by factor κ because the material polarizes, reducing the effective field and allowing more charge for the same voltage.
  • Effect of separation: increasing d decreases C because the field weakens and less charge can be stored for the same voltage.
  • Effect of area: increasing A increases C because more charge can be distributed over the larger plates at the same surface charge density.
  • Derivation from Gauss's law: the field between plates is E=σ/ϵ0​=Q/(ϵ0​A). The potential difference is V=Ed=Qd/(ϵ0​A). Hence C=Q/V=ϵ0​A/d.
  • Edge effects: the formula C=ϵ0​A/d assumes d≪A​ so edge fringing is negligible. For large separations, the actual capacitance is slightly less.
  • Force between plates: the plates attract with force F=Q2/(2ϵ0​A)=21​QE=21​ϵ0​AE2. This can be derived from the energy U=Q2d/(2ϵ0​A).
  • Practical capacitor construction: real capacitors use rolled or stacked plates to maximize area in a compact volume, with dielectric materials like plastic film, ceramic, or mica between them.
  • Variable capacitor: by rotating plates to change the effective area A, the capacitance can be varied. Used in radio tuning circuits.
  • Unit check: [ϵ0​]=F/m, [A]=m2, [d]=m, so [ϵ0​A/d]=F, confirming the formula is dimensionally consistent.

Confusion

  • The capacitance C=ϵ0​A/d applies only for a parallel plate capacitor with uniform field. It does not apply to arbitrary conductor shapes — each geometry has its own capacitance formula.
  • Inserting a dielectric while the capacitor is connected to a battery keeps V constant but increases Q and C. Disconnecting the battery first keeps Q constant but increases V if the dielectric is removed.
  • The formula uses the separation d between plates, not the thickness of the plates themselves. The conducting material of the plates does not contribute to the capacitance.
  • A larger plate area does not mean a larger charge capacity at all voltages — it means more charge can be stored at the same voltage. The voltage rating depends on the dielectric breakdown, not the area.
  • Dielectric constant κ is dimensionless. Do not confuse it with the absolute permittivity ϵ=κϵ0​, which has units of F/m.

Practice

  • A parallel plate capacitor has plates of area 200cm2 separated by 2mm of air. Find its capacitance. Solution: A=200×10−4=0.02m2, d=2×10−3 m. C=ϵ0​A/d=8.85×10−12×0.02/0.002=8.85×10−11 F ≈88.5 pF.
  • The same capacitor above is connected to a 100 V battery. Find the charge on each plate and the energy stored. Solution: Q=CV=8.85×10−11×100=8.85×10−9 C =8.85 nC. U=21​CV2=21​×8.85×10−11×10000=4.43×10−7 J.
  • A dielectric slab of κ=5 and thickness 1mm is inserted between the plates (separation 2mm). Find the new capacitance. Solution: The effective capacitance is C=ϵ0​A/(d−t+t/κ)=8.85×10−12×0.02/(0.002−0.001+0.001/5)=8.85×10−12×0.02/0.0012=1.475×10−10 F ≈147.5 pF.
  • Two parallel plates each of area 0.01m2 are separated by 1mm. The capacitor is charged to 300 V and disconnected. A dielectric with κ=3 is inserted. Find the new voltage. Solution: C0​=8.85×10−12×0.01/0.001=8.85×10−11 F. Q=2.655×10−8 C. C′=3C0​=2.655×10−10 F. V′=Q/C′=100 V.
  • Find the plate separation of a 1μF parallel plate capacitor with plate area 1m2. Solution: d=ϵ0​A/C=8.85×10−12×1/10−6=8.85×10−6 m ≈8.85μm.

Universal Facts

  • The parallel plate capacitor is the simplest model for understanding capacitance. Its capacitance depends only on geometry (A and d) and the dielectric material, not on the applied voltage or stored charge.
  • The electric field between parallel plates is uniform (ignoring edge effects): E=σ/ϵ0​=Q/(ϵ0​A), directed from the positive to the negative plate.
  • When a dielectric is inserted while the battery remains connected, the battery supplies extra charge to maintain the same voltage, increasing the stored energy.
  • The maximum voltage a parallel plate capacitor can handle is limited by dielectric breakdown: Vmax​=Ebreakdown​×d.
  • The force between the plates is attractive and independent of the sign of the charge: F=Q2/(2ϵ0​A). This force can be measured experimentally to determine ϵ0​.
