Notes

  • Energy stored: U=21​CV2=2CQ2​=21​QV.
  • Source of energy: the energy comes from the work done by the battery in moving charge from one plate to the other against the increasing potential difference.
  • Derivation: dW=VdQ=(Q/C)dQ. Integrating from Q=0 to Q gives W=∫0Q​CQ​dQ=2CQ2​.
  • Energy density: u=AdU​=21​ϵ0​E2. Energy is stored in the electric field throughout the volume between the plates.
  • Why factor 1/2: because the potential difference is not constant while charging; it starts at zero and rises to V, so the average voltage during charging is V/2.
  • Work by battery vs energy stored: the battery supplies energy QV, but only half is stored in the capacitor. The other half is dissipated as heat in the connecting wires (even with ideal wires, this appears during the charging transient).
  • Energy when dielectric is inserted (isolated): if the capacitor is disconnected, Q is constant. U=Q2/(2C). Since C increases by κ, energy decreases to U/κ. The dielectric is pulled in, doing mechanical work.
  • Energy when dielectric is inserted (battery connected): V is constant. U=21​CV2. Since C increases by κ, energy increases to κU. The battery supplies the extra energy.
  • Dimensional check: [U]=[C][V]2=F⋅V2=(C/V)⋅V2=C⋅V=J, confirming energy units.
  • Energy in terms of field: for a parallel plate capacitor, U=21​ϵ0​E2(Ad), where Ad is the volume between plates. This shows energy is spread through the field.
  • Capacitor as energy reservoir: capacitors can release energy very quickly (unlike batteries), making them useful in camera flashes, defibrillators, and pulsed power applications.

Confusion

  • The battery supplies energy QV, but only 21​QV is stored. The missing half is dissipated as heat during charging, even through ideal wires, due to the transient current.
  • Do not use U=QV for capacitor energy — this is the energy the battery supplies, not what is stored. The correct formula is U=21​QV because voltage increases linearly from 0 to V during charging.
  • When a dielectric is inserted into an isolated capacitor, energy decreases (the dielectric is pulled in, gaining kinetic energy). When inserted with the battery connected, energy increases (the battery does extra work).
  • Energy density u=21​ϵ0​E2 applies to any electric field, not just between capacitor plates. It is the energy per unit volume stored in an electric field anywhere in space.
  • The formula U=Q2/(2C) shows that for a fixed charge, smaller capacitance means more stored energy. This seems counterintuitive but is correct — a smaller capacitor has a higher voltage for the same charge.

Practice

  • A 10μF capacitor is charged to 200 V. Find the energy stored and the charge on each plate. Solution: Q=CV=10×10−6×200=2×10−3 C =2 mC. U=21​CV2=21​×10×10−6×40000=0.2 J.
  • A 5μF capacitor charged to 100 V is disconnected. A dielectric with κ=5 is inserted. Find the new energy and explain where the energy went. Solution: Q=500μC (constant). C′=25μF. U′=Q2/(2C′)=25×10−8/(50×10−6)=0.05 J. Original energy U=0.25 J. Energy lost =0.2 J, converted to work done pulling the dielectric in plus heat.
  • Find the energy density in a parallel plate capacitor with plate separation 2 mm and voltage 500 V. Solution: E=V/d=500/0.002=2.5×105 V/m. u=21​ϵ0​E2=21​×8.85×10−12×6.25×1010=0.277 J/m3.
  • Two capacitors C1​=2μF and C2​=4μF are charged to 100 V and 50 V respectively. They are disconnected and connected positive-to-positive. Find the final voltage and energy loss. Solution: Q1​=200μC, Q2​=200μC. Total Q=400μC. Ceq​=6μF. Vf​=400/6=66.7 V. Ui​=21​(2×10000+4×2500)×10−6=0.2 J. Uf​=21​×6×10−6×4444=0.133 J. Loss =67 mJ.
  • A capacitor stores 1 J of energy when charged to 100 V. Find its capacitance. Solution: U=21​CV2, so C=2U/V2=2×1/10000=2×10−4 F =200μF.

Universal Facts

  • Only half the energy supplied by the battery is stored in the capacitor; the other half is dissipated as heat during the charging process, regardless of the resistance in the circuit.
  • The energy density of an electric field u=21​ϵ0​E2 is a universal result — it applies to any electric field in vacuum, not just inside capacitors.
  • When a dielectric is inserted into an isolated (disconnected) capacitor, the stored energy decreases because the dielectric is pulled into the field, converting electrical energy to mechanical work.
  • A capacitor can release its stored energy much faster than a battery can deliver current, which is why capacitors are used in camera flashes and pulsed lasers.
  • The three equivalent expressions U=21​CV2=2CQ2​=21​QV are useful in different situations: use the first when V is known, the second when Q is known, and the third when both are known.
