Questions

1.

A student says: 'A hollow metal sphere carrying charge Q has field E ≠ 0 inside because the charge is right there on the surface.' Is this correct? What is the field inside, and why?

ID

drill-elec-1

Type

concept

Marks

2

Difficulty

medium

Concept

Field inside a conductor

Solution

Incorrect. Inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium the field is exactly zero everywhere. Mobile electrons redistribute within a microsecond until no net force remains on any charge — if E ≠ 0 inside, charges would still move, contradicting equilibrium. The entire charge Q sits on the OUTER surface, and by Gauss's law any Gaussian surface drawn inside encloses zero charge, so flux (and hence field) is zero. The student confuses proximity of charge with field: field comes from NET enclosed charge, not nearby charge.

2.

If the electric field is zero at a point, must the electric potential be zero there too? Give a concrete counterexample.

ID

drill-elec-2

Type

concept

Marks

2

Difficulty

medium

Concept

Potential vs field

Solution

No. Field is the SLOPE of potential (E=−dV/dx), not its value — zero slope does not mean zero height. Counterexample: midway between two equal positive charges, the fields cancel (E=0) but each charge contributes positive potential, so V=2kq/r=0. Conversely, V=0 does not imply E=0 either (any point on the equatorial plane of a dipole has V=0 but nonzero field). Remember: only potential DIFFERENCES and field (slope) drive charges.

3.

A charge outside a closed Gaussian surface: does it contribute to the net flux through the surface? Does it affect the field at points on the surface?

ID

drill-elec-3

Type

concept

Marks

2

Difficulty

easy

Concept

Why flux ignores outside charge

Solution

It contributes ZERO net flux but DOES affect the local field. Every field line from an outside charge that enters the surface must also exit it (lines are continuous — they start/end only on charges), so inward and outward contributions cancel: ∮E⋅dA=Qenclosed​/ε0​ counts only inside charge. However, the field E at any surface point is the vector sum from ALL charges, inside and outside. Gauss's law constrains the total flux, not the field at each point — a common mix-up.

4.

An isolated charged capacitor's energy DROPS when a dielectric is inserted (U=Q2/2C, C rises). Where does the lost energy go? Doesn't this violate conservation?

ID

drill-elec-4

Type

concept

Marks

2

Difficulty

hard

Concept

Dielectric and energy

Solution

No violation — the field does positive work pulling the dielectric slab in (polarised molecules are attracted into the stronger-field region). That work comes from the stored electrostatic energy, so U falls. If you hold the slab and insert it slowly, the work goes into your muscles (or the restraint); if released, it becomes kinetic energy of the slab. Energy bookkeeping: Ulost​=Wfield on slab​. Contrast with a battery-connected capacitor (V fixed): inserting the dielectric INCREASES stored energy (U=21​CV2) because the battery supplies extra charge — different constraint, opposite result.
