6.1.1 GENERAL INTRODUCTION 
We  are  accompanied  by  plenty  of  living  creatures  found  on the  earth.  Many  of  them  are 
harmless and useful while some are harmful. There is a science that studies all forms of such 
lives known as biology (Greek: ‘bios’ – life, ‘logos’ - knowledge). Thus, it is also known as 
life science. 
 
The major contributions  in the development  of biology were made by Hippocrates (460-320 
BC), Aristotle (384 - 322 BC), Theophrastus (370 - 285 BC), Robert Hooke (1635 - 1703 AD), 
Carolus Linnaeus (1707 - 1778 AD), Charles Darwin (1809 - 1882 AD), Gregor John Mendel 
(1822 - 1884 AD) and many other biologists. 
Do you know? 
• The term 'Biology' was coined by Lamarck and Treviranus (1802). 
• The father of biology is Aristotle. 
• The father of zoology is Aristotle.  
• The father of botany is Theophrastus. 
• The father of medicine is Hippocrates. 
The three main branches of biology which deal with the different aspects of living science 
are: 
1. Botany (Greek: 'botanē' =  herbs,  ‘logos’ - knowledge/study) - It  is  the  branch  of 
biology that deals with the study of plants. 
2. Zoology (Greek: 'zōion' =  animal,  ‘logos’ - knowledge/study) - It  is  the  branch  of 
biology that studies about animals. 
3. Microbiology (Greek ‘mikros’ - small or slight, ‘logos’ - knowledge/study) – It is the 
branch of biology that studies tiny living creatures that cannot be observed by our naked 
eyes.

6.1.3 RELATION OF BIOLOGY WITH OTHER SCIENCE 
The exploration  of life  science involves  knowledge of other basic  sciences like  Physics and 
Chemistry. The combination of these disciplines contributes to a comprehensive perspective. 
 
Relation with Chemistry 
The  physiological  processes  of  plants  and  animals  are  intricately  linked  with  chemical 
reactions. Biochemistry, situated at the intersection of Biology and Chemistry, is the discipline 
that addresses  this  intersection.  Biology is related  with Chemistry,  because of the following 
reasons: 
 
Figure 6.2 Photosynthesis vs. Cellular respiration 


 
 
 
a. Living organisms contain both organic and inorganic compounds, which play a crucial 
role in shaping various life processes. 
b. Energy is transferred within the body through organic chemicals like glucose. 
c. Chemical  changes  are  integral  to  metabolic  pathways,  including  photosynthesis, 
respiration, and metabolism. 
d. Genetic materials such as DNA and RNA are composed of chemicals. 
e. Enzymes and hormones secreted in animals are exclusively chemical in nature. 
  
Relation with Physics 
Biology is related with the Physics, because of the following reasons: 
 
Figure 6.3 Compound Microscope 
 
a. Some life  processes such as sap movement,  diffusion,  mineral  transport,  etc. rely on 
physical phenomena. 
b. Many tools  in biology, such as microscopes,  chromatography,  X-ray diffraction,  etc. 
use physical principles. 
c. The physiological activities  of plants depend on factors like light intensity and energy. 
d. Working of eyes is based on the principle of optics. 
 
  


 
 
Relation with Geography 
 
Figure 6.4 Relation of Biology with Geography 
 
The distribution  of animals, plants is influenced by various geographical factors  like altitude, 
temperature,  climate,  landscape,  and  more  in  different  locations  and  evolutionary  history. 
Understanding biogeography helps explain why certain species  are found in specific  regions 
and how they have evolved and dispersed over time. 
 
Relation with Mathematics 
Biology and mathematics  are connected  because data obtained  from  research  works can be 
analyzed  using  statistical  tools.  The  relationship  between  biology  with  statistics,  and 
mathematics is called biostatistics  and biometrics. Biology is related with Mathematic, because 
of the following reasons: 
a. Mathematics  is used in biology for analyzing data and modeling biological systems. 
b. Statistical  analysis is important  to interpret  experimental results  and draw conclusions 
in fields like genetics and ecology. 
c. Mathematical  models are  used to describe  population  growth, gene frequencies,  and 
ecological interactions. 
 
Relation with Sociology 
Sociology is the study of society, social relationships,  and social behavior. Sociologists seek to 
understand  social  processes,  structures,  and issues  by analyzing  social  interactions,  cultural 
norms, and societal  changes. Biology is related to sociology as animals  live in social  groups. 
The relation with sociology includes: 
a. Animal behavior depends on social interactions. 
b. Many animals  follow social patterns and customs, showing their social nature. 
c. Biological   and  medical  research   includes  sociological   insights  to  ensure  ethical 
practices. 
d. Studying  human   behavior  and  social   systems  involves   understanding  biological 
principles. 
e. Sociobiologists  and  social  scientists  use  biology  to  explain  social  behaviors  and 
relationships.

6.1.4 SCOPES OF BIOLOGY 
         
      
 
Figure 6.5 Scopes of Biology 
 
Biology is very important as it helps us to understand nature and living things and adjust to our 
surroundings.  Its  application  extends far  beyond basic scientific  research,  impacting  crucial 
sectors like health, genetics, nursing, agriculture, environmental science, and conservation. The 
diverse  scopes  of  biology  underscore  its  importance  in  addressing  societal  challenges  and 
advancing  technological  innovations  that  benefit  both  humans  and the  environment.  Some 
important scopes of biology can be discussed under following heads: 
 
1. In the Field of Health, Disease and Medicine: 
Biology  provides  essential  knowledge  for  understanding  human  health,  disease 
mechanisms,  and developing  medical  treatments.  It helps to maintain  health and control 
epidemic  diseases,  offering  insights  into  controlling  and curing  diseases  transmitted  by 
parasites  like Anopheles (malaria),  sandfly  (kala-azar), Tse-tse (sleeping  sickness),  and 
housefly (typhoid). Advancements in medical technology often involve testing  drugs and 
vaccines on animals. 
 
Figure 6.6 Vaccine 
 
2. In the Field of agriculture: 
Agriculture  is  a  part  of  applied  biology. The  development  of  hybrid  plants  through 
biological  knowledge has significantly  increased  agricultural  productivity  and resilience. 
Understanding  biology helps  to know the economic  importance  of various  organisms  in 
croplands  ecosystem  such as earthworms  improve  fertility  and porosity  of soil  which is 
beneficial for plants. Similarly, insects like bees help pollinate flowers, bacteria break down 
dead animals  and plants, enriching the soil with nutrients that plants absorb. Various pests 
destruct  our  cultivated  crops. Biological  knowledge  aids  in  developing integrated pest 


 
 
control  strategies.  Similarly,  biology  provides  knowledge  on  soil microbiology,  soil 
ecology, sustainable agriculture or developing good agricultural practices. 
 
 
3. In the Field of Industry: 
 
Figure 6.7 Apiculture 
 
Various  biological  products  like  foods,  antibiotics,  medicinal  herbs,  coral,  pearl,  honey, 
wax, silk,  lac,  turtle  shell,  bones,  feathers,  tusks,  leather,  and  fur  are  in  high  demand. 
Biological knowledge of producing these items can help increase their availability. 
 
 
4. In the Field of Forestry: 
Biology  is  pivotal  in  forestry,  aiding  our  understanding  and 
management  of forests  and wildlife.  Forest ecology delves into 
how  organisms  interact  with  their  environment,  crucial  for 
wildlife  habitats.  Recognizing  floral  diversity  is  key  for 
preserving  plant  species  vital  for  wildlife.  Microbiology 
uncovers  how  microorganisms  support  soil  health,  benefiting 
both  forests  and  wildlife.  Conservation  biology  ensures 
sustainable  practices,  safeguarding  wildlife  habitats  within 
forests.  These  biology  scopes  empower  foresters  to  protect 
forests  and  wildlife,  benefiting  ecosystems  and  human 
communities  alike.
