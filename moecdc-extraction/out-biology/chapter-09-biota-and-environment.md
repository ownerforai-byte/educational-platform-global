9.1.1 GENERAL INRODUCTION 
In the given pictures,  animals  living  in different  habitats  are shown. Fish lives  in water has 
developed several structures  in its body like gills for respiration,  fins for swimming in water 
and streamlined  body to pass in water easily. Similarly, Dolphin also lives in water which has 
modified fore limbs as fin like structures called flippers and its body is fish like in appearance. 
Cheetah has long and strong limbs, small head and ears thrown back for running swiftly on the 
ground to catch prey and fight. Mole lives  underground, has clawed digits, palms  are shavel 
like  and snout  pointed  for  digging  holes. Sloth  is  hanging  on a tree  branch whose body is 
modified to cope with tree dwelling habit such as strong limbs with claws, powerful muscles 
on chest etc. Birds are seen flying in the sky with their wings, streamlined  body, feathers etc. 
So, each organism of nature modifies  itself  to cope with the change in environment  where it 
lives because environment is the home for various organisms.   
The adjustment  made by the organism to survive in its habitat according to the environmental 
needs is called adaptation.  
Survival  of  animals  in  the  particular  region  is  possible  due to  adaptation.  In  other  words, 
adaptation  is the  process  of  structural,  behavioral  and physiological  changes  in animals  by 
which they can survive and reproduce  in their  habitat.  Structural  changes means developing 
body structures  by animals  such as fins and gills in fishes, flippers  in dolphin, wings in bird

Animals that live in water are called aquatic animals. Aquatic adaptation  is the adjustment  or 
acclimatization  of animals  in  water by  developing  special  structures  in their  body. Aquatic 
adaptation can further be divided as: 
1. PRIMARY AQUATIC ADAPTATION  
Those animals and their ancestors which lived primarily in water and have never come to land 
are called primary aquatic animals and the adaptation shown by them is called primary aquatic 
adaptation.  They are fishes  which respire  through  gills.  Following  adaptational  features  are 
found in primary aquatic animals: 
 


 
 
 
Figure: 9.1.1 Various structures of aquatic adaptation in fish 
i. They have streamlined and laterally compressed body. Such body shape favours swift 
movement in water by reducing friction. 
ii. Locomotion  takes place  through fins  which are  paired  (pectoral  and pelvic  fins)  and 
unpaired  (anal,  dorsal  and  caudal  fin).  Caudal  fins  also  serve  as  rudder,  helps  in 
changing direction while swimming. 
iii. Respiration  takes  place  through  gills  and  gills  are  protected  within  gill  cover  or 
operculum.  In  some  fishes  like  shark,  gill  cover  is  absent.  Some  fishes  have  also 
developed accessory air breathing organs. 
iv. Body is covered by waterproof protective scales and which are of various types such as 
placoid scales, cycloid scales,  ctenoid scales etc. 
v. They  have  mucous  cells  in skin  which  secrete mucus.  Mucus act as  lubricants  while 
swimming  and also protect body from microbial infection. 
vi. Most bony fishes have swim bladder or gas filled bladder which is a small  outgrowth 
from gut, serve as floating of buoyant organ. This  bladder is inflated while ascending 
towards the surface and deflated while descending towards the bottom in water. 
vii. They have lateral line  on either lateral side of the body which consist of large number 
of  sense  organs  called  neuromasts.  These  sense  organs detect  water  pressure  and 
temperature at different depth. 
viii. Their  eye  consists  of semitransparent,  movable  nictitating  membrane  which protects 
the eyes from dirt and foreign substances in water and provides clear vision. 
ix. Fishes  living  in  marine  water and fresh  water regulate  salt  and water in  their  body. 
Freshwater fishes have chloride cells and marine fishes have salt secreting cells in gills 
to excrete excess salt from body. 
2. SECONDARY AQUATIC ADAPTATION 
Those animals  which live  in both  land and water show secondary aquatic  adaptation.  They 
breathe through lungs. Such animals  live occasionally  on land and most of the time in water 
for food and shelter. They have developed following adaptational features: 
i. Their  body is streamlined or fish like,  neck is  short or indistinct and head is  produced 
as snout or rostrum which facilitates swimming in water easily. 


 
 
ii. Fore  limbs  are  modified as  fish fin like  structure called  paddles or flipper  in  whales, 
dugong,  sea  cow  etc.  Some  animals  like  duck,  frog,  tortoises,  crocodiles  etc.  have 
webbed toes for swimming in water. 
iii. Tail  is large,  flat and also  developed as tail fluke in  whale, dolphin, and dugongs for 
swimming. But in some animals  tail is absent or rudimentary. 
iv. Hair, sweat gland and sebaceous  glands are absent in whale, dolphin, seals,  penguins 
etc. instead, there is  a thick  layer  of dermal  fat called  blubber  which act as  insulator 
thus retain warmth.  
v. Nostrils are placed on the top of head so that they can breathe by raising only head from 
water surface. Nostrils are also protected by muscular valve like structure. 
vi. Majority  of  secondary  aquatic  animals  breathe  through  lungs.  Lung  is  large,  non-
lobulated  and  highly  elastic  so  as  to  store  large  volume  of  oxygen  before  diving. 
Amphibians breathe through skin and very few amphibians through external gills. 
vii. Eyes are small,  protected by thick sclera  and present high on face. Eye ball surface is 
protected by fatty substance secreted by harderian gland in whales. 
viii. They  have  small  ear  opening  without pinnae  and in  some  forms  like  walruses  have 
small  pinnae. 
ix. Testes  are  not embedded in  scrotal  sac  but found inside  posterior  abdominal  cavity. 
Duct of mammary  gland can  dilate to reserve  milk  which  is  later  fed to young  ones 
under water.

9.2.1 GENERAL INRODUCTION 
The activity  of all the living beings i.e. from microscopic to higher organism is influenced by 
environmental  factors. Any external  environmental  factors,  such  as heat,  cold,  touch,  pain, 
chemical,  light,  sound etc.  are called  stimuli.  In unicellular  organisms,  individual  cell  itself 
responds to stimuli.  For instance:  Euglena moves towards glucose solution.   Higher animals 
have well developed nervous system  and hence degree of sensitivity  is remarkable  in them. 
However, some plant like Touch Me Not (Mimosa pudica) also responds to touch by immediate 
overlapping  of leaves. Similarly,  directional  growth of plant parts in response to stimuli  like 
sunlight,  chemical,  earth etc. is  called tropism.  When mosquito  repellent  is burnt  inside  the 
room, mosquitos fly away. In all of the above cases, reactions shown by the organisms against 
stimuli are called responses. 
Animals  behaviour  is  the  outwardly  expressed  course  of  action  produced  in  organism  in 
response  to  stimulus  from  a  given  situation.  Behaviour  helps  animal  for  adaptation  in  the 
particular  environment.  Ethology  is  the  study  of  animal  behaviour.  Ethology  may also  be 
defined  as  the  study  of  behaviour  of  animals  in  their  normal  environment  including  both 
external  and  internal  processes  by  which they  respond  to  change  in  environment.  Khorad 
Lorenz is the father of ethology. 
Animal behaviour may be broadly classified as: 
1. Innate or inborn or stereotyped behaviour: 
Behaviours which are genetically  controlled and inherent, without patent learning during life 
time are called innate behaviour. This kind of behaviour is essentially the outcome of inherited 
properties of nervous system of the organism. Example: Fish, tortoise etc. starts swimming just 
after emerging from the egg. Young spider spins web without learning from parents, larva of 
silkworm forms cocoon etc. 
Reflexes, taxis, migration, courtship, leadership, dominance, kinesis etc. are the various types 
of innate behaviour.

2. Acquired or learnt behaviour: 
All those behaviours which an animal learns during its life time through experience and practice 
are known as learnt behaviour. This type of behaviour is not inherited from parents to progeny. 
Examples: singing, dancing, painting, learning languages etc. 
9.2.2 REFLEX ACTION 
 
In higher animals, many responses are involuntary  and instantaneous. These responses  occur 
without prior  thinking.  Example:  watering of mouth occur at the sight  or smell  of delicious 
food, immediate  withdrawal  of hand or leg when stepped  on sharp objects, blinking  of eye 
when some object is about to fall on eyes, taking  immediate  support  by an individual  while 
S/he is about to fall down from chair, coughing triggered by allergens etc. these all action are 
reflex  action. Thus reflex  action  is the involuntary or instant  and automated  response  of an 
animal towards stimuli.  Reflex action is of following types: 
a. Spinal  reflexes: Reflex  actions  which  involve  spinal  cord  and nerves.  Example: knee 
jerking, withdrawing hand from fire etc. 
b. Cerebral reflexes: Reflex actions which involve brain and nerves. Example: Eye blinking, 
sneezing, watering of mouth etc. 
c. Both cerebral  and spinal  reflexes:  Reflex actions  which  involve  both brain  and  spinal 
cord along with nerves. Example:  Hunger, coughing, hiccupping etc. 
 
Figure: 9.2.1 Quick and automated response to hot object 
In reflex action, nerve impulse or information  travels in a specific path or route from receptor 
to effector which is called reflex arc.

Figure: 9.2.2 Reflex arc 
Reflex arc is formed by following components: 
a. Receptor organs: They are sensory organs like skin eye ear tongue etc. which receive 
stimuli.  These  organs  have  nerve  endings which  generate  sensory  impulses  after the 
perception of specific stimulus. 
b. Sensory  or  afferent nerves: Those  nerves  which  transmit  impulses  or  informations 
from  sensory  organ  to brain  and spinal  cord are  called  sensory  nerves.  Cell  body of 
afferent nerve lies inside the dorsal root of spinal cord. 
c. Motor or efferent nerves: Those nerves which conduct motor impulses from brain and 
spinal  cord to effector organs are called motor nerves. Cell body of efferent nerve lies 
inside the ventral root of spinal cord. 
d. Modulator: It is  the central  nervous  system  (brain  and spinal  cord) which  modifies 
sensory  impulses  to  motor  impulses.  Inside  the  spinal  cord,  grey  mater  contains 
interneuron which forms synapse (nerves  junction) with both sensory nerve and motor 
nerves.  Interneurons  act as  the bridge  for  incoming  and  outgoing  impulses.  In  some 
reflexes  (monosynaptic  reflex),  there  may  not  be  interneuron  where  impulses  are 
directly passed from sensory neuron to motor neuron through synapse. 
e. Effector organs: Those  organs  which  respond  to stimuli  or  external  environmental 
factors are called effector organ. Effector organs are muscles and glands.  
Mechanism of reflex action: 
The  receptor  organs  are  innervated  by sensory  nerve  endings.  Such  nerve  endings  induce 
sensory impulses or information  after detecting  stimulus,  which pass through sensory neuron 
and reach  spinal  cord  or brain  where  information  is  processed  and transformed into  motor 
impulses and then such impulses  transmit  through motor nerve to reach effector organs. Four 
sequential  steps of reflex action consist  of reception, conduction, modulation  (interpretation) 
and effect. 
Example: In the case of touching cold object or ice, receptors  in finger detect stimulus  (ice) 
resulting  the  production  of  impulses.  Such impulses  are  transmitted  to spinal  cord through 
sensory  nerves. In spinal  cord, sensory impulses  are modified  as motor  impulses  which are 


 
 
transmitted through motor neuron to the arm muscles. Now, these muscles contract quickly and 
hand is pulled away. 
Significance of reflex action 
i. Reflex action prevents overloading of brain because spinal cord is also involved in this 
process. 
ii. Reflex action protects animals from harmful stimuli. 
 
9.2.3 TAXES 
In general, animals adjust their body posture in normal or balanced position in space. If animals 
are exposed to some  environmental  factors  or stimuli,  they orient  their  body either  towards 
stimuli  or  move  away  from  these  stimuli.  Such  a  directional  movement  of  organisms  in 
response  to  external  stimulus  is  called  is  called  a taxis. When the  organism  or cell  moves 
towards the source of stimulation, it is called positive taxis. If the organism or cell moves away 
from  the  source of  stimulation  it  is called negative  taxis.  Taxis can also  be defined  as  the 
behavioural response of an organism to external stimuli. 
Examples: Bacteria  are  attracted  towards  nutrient  such  as  sugar  amino  acid  (attraction  or 
positive  response)  but  repelled  by  harmful  substances  and  bacterial  wastes  (repulsion  or 
negative response). 
Euglena avoids  salt  solution  (repulsion  or negative  response)  but  attracted  towards glucose 
solution (attraction or positive response) 
Cock roaches discard lighted area (repulsion or negative response).

escape  away from  light.  Example:  Earthworm,  mosquito,  cockroach  etc. move  away 
from bright light showing negative phototaxis but Euglena, moth, housefly etc. move 
towards lighted area showing positive phototaxis. Euglena avoids bright light.

2. Chemotaxis: Chemotaxis  is  defined as  the  movement  of  organism  either  towards a 
chemical  stimulus  or away from it.  Example: Euglena moves  away from strong acid 
(negative chemotaxis) but moves towards weak acid solution (positive chemotaxis).

3. Thermotaxis: Directional movement of organism in response to temperature is called 
thermotaxis. Paramecium prefers  to live  in water of temperature ranging  from 24°c -

4. Thigmotaxis: It is the response  of an organism  to physical  contact or touch stimulus.

5. Geotaxis: It is the response of organism to Earth’s gravity. Earthworm always tends to 
move  downward away  from  earth  surface  indicating  positive  geotaxis. Paramecium 
responds by swimming  up in water against gravity which is negative geotaxis. Fruit fly 
also move up if kept in a vertical  glass tube. Arboreal animals  show negative geotaxis 
whereas animals  living  underground show positive geotaxis. 
6. Rheotaxis: Movement  of  animals  in  response  to  water  and  air  current  is  called 
rheotaxis. Some fishes like eel avoid oncoming water currents and turn away where as 
some  fish like  zebra  fish move towards the current of water. Birds and insects  show 
positive rheotaxis although they can move in any direction. 


 
 
7. Galvanotaxis  or  electrotaxis:  It is  the  response  of  organisms  to electric  current.  If 
Paramecium  is kept in weak electric field, it moves towards cathode.  
On the basis of orientation of animals 
1. Klinotaxis: It is the turning movement of animal by successive comparison of stimulus 
intensities.  Some animals  lack distinct sense  organs but they possess receptor cells  at 
the  antero-dorsal  side  of  the  body. Such  organisms  respond  by  comparing  stimulus 
intensities  of  both sides  by  bending their  head first  in  one  direction  and in  another 
direction. This process continues until both sides are equally stimulated. 
Example: Maggots of housefly, Euglena etc. move in this way or show wavy movement  
with light stimulus of different intensities thrown from two sides. 
2. Tropotaxis: It is the straight orientation of organism in which organism moves either 
towards  or  away  from  stimulus.  No  lateral  or  wavy  movement  of  organism  as  in 
klinotaxis  is found. It is found in bilaterally  symmetrical  animals  having  paired sense 
organ where these organs compare stimulus intensities of both side simultaneously  and 
show straight movement.  
Example: Tick  and lice show positive phototropotaxis. 
3. Telotaxis: If two sources of stimuli of equal intensity operated at the same time, animal 
orients to any one stimulus but not both or intermediate. 
Example: In morning time, honey bee moves with light but rest on flower when it finds. 
But while  returning hive,  uses source  of light. In above  case, flower and sunlight  are 
two stimuli operating at the same time. 
4. Menotaxis: It is the orientation or movement of organism towards a stimulus at certain 
angle. It is also called ‘light compass response’. 
Example: Insects like honey bee, ants, beetle (Geotrapes sylvaticus)  etc make constant 
angle  to  the  sunlight  while  returning  home.  If  direction  of  sunlight  is  changed 
apparently using mirror,  they also change their path. 
5. Mnemotaxis: Movement  of  organism  in  response  to  complex  stimuli  situations  is 
called Mnemotaxis.  
Example:  Hunting wasp uses  a number  of stimuli such as landmarks,  light direction, 
trails  of chemical  left by  them  etc.  while  returning  their  nest. Similarly,  many  long 
distant migratory  birds  while  returning  their  original  home  ground  use  a  number  of 
stimuli like land mark, coastal line, river,  mountain range, celestial bodies etc.

9.2.6 MIGRATORY BEHAVIOUR IN ANIMALS

The term migration is derived from a Latin word ‘Migrare’  which literally  means ‘to travel or 
going from one place to another’. Migration is the regular and periodic passing of animals from 
one place to another place for various purposes. It is a complex type of inborn behaviour which 
is  shown  by  insects,  birds  fishes,  some  mammals,  turtle  etc. Animals  migrate  for  various 
purposes viz. in search of food, to protect from adverse environmental  condition, to find mate, 
shelter and for reproduction.  
1. MIGRATION IN FISHES  
Migration in fishes can be defined as the to and fro movement from feeding and resting place 
to nesting and breeding place and vice versa. Not all fishes are migratory  in nature and some 
fishes which don’t migrate at all are called resident fishes. Some fishes migrate daily and some 
fishes migrate at the specific time in the year. The distance of migration ranges from few meters 
to thousands of kilometer. 
Fishes move by different ways during migration. For instance: some fish passively move with 
water  current  which  is  called  drifting  movement  and  some  migrating  fishes  swim  in  all 
directions called random swimming movement. Another type of movement in fishes in which 
they  swim  in  the  particular  direction  in  response  to  the  certain  stimulus  is  called  oriented 
swimming movement. 


 
 
 
Figure: 9.2.9 some migratory fishes 
Factors influencing fish migration 
i. Physical  factors:  Physical  factors  are  light  intensity,  temperature,  water  current, 
turbidity and depth of water etc. These factors influence fish migration. Any change or 
alterations  in any  of these physical  factors compel  fish to migrate  for searching  their 
favourable environment. For example those fishes which tend to remain in clear or less 
turbid water migrate for searching such places as the water of stream, river etc. become 
more turbid in rainy season. 
ii. Chemical  factors: These  factors consist of salinity  of water, smell,  pH, taste of water, 
dissolved oxygen and overall water chemistry that influence in fish migration. 
iii. Biological  factors:  They  are  gonadial  activity  of  fish,  hormones (FSH) secreted  by 
endocrine glands, availability  of food, predators etc. 
 
Types of fish migration 
Various types of fish migration can be discussed as follow: 
1. Diadromous migration:  Truly  migratory fishes like Salmon, Hilsa, Anguilla, Gobbies 
etc. perform this  type of migration.  In this, Migration  occurs  from sea  water to fresh 
water and vice versa. It is further divided into following types: 
i. Anadromous migration:  Some marine fishes migrate toward freshwater mainly for 
breeding or reproduction which is called anadromous migration. Example: Salmon, 
Sea lamprey, Hilsa, Shad etc. Various changes that occur in the body of fish during 
migration  are accumulation  of reserve  food, maturation of gonad, degeneration of 
alimentary canal etc. After the breeding process is over, young ones and adult return 
to their original  home ground or feeding places. 
ii. Catadromous  migration:  Some  freshwater  fishes  which  migrate  to  sea  water  or 
marine  water  for reproduction  which  is  called  catadromous  migration.  Example: 
Eel (Anguilla bengalensis) which is found in river system of Nepal migrate to Bay 
of  Bengal.  Various  changes  that occur  in  the  body of  fish  during  migration  are 


 
 
suspended feeding, maturation  of gonad, degeneration of  alimentary  canal,  body 
colour become silver white from yellow etc. 
iii. Amphidromous  migration:  The  type migration  which  occurs from  ocean water to 
fresh  water  and  freshwater  to  ocean  water  for  feeding  purpose  instead  of 
reproduction. Example: gobies fishes. 
2. Potadromous  migration: When  fish migration  is  confined only within  freshwater, it  is 
called potadromous migration. Example: carp fishes, trout etc. 
3. Oceanodromous migration:  The migration of fishes which is confined only to marine 
water or sea water is called oceanodromus migration. 
4. Alimental  migration:  Migration  of  fishes  for  feeding  purpose  is  called  alimental 
migration. Example:  sharks, carps etc. 
5. Shoreward migration: migration of fishes towards sea shore or bank of river is called 
shoreward migration. Example: Anabas 
6. Vertical  migration: Migration  of fishes from bottom or depth of water to the surface 
and in the reverse direction is called vertical migration.  Example: sword fish, meckerel 
etc. 
7. Latitudinal migration: Migration of fish from north to south and vice versa. Example: 
sword fish, hammer headed shark etc. 
8. Climatic  migration:  fishes  migrate  in  response  to  change  in  season  or  climate  for 
seeking safe place is called climatic  migration. 
Advantages of fish migration 
1. Migration ensures abundant supply of food, suitable breeding ground and shelter. 
2. It also  provides  suitable  condition for the survival  of juveniles  in  the migrated place 
from enemies. 
3. Migration also protects fishes from adverse environmental situations. 
4. Some ichthyologists believe that migration is an adaptation towards abundance. 
2. MIGRATION IN BIRDS 
Migration  of various types of birds is a fascinating  and spectacular  event associated  with 
bird life. Many birds can be seen flying in the sky from one place to another. According to 
Cahn (1935),  “Migration  is  the periodic  passing  of animals  from  one  place to  another”. 
Migration in birds can also be defined as the regular and periodic passing of birds from their 
breeding and nesting ground to feeding and resting ground. Such movement occurs during 
the particular period of the year and the birds usually follow the same route. Some migratory 
birds  are Siberian  crane, golden  plover of USA, black and white  cuckoos, robins, hawk, 
crane, geese etc.Those birds which do not migrate  and remain in their home ground all the 
time are called resident birds. Example: Bob white, ruffled sand grouse etc.

ii. Intensity of light stimulates the gonad affecting nervous system bringing migration.

to southern  hemisphere  and vice  versa. Golden  plover of USA migrate  to south  i.e.

2. Longitudinal: Migration of birds from east to west and vice versa is called longitudinal

3. Altitudinal  migration: Migration of birds  which takes place from higher altitude  to 
lower altitude  and vice versa. Example: wood cock, bush chat, Violet green swallows 
of U.K, Lophophorus in Nepal. 
4. Vagrant  migration: Some birds  from  their  home migrate  in  all  direction  to a  long

Mode of flight during migration  
Birds  migrating  at  night  are called  nocturnal  migrants  and those  at  day time  are  called

and diurnal migrants are crow, swallow, robins, pelican, etc.  
 
At the time  migration  some birds travel  in separate  companies and some birds in mixed 
companies. Distance travelled by birds depends on the local climatic  condition and species 
of bird. Himalayan snow partridges descend hardly about one or two mile distance whereas 
European white stork covers 8000 miles. Smaller bird fly close to earth within about 3000 
feet from earth surface and some species of birds even cross Andes and the Himalayas  at 
the altitude of 20000 feet or more. Birds usually travel 5-6 hours a day resting in between 
for food and water.

general airflow on a broad front. Birds take reference of configuration  of land, coastline, 
course  of  Great  River,  intervening  mountains  chain,  position  of  celestial  bodies, 
constellation  during migration. 
 
Advantages  
i. It helps to protect bird from harsh climatic condition. 
ii. This phenomenon helps bird to find breeding ground. 
iii. It prevents overcrowding of birds in  the particular  area  and helps  to exploit  food and 
other resources from new or untouched places. 
iv. It ensures better chance of survival of young ones from predator in the migrated place. 
 
Disadvantages  
i. At the long journey of migration, some birds may be excessively  tired and may  fall 
down on sea, river, forest, rocks and die. 
ii. There is a high risk of natural enemies during migration. 
iii. Various natural calamities like heavy rain, snow fall, storms, hurricane etc may be the 
cause of death for many migratory birds. 
iv. Several parasitic infections may occur at the time of migration.

9.3.1 GENERAL INRODUCTION 
The  word  pollution  is  derived from  a  latin  word  pollutionem which  means  ‘defilement’. 
Simply, pollution  is the introduction  of harmful  substances to the environment. Such activity 
results  degradation  of natural  or original  quality  of various  environmental  components. The 
causes of pollution are natural and anthropogenic both. Anthropogenic causes include various 
type of human intrusion on the nature such as industrial  activities,  using excessive pesticides 
and chemical fertilizer  in agricultural  land, fossil fuel combustion,  and unsystematic  disposal 
of municipal and industrial wastes, deforestation and increasing urbanization. Similarly, natural 
causes are volcanic eruptions,  forest  fire, dust storms,  decay of dead organic matter,  pollens 
etc. Environmental pollution is now being a global burning issue and posing a serious threat to 
flora and fauna including human beings. Pollution causes different types of health disorders in 
human such as asthma,  bronchitis,  poor mental health,  anaemia, COPD (chronic  obstructive 
pulmonary disease), blood pressure etc.

To conclude,  pollution  is  any undesirable  changes  in the  physical,  chemical  and biological

Any substances that make the environment dirty and polluted are called pollutants. 
TYPES OF POLLUTANTS 
On this basis of changing or unchanged nature of pollutants, they are of two types: 
a. Primary  pollutant:  Those  pollutants  which  remain  in  the  same  form  as  they  are 
discharged from the sources to the environment. Example: DDT, fly ash etc. 
 
b. Secondary  pollutant:  Pollutants  which  are  formed by  the  chemical  combination of 
primary  pollutants.  They  are  more  toxic  than  primary  pollutants.  Example:  smog, 
peroxy-acetyl nitrate (PAN), ozone etc. 
On the basis of degrading or non-degrading nature of pollutants, they are of two types: 
a. Degradable pollutants:  such pollutants  which degrade or  decompose in nature quite 
rapidly by the action of microbes and heat. Example: biological  or organic wastes. 
 
b. Non degradable  pollutants:  such  pollutants which  do not degrade or  decompose  in 
nature are called non-degradable wastes. Example: DDT, BHC, plastic, tin can etc.

household purposes and such activity also releases  harmful gases such as CO

burning of filth in dumped site, forest fire etc. also contributes to air pollution. 
f. Nuclear power plant 
There  may be occasional  or accidental release  of hazardous radiations like alpha rays, 
gamma rays from nuclear power plants.

Various effects of air pollution can be described under following headings: 
a. Effects on the environment 
i. Greenhouse effect:  
Gases such as SO
2
, NO
2
, CO
2
, methane, chlofluoro carbon water vapour etc. which trap

ii. Acid rain 
Gases  like  SO
2
, NO

Acid rain water badly affects living beings. 


 
 
iii. Depletion of ozone layer 
Some chemicals such as chlorofluoro  carbon, methyl chloroform carbon tetrachloride,

Atmosphere becomes  unclear  due to mixing  of smoke,  dust particles,  fog etc. Due to

v. Reduction in solar radiation 
Solar  radiation  is  absorbed  or  blocked  by  pollutants  in  air  which  may  increase  the 
temperature of atmosphere. 
 
b. Effects on plants and animals 
Due  to the reduction  in  solar  radiation,  stomata  of  green  plants  do not  open  which 
negatively   affects  the  several   plant  physiological   activities   like   photosynthesis, 
respiration  and transpiration.  Acid rain  has detrimental effect on the growth of plants 
because of mineral and nutrient deficiencies in soil. Less crop yielding, stunted growth 
of plants, leaves  turn yellowish, lichens  on tree are also the results of acid rain  and air 
pollution. Aquatic ecosystems will also be disturbed by acid rain. 
Air  pollution  brings  several  health  related  complications  in  animals  like  bronchitis, 
pneumonia  and cardiovascular  diseases.  Pollutants  will  also  interfere  with hormonal 
secretion  in  animals  leading  to  reduced  gonadial  activity,  infertility  and  birth  of 
defective progenies.

health,  autism,  COPD,  lung  cancer,  asthma.  Women  may  suffer  from  anaemia, 
infertility, miscarriage,  gestational diabetes, premature menopause mental illness.

iv. Least consumption of fossil fuels and excessive  emphasis on the alternative sources of 
energy such as solar energy electricity biogas should be given. 
v. Industries  should  be  open  far  from  human  settlement,  public  awareness,  strict 
implementation of law or environmental  act prevent air pollution.

9.3.3 WATER POLLUTION 
Water  pollution  is  change  in  natural  quality  of  water  due  to  the  addition  of  external 
substances or contaminants in it. It may also be defined as the degradation of the quality of 
water due to mixing of inorganic, organic, biological, radiological substance and heat. Such 
activity  makes water  unfit  and unhealthy  for human use and survival  of aquatic  life.  In 
general, normal water is colourless, odourless, and tasteless but polluted water is turbid and 
having unpleasant smell and so does its taste.  
Any  undesirable  change  in  physical,  chemical  and  biotic  components  of  aquatic 
environment is called water pollution. 
          
Figure: 9.3.3 Water Pollution 
  


 
 
1. SOURCES 
Sources of water pollution are discussed under following headings: 
i. Domestic sewage: 
Everything that passes from  houses or residential  area such as food residue, waste water 
after  washing  utensils  and clothes  by detergents,  human  excreta,  animal  wastes, wastes 
from slaughter house, and wastes from food processing industries are discharged into river, 
lakes,  streams  and  other  water  bodies.  Contents  of  domestic  sewage  are  mostly 
biodegradable.  Raw sewage contains  inorganic and organic compounds and a number of 
pathogens  causing  water  borne  disease. Inorganic  compounds  are  nitrate,  phosphate, 
sodium, calcium etc.  
ii. Industrial  effluents 
Waste water discharged from all industries is called effluents. Industrial wastes consists of 
inorganic  substances  such  as  lead,  mercury,  phosphorus,  sulphites,  sulphides  etc.  and 
organic  wastes  are  oils,  grease,  organic  solvents  etc.  some  industries  also use 
microorganisms  like  viruses  and bacteria.  These are all discharged  to water bodies  with 
water.  
iii. Run- off water from agriculture land 
Nowadays,  there  is  competition  among  farmers  to  use  different  types  of  insecticides, 
pesticides, fungicides and chemical fertilizers  in the name of more yields. Such chemicals 
decompose or degrade in nature very slowly. They are washed by rain water and mixed into 
river, lakes, ponds etc. altering the water quality.  
iv. Accidental oil spill or leakage 
Spilling and leakage of oil from tankers, pipelines pollute water sources. Oil is immiscible 
in water and thus makes a thin layer over water surface preventing aeration of water. Such 
condition badly affects aquatic life. 
v. Nuclear power plant 
Nuclear weapon investigation,  nuclear power houses, dumping of radioactive  waste and 
nuclear  calamities  are also  the  major  source  of  contamination  of  water  bodies.  Mining 
activities  of radioactive elements like uranium pollute surface water and ground water.  
2. EFFECTS 
a. Polluted water contains pathogens such as Escherichia coli, Salmonella typhii  causing 
several waterborne diseases and bring epidemic condition in the community. 
b. When organic compounds or wastes exceed optimum level in water, decaying of such 
wastes needs large number of decomposers or microorganisms.  These microorganisms 
need large amount of oxygen for decomposition of these organic wastes, leading to the 
rise of BOD or biological  oxygen demand. BOD is defined as the amount of oxygen 
required by micro-organism  while oxidizing the entire organic  matter present  in one 
litre of water at given temperature. Value of BOD is more in polluted water. 
c. Waste  water discharged from most  of  the  industries  may  be  at  higher  temperature. 
Electric  power  plant,  nuclear  power  station  and thermal  power  plant use  water as 
coolant and these units  release  hot water to nearby  sources  like  river,  sea  and lake 


 
 
raising  temperature  of these  water bodies. It is  also  called thermal  pollution.  Such 
activity causes the oxygen depletion in water and threats aquatic life.  
d. Pesticides and fertilizers after being swept or washed away by rain water mix to nearby 
water  sources  and  alter  the  overall  chemical  composition  of  water  which  affects 
freshwater and marine ecosystem. These  substances may incorporate  into food chain 
and get accumulated in organisms of higher trophic level. 
e. Nuclear  power  plants  established  in  the coastal  regions  emit  radiations  and  atomic 
wastes which  pollute  water and bring  several  health hazards  to  marine  animals  and 
human beings who consume seafood. 
f. Water bodies become enriched or overloaded by nutrients such as phosphate and nitrates 
found in  domestic sewage,  industrial  effluents and run  off from  field. Consequently, 
growth of more aquatic plants and algal bloom take place in water. This prevents aeration 
of  water  and  entry  of  sunlight. Death  and  decay  of  these  algal  blooms  need  more 
decomposer and bacteria which demand more  oxygen in water and the entire condition 
is called Eutrophication. 
g. Some  metals  such as  Mercury,  lead etc. in  polluted water bring  serious  health  related 
issues  like  cancer,  renal  disorder, eyes problem,  nervous  and cardiovascular  disorder, 
skin allergies  etc. 
3. PREVENTIVE MEASURES 
i. Household degradable organic  wastes and sewage can  be collected  and converted to 
compost manure. 
ii. Industrial effluents should be treated and made least toxic before discharging into water 
sources. 
iii. If possible wastes can be recycled or treated before dumped. 
iv. Use  of  insecticide,  pesticide  and  fertilizer  (agrochemicals)   should  be  banned  in 
agriculture field. 
v. Making    people    aware,    afforestation,   checking    population    growth   and   strict 
implementation of environment protection act are main ways to prevent water pollution. 
9.3.4 SOIL POLLUTION 
 
Figure: 9.3.4 Soil Pollution 
 
Soil  is  an important  abiotic  component  of  environment  where all  living  beings  and micro-
organisms thrive. Throwing and dumping wastes into the soil from various sources deteriorate 
the natural quality and originality of soil. Soil pollution is defined as any undesirable changes 


 
 
in the physical, chemical  and biological  aspects of soil. Soil pollution alters  texture, fertility 
and water holding capacity of soil. 
 
 
1. SOURCES 
a. Domestic wastes  
Wastes produced from houses such as plastics, rubber, unused food materials, dust, waste 
paper, tin cans, excreta of animals,  broken pieces of utensils, glasses, unused agricultural 
products etc.  are  dumped  into  the  soil.  Such  condition  diminishes  soil  fertility.  This 
situation  also  favours  growth  of  pathogenic  micro-organisms   and  spread  to  nearby 
residential areas causing epidemics.  
b. Municipal wastes 
In urban area, wastes such as plastic, leather, paper, glasses  etc. produced from hospital, 
offices, houses,  schools,  colleges,  and streets etc. which  are  collected by municipality. 
Such  wastes consist  of both degradable and non-degradable waste and they  should be 
separately managed and dumped. If not, organic content in soil and heavy metal content 
like Cu, Pb, Cr, Ni etc will increase. 
c. Wastes from industries 
Various types of industries such as paper and pulp industries, food processing  industry, 
soap  and  detergent  factory,  pharmaceuticals,  mining  industries  produce  various  bi-
products at the time of manufacture of goods. Bi-product or wastes produced are sulphide, 
bleaching  liquors,  pathogens,  polyphosphates,  drugs,  mine  wastes,  suspended  solids, 
chloride, and heavy metals etc. which are released  to external environment causing  soil 
pollution. 
d. Biocides and chemical fertilizer 
Biocides are the chemicals  used to kill insects, pest, mite, fungi and rodents which destroy 
our  cultivated  crops  and  annoy  us.  They  are  of  various  types  such  as,  insecticide, 
pesticide,  rodenticide,  fungicides  and  so  on  which  include  DDT,  aldrein  lindane, 
parathion, toxaphane. Farmers also use different types of chemical  fertilizers in the field 
to enhance  crop production. Nowadays, maximum  and haphazard use of pesticides and 
chemical  fertilizer caused soil pollution. 
e. Physical disturbances 
Unplanned   irrigation    schemes,    building    construction,    atomic    explosion,    and 
deforestation  have  resulted  soil  pollution  which  lead  to  soil  erosion,  expansion  of 
deserts and scanty rainfall. 
f. Acid rain 
Air pollution  causes  acid precipitation  in the environment which is of two types- dry 
precipitation and wet precipitation. Many chemicals  including acid particles, gases etc. 
either remain  in air or settle down on the soil or any hard surfaces which is called dry 
precipitation. During rainfall, such acidic gases or particles dissolved in water forming 
acid and fall back on soil. 
g. Radioactive wastes 
Nuclear  dust and radioactive wastes penetrate the soil  and accumulated inside due to 
Radioactivity. 


 
 
h. Overgrazing and deforestation 
Overgrazing and clearing forest removes vegetations which cover the soil surface. Due 
to this, soil erosion  occurs easily  and topsoil  layer which is rich in organic  matter and 
nutrient is  swept away by  wind and water exposing  infertile  subsoil  layer.  Similarly, 
overgrazing  and  deforestation disturb  soil  microbial  communities,  nutrients  cycling 
between soil and plants, and make soil  compact decreasing water holding capacity and 
porosity. 
2. EFFECTS OF SOIL POLLUTION 
i. Excessive use of biocides and chemical fertilizers in cropland make soil sterile thereby 
altering the physicochemical  parameters of soil making it unfit for plants and animals. 
It causes insufficient and contaminated food supply to animals. 
ii. Soil pollution also affects air and water by making them polluted. 
iii. All  organisms  including  human  being  come  in  contact with polluted  soil  containing 
heavy  metals  like  lead,  mercury  which  bring  several  genetic  disorders,  congenital 
illness  and  other  health  related  issues  such  as  cancer,  eye  problems,  skin  allergies, 
neurological  disorder, urinary problems, gastro-intestinal disorder etc. 
iv. Pollutants  like  plastic  rubber  glasses  etc.  obstruct the nutrient  absorption,  disturbing 
plant physiology, growth and finally crop failure. 
v. Polluted soil is unfit for microbial  community in the soil and its consequence is loss of 
all types of biodiversity like vegetation, insects, birds etc. from that area. 
vi. Polluted landscape or soil has no aesthetic and recreational value. 
3. CONTROL MEASURES OF SOIL POLLUTION 
i. Instead of dumping all wastes to the particular site, adopt 3R (reduce, reuse and recycle) 
principle  for degradable and non-degradable wastes. 
ii. Animal excreta can be used for the production of biogas for household purpose. 
iii. Using biocides should be banned or minimised. 
iv. Use of biofertilizer  and manures  should be encouraged  instead of applying  chemical 
fertilizer in soil. 
v. Landslide and soil  erosion  can be prevented by cutting down trees and by promoting 
afforestation.

9.3.5 PESTICIDES 
Biocides  are  the  chemicals  which  are  used to  kill  different  types  of  economically  harmful 
organisms. Biocides include pesticide, insecticide, rodenticide, fungicides, herbicide etc. A Pest 
is  any  undesired  organism  that  either  destroys  or  interferes  with  the  production  of  crops, 
livestock or forestry and annoys human beings. Pest may be various types of insects, molluscs, 
rodents, unwanted plants in cultivated  crops, annelids,  fungi, mites.  Pesticides  are poisonous 
chemicals used to kill pests. 


 
 
They are of various types: 
1. Organochlorines: They  are  chlorinated  hydrocarbons  which include  DDT (dichloro 
diphenyl trichloro ethane), Dialdrin, Lindane, etc. such insecticide are also called hard 
insecticide as they persist in the environment for long time and hard to degrade. 
They act by contact, ingestion and by vapour action in some forms. 
2. Organophosphates: They are organophosphorus compounds which include Parathion, 
Malathion etc. 
They are nerve  poison, inhibit the enzyme  choline esterase, also  act by fumigants and 
contact. 
3. Carbamates: These  chemicals  are based on the carbamic  acid nucleus.  They  include 
Carbaryl, Aldicarb, Methomyl etc. 
They are also nerve poison, inhibit the enzyme choline esterase in insects. 
4. Plant derivatives:  Certain plants have toxic properties for insects. Some parts of them 
are used to extract chemicals for killing  insects called plant derived insecticides. Which 
include Pyrethrum (extracted from the flower of Chrysanthemum), rotenone (extracted 
from root of Derris elliptica)  and nicotine (from leaves of tobacco plant). 
5. Synthetic  Pyrethroids:  They  are  synthetic  insecticides  that contain pyrethrum  and 
include Permethrin, Cypermethrin,  Fenvalerate etc 
6. Oils: Oils are also sprayed to crops for destroying insects. 
7. Insect growth regulator: Such insecticide interfere insect growth hormone system and 
kill their stages of lifecycle. They include Deminin, Penfluron, Benzoylureas  etc. 
EFFECT OF PESTICIDES 
1. Pesticides not only destroy pests but also  degrade various  component of environment 
such as air water soil causing environmental  pollution. 
2. Pesticides are generally  non-biodegradable and such chemical  enter plants body which 
then transferred through food chain and get accumulated the body of animals of higher 
trophic level which is called bio-magnification. 
        
Figure: 9.3.5 Bio-magnification  (Pink colour indicates toxin) 
3. Some pesticides have wide range of activity killing  both targeted organism (pests) and 
non-targeted organism.  Sometimes  pollinating  insects  viz. honey bees, soil  fauna viz. 
earthworm any many other animals  like bird, mammals  and fish may be badly affected 
by such poisonous chemicals. 


 
 
4. Pesticides or chemical  residue may remain in fruits, vegetable, leaf surface or any other 
part of plant leading  to contamination  such  parts are  later  consumed by  humans  and 
animals  will suffered by several health related issues in long term. 
       
Figure: 9.3.6 Effects of pesticide on human health 
5. A  variety  of  wild  animals,   plants,  aquatic  organism,  microorganism   and  overall 
biodiversity (ecological  diversity, species diversity and genetic diversity) is harmed by 
pesticides in long run use. 
6. Insect  may  genetically  develop  resistance  against  pesticides  due  to  excessive  and 
prolonged use. Later, such pesticides won’t work. 
7. Pest develops resistance to pesticides because of repeated use. As a result, farmers must 
purchase  new and often more  expensive  pesticides as pests become  resistant  to older 
and cheaper   ones.  The   continuous  introduction  of  new  pesticides  can  lead  to 
environmental  contamination  and harm  to non-target organisms,  including  beneficial 
insects, wildlife, and even human health. Such condition is called pesticide tread mill 
or ‘pesticide trap’. 
Alternative approaches for minimizing  the use of pesticides 
Besides using chemical, there are several other techniques that can be adopted to control pests 
like crop rotation,  biological  control  (inoculating  other organisms  in crops which kill  or eat 
pest), use of bio-pesticides  (ash, urine of cow, neem extracts etc), mechanical control etc 
Nowadays an effective strategy for controlling  pest called IPM (integrated  pest management) 
which is also recommended  by environment  activist. This technique adopts all the method of 
controlling pests with the least use of chemical pesticides.
