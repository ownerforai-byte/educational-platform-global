5.1.1 INTRODUCTION 
Vegetation  refers  to the plant  life  or the collection  of plant  species  in a particular  region  or 
habitat. It encompasses all types of plants, ranging from small mosses and ferns to large trees. 
Natural  vegetation  is  a plant  community  that  grows without  human  intervention,  obtaining 
everything it needs from its environment  and remaining  untouched by human activities  for a 
considerable period, often referred to as virgin vegetation. 
The natural vegetation in Nepal is influenced by its climate, altitude and slope of the region. 
Although cultivated  crops  and fruits  are part  of vegetation,  they  are not considered  natural 
vegetation. 
 
IMPORTANCE OF VEGETATION 
The importance of vegetation lies in its various essential roles in the biosphere: 
a) Vegetation  plays  a  key  role  in  regulating  biogeochemical  cycles,  like  water  cycle, 
carbon cycle, and nitrogen cycle. 
b) Vegetation significantly  affects  properties of soil like volume, chemistry,  and texture. 
These, in turn, affect the productivity and structure of the vegetation. 
c) Vegetation  serves as a habitat  for wildlife  and provides  energy for a diverse range of 
animal species on Earth. 
d) For humans, vegetation is crucial for food, shelter, and medicines. 
e) Vegetation helps reduce erosion by intercepting rainfall, decreasing overland flow, and 
improving the infiltration  of water into the soil. 
 
 
5.1.2 TYPES OF VEGETATION IN NEPAL 
In Nepal, there are different  kinds  of natural vegetation,  and their  diversity  and distribution 
depend on factors like the shape of the land, climate, soil, and altitude. 
There are six main types  of natural vegetation  (Dobremez) in Nepal based on altitude  (bio-
climatic  zones): 
a) Tropical  Forest:  It  is  found  at  altitudes  below  1000  m  in  the  Terai  and  Shiwalik 
(Churia)  hills.  It  covers  about  14% of  Nepal's  area. Dominant  trees  include Shorea

Quercus leucotrichophora (oak). 
e) Alpine Vegetation:  It grows at altitudes  between 4000 to 5000 m in the Himalayan

f) Tundra vegetation:  It  is found  at  altitudes  above 5000 m  in the  higher  Himalayan 
regions covered with snow, forming  a cold desert area. This area contains vegetation 
such as lichens and mosses. 
 
PRESENT STATE OF VEGETATION IN NEPAL 
A joint five-year survey conducted by the Department of Forest Research and Survey and the 
National  forest  Products  Survey  Project  in  between  2067  to  2071  BS,  has  come  up  with 
following findings: 
1. Roughly 44% of Nepal's landmass is occupied by forest. 
2. Forest roughly occupies 5.96 million hectares of landmass of the country. 
3. Protected  forests  in Nepal make  up about 12.1%  of the total,  and conserved forests 
account for around 21.4%. 
4. Approximately 5.1% of Nepal's forests are classified  as production forests. 
5. Between 2000 and 2005, Nepal lost about 2,640 square kilometers (1,019 square miles) 
of forest, with an annual deforestation  rate of about 1.4%, equivalent to an average loss 
of 530 square kilometers  (205 square miles) each year. 
6. A 2010 study  estimated  that  39.1%  of  Nepal is  covered  by forests,  and  agriculture 
occupies 29.83% of the land. 
7. Patch  and  edge  forests,  constituting  23.4%  of  the  national  forest  cover,  show 
interactions  with surrounding living things. 
8. Core forests make up 79.3% of protected areas and 63% of the area outside protected 
areas. 
 
  


 
 
5.1.3 CONCEPT OF IN-SITU AND EX-SITU CONSERVATION 
Natural vegetation  conservation refers  to the preservation  and protection  of indigenous plant 
species,  ecosystems,  and  habitats  in  their  natural  state.  It  involves  efforts  to  maintain  the 
diversity,  health, and balance of plant communities  and their associated  fauna, often through 
the implementation  of various strategies such as protected area designation, habitat restoration, 
sustainable  land management  practices, and biodiversity  conservation initiatives.  The goal of 
natural vegetation conservation is to safeguard ecosystems from degradation, habitat loss, and 
extinction, ensuring their long-term  survival and ecological function for future generations. 
 
Types of Conservation 
a. In-situ conservation: This strategy focuses on the protection of rare, endangered, and 
threatened  species  of  plants  and  animals  in their  own natural  habitat.  For  example, 
national parks and other protected areas. 
b. Ex-situ conservation: This strategy focuses on the protection of rare, endangered, and 
threatened species of plants and animals outside their natural habitat. It focuses on the 
organisms which are at the verge of extinction.  For example, botanical gardens, zoos, 
conservation efforts in places like seed bank, safari  parks, Elephant Breeding Center, 
Gharial  Conservation  and Breeding  Center, and Vulture  Conservation  and Breeding 
Center, etc. 
 
CONCEPT OF IN-SITU (PROTECTED AREAS) CONSERVATION 
Protected or conservation areas are special places that get extra care because they are important 
for nature, the environment, or culture. There are different kinds of these areas, and how much 
protection they get depends on the rules of each country or international  groups. 
 
A protected area is a specific  space on the map that is clearly marked, known, and taken care 
of through laws or other  effective  methods. The goal is to keep nature safe for a long time, 
along with its ecosystems and cultural values. These areas usually include places like national 
parks, wildlife reserves, natural forests, conservation areas, community forests, religious forest, 
and lease-hood forests. 
 
Protected areas of Nepal 
Nepal's protected  areas mainly  cover forested  land across different  elevations,  including  the 
Terai,  Himalayan  foothills,  and mountains,  to safeguard  biodiversity.  Nepal, situated  in  the 
central part of the Himalayas, spans 7,181 km. Altitudes range from 67 m in the southeastern 
Terai to 8,848 m at Sagarmatha (Mt. Everest). 
Botanists have identified 1,120 species of non-flowering plants and 5,160 species of flowering 
plants. Zoologists have documented 151 mammal species, 844 bird species, 100 reptile species, 
43 amphibian species, 185 freshwater fish species, and 635 butterfly species. Recognizing the 
significant  biodiversity,  the government of Nepal established  a network of 20 protected areas 
since 1973, including ten national  parks, three  wildlife  reserves,  six conservation  areas, and 
one  hunting  reserve.  In  2017,  Shuklaphanta  and  Parsa  wildlife  reserves  were upgraded  to 
National Parks.

BOTANICAL GARDEN (EX-SITU CONSERVATION) 
Botanical gardens are large areas dedicated to growing various types of plants for scientific and 
educational  purposes. The idea of botanical  gardens dates back to Theophrastus, but modern 
botanical gardens have evolved significantly.  In addition  to outdoor plants, they now include 
greenhouses, research laboratories,  libraries,  and herbariums  with documented collections  of 
different plant species. The International Association of Botanic Gardens (IABG), established 
in 1962, plays a crucial  role  in coordinating  research  and facilitating  the exchange of plant 
materials. 
 
Importance of Botanical Gardens: 
a) Botanical gardens provide living plant materials  essential for systematic studies. 
b) They cultivate important plants from the local area. 
c) Botanical gardens maintain records of the local flora. 
d) They supply seeds and materials  for various botanical research activities. 
e) Botanical gardens grow and preserve rare and endangered plant species. 


 
 
f) They cultivate and maintain  plants that wouldn't naturally grow during certain seasons 
by regulating greenhouse temperatures.

SEED BANK (EX-SITU CONSERVATION) 
A seed bank or germplasm bank is a location where specific conditions are upheld to safeguard 
seeds  from  various  plant  species,  whether  they  are  wild  or  cultivated.  The  main  goal  is 
straightforward:  to  secure  the long-term  preservation  of a  wide variety  of plants  for  future 
generations. 
Originally, they were created for important  food crops, but now they also store seeds of many 
endangered wild plants. These seeds can stay dormant for thousands of years if kept at a low 
temperature (below -5°C) and humidity. 
They include major crop plants such as cereals, soyabean, cotton and many vegetable species. 
A challenge in a seed bank is making sure the seeds stay alive. Periodic tests are conducted on 
seed  samples  to  check their  ability  to sprout.  If  the  germination  capability  drops  below an 
acceptable level, around 85%, new seeds are produced by growing the stored seeds and then 
harvesting them for storage. 


 
 
 
The importance of seed bank 
The undeniable and significant  threat of species extinction,  caused by both natural factors and 
human activities, poses a serious risk to our planet. Once a species disappears, it is lost forever. 
Seed banks play a crucial role in addressing this threat, particularly for plants. They are integral 
to a strong seed system that ensures food security. 
1. The  importance  of  seed  banks  includes  preserving  the  genes  necessary  for  plant 
breeders to enhance crop yield, disease resistance, drought tolerance, nutritional quality, 
and more. 
2. Additionally, seed banks help prevent the loss of genetic diversity in rare plant species, 
contributing to the conservation of biodiversity  outside their natural habitats (ex-situ). 
 
5.1.4 NATURAL ENVIRONMENT: VEGETATION AND HUMAN ACTIVITIES 
The natural  environment  is  everything  around  us that  happens without  human  influence.  It 
includes  plants, animals,  soil,  rocks, air, and natural  events. The place we live in, called  the 
ecosystem, provides important services for us and other living things, like clean air and water. 
However, humans are affecting the environment a lot by things like too many people, pollution, 
using  too  much  of certain  resources,  and cutting  down too many  trees.  These  changes  are 
causing problems like climate  change, soil erosion, bad air, and a shortage of clean water. 
People's  actions,  like  building  cities,  farming,  putting  up factories,  and constructing  things, 
have a big impact on the plants that grow in an area. These human activities  change how plants 
are spread out in different places. 
 
DEFORESTATION 
Cutting down a lot of trees, known as deforestation,  is a significant  human activity  that has a 
big effect on plants. This happens when forests  lose  their trees.  It causes problems  like soil 
erosion and brings negative changes to the entire environment. Right now, about 100 thousand 
hectares of forests are getting cut down each year. If we keep doing this, Nepal might have no 
forests left in about 20-25 years. 
 
CAUSES OF DEFORESTATION 
1. The escalation of human and livestock populations has intensified the demand for food, 
space, and natural resources derived from forests. 
2. The heightened need for timber and fuel wood has led to the degradation of numerous 
areas, with an estimated annual destruction of approximately 1000 square km of forest 
land for fuel wood purposes. 
3. Overgrazing of forest trees by domesticated animals has further exacerbated the impact 
on vegetation. 
4. Agricultural  expansion  is  encroaching  upon  forested  areas,  resulting  in  the 
displacement of natural woodland. 


 
 
5. Deforestation  is driven by infrastructure  development  such as road construction,  dam 
projects, urbanization, and mining activities. 
 
HAZARDS OF DEFORESTATION 
Forests  are  really  important  for  the  environment.  When  we  cut  down  too  many  trees 
(deforestation),  it can lead to several problems: 
1. Deforestation  makes  soil  erosion  worse,  and a  lot  of  soil  (about  240  million  cubic 
meters) gets washed away every year in Nepal. 
2. Good  soil  with  important  nutrients  is  lost  due  to  erosion,  making  the  land  less 
productive and fertile. 
3. It messes up the climate,  the balance of oxygen and carbon dioxide, and even rainfall. 
This harms the environment. 
4. Animals lose their homes because of the loss of forest trees. 
5. Deforestation and soil erosion can cause floods and landslides to happen more often. 
6. There's  less  wood, herbs,  and  other  forest  products  because  of  deforestation.  This 
shortage affects people and industries that rely on these products, causing problems for 
the economy of Nepal. 
 
FOREST CONSERVATION OR FOREST MANAGEMENT 
There are different ways to protect and take care of the forests: 
1. Afforestation  or Reforestation:  Planting  new trees, both by the government  and local 
communities,  on a larger scale can help. 
2. Community  Forests:  Local communities  manage and look after these forests  to meet 
their own needs. 
3. Private Forests: People who own land are encouraged to plant trees on their property, 
and they get help and resources to do it. 
4. Modern Pest Control Techniques: Using new methods to control pests helps save the 
forest without disturbing the natural balance. 
5. Agroforestry: Growing trees along with crops on less productive farmland. 
6. Strip Plantation: Planting trees along roads, canals, and rail lines. 
7. Alternative Energy Sources: Using different sources of energy instead of cutting down 
trees. 
8. Herbal Farming: Growing more herbs to meet the demand. 
9. Public Awareness Programs: Teaching people about the importance of forests.
