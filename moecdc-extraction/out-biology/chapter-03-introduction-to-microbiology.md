3.1.1 GENERAL INTRODUCTION 
The kingdom Monera includes the most ancient, smallest, simplest form of life. All prokaryotes 
are  included  in  this  kingdom. Prokaryotes  (Greek- Pro-before,  karyon-nucleus)  means  the 
organisms lacking true nucleus.They exist everywhere on the earth even in thermal hot springs, 
supersaturated  saline environment, within other living organism, deep within the earth, at the 
bottom of the ocean, etc. Bacteria were probably the earliest life forms on earth. A teaspoonful 
of soil may contain millions  of bacteria.  
Organisms  present  in  this  kingdom  are  microscopic,  unicellular and  consist of 
prokaryotic type of cell (i.e. cell lack true/organized nucleus and membrane-bound organelles) 
with rigid cell wall. They contain circular, naked DNA (i.e. without histone protein covering) 
in cytoplasm  as genetic material.  This also  represents  the nucleus and called  as nucleoid or 
incipient nucleus. They are autotrophic as well as heterotrophic and are the major decomposers 
of an ecosystem. The sexual reproduction is absent.

1. Autotrophic Bacteria 
A small  group of bacteria show autotrophic  nutrition.  The bacteria  that synthesize  their  own 
organic food from inorganic substances are called autotrophic bacteria. They utilize sunlight or 
chemical energy as a source of energy. Autotrophic bacteria are of two types: 
a. Photoautotrophic bacteria:  These bacteria  utilize  light  energy to perform  photosynthesis 
process.  However,  bacterial  photosynthesis  is  different  from  green  plant  photosynthesis 
because water is not utilized as hydrogen donor so, oxygen is not evolved. This process is called 
anoxygenic  photosynthesis.  Instead  of  water  they  utilize  hydrogen  sulphide  (H
2
S), 
thiosulphate, hydrogen or other organic compounds. 
b. Chemoautotrophic bacteria:  
These bacteria  utilize  chemical  energy to convert inorganic compound to organic compound 
and this process is called as chemosynthesis. They get energy from ammonia, nitrate,  nitrite, 
ferrous iron, hydrogen sulphides and other inorganic compounds for the synthesis of their food. 
On the basis of chemical compounds they use as a source of energy, chemoautotrophic bacteria 
are  grouped  as  nitrifying  bacteria  (utilize  ammonia  or  nitrate),  Sulphur  bacteria  (utilize 
Hydrogen  sulphide),  Iron  bacteria  (utilize  ferrous  compound),  Hydrogen  bacteria  (utilize 
hydrogen), methane bacteria (utilize methane) etc.  
2. Heterotrophic Bacteria 
These are the bacteria which are unable to synthesize their organic food. They depend on other 
organisms  for  their  nutrition.  Most  of  the  bacteria  show  heterotrophic  mode  of  nutrition. 
Heterotrophic bacteria are of three types – 
a. Saprophytic bacteria: The bacteria that obtain their nutrition from dead and decaying organic 
matter  are called saprophytic bacteria. These bacteria breakdown the complex organic matter 
into  simpler  forms by secreting  different  types  of  digestive  enzymes. The bacteria  play  an 
important  role in the recycling of nutrients  by decomposition.  So, they are also called natural 
scavengers. They are the decomposers  of an ecosystem.  Some examples  are: Pseudomonas, 
Acetobacter, Clostridium, etc.  
b. Parasitic  bacteria: The parasitic  bacteria  obtain  their  nutrition  from  living  cell  of other 
organisms.  The  parasitic  bacteria  may  cause  diseases (pathogenic)  like Mycobacterium 
tuberculosis, Clostridium tetani, and Salmonella typhi. 
c. Symbiotic bacteria: The bacteria that live in close association  with other organism in such a 
way that both the partners get mutual benefit from this association are called symbiotic bacteria. 
Example: Rhizobium bacteria present in the root nodules of leguminous plant, Escherichia coli 
in the human intestine.

3.1.4 BACTERIAL GROWTH 
In higher organisms growth refers to an increase in size and volume of organism but in bacteria 
growth refers to an increase in number. The growth of Bacteria takes place by binary fission. 
Binary fission is a form of asexual reproduction  in single celled organisms by which one cell 
divide  into  two  cells  of  the  same  size.  A  bacterial  population  grows  in  a  geometric  or 
exponential manner, with each generation producing 2 cells, 4 cells, 8 cells, 16 cells, 32 cells, 
64 cells and so on. Bacteria need certain conditions  to grow, and these conditions are not the 
same for all bacteria. Factors such as oxygen, pH, temperature  and light affect the growth of 
microorganisms. Osmotic  pressure,  atmospheric  pressure,  and  available  moisture are  some 
other additional factors that affect the growth of bacteria. The growth rate is also influenced by 
the composition of the growth medium. When the nutritive  culture medium is inoculated with 
a given number of bacteria and incubated for a sufficient period, it gives a characteristic  growth 
pattern  of  bacteria.  The graphical  representation  of the number  of living  bacteria  cells  in a 
population over a period of time is called  bacterial  growth curve. The growth curve has four 
phases:  
1. Lag phase 
During   this   phase,   the   cells   are 
metabolically  active  but  not  dividing.  The 
cells  prepare themself  for  division  by 
synthesizing  enzymes  and  various  other 
molecules.  
2. Log phage (Exponential phase) 
In  this  phase  the  bacterial  cell  divides 
continuously.  The  cells  are  most  active 
metabolically  in  this  phase.  The  cells  are 
growing, dividing  and increasing  in number 
exponentially. 
3. Stationary phase 
The  growth  of  bacteria  reaches  a  state  during  which  there  is  no  net  increase  in  bacterial 
population as the number of dividing cells is equal to the number of dying cells and this phase 
is called as stationary  phase. This is due to depletion  of nutrition  and accumulation  of toxic 
wastes products as a byproduct in log phase.  
4. Decline phase (Death phase) 
As the nutrition  decreases and accumulation  of toxic waste product increases, the number of 
dying cells begins to exceed the number of newly born cell during this phase.

3.2.1 GENERAL INTRODUCTION 
Viruses  are  ultramicroscopic  intracellular  parasites,  which  consist  of  a single  nucleic  acid, 
either DNA or RNA covered with a protein coat called capsid. Viruses do not consist of cellular 
organization.  These  are  acellular  pathogens.  Hence,  they  are  neither prokaryotes nor 
eukaryotes. Viruses crystalize like non-living things (chemicals) outside the host cell but show 
only living characteristics  that is they replicate only inside the host cell. So, they are considered 
midway/borderline between the living and nonliving things. They may even be purified  and 
crystallized  like chemical substances. Viruses are completely  devoid of the machinery for life 
processes so it  requires  the presence  of  host  cell  for  its  multiplication/replication. As  they 
compulsorily need host cells to replicate or show their living characteristics,  it is considered an 
obligatory parasite. In five kingdom classification  of Whittaker  virus doesn’t get any defined 
place. A newly formed complete infectious virus particle is called virion.  
Until the end of the nineteenth  century, the history  of viruses  is just  a part of the history  of 
infectious diseases. The name ‘virus’ is coined by Beijerink from Latin word meaning “venom” 
or similar  poisonous fluid. The branch of Biology that deals with the study of viruses is called 
virology.  
Viruses are ultramicroscopic acellular obligatory parasites having either DNA or RNA as a 
genetic material and can replicate only inside the living host cell.  
Viruses have following characteristics:  
1. Viruses are ultramicroscopic intracellular  parasites.  
2. They are acellular i.e. they do not have cellular level of organization. 
3. They  are obligate  parasite and can  multiply  only within living  host cell as they lack 
enzyme system. 
4. Viruses do not have a system to produce ATP. 
5. They contain only a single type of nucleic acid, either DNA or RNA.  
6. The nucleic acids are enclosed by proteinous coat called capsid.

3.2.2 STRUCTURE 
The shape and size of virus particles  vary widely. The size of viruses ranges from 10-300 nm 
in diameter. The viruses may be rod-shaped, filamentous,  roughly spherical, tadpole like etc.  
Viruses  are  non-living  objects,  because  they  lack  cellular  organization  and  protoplasm. 
However, the structure of virus consists of two main types of chemicals viz. nucleic acid (about 
6%) and protein (about 96%). Nucleic acid presents at the center and form core. Nucleic acid 
exists  either  in the form of DNA or RNA or both. DNA or RNA may be single-stranded  or 
double-stranded.   Single-stranded  DNA and double-stranded  RNA are  characteristics  never 
found in living system and, hence unique to the viruses.  
 
Figure 3.2.1 Structure of viruses 
 
The protein  molecule  surrounds  around  the  nucleic  acid  filament  and  forms  the  protective 
sheath called as capsid. This capsid is formed of identical repeated subunits called capsomeres. 
Size, shape, arrangement, and composition  of sub-units  are specific  characteristics,  i.e. fixed 
for each type  of virus. The combined  structure  formed  by the core  and capsid is  termed  as 
nucleocapsid.  
In some viruses, there is a thin envelope around the capsid that resembles typical cell membrane 
having a phospholipid bilayer. Viruses possess lysozyme in the region of their contact with host 
cell by the help of which they drill a hole in the host’s cell. 
 


 
 
3.2.3 ECONOMIC IMPORTANCE OF VIRUS 
Viruses have both positive effects as well as negative effects: 
Positive effects: 
1. Viruses  act as  simple  systems,  which can  be  used as  tools for research  and analysis. 
Studies  on  recombinant  DNA  technology  were  developed  using  bacteriophages 
(Bacterial Viruses). 
2. The  lysogenic  life  cycle  of  certain  viruses  allows  easy  manipulation  of  the  viral 
genome. Viruses  are used as vectors to deliver therapeutic genes into humans in gene 
therapy.  
3. They are  also used to cure cancer in several ways like virotherapy  and VDEPT (virus 
directed enzyme pro drug therapy). 
4. Viruses are also the primary sources of vaccines used to prevent the viral infections. 
5. Some of the viruses can also be used for the control of insect pests of our crop plants.  
6. Some virus infected plant parts appear beautifully variegated. E.g. Tulip flowers.  
7. Viruses may destroy the bacterial cells  and make water free from bacteria.  
 
Negative effects: 
1. Viruses cause diseases in plants and animals including human beings.  
2. Viruses  cause  danger  to many  biochemical  industries  especially  bacteriophages  that 
destroy the useful bacteria for such industries.  
3. Viruses may be used as biological weapons.

3.3.1 INTRODUCTION 
The word biotechnology is derived from two words i.e. biology and technology. Biotechnology 
is the controlled use of biological agents, such as microorganisms  or cellular components, 
for beneficial use.  
 
Microbes such as bacteria, fungi and viruses are found in nearly every environment and make 
up much of the biomass on earth. They have long been used by humans for several purposes. 
The use of microbes in biotechnology is known as microbial biotechnology.  
 
Microbial biotechnology is a dynamic field that provides a wide range of products and services 
to a diverse number of industries.  These include food production, drug development,  disease 
prevention  and  diagnostics,  nutritional  supplements,  energy  and  alternative  fuels, 
environmental monitoring  and waste management, forensic sciences, and many more.  
 
3.3.2 IMPACT OF BIOTECHNOLOGY IN THE FIELD OF MICROBIOLOGY 
The use of microbial biotechnology is described below:  
1. In Medicine:  
The concept  of  microbial  biotechnology  is  used  in human  and  veterinary  medicine  by the 
following ways:  
a. Antibiotics:  
Antibiotics  are  substances obtained  from  microorganisms   that  help  in  controlling 
different types of diseases. They are obtained from different types of microorganisms.  
b. Vaccines:  
Vaccines  are  the substances derived from  microorganisms  used to immunize  against 
disease. Vaccines  are  produced by using  the concept of fermentation technology and 
recombinant DNA technology. The use of vaccines such as smallpox, polio, tetanus etc. 
led to the virtual elimination of these diseases in regions of the world where the vaccines 
have been deployed.  
c. Gene therapy:  
It  is  a  technique  that  uses  genes for  the  treatment  of  genetic  disorders or  chronic 
diseases. In this technique, new copies of genes are added or replaced by a defective or 
missing gene in a patient’s cells by the use of genetic engineering.

leaves, give flavor, colour and taste.  
c. Enzymes production:  
Certain type of bacteria are used to produced enzymes like proteases, pectinase etc. 
d. Biofuel production:  
Biofuel is produced by the use of microbial  biotechnology which may be used instead

3. In Agriculture: 
Micro-organisms  are  found  in  the  soil  and  help  to improve  agricultural productivity  in 
following ways: 
 
a. Biofertilizers: Phosphate and nitrogen are important elements for the growth of plants. 
These  elements  are  found naturally  in the environment;  however, plants  have limited 
ability  to extract them. Microbes,  like Penicillium  bilaii dissolve  the phosphate in  the 
soil and make available  to the root of the plants. The bacteria like, Rhizobium, present 
in  root  nodules  of  leguminous  plants  convert  the  atmospheric  nitrogen  into  nitrate 
which  are  absorb  by  the  root  of  the  plant.  These  processes  take  place  naturally. 
However, Biofertilizers are made from these types of organisms when applied by either 
coating seeds, or putting directly into the soil.  
 
b. Bio-herbicides:  Weeds  are  the  plants  that compete  with  crops  for  water,  nutrients, 
sunlight and space. It also harbors  insect and disease. Some micro-organisms  function 
as bio-herbicides because they possess invasive genes that can attack the defense genes 
of the weeds, thereby killing  it. The  advantage of using bioherbicides  is that it cannot 
harm the environment and survive long enough for the next growing season.  
c. Improvement of variety: 
By  transferring  useful  genes  from  microorganisms,  improved  varieties  of crops  are 
produced, enhancing various desirable traits. Improved varieties  include the characters 
like high yielding, high nutrients content, disease resistance variety, pest resistance etc.  
  


 
 
 
d. Development of GMO: 
The  genetically  modified organism  (GMO), also  known as  transgenic  organisms  are 
having foreign genes or foreign DNA.  
 
4. In Environment field:  
With  recent  advances  in  biology,  materials,  computing,  and  engineering,  environmental 
biotechnologist  now is able to use microbial  communities  for a wealth of services  to society. 
These include detoxifying contaminated water, wastewater, sludge, sediment, or soil, capturing 
renewable energy from biomass, sensing contaminants or pathogens, and protecting the public 
from dangerous exposure to pathogens. 
 
a. In waste disposal:  
It includes treatment of domestic and industrial effluents or wastes by using microbes.  
b. Bioremediation: One  of  the  most  beneficial  uses  of  microbial   biotechnology  is 
bioremediation,  which is  the use of bacteria to clean up environmental  contaminants. 
This  process involves  introducing a certain species  of bacteria  into contaminated sites 
to break  down toxic substances.  Example:  genetically  modified bacteria  are  used  to 
clean up oil spills.  
c. Bioindicator: Microbes   are   useful   as   bioindicators   to   detect  the   presence   of 
environmental pollutants, such as heavy metals, and other hydrocarbons.  
 
3.3.3 ETHICAL ISSUES OF MICROBIAL BIOTECHNOLOGY  
Microbial  biotechnology  is a controversial  topic  because it  comes  with some  concerns  and 
issues. Ethical issues of microbial  biotechnology include the availability  and use of privileged 
information, the potential for ecological harm, access to new drugs and treatments, and the idea 
of interfering with nature. These issues include many defects and side effects of products, tools 
and techniques along with economic, and environmental loss and other negative impacts.  
 
A huge risk of biotechnology in the field of medicine is its impact during clinical trials. Because 
of these risks, extensive research should be performed before even thinking of introducing  to 
human. The technology may be often expensive compared to traditional  treatments.  
 
Another objection  is that it interferes  with nature and violates the natural integrity  of species 
by deliberately  bringing  about  genetic  modification.  Genetically  modified  plants  have also 
reduced the need of fertilizers  thus minimized the pesticides pollution to rivers and costal water 
resources. However the tools and products caused many defects in environment.  
 
Legal  and  religious  issues  are  being  arises  in  the  use  of  biotechnological  techniques. 
Particularly  in  techniques  such  as  stem  cell  technology,  gene therapy,  and human  genome 
projects.
