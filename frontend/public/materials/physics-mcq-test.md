## 1. Top 10 Exam Traps (NEB Class-11 Physics)
| # | Trap | Defense |
|---|------|---------|
| 1 | Using °C instead of K in gas laws / PVT equations | **EVERY** gas law needs absolute T (K): T(K) = °C + 273.15. A catastrophic, recurring error. |
| 2 | Inverting Graham's Law ratio | r₁/r₂ = √(M₂/M₁): the **lighter** gas (smaller M) is **faster**. Heavy-gas-faster is the classic flip. |
| 3 | Mixing units (cm vs m) in formulas | Convert everything to SI before substituting; answer-check dimensions at the end. |
| 4 | Forgetting latent heat in steam burns | Steam at 100°C burns worse than water at 100°C: it **releases ~2260 kJ/kg** on condensing first. |
| 5 | Mirror sign convention slips | Cartesian convention: distances against incident light are **negative**; concave f < 0, convex f > 0. f = R/2 always. |
| 6 | m = −v/u sign misread | Negative magnification = **real, inverted** image; positive = virtual, erect. |
| 7 | Precision vs accuracy confusion | Accuracy = closeness to TRUE value; Precision = closeness to EACH OTHER. “Cluster far from target” = precise but not accurate. |
| 8 | Significant-figures rounding rule | +/−: round to least DECIMAL PLACES. ×/÷: round to least SIGNIFICANT FIGURES. |
| 9 | Dimensional analysis overuse | It CANNOT give dimensionless constants (½, 2π) nor relations with +,−, sin, log, exp. |
| 10 | Work as F·d with the wrong angle | Work = Fd cosθ with θ between **force and displacement**; 90° ⇒ W = 0. |

## 4. Boundary & Limit Cards
- **Dimensional analysis boundary:** valid only for multiplicative relations; cannot derive constants, trig/exp relations, or multi-term expressions.
- **Curved-mirror boundary:** mirror formula assumes paraxial (small-angle) rays; spherical aberration appears for large apertures → use parabolic mirrors for telescopes.
- **Newton's cooling boundary:** rate ∝ ΔT holds only for SMALL temperature differences between body and surroundings.
- **Gas-law boundary:** ideal behaviour is best at LOW pressure / HIGH temperature; real-gas deviation grows at high P, low T (intermolecular forces + finite molecular volume matter).
