Kinetic and potential energy

Q1.Find the kinetic energy of a 2 kg body moving at 5 m/s. [1]

KE = ½mv² = ½(2)(25). = 25 J.

25 J

Kinetic and potential energy

Q2.Find the potential energy of a 2 kg body raised 10 m (g = 10 m/s²). [1]

PE = mgh = 2(10)(10). = 200 J.

200 J

Kinetic and potential energy

Q3.A body falls 5 m from rest. Find its speed at the bottom (g = 10 m/s²). [2]

mgh = ½mv² ⇒ v = √(2gh) = √100. = 10 m/s.

10 m/s

Conservation of mechanical energy

Q4.A pendulum bob is released from a height of 0.8 m. Find its speed at the lowest point (g = 10 m/s²). [2]

v = √(2gh) = √(2 × 10 × 0.8) = √16. = 4 m/s.

4 m/s

Conservation of mechanical energy

Q5.A 1 kg ball is projected up a smooth plane inclined at 30° at 10 m/s. How far along the plane does it travel before stopping? [3]

Rise h = u²/2g = 100/20 = 5 m. Along the plane s = h/sin 30° = 10 m.

10 m

Elastic collisions

Q6.Define an elastic collision and state its defining property. [2]

A collision in which both momentum and kinetic energy are conserved; the coefficient of restitution e = 1.

KE is conserved, e = 1

Elastic collisions

Q7.Two equal masses collide elastically, one at rest. Describe the motion afterwards. [3]

For equal masses the formulas give v₁ = 0 and v₂ = u. The moving body stops and the stationary one moves off with the original velocity.

They exchange velocities

Inelastic collisions

Q8.What is a perfectly inelastic collision? [2]

One in which the bodies stick together after impact: momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not, and e = 0.

The bodies move off together, e = 0

Inelastic collisions

Q9.A 2 kg body at 6 m/s sticks to a 4 kg body at rest. Find the kinetic energy lost. [3]

v = 12/6 = 2 m/s. KE before = ½(2)(36) = 36 J, after = ½(6)(4) = 12 J, so 24 J is lost.

24 J

Inelastic collisions

Q10.For that collision, what fraction of the kinetic energy survives? [2]

Final 12 J against an initial 36 J. The ratio is 12/36 = 1/3.

One third

Inelastic collisions

Q11.Why is momentum conserved in an inelastic collision when kinetic energy is not? [2]

Momentum conservation depends only on the absence of external forces during the brief impact. Kinetic energy is converted into heat, sound and deformation instead of being retained.

No external force acts; energy changes form

Inelastic collisions

Q12.A 10 g bullet at 500 m/s embeds in a 990 g block at rest on a smooth surface. Find the loss of kinetic energy. [3]

v = (0.01)(500)/1 = 5 m/s. KE before = ½(0.01)(250000) = 1250 J; after = ½(1)(25) = 12.5 J, so 1237.5 J is lost.

≈1238 J
