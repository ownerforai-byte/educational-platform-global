Moment of a force and torque

Q1.Find the torque of a 10 N force applied perpendicularly at 0.5 m from a pivot. [1]

τ = Fr sin θ = 10(0.5)(1). = 5 N m.

5 N m

Moment of a force and torque

Q2.A 20 N force acts at 30° to a 2 m rod, at its end. Find the torque about the other end. [2]

τ = Fr sin θ = 20(2)(0.5). = 20 N m.

20 N m

Moment of a force and torque

Q3.Two children of mass 30 kg and 20 kg sit on a 4 m seesaw. Where must they sit to balance it? [2]

Taking moments about the pivot: 30x = 20(4 − x). 50x = 80 ⇒ x = 1.6 m from the 30 kg child.

1.6 m and 2.4 m from the pivot

Conditions of equilibrium

Q4.State the two conditions for a rigid body in equilibrium. [1]

The vector sum of the forces must vanish (no translation) and the sum of the moments about any point must vanish (no rotation).

ΣF = 0 and Στ = 0

Conditions of equilibrium

Q5.A uniform 2 m rod of weight 20 N is pivoted at one end and held horizontally by a string at the other end, making 30° with the rod. Find the tension. [3]

The weight acts at the centre (1 m). Moments about the pivot: 20(1) = T sin 30°(2). T = 20/1 = 20 N.

20 N

Conditions of equilibrium

Q6.A uniform metre rod of weight 10 N is supported at its centre. A 20 N weight hangs at the 20 cm mark. Where must a 30 N weight be hung to balance it? [2]

The 20 cm mark is 30 cm from the centre, giving a moment 20(30) = 600 N cm. 30d = 600 ⇒ d = 20 cm on the other side, at the 70 cm mark.

At the 70 cm mark

Laws of solid friction

Q7.Define the coefficient of friction. [1]

It is the ratio of the limiting friction to the normal reaction, μ = F/N; it is dimensionless.

μ = F/N

Laws of solid friction

Q8.A 5 kg block rests on a horizontal table with μ = 0.4. Find the limiting friction (g = 10 m/s²). [2]

N = mg = 50 N, so F = μN = 0.4(50). = 20 N.

20 N

Laws of solid friction

Q9.A 30 N horizontal force acts on that block. Find its acceleration. [3]

Net force = 30 − 20 = 10 N (friction acts while it slides). a = 10/5 = 2 m/s².

2 m/s²

Laws of solid friction

Q10.Why is the force of static friction greater than kinetic friction? [2]

At rest the microscopic contact points weld together and every one of them must be broken before sliding starts. Once sliding, contact is renewed continuously and the average bond is weaker.

Static bonds are stronger than sliding ones

Angle of friction and repose

Q11.If μ = 1/√3, find the angle of friction. [2]

tan λ = μ = 1/√3. λ = 30°.

30°

Angle of friction and repose

Q12.A body just begins to slide down a plane inclined at 30°. Find the coefficient of friction. [3]

At the angle of repose θ, μ = tan θ. μ = tan 30° = 0.577.

μ = 1/√3 ≈ 0.577
