Significant figures

Q1.How many significant figures are there in 0.00450? [1]

Leading zeros are not significant; the digits 4, 5 and the trailing 0 are. 3 significant figures.

3 s.f.

Significant figures

Q2.How many significant figures are there in 2.50 × 10³? [1]

The trailing zero after the decimal point is significant; the power of ten is not counted. 3 significant figures.

3 s.f.

Precision and accuracy

Q3.Which is the more precise measurement: 4.6 cm or 4.65 cm? [2]

Precision depends on the smallest division a scale can read. 4.65 cm is read to 0.01 cm, 4.6 cm only to 0.1 cm.

4.65 cm is more precise

Q4.Add 12.11, 0.2 and 3.1234, keeping the correct number of significant figures. [2]

For addition the answer keeps the fewest decimal places (0.2 has one). 12.11 + 0.2 + 3.1234 = 15.4334.

15.4 (one decimal place)

Q5.Multiply 2.5 by 1.24 and round correctly. [2]

For multiplication the answer keeps the fewest significant figures (2.5 has two). 2.5 × 1.24 = 3.10.

3.1 (two significant figures)

Scientific notation

Q6.Express 0.000456 in scientific notation to two significant figures. [2]

Shift the point four places right: 4.56 × 10⁻⁴. Rounding 4.56 to two figures gives 4.6.

4.6 × 10⁻⁴

Rounding and fractional error

Q7.The mass of a body is (12 ± 0.5) kg. Find the fractional and percentage error. [3]

Fractional error = Δm/m = 0.5/12 = 0.0417. Percentage error = 0.0417 × 100%.

0.042 and 4.2%

Rounding and fractional error

Q8.The radius of a circle is (2.1 ± 0.05) cm. Find the percentage error in its area. [3]

Area A = πr², so the relative error doubles: ΔA/A = 2(Δr/r). = 2(0.05/2.1) = 0.0476.

4.8%

Significant figures

Q9.A mass of 5.00 g occupies 1.25 cm³. Find the density to the correct number of significant figures. [3]

ρ = m/V = 5.00/1.25. = 4.00 g/cm³, and three significant figures are justified by both data.

4.00 g/cm³

Q10.Why is 3.14 × 2.0 written as 6.3 rather than 6.28? [2]

The factor 2.0 carries only two significant figures. 3.14 × 2.0 = 6.28, which must be rounded to two figures.

6.3

Precision and accuracy

Q11.A true length is 10 m. One student measures 9.98 m and another 10.0 m. Whose reading is more accurate? [2]

Accuracy is closeness to the true value, so compare the errors: 0.02 m and 0.00 m.

10.0 m — its error is zero

Rounding and fractional error

Q12.The period of a pendulum is measured five times: 2.0, 2.1, 1.9, 2.1 and 1.9 s. Find the mean and the percentage error. [3]

Mean = (2.0 + 2.1 + 1.9 + 2.1 + 1.9)/5 = 2.0 s. Maximum deviation = 0.1 s, so the percentage error = (0.1/2.0) × 100%.

2.0 s with a 5% error
