Work done by a constant force

Q1.Find the work done by a 20 N force in moving a body 5 m along its direction. [1]

W = Fs cos θ = 20(5)(1). = 100 J.

100 J

Work done by a constant force

Q2.Find the work done by a 10 N force acting at 60° to a displacement of 4 m. [2]

W = Fs cos 60° = 10(4)(0.5). = 20 J.

20 J

Work done by a constant force

Q3.Find the work done by gravity on a 2 kg body falling 5 m (g = 10 m/s²). [2]

W = mgh = 2(10)(5), positive because gravity and the displacement agree. = 100 J.

100 J

Work done by a constant force

Q4.A friction force of 15 N acts over 10 m. Find the work done by friction. [2]

Friction opposes the motion, so θ = 180°. W = 15(10)(−1) = −150 J.

−150 J

Work-energy theorem

Q5.A 4 kg body speeds up from rest to 10 m/s. Find the work done on it. [2]

Work–energy theorem: W = ΔKE = ½(4)(100) − 0. = 200 J.

200 J

Work-energy theorem

Q6.A 2 kg block moving at 6 m/s is brought to rest by friction over 9 m. Find the friction force. [3]

½mv² = Fs ⇒ ½(2)(36) = F(9). F = 36/9 = 4 N.

4 N

Power

Q7.Find the power when 300 J of work is done in 5 s. [1]

P = W/t = 300/5. = 60 W.

60 W

Power

Q8.A 60 kg person climbs 10 m in 20 s. Find the average power developed (g = 10 m/s²). [2]

W = mgh = 60(10)(10) = 6000 J. P = 6000/20 = 300 W.

300 W

Power

Q9.A motor lifts a 200 kg load at a steady 0.5 m/s. Find its power output. [2]

F = mg = 2000 N, and P = Fv = 2000(0.5). = 1000 W.

1 kW

Power

Q10.Define one watt. [1]

One watt is the power developed when one joule of work is done in one second: 1 W = 1 J s⁻¹.

1 J/s

Efficiency

Q11.A pump delivers 800 W of useful power from a 1 kW input. Find its efficiency. [2]

η = (useful power/input power) × 100 = (800/1000) × 100. = 80%.

80%

Efficiency

Q12.A machine lifts a 50 kg load through 6 m in 30 s using an input power of 150 W. Find its efficiency (g = 10 m/s²). [3]

Useful work = 50(10)(6) = 3000 J, so useful power = 3000/30 = 100 W. η = (100/150) × 100 = 66.7%.

66.7%
