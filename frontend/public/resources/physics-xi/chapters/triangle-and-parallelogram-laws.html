Parallelogram law and the resultant

Q1.Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles. Find the resultant. [1]

R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQcos 90°) = √(9 + 16). = 5 N.

5 N

Parallelogram law and the resultant

Q2.Find the resultant of two equal forces P acting at 120°. [1]

R = √(P² + P² + 2P²cos 120°) = √(2P² − P²). = P.

R = P, bisecting the angle

Triangle law of vector addition

Q3.What is the greatest possible resultant of 5 N and 3 N? [1]

The resultant is largest when the forces act in the same direction. Rmax = 5 + 3.

8 N

Triangle law of vector addition

Q4.What is the least possible resultant of 5 N and 3 N? [1]

The resultant is smallest when they act in opposite directions. Rmin = 5 − 3.

2 N

Parallelogram law and the resultant

Q5.Find the resultant of 6 N and 8 N acting at 60°. [2]

R² = 36 + 64 + 2(6)(8)cos 60° = 100 + 48 = 148. R = √148 = 12.2 N.

12.2 N

Q6.Two equal forces of 10 N give a resultant of 10 N. Find the angle between them. [2]

100 = 100 + 100 + 200cos θ ⇒ cos θ = −0.5. θ = 120°.

Special cases and equilibrium

Q8.The resultant of P and Q has the same magnitude as P. Find the angle between them. [3]

P² = P² + Q² + 2PQcos θ ⇒ cos θ = −Q/(2P). So θ = cos⁻¹(−Q/2P).

θ = cos⁻¹(−Q/2P)

Polygon law

Q9.Forces of 3 N, 4 N and 5 N act along the sides of a triangle taken in order. Find the resultant. [3]

The vectors close the triangle head to tail, so the polygon law gives a zero vector. No unbalanced force remains.

Zero — the body stays in equilibrium

Special cases and equilibrium

Q10.A body is in equilibrium under three forces, two of which are 6 N and 8 N at right angles. Find the third. [2]

Their resultant is √(36 + 64) = 10 N. The third force must be the equilibrant: equal and opposite.

10 N opposite to the resultant

Parallelogram law and the resultant

Q11.Forces of 8 N and 6 N have a resultant of 10 N. Find the angle between them. [3]

100 = 64 + 36 + 96cos θ ⇒ cos θ = 0. θ = 90°.

90°

Polygon law

Q12.State the polygon law and use it to find the resultant of four forces along the sides of a closed quadrilateral. [2]

If several vectors are represented in magnitude and direction by the sides of a polygon taken in order, their resultant is the closing side. For a closed polygon the closing side is zero.

Resultant = 0
