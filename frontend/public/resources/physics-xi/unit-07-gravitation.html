Unit 7: Gravitation

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Mechanics

Newton's universal law is the first law of physics that is genuinely universal: the same constant appears for a falling apple and for a galaxy cluster. The unit also contains the sharpest tension between what the law predicts and what we can see.

What the model asserts

  1. F = G m1 m2 / r^2 with G = 6.67430e-11 m^3 kg^-1 s^-2, a constant fixed exactly in the 2019 SI and equal and opposite between the two bodies.
  2. g = GM/R^2 = 9.80665 m/s^2 at the surface by definition; the local value varies with latitude and altitude, and in Nepal it is roughly 9.78 to 9.83 m/s^2.
  3. Gravitational potential energy is U = -GMm/r, and the field satisfies g = -grad V, so field and potential are two views of one object.
  4. Escape velocity from the surface is v_e = sqrt(2GM/R) = 11.2 km/s for Earth.
  5. Kepler's three laws follow from the inverse-square law: elliptical orbits with the Sun at a focus, constant areal velocity, and T^2 proportional to a^3.
  6. Galaxy rotation curves and gravitational lensing by clusters both show more gravitating mass than the visible matter accounts for.
  7. The shell theorem makes spherical mass distributions tractable: outside a spherically symmetric body the field is exactly that of a point mass at the centre, which is why planetary orbits have closed-form solutions.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • Newtonian gravity is an excellent approximation for weak fields (GM/rc^2 much less than 1) and slow speeds (v much less than c); near a black-hole horizon and in the early universe it must be replaced by general relativity.
  • The shell theorem - treating an extended body as a point - needs spherical symmetry, and it fails for flattened or irregular mass distributions such as a disc galaxy or a mountain.
  • The constant-g treatment near the surface is valid only for heights small compared with Earth's radius; at 3000 m the reduction of g is already about 0.1 %.
  • Tidal effects, which come from the differential part of the field and scale as r^(-3), govern ocean tides and the Roche limit; the plain 1/r^2 force does not.
  • Kepler's laws are exact only for two bodies in an inverse-square field; with three or more bodies the orbits are not closed and no exact solution exists.

Open questions and mysteries

What is dark matter made of, and has any candidate particle ever been detected directly?

Open question

No candidate has been confirmed; the evidence is gravitational and the identity is unknown (NASA).

How did the first stars form, and how long before the first one lit up?

Open question

Dark matter provides the gravitational scaffolding, but the details of the first objects remain an active modelling challenge.

Can a stable system of planets be configured, and do real systems migrate between configurations?

Frontier

Planet-planet scattering, the Lidov-Kozai mechanism and post-Newtonian corrections are all active research areas.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Compute the escape velocity from the Moon and compare it with the value needed for a crewed mission from Earth.
  2. Measure g in your schoolyard with a simple pendulum, estimate the uncertainty, and compare with the predicted local value.
  3. Derive Kepler's third law from Newton's law of gravitation and check it against the Moon's and Earth's orbital data.
