Unit 18: Dispersion

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Waves & Optics

Dispersion is the wavelength dependence of refractive index. It explains rainbows and chromatic aberration, it is the reason a spectrum carries chemical information, and it contains one of the sharpest traps in optics: the difference between phase velocity and the speed that carries information.

What the model asserts

  1. Dispersion means n depends on wavelength, so different colours bend by different amounts and a lens focuses them at different distances.
  2. Cauchy's empirical form n = A + B/lambda^2 gives an approximate wavelength dependence in transparent regions.
  3. Normal dispersion - n rising as lambda falls - is what produces the familiar spectrum order from red to violet.
  4. Anomalous dispersion near an absorption band reverses the sign of dn/dlambda, and a dispersive prism can then select a single narrow band from white light.
  5. The group velocity v_g = c/(n - lambda dn/dlambda) can exceed c or become negative in anomalously dispersive media without violating relativity, because the phase velocity carries no information.
  6. A spectrum is a fingerprint: the blackbody shape gives temperature, the line positions give composition, and the Doppler shift gives motion.
  7. Glass has a larger refractive index for blue light than for red, so short wavelengths are bent more and a prism sends violet towards the inside of the spectrum.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • Cauchy's two-parameter fit is valid only for normal dispersion in transparent regions; it diverges near a resonance and cannot describe absorption at all.
  • Information travels at the group velocity, so superluminal phase velocity is a feature of the mathematics, not a violation of causality.
  • At a boundary between media the frequency of light does not change; what changes is the speed and hence the wavelength - a classic examination trap.
  • Spectral lines are broadened by temperature, pressure and Doppler motion, and that broadening sets the precision with which a line centre can be measured.
  • In a dispersive medium the group velocity differs from the phase velocity, so the speed of light in glass quoted as c/n is a phase speed and not the speed at which a pulse front travels.

Open questions and mysteries

Can a dispersion profile be engineered that resolves wavelengths beyond the diffraction limit of the device itself?

Frontier

Metasurface and metamaterial designs are actively pursuing this; causality and usable bandwidth are the limiting constraints.

How are atomic energy levels and their linewidths set, and how far can they be predicted from first principles?

Under active scrutiny

Spectroscopy is precise enough to test atomic theory at parts per billion, which keeps refining rather than contradicting it.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Using Cauchy's formula and a measured index at two wavelengths, predict the order in which colours emerge from a prism.
  2. Explain why the same prism shows a much narrower spectrum when immersed in water.
  3. Compare the resolving power of a prism and a diffraction grating for the same beam width, and identify which is better for faint astronomical sources.
