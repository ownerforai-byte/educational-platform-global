Unit 21: Potential & Potential Energy

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Electric & Magnetism

Potential is the scalar alternative to the vector field. Because only differences are measurable, the zero of potential is a convention - and because the field is conservative, path never enters the answer.

What the model asserts

  1. Electric potential is energy per unit charge, V = U/q, and a potential difference is the work done per unit charge by the field.
  2. For a point charge V = kq/r with V tending to zero at infinity, and the potential difference is dV = -E.dl along any path.
  3. Only potential differences are physically meaningful; the choice of zero is a convention, and it changes no experiment.
  4. The potential energy of a system of point charges is the sum of pairwise terms q1 q2/(4 pi epsilon0 r_ij); the self-energy of a continuous distribution requires a self-consistent calculation.
  5. Equipotential surfaces are perpendicular to field lines, and the work in moving a charge between two points on the same equipotential is zero.
  6. Potential is a scalar, which is why circuit and field problems are often shorter in potential language than in field language.
  7. Potential is defined only up to an additive constant, so only differences are physical; a conductor is one equipotential surface, and the field is minus the gradient of the potential.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The 1/r potential law assumes a point charge in vacuum; for a conductor the entire surface is one equipotential at a single value.
  • Potential differences are path-independent, so only the endpoints matter - but a potential difference between two points is not simply E.d unless the path is straight and along the field.
  • Moving a charge changes the stored energy of the whole system, and the work is shared between the field and the agent that moves it.
  • In a medium with permittivity epsilon the potential of a charge is reduced by the factor epsilon_r; the free charge does not change, the field does.
  • The expression U = qV holds for a conservative, time-independent field; in a time-varying field the work done on the charge also changes the energy stored in the field itself.

Open questions and mysteries

How much energy is stored in an electromagnetic field, and how is it distributed in space?

Under active scrutiny

Classically the energy density is (1/2) epsilon E^2 plus a magnetic term; the quantum description in terms of photons remains less intuitive.

Can energy be extracted usefully from the vacuum?

Frontier

Casimir and related effects are real and measurable but tiny; extracting macroscopic energy from vacuum fluctuations is not established.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Compute the potential on the axis of a uniformly charged disc and identify its far-field limit.
  2. Show in one dimension that E = -dV/dx, using a finite-difference argument rather than calculus.
  3. Compare the energy stored in a charged capacitor with the work required to charge it, and explain why they are equal.
