Unit 11: Quantity of Heat

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Heat & Thermodynamics

Quantity of heat is where the first law becomes arithmetic: energy accounting with a heat capacity, a latent heat and a clear statement of which system you are describing.

What the model asserts

  1. Q = m c dT requires no phase change, a constant specific heat over the range, and a clearly defined system.
  2. Specific heat capacity is per unit mass; the molar heat capacity is C = M c, where M is the molar mass.
  3. During a phase change the temperature stays constant while heat is added, because the energy goes into changing the structure rather than the motion of the particles.
  4. Latent heat enters as Q = m L; the specific latent heat of vaporisation of water at 100 degrees C is about 2.26e6 J/kg.
  5. Internal energy is a state function of the body; heat is the transfer, and the first law writes dQ = dU + dW with work done by the system positive.
  6. Calorimetry rests on the statement that heat lost by the hot body equals heat gained by the cold one - true only for an isolated system.
  7. Heat capacity is quoted along constant volume or constant pressure because only the path-independent change of internal energy is the same for both; the difference between them is the work done by expansion.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The constant-c approximation fails for gases, whose heat capacity varies with temperature, and for any interval that crosses a phase change.
  • Latent heat is not a universal constant: it varies with pressure and temperature, and for water it falls as the critical point is approached.
  • Calorimetry assumes an isolated system; in a real cup the heat capacity of the container must be included, and it often dominates the error.
  • Specific heat of water is commonly approximated as 4.18 kJ/(kg.K) over 0 to 100 degrees C, but it varies by more than 1 % across that range.
  • Water's large specific heat is a symptom of hydrogen-bonded network rearrangements, not a separate slow-to-heat mechanism, and it is the reason oceans moderate coastal climate.

Open questions and mysteries

How is energy distributed among the translational, rotational and vibrational modes of a molecule as its temperature rises?

Under active scrutiny

Equipartition gives the high-temperature limit and quantum theory gives the low-temperature limit; the crossover in real materials is modelled, not derived.

Can the heat capacity of a complex solid be computed from first principles to experimental accuracy?

Frontier

Modern ab initio methods approximate it well for simple crystals; no exact general theory exists.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Plan a calorimetry experiment to determine the specific heat of a metal and list every systematic error you can foresee.
  2. Show quantitatively why 1 kg of steam at 100 degrees C can release more energy than 1 kg of water at 100 degrees C.
  3. Estimate the energy needed to heat a classroom by 10 degrees C and compare it with a student's daily food intake.
