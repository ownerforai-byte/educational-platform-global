Unit 20: Electric Field

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Electric & Magnetism

The electric field turns a force law into a description of the space around a charge: it says what force a test charge would feel at every point, whether or not one is actually there.

What the model asserts

  1. The electric field is force per unit positive test charge, E = F/q, directed along the force on a positive test charge.
  2. A point charge produces E = kq/r^2 directed radially, and superposition means the total field is the vector sum of the individual fields.
  3. Field lines are a visualisation, not a count of anything: their density represents field strength, they start on positive charge and end on negative charge, and they never cross.
  4. The field inside a conductor in electrostatic equilibrium is zero; the field of an infinite non-conducting sheet is sigma/(2 epsilon0), and of a conducting sheet sigma/epsilon0.
  5. Because F = qE for any charge, the field can be treated as an independent variable - the reason Gauss's law is so powerful.
  6. A moving charge creates a magnetic field as well, so the electric and magnetic fields of a charge are two aspects of a single electromagnetic field.
  7. Field lines are bookkeeping: the number crossing a surface is proportional to the flux, so a denser bundle means a stronger field, even though the lines themselves have no physical substance.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • Closed-form field results exist only where symmetry is high enough - spherical, cylindrical or planar; any other distribution requires integration.
  • Electrostatic fields are conservative, so the work around a closed path vanishes and a scalar potential can be used instead.
  • In a dielectric the field is reduced by the relative permittivity, and polarisation P must be introduced; writing E = F/q with a 'free' charge only is wrong inside matter.
  • Electrostatic reasoning does not extend to time-varying fields: Maxwell's displacement current means a changing electric field generates a magnetic field.
  • Superposition is a statement about linear response, and real dielectrics are generally not linear, so polarisation and non-linear media need their own treatment.

Open questions and mysteries

How does a charge act on matter that is not charged?

Under active scrutiny

The field picture handles it through polarisation; whether the field itself is physical or a bookkeeping device is a deeper interpretational question.

Can a static field be completely screened in any geometry?

Open question

Only conductors screen, and the shape of the screening conductor matters; a fully screened, shape-independent result is not generally available.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Find the field on the axis of a uniformly charged ring and show where it reaches its maximum.
  2. Show that two infinite sheets carrying opposite uniform charges produce a uniform field between them and zero outside.
  3. Plot the field of an electric dipole along its axis and compare with the far-field 1/r^3 law.
