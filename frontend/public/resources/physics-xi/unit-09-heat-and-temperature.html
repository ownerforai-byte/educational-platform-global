Unit 9: Heat and Temperature

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Heat & Thermodynamics

The distinction between heat and temperature is the doorway to thermodynamics. Heat is energy in transit, temperature is a property of a body, and the zeroth law is what makes thermometers possible at all.

What the model asserts

  1. Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; it is a process quantity, not a substance stored inside an object.
  2. Temperature measures the average microscopic kinetic energy of particles, with the equipartition theorem distributing energy across the available degrees of freedom.
  3. The zeroth law - if A equals B thermally and B equals C, then A equals C - is what makes a thermometer a thermometer.
  4. Temperature is intensive; heat and internal energy are extensive, so they scale with the amount of matter.
  5. The kelvin is defined by the Boltzmann constant, k = 1.380649e-23 J/K exactly, and is the only SI scale proportional to a true zero.
  6. Thermal equilibrium is dynamic: microscopic energy exchange continues forever, but with no net flow in either direction.
  7. Temperature is a state variable made meaningful by the zeroth law, while heat is a path-dependent transfer; the two are different kinds of quantity and are not interchangeable inside an equation.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • 'Temperature equals average kinetic energy' is exact for a monatomic ideal gas; for molecules with rotation and vibration the equipartition theorem spreads the energy over more degrees of freedom.
  • A thermometer reading is the equilibrium temperature of the thermometer-object pair, so the reading lags and can measurably disturb a small object.
  • At nanometre and smaller scales, different definitions of temperature (electronic, vibrational, configurational) need not agree, so 'the temperature' of a nanoscale system can be ambiguous.
  • Temperature scales other than kelvin are proportional only over a limited range; Celsius and Fahrenheit diverge as you approach absolute zero.
  • Thermodynamic equilibrium requires thermal and mechanical balance together, so a sealed system can be at thermal equilibrium and still be pushing on its surroundings.

Open questions and mysteries

Can two bodies at the same temperature still exchange heat?

Under active scrutiny

Yes - equilibrium is statistical and exchange continues in both directions, but the net flow vanishes; the statistical statement is what makes the zeroth law a law.

Is there a single, unique temperature for a system far from equilibrium?

Open question

Different definitions diverge for non-equilibrium and nanoscale systems, and no universal choice is settled.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Rewrite the sentence 'the bucket of water has a lot of heat in it' so that it is physically correct, and explain what is wrong with the original.
  2. Explain why a metal spoon feels colder than a wooden spoon at the same room temperature.
  3. Derive the final temperature of two bodies placed in thermal contact from conservation of energy and a constant-heat-capacity model.
