Unit 14: Reflection at Curved Mirrors

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Waves & Optics

Curved mirrors turn the law of reflection into an imaging device. Everything in the unit rests on one approximation - rays close to the principal axis - and on one convention that, used inconsistently, produces most of the wrong answers.

What the model asserts

  1. The focal length of a spherical mirror is half the radius of curvature, f = R/2; concave mirrors converge and convex mirrors diverge.
  2. A convex mirror always produces a virtual, upright and diminished image of a real object, which is why it is the safe choice for a vehicle mirror.
  3. The mirror equation 1/f = 1/v + 1/u and the magnification m = -v/u hold under the New Cartesian sign convention.
  4. Three principal rays suffice for any ray diagram: parallel to the axis (reflected through the focus), through the centre of curvature (reflected back on itself), and through the focus (reflected parallel to the axis).
  5. The point focus of a spherical mirror exists only in the paraxial approximation; spherical aberration grows rapidly with the aperture.
  6. The 18 hexagonal segments of the JWST mirror are aligned to act as a single 6.5 m primary, focused to a precision of a small fraction of the width of a human hair.
  7. A spherical mirror has a single focus only while the aperture is small compared with the radius of curvature; beyond that, spherical aberration spreads the focus and the image softens with age.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • Mirror and lens formulae are paraxial: the rays must be close to the principal axis. Wide apertures require real ray tracing or an aspherical surface such as a parabola.
  • A real image can be caught on a screen; a virtual image cannot be projected, which is the physical difference behind the two categories.
  • Mixing sign conventions is the single most common source of wrong signs; fix one convention and use it in every diagram.
  • For a mirror moving at speed, the image can appear to move faster than light; no information travels faster than light because the image is an optical construction, not a material object.
  • The mirror equation and magnification are paraxial results; a wide field of view needs aberration-corrected designs such as the Ritchey-Chretien and active mirrors like JWST's.

Open questions and mysteries

Can an optical system form a perfect image of an extended object across a wide field of view?

Open question

Aberration theory rules out perfect imaging with simple surfaces; multi-element and wave-optics designs trade cost against the remaining errors, and no system is perfect.

How far can real-time adaptive optics push atmospheric correction for ground-based telescopes?

Engineering frontier

Correction works extremely well for well-resolved guide stars, and the limit is set by sensor speed, guide-star availability and photon flux.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Construct ray diagrams for an object at infinity, beyond C, at C, between C and F, and at F for a concave mirror, and tabulate image position, nature and magnification.
  2. Explain why a vehicle's wing mirror is convex rather than flat, and why the image of a following car looks smaller than it is.
  3. A concave mirror has f = 20 cm and the object is 30 cm in front of it: find the image position and magnification, and decide whether the image is real.
