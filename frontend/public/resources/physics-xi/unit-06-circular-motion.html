Unit 6: Circular Motion

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Mechanics

Circular motion is where the vector treatment of force has to be taken seriously: the speed can be constant while the force is anything but, and the word 'centripetal' names a direction rather than a new kind of force.

What the model asserts

  1. Uniform circular motion has constant speed but constantly changing velocity, so the acceleration is a = v^2/r directed toward the centre.
  2. 'Centripetal force' is simply the name for the net inward force; it is supplied by tension, friction, gravity, magnetism or a normal reaction, not by an extra interaction.
  3. In an inertial frame there is no outward force; the 'centrifugal force' of a rotating frame is a bookkeeping pseudo-force.
  4. Banking a road removes the need for friction: the horizontal component of the normal reaction supplies the centripetal force.
  5. For a conical pendulum omega^2 = g/r; for a circular orbit v^2 = GM/r.
  6. The ground itself accelerates: Earth's rotation contributes about 0.034 m/s^2 at the equator, roughly 0.35 % of g, which is why effective g falls with latitude.
  7. In uniform circular motion the acceleration has constant magnitude but a continuously rotating direction, so the velocity vector changes even though its size does not.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The centripetal analysis assumes a circular path at constant speed; if the speed also changes, a tangential acceleration must be added to the radial one.
  • The maximum safe speed of a vehicle on a curve is set by the friction limit mu, not by the acceleration itself, and the banking angle shifts that limit.
  • Working in Earth's rotating frame requires fictitious forces; ignoring them underestimates the effective weight by about 0.35 % at the equator.
  • Orbital motion neglects drag, tidal perturbation and orbital precession, all of which matter for long-lived satellites.
  • The centrifugal force is real inside a rotating frame and fictitious in an inertial one; describing a rotating system only works if you say which frame you are using.

Open questions and mysteries

Why do the outer parts of spiral galaxies rotate faster than their visible matter allows?

Open question

The standard explanation is an extended dark-matter halo, but the nature of that halo is unknown and no candidate particle has been confirmed (NASA).

Why do large, stable polygonal vortices form in rotating fluids, such as the hexagon on Saturn?

Open question

Laboratory experiments reproduce polygonal vortices but do not yet explain the range of sizes and their stability in full.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Derive the minimum speed at which a car can take a banked curve without depending on friction.
  2. Explain with a free-body diagram why a standing passenger feels pushed outward on a turning bus.
  3. Calculate the change in effective g at the equator caused by Earth's rotation and compare it with the measured value.
