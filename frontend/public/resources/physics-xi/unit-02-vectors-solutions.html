Unit 2 — Vectors

Physics XI (Phy. 101)  •  NEB Grade 11  •  4 teaching hours  •  Exercise solutions + conceptual bank

Every exercise of the unit — three laws of combination, resolution and unit vectors, scalar and vector products, condition of equilibrium — solved with the formulas NEB expects to see written, plus the short conceptual questions with model answers.

How the unit is examined.

  • 2 marks — dimensions of a vector product; state the condition of perpendicularity; define unit vector; find the angle between two vectors from their dot product.
  • 4 marks — resultant of two vectors by the parallelogram law (magnitude and direction); resolve a force into components and find the balancing force; compute work as F·d or torque as r×F.
  • 5–6 marks — prove the relation |A×B|² + (A·B)² = |A|²|B|², or derive tanα = Q sinθ/(P + Q cosθ) for the direction of a resultant.

Rule that keeps losing students marks: every vector answer is incomplete without its direction (an angle measured from a named reference) or, in 3-D, without the î, ĵ, k̂ components written out.

Exercise 2.1 — Resultant of two vectors: triangle and parallelogram laws

Q1. Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles to each other. Find the magnitude and direction of the resultant.

R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cosθ), with θ = 90° ⇒ cosθ = 0 R = √(3² + 4²) = √25 = 5 N tanα = Q sinθ/(P + Q cosθ) = 4(1)/(3 + 0) = 1.333 α = tan⁻¹(1.333) = 53.1° from the 3 N force

R = 5 N, 53° from the 3 N force (i.e. 37° from the 4 N force)

Q2. Two equal forces of magnitude P act at an angle of 120°. Show that their resultant has magnitude P.

R = √(P² + P² + 2P² cos120°), cos120° = −½ = √(2P² − P²) = √(P²) = P tanα = P sin120°/(P + P cos120°) = (0.866P)/(P − 0.5P) = 1.732 α = 60° → the resultant bisects the 120° angle.

R = P, bisecting the angle between the two forces — three equal forces at 120° are in equilibrium.

Q3. Two vectors of magnitudes 4 units and 3 units act at 60°. Find the magnitude of their resultant and the angle it makes with the 4-unit vector.

R = √(16 + 9 + 2×4×3×cos60°) = √(25 + 24 × 0.5) = √37 = 6.08 units tanα = 3 sin60°/(4 + 3 cos60°) = 2.598/(4 + 1.5) = 2.598/5.5 = 0.4724 α = 25.3°

R ≈ 6.08 units at 25° to the 4-unit vector

Q4. At what angle must two forces of 5 N and 8 N act so that their resultant is 10 N?

R² = P² + Q² + 2PQ cosθ 100 = 25 + 64 + 2(5)(8) cosθ 100 − 89 = 80 cosθ cosθ = 11/80 = 0.1375 θ = 82.1°

θ ≈ 82°

Q5. State and explain the triangle law of vector addition. What is the polygon law?

Triangle law: if two vectors are represented in magnitude and direction by the two sides of a triangle taken in order, their resultant is represented by the third side taken in the opposite order.

Polygon law: if several vectors are represented by the sides of a polygon taken in order, the resultant is represented by the closing side taken in the opposite direction. It follows from applying the triangle law repeatedly.

Closed polygon ⇒ zero resultant (equilibrium); the closing side reversed is the resultant.

Q6. The resultant of two equal forces is √3 times one of them. Find the angle between them.

R = √(P² + P² + 2P²cosθ) = P√(2 + 2cosθ) √3P = P√(2 + 2cosθ) ⇒ 3 = 2 + 2cosθ ⇒ cosθ = ½ ⇒ θ = 60°

θ = 60°

Exercise 2.2 — Resolution of vectors and resultant of many vectors

Q7. Resolve a force of 50 N acting at 30° above the horizontal into horizontal and vertical components.

Fx = F cosθ = 50 cos30° = 50 × 0.866 = 43.3 N Fy = F sinθ = 50 sin30° = 50 × 0.5 = 25.0 N Check: √(43.3² + 25²) = √(1875 + 625) = √2500 = 50 N ✓

Fx = 43.3 N, Fy = 25 N

Q8. A body is acted on by three forces: 10 N along the x-axis, 20 N at 120° and 30 N at 240°. Find the resultant.

Fx = 10 + 20 cos120° + 30 cos240° = 10 − 10 − 15 = −15 N Fy = 0 + 20 sin120° + 30 sin240° = 17.32 − 25.98 = −8.66 N R = √(225 + 75) = √300 = 17.3 N tanα = |−8.66|/|−15| = 0.577 ⇒ α = 30° measured from the −x axis toward −y, i.e. the resultant points at 210° from +x.

R = 17.3 N at 210° from the +x axis (or 30° below the −x axis)

Q9. A man can swim at 5 km h⁻¹ in still water and rows straight across a river flowing at 3 km h⁻¹. Find his resultant velocity and the direction in which he must head to land directly opposite.

Crossing straight across: velocities are perpendicular v = √(5² + 3²) = √34 = 5.83 km h⁻¹, drifting downstream, tanα = 3/5 ⇒ α = 31° downstream. To land opposite, he must head upstream at angle φ such that 5 sinφ = 3 ⇒ sinφ = 0.6 ⇒ φ = 36.9° upstream of the perpendicular, and the crossing speed becomes 5 cosφ = 4 km h⁻¹.

Heading straight across: 5.83 km h⁻¹ at 31° downstream. To land opposite: head 37° upstream, crossing at 4 km h⁻¹.

Q10. Explain why the maximum resultant of two vectors P and Q is P + Q and the minimum is |P − Q|.

R² = P² + Q² + 2PQ cosθ. Since −1 ≤ cosθ ≤ 1:

  • θ = 0° ⇒ R² = (P + Q)², so R = P + Q (maximum, parallel).
  • θ = 180° ⇒ R² = (P − Q)², so R = |P − Q| (minimum, antiparallel).

R lies between |P − Q| and P + Q; equality holds for parallel and antiparallel vectors respectively.

Exercise 2.3 — Unit vectors and vector addition in components

Q11. If A = 3î + 4ĵ, find its magnitude, direction and unit vector.

|A| = √(3² + 4²) = 5 θ = tan⁻¹(4/3) = 53.1° with the x-axis Â = A/|A| = (3î + 4ĵ)/5 = 0.6î + 0.8ĵ Check: |Â| = √(0.36 + 0.64) = 1 ✓

|A| = 5, direction 53° from +x, Â = 0.6î + 0.8ĵ

Q12. Given A = 2î + 3ĵ and B = î − ĵ, find A + B, A − B, |A + B| and the unit vector along A − B.

A + B = (2+1)î + (3−1)ĵ = 3î + 2ĵ |A + B| = √13 = 3.61 A − B = (2−1)î + (3+1)ĵ = î + 4ĵ |A − B| = √17 = 4.12 unit vector along A − B = (î + 4ĵ)/√17 = 0.243î + 0.970ĵ

A+B = 3î+2ĵ (3.61 units); A−B = î+4ĵ (4.12 units); û = (î+4ĵ)/√17

Q13. Find a vector of magnitude 10 units parallel to the vector 3î + 4ĵ.

Direction of 3î + 4ĵ: Â = (3î + 4ĵ)/5 Required = 10Â = 10(3î + 4ĵ)/5 = 6î + 8ĵ Check: √(36 + 64) = 10 ✓

6î + 8ĵ

Q14. Show that if A + B is perpendicular to A − B, then |A| = |B|.

(A + B)·(A − B) = 0 A·A − A·B + B·A − B·B = 0 |A|² − |B|² = 0 (since A·B = B·A) ⇒ |A| = |B|

Perpendicularity of the sum and difference forces equal magnitudes — the diagonals of a rhombus are perpendicular.

Exercise 2.4 — Scalar (dot) product

Q15. Find the angle between A = î + ĵ and B = î − ĵ.

A·B = (1)(1) + (1)(−1) = 0 |A| = √2, |B| = √2 cosθ = A·B/(|A||B|) = 0 ⇒ θ = 90°

θ = 90° (they are perpendicular)

Q16. A force F = 3î + 2ĵ − k̂ N displaces a body through d = 2î − 3ĵ + 4k̂ m. Find the work done.

W = F·d = (3)(2) + (2)(−3) + (−1)(4) = 6 − 6 − 4 = −4 J

W = −4 J; the negative sign means the force removes energy from the body (the force is partly opposed to the displacement).

Q17. If |A| = 3, |B| = 4 and A·B = 6, find the angle between them and |A × B|.

cosθ = 6/(3×4) = 0.5 ⇒ θ = 60° |A × B| = AB sinθ = 3 × 4 × sin60° = 12 × 0.866 = 10.39

θ = 60°, |A×B| = 10.4 units

Q18. For what value of p are the vectors 2î + pĵ + k̂ and 4î − 2ĵ + 2k̂ perpendicular?

A·B = 0 (2)(4) + (p)(−2) + (1)(2) = 0 8 − 2p + 2 = 0 ⇒ 2p = 10 ⇒ p = 5

p = 5

Q19. Find the projection of A = 2î + 3ĵ on B = î + ĵ.

Component of A along B = A·B̂ = (A·B)/|B| A·B = 2 + 3 = 5; |B| = √2 projection = 5/√2 = 3.54 units

3.54 units along B

Exercise 2.5 — Vector (cross) product

Q20. If A = 2î + ĵ and B = î + 3ĵ, find A × B and the area of the triangle formed by them.

A × B = | î ĵ k̂ | | 2 1 0 | | 1 3 0 | = î(1×0 − 0×3) − ĵ(2×0 − 0×1) + k̂(2×3 − 1×1) = 0î − 0ĵ + 5k̂ = 5k̂ Area of triangle = ½|A × B| = 2.5 square units Area of parallelogram = |A × B| = 5 square units

A × B = 5k̂; triangle area 2.5 units², parallelogram area 5 units²

Q21. A force F = 2î + 3ĵ N acts at the point (1, 2) m. Find the torque about the origin.

r = î + 2ĵ τ = r × F = | î ĵ k̂ | | 1 2 0 | | 2 3 0 | = k̂(1×3 − 2×2) = (3 − 4)k̂ = −k̂ N m

τ = −k̂ N m, i.e. 1 N m clockwise in the xy-plane

Q22. Show that î × ĵ = k̂, ĵ × k̂ = î, k̂ × î = ĵ, and that î × î = 0.

For unit vectors at right angles, |î × ĵ| = 1·1·sin90° = 1, and the direction is perpendicular to both, i.e. along k̂ by the right-hand rule. The cyclic order î → ĵ → k̂ reproduces the same rule; reversing the order reverses the sign (ĵ × î = −k̂).

For î × î, sin0 = 0, so the product vanishes.

Mutually perpendicular unit vectors in cyclic order give the next unit vector; a vector crossed with itself is the null vector.

Q23. Two vectors of magnitude 5 and 6 have a vector product of magnitude 15. Find the angle between them and the dot product.

|A × B| = AB sinθ = 30 sinθ = 15 ⇒ sinθ = 0.5 ⇒ θ = 30° or 150° A·B = AB cosθ = 30 cos30° = 25.98 (if θ = 30°) or 30 cos150° = −25.98 (if θ = 150°)

θ = 30° or 150°; A·B = ±26.0 — the dot product distinguishes the two cases by its sign.

Exercise 2.6 — Equilibrium and applications

Q24. State the condition for a body to be in equilibrium under three concurrent forces, and use it to find the force that balances 6 N and 8 N acting at 90°.

Three concurrent forces are in equilibrium when their vector sum is zero: the resultant of any two is equal and opposite to the third. (Equivalently, by Lami's theorem, P/sinα = Q/sinβ = R/sinγ.)

Resultant of 6 N and 8 N at 90° = √(36 + 64) = 10 N Balancing force = 10 N, opposite in direction to the resultant i.e. at 180° + tan⁻¹(8/6) = 180° + 53° = 233° from the 6 N force

10 N acting along the line opposite the resultant (equilibrant)

Q25. A body of weight 20 N hangs from two strings making 30° and 60° with the vertical. Find the tensions.

Resolving horizontally: T₁ sin30° = T₂ sin60° ⇒ T₁(0.5) = T₂(0.866) ⇒ T₁ = 1.732 T₂ Resolving vertically: T₁ cos30° + T₂ cos60° = 20 1.732T₂(0.866) + 0.5T₂ = 20 1.5T₂ + 0.5T₂ = 20 ⇒ T₂ = 10 N, T₁ = 17.32 N

T₁ = 17.3 N (string at 30°) and T₂ = 10 N (string at 60°)

Conceptual bank — 2-mark questions with model answers

C1. Can the magnitude of the resultant of two vectors be greater than the sum of their magnitudes?

No. R² = P² + Q² + 2PQcosθ is largest when cosθ = 1, giving R = P + Q. The magnitude of a sum can never exceed the sum of magnitudes (the triangle inequality).

C2. What is a unit vector and what is its use?

A unit vector has magnitude exactly 1 and is used to give a direction only: Â = A/|A|. Any vector can then be written A = |A|Â, separating magnitude from direction.

C3. Why is the dot product a scalar while the cross product is a vector?

The dot product measures the product of magnitudes and the cosine of the angle between them — a pure number times a unit, so it has no direction (work = F·d). The cross product measures the product of magnitudes and the sine, and its direction is perpendicular to the plane of the two vectors by the right-hand rule (torque = r×F).

C4. What is the physical meaning of A·B = 0 and A × B = 0?

A·B = 0 means the vectors are perpendicular (cosθ = 0); A × B = 0 means they are parallel or antiparallel (sinθ = 0), including the case where one is the null vector.

C5. Can three vectors of equal magnitude placed at 120° sum to zero? What is this result used for?

Yes. Each pair has resultant P, and the third vector cancels it: P + P cos120° + ... = 0. It is the standard result for three equal forces in equilibrium (e.g. a body pulled by three symmetric strings).

C6. State any two examples where resolution of vectors is essential.

(i) Projectile motion: the velocity is resolved into horizontal (uniform) and vertical (accelerated) parts. (ii) A body on an inclined plane: the weight is resolved into mg sinθ along the incline and mg cosθ normal to it.

C7. Why is displacement a vector while distance is a scalar?

Distance is the total path length, a pure magnitude. Displacement is the straight-line change of position from start to finish and needs a direction as well as a length; that is why a round trip has a non-zero distance but zero displacement.

C8. Give the dimensions of a vector product and of the area of a parallelogram in vector form.

A vector product has the dimensions of the product of the two vectors: for r × F, [L][MLT⁻²] = [ML²T⁻²], the dimensions of torque (same as energy but a different kind of quantity). The area of a parallelogram is ½... — |A×B| has the dimensions of the product of the two vectors, e.g. [L²] for two lengths.

C9. What is the condition for a particle to be in equilibrium under several coplanar forces?

Both the vector sum and the total moment must vanish: ΣF = 0 (so ΣFx = 0 and ΣFy = 0) and Στ = 0 about any point. For concurrent forces the moment condition is automatic, so only ΣF = 0 is needed.

C10. Two vectors have unequal magnitudes. Can their resultant have the same magnitude as one of them? Give an example.

Yes. 5 N and 5 N give 5 N at 120° (Q2). More generally R = P when Q² + 2PQcosθ = 0, i.e. cosθ = −Q/(2P), which is possible whenever Q ≤ 2P.

C11. What is a null vector? Give one situation in which it appears.

A null vector has zero magnitude and an undefined (or arbitrary) direction. It appears as the resultant of two equal and opposite vectors (a body in equilibrium) and as A × A.

C12. Is the vector product commutative? Justify.

No. A × B = −(B × A), because reversing the order reverses the right-hand-rule direction while the magnitude AB sinθ is unchanged. It is anticommutative.

C13. What does the magnitude of the vector product represent geometrically?

|A × B| = AB sinθ is the area of the parallelogram whose adjacent sides are A and B; half of it is the area of the triangle formed by them.

C14. Why can components of a vector be treated independently?

Because mutually perpendicular directions do not interfere: an acceleration along x produces no change of the y-component of velocity. This independence is what lets a projectile's horizontal uniform motion and vertical acceleration be solved separately.

Long-answer set — 4 to 6 marks, fully worked

L1. [5] Derive the expression for the magnitude and direction of the resultant of two vectors P and Q inclined at an angle θ.

Place P along the x-axis. Resolving Q:

Rx = P + Q cosθ, Ry = Q sinθ R = √(Rx² + Ry²) = √[P² + 2PQcosθ + Q²cos²θ + Q²sin²θ] = √(P² + Q² + 2PQ cosθ) tanα = Ry/Rx = Q sinθ/(P + Q cosθ)

R = √(P² + Q² + 2PQcosθ); α = tan⁻¹[Q sinθ/(P + Qcosθ)] measured from P. Special cases: θ = 0 → P + Q; θ = 90° → √(P²+Q²); θ = 180° → |P − Q|.

L2. [6] Prove that |A × B|² + (A·B)² = |A|²|B|².

|A × B| = AB sinθ ⇒ |A × B|² = A²B² sin²θ A·B = AB cosθ ⇒ (A·B)² = A²B² cos²θ Adding: A²B²(sin²θ + cos²θ) = A²B² = |A|²|B|²

Both products depend on θ only through sinθ and cosθ, and these combine to 1.

Proved — the identity shows why a cross product and dot product are "complementary" measures of the same pair of vectors.

L3. [5] A boat crosses a 1 km wide river at 4 km h⁻¹ heading perpendicular to the current, which flows at 3 km h⁻¹. How long does it take, and how far downstream does it land?

Crossing component = 4 km h⁻¹ (unchanged by the current) t = d/v = 1/4 h = 15 min Drift = (current)(t) = 3 × 0.25 = 0.75 km

Resultant speed = √(4² + 3²) = 5 km h⁻¹, along a path 37° downstream of the perpendicular.

15 min; 0.75 km downstream (resultant velocity 5 km h⁻¹ at 37° to the perpendicular)

L4. [4] If A = 2î − 3ĵ + 4k̂ and B = î + 2ĵ − k̂, find A + B, A − B, A·B, and the angle between them.

A + B = 3î − ĵ + 3k̂ A − B = î − 5ĵ + 5k̂ A·B = (2)(1) + (−3)(2) + (4)(−1) = 2 − 6 − 4 = −8 |A| = √(4 + 9 + 16) = √29 = 5.39 |B| = √(1 + 4 + 1) = √6 = 2.45 cosθ = −8/(5.39 × 2.45) = −8/13.2 = −0.606 ⇒ θ = 127°

A+B = 3î−ĵ+3k̂; A−B = î−5ĵ+5k̂; A·B = −8; θ ≈ 127° (obtuse — the negative dot product signals an angle greater than 90°)

The five traps that cost the most marks in Unit 2

1. Forgetting the direction. "R = 6.08 units" loses the second mark; "6.08 units at 25° to the 4-unit vector" earns it.

2. Using cosθ when the formula needs 2PQcosθ. The parallelogram law has the factor 2; the cosine rule for a triangle with included angle 180° − θ does not. Write the formula before substituting.

3. Sign errors in components. Always sketch the axes and write each component with its sign; the third-quadrant resultant of Q8 is a classic place to lose a sign.

4. Crossing in the wrong order. r × F and F × r differ by a minus sign; torque is r × F. State the point about which the moment is taken.

5. Treating dot and cross product answers as interchangeable. Work is F·d (scalar); torque is r×F (vector). A scalar answer to a torque question is marked wrong even if the magnitude is right.

Answer-writing habit for this unit. Sketch → formula → components with signs → magnitude → direction. In a 4-mark question the sketch itself usually carries a mark.
