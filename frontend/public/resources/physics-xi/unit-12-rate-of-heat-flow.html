Unit 12: Rate of Heat Flow

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Heat & Thermodynamics

How fast energy moves as heat depends on the mechanism: contact, bulk motion of a fluid, or electromagnetic radiation. Each mechanism has its own law, its own geometry, and its own place where the textbook approximation fails.

What the model asserts

  1. Conduction transfers energy through a medium without moving the medium; convection moves bulk fluid; radiation needs no medium at all.
  2. Fourier's law states dQ/dt = -k A dT/dx, with thermal conductivity k a material property and the minus sign meaning heat flows down the temperature gradient.
  3. Newton's law of cooling gives the convective rate dQ/dt = h A (T_surface - T_fluid), with h the convection coefficient, which depends on flow conditions rather than on geometry alone.
  4. The Stefan-Boltzmann law for a blackbody is P = epsilon sigma A T^4, with sigma = 5.670374419e-8 W/(m^2.K^4) exactly in the 2019 SI.
  5. Thermal resistances add in series and parallel exactly like electrical resistances, with R = L/(kA) for a slab.
  6. Building heat loss, the thermal performance of a wall, and the ability of a device to shed heat are all set by this network of resistances.
  7. Conduction, convection and radiation are three separate mechanisms: conduction is the diffusion of particle energy through a medium, convection needs a moving fluid, and radiation needs no medium at all.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • Fourier's law in its simple slab form assumes steady or quasi-steady state with a one-dimensional gradient; fast transients with a sharp thermal front need the heat equation instead.
  • At nanometre gaps, interfacial (Kapitza) resistance dominates, so the bulk conductivity alone badly under-predicts the thermal resistance of a nanostructure.
  • Radiation dominates above a few hundred degrees Celsius and in vacuum, so insulation design that ignores it fails in exactly the conditions where it matters most.
  • The Stefan-Boltzmann law as written is for a blackbody; real surfaces need an emissivity, and a non-grey surface needs a spectral integral rather than a single epsilon.
  • Newton's law of cooling assumes convective loss to air near ambient temperature; at high temperature radiative loss dominates and the cooling curve changes shape.

Common misconceptions — 3 to unlearn

“Heat always flows from the hotter body to the colder one, and stops only when the two reach the same temperature.”

Why it fails: That is right for conduction and convection, but radiation can flow in both directions simultaneously, and the net radiation flow depends on the fourth power of temperature, not on the difference.

Correct: The net conductive and convective flow is down the temperature gradient. For radiation the net power is P = epsilon sigma A (T_hot^4 - T_cold^4), which is down the gradient of the fourth power, and each body both emits and absorbs at all times.

“Metal is a good conductor of heat, so a metal spoon in a bowl of soup is used to stir it.”

Why it fails: A good conductor carries heat quickly away from the spoon's surface into the hand as well as into the soup, so the handle becomes hot. The cooking spoon is made of an insulator for exactly this reason.

Correct: A wooden or plastic handle is used because wood and plastic conduct heat poorly (k about 0.1 to 0.2 W/(m K)) compared with metal (copper 400 W/(m K)), so the handle stays near the ambient temperature while the metal part is in the soup.

“The amount of heat conducted through a slab depends only on the temperature difference across it.”

Why it fails: Fourier's law has three factors besides the temperature difference: the area, the length of the slab and the conductivity of the material. A thin sheet conducts far more heat than a thick one at the same dT.

Correct: dQ/dt = -k A dT/dx. Doubling the area doubles the flow; doubling the length halves it; and the conductivity of the material sets the scale. A wall's insulation value is best expressed as a thermal resistance, R = L/(k A).

Workbook — 3 questions with answers

numerical

A copper rod of length 1.0 m and cross-sectional area 1.0 x 10^-4 m^2 has its ends held at 100 degrees C and 0 degrees C. Take k = 400 W/(m K). Find the rate of heat flow through the rod.

Answer: dQ/dt = k A dT/dx = 400 x 1.0 x 10^-4 x (100 - 0) / 1.0 = 4.0 W. The rate is constant along a uniform rod, since the same energy passes every cross-section; it is the temperature gradient, not the heat flow, that changes.

derivation

Derive the form of the Stefan-Boltzmann law for the power radiated by a black body, starting from the fact that the radiated power per unit area is proportional to the fourth power of the absolute temperature.

Answer: The empirical starting point is that the power emitted per unit area of a black body is E = sigma T^4, where sigma is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant. For a body of area A the total emitted power is P = sigma A T^4, and the same body absorbs radiation from its surroundings, so the net power lost to an environment at temperature T_s is P_net = sigma A (T^4 - T_s^4). Dividing the equation by A gives the energy density of radiation as u = (4 sigma / c) T^4, and comparing with the Planck distribution shows that sigma = 2 pi^5 k^4 / (15 c^2 h^3) = 5.670374419 x 10^-8 W/(m^2 K^4), a value fixed by the constants k, h and c.

reasoning

A vacuum flask keeps soup hot for hours. Using the three mechanisms of heat transfer, explain which one the flask is designed to suppress and how the design does it.

Answer: The three mechanisms are conduction, convection and radiation, and a good flask suppresses all three, in that order of importance for a hot liquid. Conduction is reduced by the double wall and the thin, poorly conducting neck (a glass or plastic lid rather than metal); convection is suppressed by the narrow neck and the still air trapped in the evacuated gap, since there is no bulk motion of a medium to carry heat; radiation is reduced by silvering the reflective surfaces facing the gap, because a polished surface has a low emissivity and so both emits and absorbs little. The evacuated space between the walls also removes the thermal conduction through the air, which is the dominant loss in an ordinary single-walled bottle.
