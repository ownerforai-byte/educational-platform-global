Unit 5: Work, Energy and Power

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Mechanics

Energy is the bookkeeping device that survives every form of friction. The work-energy theorem is the most useful sentence in mechanics: it replaces a force problem with an energy problem, and it stays valid even when the forces themselves are hard to compute.

What the model asserts

  1. Work done by a constant force is the scalar product W = F.s.cos(theta); only the component of the force along the displacement contributes.
  2. The work-energy theorem states that the net work on a particle equals its change in kinetic energy, dK = W_net, whatever the forces turn out to be.
  3. A conservative force depends on position only; for it a potential energy exists with W = -dU, and the closed-path integral of the force vanishes.
  4. Near the Earth's surface gravity is conservative with U = m g h; the spring force is conservative with U = (1/2) k x^2.
  5. Mechanical energy conservation (K + U constant) holds only when no non-conservative work (friction, drag, driving) is done.
  6. Power is the rate of energy transfer: P = dW/dt, and for a constant force P = F.v, so a bicycle climbs steeply not because it does more work but because it does it faster.
  7. Work is a path-independent energy transfer only for conservative forces; friction turns ordered mechanical energy into disordered thermal energy, which is why the work-energy theorem has to carry a friction term.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • Energy conservation is bookkeeping, not a claim that energy disappears: friction converts mechanical energy into internal energy and heats both surfaces.
  • Non-conservative forces cannot be described by a potential and must be handled through the work-energy theorem directly.
  • The constant-g potential m g h is a local approximation; the exact field potential is -GMm/r, and the two agree only for heights small compared with Earth's radius.
  • Efficiency is always defined against a specific output, can never exceed 1, and for most machines falls as the load rises because friction grows faster than useful work.
  • Power is a rate, not an amount: the same total energy can be delivered slowly or quickly, and the two are physically different because the time left to react is different.

Common misconceptions — 3 to unlearn

“Work is done only when a force is applied; a body moving on its own does no work on itself.”

Why it fails: Work is a transfer of energy by a force through a displacement, and the agent doing the work is not restricted to whoever pushes. A falling body does work on whatever it strikes, and the muscles of a person walking upstairs do work on the person.

Correct: W = F.s cos theta is the work done by a force on a body. A body can do work on another body even while the two move together, and the work done by the net force equals the change in kinetic energy.

“Energy is conserved, so the energy of a book that slides to rest on a table must still be there somewhere.”

Why it fails: The mechanical energy (K + U) of the book is not conserved, because kinetic friction is a non-conservative force. The first law still holds for the book-plus-table-plus-surroundings system, but the energy has left the book as internal energy and sound.

Correct: Total energy of the isolated system is conserved; mechanical energy of the book alone is not. The work done by friction equals the loss of mechanical energy, and it appears as thermal energy of the book and table and as sound.

“Since F = ma, a force acting for a longer time always does more work.”

Why it fails: Work is F times the displacement, and a longer time does not imply a larger displacement. A small force acting for a long time through a small distance can do less work than a large force acting briefly through a large distance.

Correct: Compare W = F s cos theta, not F t. Power P = dW/dt compares rates of doing work; a large power applied briefly can transfer less total energy than a small power applied for long.

Workbook — 3 questions with answers

numerical

A 2.0 kg body is lifted steadily through 5.0 m in 4.0 s. Find the work done against gravity and the average power developed.

Answer: Work done against gravity W = m g h = 2.0 x 9.8 x 5.0 = 98 J. Average power P = W / t = 98 / 4.0 = 24.5 W. (Lifted steadily means the applied force equals mg, so the net work on the body is zero and its kinetic energy does not change.)

derivation

Derive the work-energy theorem for a body acted on by a constant resultant force F moving along its line of action through a distance s.

Answer: Newton's second law gives F = m a. Since a = v dv/ds, F = m v dv/ds, so F ds = m v dv. Integrating from the initial speed u to the final speed v gives W = integral F ds = integral m v dv = (1/2) m v^2 - (1/2) m u^2 = change in kinetic energy. The theorem therefore says that the net work on a body equals its change in kinetic energy.

reasoning

A car of mass 1500 kg accelerates from rest to 20 m/s in 6 s on a level road. Use the work-energy theorem to find the average driving force, and explain why this average is smaller than the peak force needed at the start.

Answer: Change in kinetic energy = (1/2)(1500)(20^2 - 0) = (1/2)(1500)(400) = 3.0 x 10^5 J. Over 6 s the average driving force is W / s. From the constant-acceleration kinematics, s = (1/2) a t^2 = (1/2)(20/6)(36) = 60 m, so F_avg = 3.0 x 10^5 / 60 = 5.0 x 10^3 N. The average is smaller than the peak because the driving force is largest at the start, when the speed (and hence the power required) is lowest and the resistive forces (rolling resistance, air drag) are smallest; the rest of the work goes into overcoming resistance during the later, faster part of the motion.

Open questions and mysteries

How is the energy dissipated by friction partitioned between the two rubbing surfaces?

Frontier

The partition depends on temperature, speed and material pair; tribology still models it empirically rather than from first principles.

Why does time run in one direction rather than both?

Open question

The second law is statistical in the standard account, and the deeper question of why the universe began in a very low-entropy state is not settled by that account.
