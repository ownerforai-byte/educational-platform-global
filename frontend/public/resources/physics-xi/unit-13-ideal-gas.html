Unit 13: Ideal Gas

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Heat & Thermodynamics

The ideal gas is a model with a stated domain: it works when molecules are far apart and barely interacting. Its real value is that it links temperature to microscopic motion, so the same equation can be read as a statement about atoms.

What the model asserts

  1. PV = nRT with R = 8.314462618 J/(mol.K) exactly in the 2019 SI, where n is the amount of substance in moles.
  2. Boyle's, Charles's and Avogadro's laws are limiting cases of the ideal-gas equation, valid at low density and at temperatures well above condensation.
  3. Kinetic theory gives P = (1/3)(N/V) m <v^2>, so the pressure of a gas is the momentum transfer from its molecules hitting the walls.
  4. For a monatomic gas the mean translational kinetic energy is (3/2) k T, and the rms speed is sqrt(3RT/M).
  5. The internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature, U = n C_V T, and for a monatomic gas C_V = 3R/2.
  6. Mayer's relation C_P - C_V = R follows from the first law together with the ideal-gas equation.
  7. The ideal gas law is a limit rather than a definition: it holds exactly only at infinite dilution, and real gases show the condensation and critical behaviour the law cannot describe.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The model needs negligible molecular volume and negligible intermolecular forces: low density and a temperature well above the condensation temperature.
  • The van der Waals equation (P + a n^2/V^2)(V - nb) = nRT corrects the two main failures - attractive forces (a) and finite molecular size (b).
  • Equipartition, and therefore the plateau value of the heat capacity, fails at low temperature where the quantum energy levels are not thermally populated.
  • A gas in equilibrium has a single pressure and temperature; a non-equilibrium gas has neither, so the equation of state cannot even be written down.
  • Kinetic theory needs equipartition, which fails once the quantum level spacing is comparable to kT - exactly why helium must be cooled below about 4 K to liquefy.

Open questions and mysteries

Can translational motion be cooled below the quantum ground state of the confining potential?

Frontier

Laser and evaporative cooling reach picokelvin temperatures for motion, but a confinement potential has a lowest level that cannot be crossed; how that floor depends on the geometry is still open.

Why does the heat capacity of a gas rise in steps at low temperature while a solid's varies smoothly?

Under active scrutiny

Rotational and vibrational levels freeze out one by one below their characteristic temperatures - the Debye, rotor and oscillator models - which is settled for idealised cases and still refined for real materials.

Inquiry prompts

  1. Compute the rms speed of nitrogen molecules at room temperature and compare it with the speed of sound in air.
  2. Use the first law and C_V to show that the internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature.
  3. Plot the P-V diagrams of an ideal gas and a van der Waals gas and mark the region where real behaviour departs.
