Unit 22: Capacitor

Physics XI | NEB (Grade 11) | Phy. 101  •  Content area: Electric & Magnetism

A capacitor is an energy store defined by geometry and dielectric. The unit is where the parallel-plate formula gets derived from Gauss's law, and where students first meet the gap between an ideal component and a real one.

What the model asserts

  1. Capacitance is C = Q/V; for a parallel-plate capacitor C = epsilon0 epsilon_r A/d, so capacitance grows with area and shrinks with separation.
  2. The energy stored is U = (1/2) C V^2 = Q^2/(2C), and the energy density in the dielectric is u = (1/2) epsilon E^2.
  3. Capacitors in parallel add capacitance; in series the reciprocals add.
  4. A capacitor stores energy rather than supplying it: an ideal charged capacitor discharges through any available path, and the discharge time constant is R C.
  5. Real capacitors are limited by dielectric breakdown, leakage current, equivalent series resistance and self-inductance at high frequency.
  6. Supercapacitors deliver far more power than batteries but store far less energy per kilogram, and the two technologies occupy different niches for that reason.
  7. Capacitance depends on geometry and dielectric only, never on the charge or the voltage: adding dielectric raises the capacitance by the permittivity ratio, and a connected battery supplies the extra charge at unchanged voltage.

Conditions, assumptions and limits

  • The parallel-plate formula assumes the separation is much smaller than the plate dimensions, so fringing can be ignored, and that the dielectric fills the gap uniformly.
  • In a dielectric the bound charges reduce the field by 1/epsilon_r, but the energy per unit volume usually still increases because polarisation contributes.
  • At DC, leakage limits how long a charge can be held; at high frequency, inductance and ESR dominate, and the ideal capacitor stops being a capacitor.
  • Electrolytic capacitors are polar and will be destroyed by reverse voltage, while ceramic and film types are non-polar but have different leakage and stability behaviour.
  • The stored energy lives in the electric field, not in the charges; discharging a capacitor does not lose that energy, it converts it back into current and heat.

Common misconceptions — 3 to unlearn

“A capacitor stores charge, and the energy of a charged capacitor is the energy of the charges it holds.”

Why it fails: The energy is stored in the electric field between the plates, not in the charges themselves. A charged capacitor that is short-circuited loses all its energy in the spark, even though the charge on the plates was never 'used up'.

Correct: The stored energy is U = (1/2) C V^2 = Q^2 / (2C) = (1/2) Q V, and it is the energy of the field. Equivalently the energy density is (1/2) epsilon0 E^2, so energy is stored wherever the field is - between the plates, not on them.

“Capacitors in series add their capacitances, exactly as resistors in series add their resistances.”

Why it fails: In series the same charge is on every capacitor, and the total voltage is the sum of the voltage drops; since V = Q/C, the reciprocal rule follows. In parallel the voltages are equal and the charges add, so the capacitances add directly.

Correct: Parallel: C = C1 + C2 + ...; series: 1/C = 1/C1 + 1/C2 + ..., so the series combination is always smaller than the smallest capacitor. This is the opposite of the resistor rule, and the standard exam trap.

“The capacitance of a capacitor depends on the charge stored on it.”

Why it fails: Capacitance is fixed by the geometry and by the dielectric; it is the property that makes V proportional to Q. If C depended on Q, the relation V = Q/C would not be linear.

Correct: C = epsilon0 epsilon_r A / d for a parallel-plate capacitor depends only on the plate area, the separation and the dielectric. The charge and voltage change with the situation; C does not.

Workbook — 3 questions with answers

numerical

A 10 microfarad capacitor is charged to 12 V and then disconnected from the battery. Find the charge stored and the energy in the capacitor, and the energy per unit volume if the plates are 5 cm square and separated by 2 mm.

Answer: Q = C V = 10 x 10^-6 x 12 = 1.2 x 10^-4 C (120 microcoulomb). Energy U = (1/2) C V^2 = 0.5 x 10 x 10^-6 x 144 = 7.2 x 10^-4 J. The plate area is A = (0.05)^2 = 2.5 x 10^-3 m^2 and the volume of field is A d = 2.5 x 10^-3 x 2 x 10^-3 = 5 x 10^-6 m^3, so the energy density is 7.2 x 10^-4 / 5 x 10^-6 = 144 J/m^3. (After disconnection the charge and the energy are unchanged.)

derivation

Derive the capacitance of a parallel-plate capacitor and the energy it stores, using Gauss's law for the field between the plates.

Answer: Between large parallel plates of area A carrying charges of density sigma = Q/A, a Gaussian pillbox straddling one plate gives E A = sigma A / epsilon0, so E = sigma / epsilon0 = Q / (epsilon0 A). The potential difference between the plates separated by d is V = E d = Q d / (epsilon0 A). Hence C = Q / V = epsilon0 A / d, and with a dielectric of relative permittivity epsilon_r, C = epsilon0 epsilon_r A / d. The energy stored is obtained from the work done in charging: U = integral from 0 to Q of (q/C) dq = Q^2 / (2C) = (1/2) C V^2. The field is uniform only away from the edges, so the derivation assumes the plate separation is small compared with the plate dimensions.

reasoning

A 2 microfarad and a 3 microfarad capacitor are connected in series across a 12 V battery, and then, while still connected, a third capacitor is added in parallel with the 2 microfarad one. Find the charges and voltages across the capacitors before and after the change.

Answer: Initially, in series, the same charge is on both: 1/C = 1/2 + 1/3, so C = 1.2 microfarad and Q = C V = 1.2 x 12 = 14.4 microcoulomb on each. The 2 microfarad capacitor has 7.2 V across it and the 3 microfarad one 4.8 V. Adding a 3 microfarad capacitor in parallel with the 2 microfarad one makes a 5 microfarad combination; in series with 3 microfarad the total is 1/(1/5 + 1/3) = 1.875 microfarad, so the charge on the series string becomes 1.875 x 12 = 22.5 microcoulomb. The 5 microfarad pair carries 22.5 microcoulomb at 4.5 V and the 3 microfarad one carries 22.5 microcoulomb at 7.5 V. The lesson is the practical one: a small capacitor in series with a large one takes a disproportionate share of the voltage, and connecting a battery across an isolated small capacitor can exceed its rating.

Open questions and mysteries

How can more energy be stored at a higher voltage in a smaller volume?

Engineering frontier

Solid electrolytes, nanostructured dielectrics and breakdown engineering are all being pursued, and each trades cost, reliability and lifetime against energy density.

Is there a fundamental upper bound to the energy that can be stored in a given volume?

Open question

Fundamental limits exist, including the scale at which the vacuum itself breaks down, but they lie far above present engineering practice.
