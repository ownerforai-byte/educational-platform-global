6.8 · Definite Integrals and Area — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Evaluation · Properties · Area under a curve · Area between curves

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Evaluation 2 marks

C1. What does a definite integral represent geometrically? [2]

Ans. The signed area between the curve and the x-axis, from the lower limit to the upper limit.

Evaluation 2 marks

C2. Why is the area described as signed? [2]

Ans. Parts of the curve below the x-axis contribute negative values, because the function takes negative values there.

Properties 2 marks

C3. Why do we subtract the lower curve from the upper one? [2]

Ans. The integrand must be the height of the region above each point, and that height is the difference of the two y-values.

Properties 2 marks

C4. What happens to a definite integral if the limits are interchanged? [2]

Ans. Its sign changes: ∫ᵇₐ f = −∫ₐᵇ f, because the step dx reverses direction.

Area under a curve 2 marks

C5. Why must the points of intersection be found first? [2]

Ans. They are the boundaries of the enclosed region, so without them the correct limits of integration are unknown.

Area between curves 2 marks

C6. What if the curve crosses the x-axis inside the interval? [2]

Ans. Integrate each piece separately and take absolute values before adding, otherwise positive and negative areas cancel.
