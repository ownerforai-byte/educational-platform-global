2.3 · General Values of Trigonometric Equations — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics sin θ = k · cos θ = k · tan θ = k · Equations reducible to quadratics

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

sin θ = k 2 marks

C1. Why do trigonometric equations have a general solution? [2]

Ans. Trigonometric functions are periodic, so once one solution is found every angle differing by a whole period is also a solution.

sin θ = k 2 marks

C2. What is the general solution of sin θ = k, with k = sin α? [2]

Ans. θ = nπ + (−1)ⁿα, n ∈ ℤ. The (−1)ⁿ switches between the two symmetric positions.

cos θ = k 2 marks

C3. What is the general solution of cos θ = k, with k = cos α? [2]

Ans. θ = 2nπ ± α, n ∈ ℤ, because cosine is symmetric about 0 and repeats every 2π.

cos θ = k 2 marks

C4. What does the statement n ∈ ℤ mean in a general solution? [2]

Ans. n is any integer; each value of n produces one member of the infinite family of solutions.

tan θ = k 2 marks

C5. What is the general solution of tan θ = k, with k = tan α? [2]

Ans. θ = nπ + α, n ∈ ℤ, since tangent repeats with period π.

Equations reducible to quadratics 2 marks

C6. How do you obtain the solutions lying in [0, 2π] from a general solution? [2]

Ans. Substitute successive integers for n and keep only the values that fall inside the interval; usually two or three values survive.
