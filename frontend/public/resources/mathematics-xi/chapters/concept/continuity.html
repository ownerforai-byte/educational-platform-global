6.2 · Continuity and Discontinuity — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Continuity at a point · Types of discontinuity · Finding constants · Continuity and differentiability

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Continuity at a point 2 marks

C1. What are the three conditions for continuity at x = a? [2]

Ans. f(a) must be defined, lim(x→a) f(x) must exist, and the two must be equal.

Types of discontinuity 2 marks

C2. Does continuity imply differentiability? [2]

Ans. No. f(x) = |x| is continuous at 0 but has no derivative there, because the slope is −1 on the left and +1 on the right.

Types of discontinuity 2 marks

C3. Does differentiability imply continuity? [2]

Ans. Yes. If the derivative exists the difference quotient has a limit, and that forces f(x) → f(a), which is exactly continuity.

Finding constants 2 marks

C4. What is a removable discontinuity? [2]

Ans. One where the limit exists but differs from f(a), or f(a) is undefined; redefining the value at that single point makes the function continuous.

Finding constants 2 marks

C5. What does a jump discontinuity look like? [2]

Ans. The left-hand and right-hand limits both exist but are different, so the graph breaks with a vertical gap at that point.

Continuity and differentiability 2 marks

C6. Why must we test continuity at a piecewise point separately? [2]

Ans. The formula changes there, so the value and the two one-sided limits must be checked individually against the definition.
