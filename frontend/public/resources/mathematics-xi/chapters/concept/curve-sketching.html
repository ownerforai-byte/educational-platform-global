1.4 · Curve Sketching, Odd/Even and Periodicity — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Odd and even functions · Periodicity · Standard curves · Transformations

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Odd and even functions 2 marks

C1. Why is the vertex of a parabola important? [2]

Ans. It is the turning point: the lowest point when the curve opens upward and the highest when it opens downward, and the axis of symmetry passes through it.

Odd and even functions 2 marks

C2. What does the sign of the x² coefficient decide? [2]

Ans. A positive coefficient makes the parabola open upward and a negative one makes it open downward.

Periodicity 2 marks

C3. How do you find the y-intercept of a curve? [2]

Ans. Put x = 0 and evaluate y. For y = ax² + bx + c the y-intercept is c.

Standard curves 2 marks

C4. What does the discriminant tell you about the graph? [2]

Ans. Δ > 0 gives two x-intercepts, Δ = 0 gives one (the vertex touches the axis), and Δ < 0 means the curve never meets the x-axis.

Standard curves 2 marks

C5. What is the axis of symmetry of y = ax² + bx + c? [2]

Ans. The vertical line x = −b/2a, which passes through the vertex and splits the parabola into two mirror halves.

Transformations 2 marks

C6. Why does y = |f(x)| have a corner where f changes sign? [2]

Ans. The part below the axis is reflected upward, so the slope changes from negative to positive there and the curve has no unique tangent.
