5.2 · Mathematical and Empirical Probability — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Sample space · Classical probability · Complementary events · Empirical probability

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Sample space 2 marks

C1. What is a sample space? [2]

Ans. The set of all possible outcomes of an experiment; an event is a subset of it.

Classical probability 2 marks

C2. State the classical definition of probability. [2]

Ans. P(E) = number of favourable outcomes / total number of equally likely outcomes. It applies only when the outcomes are equally likely and finite.

Classical probability 2 marks

C3. What is the complementary event of E? [2]

Ans. The event that E does not happen, written E′ or Ē. Since E and E′ cover the sample space, P(E′) = 1 − P(E).

Complementary events 2 marks

C4. Why must the probabilities of all outcomes add to 1? [2]

Ans. They are mutually exclusive and together they exhaust the sample space, and the probability of the certain event is 1.

Complementary events 2 marks

C5. What is the probability of an impossible event? [2]

Ans. Zero — such an event contains no outcome of the sample space.

Empirical probability 2 marks

C6. Why are the 36 outcomes of two dice treated as ordered pairs? [2]

Ans. Only then are all the outcomes equally likely; the unordered pair “a 3 and a 5” hides two distinct outcomes.
