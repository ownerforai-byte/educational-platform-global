6.6 · Monotonicity, Extrema, Tangents and Concavity — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Increasing and decreasing · Maxima and minima · Tangents and normals · Concavity and absolute extrema

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Increasing and decreasing 2 marks

C1. What is a stationary point? [2]

Ans. A point where f′(x) = 0, so the tangent is horizontal; it may be a maximum, a minimum or a point of inflection.

Increasing and decreasing 2 marks

C2. Describe the first derivative test. [2]

Ans. If f′ changes from + to − at a critical point it is a maximum, and from − to + it is a minimum; no sign change means neither.

Maxima and minima 2 marks

C3. Describe the second derivative test. [2]

Ans. At a stationary point, f″ < 0 gives a maximum, f″ > 0 gives a minimum, and f″ = 0 is inconclusive, so the first test must be used.

Maxima and minima 2 marks

C4. Why must the end points be checked when finding an absolute extremum? [2]

Ans. On a closed interval the largest or smallest value may occur at an end point even though the derivative is not zero there.

Tangents and normals 2 marks

C5. What is the normal to a curve at a point? [2]

Ans. The line perpendicular to the tangent there; its slope is −1/(dy/dx).

Concavity and absolute extrema 2 marks

C6. When is a function increasing on an interval? [2]

Ans. When f′(x) > 0 throughout that interval, so the graph rises from left to right.
