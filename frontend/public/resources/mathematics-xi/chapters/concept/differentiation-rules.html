6.4 · Rules of Differentiation — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Power and sum · Product and quotient · Chain rule · Exponential, log and trig

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Power and sum 2 marks

C1. When do you use the product rule? [2]

Ans. When the function is a product of two differentiable factors; then (uv)′ = u′v + uv′.

Power and sum 2 marks

C2. State the chain rule. [2]

Ans. dy/dx = dy/du · du/dx when y depends on u and u depends on x: differentiate the outer function and multiply by the inner derivative.

Product and quotient 2 marks

C3. State the quotient rule. [2]

Ans. (u/v)′ = (u′v − uv′)/v², valid wherever v ≠ 0.

Product and quotient 2 marks

C4. Why must the inner derivative be multiplied in a composite function? [2]

Ans. The inner change is measured with respect to u, not x; the extra factor converts the rate per unit u into a rate per unit x.

Chain rule 2 marks

C5. What is the derivative of ln x? [2]

Ans. 1/x for x > 0. The natural logarithm is defined precisely so that its derivative is the simplest possible.

Chain rule 2 marks

C6. What is the derivative of e^{kx}? [2]

Ans. k e^{kx}: the exponential reproduces itself and the constant rate appears as a factor.
