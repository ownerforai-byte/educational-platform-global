3.1 · Perpendicular Distance and Angle Bisectors — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Perpendicular distance · Distance between parallel lines · Angle bisectors · Bisector of a given region

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Perpendicular distance 2 marks

C1. What is the perpendicular distance of a point from a line? [2]

Ans. For Ax + By + C = 0 and the point (x₁, y₁) it is |Ax₁ + By₁ + C| / √(A² + B²), measured along the normal.

Perpendicular distance 2 marks

C2. Why does the distance formula carry an absolute value? [2]

Ans. Distance is never negative, while Ax₁ + By₁ + C may be negative depending on which side of the line the point lies.

Distance between parallel lines 2 marks

C3. What is the locus of points equidistant from two intersecting lines? [2]

Ans. The two angle bisectors of the lines, each carrying the points that are equally far from both.

Angle bisectors 2 marks

C4. Why must the coefficients match before using the distance between parallel lines? [2]

Ans. The formula |C₁ − C₂|/√(A² + B²) assumes both lines share the same normal, which requires identical A and B.

Angle bisectors 2 marks

C5. What is the distance of the origin from Ax + By + C = 0? [2]

Ans. It is |C|/√(A² + B²), which is just the point-distance formula with (0, 0) substituted.

Bisector of a given region 2 marks

C6. What does an angle bisector do? [2]

Ans. It divides an angle into two equal parts, so every point on it is at the same perpendicular distance from both arms.
