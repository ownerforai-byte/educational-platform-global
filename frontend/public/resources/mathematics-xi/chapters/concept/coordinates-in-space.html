3.3 · Coordinates in Space — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Distance and section formulae · Direction cosines and ratios · Angle between lines · Parallel and perpendicular tests

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Distance and section formulae 2 marks

C1. What do the three coordinates of a point give? [2]

Ans. Its position in space: distances measured along the x, y and z axes, so three numbers are needed where the plane needs two.

Distance and section formulae 2 marks

C2. What is the equation of the yz-plane? [2]

Ans. x = 0: every point on that plane has zero x-coordinate, just as the xy-plane is z = 0.

Direction cosines and ratios 2 marks

C3. How does the section formula work in space? [2]

Ans. It is applied coordinate by coordinate, exactly as in the plane, giving the same ratio in all three coordinates.

Direction cosines and ratios 2 marks

C4. What are direction ratios of a line? [2]

Ans. Any three numbers proportional to the differences of the coordinates of two points on the line; they fix its direction, not its position.

Angle between lines 2 marks

C5. Why do the squares of the direction cosines add up to 1? [2]

Ans. They are the components of a unit vector along the line, and a unit vector has length 1: l² + m² + n² = 1.

Parallel and perpendicular tests 2 marks

C6. What is the distance between two points in space? [2]

Ans. √((x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)² + (z₂ − z₁)²), the three-dimensional form of Pythagoras.
