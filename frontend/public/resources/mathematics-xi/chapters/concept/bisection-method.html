7.1 · Bisection Method — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Sign-change test · Bisection steps · Iteration tables · Accuracy and error

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Sign-change test 2 marks

C1. What is the idea of the bisection method? [2]

Ans. If f(a) and f(b) have opposite signs, the continuous curve crosses the axis between them; halving the interval repeatedly traps the root in a smaller and smaller bracket.

Sign-change test 2 marks

C2. Why must f be continuous for the method to work? [2]

Ans. The intermediate value theorem, which guarantees the crossing, requires continuity on the closed interval.

Bisection steps 2 marks

C3. How fast does the bisection method converge? [2]

Ans. Linearly: the error is at least halved at every step, so each iteration gains one binary digit of accuracy.

Bisection steps 2 marks

C4. How do you bound the error after n steps? [2]

Ans. The root lies in an interval of width (b − a)/2ⁿ, so the error is at most that width, or half of it from the midpoint.

Iteration tables 2 marks

C5. When does the bisection method fail? [2]

Ans. When the function does not change sign across the interval, or when a root has even multiplicity and only touches the axis.

Accuracy and error 2 marks

C6. What does the midpoint of the final bracket estimate? [2]

Ans. The root itself, with an error no larger than half the width of that final bracket.
