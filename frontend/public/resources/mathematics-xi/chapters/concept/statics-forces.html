7.5 · Statics: Resultant and Resolution of Forces — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Resolution of a force · Resultant of two forces · Equilibrium · Applied problems

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Resolution of a force 2 marks

C1. What is the resultant of a system of forces? [2]

Ans. The single force that produces the same effect as all the forces acting together, found by vector addition.

Resolution of a force 2 marks

C2. State the condition for a body to be in equilibrium under several forces. [2]

Ans. The vector sum of all the forces must be zero, so the resolved sums along any two perpendicular directions vanish.

Resultant of two forces 2 marks

C3. What is the equilibrant? [2]

Ans. The force equal in magnitude and opposite in direction to the resultant; it is what keeps the body in equilibrium.

Resultant of two forces 2 marks

C4. State Lami's theorem and its condition. [2]

Ans. For three concurrent forces in equilibrium, P/sin α = Q/sin β = R/sin γ, where each angle is opposite its force. Exactly three forces must act at one point.

Equilibrium 2 marks

C5. Why do we resolve forces into components? [2]

Ans. Resolving turns one vector equation into two scalar equations along perpendicular directions, which can be solved separately.

Applied problems 2 marks

C6. What is the reaction of a smooth plane? [2]

Ans. A single force perpendicular to the surface, since a smooth plane can exert no friction along it.
