6.1 · Limits and Indeterminate Forms — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Direct substitution · Indeterminate forms · Standard limits · Limits at infinity

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Direct substitution 2 marks

C1. What does lim(x→a) f(x) = L mean? [2]

Ans. As x gets arbitrarily close to a — without necessarily reaching it — f(x) gets arbitrarily close to L. The value f(a) itself is irrelevant.

Direct substitution 2 marks

C2. When is a limit called indeterminate? [2]

Ans. When direct substitution gives 0/0, ∞/∞, ∞ − ∞ or 0 × ∞. The form hides the true limit, so the expression must be simplified first.

Indeterminate forms 2 marks

C3. Why do we cancel factors before substituting? [2]

Ans. The cancellation removes the factor that made both numerator and denominator zero at the point, leaving a function that is defined there.

Indeterminate forms 2 marks

C4. What does lim(x→a⁻) mean? [2]

Ans. The left-hand limit: x approaches a through values smaller than a. The limit exists only when the right-hand limit agrees with it.

Standard limits 2 marks

C5. What is the standard limit (sin x)/x as x → 0? [2]

Ans. It equals 1, measured in radians. It is the basis of the derivative of sine.

Limits at infinity 2 marks

C6. Why is lim(n→∞)(1 + 1/n)ⁿ equal to e? [2]

Ans. The sequence increases and is bounded above, and its limit is the definition of e ≈ 2.71828.
