2.2 · Trigonometric Combination Formulae — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Addition and subtraction · Double and half angles · Product-to-sum · Conditional identities

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Addition and subtraction 2 marks

C1. Write cos 2A in its three useful forms. [2]

Ans. cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A = 2cos²A − 1 = 1 − 2sin²A. The last two are the ones that convert an expression into a single trigonometric ratio.

Double and half angles 2 marks

C2. Why do we convert sums into products? [2]

Ans. The product form shows the zeros and the amplitude directly, and it is far easier to solve sin A + sin B = 0 when it is written as a product.

Product-to-sum 2 marks

C3. What is the maximum value of a sin x + b cos x? [2]

Ans. It is √(a² + b²), reached when the angle equals tan⁻¹(b/a) in the right sense.

Product-to-sum 2 marks

C4. What is the formula for tan 2A? [2]

Ans. tan 2A = 2tan A / (1 − tan²A), valid where all three tangents are defined.

Conditional identities 2 marks

C5. Why is the triple-angle formula useful? [2]

Ans. It reduces sin 3A, cos 3A and tan 3A to powers of the single angle, which turns many equations into polynomial equations in sin A or tan A.

Double and half angles 2 marks

C6. Write sin A + sin B as a product. [2]

Ans. sin A + sin B = 2 sin((A + B)/2) cos((A − B)/2).
