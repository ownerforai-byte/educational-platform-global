5.1 · Dispersion, Variation and Skewness — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Range and mean deviation · Variance and standard deviation · Coefficient of variation · Skewness

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Range and mean deviation 2 marks

C1. Why is standard deviation preferred to range as a measure of spread? [2]

Ans. Range uses only two observations and ignores everything between them, while standard deviation uses every value through its deviations from the mean.

Variance and standard deviation 2 marks

C2. What does the coefficient of variation compare? [2]

Ans. Relative spread: CV = (σ/x̄) × 100 lets two distributions with different means or units be compared on the same footing.

Variance and standard deviation 2 marks

C3. What does skewness = 0 mean? [2]

Ans. The distribution is symmetric about its mean and the mean, median and mode coincide.

Coefficient of variation 2 marks

C4. Why is variance measured in squared units? [2]

Ans. It averages the squared deviations, so its unit is the square of the data's unit; taking the square root brings it back to the original unit.

Coefficient of variation 2 marks

C5. What does a large coefficient of variation indicate? [2]

Ans. The data are widely spread relative to their mean, so the mean is a less reliable summary of the set.

Skewness 2 marks

C6. What happens to the standard deviation if every observation is increased by 5? [2]

Ans. It is unchanged: shifting all the data does not alter the deviations from the mean, so the spread is the same.
