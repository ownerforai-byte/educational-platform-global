7.2 · Newton–Raphson Method — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics The formula · Iteration steps · Convergence · Comparison with bisection

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

The formula 2 marks

C1. What is the geometric idea of the Newton–Raphson method? [2]

Ans. Draw the tangent at the current estimate and take its intersection with the x-axis as the next estimate; repeat until the values stop changing.

The formula 2 marks

C2. Write the Newton–Raphson iteration formula. [2]

Ans. xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − f(xₙ)/f′(xₙ), which comes from the tangent line at xₙ.

Iteration steps 2 marks

C3. When does Newton–Raphson fail? [2]

Ans. When f′(xₙ) = 0 (horizontal tangent), or when the starting value is too far from the root so the iterates wander or oscillate.

Iteration steps 2 marks

C4. How fast does Newton–Raphson converge near a simple root? [2]

Ans. Quadratically: the number of correct decimals roughly doubles each step, which is why two or three steps often suffice.

Convergence 2 marks

C5. What is its advantage over bisection? [2]

Ans. Much faster convergence for the same number of evaluations, and it needs only one starting value rather than a sign-changing bracket.

Comparison with bisection 2 marks

C6. Why does the method require f′(x) ≠ 0 near the root? [2]

Ans. The correction divides by the derivative; a small derivative makes the step enormous and throws the next estimate far away.
