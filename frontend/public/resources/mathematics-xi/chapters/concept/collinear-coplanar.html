4.2 · Collinear and Coplanar Vectors — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Collinearity · Coplanarity by triple product · Perpendicular and unit vectors · Volume applications

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Collinearity 2 marks

C1. When are three points A, B, C collinear? [2]

Ans. When AB and AC are parallel, i.e. AB = λAC for some scalar λ, or equivalently AB × AC = 0.

Coplanarity by triple product 2 marks

C2. When are three vectors coplanar? [2]

Ans. When their scalar triple product is zero, [a b c] = 0, because the parallelepiped they build has no volume.

Coplanarity by triple product 2 marks

C3. Are any two vectors always coplanar? [2]

Ans. Yes. Two vectors always lie in some plane through the origin, so only three or more vectors can fail to be coplanar.

Perpendicular and unit vectors 2 marks

C4. Why does linear dependence imply coplanarity? [2]

Ans. If one vector is a combination of the other two, it lies in the plane those two span, so all three lie in one plane.

Perpendicular and unit vectors 2 marks

C5. What is the condition for four points to be coplanar? [2]

Ans. With A as base, the triple product AB · (AC × AD) must be zero, so the three edge vectors are coplanar.

Volume applications 2 marks

C6. What does [a b c] = 0 mean for the volume of the parallelepiped? [2]

Ans. The volume is zero, so the parallelepiped flattens into a plane — the three vectors are coplanar.
