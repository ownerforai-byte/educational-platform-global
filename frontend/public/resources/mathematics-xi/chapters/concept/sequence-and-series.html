1.5 · Sequence and Series — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Arithmetic progression · Geometric progression · Sums to n and to infinity · Means

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Arithmetic progression 2 marks

C1. What is the difference between a sequence and a series? [2]

Ans. A sequence is an ordered list of terms, while a series is the sum of those terms.

Arithmetic progression 2 marks

C2. When is a sequence an arithmetic progression? [2]

Ans. When the difference between consecutive terms is the same constant d, so tₙ = a + (n − 1)d.

Geometric progression 2 marks

C3. Why must |r| < 1 for a geometric series to have a sum to infinity? [2]

Ans. Only then do the terms shrink towards zero fast enough for the partial sums to settle; the sum is a/(1 − r).

Sums to n and to infinity 2 marks

C4. What is the arithmetic mean of a and b? [2]

Ans. The single number (a + b)/2, which lies midway between them so that a, (a + b)/2, b form an AP.

Sums to n and to infinity 2 marks

C5. Why is tₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁? [2]

Ans. The sum of the first n terms is the sum of the first n − 1 terms plus the nth term, so subtracting removes everything except tₙ.

Means 2 marks

C6. What is the geometric mean of two positive numbers a and b? [2]

Ans. √(ab), the middle term that makes a, √(ab), b a geometric progression.
