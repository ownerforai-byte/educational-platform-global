1.6 · Matrices and Determinants — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Algebra of matrices · Determinants · Inverse of a matrix · Solving systems

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Algebra of matrices 2 marks

C1. When can two matrices be multiplied? [2]

Ans. When the number of columns of the first equals the number of rows of the second; the product then has the outer dimensions.

Algebra of matrices 2 marks

C2. What does det A = 0 mean? [2]

Ans. The matrix is singular: it has no inverse, and the corresponding system either has no solution or infinitely many.

Determinants 2 marks

C3. What is the identity matrix? [2]

Ans. The square matrix with 1s on the main diagonal and 0 elsewhere; it satisfies AI = IA = A for every compatible A.

Inverse of a matrix 2 marks

C4. Why is AB usually not equal to BA? [2]

Ans. Matrix multiplication is not commutative: the product depends on the order of the factors, and one order may not even be defined.

Inverse of a matrix 2 marks

C5. What is the adjugate used for? [2]

Ans. It gives the inverse: A⁻¹ = adj A / det A, which exists only when det A ≠ 0.

Solving systems 2 marks

C6. When does a linear system have a unique solution? [2]

Ans. When the determinant of the coefficient matrix is non-zero, so the matrix is invertible.
