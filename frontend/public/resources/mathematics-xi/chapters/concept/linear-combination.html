4.3 · Linear Combination, Dependence and Independence — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Linear combination · Dependence and independence · Basis in the plane and in space · Vector proofs

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Linear combination 2 marks

C1. What is a linear combination of vectors? [2]

Ans. Any sum of the form xa + yb + zc with scalar coefficients; it is the set of all vectors the given ones can build.

Dependence and independence 2 marks

C2. When do three vectors form a basis of space? [2]

Ans. When they are linearly independent, i.e. [a b c] ≠ 0, so they span all of three-dimensional space.

Dependence and independence 2 marks

C3. Why can three coplanar vectors not form a basis? [2]

Ans. Every combination of them stays in that one plane, so no vector off the plane can ever be produced.

Basis in the plane and in space 2 marks

C4. What is the dimension of ordinary space, and why? [2]

Ans. Three, because three independent directions are needed and any three independent vectors already span everything.

Vector proofs 2 marks

C5. How do you find the coefficients in a linear combination? [2]

Ans. Write the combination as a single vector and equate the i, j and k components, which gives three linear equations in the unknowns.

Vector proofs 2 marks

C6. Why is the representation in a basis unique? [2]

Ans. If two different sets of coefficients gave the same vector, their difference would be a non-trivial relation of dependence, contradicting independence.
