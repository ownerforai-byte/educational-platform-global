6.5 · Implicit, Parametric and Higher-Order Derivatives — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Implicit differentiation · Parametric differentiation · Second derivatives · Motion and rates

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Implicit differentiation 2 marks

C1. What is implicit differentiation? [2]

Ans. Differentiating both sides of a relation in x and y with respect to x, treating y as a function of x and applying the chain rule to every y term.

Parametric differentiation 2 marks

C2. When is implicit differentiation necessary? [2]

Ans. When y cannot be isolated as an explicit formula in x, for example in x³ + y³ = 3xy.

Parametric differentiation 2 marks

C3. What does d²y/dx² measure? [2]

Ans. The rate of change of the slope — the concavity of the curve. Positive means concave up, negative concave down.

Second derivatives 2 marks

C4. What is the formula for dy/dx when x and y are given parametrically? [2]

Ans. dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt), provided dx/dt ≠ 0.

Second derivatives 2 marks

C5. Why do we differentiate the first derivative again implicitly for the second? [2]

Ans. The first derivative still contains y, which depends on x, so another chain-rule step is needed to eliminate it in favour of dy/dx.

Motion and rates 2 marks

C6. When is the tangent to a curve horizontal? [2]

Ans. When dy/dx = 0 at that point; the tangent is then parallel to the x-axis.
