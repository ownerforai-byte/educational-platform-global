6.3 · Derivatives from First Principles — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics The definition · Polynomials · Rational and root functions · Trigonometric functions

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

The definition 2 marks

C1. State the definition of the derivative from first principles. [2]

Ans. f′(x) = lim(h→0) [f(x + h) − f(x)]/h, provided the limit exists.

The definition 2 marks

C2. What does the difference quotient represent? [2]

Ans. It is the slope of the secant line through (x, f(x)) and (x + h, f(x + h)) — the average rate of change over that interval.

Polynomials 2 marks

C3. Why do we let h → 0? [2]

Ans. The secant then becomes the tangent, so the limit of the average rate gives the instantaneous rate of change.

Polynomials 2 marks

C4. What does the sign of f′(x) tell you? [2]

Ans. A positive derivative means the function is increasing there, a negative one means it is decreasing, and zero marks a stationary point.

Rational and root functions 2 marks

C5. What is the derivative of xⁿ? [2]

Ans. nxⁿ⁻¹ for any real n where the power is defined, obtained by expanding (x + h)ⁿ in the difference quotient.

Trigonometric functions 2 marks

C6. Why is f′(a) the slope of the tangent at x = a? [2]

Ans. It is the limit of the slopes of the secant lines through that point, and that limit is by definition the tangent slope.
