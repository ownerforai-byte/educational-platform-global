4.1 · Vector Algebra, Dot and Cross Products — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Magnitude and unit vector · Dot product · Cross product · Area, angle and projection

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Magnitude and unit vector 2 marks

C1. What is the difference between the dot and the cross product? [2]

Ans. The dot product gives a scalar |a||b|cos θ and measures how much the vectors agree in direction; the cross product gives a vector |a||b|sin θ perpendicular to both and measures the area they span.

Magnitude and unit vector 2 marks

C2. When is a·b largest for fixed magnitudes? [2]

Ans. When the angle between them is 0, since cos 0 = 1; the dot product is then |a||b|.

Dot product 2 marks

C3. What does a × b = 0 tell you? [2]

Ans. Either one vector is zero or the vectors are parallel, because sin θ = 0.

Dot product 2 marks

C4. Why is a·(b × c) called the scalar triple product? [2]

Ans. It is a scalar formed from three vectors, and its magnitude equals the volume of the parallelepiped built on them.

Cross product 2 marks

C5. What is the geometric meaning of |a × b|? [2]

Ans. The area of the parallelogram whose adjacent sides are a and b; half of it is the area of the triangle on the same two sides.

Area, angle and projection 2 marks

C6. Why is a·a = |a|²? [2]

Ans. The angle of a vector with itself is 0, so a·a = |a||a|cos 0 = |a|².
