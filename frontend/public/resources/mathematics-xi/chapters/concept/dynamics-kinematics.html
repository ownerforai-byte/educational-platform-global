7.6 · Dynamics: Uniform Acceleration — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics The three equations · Free fall · Retardation · Multi-stage motion

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

The three equations 2 marks

C1. What is uniform acceleration? [2]

Ans. Motion in which the velocity changes by equal amounts in equal times, so a is constant and the three standard equations apply.

The three equations 2 marks

C2. Write the three equations of motion for constant acceleration. [2]

Ans. v = u + at, s = ut + ½at², and v² = u² + 2as.

Free fall 2 marks

C3. What assumption underlies v = u + at? [2]

Ans. That the acceleration is constant; for varying acceleration the relation must be integrated instead.

Free fall 2 marks

C4. What is the difference between distance and displacement? [2]

Ans. Displacement is the straight-line change of position with direction, while distance is the total path length travelled; they differ whenever the body turns back.

Retardation 2 marks

C5. What is special about the highest point of a vertical throw? [2]

Ans. The velocity is momentarily zero while the acceleration is still g downward, and that is why the body cannot stay there.

Multi-stage motion 2 marks

C6. Why is g taken as negative when a body moves upward? [2]

Ans. With upward as positive, gravity acts downward, so the acceleration is −g and the equations then give the correct deceleration.
