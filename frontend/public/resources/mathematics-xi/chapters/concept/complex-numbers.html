1.8 · Complex Numbers — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Algebra of complex numbers · Modulus and argument · Polar form · Roots and equations

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Algebra of complex numbers 2 marks

C1. What is i? [2]

Ans. The imaginary unit, defined by i² = −1. It lets every negative number have a square root, e.g. √−4 = 2i.

Algebra of complex numbers 2 marks

C2. Why is the conjugate useful? [2]

Ans. Multiplying by the conjugate removes i from a denominator and gives the modulus: z z̄ = a² + b² = |z|².

Modulus and argument 2 marks

C3. What is the geometric meaning of |z|? [2]

Ans. It is the distance of the point representing z from the origin in the Argand plane, |z| = √(a² + b²) for z = a + ib.

Polar form 2 marks

C4. What is arg z? [2]

Ans. The angle the line from the origin to z makes with the positive real axis, taken in the correct quadrant from the signs of a and b.

Polar form 2 marks

C5. Why is |z₁z₂| = |z₁||z₂|? [2]

Ans. Moduli multiply while arguments add when complex numbers are multiplied, so the resulting distance is the product of the two distances.

Roots and equations 2 marks

C6. What does the Argand plane represent? [2]

Ans. The complex number a + ib as the point (a, b): the real part along the horizontal axis and the imaginary part along the vertical axis.
