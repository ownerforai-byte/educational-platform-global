1.7 · Quadratic Equations — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Roots and coefficients · Nature of roots · Forming equations · Applications

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Roots and coefficients 2 marks

C1. For ax² + bx + c = 0, what are the sum and the product of the roots? [2]

Ans. α + β = −b/a and αβ = c/a, which follow from comparing x² − (α + β)x + αβ with the equation divided by a.

Nature of roots 2 marks

C2. What does the discriminant decide? [2]

Ans. Δ = b² − 4ac: positive gives two real roots, zero gives equal real roots, negative gives a pair of complex conjugate roots.

Nature of roots 2 marks

C3. How do you form the quadratic whose roots are α and β? [2]

Ans. Use x² − (α + β)x + αβ = 0, so the sum and product of the roots determine the equation up to a constant multiple.

Forming equations 2 marks

C4. Why do complex roots of a real quadratic come in conjugate pairs? [2]

Ans. The coefficients are real, so taking conjugates of the whole equation leaves it unchanged; if a + ib is a root, so is a − ib.

Forming equations 2 marks

C5. How is the vertex related to the roots? [2]

Ans. The axis of symmetry x = −b/2a lies exactly midway between the roots, so the vertex x-coordinate is their average.

Applications 2 marks

C6. When does a quadratic have equal roots? [2]

Ans. When Δ = 0, i.e. b² = 4ac; the parabola then just touches the x-axis at its vertex.
