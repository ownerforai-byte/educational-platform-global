1.2 · Real Numbers, Intervals and Modulus — two-mark conceptual answers

Topics Modulus and distance · Intervals · Surds and rationalisation · Inequalities

Each question is the two-mark kind NEB asks at the start of the paper; the model answer is one or two precise sentences.

Modulus and distance 2 marks

C1. What does |x| mean? [2]

Ans. The distance of x from 0 on the number line: |x| = x for x ≥ 0 and |x| = −x for x < 0. It is never negative.

Intervals 2 marks

C2. Why does |x| = a, with a > 0, give two solutions? [2]

Ans. Exactly two points lie at distance a from 0, namely x = a and x = −a. For a = 0 there is one solution and for a < 0 none.

Intervals 2 marks

C3. What does |x − 3| < 2 describe? [2]

Ans. All points whose distance from 3 is less than 2, i.e. 1 < x < 5, or x ∈ (1, 5).

Surds and rationalisation 2 marks

C4. Is |x + y| = |x| + |y| always true? [2]

Ans. No. The triangle inequality gives |x + y| ≤ |x| + |y|, with equality only when x and y have the same sign or one of them is zero.

Surds and rationalisation 2 marks

C5. What is the geometric meaning of |x − a|? [2]

Ans. The distance between the point x and the fixed point a on the number line; that is why modulus equations describe distances.

Inequalities 2 marks

C6. Why do we split at critical points when solving a modulus equation? [2]

Ans. The sign of the expression inside each modulus changes there, so the equation takes a different algebraic form on each piece of the number line.
