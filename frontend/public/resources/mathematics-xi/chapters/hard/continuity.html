Finding constants Finding a constant hard

H1.Find k so that f(x) = (1 − cos 2x)/(x sin x) for x ≠ 0, f(0) = k is continuous at x = 0. [5]

  1. H1. Use the identity 1 − cos 2x = 2 sin²x.
  2. H2. Cancel one factor of sin x with the denominator.
  3. H3. Then 2 sin x/x → 2.

2sin²x/(x sinx) = 2 sinx/x → 2

k = 2

Continuity and differentiability Continuity vs differentiability hard

H2.Show that f(x) = |x − 2| is continuous but not differentiable at x = 2. [5]

  1. H1. Continuity: check that the two one-sided limits equal f(2) = 0.
  2. H2. Differentiability: compute the left and right difference quotients.
  3. H3. They give the slopes −1 and +1, which differ.

lim(x→2⁻)|x−2| = 0 = lim(x→2⁺)|x−2| = f(2) f′₋ = −1, f′₊ = +1

continuous at 2, not differentiable there (a corner)

Finding constants Smooth join hard

H3.Find a and b so that f(x) = ax + b for x < 1 and f(x) = x² for x ≥ 1 is both continuous and differentiable at x = 1. [5]

  1. H1. Continuity gives a·1 + b = 1².
  2. H2. Differentiability gives the left slope a equal to the right slope 2x at x = 1.
  3. H3. Substitute a into the first equation to get b.

continuity: a + b = 1 differentiability: a = 2x|₁ = 2 ⇒ 2 + b = 1 ⇒ b = −1

a = 2, b = −1

Finding constants Finding a constant hard

H4.Find k if f(x) = (sin 9x)/(sin 3x) for x ≠ 0 and f(0) = k is continuous at 0. [4]

  1. H1. Divide numerator and denominator by x.
  2. H2. Use sin9x/x → 9 and sin3x/x → 3.
  3. H3. The ratio of the two standard limits is k.

[9·(sin9x/9x)]/[3·(sin3x/3x)] → 9/3

k = 3

Continuity and differentiability Piecewise function hard

H5.For f(x) = x² when x < 1 and f(x) = 2x − 1 when x ≥ 1, test continuity and differentiability at x = 1. [5]

  1. H1. Evaluate both branches at x = 1.
  2. H2. Compare the one-sided derivatives: 2x and 2.
  3. H3. Equal values and equal slopes mean the function is differentiable.

f(1) = 1 and both one-sided limits are 1 f′₋ = 2x|₁ = 2, f′₊ = 2

continuous and differentiable at 1, with f′(1) = 2

Types of discontinuity Classification hard

H6.Classify the discontinuity of f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x − 1) at x = 1. [4]

  1. H1. Substitution gives 0/0, so look at the limit rather than the value.
  2. H2. Factor and cancel for x ≠ 1.
  3. H3. The limit exists but f(1) is undefined — a missing point.

(x − 1)(x + 1)/(x − 1) = x + 1 → 2 f(1) is not defined

removable discontinuity: define f(1) = 2 to repair it
