Product and quotient Product rule hard

H1.Differentiate y = x² e^{3x}. [4]

  1. H1. Use the product rule with u = x² and v = e^{3x}.
  2. H2. Differentiate v by the chain rule: v′ = 3e^{3x}.
  3. H3. Factor e^{3x} out of the two terms.

y′ = 2x·e^{3x} + x²·3e^{3x}

dy/dx = e^{3x}(3x² + 2x)

Exponential, log and trig Logarithm hard

H2.Differentiate y = ln(sin x). [3]

  1. H1. This is ln u with u = sin x.
  2. H2. dy/dx = (1/u)·du/dx.
  3. H3. cos x/sin x simplifies to cot x.

y′ = (1/sinx)·cosx

dy/dx = cot x

Product and quotient Quotient rule hard

H3.Differentiate y = sin x/(1 + cos x). [5]

  1. H1. Apply the quotient rule with u = sinx, v = 1 + cosx.
  2. H2. The numerator becomes cosx(1 + cosx) + sin²x.
  3. H3. Use sin²x + cos²x = 1 and factor (1 + cosx).

y′ = [cosx(1 + cosx) + sin²x]/(1 + cosx)² = (1 + cosx)/(1 + cosx)²

dy/dx = 1/(1 + cos x)

Chain rule Chain rule hard

H4.Differentiate y = tan⁻¹(2x/(1 − x²)). [5]

  1. H1. Substitute x = tan θ so the argument becomes tan 2θ.
  2. H2. For |x| < 1 the function is 2θ = 2 tan⁻¹x.
  3. H3. Differentiate the simplified form.

x = tanθ ⇒ 2x/(1 − x²) = tan2θ y = 2tan⁻¹x ⇒ y′ = 2/(1 + x²)

dy/dx = 2/(1 + x²)

Product and quotient Product rule hard

H5.Differentiate y = e^{x²} ln x. [4]

  1. H1. Product rule with u = e^{x²} and v = ln x.
  2. H2. u′ = 2x e^{x²} by the chain rule.
  3. H3. Write the result over a common denominator if you wish.

y′ = 2x e^{x²}·lnx + e^{x²}·(1/x)

dy/dx = e^{x²}(2x ln x + 1/x)

Product and quotient Quotient rule hard

H6.Find dy/dx at x = 2 for y = (1 + x²)/(1 − x²). [5]

  1. H1. Quotient rule: (u′v − uv′)/v² with u = 1 + x², v = 1 − x².
  2. H2. At x = 2: u′ = 4, v = −3, v′ = −4.
  3. H3. Substitute carefully, keeping the signs.

y′ = [4(−3) − 5(−4)]/9 = (−12 + 20)/9

dy/dx at x = 2 is 8/9
