Addition law Addition law hard

H1.If P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7, find P(A ∩ B), P(A | B) and test independence. [5]

  1. H1. Use the addition law: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B).
  2. H2. P(A ∩ B) = 0.5 + 0.4 − 0.7 = 0.2.
  3. H3. Compare P(A ∩ B) with P(A)P(B), and use P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B).

P(A ∩ B) = 0.2, P(A|B) = 0.2/0.4 = 0.5 P(A)P(B) = 0.5·0.4 = 0.2

P(A ∩ B) = 0.2; P(A|B) = 0.5; since P(A∩B) = P(A)P(B), the events are independent

H3.A bag has 3 red and 2 blue balls. Two are drawn without replacement. Find P(second is red | first is red). [3]

  1. H1. After a red ball is drawn, 2 red remain.
  2. H2. There are 4 balls left in total.
  3. H3. The conditional probability is 2/4.

H4.If P(A) = 1/3 and P(B) = 1/4 with A and B independent, find P(A ∪ B). [4]

  1. H1. Independence gives P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B) = 1/12.
  2. H2. Apply the addition law.
  3. H3. 1/3 + 1/4 − 1/12 over the denominator 12.

= 4/12 + 3/12 − 1/12

P(A ∪ B) = 1/2

Multiplication law Multiplication law hard

H5.A problem is solved independently by two students with probabilities 1/2 and 1/3. Find the probability that the problem is solved. [4]

  1. H1. The problem is unsolved only if both fail.
  2. H2. P(both fail) = (1/2)(2/3).
  3. H3. Subtract that from 1.

1 − (1/2)(2/3) = 1 − 1/3

P(solved) = 2/3

H6.P(A) = 0.6 and P(B) = 0.5. Can A and B be mutually exclusive? Justify. [4]

  1. H1. Mutually exclusive means P(A ∩ B) = 0.
  2. H2. The addition law would then give P(A ∪ B) = 1.1.
  3. H3. No probability can exceed 1.

if exclusive: P(A ∪ B) = 0.6 + 0.5 = 1.1 > 1

impossible — the events must overlap; at least P(A ∩ B) = 0.1
