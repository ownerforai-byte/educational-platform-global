Increasing and decreasing Monotonicity hard

H1.For f(x) = x³ − 3x² + 4 find the intervals of increase and decrease and the local extrema. [5]

  1. H1. f′(x) = 3x² − 6x = 3x(x − 2).
  2. H2. Test the sign of f′ on the three intervals split by 0 and 2.
  3. H3. Evaluate f at the turning points to get the extreme values.

f′ > 0 on (−∞,0) ∪ (2,∞), f′ < 0 on (0,2) f(0) = 4, f(2) = 0

increasing outside (0,2); local max 4 at x = 0, local min 0 at x = 2

Maxima and minima Maxima and minima hard

H2.Find the local maxima and minima of f(x) = 2x³ − 15x² + 36x + 1. [5]

  1. H1. f′ = 6x² − 30x + 36 = 6(x − 2)(x − 3).
  2. H2. Use f″ = 12x − 30 at each stationary point to classify it.
  3. H3. Evaluate f at x = 2 and x = 3.

f′(2) = 0, f″(2) = −6 < 0 ⇒ local max f(2) = 29 f′(3) = 0, f″(3) = 6 > 0 ⇒ local min f(3) = 28

local max 29 at x = 2; local min 28 at x = 3

Tangents and normals Tangent and normal hard

H3.Find the equation of the tangent and the normal to xy = 4 at the point (2, 2). [5]

  1. H1. Differentiate implicitly: y + x y′ = 0.
  2. H2. The tangent slope at (2, 2) is −y/x = −1.
  3. H3. The normal slope is the negative reciprocal, +1.

tangent: y − 2 = −1(x − 2) ⇒ x + y = 4 normal: y − 2 = 1(x − 2) ⇒ y = x

tangent x + y = 4; normal y = x

Maxima and minima Optimisation hard

H4.Show that among all rectangles with perimeter 40 cm, the square has the greatest area. [5]

  1. H1. If one side is x, the other is 20 − x.
  2. H2. Write A(x) = x(20 − x) and differentiate.
  3. H3. Show the stationary point is a maximum with A″ < 0.

A = 20x − x² A′ = 20 − 2x = 0 ⇒ x = 10 A″ = −2 < 0 ⇒ maximum A(10) = 100 cm²

the 10 cm × 10 cm square, area 100 cm²

Concavity and absolute extrema Absolute extrema hard

H5.Find the absolute maximum and minimum of f(x) = x³ − 12x on [−3, 3]. [5]

  1. H1. Stationary points come from 3x² − 12 = 0, i.e. x = ±2.
  2. H2. Evaluate f at the stationary points and at both endpoints.
  3. H3. Compare the four values — the interval forces the endpoints in.

f(−3) = 9, f(−2) = 16, f(2) = −16, f(3) = −9

absolute max 16 at x = −2; absolute min −16 at x = 2

Concavity and absolute extrema Inequality hard

H6.Show that x + 1/x ≥ 2 for every x > 0. [4]

  1. H1. Find the minimum of f(x) = x + 1/x on x > 0.
  2. H2. f′ = 1 − 1/x² = 0 gives x = 1.
  3. H3. f″ = 2/x³ > 0 confirms a minimum there.

f′(1) = 0 and f″(1) = 2 > 0 ⇒ minimum f(1) = 2

minimum value 2 at x = 1, hence x + 1/x ≥ 2
