Classical probability Two dice hard

H1.Two dice are thrown. Find the probability that the total is at least 10. [4]

  1. H1. The sample space has 36 equally likely outcomes.
  2. H2. Count pairs for totals 10, 11 and 12.
  3. H3. 10 has 3 pairs, 11 has 2, and 12 has 1.

favourable = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 P = 6/36

P(total ≥ 10) = 1/6

Classical probability Without replacement hard

H2.A bag contains 5 red and 4 white balls. Two are drawn without replacement. Find the probability that both are red. [4]

  1. H1. The first draw has 5 red out of 9.
  2. H2. After a red is removed, 4 red remain out of 8.
  3. H3. Multiply the two conditional probabilities.

P = (5/9)(4/8)

P(both red) = 5/18

Complementary events Complement hard

H3.A coin is tossed four times. Find the probability of at least one head. [4]

  1. H1. Work with the complement: no head at all.
  2. H2. P(all tails) = (1/2)⁴ = 1/16.
  3. H3. Subtract from 1.

P(at least one head) = 1 − P(TTTT) = 1 − 1/16

15/16

Classical probability Cards hard

H4.From a full deck one card is drawn. Find the probability that it is a king or a heart. [4]

  1. H1. Add the two separate probabilities.
  2. H2. The king of hearts has been counted twice, so subtract once.
  3. H3. 4/52 + 13/52 − 1/52.

P = 4/52 + 13/52 − 1/52 = 16/52

P = 4/13

Sample space Sample space hard

H5.Three coins are tossed. Find the probability of exactly two heads. [3]

  1. H1. List the sample space of 8 outcomes.
  2. H2. Exactly two heads means HHT, HTH, THH.
  3. H3. Three favourable outcomes out of eight.

P = 3/8

3/8

Classical probability Mutually exclusive hard

H6.Two dice are thrown. Find the probability that the total is 7 or 11. [4]

  1. H1. The two events cannot happen together, so the probabilities add.
  2. H2. Total 7 has 6 favourable pairs; total 11 has 2.
  3. H3. Add 6/36 + 2/36.

P = 6/36 + 2/36 = 8/36

P(total 7 or 11) = 2/9
