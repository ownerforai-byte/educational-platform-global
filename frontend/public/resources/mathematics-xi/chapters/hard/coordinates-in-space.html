Distance and section formulae Distance formula hard

H1.Find the distance between the points (3, −1, 2) and (1, 2, 3). [3]

  1. H1. Subtract corresponding coordinates to get the displacement vector.
  2. H2. The distance is the magnitude of that vector.
  3. H3. (−2)² + 3² + 1² = 14.

d = √((3−1)² + (−1−2)² + (2−3)²) = √(4 + 9 + 1)

√14 units

Distance and section formulae Section formula hard

H2.Find the point that divides A(2, −1, 3) and B(4, 3, −1) internally in the ratio 2 : 3. [4]

  1. H1. Use ((mx₂ + nx₁)/(m + n), (my₂ + ny₁)/(m + n), (mz₂ + nz₁)/(m + n)).
  2. H2. With m = 2, n = 3: 2B + 3A over 5.
  3. H3. Compute each coordinate separately.

x = (2·4 + 3·2)/5 = 14/5 y = (2·3 + 3·(−1))/5 = 3/5 z = (2·(−1) + 3·3)/5 = 7/5

(14/5, 3/5, 7/5)

Direction cosines and ratios Direction cosines hard

H3.Find the direction cosines of the vector (2, 2, −1). [3]

  1. H1. The direction cosines are the components divided by the magnitude.
  2. H2. |v| = √(4 + 4 + 1) = 3.
  3. H3. Divide each component by 3 and check l² + m² + n² = 1.

|v| = 3 ⇒ (2/3, 2/3, −1/3)

l = 2/3, m = 2/3, n = −1/3

Angle between lines Angle between lines hard

H4.Find the angle between the lines whose direction ratios are (1, 2, 2) and (2, 3, 6). [4]

  1. H1. Use cosθ = (a₁a₂ + b₁b₂ + c₁c₂)/(|v₁||v₂|).
  2. H2. The dot product is 2 + 6 + 12 = 20.
  3. H3. |v₁| = 3 and |v₂| = 7.

cosθ = 20/(3·7) = 20/21

θ = cos⁻¹(20/21) ≈ 17.8°

Parallel and perpendicular tests Perpendicular test hard

H5.Find k so that the lines with direction ratios (1, k, 3) and (2, −1, 4) are perpendicular. [3]

  1. H1. Perpendicular lines have zero dot product of direction ratios.
  2. H2. 1·2 + k·(−1) + 3·4 = 0.
  3. H3. Solve the linear equation for k.

2 − k + 12 = 0

k = 14

Direction cosines and ratios Collinearity hard

H6.Show that the points (1, 2, 3), (2, 4, 6) and (3, 6, 9) are collinear. [4]

  1. H1. Form two displacement vectors from the same base point.
  2. H2. One vector should be a scalar multiple of the other.
  3. H3. Here (2, 4, 6) − (1, 2, 3) = (1, 2, 3) and (3, 6, 9) − (1, 2, 3) = (2, 4, 6).

AB = (1, 2, 3), AC = (2, 4, 6) = 2·AB

the vectors are parallel, so all three points lie on one line
