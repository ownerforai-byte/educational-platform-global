Collinearity Collinearity hard

H1.Show that (1, 2, 3), (2, 3, 4) and (3, 4, 5) are collinear. [4]

  1. H1. Take differences from the first point.
  2. H2. AB = (1, 1, 1) and AC = (2, 2, 2).
  3. H3. Proportional displacement vectors mean one line.

AB = (1, 1, 1), AC = (2, 2, 2) = 2·AB

AC is a multiple of AB, so the three points are collinear

Coplanarity by triple product Coplanarity hard

H2.Are (1, 0, 0), (0, 1, 0) and (1, 1, 1) coplanar? Justify by the triple product. [4]

  1. H1. Coplanar vectors have zero scalar triple product.
  2. H2. The triple product is the determinant with these vectors as rows.
  3. H3. The determinant of a triangular-looking array here equals 1.

[a b c] = |1 0 0; 0 1 0; 1 1 1| = 1·(1·1 − 0·1) = 1 ≠ 0

not coplanar — the triple product is 1, so they span a solid

Coplanarity by triple product Coplanarity hard

H3.Find λ so that (1, 1, 1), (2, 3, 4) and (1, λ, 3) are coplanar. [5]

  1. H1. Set the scalar triple product to zero.
  2. H2. Expand the determinant along the first row.
  3. H3. Solve the resulting linear equation in λ.

|1 1 1; 2 3 4; 1 λ 3| = 1(9 − 4λ) − 1(6 − 4) + 1(2λ − 3) = 4 − 2λ = 0

λ = 2

Volume applications Volume hard

H4.Find the volume of the parallelepiped whose edges are (1, 2, 3), (0, 1, 2) and (0, 0, 1). [3]

  1. H1. The volume is the absolute value of the scalar triple product.
  2. H2. With an upper-triangular array the determinant is the product of the diagonal.
  3. H3. 1 · 1 · 1 = 1.

|[a b c]| = |1·1·1| = 1

volume = 1 cubic unit

Volume applications Proof hard

H5.Prove that if a + b + c = 0 then [a b c] = 0. [4]

  1. H1. The scalar triple product is linear in each vector.
  2. H2. Substitute c = −a − b and expand.
  3. H3. A triple product with a repeated vector vanishes, so both terms are zero.

c = −(a + b) [a b c] = −[a b a] − [a b b] = 0 − 0

[a b c] = 0 — hence a, b, c are coplanar

Volume applications Volume hard

H6.Find the volume of the tetrahedron with vertices (0,0,0), (1,0,0), (0,1,0) and (0,0,1). [4]

  1. H1. Three edges from the origin are (1,0,0), (0,1,0), (0,0,1).
  2. H2. The tetrahedron is one sixth of the corresponding parallelepiped.
  3. H3. The triple product is 1.

V = (1/6)|[a b c]| = (1/6)(1)

volume = 1/6 cubic units
