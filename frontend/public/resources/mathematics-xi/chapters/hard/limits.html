Indeterminate forms Rationalisation hard

H1.Evaluate lim(x→0) (√(1 + x) − 1)/x. [3]

  1. H1. Direct substitution gives 0/0, so the form is indeterminate.
  2. H2. Rationalise the numerator by multiplying by its conjugate over itself.
  3. H3. The numerator then collapses to x, which cancels with the denominator.

[(1 + x) − 1]/[x(√(1 + x) + 1)] = x/[x(√(1 + x) + 1)] = 1/(√(1 + x) + 1) → 1/2

Standard limits Standard limits hard

H2.Evaluate lim(x→0) (tan x − sin x)/x³. [4]

  1. H1. Write tan x = sin x/cos x and take sin x out as a common factor.
  2. H2. The bracket becomes (1 − cos x)/cos x.
  3. H3. Split into three standard limits: sinx/x → 1, (1 − cosx)/x² → 1/2, 1/cosx → 1.

sinx(1 − cosx)/(x³ cosx) = (sinx/x)·[(1 − cosx)/x²]·(1/cosx) → 1 · 1/2 · 1

Limits at infinity Limits at infinity hard

H3.Evaluate lim(x→∞) (√(x² + x) − x). [4]

  1. H1. Multiply and divide by the conjugate √(x² + x) + x.
  2. H2. The numerator becomes (x² + x) − x² = x.
  3. H3. Divide numerator and denominator by x before letting x → ∞.

x/[√(x² + x) + x] = 1/[√(1 + 1/x) + 1] → 1/2

Indeterminate forms Factorising hard

H4.Evaluate lim(x→1) (x³ − 1)/(x² − 1). [3]

  1. H1. Both numerator and denominator vanish at x = 1.
  2. H2. Factor x³ − 1 = (x − 1)(x² + x + 1) and x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1).
  3. H3. Cancel (x − 1), then substitute x = 1.

(x² + x + 1)/(x + 1) → 3/2

Standard limits Standard limits hard

H5.Evaluate lim(x→0) (1 − cos 4x)/x². [3]

  1. H1. Use 1 − cos 2u = 2 sin²u with u = 2x.
  2. H2. Write sin²2x/x² as 4·(sin2x/2x)².
  3. H3. Use sinθ/θ → 1 as θ → 0.

1 − cos4x = 2sin²2x 2sin²2x/x² = 2·4·(sin2x/2x)² → 8

Indeterminate forms Proof hard

H6.Using first principles of limits, show that lim(x→a) (x³ − a³)/(x − a) = 3a². [4]

  1. H1. Factor the difference of cubes.
  2. H2. x³ − a³ = (x − a)(x² + ax + a²).
  3. H3. Cancel and substitute x = a.

(x − a)(x² + ax + a²)/(x − a) = x² + ax + a² → a² + a² + a²

3a² — the n = 3 case of lim (xⁿ − aⁿ)/(x − a) = n a^{n−1}
