Evaluation Evaluation hard

H1.Evaluate ∫₀^{π/2} sin²x dx. [4]

  1. H1. Linearise the integrand with the half-angle identity.
  2. H2. An antiderivative is x/2 − sin2x/4.
  3. H3. Substitute the limits; sin π = 0 removes the second term.

[x/2 − sin2x/4]₀^{π/2} = π/4 − 0

H2.Evaluate ∫₀¹ x eˣ dx. [4]

  1. H1. Integrate by parts with u = x, dv = eˣ dx.
  2. H2. The antiderivative is x eˣ − eˣ = eˣ(x − 1).
  3. H3. Evaluate at 1 and at 0.

[eˣ(x − 1)]₀¹ = e⁰(0) − e⁰(−1)

H3.Evaluate ∫₁^e ln x dx. [4]

  1. H1. Integrate by parts with u = ln x, dv = dx.
  2. H2. The antiderivative is x lnx − x.
  3. H3. Use ln e = 1 and ln 1 = 0.

[x lnx − x]₁^e = (e − e) − (0 − 1)

Area under a curve Area under a curve hard

H4.Use integration to find the area of the circle x² + y² = a². [5]

  1. H1. The upper semicircle is y = √(a² − x²) for −a ≤ x ≤ a.
  2. H2. By symmetry the area is 4∫₀^a √(a² − x²) dx.
  3. H3. Substitute x = a sin θ: the integrand becomes a²cos²θ and the limits 0 to π/2.

A = 4∫₀^a √(a² − x²)dx = 4·(πa²/4)

area = πa² square units

H5.Find the area enclosed between y = x² and y = x + 2. [5]

  1. H1. Solve x² = x + 2 to get the intersection abscissae.
  2. H2. On −1 < x < 2 the line lies above the parabola.
  3. H3. Integrate (x + 2) − x² between those limits.

x² − x − 2 = 0 ⇒ x = −1, 2 ∫₋₁² (x + 2 − x²)dx = [x²/2 + 2x − x³/3]₋₁² = (2 + 4 − 8/3) − (1/2 − 2 + 1/3) = 9/2

9/2 square units

H6.Evaluate ∫₀^{π/2} sin x/(1 + cos²x) dx. [5]

  1. H1. Substitute u = cos x, du = −sin x dx.
  2. H2. The limits become 1 and 0, which flips to ∫₀¹ du/(1 + u²).
  3. H3. That is the standard tan⁻¹ integral.

= ∫₀¹ du/(1 + u²) = [tan⁻¹u]₀¹ = tan⁻¹1 − tan⁻¹0 = π/4 − 0

π/4
