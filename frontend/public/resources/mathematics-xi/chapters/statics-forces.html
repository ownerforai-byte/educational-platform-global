Resolution of a force

Q1.Resolve 50 N acting at 30° to the horizontal. [4]

Fₓ = 50cos30° = 25√3, F_y = 50sin30° = 25

43.3 N and 25 N

Q2.Resolve 100 N at 45°. [4]

100cos45° and 100sin45°

50√2 ≈ 70.7 N each

Q3.Find the resultant of 6 N and 8 N at right angles. [2]

√(36 + 64)

Q4.Find the resultant of 7 N and 24 N at right angles. [2]

Resultant of two forces

Q5.Find the resultant of two 5 N forces at 60°. [5]

R = √(25 + 25 + 2·25·cos60°) = √75

5√3 ≈ 8.66 N

Resultant of two forces

Q6.Find the resultant of two 10 N forces at 120°. [5]

R = √(100 + 100 + 200(−0.5)) = √100

10 N

Equilibrium

Q7.Two forces of 3 N and 4 N act at right angles. What equilibrant is needed? [4]

Resultant 5 N; the equilibrant is equal and opposite

5 N, opposite the resultant

Equilibrium

Q8.State the equilibrium conditions for a particle. [2]

Both component sums must vanish.

ΣFₓ = 0 and ΣF_y = 0

Resultant of two forces

Q9.What is the maximum and minimum resultant of 10 N and 5 N? [4]

At 0° the forces add; at 180° they subtract

Maximum 15 N, minimum 5 N

Resultant of two forces

Q10.At what angle of the two equal forces is the resultant equal to either force? [5]

R² = 2F²(1 + cosθ) = F² ⇒ cosθ = −1/2

θ = 120°

Applied problems

Q11.A 20 N weight hangs from two strings each making 30° with the vertical. Find each tension. [5]

2T cos30° = 20 ⇒ T = 20/(2·0.866)

T ≈ 11.55 N

Equilibrium

Q12.Two forces give a zero resultant. What does that mean physically? [2]

The forces cancel exactly.

The particle is in equilibrium

Applied problems Explain

Q22.Why resolve forces before adding them? [2]

Components along one direction add as ordinary numbers.

Because perpendicular components are independent, so they can be summed separately

Resolution of a force Match

Q23.Match the cos and sin components with (a) vertical, (b) horizontal. [2]

Cosine follows the angle's adjacent side.

cos → (b), sin → (a)
