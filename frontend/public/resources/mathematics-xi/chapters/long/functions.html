Composite functions Composite and inverse long answer

L1.If f(x) = 2x + 1 and g(x) = x², then (a) find (g∘f)(x) and (f∘g)(x), (b) show that the two are not the same function, (c) find f⁻¹ and verify that f(f⁻¹(x)) = x. [6]

  1. H1. (g∘f)(x) means g(f(x)) — substitute f into the squaring function.
  2. H2. (f∘g)(x) means f(g(x)) — substitute x² into 2x + 1.
  3. H3. For the inverse, write y = 2x + 1 and make x the subject.

Given: f(x) = 2x + 1, g(x) = x² Required: both compositions, their difference, f⁻¹ and its check 1. (g∘f)(x) = g(2x + 1) = (2x + 1)² = 4x² + 4x + 1 2. (f∘g)(x) = f(x²) = 2x² + 1 3. at x = 1: 9 vs 3 ⇒ the functions differ 4. y = 2x + 1 ⇒ x = (y − 1)/2 ⇒ f⁻¹(x) = (x − 1)/2 5. f(f⁻¹(x)) = 2·(x − 1)/2 + 1 = x − 1 + 1 = x ✓ 6. domain and range of f are both ℝ, so f⁻¹ is defined on ℝ

(g∘f)(x) = 4x² + 4x + 1, (f∘g)(x) = 2x² + 1 (not equal); f⁻¹(x) = (x − 1)/2 and f(f⁻¹(x)) = x.

Marking split — each composition 1.5 + comparison 1 + inverse 1.5 + verification 1 = 6
Common pitfall — Writing f⁻¹(x) = 1/f(x). The inverse undoes the operation; it is never a reciprocal.
