Independence Laws of probability long answer

L1.For two events A and B, P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.2. Find (a) P(A ∪ B) and P(A | B), (b) P(A′ ∩ B′), (c) whether A and B are independent, with justification. [6]

  1. H1. The addition law gives the union; the multiplication law gives the condition.
  2. H2. A′ ∩ B′ is the complement of the union (De Morgan).
  3. H3. Independence means P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B).

Given: P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5, P(A ∩ B) = 0.2 Required: union, conditional, both-complements, independence 1. P(A ∪ B) = 0.4 + 0.5 − 0.2 = 0.7 2. P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B) = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4 3. P(A′ ∩ B′) = 1 − P(A ∪ B) = 1 − 0.7 = 0.3 4. P(A)P(B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2 = P(A ∩ B) 5. also P(A | B) = 0.4 = P(A), a second confirmation 6. hence A and B are independent but not mutually exclusive

P(A ∪ B) = 0.7, P(A | B) = 0.4, P(A′ ∩ B′) = 0.3; A and B are independent since P(A ∩ B) = P(A)P(B).

Marking split — union 1.5 + conditional 1.5 + complement 1.5 + independence with reason 1.5 = 6
Common pitfall — Concluding "independent" from "not mutually exclusive". Independence compares a product; exclusivity compares with zero.
