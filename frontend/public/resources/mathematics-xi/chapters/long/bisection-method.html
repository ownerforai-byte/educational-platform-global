L1.The equation x³ − 2x − 5 = 0 has a root in [2, 3]. (a) Perform three bisection steps. (b) State the bracket after each step and the maximum error after the third step. [6]

  1. H1. f(2) = −1 and f(3) = 16, so the sign change is confirmed.
  2. H2. Take the midpoint, evaluate f, and keep the half where the sign change survives.
  3. H3. After n steps the error is at most width/2ⁿ.

Given: f(x) = x³ − 2x − 5, bracket [2, 3] Required: three bisection steps, the final bracket, the error bound 1. f(2) = 8 − 4 − 5 = −1, f(3) = 27 − 6 − 5 = 16 ⇒ root in (2, 3) 2. x₁ = 2.5, f = 15.625 − 5 − 5 = 5.625 > 0 ⇒ [2, 2.5] 3. x₂ = 2.25, f = 11.3906 − 4.5 − 5 = 1.8906 > 0 ⇒ [2, 2.25] 4. x₃ = 2.125, f = 9.5957 − 4.25 − 5 = 0.3457 > 0 ⇒ [2, 2.125] 5. error bound after 3 steps: 1/2³ = 0.125 6. estimate of the root: x₄ ≈ 2.0625, close to the true root 2.0946

brackets [2, 2.5] → [2, 2.25] → [2, 2.125]; maximum error 0.125 and root ≈ 2.06 (true value 2.0946).

Marking split — sign check 1 + three steps 4.5 + error bound 0.5 = 6
Common pitfall — Keeping the half with the same sign at both ends. The bracket must always straddle the sign change.
