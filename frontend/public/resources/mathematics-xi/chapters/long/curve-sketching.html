Standard curves Sketching long answer

L1.For y = x² − 2x − 3, (a) write the vertex form and state the vertex, (b) find all intercepts and the axis of symmetry, (c) describe the graph of y = |x² − 2x − 3| and say where it is not differentiable. [6]

  1. H1. Complete the square for the x-terms: x² − 2x = (x − 1)² − 1.
  2. H2. Factor the quadratic for the x-intercepts.
  3. H3. The modulus reflects the part below the axis upward, creating corners at the roots.

Given: y = x² − 2x − 3 Required: vertex form, intercepts, axis, the modulus graph and its corners 1. y = (x − 1)² − 4 ⇒ vertex (1, −4), opening upward 2. y = (x − 3)(x + 1) ⇒ x-intercepts −1 and 3 3. x = 0 ⇒ y = −3, so the y-intercept is −3; axis of symmetry x = 1 4. |y| keeps the branches outside [−1, 3] and folds the dip upward 5. the folded graph has corners at x = −1 and x = 3 with different one-sided slopes 6. hence |y| is continuous everywhere but not differentiable at x = −1, 3

vertex (1, −4); intercepts (−1, 0), (3, 0), (0, −3); axis x = 1; the modulus graph has corners at x = −1 and x = 3 where it is not differentiable.

Marking split — vertex form 1.5 + intercepts 1.5 + axis and y-intercept 1 + modulus description 1 + corners 1 = 6
Common pitfall — Reflecting about the x-axis instead of folding the negative part up — |y| never goes below the axis.
