Coplanarity by triple product Scalar triple product long answer

L1.Show that the points A(0, 0, 0), B(1, 2, 3), C(2, 1, 4) and D(3, 3, 7) are coplanar, and state what the vanishing scalar triple product means geometrically. [6]

  1. H1. Form the three vectors AB, AC, AD from the given points.
  2. H2. The points are coplanar exactly when the scalar triple product is zero.
  3. H3. Evaluate the 3×3 determinant with those vectors as rows.

Given: A(0,0,0), B(1,2,3), C(2,1,4), D(3,3,7) Required: coplanarity proof and its geometric meaning 1. AB = (1, 2, 3), AC = (2, 1, 4), AD = (3, 3, 7) 2. [AB AC AD] = 1(1·7 − 4·3) − 2(2·7 − 4·3) + 3(2·3 − 1·3) 3. = 1(7 − 12) − 2(14 − 12) + 3(6 − 3) 4. = −5 − 4 + 9 = 0 5. the scalar triple product is the volume of the parallelepiped; volume zero 6. so the three vectors are linearly dependent and all four points lie in one plane

[AB AC AD] = 0, so A, B, C, D are coplanar — the parallelepiped has zero volume and one vector is a combination of the other two.

Marking split — vectors formed 1.5 + determinant 2.5 + conclusion 2 = 6
Common pitfall — Forming vectors between the wrong pairs of points, which mixes up the determinant rows.
