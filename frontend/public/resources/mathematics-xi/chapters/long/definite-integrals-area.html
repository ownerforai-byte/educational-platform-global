Area under a curve Area by integration long answer

L1.(a) Find the area enclosed by y = 4x − x² and the x-axis. (b) Find the area of the region bounded by y = x² and the line y = 4. [6]

  1. H1. The parabola cuts the x-axis at x = 0 and x = 4.
  2. H2. Area under a curve is the definite integral between the two roots.
  3. H3. For (b) the curves meet where x² = 4, so x = ±2; integrate the upper curve minus the lower one.

Given: y = 4x − x² and the pair y = x², y = 4 Required: both enclosed areas 1. (a) roots: x(4 − x) = 0 ⇒ x = 0, 4; the vertex (2, 4) lies above the axis 2. A = ∫₀⁴(4x − x²)dx = [2x² − x³/3]₀⁴ 3. = 32 − 64/3 = 32/3 square units 4. (b) x² = 4 ⇒ x = ±2, and y = 4 is the upper boundary 5. A = ∫₋₂²(4 − x²)dx = [4x − x³/3]₋₂² 6. = (8 − 8/3) − (−8 + 8/3) = 32/3 ≈ 10.67 square units

(a) 32/3 ≈ 10.67 square units (b) 32/3 ≈ 10.67 square units.

Marking split — roots and integral 2 + value 1 + intersection points 1 + symmetric integral 2 = 6
Common pitfall — Integrating without checking where the curves cross, which silently adds signed areas of opposite sign.
