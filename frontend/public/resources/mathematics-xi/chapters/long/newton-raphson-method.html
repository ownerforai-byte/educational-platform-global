L1.Use the Newton–Raphson method with x₀ = 2 to find the root of x³ − 2x − 5 = 0 correct to three decimal places. (a) State the iteration formula. (b) Perform two iterations and comment on the speed of convergence. [6]

  1. H1. f′(x) = 3x² − 2, and x₁ = x₀ − f(x₀)/f′(x₀).
  2. H2. f(2) = −1 and f′(2) = 10.
  3. H3. Compare with the three bisection steps needed for the same accuracy.

Given: f(x) = x³ − 2x − 5, x₀ = 2 Required: the iteration formula, two steps, and a note on convergence 1. xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − (xₙ³ − 2xₙ − 5)/(3xₙ² − 2) 2. x₁ = 2 − (−1)/10 = 2.1 3. f(2.1) = 9.261 − 4.2 − 5 = 0.061; f′(2.1) = 13.23 − 2 = 11.23 4. x₂ = 2.1 − 0.061/11.23 = 2.1 − 0.00543 = 2.09457 5. x₂ = 2.095 to three decimals, matching the true root 2.09455 6. two steps beat three bisection steps, and each further step roughly doubles the correct digits

xₙ₊₁ = xₙ − (xₙ³ − 2xₙ − 5)/(3xₙ² − 2); x₁ = 2.1, x₂ ≈ 2.0946, so the root is 2.095 (three decimals) after two steps — quadratic convergence.

Marking split — formula 1.5 + x₁ 1 + x₂ 2 + comment 1.5 = 6
Common pitfall — Starting where f′ is small. Newton's method fails or wanders when the tangent is nearly horizontal.
