Continuity and differentiability Continuity and differentiability long answer

L1.Let f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x − 1) for x ≠ 1 and f(1) = k. (a) Find k so that f is continuous at x = 1. (b) With that k, examine the differentiability of g(x) = |x − 2| at x = 2. [6]

  1. H1. Continuity at 1 requires lim(x→1) f(x) = f(1).
  2. H2. Factor x² − 1 and cancel before taking the limit.
  3. H3. At x = 2 compare the left and right derivatives of the modulus.

Given: f(x) = (x² − 1)/(x − 1) for x ≠ 1, f(1) = k; g(x) = |x − 2| Required: k and the differentiability of g at 2 1. lim(x→1) (x² − 1)/(x − 1) = lim (x + 1) = 2 2. continuity needs lim = f(1) ⇒ k = 2 3. g(2) = 0 and lim(x→2) g(x) = 0 ⇒ g is continuous at 2 4. left derivative: d/dx(2 − x) = −1; right derivative: d/dx(x − 2) = 1 5. the one-sided derivatives differ, so the derivative at 2 does not exist 6. geometrically: a corner — continuity does not imply differentiability

(a) k = 2 (b) g is continuous at x = 2 but not differentiable there, because the left and right derivatives are −1 and 1.

Marking split — limit 1.5 + k 1 + continuity of g 1.5 + one-sided derivatives 2 = 6
Common pitfall — Assuming a removable discontinuity is automatically differentiable once repaired — differentiability needs equal one-sided slopes.
